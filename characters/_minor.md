# Walk-ons and figures too small for a sheet

*One line each: who they are, where they are seen, and in which chapter. Named characters with a real role get a full sheet instead. This file is private and is not printed in the book.*

- **The child under the dashboard** — the nine- or ten-year-old in the minivan on the expressway, whom Dan went back for and got out before the fire took the van; never named in the book. Seen in the Chicago break, Ch 1. She lives and grows on Earth, and it is the hot trace attached to her, not to Nina, that one day offers a way home (T020); his last warmth went into her and he never knew her name.
- **The woman singing below the birth room** — a neighbour in Low Kiln whose low song comes up through the floor of House Silt with its words worn flat by work; heard, never seen or named, Ch 1. Her song is the room's second pulse, counted against Dessa's breathing.
- **The woman at the tub** — an unnamed neighbour in the Clay Yard who looks up as Sella carries the covered bundle up the yard, then goes back to her work without appearing to have seen it; glimpsed through the fold of the wool, Ch 2. The district's ordinary witness, who saw a woman carrying a bundle and nothing more.

- **The Sill assessor** — an officer of the Warm Court at the Account-day moot at the Cross; narrow clean collar and hands that have never touched a pot, pig or carrying cord, so that his work can be read off his grip before his collar. Gave one line, *The figure stands as read*, to the slate rather than to the crowd, and settled his shut ledger under his arm; Ch 3. He is an officer with a reason, not a monster, and he closes a matter before it has properly opened. If he speaks a second time, give him a full sheet.

- **The collector's clerk at the Cross** — a narrow man at the Court's table at the Slate Row end of the Cross, with a cuff gone soft from washing and ink in the creases of two otherwise ordinary-coloured fingers. Put his hand on the top slate before he looked at the woman waiting and said, in a soft voice with no edge, *It's entered as it stands. There's nothing in it the household need correct.* Ch 3. He is not Marra and must not be joined to the grey coat or to grey fingers; the neat upper line on his slate is unread to the boy, who sees only the pause between strokes. If he speaks a second time, give him a full sheet.

- **The collector at the Hearth Drawing** — an unnamed officer who came in out of the cold with a flat leather satchel, took a cold iron tube from a roll of cloth, found the way in a dying neighbour's forearm by touch and held it without pressing; cuff soft from washing and folded back twice, fingertips grey at the tips the colour of skimmed milk gone off. Set his hand on the slate before he looked at the bed and spoke once, to the slate: *The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded.* He has a reason --- he believes he keeps the world alive --- and is not Marra and not the clerk at the Cross; the neat ordinary hand he writes in has no pause in it. Ch 4. If he speaks a second time, give him a full sheet.
- **The neighbour at the Drawing** — a man of Low Kiln whose age the grey had taken off, lying on a board-bed against the far wall with his hands crossed on his belly; gave under the cold iron tube with the small flat knock and was left with a pale round place on his arm where the iron had been, not a wound and no blood. Ch 4.
- **The neighbour mother of the rekindled child** — came under the Vail roof on Stone 11 with a child three days out of a thin return and sat later beside him on a quilt in the death-house with a cup of warm milk she never drank; asked whether he would know her if he came through and was told a child knows warmth before it knows a face. Ch 4. Her child, grey at the fingertips and worse on the third day, with a red mark at the hairline, gave before the day was out; the room called it a graft settling. Ch 4.
- **The death-house women** — the woman at the wrist who counted a pulse and began the count again when it was interrupted; the woman who pressed a fold flat over a chest as a hand might keep warmth in by holding it down; the girl at the hearth who moved the pot out of the draught and spoke to it about the thaw; and the older one who said *warmth goes in slow; you can't hurry a graft; my mother knew it and her mother knew it*. They corrected the boy without knowing he had spoken. Ch 4.
- **The woman from two houses down** — put her head in at the Vail threshold in the middle of Stone 11 and asked after the boy without asking after him; got *warm* from Sella, said to the fire, and went away with the answer she had come for. Ch 4.
