## Appearance

Twenty-seven, broad-hipped, with eel slime dried on her sleeves and a small smile she keeps on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch: it can be shut without anybody noticing the movement. The top half of her left ear is missing, taken by a knife in a Fishstairs quarrel when she was nineteen; she says a hook did it, and the scar says otherwise, and nobody has ever made anything of it. She smells of river silt, salted perch, tallow smoke and the cold off the water. She carries a string of eel-bones in her pocket and flicks them against her thumb when she is deciding whether to lie, which is often. The thing people notice first is the smile; the thing they notice last is that she never sits down at a table where a slate is open.

## Voice & Manner

Fast, low, with the proverb turned sideways. She answers a question with a question and then answers it herself, which gives her the room to choose which answer the listener gets. She never tells a story with a beginning; she starts at the part where somebody is already in trouble. She has learned from Fen traders to mark accounts in knots rather than on slate, and will not write a name down if a knot will hold it. When she is being kind she becomes brisk, because kindness in her house is a thing done quickly before anyone can make a speech about it.

- “You want to know. You don't want to know. I can see which one's winning.”
- “The secret's in what happens to it after. I didn't tell anybody anything. There's nothing to tell. That's the whole of it.”
- “It came from a warm house, that's all. There's barley in it and a little smoke and it's a crust, not a sermon.”
- “Nineteen steps. Your sister counts them. I count the ones she doesn't take.”
- “Don't ask a Fishstairs woman what she saw. She'll sell it to you and then it's yours and you'll have to carry it.”
- “He's warm. That's all I'll say on a slate, and I'm saying it off the slate.”
- “I kept your secret for three years, Dessa. Don't make it a fourth.”

## History

Born in Low Kiln to the same thin-returned mother as Dessa, Mira learned the eel trade at the Fishstairs at twelve and has worked the dawn market there ever since. She knows which households pay and which owe, which collectors take their ale before a collection and which after, and which boats on the Ilm are carrying something other than fish. She has been the district's informal ledger of who is warm and who is cooling for fifteen years, and has never once put a name on paper.

She was in the room on Stone 7, Y.H. 812, when her sister bore the child with the branching red flower. She saw Sella Vail carry a covered bundle up the Clay Yard three days later, and she understood what had been done without being told. Alda Reed told Dessa the boy was among the Forty; Mira let her believe it. She has let her believe it for three years, and has taken a share of the barley Sella pays every winter, which she spends on Dessa, who does not know where the barley comes from. She has a child of her own, returned thin in Y.H. 802 to a household in Nine Pots, who does not recognize her; she has walked to Nine Pots twice and never knocked.

## Wounds & Fears

Her sister's lie is hers too, and she has begun to feel the weight of it in the hand that holds the eel knife. She fears that telling Dessa the truth will put the boy on a slate that someone can use against him; she fears more that if she waits much longer the truth will come out in a quarrel, which is the worst way for a thing like that to be said. She fears she has become a woman who trades in other people's warmth and calls it looking after them. Her contradiction: she gossips all day at the Fishstairs and has kept the one secret that matters for three years without a single slip.

## Desires

She wants Dessa to stop reaching for a child she believes is dead, and she wants to be believed once about something that is not a rumour. She thinks she wants the truth to come out, and has arranged her life so that it never has to. What she needs is to understand that a secret kept out of love is still a debt, and that it gathers Ash like any other debt, and that the only way to stop paying interest on it is to say the thing.

## Bonds

**Dessa Silt** — her sister, her room-mate, the woman whose child she has hidden for three years. Dessa suspects; Mira has begun to be afraid of the moment Dessa stops suspecting and knows. Everything between them is said in the language of barley, coughs and the nineteen steps. *Ch 1:* she crossed the room and pressed a crust of Vail-smoked barley into Dessa's hand in two brisk movements, stopping short of the slate to do it.

**Corin** — the nephew she watches from a distance at the market and once followed as far as the Clay Yard. She has never called him anything, not even in her head, because a name is a thing that can be overheard. *Ch 1:* she would not hold him and would not sit where the slate lay open, but pushed the wool back under him briskly with two movements before anyone could make a speech about it. *Ch 3:* a background figure at the eel stall in the afternoon, eel slime dried pale on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone; she flicked an eel-bone against her thumb and never once looked at the basket. When she turned to go she shoved a loose board clear of the barrow men's path with the side of her hand, brisk, her face still toward the eels. She had not looked at him once all day. He put her not-looking beside the man on the block's: one priced him, the other kept him, and he had no word in Grathic for either.

**Sella Vail** — they have an arrangement: barley and silence. Mira has begun to resent the barley and has never once refused it, which is the precise shape of her complicity. *Ch 1:* she watched the hand in the door without moving, her thumb quiet in her pocket and the eel-bone put away.

**Alda Reed** — they trade small facts and never the large one. Alda knows more about the birth; Mira knows more about what happened after it. Neither will sit at a table with a slate on it, and they have built a friendship entirely out of things said standing up. *Ch 1:* they worked the same room standing, each with her back to the writing.

**Orra Weir** — her supplier at the Weir and her oldest friend, who wants the secret out and has said so twice. Mira has answered both times with the weather.

**Berr Clay, the Thane of Clay** — buys her eels for the levy feast and pays in marks that are short by two. She short-weights him by two eels. This small war has been going on for six years and neither has acknowledged it. *Ch 3:* at the eel stall she asked him the price and answered it herself --- *two marks the pair or two marks the pair and two short? Don't trouble yourself. It's two marks the pair and two short, and I've counted them twice, and I'll count them again after I've eaten.* He did not answer, because she had left him nothing in the sentence that wanted an answer. She moved an eel-bone from hand to hand and said a figure under her breath. The knot-string is still not shown; only the counting is.

**Tarn** — the Vail's clay-bound hand. She has suspected for a year that he is more than the debt he says he is, and has not asked, because she recognizes a person who keeps a secret and knows better than to open him.

## Arc So Far

She is in the birth room from the first hours of Day 1 and is already arranged: the smile kept on the face like a door on the latch, the back turned to the slate, the eel-bones flicked and put away. She says the one true thing in the room --- that the secret is in what happens to a child after it is born --- in a form that can be taken as nothing, and follows it with *there's nothing to tell*. In Chapter 3 she is seen for the first time in her own element, the eel stall at the Cross, and the arrangement holds: dried slime, the missing top of her left ear, a question asked and answered by herself, a board shoved clear for the barrow men, and not one look into the basket all afternoon. The knot-string and the barley account are still offstage; the woman who knows and will not say is not. Her marker is reconciled to Chapter 1: she is on stage here, not first seen at the Fishstairs in Chapter 5.

On Days 8 and 9 the woman who knows is seen in her own element for two mornings, and the keeping becomes a craft with tools: eel-bones moved while an answer is chosen, a cord touched when a figure is spoken, a second cord kept apart and never taken to the door it belongs to. The little war with Berr is given its exact arithmetic --- four eels for six, two marks short for six years --- and neither will name it. She answers the question about the basket without letting the sentence want a reply, and gives the warmth briskly before anyone can make a speech about it, which is how kindness is done in her house. The knot-string itself is still not shown plainly and the knots are not counted; what the boy sees is the hand, the movement and the difference between two cords, and he has no word for any of it.

On Days 15 through 24 the two records stand side by side in the fold and the yard. On Stone 28 she answers a question about the basket with a lie in the form of a proverb, touches the grey cord when a figure is spoken, and gives the boy a fresh crust briskly, the second warmth in the fold beside the spent one from Stone 15. Both are entered nowhere and neither is named; a gift done quickly is a gift no one can make a speech about. The separate cord twisted twice below the grey shows again as she shifts the basket, still unknotted by any hand but her own and still not taken to the thin child upriver. Dessa's cough comes up from the pits three doors down and is called weather, and the sister who knows keeps the knowing under the same small smile. The boy sees the hand and the twist and counts neither, and cannot thank either woman for the bread.

## Secrets (private)

1. She has known since Y.H. 812 that Corin is Dessa's son, and has never told a soul, including the person who would most want to know.
2. She once followed Sella to the Vail kiln-house and saw the boy through a window, standing at a pot with his head tilted. She did not go in, and has replayed the moment of not going in more often than she would admit.
3. The barley she takes from Sella is spent on Dessa. She has therefore been paid for three years to keep her own sister ignorant, and has never entered this in any account, not even the knot-string.
4. Her child in Nine Pots is alive, thin, and does not know her. She has a knot for him too, on a separate cord, and has never shown it to anyone.
5. She will be the one who finally tells Jorra Vail where Corin came from, during a quarrel about eels, and will regret it before the sentence is finished.

## Plans (private)

Keep her as the woman who knows — the everyday intelligence network of Low Kiln, the person who can tell Dan who is warm and who is cooling without ever appearing to tell him anything. She dies late, not early, and her last act is to refuse to sell a secret to a Court clerk who has put marks on the table; she slips out of the room instead, which is how she has always left rooms.

Let her knot-string be a quiet counterpoint to Orra's forty knots. One woman counts what was taken; one counts what was kept. When Dan finally reads both, he will understand that the district has been keeping its own accounts in materials the Court cannot confiscate, which is the first lesson in the book about how to keep a record that an institution cannot own.
