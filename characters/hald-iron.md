## Appearance

Fifty-four, short and built sideways, with the heavy shoulders of a man who has spent thirty years bracing against a stone face. His measure-number is tattooed on his left forearm in Keldish numerals and has been cut out and replaced twice, because a mastery that carries more than it did must be entered again; around the old numbers the skin is pale and puckered, so that the arm reads like a ledger with corrections. His right palm is a flat shiny graft from a trial of hot iron he lost at thirty and has never explained. He wears a clamp at his belt, not as a weapon but as a tool a smith carries, and he cleans it while he thinks. He smells of hot iron, lead dust, coal smoke and the metallic sweat of the deep seam. The thing people notice first is the palm; the thing they notice last is that when he is genuinely interested in a man, he states that man's measure aloud, which in Keld is a kind of attention and in the lowlands is an insult.

## Voice & Manner

Clipped, numerical, with the number before the noun — *forty of head, not capacity; two fingers, gone; one way, open*. He states a measure before a name and expects the same courtesy. He never uses a word where a figure will do and has a habit of counting aloud under his breath while he watches a person move, which unnerves lowlanders and comforts Keldish men, who know he is reading their ways. He does not explain a procedure twice. When he is pleased he says *that will hold*, which is the highest praise the floor knows and sounds like a judgement on a pot.

- "Number first. Then the name. Everyone in Keld knows this. The lowlands are full of men who won't learn it and are surprised when they're checked."
- "You want the first way. I can cut it. I can't make you want it afterwards. That's the part nobody teaches and everybody pays."
- "Forty-two. No. Forty and a half. You're carrying more than you think, and it's showing at the wrist."
- "The paper is read once a year. It is not used. There's a difference, and the Order has lived on that difference for a hundred years."
- "That will hold. Don't make me say it twice. I won't."

## History

He was born in Keld Keep and went down the First Seam at thirteen for his Descent, where his number changed from thirty-one to thirty-eight and he was given the keys instead of the pick, which the Keldish consider a kindness and is not one. He became a duellist of the Order of the Tenth Clamp, won the floor at twenty-nine, and lost the use of his right palm in a trial of hot iron at thirty, after which he had it grafted flat and never held a clamp in that hand again. He has taught the first way to four hundred young Keldish men and has watched a third of them open a way they could not close and go cold in a season. He has never taught a lowlander, because the Order's method is the only thing Keld owns that the Court cannot tax.

In Y.H. 710 he was given one of the nine copies of Haldre's suppressed paper to burn. He burned eight. He has kept his copy in a tin lined with warm-stone in the First Seam and has read it on the Last Day of every year since, which is fifty-two readings, and has never once agreed with it in a way that would make him act. In Y.H. 806 he was shown the crossed-out survey entry of Y.H. 588 — a returned man with a branching red mark on his forearm and ribs — and he copied the shape of the mark onto the back of his own tally, where it sits under the numbers. He has never shown it to a Vorr, who would only say that the numbers were correct.

When a lowlander boy with an unusual ear arrives at the floor he will be measured before he is welcomed, and Hald will find something in the boy's ways that he does not have a number for, which will interest him more than anything in thirty years. He will offer to cut the first way and will name the price honestly: a fortnight of uselessness, a permanent mark, and the understanding that a way once cut is never closed. He will not tell the boy the other price, which is that the Order has kept Haldre's paper for a century precisely so that it never has to be used.

## Wounds & Fears

The palm. And the thirty-four young men whose names he keeps in a tally he does not show, because a man who teaches the first way is responsible for what opens afterwards. He fears the Sweating, which he has survived twice and smelled a hundred times in the hot-lead warning; he fears that the Order's secrecy has made it a museum instead of a guild; and he fears the boy with the mark, because a mender's failure is a thing the Keldish call a birth-flower and consider unlucky, and Hald has spent fifty years deciding that he does not believe in luck. His contradiction: the man who would not burn the paper has built his life on not using it, and calls that prudence, when it is the same refusal Garl Vail made with a different tool.

## Desires

He wants the method to stay in Keldish hands and the floor to keep its men alive, which are sometimes the same want and often not. He thinks he wants the boy to fail, so that the Order's caution is vindicated. What he needs is to teach a man who will use the way for something other than a duel — who will cut a way to stop a leak rather than to push a man off a ledge — and to learn that a method kept unused for a hundred years is not knowledge but a debt.

## Bonds

**Rusk of the Weir** — offered a place on the floor thirty years ago and refused it. Hald has never forgiven the refusal and has never stopped being glad of it, because a man who asks questions in that voice would be dangerous with a first way. They have not spoken since.

**Siv of the First Seam** — his surveyor and the keeper of the crossed-out page. Hald respects the man's patience and distrusts what he finds in the stone, which is the proper Keldish attitude toward the deep records. Siv has shown him the mark once; Hald has copied it; neither has said what they think it means.

**Corin** — the lowlander with the ear. Hald has decided he is a student and a threat in the same person, which is how the floor has always classified its best men. He will be fair to him, which in Keld is a form of respect, and will not protect him from the price of the way.

**Karric VII, Warden of the Rim** — the ruler who keeps the true-Hearth claim alive for reasons both men know are commercial. Hald serves him and has never believed him, which is another difference that has kept Keld independent.

**Haldre** — whose paper he reads annually and whose ending he intends never to bring about. Hald has begun, in the last ten years, to wonder whether refusing to use a thing is the same as refusing to believe it, and has not entered the question in any tally.

## Secrets (private)

1. He burned eight of the nine copies of Haldre's paper in Y.H. 710 and has kept the ninth. He reads it on the Last Day and intends never to act on it.
2. He has copied the branching red mark from the crossed-out survey of Y.H. 588 onto the back of his tally, and has recognised the same shape described in a report from the lowlands about a foundling with a flower on his ribs. He has not connected the two aloud.
3. He keeps a tally of the thirty-four men he taught who went cold after opening a way. Their names are entered in numerals, because a Keldish man writes a number where another would write a grief.
4. He will offer Corin the first way without telling him the full price, and will be honest about the partial price, which is how the Order has always taught: truth enough to consent, not enough to refuse.
5. When Dan reaches the Welled rank and overreaches, Hald will be the one who says *that will hold* and then watches it not hold, and will not comfort him, because comfort is a lowland habit and the floor has no use for it.

## Plans (private)

Let him be the mentor whose price is arithmetic: a number, a palm, a fortnight, a way that does not close. He is the first teacher who treats Dan as a vessel with a measurable head rather than a curiosity, and the first who will not flatter him. His kindness is a correction given in time.

Keep the paper alive in his tin for hundreds of chapters — a plant that looks like a footnote about a heretic and will one day be read as the same ending the Court wants, spoken in Keldish numerals. Hald never becomes Unbanked and never becomes Dan's ally in the full sense. He becomes a man who taught a mender to cut and then had to watch what the mender cut with it, and who will say, at the end, *that will hold*, meaning the boy, meaning the floor, meaning nothing that can be proved.
