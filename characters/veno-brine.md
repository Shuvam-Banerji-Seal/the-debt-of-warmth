## Appearance

Forty-six and carefully dressed in a lowland cut that he has never quite got right, which he does not care about because the clothes are for the people he deals with and not for himself. He is a narrow Vhaleni man with a client's smile that he can hold for an entire negotiation and drop the instant the door closes, which the clerks of Greybank have learned to read as the true end of the meeting. His hands are soft, with salt-paper stains at the fingertips, and he never carries a pen, because a factor who is seen writing is a factor whose word can be checked. He has a gold crown mounted in a ring which he wears on his little finger and which he has never earned in Grath, a detail he considers the only beautiful thing about the place. He smells of tar, wet rope, expensive soap and — when a Sere matter is in hand — myrrh, which is the smell of the empire and of a room where a person has been entered. The thing people notice first is the smile; the thing they notice last is that he never touches a slate, not even to move it, which in a counting house is a form of armour.

## Voice & Manner

Vhaleni sibilance flattened for lowland ears, with long pauses and questions that contain their own answers. He says *the document*, *the weather*, *the understanding*, and never *the contract*, because a contract is a thing that can be read and he has built his life on things that cannot. He speaks of people as positions — *the widow, thane, the collector* — and has a way of offering kindness with a figure attached, so that the recipient must decide which part was the gift, which is the whole art of his trade. When he is genuinely angered he becomes courteous to a degree that makes the room cold, because everyone has seen what precedes it. He has two laughs: one for clients, warm and brief, and one for himself, which is a single breath through the nose and means he has just been found out and does not intend to admit it.

- "The paper is damp. It's a bad season for documents. In Vhalen we consider that a virtue. A law that lasts too long stops earning, doesn't it?"
- "I have not paid you. I have advanced you. You'll see the difference when the grain comes."
- "Forty measures for one return. Ask me why forty and I'll tell you the figure is the figure. Ask me again in private and I'll still tell you the figure is the figure."
- "I don't buy people. I buy the shape of them. A beautiful return must remember enough to be exhibited. There's a difference, and the Council calls it civility."
- "You want the girl. I want the share. We'll write it down in the rain and see which of us has a document in the morning."

## History

He was born in Vhal to a family with no boat-share and an unusual talent for reading documents that had been left in the rain, which in the salt republic is the most marketable gift a man can have. He worked the counting house, learned that a contract on salt-paper in damp weather is no contract at all, and spent twelve years turning that fact into a fortune and two enemies, one of whom he later bought out with a damp document and a warm smile. He came up the Salt Road to Greybank in Y.H. 796 as a factor for Vhaleni glass and stayed, because the north buys measures and a factor who can buy a debt before it is due is a factor who never needs to buy the person. He has represented Sere interests in the north since Y.H. 803 without ever admitting to the Sun-Emperor's Council that he works for them, and has sold grain, glass, quiet ash and whole returns to people who believe they are dealing with a Vhaleni and not an empire.

In the first weeks of Stone, Y.H. 812, he carried the slate for a private whole return from the Greybank factor to the collector in Sill, and could not spell the purchaser's name, which he has never told anyone and has never forgotten. He has spent the years since trying to work out who bought forty children's warmth for a foundling with a flower, because a man who cannot identify a buyer is a man who cannot charge him again, which in his profession is a form of insult. He has begun to suspect the Unbanked, then the Court, then a third thing that looks like both, and has kept each suspicion in a different document, all of them damp.

He has kept Solene Ember in a room with good light since Y.H. 810, which he describes to visitors as protection and to himself as an investment that has not yet matured. He has twice offered to buy Pia of the Quay's mother's boat-share and been refused twice, which he has entered as a debt owed to him by a girl who does not know she owes it.

## Wounds & Fears

He has no house and no share, which in Vhalen makes him a man whose account is always current and never his own. His wound is that he can turn any document into nothing and therefore cannot make anything that lasts, which is a strange hunger in a man who deals in permanent arrangements. He fears wet paper in the wrong hands, which is the only thing that can undo him, and fears more than that a person who reads a contract as a person rather than a shape, which is what the foundling from Low Kiln does when he looks at a vessel. His contradiction: the man whose whole trade is the convenient disappearance of evidence has kept three damp documents dry in a private drawer for ten years, because a factor who keeps nothing can never be blackmailed and a factor who keeps everything can, and he has chosen to be the second kind in secret and the first kind in public.

## Desires

He wants a boat-share, which would make him a Vhaleni instead of a man who deals with them, and he wants to know who bought the whole return in Y.H. 812, because an unspelled name is the only account in his life he cannot close. He thinks he wants the north to remain a place where a damp document is a fortune. What he needs is to understand that a person with no account is cheaper than a person with one, which is the Council's doctrine and his own, and that he has spent his life making people cheaper in order to call himself civility. When he finally learns the purchaser's name, he will not say it aloud; he will write it on salt-paper in the rain and watch it disappear, which is the only honest thing he has ever done with a document.

## Bonds

**Solene Ember** — the beautiful return he keeps in a room with good light. He is kind to her in the exact way a creditor is kind, and she has never mistaken it, which is why he keeps her and why he distrusts her. He has begun to fear that she will refuse a fourth return, which would be a loss and a precedent, in that order.

**Pia of the Quay** — the salt girl who refused his offer for her mother's share twice. He has entered the refusal as a debt and has never collected, which in his vocabulary is the beginning of an interest he cannot name.

**Marra** — the collector whose slate passed through his hands in Y.H. 812 and whose name appeared on a document he was not supposed to read. He has not told anyone and has used the knowledge once, gently, in a negotiation, which is how he tests what a thing is worth.

**Berr Clay, the Thane of Clay** — who signed the levy he carried and forgot the purchaser's name deliberately. Veno has never pressed him, because a Thane who has forgotten a thing on purpose is a Thane who can be made to remember it at the right moment, which is a kind of leverage he has kept warm for ten years like a coal under ash.

**Corin** — the foundling whose name was written before a face. Veno has tried three times to buy his ear for the College or the Sere factors and has been refused three times, and has begun to suspect that the boy is not an account at all but a creditor, which is a category he has no word for.

**Avis Wake** — a Council member who votes against taxing the glass boats and whom Veno pays to do so, which is an arrangement the Council calls representation and Veno calls the cheapest insurance in Vhalen.

## Secrets (private)

1. He carried the slate for Corin's whole return in Y.H. 812 and could not spell the purchaser's name. He has kept a damp copy of that document for ten years and has never shown it to anyone.
2. He represents Sere interests in the north without admitting it to the Council of Tides, and has sold northern measures to southern houses for nine years, which is the trade that buys poor districts and returns them beautiful.
3. He has kept three incriminating documents dry in a private drawer while keeping every other document in his life damp, which is the private architecture of a man who has built a fortune on the convenient disappearance of evidence.
4. He sold quiet ash once, unlicensed, to a Fen runner, and has never used it himself, because a man who forgets a previous life is a man who cannot add, and adding is the only thing he has ever trusted.
5. When he finally learns who paid for the return, he will not say the name; he will write it on salt-paper in the rain, and watch it go, which is the closest thing to a confession a Vhaleni factor can make.

## Plans (private)

Let him be the morally grey power-broker who is never quite a villain and never quite an ally: a man whose trade is the convenient disappearance of evidence, who keeps the one document he cannot destroy in a dry drawer. He moves between Sill, Greybank, Vhal and the Bowl of Sere, which makes him the saga's useful door into the wider world and the person who can put a price on anything, including a person.

Keep him courteous and keep him costing somebody. When he is kind it should always be attached to a figure, and the reader should never be sure which part was the gift. He survives to the end, which is his punishment: a man who can turn any account into nothing and has never once been able to make one that is his own. His last scene should be a damp document and a smile, and the reader should understand that he has finally done something honest and will never admit it.
