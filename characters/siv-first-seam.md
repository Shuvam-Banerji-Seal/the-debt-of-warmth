## Appearance

Forty-two, with the stillness of a man who has spent twenty years sitting in a shaft listening to stone, which makes him seem slower than he is and much harder to interrupt. He is tall for Keldish and slightly stooped from the low roof of the survey, with a thin grey scar across the bridge of his nose from a Warm-flood he did not get out of fast enough, and eyes that have adjusted to dark seams and therefore look vaguely at people in daylight. His measure-number is tattooed on his forearm in hard Keldish numerals and has never been cut out, which in the First Seam is a statement of confidence or stubbornness. He carries a bone tally on a cord and makes marks on it with a nail when a number changes, and he does not carry a clamp, which is unusual for a man of the Order and is the first thing Hald Iron notices about him. He smells of cold stone, hot lead, coal smoke and the particular mineral sweetness that comes just before the Sweating. The thing people notice first is the scar; the thing they notice last is that he listens to walls before he listens to the person standing next to one.

## Voice & Manner

Slow, clipped, numerical, with the Keldish habit of putting the number before the noun — *two seams, one passing, no name for it*. He gives dates where another man would give a judgement and has never in his life offered an opinion about a thing he has not measured. When the Vorr say *the numbers are correct*, he writes it down and does not ask them to expand, because he has learned that a one-sentence answer from a Vorr is a complete answer and that asking for more is a way of losing the number. He is the only Keldish man Hald Iron will allow near the crossed-out file, which is a compliment neither man has ever spoken. When he is frightened he starts counting under his breath, which sounds like the seam itself.

- "Five repairs in this wall. Four of stone. One of something the translation says is Ash and the Court's copy says is language. Both translations are guesses. I have entered them both."
- "The serpent passed at the third hour. The numbers changed by two. I did not see it whole. Nobody does."
- "Y.H. 588. A man with a mark. The entry is crossed out because a surveyor wrote the shape of it and then thought better of it. I have kept the shape. I do not know what it means. That is not the same as not keeping it."
- "You ask what holds the world together. I can tell you how many repairs I have counted. I cannot tell you what they were made of. Nobody can. The Vorr will only say the numbers are correct."
- "I don't hunt the thing because it's a monster. It drinks through the stone where a man can't reach. So I reach where it does. That's the whole of it."

## History

He went down the First Seam at thirteen, as every Keldish child does, and his number changed from thirty-four to forty-one on the day of his Descent, which is unusual and made the mine-reeve look at him twice. He became a surveyor of the rim-seam, a hunter of seam-serpents when a passing was measured in the deep shafts, and a man who has spent twenty years entering the repairs of the world in a bone tally that no officer below the rank of assessor is allowed to read. He has survived two Sweatings by being further down than the men who died and by knowing, from the smell of hot lead, that there were minutes and not hours. He has sat with Vorr at the Listening Stones for a season and come away with three sentences, all of them *the numbers are correct*, and has entered all three.

In Y.H. 806 the First Seam survey was sealed and the crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588 was copied into the sealed file at the Hearthseat. Siv made the copy. He is the clerk who could not have known why he was asked, and he has known ever since. He copied the shape of the branching red mark onto the back of his own tally, under the numbers, and has carried it for six years without showing it to anyone except Hald Iron, who copied it onto the back of his own and has not spoken of it since. In Y.H. 811 he recorded a repair in the rim-seam as *words that hold* and was not removed from the shaft, which he attributes to the death of the man who would have removed him and not to luck. He has entered both explanations and kept the slate.

When a lowlander boy arrives in Keld with a branching red flower across his ribs and an ear that hears a faulty vessel through a wall, Siv will not know what the mark means. He will know that the same shape was crossed out in Y.H. 588 and written in the margin of a Low Kiln slate in Y.H. 803, and that the world has an unlucky habit of producing the same man in different vessels. He will tell the boy the numbers, which is all he has, and the boy will hear something in them that Siv has never heard, because the boy knows what a spiral is.

## Wounds & Fears

The scar, and the twenty years of numbers that do not resolve into anything a Court will print. He fears the Sweating, which he has smelled twice and dreamt of a hundred times, and fears more than that the day the Vorr finally translate the oldest repairs and the translation turns out to be a thing the Keeping has already entered under another name. He fears that he has spent his life measuring a wound and will die without having said what it is, which is the fate of a surveyor and, in Keld, an honourable one. His contradiction: the man who enters everything has kept one thing — the shape of the mark — outside all his tallies, on the back, where only another surveyor would think to look.

## Desires

He wants the numbers to be right and the shaft to keep its record after he is gone, which in Keld is a form of immortality available to a man with no house. He thinks he wants the translation. What he needs is to understand that a repair made of unkept promises is still a repair, and that the world has been held together by the very thing the Court's economy produces, which is a thought a surveyor can enter but cannot act on without becoming something other than a surveyor. He will one day give the boy the crossed-out page, and it will be the first thing in his life he has given away rather than entered.

## Bonds

**Hald Iron** — master of the floor, who respects the man's patience and distrusts what he finds. They have shared the shape of the mark in silence, each copying it onto the back of a tally, and neither has said what they think, which is how Keldish men show that a thing is too large for speech.

**Corin** — the lowlander with the flower. Siv will not tell him what the mark means because he does not know, and will show him the crossed-out page because the boy is the first person who might. The tension is a gift given by a man who has spent his life keeping, to a boy who will read it with an ear the surveyor does not have.

**The Vorr** — Ao of the Third Seam and Iyn of the Listening Stone, who sit against the stones and come away with numbers. Siv is patient with them in a way no other Keldish man is, because he has learned that a one-sentence answer can be a whole answer if you write it down exactly.

**Karric VII, Warden of the Rim** — the ruler who keeps the true-Hearth claim for reasons of trade. Siv serves the claim without believing it, which in Keld is not hypocrisy but a way of keeping the shaft open.

**Rusk of the Weir** — a lowlander who asked him once, twenty years ago, whether the oldest repairs were made of language. Siv said the numbers were correct and told him nothing else, and has wondered ever since whether the man asked the same question of the stones.

**Marra** — whose name appears in the margin of a Low Kiln slate in Y.H. 803 beside a shape Siv recognises. He has never shown that slate to anyone and has copied the shape into his tally, which is the closest thing to a friendship the Hearthseat will ever have with the First Seam.

## Secrets (private)

1. He made the copy of the Y.H. 588 crossed-out entry in Y.H. 806 and has kept the shape of the mark on the back of his tally for six years, outside all his numbered records.
2. He entered a repair as *words that hold* in Y.H. 811 and was not removed from the shaft. He has entered two explanations for this and kept both slates.
3. He saw the branching mark in the margin of a Low Kiln slate in Y.H. 803, beside a slate-name he has since learned is Marra's. He has connected the Low Kiln mark to the First Seam mark and to the boy, and has told no one the full chain.
4. He has sat with the Vorr for a season and heard three sentences. He has written down a fourth that they did not speak, which is the shape of the answer he expects, and has kept it face-down in his tally.
5. He will give Corin the crossed-out page late, after the boy has already found the spiral for himself, and the gift will be useless and necessary, which is how the truth usually arrives in Sarn.

## Plans (private)

Let him be the keeper of the physical evidence for the reincarnation mystery — not a mystic, not a guide, a surveyor with a bone tally. He knows a truth without understanding it, which is the most Sarnish way to know anything. When he shows the boy the crossed-out shape, the reader should feel the ground shift: the same branching flower has now appeared on an infant, in a survey from two centuries before, and in the margin of a Court slate signed M.

Keep him slow and numerical to the end. He never solves the Deep Hand and never translates the oldest repairs, because the Vorr will not and because a translation would be a plot device rather than a life's work. He gives the boy the numbers and lets the boy hear the spiral in them, which is the entire difference between a surveyor and a mender.
