## Appearance

He was beautiful in the way a well-kept instrument is beautiful: pressed, polished, and entirely at ease in the hand. Expensive shoes, a good watch, a warm, amused voice that never carried. His hands were long-fingered and dexterous, a pianist's hands, and he thought of Dan's short steady ones as a butcher's — a judgement he made in affection first and in malice afterwards, until the two became indistinguishable. He smelled of clean soap and a little cologne, which in a hospital reads as a kind of aggression. He never looked tired, which was his greatest affectation and, in the end, his greatest lie.

## Voice & Manner

Warm, amused, unstoppable. He never raises his voice and never says an unkind thing in a tone that could be reported as unkind. He speaks in the passive grammar of the institution he represents, which is why the Warm Court will one day sound like him to Dan: *nothing happened; it was a systems issue; the chart was closed.* He calls Dan "the butcher" in a tone of such fondness that only Dan hears the knife in it.

- “Dan. Still cutting things open? Some of us learned to put them back.”
- “You reported me. Over a junior. A junior, Dan.”
- “Nothing happened. That's what a chart is for.”
- “The butcher. Always the butcher. It's affectionate, if you must know.”
- “We all sign charts we haven't read. You of all people should understand that.”

## History

They were friends in residency and then not. Vesci was a plastic surgeon with a beautiful manner and a talent for taking credit, the kind of man who could make a complicated repair sound like a small favour he had done for a colleague. The rupture came when he left a junior alone with a bleeding patient and Dan reported it. Vesci never forgave him, and spent fifteen years referring to him, in a warm amused voice, as the butcher. Dan pretended not to care. He cared.

Vesci was the one who, at the review of Tasha Broussard's death, said the words *systems failure* in a gentle voice and gave the hospital the language it needed to close the matter. He did not know that Dan had signed the chart without reading the nurse's note. Or rather, he did know — he learned it later, in passing, from Des, and he has never used it. Using it would mean admitting he had been watching Dan for fifteen years, and that the two of them were the same man in different coats. He keeps a file on Dan's career that he has never shown anyone. He keeps it the way a man keeps a photograph of a woman who left him: not to look at, but to know it exists.

He said something once about Nina, in a voice he thought was harmless: that she would learn to stop waiting, like the rest of them, and that it was kinder that way. Dan did not laugh. Vesci has never understood why that line ended the friendship more completely than the report did.

## Wounds & Fears

His wound is that his reputation is his vessel and he believes it cannot be allowed to leak. He fears being ordinary, being reported again, being seen clearly by anyone for longer than a minute. He is not a villain; he is a man who has decided that his own standing is the most important account in the world, which is precisely the logic of the Warm Court, and he would be genuinely surprised to hear it described that way. His contradiction is that he is warm while being cruel and believes his warmth makes the cruelty kind. He genuinely thinks he did nothing wrong. He also genuinely knows he did, which is why he watches Dan.

He fears Dan most of all, because Dan is the only man who knows that the institution's forgiveness is a transaction and not a fact. Every time Vesci says *nothing happened* he is asking Dan to agree, and Dan's silence is the only answer he has ever got, and he has chosen to read it as assent.

## Desires

He wants Dan to admit he overreacted — one sentence, said in a warm voice over a drink, in which the butcher concedes that the butcher was right to report him. He thinks he wants vindication. What he needs, unknowingly, is to be the mirror Dan cannot look at: the human face of a world that absorbs a man's guilt and hands him back clean. In Sarn he becomes the model for the reasonable men with ledgers — not a dark lord, a man with a good reason and a neat hand. When Dan hears the Court say *the account stands*, he will hear Vesci saying *that's what a chart is for*, and understand they are the same sentence spoken in two rooms.

## Bonds

**Daniel Marchetti** — the rival and the mirror. The nickname is a small cold pressure applied for fifteen years, and it lands because Dan secretly believes it. Vesci has a file on him; Dan has a secret that proves the nickname true. They pull against each other across a gulf of mutual, unspoken recognition: each knows exactly what the other is, and neither can say it without incriminating himself.

**Lena Ostrowski** — she urged the report and never trusted him. Vesci called her "the iron maiden" in front of residents. She was right about him and he has never forgiven rightness in a woman; it is the one thing his beautiful manner cannot smooth over.

**Deshawn Carter** — the paramedic who saw the junior incident and said nothing for fifteen years. Vesci called him "the entertainment," which is how he thinks of most people who are not useful to him. Des half-told Dan the truth years later, in a joke that Dan did not laugh at. Vesci does not know how much Des knows, which is a small permanent unease.

**Nina Marchetti** — a girl he made a careless remark about, in a voice he thought was harmless. He has forgotten it. Dan has not, and Nina, who was never told, has spent her life sensing the shape of it without the words.

## Secrets (private)

1. He learned about Tasha's chart from Des and has kept it, unused, for years. The file he keeps on Dan contains one page about it and nothing else.
2. He said the line about Nina that ended the friendship and does not remember saying it. That is the truest thing about him.
3. He has never performed a repair as difficult as the ones Dan does in the trauma bay; he takes credit for them and has built a career on the taking. The butcher is the one who actually cuts.
4. He wrote the phrase *systems failure* at the review, and gave Dan the language he used to excuse himself. Dan has never known whose sentence he borrowed.
5. In the private practice with good light, he has begun to refer to himself, in a warm amused voice, as the last decent surgeon in the city. He believes it, which is the most frightening thing in this file.

## Plans (private)

Bring him in late and small — a sentence at a review, a nickname in a corridor — and let him grow in the reader's mind the way a bad smell grows in a warm room. He is not a recurring presence in Sarn; he is the human grammar the Court will speak in, and Dan must learn to hear him in the soft voice of an assessor saying the measure is banked.

Use him as the proof that Dan's guilt is not original. Dan did not invent the gesture of letting an institution absorb his conduct and hand him back clean; he learned it from a man in a beautiful coat, and he has been doing it ever since. When Dan finally rejects the Court's accounting, the real adversary in that scene is Vesci's voice saying *nothing happened, that's what a chart is for.* Let the warm, amused tone survive into the Sarn officers who will pity Dan for his trouble with the second duty. Keep him unrepentant and keep him right about one thing: Dan is a butcher. The saga's answer is not that Vesci is wrong, but that Dan will do the cutting with his eyes open and carry the name differently.
