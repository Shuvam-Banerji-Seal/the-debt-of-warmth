## Appearance

Thirty-four, with hands like old rope and a grey streak through black hair from a winter she spent on the weir when she was twenty and would do again. Eel scars lie across both forearms in pale arcs, and she never covers them, because a trap-keeper without scars is a trap-keeper who does not work. She smells of river silt, cold fish and tallow smoke, and in the spring of wet mud so strongly that people at the Cross know she has come before they see her. She keeps her accounts in knots on a cord of grey wool and will not sit at a table where a slate is open; she learned the habit from a Fen woman who sold her reed and taught her that a written name is a name somebody else can read. The thing people notice first is the grey streak; the thing they notice last is the cord, which she touches when a number is spoken.

## Voice & Manner

Slow, and when she is tired the nasal half-song of the Fen comes into it, which she has never entirely lost. She tells a story sideways and gives a date where another person would give an opinion. She never answers a question about the levy directly, but she will tell you which night the collector's boat came and how many oars it had. When she is lying she ties a knot, which is invisible to anyone who does not know the cord.

- “I don't keep names on slate. Slates burn. Knots don't.”
- “Forty. I tied forty knots in the year of the flower. I tied one more and I don't know what to call it.”
- “The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.”
- “A trap works because the eel can't see the way out. That's all a trap is. Don't make it more than that.”
- “You're the Vail boy. You've got her eyes and somebody else's listening.”

## History

She was born at the Weir, one league upriver of Sill, where the eel traps stand on a plank walk and the Fish Blessing is held every Reed 15. She has kept the traps since she was eighteen, knows the river's moods better than the weather, and can tell a collector's boat by its oars before it comes round the bend. She married a man who went downriver with a cart of pots and never came back, and has not looked for him, which she calls *not being a fool about it*. Her son Kess was one of the forty children of Low Kiln banked in the first weeks of Stone, Y.H. 812. No return has been announced for him. She tied forty knots that year and then tied a forty-first, because on the night the children were taken she saw Sella Vail carry a covered bundle up the Clay Yard, and the bundle was small and warm and had a flower on it, and she could not account for it.

She was in the room on Stone 7 when the child with the branching red flower was born, and she saw the collector's slate that night, and saw a name written on it before anyone had seen the infant's face. She cannot spell the name; she remembers its shape, which is a thing no Court clerk can take away.

## Wounds & Fears

A son spent for a return that never came. She has gone to the Bone Orchard twice looking for a shallow grave with his name on it and found none, which is worse than finding one, because it means his warmth was banked and not wasted and is somewhere in an account she will never see. She fears the Weighing, where whole returns are announced in a clean voice to people whose children are not coming back. She fears that the forty-first knot is a thing she has made into a mystery because a plain answer would be too ordinary: the boy she saw carried was simply another child, and she has needed him not to be. Her contradiction: she sells eels to the Court's clerk and hates the Court, and has learned the collector's schedule in order to avoid him and also in order to be there when he comes.

## Desires

She wants her son returned whole, which she knows will not happen, and she wants somebody else to say his name who is not herself. She thinks she wants to know what happened to the boy in the covered bundle. What she needs is to understand that a mother's account is never settled by a return, whole or thin, and that the knot cord is not a ledger but a way of continuing to hold on to something the Court has already booked.

## Bonds

**Kess** — her son, one of the Forty. She has a knot for him and no return, and has begun to speak to the knot in the evening, which she knows is not sensible and does not stop.

**Corin** — the Vail boy. She sees something in him that belongs to the year of the flower and cannot place it: the way he turns his head, the mark under his sleeve, the attention of a person much older than his body. She has never said this to anyone except the cord.

**Sella Vail** — she blames Sella for taking a child at the very moment forty others were taken, and has never said why she blames her, which leaves the blame floating free and available for anything.

**Dessa Silt** — they were both in the room on Stone 7. Dessa believes her baby died; Orra knows she is lying and has not said. It is the one thing she has never sold at the Fishstairs.

**Alda Reed** — the only person with whom she has ever discussed the third day. They do not discuss the birth-flower. They discuss the weather and the river and occasionally the number forty, and then one of them touches something and the subject changes.

**Mira Silt** — her eel buyer, her friend, and the one person she lets near the knots. Mira wants the secret out; Orra wants it kept, which is the only disagreement in a friendship of fifteen years.

**Berr Clay, the Thane of Clay** — bought her eels for the levy feast and paid two marks short. She has never forgiven the arithmetic, and has served him the same short measure ever since.

## Secrets (private)

1. She has forty-one knots and cannot account for the last one. It is Corin's, tied the night she saw Sella carry the bundle up the yard.
2. She saw the collector's slate that night and saw the name written before the face. She cannot spell it, but she remembers the shape of it, which will matter when she sees the same name on a Court document years later.
3. She has been to the Bone Orchard twice and found no grave for Kess, which is how she knows his warmth was banked rather than wasted. She has never told Dessa this, because Dessa has enough to carry.
4. She sold quiet ash once, to a Fen runner, to forget the smell of her son's coat. It did not work, and the failure is the truest thing about her.
5. She will be the first to say the word *forty* within Corin's hearing, and will watch what he does with it. He will be too young to answer, which will not stop her watching.

## Plans (private)

She is the district's living count of the Forty — the physical plant of the private account that bought Dan. Long before he understands the number, she has tied it in a cord that no officer can read and no kiln can burn. When she dies late, she leaves the cord to Mira, who eventually gives it to Dan, and he reads it as a man reads a chart in a language he half remembers.

Her forty-first knot is a trap set for the reader: it looks like a mystery about the boy, and it is really a record of a woman who needed one more thing to hold. Keep her slow, keep her half-sung, keep her giving dates instead of opinions. She never solves anything. She keeps the count, which in Sarn is the most subversive act available to a person with no slate.
