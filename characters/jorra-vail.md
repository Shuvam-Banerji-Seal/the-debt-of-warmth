## Appearance

Sixteen and built like a gate hinge: tall for a clay boy, narrow, with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and a permanent bend in his left little finger from a splinter off the wheel when he was eleven. He cuts his hair short so Ash cannot nest in it, which is a thing the district notices and he never explains. His eyes do not finish sentences; they go to the door, to the fire, to the wheel, and come back without reporting. He smells of damp clay, tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze carried up from High Kiln on a bad wind. The thing people notice first is his hands, which are the hands of a thrower and have been working since he was nine. The thing they notice last is that he never looks at Sella when she speaks to him, though he obeys her instantly.

## Voice & Manner

Short, flat, declarative. He asks almost no questions, and when he does it is the tool rather than the clay: *Is it dry? Did you fire it? Who wrote it?* He speaks to the wheel and to the pots as other men speak to animals, and only then to people. When he is angry he gets quieter, the way Sella does, and the household has learned to read the lowering of his voice as a weather signal. He never says *with* or *against* when *I haven't said* will do.

- “The wheel doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.”
- “You want me to say it. I'm not saying it.”
- “Sella. Don't feed him from your own dish. He's not Vail.”
- “A slate burns in four minutes. Four. I've timed it.”
- “I didn't say I was with them. I said I wasn't against them. That's different. In this district it isn't, but it's what I said.”

## History

He is the son of Garl and Sella Vail, and he took the wheel at thirteen when Garl died in the Long Cold and did not come back and nobody came to draw him. The wheel had been Garl's for twenty years and the seat was worn hollow; Jorra has spent three years trying to fill that hollow with his own weight. He has thrown pots since he was nine, fired them since eleven, and knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves his hands, though he will not say so in front of the kiln-masters, who would call it early and make something of it. His house has remembered the Ash Rising since Y.H. 668, when children were taken and the rising was put down by a man with a list; the memory is kept not in stories but in the way certain doors are never closed and certain names are never entered. Since he was fourteen men from the Broken Slates have come into the back room of the kiln-house and spoken in low voices. He has listened to all of them and told none of them yes.

He watched Enna, his sister, die of the second chill at four, and has never forgiven the fire for being warm while she cooled. He watched his father die and was sent to find a collector and could not cross the ford, and has never asked whether he was sent too late or whether he was sent at all. When Corin arrived he said one sentence — *He's not Vail* — and has said nothing about it since, which in that house is a form of argument.

## Wounds & Fears

A father who went uncollected, a sister who went cold in a warm room, and a wheel he was given before he was ready. He fears Sella will die with something unsaid and leave him holding the house and the lie together. He fears the Broken Slates will decide for him what he is, because he has not decided and the longer he waits the more their decision looks like his own. He fears the boy most of all, because the boy hears things. His contradiction: he is the most careful keeper of the Vail fire in four generations and he is quietly helping the people who want to burn every slate in Sill. He has never once thought these two facts cannot live in the same man.

## Desires

He wants the wheel to stay in the house and the fire to hold and nobody to write the boy down as Vail without asking him first. He thinks he wants Sella to say that he has done well with the wheel. What he needs is to learn that burning a slate does not return a person to you, and that the anger he has been nursing for three years is not his alone — it has been lent to him by men who intend to collect on it.

## Bonds

**Sella Vail** — his mother. He believes she took Corin in to replace Enna and to keep the house's standing after Garl died. She has never contradicted him. Every obedience he gives her is therefore double: it is love and it is a correction neither of them will make.

**Corin** — the foundling. Jorra was the first in the house to notice that the boy turns his head toward a pot before anyone taps it, and that he does it to hear the note, not the noise. He has told nobody. He resents the boy for being given a bed in the house while the clay-bound hand sleeps in the loft, and he cannot stop watching him.

**Alda Reed** — attended Enna's death and Corin's birth, and has never met Jorra's eye for longer than a moment. He knows she wrote something down the night Enna died and has never asked to see it, because asking would make it a thing between them.

**Tarn** — the Vail's clay-bound hand. Jorra treats him as a brother when the wheel needs him and as a servant when it does not. Tarn has decided to hate him for it and keeps failing, because Jorra once sat up all night with him through a fever and has never mentioned it.

**Berr Clay, the Thane of Clay** — Jorra threw a stone at his door in Y.H. 810 and denied it. The Thane suspects the boy is with the Broken Slates and lets him be, which Jorra finds more frightening than being watched.

No chapters are written yet. He enters as the young man at the wheel who says *he's not Vail* and then says nothing more on the subject for years, which in a Grathic house is the loudest possible way to say it.

## Secrets (private)

1. He has broken three birth-slates in the last two years and buried the pieces under the drying rack. He has never told the Broken Slates, because he wants them to believe he is still deciding.
2. He knows where the district's second set of birth notes is kept — Alda Reed's tin under a floorboard — and has not burned it because he is afraid of what is written there about the year of the flower.
3. He has promised a slate with Corin's name on it to the Broken Slates. If the boy has no house, his name is easy to give; it is the one thing Jorra has that costs him nothing except, perhaps, the boy.
4. He does not want the wheel at all. He wants to leave Low Kiln, which he would say is impossible, which is what he says whenever he is asked anything real.
5. The bent finger came from the wheel, not from a fight. He tells people it was a fight because a fight sounds more like him.

## Plans (private)

Make him dangerous by the smallness of the act, not the size: he will hand Corin's name to people who burn slates, and he will do it while believing he is protecting the house. He is not a villain and will never be given a villain's speech; he is a young man with a wound and a wheel and a grievance that has been lent to him.

He becomes the one who tells Corin, years later and cruelly, that the woman who bore him was Dessa Silt and that she has been dead since the Slip Brook took her — a truth used as a weapon because he cannot say the thing he actually means, which is *I was here first*. He stays in Low Kiln, keeper of the wheel, a man who has never said which side he is on until the day he says it and it costs him the house. His arc is the saga's first proof that a person can be both wounded and wrong without being either one exclusively.
