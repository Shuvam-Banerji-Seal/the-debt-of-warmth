I keep the table at the Cross. On market mornings I lay my hand on the top slate before I look at the woman waiting, and say that it is entered as it stands and there is nothing in it the household need correct. My cuff is gone soft from washing and the ink sits in the creases of two fingers and will not come out. What follows is copied out of the ordinance for the use of this table, so that a man standing in the square may know what he is being told before he is told it. I have put the verbs back where I could. The ordinance has no need of them, being always true; I have. A thing that is always true is a thing a woman cannot argue with, and I have had to stand here and argue with a great many women.

> *At the head of the first leaf, in the same hand, written very small:* Copied by the table, not for the table. If this is read in the Hearthseat, let it be seen that the verbs were put back by me and struck out again by another. Let both stand.

# The extract, as it stands in the book

*From the Ordinances of the Keeping, Title the Third: Of the Drawing, Banking and Returning of Measures. As confirmed to the factor at Greybank by the treaty of Y.H. 418, and added to in Y.H. 782 and Y.H. 812.*

## I. Of the entry of vessels

1. Every vessel born within the Keeping entered upon a slate within three days of the first breath. Entry in Ledgerhand, without verbs. Birth-name not entered. Slate-name entered in its place where the vessel enters the service. Slate kept face up upon the bench. The hand that writes not the hand that holds.
2. Where entry made after three days, the reason entered also. No reason entered for the third day itself.

> *Margin:* The third day is a bad day to choose. A child that has taken a chill will grow worse on the third day and be entered anyway, and the entry will stand as a birth and not as a worsening. I have seen this and I have not put it in the article. There is no article for it.

> *Margin, in a second and narrower hand:* This article was added in Y.H. 782, which is the year the maids began to be paid, and the two things are not unrelated, though the ordinance does not say so. A hearth-maid lays the slate on the bench before she goes to the child. I have watched them do it. They do not say why, and I have not asked.

## II. Of the drawing

3. Measure drawn by an officer appointed thereunto, a cold iron tube set against a way. Walls giving way; drain entered to the account. The draw witnessed by a slate. Where consent withheld, the account unclosed; the vessel clip-held by the wrist until consent entered. Consent entered in the hand of the officer.
4. Theft of warmth, being a draw without a slate, punished by a cold iron brand upon the wrist and a deduction from the thief's own measure. Quiet ash sold to any not licensed to buy it punished by a greyed hand.
5. Drawing from the Hearth without a keeper's key forbidden. The Nine Wells sealed; keys kept. A keeper who drew without authorisation was hanged in Y.H. 618.

> *Margin:* I have seen the wrist. The clip leaves a pale ring that shows through the winter and is spoken of as nothing. The woman asked me if the ring would go, and I said it would in the thaw, which is what my mother said about everything, and is not in the ordinance.

> *Margin:* The officer who came on Stone 12 had fingers the colour of skimmed milk gone off, under a cuff softer than my own. I wrote him down on a slate of my own. I did not say what the fingers meant, because I do not know, and I did not join him to anybody.

## III. Of the figure and the reading

6. One part in twelve of every measure kept back for the Hearth, as the notice says. Figure printed upon the Weighing notice and read aloud at the Cross. Read twice: once for the moot, and again for those that came late. Account-day the tenth, twentieth and thirtieth of every tend. No market held during the Five.
7. Correction of a slate by one hand only. Lawful deduction entered only by the College of Assessors. A house roll kept close and not shown; the keeping of it not explained, only kept.

> *Margin:* The figure is one in twelve and I have never known a household in Low Kiln that paid it. I do not say what it pays. I have no slate for that and will not invent one.

> *Margin:* The bell at the Cross goes longer than a church bell and nobody crosses himself at it. I have counted it four, four, and a place where the fifth should be. I have put no count of it in the ordinance, because the ordinance has no place for a thing that is not a debt.

> *Margin:* There is a small stop in the neat upper line of the top slate, between two clusters, in a hand I do not know. I have seen it five times and joined none of them. When I say *there's nothing in it the household need correct*, I am not lying. There is nothing in it that I can correct.

## IV. Of the five days outside the months

8. Five days outside the months, named and not numbered, outside the tend. On Ilmen 1 the count begins again at Firstday.

| Day | As the ordinance stands | In the margin |
|---|---|---|
| **Kindling** | Every hearth lit from the Hearth-flame. Collectors take the year's first measure. | Here the first hand wrote *Weighing*. I have struck it and set *Kindling* above it. The measure was taken before first light in Y.H. 812. No figure reached the roof, which is another way of saying I did not see it. |
| **Weighing** | The year's measures read aloud. The rich learn how whole they are. | An officer read them in a clean voice and the district learned its figure from him. Berr Clay passed a finger over his own slate and wiped his thumb. Neither of them said the figure twice. |
| **the Long Night** | No fire after dusk. Stories of the Return told in the dark. | The stories are not entered. I sat in one room where the bell was told as four, four and a gap. I kept the told bell in my head away from the heard bell. They are not the same bell and I will not make them one. |
| **the Remembering** | Names of the dead spoken. Whole returns announced in a clean voice. | A whole return was read for one not in the room at the Weighing in Y.H. 812, and the household did not repeat the name. Under another roof a name was not spoken at all. I entered the return and not the name. |
| **the Last Day** | Bread baked. The grey Ash in the corners not swept away; burnt the next morning and called dealt with. | Two ridges were measured with one thumb. I put my cuff into a corner I had no business in and the grey came off on it. The streak in this book's margin came from the same hand. I washed the cuff. The ink is still in the two fingers. |

## V. Of the keeping and the returning

9. Measure banked. Account stands. Nothing lost that has been kept. Embers booked. Rolls of the dead kept open. Where one measure poured into one vessel, the return whole, with memory; where many pooled and divided among many, the return thin. Return announced in a clean voice. The Kept held in the Weighing Hall, fewer than twenty, spoken of with pity.
10. No article provides for the coming back wrong. In the low houses they keep these also.

> *Margin:* I have seen one of the Kept go through the Hall. It was courteous and asked after the weather. There are fewer than twenty and they are spoken of with pity. I do not know what the pity is for, and I have never asked.

> *Margin:* The rolls of the dead are kept open. I have entered a name twice, once in the house roll and once upon the Court's slate, and the two entries did not agree, and neither was corrected. A dead man with two figures is worth more than one with none. That is not in the ordinance either, and yet I have done it.

> *Margin, at the foot, struck through and written again in the same hand:* No article provides for the coming back wrong. In the low houses they keep these also. *Written again because I struck it the first time, and then thought better of striking it. Let it stand. A clerk who takes a line out of a law because it frightens him has no business at a table where women stand.*

# What I corrected, and why

In article one the first hand wrote *entry made*. I set *is* above it. A man come down from Slate Row in a clean coat passed the table, looked at my *is*, and struck it out again, saying that an ordinance has no need of a verb, being always true. I have left both stand. A slate with two hands on it is the only kind the household believes.

Where the book said *Weighing* for the year's first measure, I struck it and wrote *Kindling*. A collector drew before light on Kindling in the Thin Year and the draw went into the account without a figure anyone in the yard could spend. I know the day because I set my hand on the slate that morning. I do not know the figure and have not asked for it.

In the schedule of the Five the first hand allowed four days. I have set five above it. There are five, and no market in any of them, and the count begins again on the first day of Ilmen. I cannot think how a man came to write four unless he left one out on purpose, and I have no mind to find out which.

The candle-singe at the top is mine. I set the book too near the lamp in the Waiting Room, where the collected are kept overnight before banking, and held it there longer than I should, because a woman in the room was telling me something and I wanted the end of it. She did not tell me the end. She looked at the book burning and stopped, and went back to sitting. I have left the singe as it is. It is the only entry in this quire that nobody has corrected.

The book goes with the table. On Ilmen 1 the slates opened and I copied it again, and struck the same word out again. A woman will come and set her hand on the top slate, and I shall say it is entered as it stands. It is. That is the trouble with it.
