title: The Sixth Repair, Being a Letter from the First Seam to Nine Pots, Answered in the Margin and Entered Over by the Court
kind: A letter between brothers, the one hand Keldish and number-first, the other Grathic and given to proverbs; a tally of repairs set down in a small table, the sentence of the Vorr kept as it was spoken, the answers of the man addressed written in the margins, the corrections of a Court survey-clerk laid in Ledgerhand over both hands, and one line at the foot in a hand nobody will own
provenance: Written in Bone of Y.H. 812, the Thin Year of the Long Cold, in the survey room off the Duelling Floor beneath Keld Keep, by Varek of the First Seam, a clerk of the rim-seam tally, to Ollen of Nine Pots, a thrower, his brother by a mother who came down from the clay country and never learned to say a number before a noun. Set first on the back of a survey sheet spoiled by wet slate and carried down the Rim Road and the Clay Road in Seed of Y.H. 813 with a survey party bound for Greybank; answered in the margins at Nine Pots and corrected at Greybank by a clerk of the factor's office who borrowed the leaf on a market morning and would not give it back until the verbs were out of it. Kept since in a clay tube stoppered with wool on the shelf of the thrower's shed in Nine Pots. To Ollen, thrower, of Nine Pots, from Varek of the First Seam, his brother. Six repairs counted this week. One letter. No answer looked for.

I write from the room off the floor where the survey sheets are kept, which is warm in a way a room should not be warm. The stone sweats here in Bone and the smell comes up before anything shows, hot lead and a mineral sweetness like a glaze pot left too near the fire. Two men went out of this shaft in my first season and came back with their fingers the colour of a skimmed milk skin. I have counted the repairs in the wall behind the tally desk six times and got six each time, which is a small thing to be glad of. A number that comes back the same is the only honest company down here.

We had the same mother, who came down from Nine Pots with clay under her nails and a way of addressing the pot before she addressed the room. She taught you the wheel and taught me to hold a slate. I have kept the slate. You got the wheel, which is the better inheritance and I have never once said so in a letter until now, so you may put this down to the lead smell.

# The tally, as I keep it

The wall behind the desk is not the oldest in the seam. It is only the one I can see from my stool. I have entered what I can see and left the rest where it is, which is a rule the Order is not so strict about as it used to be.

| Repair | What it is made of, by the Keldish translation | What the factor's copy at Greybank calls it | How many times repaired |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | Stone, dressed and keyed | Stone | Twice |
| Two | Stone and iron pins | Stone, iron not entered | Once |
| Three | Stone | Stone | Three times; the third over the second |
| Four | Warm-stone dust in a clay bed | Material not entered | Once |
| Five | Stone | Stone | Once |
| Six | Not stone | Language | Once; crossed out and written again |

Five of the six are stone and are no trouble. The fourth wants a word I will not use of a wall, because warm-stone is a thing a man carries against a child's belly and not a thing he puts in a hole in the world. I have entered it as dust in a clay bed and let the translation stand beside it.

The sixth is the one I am writing to you about. The Keldish translation calls it Ash. The factor's copy calls it language. I have copied both words into the margin and chosen neither, because a surveyor who chooses between two guesses has made a fact of one of them and will have to answer for it at the Weighing. The Vorr were asked. One of them sat against a stone for a season and came back with three sentences, all the same sentence, which was that the numbers were correct. I have written that down too. It is a complete answer if you are a Vorr and a very thin one if you are a man trying to sleep above a seam that sweats.

# The entry of Y.H. 588

There is an entry in the old survey of a man with a branching red mark on his forearm and ribs. The entry is crossed out. It was crossed out before I came down the shaft and I do not know whose hand did it, which is a thing I have said to myself often enough that it now sounds like an answer.

I have copied the shape of the mark onto the back of this sheet so that you can look at it and tell me whether clay ever makes it. I do not want an opinion. I want to know whether you have seen it under a glaze or in the bottom of a jar, and if so, on what day and out of which pit. Give me the day and the pit and leave the rest where you found it.

There is a paper in a tin lined with warm-stone in the room off the floor. The men who keep the floor read it once a year and do not act on it, which I am told is a great skill and is certainly an old one. I have read it twice. The second time I found a line about a vessel that holds without leaking and went back to counting the wall, which was easier and paid the same.

# What I want to know from you

This is the question I cannot put to any man in Keld without being told to state my measure first and ask afterwards:

A pot that has cracked all the way through and been mended, does it hold the same as a pot that never cracked? I do not mean whether it holds water. I mean whether the holding is the same holding. Tap a sound pot with the knuckle and it answers full. Tap a cracked one and it answers flat, a little dead sound that stops at the surface. I know the two sounds as well as I know my own number, which was thirty-three when I went down and thirty-nine when they changed it, and has been cut out and written again twice since, so that my forearm reads like this letter does. But nobody has ever tapped a repaired pot for me and asked it the question twice, once before the mending and once after, and told me what changed.

You will say a pot is a pot. You will say this with slip on both forearms and the little finger bent where the splinter went in at eleven, and you will be right in the way that potters are always right, which is from the wrist and not from the mouth. Answer me from the wrist.

# In the margin, in a second hand, the letters round and pressed hard, with slip in the strokes

You always did ask the wrong question first. A pot that never cracked is a pot that has not been mended, and there is no holding to compare with it. Tap mine and it answers full because I set the seam myself and it sang when it went home. If it had not sung I would have sold it for a flower pot and said nothing. A crack that is mended keeps the crack. Ask it to be a pot it was not and you are asking a lie of the clay, which is a thing the clay charges for in the thaw.

On the red mark: third firing of Y.H. 806, a jar from the Old Kiln clay that would not take a glaze and went grey in the firing. The mark was on the bottom, branching like frost on a pane. A girl from the terrace crossed two fingers at it and said it was a flower. I asked her which flower. She gave me a date. I have kept the jar under the bench and I have never turned it up in company.

On the road: it is ours and Sill's both, which is why neither of us mends it. Come down in Seed when the clay has set and bring the warm-stone if you are still carrying one. Six repairs in a wall is enough for a man to carry without a stone as well.

> One for the wheel and one for the fire,
> one for the crack that the glaze won't hide;
> tap it at noon, it answers at night,
> and the seam keeps the word the potter denied.

I have never written a verse in my life and I would not begin now if there were not a man above me in a shaft who needs one. Put it under the numbers where it belongs.

# What the Court hand did, at Greybank, in the second tend of Seed

A clerk of the factor's office borrowed the leaf on a market morning; his cuff was gone soft at the edge from washing and he had ink in the creases of two fingers. He laid his hand on the top sheet before he looked at either of us, which is the manner of that table. He took the verbs out of both hands as though a verb were a thing a man could smuggle in a cuff, and set a figure against each repair, and struck the question about the mended pot and wrote *No such comparison* above it. Against the red shape he put a round nought and wrote *mark, not entered*. Where my brother wrote that the seam keeps the word, the clerk wrote *repair retained; material not entered* and left mine underneath, which is more than he usually leaves.

| In my hand | In the Court hand |
|---|---|
| Six repairs counted this week | Repairs entered: six |
| The sixth is not stone | Material not entered |
| The Vorr said the numbers were correct | Figures confirmed |
| Does a mended pot hold the same? | No such comparison |
| A mark on the bottom of a jar | Mark, not entered |
| The seam keeps the word | Repair retained |

He asked me what a verse was worth and I said nothing a clerk could enter, which was the first true thing he said all morning. I have left both hands standing, mine under his and my brother's under both, as a repaired wall keeps the old break under the new keying and does not pretend the first hand never touched it.

# At the head and in the gutter

The coal dust at the head is mine; I held the sheet down with my whole hand because the draught in the survey room comes along the floor and takes paper before it takes a man. The hot lead at the fold came off a cuff on the day a passing was measured two hours early and we all went up faster than we had any right to. The slip in the gutter is my brother's and has gone bone-coloured with dry, which is the colour of his forearms by noon and of mine by never. The tallow thumb is mine. The ring came off a cup that stood from the first question to the verse and was drunk by neither of us, which is how small ale is drunk in Keld when there is a thing to say and no proper word for it. The grey along the outer fold is not mine and not the Court's. I have not swept it. There is no Last Day in the First Seam.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

> Six repairs in the wall and one with no name. The Court has entered the five it can spell. The wall keeps all six and has never once been asked to explain itself.
