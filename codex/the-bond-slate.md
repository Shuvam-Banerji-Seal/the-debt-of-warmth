I keep the bond-slate at this counter. A man comes up from the yard with a figure in the thumb of his right hand and wants it entered before the factor's man comes up from Greybank; a woman comes who has paid a debt in loaves for six years and wants me to call the six a seven. I write what the ordinance tells me and I set the verbs back underneath the words it has taken out, because a debt without a verb is a debt nobody did, and that is the kind the Court has always liked best. What follows is Title the Fourth, copied out so that a person may know what is being read to him before it is read. I have kept the blanks in the form of entry blank. I have never had a name to put in them that the household wanted spoken in this room.

> *At the head of the first leaf, in the same hand, written very small:* Copied by the counter, and for it. The verbs were put back by me and struck out again by a man who does not keep a counter. Let both stand. A slate with two hands on it is the only kind a woman believes.

# Title the Fourth, as it stands

*From the Ordinances of the Keeping, Title the Fourth: Of Bond, Service, the Assignment and the Reckoning of Debts. Confirmed to the factor at Greybank by the treaty of Y.H. 418; added to in the year after the Rising, Y.H. 668, in Y.H. 782, and in the Thin Year, Y.H. 812.*

## I. Of the entering of a debt

1. Every debt is entered upon the bond-slate within three days of the promise, in Ledgerhand and without verbs. The sum is entered in marks, or in loaves, or in days of service, and is not changed from one kind to another without a second hand set beside the first.
2. Where the promise was made in the yard and not at the counter, the entering officer is not the man who heard it. What was heard is not written. The debt is named by the debtor and the creditor together; the house roll is kept close and is not shown.
3. A debt entered only in a house roll is a debt the Court has not picked up. No entry is made against it and no figure is struck.

> *Margin:* A debt left lying is not a debt forgiven. It is a debt nobody has taken up. I have three such standing in this book from the winter, with nobody on either side of them, and they are entered in the same neat hand as the debts that are being paid, so that no man can tell by the writing which is which. I have set the word *stands* above them, which is the word used for an account in good order. I have not been corrected, and that is how I know nobody reads this column.

> *Margin, in a second and narrower hand:* Article 3 was added in Y.H. 782, the year the maids began to be paid. A hearth-maid lays the birth-slate on the bench before she goes to the child. A woman lays her house roll on this counter before she says what she came for. These two things are of a kind, and the ordinance does not say so.

## II. Of the term and what gathers upon it

4. A clay-bound term is seven years, or such other years as a household has set and entered. It is reckoned from the day of entering and not from the thaw.
5. Interest gathers as grey Ash in the household account and not upon the Court's slate. It is not counted in marks, being a thing that collects where promises are left unkept. It is not swept away before the Last Day.
6. In the Fenmarch a breath is bought for a season or for a year, which is called bought breath. The name of the buyer is not entered where the debt is paid.
7. Where a term is spoken of as running *till the thaw*, no day is entered and the debt is not discharged by the coming of warm weather.

| Debt, as entered | Term | What gathers | Discharged |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clay-bound hand | Seven years from the day of entering | Grey in the house roll | In the thaw, when the house says |
| Bought breath, the season | One season, and no day | Nothing entered | To the buyer at the season's end |
| A tile debt left lying | No term entered | Grey, unmeasured | Nowhere; not forgiven |
| Bread given at a house door | No entry | Nothing | Nothing; given and not entered |
| A loan that can hear | The term of the debt | What the borrower hears | By the lender, who does not always call |

> *Margin:* The woman who came on the last Account-day had six years in her thumb and wanted me to write seven. I wrote the day of entering, which her household had set, and not the six she carried. She said the seventh was as good as kept. I said a kept year and a paid year are different vessels, which is a thing I have no right to say at a counter, and so I said nothing and let her say it. The ordinance gives me no word for the year a man holds back so that he need not pay. I have scored a line in the lower margin with my knife instead, which is not an entry and cannot be added.

## III. Of the keeping of those in service

8. A bond-servant is lodged apart from the household's children, in the loft or the out-room, and is not entered at the family hearth. The household's own child is not lodged there.
9. Where consent to a lawful collection is withheld, the vessel is clip-held by the wrist until consent is entered. A pale ring left at the wrist is spoken of as nothing and is not entered as a wound.
10. No cold iron is set to a living wrist save by the officer appointed thereunto. The tubes are mended at the smithy, which keeps no kiln-mark and is not a great house.

> *Margin:* A foundling was given a bed under the roof here, in a house three doors down the yard, while a debt was given the loft. The house did not call it kindness and I have not entered it. A child under a roof and a debt above a wheel-shed are two vessels in one house, and the house knows which it means to keep. I have put no figure against either.

> *Margin:* The ring shows through the winter. I have seen a man rub his cuff down over it while he was being told the figure, and go out saying the ring would go in the thaw. That is what my mother said about everything. It is not in the ordinance.

## IV. Of assignment and purchase before due

11. A debt may be assigned before it is due, and the assignment entered in the hand of the buyer or his factor. The debtor is not asked.
12. Where a debt is bought for the measure rather than the marks, the entry is made in marks. The measure is not entered.
13. The widow's mark is not assigned. The factor at Greybank confirms it, and the confirmation is spoken of as a kindness and entered as one.
14. Debts of Grath sold south to the bowls of Sere are entered as goods and not as persons. The figure is copied from the factor's slate.

> *In the leaning hand of the factor's man come up from Greybank, written over article 11:* assigned in due course. *Underneath, in mine:* bought before due. He strikes the verb wherever he finds one, as though a verb were a thing a man could carry off in a cuff and sell at the crossing. I have left both stand. A debt bought before it is due has a different smell in the room from one assigned in due course, and I am the man who has to stand at the counter when the smell arrives.

> *Margin:* Against article 13 the factor's man wrote *kindness entered*, which is exactly what the ordinance says and exactly what the house means when it does not want a question. There is no column for it and no figure in it, and still it is entered every time.

## V. Of faults and their correcting

| Fault | As entered | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A verb in the roll | Page copied again, in the same hand | The verb set underneath, where the clerk put it |
| A debt in a house roll only | Stands | Not paid, and not called forgiven |
| A term called *till the thaw* | The day of entering | If no day stands, none is supplied |
| A seventh year claimed early | Six, and the seventh held | No figure given to the holding |
| A sealed bond carried beyond the room | The carrier's own debt entered in its place | The first entry left standing beside it |

# The form of entry

*Set out at the end of Title the Fourth. I have copied it with the blanks as they stand in the book. In nine years nobody has filled one.*

> Entered this day, being ---day of -------, in the year of the Hearth ------, the debt of ----------------------, set at -------- marks, or ------- loaves, or ------- days of service, to be served from the day of entering and not from the thaw; the interest to gather as grey in the house account; the hand that writes not the hand that holds; the debt to stand until claimed, and to be discharged where the household says. Entered by ----------------------, who is not the man who heard it.

# Seven lines pasted inside the cover

*Not part of the ordinance. Said under the breath at the counter while the tally is cut, and never when the factor's man is in the room. I found the leaf pasted in and have not taken it out.*

> Six on the sill and a seventh not cut,
> the wood keeps the year that the slate left out;
> grey in the corner and crust by the door,
> what nobody claimed stands and stands, and no more.
>
> One in twelve, says the notice, read twice at the Cross;
> the house says the thaw, and the thaw says a sum;
> the notch is a place and the slate is a figure,
> and the place goes on when the figure is done.

> *In the narrow upright hand, against the last line:* Seven lines pasted. *In mine:* I count seven and so does he. He has put no number against the notch, which is how I know he has looked at it. A man does not number what he has not looked at.

# At the head and in the gutter

The brown at the head is iron-gall and mine, made by holding the quire open through a market morning; it will not come out of the creases. The bone-pale streak in the gutter is slip off the forearms of a thrower who held her slate away from her coat while she waited and set it for a moment against this leaf. The line scored deep in the lower margin is mine and was cut with the small knife I keep at the counter, the same morning the woman wanted six called seven; a knife goes into paper more easily than into a bench, but the mark stays in both. The tallow at the fold came off the lamp I keep turned low while I write, so that a woman at the counter will not change her tale for me. The grey along the outer edge came off my own cuff going down to the Cross on Account-day, where I put my hand into a corner I had no business putting it into. The green bead in the corner is not slip; it is lead glaze from a girl come up from High Kiln with the sweetness still at her mouth, who leaned to see the form of entry and did not ask to have it filled. I have kept everything where it fell. There is no Last Day behind a counter.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody has claimed

> Under the roof they count in loaves; above the wheel they count in years; and the bench counts both in wood and says neither thing.

I have asked the factor's man, a man come down from the College, and a woman who stood here about her house, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the line where it was written. Tomorrow the factor's man will come up from Greybank with a debt bought before it is due and want the entry in his own hand, and I shall write *bought* underneath as I have written it every tend; a woman will come with six years in her thumb and ask me to make them seven, and I shall write the day that stands. The bench behind the counter has six notches cut into it and a seventh place scored deep with a knife and not cut through. I did not cut it and cannot say who did. It will be there in the morning.
