I keep the firing-tally at the fourth kiln and I have set this down because the women at the pit asked for a saying they could use with their hands in the clay. A woman who has just lost a pot does not want an argument, and a woman who has just lost a person does not want a figure; both can stand a sentence said to the ground. The order that comes down from Sill is a good order for a room with a table in it. We have no table, only the wet bank and the boards laid across it, and a broken pot goes into the ball before the next firing as a matter of course. The Return seemed the same sort of work. We have said it so since the year of the flower, when three went in one tend and two names came back.

I have set the clerk's words where he put them, above mine. He came up about the road, the quarrel between Nine Pots and Sill that outlives everybody who started it, and while he waited he borrowed the leaf and took the doing out of it. I have put the doing back underneath, as a potter puts the thumb back in a handle smoothed by somebody who never held a pot.

The slip at the head is mine and dries bone-pale, the colour of a potter's forearms at the end of a day in the pits. The red dust came off the kiln crown when the fourth was drawn and has gone into the grain. The sherd in the fold came up with the cupworm's bore already through it and lies over the word *whole* in station III, so that the word shows hollow when the fold is shut; I have not written it again. The green is not ours, for our ware is coarse; a girl come up from the Cross with the sweetness at her mouth leaned to see the tally and left it. The wool is a woman's, the ale ring has gone dark and will go no darker. None of it is a figure and none has been entered.

# The manner of it, written small at the head

- Open the kiln before you speak. A pot still in the fire has not answered, and a saying over it is a saying to the fire, which is done in Sill.
- Say it to the clay, the boards or the bank. Said looking at a woman it becomes a question, and there is always a woman who will answer it.
- Grind the broken pot small before the ball is made. A sherd ground fine cannot be told from the rest of the clay, which is the whole of what we mean by a return.
- The answer belongs to everybody at the pit, including the child with no birth-slate and the one who came back quiet and does not know which name to answer to. Neither is corrected.
- When a man comes up from Sill about the road, give him the tally and not the leaf. The tally is a document and the road has an entry. The leaf is a saying and has none.
- If the bell at the Cross carries over the fields, stop. It is counting the pigs. Begin again after.

# The litany

*One woman reads from the tally. The pit answers. The answer is the same at every station, which is why it is a litany and not a firing.*

## I. Of the pit

> Before the pot there was the bank, and before the bank
> the clay lay wet with no shape laid on it.
> A shape is a thing done to clay. It is not the clay.
> We dig, we wet, we tread. The pit does not ask why.

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## II. Of the firing

> The world was fired whole and the lid lifted in one anger.
> The Warm went out where no wall held it
> and ran into the country that keeps nothing.
> We do not ask where the country is. A potter knows
> a thing can be lost without leaving the yard.
> The crack has a name. Say it once to the bank.

*Here the fourth kiln is drawn and the pots come out hot. Nobody touches one before the station is said.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## III. Of the nine

> Nine hands went after what had run away.
> Four kilns stand at this pit and a fifth never did;
> the place where it would stand is the road to Sill,
> and we do not fire upon the road.
> Nine went. One is still abroad.
> Count the kilns from the west and you will get four.
> Count from the east and you will get four. The other is not here.

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## IV. Of the grinding

> The broken pot is ground back into the ball.
> Nothing is lost of it that was clay.
> The lip that went first is in the new lip;
> the belly that held is in the new belly.
> It comes up out of the pit with another name on it
> and answers when it is struck.
> What was carried out is carried in. That is the Return.

*Here a sherd is ground under the reader's heel. It is not a ceremony. It is the work, and the work is the ceremony.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## V. Of the note

> Strike a sound pot and it answers full.
> Strike a cracked one and it answers flat.
> The cupworm goes in where the clay was worked too long
> and the pot looks sound until the first pouring.
> A flat note is a thing said plainly.
> Carry it anyway. Set it where the light can find it.
> Do not glaze a crack and call it mended.

*The sherd with the bore lies here. The hole covers a word; the reader says the word and the hole stays.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## VI. Of the figure

> One in twelve, as the notice says. Printed. Read twice.
> The notice does not lean and the figure does not sweat.
> Count pots, not years. A year is a kiln nobody weighs.
> The figure read in the square and the figure the household pays
> are two pots set down side by side;
> nobody here has made them agree.

*Here the reader touches the tally. The other figure is not said aloud, nor said quietly. It is left in the clay, where it goes soft in winter and comes back in the thaw.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## VII. Of the pouring

> One measure into one vessel: he comes back with his name,
> with the hand that turned the wheel and the debt it carried.
> Many into many: they come back as they can,
> with a taste for a table they never sat at
> and a hand that goes out toward a cup already cracked.
> Both are ground. Both are thrown. Both go into the same fire.
> The pit does not know which it has been given.

*Here the leaf leaves a space the length of a name. It is not a name forgotten. The woman whose boy came back has written in it, and then written no more.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

## VIII. Of the coming back

> Say the name into the bank, not over the cradle.
> Once for the house, once for the one who came up the road
> after the kiln was closed.
> If you have no name, leave the place where the name would stand
> and set a pot down in it.
> Do not sweep the bank. It is not dirt.
> Open the kiln. Strike the pot. Carry it home.

*Here the leaf ends and the firing begins. No clerk from Sill has ever understood why the reading comes before the heat.*

*Answer:* Grind it small. Throw it new. Come back in the pot.

# What the clerk did

He came up about the road on an Account-day with his cuff gone soft at the edge from washing and the ink settled in two fingers of it. He borrowed the leaf while a cart was being loaded and gave it back with the doing taken out, which is what they teach in the counting house at Sill. I have left his hand above mine and mine under it, as a repaired pot keeps the old break under the new slip.

- At station IV, against *what was carried out is carried in*, he wrote: *the measure is drawn, banked and pressed into a newborn vessel.* Under it: *That is the account of it. Ours is what the hands do, and the hands are in the pit.*
- At station VI he put a small stop against *the figure the household pays* and wrote the printed figure again above it, larger. It is the figure they like to see. I have left both, and nobody here has asked me to choose.
- At station III, where the fifth kiln would stand, he put a round nought and wrote *vacancy* beside it, which is a true figure about a road. Under mine: *The road has a place and no fire. He has entered the place and not the quarrel, which is the whole art of that hand.*
- At the answer he wrote *one answer, entered eight times.* True in the figure and false at the pit, where eight mouths say it and the ninth, at the back, says it a half-beat late. We do not correct her. She is grinding something of her own.

He asked what the women learned by it. I told him the grinding and the note and the carrying home. He said that was the Return taught with a sherd in the hand, and wrote it down. We said it again after he went, because a saying said to a man goes off with the man.

# What the woman whose boy came back put in the margin

The hand is charcoal and slips at the *th*, being letters learned off a tally and not off a book. She was at the pit when the fourth was drawn and came back in the dark to read what I had written.

At II: *It was a good pot. It split at the lip, not in the belly. We ground it with the rest and made a cup out of it, and I have that cup by me. Nobody knows it was him but me, and the cup does not know either.*

At the worm: *A worm does not break a pot. A pot breaks where the clay was worked too long and the worm goes in where it is already hollow. We say the worm did it because a worm is a thing you can point at.*

At VII: *He came back quiet and wanted a cup. We gave him one with a crack in it and he held it the right way up. That was the only whole thing he did in six years, and we did not tell the clerk, because he would have entered it against him.*

At the answer: *Say it for the pots. I do not say it for him. He is here and he is not the one we said it for.* Then, underneath and very small: *I have ground him. That is not the same as having him back.*

I have left both lines standing. A pit that scores itself will soon be a Court, and I have seen how those rooms go.

# At the head and in the fore-edge

The slip thumb is mine and has dried the colour of old bone in every crease; it is how the fourth kiln signs anything it has touched. The red along the fore-edge is crown dust and comes off whenever the leaf is carried past the kiln mouth, which is every day in Ember. The sherd lies over the hollow word and I have not moved it, because a word with a hole in it is a word said through the teeth, and most of ours are. The green is not glaze of ours, for Nine Pots makes coarse ware; a girl who had tasted at the Cross left it and went back up the field without saying what she had seen. The wool is a woman's; the dark of the ring came from a quart that stood from the first station to the eighth with nobody drinking it, which is how a quart usually goes at a pit in Ember.

# At the foot

In a hand that is not mine, not the clerk's and not the woman's, struck once and written again underneath, very small:

> The clay keeps what it is given and gives it back in the pot.
>
> The Hearth keeps what it is given and gives it back when it is paid.

I asked the woman at the bank, the girl who carries pots down to Sill and a man who came up about the road, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the line where it was struck. The leaf goes back into the sacking and under the flat stone, and next Ember the fourth kiln will be drawn cold and we shall say the eight stations to the bank and grind the broken pots in before the ball, and the clerk will come up about the road with his soft cuff and put the doing back where the doing was, and I shall set it back as I did this year. The ninth has not come down the road yet, the fifth kiln has never been built, and the cup by the woman's bed does not know what it is.
