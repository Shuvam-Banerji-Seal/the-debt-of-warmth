title: The Ninefold Coming Back, Called the Litany of the Long Night
kind: A prayer of the Return, said in the dark on the Long Night, at the bedside of the dying, and over a birth-slate; with the household's answer
provenance: Written in charcoal and tallow on a leaf of birch bark by a hearth-maid of Low Kiln in Sill in Y.H. 811, the Long Cold, and added to in Y.H. 812, the Thin Year; she set her mark in the corner in place of a name, being two fingers crossed. The leaf is kept folded in the fold of a grey wool birth-cloth that passes from hearth-maid to hearth-maid in the low houses, and is taken out only after dark. It bears a tallow thumb, a smear of bone-pale slip, a spot of green from a glaze-room mouth, a charcoal print shaped like a pot's foot, and two corrections in different hands. It survives because the cloth is washed oftener than the leaf.

I am the fourth hand to write on this leaf and the first to say whose it is, except that I shall not say it. The fold had gone soft and I have not mended it, because a fold that has been mended sits shut and a prayer needs to be got at in the dark. What follows is what is on the leaf, including the parts a clerk would take out. It is not a thing to read standing up.

The manner of it, written small at the head in the first hand:

- Turn the slate face down. It has heard enough for one night.
- Bank the fire but do not cover it. A prayer needs somewhere to go.
- Warm the stone in the hand before you put it to the belly. This is not part of the prayer. It is the part before.
- The answer is said by whoever is in the room, including the child, who cannot say it and says it anyway.
- When the bell goes at the Cross, stop. It is counting something else. Begin again after.

# The nine callings

*The voice is the hearth-maid's. The answer belongs to the room.*

**I**

The light under the Keel was here before the counting.
It did not begin in a house. It was found in the dark.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**II**

Nine hands went after what had run away.
Nine repairs were driven home, and the ninth is not finished.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**III**

The world was whole in the telling and cracked in one anger.
What fell out of it went a long way and is going still.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**IV**

The pot was set down and answered full.
Set down again, it answered flat.
It was carried anyway. It is carried anyway.
Leave the crack showing. Glaze what you will.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**V**

Write him in the house roll. Write him in the other book.
Write him small. The slate is clean and does not care.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**VI**

One in twelve, as the notice says, for the keeping of the fire.
The fire was kept. The fire is always kept.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**VII**

The iron is cold before anybody asks it.
Take what you are owed and leave the shape.
The fingers go grey. Somebody's fingers go grey.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**VIII**

Warm the stone first. A cold stone is a stone, not a kindness.
He will get worse before he gets better. That is the rule.
If he comes back wrong, keep him. Keep him anyway.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

**IX**

Say the name. Say it twice: once for the moot, once for them that came late.
If you have no name, leave the place where the name would stand.
Do not sweep the corner. It is not dirt. It is somebody.

*Answer:* Come back warm. Come back as you can.

Here the leaf leaves a space. It is not a line forgotten. It is four, four, and the place where the fifth should be, which the kiln keeps and the bell keeps and this house keeps. Nobody fills it.

Four for the corners. Four for the walls.

And one for the one that is not in the room.

*Answer:* Come back warm.

# What stands in the margins

At calling IV, a hand that writes with its verbs missing struck out *Leave the crack showing* and set above it, *A return is whole by definition; strike the line.* Under this, in a different hand, the thick round letters of the birth-slates: *It is a proverb about pots, and pots are honest. Put it back.* Both lines stand. I have not chosen between them. It is a prayer, not a slate; it is allowed to disagree with itself.

The *one in twelve* above was added by the same verbless hand, which borrowed the leaf one Long Night and underlined its own figure twice. We left it where it was. It is the figure they like to see. In this house we give the other one, and say it into the clay, where it goes soft in the winter and comes back in the thaw.

The line about the fingers was put in by a woman who had sat with a neighbour two doors down. She wrote *somebody's* and then scratched the word until the bark showed through, and wrote it again the same. I have left both.

The ninth calling was not on the leaf in the Long Cold. It was added in the Thin Year by a hand that had stood at a Drawing and watched a child grow worse on the third day. She wrote it, turned the leaf face down, and went home without the answer.

The green at the top right is not from our glaze room. A girl with lead at her mouth leaned over on the Long Night to see what the words were and touched the leaf with her thumb. She may not have known it was a prayer. She may have known and not cared. I have not washed it, and neither did the third hand, who washed everything else she came near.

At the foot, in the first hand, two fingers crossed and under them: *Say it in the dark. It will not bear the light, and neither will we.*

The leaf is not for the Hearthseat. If it goes there it will be copied properly, with the verbs put back and the empty place filled, and then it will be a notice, and no use to anybody.
