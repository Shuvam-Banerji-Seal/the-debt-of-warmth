I keep the watch-book at the post where the Rim breaks and the road goes through without a gate. My head stands at forty-four embers when I have eaten and forty-one when I have not. My name is Senn. I give the number first because that is the order at this gate and because the cold does not care what a man is called. Karric shut the pass in Y.H. 741 and the agreement of Y.H. 747 made it a border; there is a post here and no gate, and the Warden's surveyors do not promise to bring a man back. I have written nothing in this book that I have not stood at. What I have only heard I have set down as heard, and have put no number against it.

# What comes

It is tall and thin, with the shape of a man who has stood a long while with his arms at his sides. It is the colour of the old snow in shadow and it gives off no smell, which is the first thing a new man does not believe. I have put a hand near one in still air and smelled nothing but my own coat.

It takes notice of a head. I have laid a stone carved to a man's shape at the edge of the road and left it three nights; nothing came to it. I have seen a man lying face down under a coat with his head turned away be passed over in the dark and then found again, the way a man finds a thing he had mislaid. It is not a man it wants and it is not a piece of a man. It is the place where the ways run close under the skin of the skull.

It says nothing. Knuckle, who is of the Waste and comes to the post to trade words he has no use for, says it has nothing to say because there is nobody in it left to say with. I have set that down as his saying and not as mine.

It does not kill. This is the part that the hearths get wrong when they tell it, and the part that stays with me. A thing that kills is done when the man is dead. This one is not done. It stands and goes on standing, and when the man has nothing more to give it goes and stands somewhere else.

# The order of a taking

In Bone I watched one take Derro of the survey. He was a Drawn man with both forearms opened, and when he went up the road past the post his note rang high and thin under the knuckle, eighty-eight embers by the gate's own tap. He went for a stone the survey had asked for and had not found.

It came up behind him where the road lifts before the last turning. I was below with a lamp and a stick and I did not go in, because I had been told what to do and because there were three of us and none of us was Drawn. The thing came close and stood. It did not touch him. Derro said something, then said the same thing again in a smaller voice, and then put his hand into his coat for the stone that was not there. The cold came off the place where it stood the way cold comes off a north wall at dusk. I felt it at thirty paces.

We got him down before noon. I tapped him at the gate and the note was a coin dropped on wet boards. Forty-one. In the days after he lost a little more and then stopped losing, and he lived. He asks every morning for the stone he went out to fetch, and there was no stone. He does not remember the road, or the turning, or why his arms are marked. He is warm now by the fire at the keep and he will never be told what he went for.

I have set down the order of it because a man who has to stand watch should know the order: it comes close, it stands, the warm goes out slowly at first and then faster, and the man stops wanting the thing he went for. There is no struggle to see. There is no anger in it that I could find, and I have written *no anger* in the margin and have not taken the word out.

# The watch-chant

*Said low at the post when the lamp is set in the niche and the road is out. The last line is said by all.*

> Three for the stone and one for the head,
> one for the road the snow took in;
> say the figure going and say none coming,
> for the post keeps a count it will not spend.
>
> It does not bite. It does not cry.
> It stands where a man's warm stood.
> Keep your hand on the side away from it
> and keep your number under your coat.
>
> Ten days to a tend and one to the pass,
> and a man goes through and the count goes dry;
> the post has no gate and the Warden no promise,
> and the cold has no hand to sign for it.

> *In the assessor's narrow upright hand, against the last line:* No payer. Obligation not entered. *In mine, underneath:* There is no payer. I have put that word back where he took it out, because a man who goes through here and does not come back is a man and not an entry.

# The tally

These are the watches I kept a true account of. The figure in the third column is mine, taken at the gate with the full note before a man went out. The fourth is mine again, taken when he came in. Some days I did not write because my hands were full and some because the lamp went out; those days are not in the book.

| Watch | Who went | Head out | Head in | What came back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bone 3 | Senn, post-duty | 44 | 44 | a stone with a scratch on it, set in the gutter |
| Bone 11 | Derro of the survey, Drawn | 88 | 41 | nothing; asks for the stone |
| Bone 19 | Knuckle, of the Waste | not taken | not taken | a day's warmth, given away |
| Stone 2 | two of the gate, with a lamp | 51 and 47 | 50 and 46 | the lamp still lit, the wick not touched |
| Stone 9 | Derro, a second time | 41 | 22 | no name for the place he went |

> *Written above the columns in the assessor's narrow upright hand:* Depletion entered as exposure. No person lost, no payer. The Waste keeps no account and owes nothing.
>
> *In mine, beneath:* He has put *exposure* where I put the standing shape, and a word for weather is a word nobody has to answer for. I have left his hand where he set it. I have not struck mine.

Knuckle's row is the one I cannot enter. He came to the post on Bone 19 with his day's warmth held in both hands and gave it to a man going down the road with no coat, and asked for nothing, not thanks and not a mark on a stick. Wastelings do this. They will give a stone, a name, or a day's warmth and expect nothing back. I have been a soldier eleven years and I have no column for it. A gift with no account in it has no place in a tally, and I have set it in the tally anyway, with *not taken* written in both figures.

# What the hearths say and what I have seen

The hearths say it is the Waste itself come to fetch what is owed. Knuckle says no: it is a vessel that has emptied itself so far that it has become a place where the warm goes in and does not stop. He said it with his finger pointing at the cold, which is how a Wasteling gives a second name. I have no learning and I will not say he is right. I will say what I have put my hand to.

I have laid my hand on the stone where one stood and on a stone a spear-cast away. The one under it is cold in a way the other is not, and it stays cold after the sun has been on it half the morning. I have followed a man who had one behind him back over the Rim and watched what it did to the house he went into: not a death, not a sickness, only a household that went on with less in it than it had before, and nobody in it able to say what had been taken. A whole village has to turn out to drive one off, and even then it does not go far. It goes and stands somewhere else.

The thing that walks upright with its face set the way it was facing when it broke is another thing. That is a dead man whose warm was never drawn. I have seen three on the road below the post and did not mix them with this one, and have not entered them here. A man who mixes two shapes because both are cold will write the wrong thing and then wonder why his book does not agree with itself.

I asked a Vorr at the Listening Stones how many of these there are. He said the numbers were correct. I have set that down and left it where it is.

# At the head and in the gutter

The smut at the head is coal off the sleeve I wipe the leaf with, and it is in the grain and no damp takes it out. The frost came up out of the paper on the three nights the tin stood open and is gone by noon; it is the only thing in this book that entered without a hand. The warm-stone shaving in the fold came off a piece a man of the survey set down while he counted his marks and went away without it. It is still warm on the coldest morning and I have not sold it. The stone with the scratch is the one I laid out on Bone 3 and the one I stood watch against; Knuckle looked at it and said the scratch was the shape of where it had stood, and would not say whose. The brown thumb is mine. The line that went soft under snow I have not written back in, because a count with a figure gone is a count said slowly, and everything here is said slowly. The assessor's correction stands above my words and I have not struck it out. The single stroke in the margin was made by Knuckle with a charcoal point and means, as far as I could make out, *point at the cold*. He would not say it in Grathic and I have not asked twice. The grey print along the outer fold is not mine and is not the assessor's, and I have not swept it. There is no Last Day at a post.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will claim

> The post keeps no man. The cold keeps what the post will not.

Last night a man came up from the lower road and said he had seen one going the other way, toward the keep and not away from it, following a surveyor who had come over the Rim two days before and thought he had left it behind. I did not see it. I have set it down as a story and put no mark against it. In the next Bone I will take a fresh stick and set down only what I stand at, and if it follows one of us home I will write the number of the house it stops at, in my own hand, and leave the rest for the next hand that opens this book.
