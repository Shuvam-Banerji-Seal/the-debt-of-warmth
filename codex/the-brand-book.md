I keep the irons.

They lie in the drawer under this counter, the brand and the clip together in a cloth gone grey where the iron touches it. An officer comes in from the yard, I give him whichever the ordinance asks for, and when he has used it I enter the figure. There is a woman will come with a cup in her apron and a neighbour's warmth in it, or say there is, and a man who will not consent. I set down the ordinance's figure, and in the margin, in whatever hand the person has, the mark that was left. A brand entered without a man is a brand nobody wore, and that is the kind of entry the Keeping likes best.

The ordinance is Law, and Law is the slate: what is written stands; what is not written did not happen. By that rule a brand has no pain in it and a theft is a figure like any other. I have kept this book eleven years and have never been able to add a mark. I can add the figure.

> *At the head, in mine, written very small:* Copied for the counter and from the drawer. The marks are in the hands of those who wore them; I asked nobody to write a word they did not choose. If this is read in the Hearthseat, let it be seen that the ordinance was copied by one hand and the consequences by several.

# Title the Sixth, as it stands in the Court's room

*From the Ordinances of the Keeping, Title the Sixth: Of the Theft of Warmth, the Cold-Iron Brand and the Unclosed Account. Confirmed to the factor at Greybank by the treaty of the Blue Cliff in Y.H. 418; added to after the Rising of Y.H. 668, in Y.H. 782, and in the Thin Year, Y.H. 812.*

## I. Of the theft

1. Theft of warmth is a draw made without a slate. Warm taken from another vessel by tube, by cup, by hand or by standing close, where no slate stands, is a theft. The figure entered is the embers taken, and not the marks the taking left.

> *Margin, in a sloping hand I do not know, the letters pressed hard:* I took four embers out of a cup on the bench; the girl was at the tubs. *In mine:* Written by the man before the iron came out; he held the slate while I read it back.

2. A cup has no standing to complain. Where warmth is taken from a vessel that cannot speak, the house in which it stood is complainant, and the entry is made to the house. The cup is not asked.

> *Margin:* A pot cracked through gives a small flat knock when it is set down, and that is the only testimony a vessel here may give. I have not entered the knock. The cup cannot speak, which is convenient for the house and for the Court.

3. Where the warmth taken has been spent, the figure stands against the thief's own measure and is carried forward. An account is never closed. What is taken is taken; what is owed is owed; what is carried forward is carried forward, and the three are written in the same hand.

4. One part in twelve of every measure banked is kept back for the Hearth, as the notice says. The same figure is kept back from a measure entered as a theft, so that the wrong done to a household is booked at the same rate as the right done to the Court.

> *Margin:* I have no second figure to enter. I heard a figure in the yard that would not agree with the notice; a clerk who enters a figure he cannot show on a slate is looking for another trade. I have not put it beneath this one.

## II. Of the brand

5. Theft punished by a cold iron brand upon the wrist and a deduction from the thief's own measure. The brand is set by the officer appointed thereunto and in the presence of a slate. The hand that sets is not the hand that holds the book.

> *Margin, in a small careful hand:* He set it on the left. Cold: it took the warmth out and gave none back. A pale place the width of my thumb, come up in the crease where my cuff had been. *In mine:* He wrote this before he put his cuff down. I have kept his word and the cuff's.

6. The brand is pale and not red. It shows through the winter. It is spoken of as nothing and is not entered as a wound.

> *Margin:* I have seen a man hold his wrist to the light under the door and rub his cuff down over it while the figure was read, and say it would go in the thaw. That is what my mother said about everything. It is not in the ordinance.

7. Where a second brand is set over a first, the later mark stands and the earlier is not struck out. The figure is carried forward on the same rule.

> *Margin, in a hand that shakes at the ends of the lines:* Twice now. The second over the first, the first showing at the edge. They entered the second. I have kept both where they can be seen.

## III. Of the unclosed account

8. Refusal of a lawful collection is the offence called an unclosed account. The vessel is clip-held by the wrist until consent is entered. The clip is cold iron and gives nothing back. The pale ring it leaves is spoken of as nothing and is not entered as a wound.

> *Margin, in a woman's hand, the thumb of the other steadying the slate:* I said no three times. The ring came up where the iron had been. *In mine:* She consented, and the consent was entered by the officer in his own hand, as the ordinance provides. Her three words have no column. I have put them in hers.

9. Refusal changes no figure. The measure drawn is the measure that stood before the refusal; the account opened is the same account. What refusal adds is a mark.

> *Margin:* A woman asks what her no cost her; I show her the figure, which has not moved, and she looks at the wrist, which has. There is no column for the difference. I have made one here and drawn no figure in it.

10. Where the vessel has no house to consent for it, consent is entered by the hand of the officer. The foundling and the clay-bound are so entered. The hearth-maid lays the birth-slate on the bench before she goes to the child; this house does not say so, and the ordinance does not say so, and it is so.

## IV. Of what the articles leave outside

11. The articles reach the wrist and no further. There is no entry for warmth given at a house door, nor for a pot broken at a death-house to let the heat out, nor for a measure left in a body where no officer was called.

> *Margin:* Three things I have entered nowhere: a bowl of gruel given at a door; a pot broken at the Bone Orchard; a room I was not called to on the last night of the Long Cold, where somebody lay warm and nobody came. I have no slate for any and will not invent one. Beyond the wrist is kept here, in a hand with no standing at any Weighing.

12. An officer of the Keeping has no house and no kin. The account is her family. The brand she sets is not set in anger and is not set in mercy; it is entered.

> *Margin:* I asked the officer who came in the Thin Year whether he had a house. He said the account was his family, as the ordinance says, and asked for a string to tie his cuff, the clip having taken the feeling out of two fingers. I gave him one and did not enter it.

# The table of offences

> *Margin:* The greyed hand and the pale brand come off the same iron and go into the book by different Titles. I have not joined the rows. A clerk who joins them has begun to write a sermon, and sermons get copied.

# The form of entry

*Set out at the end of Title the Sixth. Copied with the blanks as they stand. In eleven years I have never had a name for the last line that a household wanted spoken in this room.*

> Entered this day, being ---day of -------, in the year of the Hearth ------, the theft of ------- embers of warmth from the vessel of ----------------------, the same taken without a slate; the cold iron brand set upon the wrist by ----------------------, in the presence of a slate; the deduction carried forward to the account of ----------------------; the mark entered in the margin in the hand of ----------------------, who is the hand that wore it and not the hand that took. Entered by ----------------------, who was not there when the warmth went out.

# The ring-song

*Said low at the counter while the cuff comes down, never while the iron is out of the drawer. Never in the yard, never at a Weighing.*

> The cup keeps a crack and the wrist keeps a ring,
> the iron keeps neither, and that is the art;
> a figure goes up for the warmth that went out
> and a mark goes down for the part.
>
> Cold in the morning and pale at the cuff,
> the thumb marks the place where the thumb was set;
> a no is a word and a ring is a place,
> and the slate has a column for neither of those.
>
> They read the figure twice at the Cross,
> the cup cannot answer, the house cannot choose;
> I hold the slate while the man writes his row
> and put no figure to what he must lose.

> *In a narrow upright hand, against the last line of the first verse:* One figure entered. *In mine:* Two columns here and he has numbered one. A man who numbers the other has looked at the wrist, which is how I know the College has been reading my margin.

# What the two borrowed hands did

The College man came down from the Hearthseat with ink in two fingers. He struck *took* out of the first article and set *was taken* above, as though a theft were a thing that happened to nobody, in a room with nobody in it. I have not rubbed him out. I have written *took* underneath in the margin, in the hand of the man who wrote it there himself, where the next person at this counter can see which hand had a wrist in it.

The other hand is a factor's, leaning and brown at the edges, come up from Greybank with the treaty in its pocket. Against article five it wrote *correction entered* above my *brand set*, the word the ordinance uses when it wants a room to go quiet. I have left both. A correction is done to an account; a brand is done to a man.

# At the head and in the gutter

The grey at the head is Ash off my own thumb, turning the leaf toward the lamp while the officer read a figure over it; the colour of a corner swept too late, and it will not come out of the creases. The filings in the gutter are iron off the cloth the irons lie in; some comes away whenever the drawer is opened, and it has never once drawn the warmth out of the paper, which is the only mercy this drawer holds. The pale round ring at the foot is skin-grease where a branded wrist was laid while I read the figure back; it is not a figure and cannot be added, and I have not washed it out. The tallow thumb beside it is mine. The green bead is lead glaze from a girl come up from High Kiln with the sweetness still at her mouth, who leaned to see the form of entry and set her hand down instead of asking. The bare square in the lower margin is where I scraped a word away with the point of the clip-key and put none in its place; I have not written it back. The tide-line came from the Waiting Room, where the collected are kept overnight before banking. There is no Last Day at this counter, and nothing here is swept up and burnt and called dealt with.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

> They can add the ember. They cannot add the ring.

I asked the officer, a man come down from the College, and a woman who stood here about her wrist, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the line where it was written.

The book goes back into the drawer and the irons into their cloth. Tomorrow a woman will come with a cup in her apron, or say there is, and a man who will not consent, and I shall hand out the iron the ordinance asks for and enter the figure, and ask the person whose wrist it was to write the mark in whatever hand they have. They can add the figure. They cannot add the other thing. That is why I keep a second column, and why nobody has asked to see it.
