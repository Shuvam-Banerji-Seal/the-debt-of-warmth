I keep the found-slate in the Court's room at Slate Row. A man comes in off the north road with a boot in his hand and tells me where the other one was found, and I read him what stands below before I open a slate. A finder wants two things before he will say where a body lay: that he will not be asked what he was doing there, and that nothing will be entered against his house. The order gives him the first and takes the second. It is in Ledgerhand, with nobody in its sentences, which is how the Keeping makes a finding read as though the ground had found the body and carried it in. I put the people back underneath.

> At the head of the first leaf, in mine, written small: Copied for the found and from the found. The verbs are mine. The absence of them is not. Let both stand.

The order was set down after the Rising, when it was found that a house would send for the collector for a body it knew and for nobody for a body it did not, and that the Court was being asked to enter what it had not been asked to keep. A rule with no house on it is a rule nobody will claim. These have been claimed, and not always by the dead.

# The standing order, as it stands in the Court's room

*From the ordinances of the Keeping: Of the Body Found, the Uncollected Dead, and the Ash Gathered Where No House Sent for the Collector. Sent down to the factor at Greybank in Y.H. 418; set down in this form after the Rising of Y.H. 668; added to in Y.H. 782 and in the Thin Year, Y.H. 812.*

## I. Of the finding

1. A vessel found dead outside the door of a house is entered as found and not as dead.
2. The finder is entered as finder and not as kin. He is not asked what he was doing where the body lay.
3. No slate is opened over a found body until a house is named. Where no house is named, no slate is opened.
4. The face is covered before the body is moved. The hand that covers is not the hand that writes.

> *Margin:* I asked a man come down from Cold Acre why the finder is not asked what he was doing there. He said a man who has found a body has already answered enough by finding it. The order has no place for his words and the slate has room at the edge.

> *Margin:* A woman came in with her apron held over her face and would not say whether she had turned the body over. I entered the finding and not the turning. There is no column for what a hand does before it sends for anybody, and I have left a place the length of it.

## II. Of the carrying

5. The body is carried to the Bone Orchard by the north road and laid in the east ground where the pots are broken, and not among the shallow rows.
6. No pot is broken over a found body. There is no house door at which to break it, and a pot broken without a door is entered to the finder.
7. The ash under the body is gathered and entered to the ground where it lay, and not to the vessel. The ground is not asked.
8. What the body held is entered as found: a boot, a cord, a copper, a crust of barley bread. Nothing is returned, because nothing was given.

> *Margin:* A pot was broken once over a man found on the north road, by a woman who said the house would want it done. The pot was entered to her and the finding stood, and she has not found another body since. Both happened on the same day, and the order has no way of joining them.

## III. Of the collector

9. No tube is set to a found body. There is no mouth at which to draw and no consent to enter.
10. Where a house claims the body after the finding, the drawing at the mouth is done as in the order for the last bed, the finding is struck, and the death is entered. A finding struck is not carried forward as a death.
11. Where no house claims it, the measure is entered to no account, and the roll is carried forward over the finding.
12. A collector who sets a tube to a found body without the factor's word in writing has made a fault, and the figure is struck from his own slate.

> *Margin:* A house claimed a body on the fourth day, and the finding was struck as the order provides. The woman stood here while I struck it and asked whether the four days were entered anywhere. I told her no. That is true, and was not what she asked. I have set the asking at the edge, the length of it.

> *Margin:* A collector set his tube down here while he went for a cup, and the pale ring it left is in the foot of this leaf. He did not set it to the body. I have entered the ring nowhere. It is the thinnest part of the paper.

## IV. Of the roll and the Last Day

13. A finding has no name to speak. Where a name is entered afterward by a house, it is spoken on the Remembering as a return and not as a finding.
14. Ash gathered from a finding is swept with the house ash on the Last Day and burned, and called dealt with.
15. The finding is carried forward and never closed.

> *Margin:* On the Remembering a woman stood here to hear a name, and a clean voice announced a return. She asked whether the roll had closed. I told her no roll closes, which is true, and was not what she asked. I have written the return and set the standing at the edge.

> *Margin:* The ash from a finding is swept with the rest and burned with the rest, and the two are the same ash in the order and not the same ash in the yard. A house sweeps its corner because the Last Day has come. The east ground is swept because somebody remembered to bring a broom. I have left the difference here, where a man must lean to read it.

## V. Of faults and their correcting

| Fault | As entered in the Court's room | What the clerk sets beneath |
|---|---|---|
| A verb in the finding | The page copied again, in the same hand | The verb restored where it stood |
| A body carried before the face was covered | The finder corrected | The hand that covered, which came after |
| A pot broken without a door | Entered to the finder | The door that was not there |
| A tube set to a found body | Figure struck from the drawer's slate | The mouth that was not there |
| A finding called a death | Struck and entered as a death | The four days, which no column holds |
| A name spoken over a finding | Entered as a return | The name, at the edge, the length of it |

16. A fault copied twice in one tend is a fault the finder or the officer has not understood, and the senior clerk sits with him at the counter until he has. The found-slate is not a rod. It is a piece of stone with a place where a person was not.

Findings on this leaf: five. Deaths entered among them: one. Names spoken: none from this counter.

# The form of finding

*Set out at the end of the order, with the blanks as they stand. In nine years no finder has filled the last line in the hand the Court requires.*

> Found this day, being ---day of -------, in the year of the Hearth ------, a vessel in ----------------------, the face covered before the moving and by a hand other than this hand, no slate opened, no house named, the body carried to the east ground and laid apart from the rows, no pot broken, the ash gathered to the ground and not to the vessel, the tube not set and no consent entered; the finding carried forward and not closed; entered by ----------------------, who found and did not ask. The body held ----------------------, which is returned to nobody.

# The covering-song

*Said low over the face at the finding, before the moving and never afterward. Never within hearing of a house that has sent for the collector.*

> Cover the face and leave the boots,
> the road keeps what the house might claim;
> four at the bell and a gap for the fifth,
> no slate goes out for a body's name.
>
> One for the ground and one for the ash,
> and one for the hand that turned the head;
> the pot stays whole because there's no door,
> the name stays where the finder said.
>
> Ash in the morning, ash at the Last,
> burnt together and called dealt with;
> the roll goes on and the finding goes on,
> and nobody comes to close it.
>
> Cover the face and do not ask
> what the tube would take and leave unsaid;
> the cold keeps count and the count keeps none,
> and the road brings back what the road has carried.

The fourth verse came in with the Thin Year and is not said by the clerks. I have copied it as it came. The word scored in the lower margin was written against the second line by a hand that thought a name was a thing a finder could give.

# What the two borrowed hands did

The College man came down from the Hearthseat with ink in two fingers of a cuff gone soft from washing. He struck *the hand that covers* out of rule four and set *the face is covered* above it, as though a face could cover itself and nobody had knelt to do it. On rule eleven he wrote *no deduction arises*, which is true of the roll and false of the ground. I have put both of mine back underneath and left his above.

The other hand is a factor's from Greybank, leaning and brown at the edge, with a little gold in it. Against the finding entered to no account he wrote *uncollected vessel; no figure outstanding*, and against the boot in the table he set a figure in bits. A hand with gold in it can put a figure on a boot, because a figure is a thing that can be entered and handed on; a man who has held the boot while the foot was still in it calls it a boot. I have not struck him through. Both stand.

# At the head and in the gutter

The grey at the head is Ash, off my own thumb turning the leaf toward the light; it does not come out of the creases. The black in the gutter is peat, worked in under the nails of a man who found a body in a cutting and held the leaf while I read; it is darker than Ash and it will not come out either. The iron filings came off a tube-wrap laid here while its owner went for a cup; no tube was set to anything in this room, but the filings stayed. The brown at the second fold is a crumb from a man who carried a body in and had not eaten since the day before. The pale ring is where a pot was set down and not broken, which is the whole of the mercy the order allows a finder. The grey wool through the tie-hole is off a cuff gone soft from washing; it is mine and I have not untied it. The green bead is lead glaze, pressed in by a girl come down from the glaze room who leaned to see the form of finding, asked nothing, and went back up the road with the sweetness still at her mouth. The tide-line along the foot came up off the market boards after rain. At the Last Day the houses sweep their corners and burn what they find and call it dealt with. This counter is not a house, and the Ash stays where it fell.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

> The house breaks its pot. The road breaks nothing.
> Between the two a body is found, covered, carried, and not entered.

I asked the finder, a man come down from the College, and a woman who stood here to claim a body, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the lines where they were written.

The leaf goes back into the tin. Tomorrow a man will come in off the north road with a boot in his hand, and I shall read him what stands here, open the slate and enter what was found. Something will be said that has no column. The face will be covered by one hand and written by another, and the finding will go forward and not be closed. That is my trade: to write down what was carried and leave a place the length of what was not.
