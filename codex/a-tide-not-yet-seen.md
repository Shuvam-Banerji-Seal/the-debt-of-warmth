kind: A private letter on salt-paper, from the Salt Pans to the Quay at Vhal, carried out in Ilmen and returned in Silt, with the answers of the woman addressed, one correction from a Council clerk, and a word of an older law stuck fast to the leaf

To Vea of the Quay, who holds a twelfth through her mother and will not say it before company. From Enna at the pans, where the wind has been in the east nine days and the paper will not take ink.

I have written this in the rain. The factor come up from Greybank does it and gets a fortune by it, so there must be something in the practice. If the Council reads what is left of this there will be nothing in it to read, and a letter that cannot be read is the only kind I have ever wanted to send you.

The seventh lot is short. I raked at the fourth hour and the crust came up as thin as the skim on the Sallow on a still morning, and when I told the pan-master he said the weather and went on with his counting, which is what a pan-master does when a woman has told him the truth. A brine-gull took a coal off the brazier while my back was turned. I threw a chip of cliff-glass at him and missed, and he went off with the coal warm under his crop, as though he had been invited.

The dippers came at dawn and trailed the nets and said the draw ran against the wind again, which it always does and which the Council is always surprised by. The clerk entered the figure that would be profitable and left the other standing in the wet where he could not see it. My mother used to say a law that lasts too long stops earning; the Council has made a virtue of it and then hired men to write things down. It is the funniest thing I know and I have never once laughed at it.

Old Iven went out on the ebb in the second tend, sealed in a salted boat, and we stood on the Quay and watched until it was out of sight, which is held to be enough. I thought of you the whole while and have not entered it. If I entered it I would have to charge you for it, and you would pay, and then there would be an end of it.

A man in a clean coat come along the Salt Road from Greybank asked after your mother's twelfth on the third day of the tend. I told him the document had been left in the rain. He said that in Vhal that was a virtue, and laughed as though he had invented it. He asked after quiet ash as well, though a salt-master has none to sell; I told him so and watched his smile go out like a lamp in a draught. He has not been back. I have not told your mother. If he comes again she will hear it from him, which is the proper order of things and costs me nothing.

I go up the Salt Road with the glass at the turn of the month. The carrier says two tendays and the pan-master says a season, and the pan-master is generally right about the length of other people's absences. I will be gone when the ebb comes that I said I would be back for. Do not hold it against me. Hold it against the road, which has been in a bad way since Silt; a thing said in Ilmen does not travel well once the mud takes it.

When we were fourteen you walked the pans barefoot at the Salt Walk and named a tide nobody had seen, and the dippers wrote it in the salt and it was gone by noon. I have tried all winter to get the word back. I have the shape of it and not the word. It went up at the end, or it went down, and I cannot bear to choose between the two, because I have chosen wrong every other time there was a thing to choose.

# In the margin, in a second hand, small and sibilant, the letters leaning

Iri. It went up at the end. You have put your stop in the wrong place and always did.

Do not tell me the seventh lot is short. Tell me you are going up the road. I am not a pan-master, and I can bear a thing said straight if it is said once.

# What the clerk did

A clerk of the Council borrowed the leaf on a market morning in Silt, being a man who cannot leave paper alone. He struck out the word *short* and set *current* above it. Against the tide-name he wrote *tide not entered*, in the narrow upright hand they teach at the counting house, with the verbs left out as though a verb were a thing a man could smuggle in a cuff. He put a figure in the margin where I had put none: *one mark and a binding promise*, which is what a salt-worker is paid, and which I suppose he thought he was being kind to enter. I have left all of it standing. He has entered the promise and not the tide, which is exactly what the Council does with both.

# In the margin again, lower down, the same hand

> Rake at the fourth, rake at the turn,
> the crust comes up where the wind has been;
> the gull takes the coal, the clerk takes the mark,
> and the pan keeps what the rain cannot reach.

I have never written a rhyme in my life and I would not begin now if I had a slate to write it on. You have written it in the rain, so there is no letter, and I have answered nothing. Ask me again in the dry and I will charge you.

There is a tide that has not come. I have named it for the day you come back down the Salt Road. Do not ask me what it is like. I have not seen it, and if I had seen it, it would not be the one.

# At the foot, in the first hand, written on the return

It came back wetter than it went. Most of the words are gone. The ones that went deepest are the ones that stayed, which is the whole history of salt and a fair part of mine. The tide-name is still here because I pressed it with the point of a nail instead of a pen, and the rain had nothing to take. A thing written deep enough is a thing the rain cannot call a contract.

I have kept the leaf six tendays in a dry tin. I would not line the tin with glass; glass sweats in this weather and would have it away in a month. There is no word in Vhaleni for a person you write to without wanting something. There is a word for a person whose account is current, and I have used it of you in my head for nine years, and never once charged you.

# Stuck to the leaf

A scrap of an older Council decree has stuck fast to the back of the sheet, older than the tin and older than the woman who gave it to me. Only one word of it survives: *earning*. The rest has gone the way the Council's laws go, quietly and in good time and with everybody paid.

# At the back, in a hand that is nobody's

The ebb takes and does not keep. I have tried to learn that from the water and have not managed it.
