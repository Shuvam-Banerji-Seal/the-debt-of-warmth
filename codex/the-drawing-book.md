I keep the tally for the Sill district in the factor's house at Greybank. The officer makes the draw and gives me the figure; I set it down. The figures go in Ledgerhand, without verbs, because a figure with a verb in it can be argued with and one without cannot. The remarks are the officer's own, in the hand that held the tube, and I have not changed a word of them. The College would take the verbs out of those as well if it knew they were there. The factor allows them. He says a remark is not part of the account, which is his way of saying that a thing may be written down so long as it is never entered.

The grey at the head of the ember column came off on the officer's fingers. He laid them on the page while he gave me the number and did not know he had marked it. I have not rubbed it out. It is the only mark on the leaf that I did not set here of purpose, and it is the one the factor's clerk asked me about.

# The column of embers

An ember is said to be worth five marks and a newborn vessel holds twelve to fifteen. A labourer in good case carries forty to sixty and does not know it. The book does not ask what the walls are like; it asks only what went through them and where it was put. I have set the figures down as they were given. I have not added them, because adding is a thing the College does in the Hearthseat and not a thing a tally-clerk does at Greybank, and because the last time I added a column I got a number that would not agree with the printed one and the factor told me to copy the printed one again.

The officer gave me thirteen for the child on Stone 12. The factor's clerk struck it and set twelve above in his own narrow hand, saying a vessel three days returned cannot hold thirteen after the draw. Both figures stand. I have asked which is right and neither of them has told me, and I have stopped asking, because a clerk who asks after a correction is a clerk who will soon be correcting next.

# The remarks

I struck out one of my own words on this leaf. Where the officer said *the warmth was drawn*, I had written *taken*, and he looked at the word and said a draw is not a taking when there is a slate. I set *drawn* above and left *taken* standing under it, which is the only honest thing on the page. A taking and a draw are the same heat going the same way; the slate is the difference, and the slate is a piece of stone with a neat hand on it.

The remarks are the only place in this book where a person appears, and even there they appear as a pale round place on an arm, a line at the gum, a word not entered, a wrist held until consent. The officer writes a good remark. He does not explain himself in them and does not plead. *The neighbours called it merciful.* That is all he set down for the man of Mud End, and it is more than the ordinance would allow and less than a man would say.

# What I did not enter

The streak of slip in the gutter came off the forearms of a thrower who came in from Sill while I was copying the Stone 20 figures. She stood at the counter with her hands held away from her coat and waited for the figure, and the grey came off on the edge of the quire when she set her slate beside it. The green bead in the lower corner is not slip. It came from a girl who leaned over to see the writing with sweetness still at her mouth, and touched the paper and said nothing. I have left it where she put it. The drag of Ash along the foot was there when I opened the tin; it is not mine and not the factor's clerk's. I have not swept it. On the Last Day they sweep the corners of the houses and burn what they find there in the morning and call it dealt with. There is no Last Day for a tally-book.

One in twelve is printed on the notice and I have copied it eight times in these two tends. I have not checked it against the roll, and the officer has not asked me to. The figure goes into the book as it comes off the notice and out of the book into the Weighing, and a district hears it read in a clean voice and goes home knowing what it owes without having been told what it paid.

# At the foot

The factor's clerk has written a line here that is not mine and is not in Ledgerhand:

> Nothing is lost that we have kept.

I have not struck it out. It is not a figure and has no business on a tally; but it is in the book now, and when I copy the leaf on Ilmen 1 I shall copy it again, in his hand and not my own, as it stands. The tin goes back on the shelf between the salt scales and the damp copies from the coast. The grey thumb will come off on the next leaf. I shall not rub that out either.
