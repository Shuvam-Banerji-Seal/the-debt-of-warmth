I am a hearth-maid of Low Kiln and nobody asked me to write this. I write it because the women said it after him and I did not want it to go out of the room with their breath. He was a priest of the Temple of the Return come up from Greybank and spoke in the clean voice they use at the Weighing, the one that takes the speaker neatly out of his own sentences; I have heard that voice announce a whole return for a person not in the room. He stood at the niche with the slates under his hand and did not once look up at the crossed mark cut above it. I set his words down as he said them, with his verbs left out where he left them out, and I have put the verbs back underneath in my own hand, because a thing said over a child is a thing a mother has to use. What the women answered is set beside. A sermon is not a thing a room swallows whole.

The tallow thumb at the head is mine; I held the slate down in the draught under the niche and did not know I had marked it. The green is not slip. The grey came off my cuff when I measured the ridge in a house two doors down and came back to write, and I have not swept it off the leaf.

# The sermon, as he said it

## Of the vessel

Mothers, and you that carry what you have not yet held, and you children that cannot yet say the word I am saying: hear the first thing you were taught before you could hear it.

The world was a vessel. It was fired whole in the Hearth and cracked in one anger, and the crack has a name, which is the First Breaking. What fell out of it went a long way and is going still. Do not ask where it went. The Waste is not a country a household can walk into and come back from with the same number of years.

The Nine Who Kept went after what had run away. They drove the repairs and left us the Return. By the Return, what is banked is not lost. The measure is banked. The account stands. Your return is recorded.

> *The room answered, and the children said it twice, once for the moot and once for them that came late:*
>
> Come back warm. Come back as you can.

## Of the flower

Some of you have had a child come among you with a red branch across the ribs or down the arm. The district calls it a birth-flower. It is not a sickness and it is not a debt. It is the mark the Hearth sets on a vessel before the vessel has a name, so that the Keeping may know its own when it comes back. Do not wash it off. Do not cover it with a word. A covered thing is still entered.

Concerning the mark cut above this niche, I am not empowered to speak. It is older than this niche and older than the slate in my hand. Ask me when the niche was built and I will give you a date. Ask me what the mark signifies and you will be given the same date, because a date is an answer that does not spoil in the mouth.

> *The room answered:*
>
> Come back warm. Come back as you can.

## Of the figure

One part in twelve of every measure is kept back for the fire, as the notice says. This is not a tax. A tax is taken by a man who wants a thing; this is kept by the Hearth, which wants nothing and therefore cannot be refused.

The tube the officer carries is cold because the fire is behind iron and no priest may touch it. Understand this plainly: the iron does not take. The slate takes. The iron only opens the way. A draw with a slate is a keeping. A draw without one is theft, and the wrist is marked so that the account may find the hand again. You will see a pale ring on a wrist in this district that shows through the winter. The neighbours call it nothing. It is the account, and the account is merciful, and it is never wrong.

> *The room answered:*
>
> Come back warm. Come back as you can.

## Of the grey

On the Last Day the grey in the corner is left where it lies. In the morning it is burnt, and it is called dealt with. The Court does not teach that the grey is a person. It teaches that the grey is what remains when the Warm has gone out, and that what cannot be kept is not a loss. Do not weep over dust. Sweep it. Burn it. Say it is dealt with, and go back to your bread.

> *The room answered:*
>
> Come back warm. Come back as you can.

## Of the Kept and the close

Blessed are they that are Kept. No want can reach them; they no longer feel the cold; there is nothing left in them for the Hearth to take, and so they are past the need of a return. There are fewer than twenty in the world. Speak of them with pity.

For you the account is still open, and the days go into it one by one, and the world is being mended. The ninth repair is not finished. Your names go into it.

The measure is banked. The account stands. Your return is recorded.

> *The room answered:*
>
> Come back warm. Come back as you can.

# What the women said after him

*Written as they said it, standing about the niche with the children sending them in the last line. I have not joined the two columns. Where he said a thing and they said a different thing, both stand.*

| As he said it | What the women said after |
|---|---|
| The world was fired whole and cracked in one anger. | A pot goes flat when it goes wrong. We do not ask who was angry. |
| Nine hands went after what had run away; the ninth is still abroad. | The ninth has a place under the rack where nobody sets a pot. The yard goes around it. |
| The birth-flower is the Hearth's mark set before the naming. | It is a red branch. A woman crossed two fingers at the corner of it and gave a date nobody asked for. I have set the date in the margin. |
| One in twelve, as the notice says, for the keeping of the fire. | One in twelve is printed and unchallenged. The other is not printed. Nobody in this room needs me to write it. |
| The iron does not take. The slate takes. | The pale ring shows through the winter and the neighbours call it weather. A slate is a piece of stone with a neat hand on it. |
| What the Court has kept is not lost. | The grey in the corner is not dirt. It is somebody. In the room two doors down it turns the corner without a break. |
| They that are Kept are past want and to be pitied. | A woman asked one of them whether he was cold. He said no. She did not ask him anything else. |
| Say the name. Your return is recorded. | Say it twice, once for the moot and once for them that came late. If you have no name, leave the place where the name would stand. |
| Sweep it, burn it, call it dealt with. | We leave it. It is swept on the Last Day and burned in the morning, and the clean line where it stood is not the same as gone. |

# What stands in the margins

At *the mark the Hearth sets on a vessel*, a girl of the drying racks asked him outright what the mark above the niche was. He gave her a date: Y.H. 782, the year the maids began to be paid. He gave her the same date a second time when she asked again in a different way. My mother used to say a date is safe; the Court likes a date and does not ask what it covers. I have put his date in the margin and left the question where he left it, which is standing. The mark is cut into the stone above the niche and drawn in the yard chart, and the chart does not explain it, and neither does this leaf.

Where he said *a draw with a slate is a keeping*, the narrow hand of a clerk borrowed the leaf on an Account-day and set *a keeping* out above and wrote *a taking, lawfully entered* over it. Under that, in mine: *the neighbours call it weather.* Both stand. He has a word for the heat going and a word for the heat going with a slate, and the same heat goes both ways.

The narrow hand also struck the verbs out of *the world was fired whole* and wrote above it *the world is a vessel, fired whole*, saying that a thing always true has no need of a verb. I have put *was* back underneath. A priest can speak without verbs because he is never in the room he is speaking about; a woman cannot.

In the space where the fifth line of the answer should be, the same hand put a round nought and wrote *vacancy* beside it. We said four and four and the place where the fifth should be, and I have left his nought standing in the place and my words under it. He numbered the Five as four on that visit also. I did not correct him. The five are not ours to give away.

# The barley grease and the bead

The crust was laid on the leaf by a woman who was telling her little one to eat and would not, and lay there while the priest spoke of bread. I left it. A crust put down and not eaten is a thing this district knows how to read. The green bead at the top right is not slip: a girl with sweetness still at her mouth leaned over to see what I was writing, touched the slate with her thumb, and went back to the rack. I have not washed any of it, and I would not if I knew how.

After he went, the women laid their birth-slates back in the niche overnight as they do, and the children were sent home before the fire was put out. The priest's words went up the road toward Greybank in a clean coat. Our words stayed in the room, which is why I have written them down.

# At the foot

In a hand that is not mine, not the clerk's and not the priest's, struck through once and written again underneath, very small:

> The mark was here before the niche. The niche was here before the name.

I have asked four women whose it was and had four dates and no hand. I have left it struck through and standing, which is the only honest way to keep a sentence you cannot sign.

The cloth goes back in the fold. They will bring the slates again on the next Long Night and set them in the niche, and another reader may come up from Greybank and say the same four sentences over them, and the women will answer him with the place where the figure is not. I shall put the verbs back as I did this time. I have copied the leaf once into Ilmen and shall copy it again, with the nought in its place and the grey along the gutter, and the space at the end of the answer left the length it is.
