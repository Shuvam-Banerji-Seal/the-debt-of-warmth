I dip the second net at the pans outside Vhal and I have read the draw for eleven years without once reading it the way the Council prints it. The Council prints a figure. The pan gives back a level. Both are taken from the same water and neither puts anything back, but only one of them is called a virtue when the rain comes.

Our tide-words are not written. They are carried in the mouth and knotted into the head-rope, so that a word which will not go into Grathic is still there after the rope has been wet a hundred times. I learned my letters off a factor's man come up the Salt Road with a tally he could not read back. I write so that a girl of the Quay may one day know what her grandmother said to a net, which is a thing no Council clerk has ever had cause to know.

Nothing in this leaf was asked for. A woman of the boats stood at the pan with salt dry white on her wrists and asked what we said before the net went in, and I heard myself answer in my mother's voice. The leaf is the back of a Council tide-tally left out in the rain; the figures have gone where Council figures go, which is nowhere a man can put his hand on, and what remains is paper enough for a prayer. The Council clerk has been at it since. The damp has eaten most of what he wrote, which the Council calls a virtue. I have left what survives of his hand standing above mine, as the pan keeps the salt after the water has gone.

# The manner of it, written small at the head

- Say it at the turn of the flood, before the net goes in. A prayer said on the ebb goes out with the thing it prays about.
- Stand with the sea in front and the sun behind you. Do not look at the water during the second drawing. A man who looks at the water while he names the Nine will come away with a number instead of a name.
- Say it to the net, never to the sea. Said to the sea, the third line becomes a question, and the sea answers with a wave, which is an answer no clerk can enter and no dipper can repeat.
- Count the knots on the head-rope, not the meshes. A mesh counted is a mesh entered, and a mesh entered is a net that has been bought.
- If the quay-crier calls the turn, stop where you are and begin again after. He is counting something that is not in your net.
- If it rains, say only the first two drawings. Words on salt-paper go soft in the wet, which is a virtue in this country and a folly in every other.
- The answer is said by every dipper present, including the one up to his knees in the pan who cannot be heard from the bank. We do not correct him. He is reading a different water.

# The five drawings

*I say. The dippers answer to the net. The water takes no part and is not asked to.*

## The first drawing, of the going out

> The Sallow runs against the wind and follows the Warm, not the moon.
> The world was a vessel set down whole, and answered full when it was tapped.
> It was cracked in one anger and the heat went out where no wall held it,
> and ran into the country that keeps nothing.
> That country has a name. Say it once, into the net.
> Do not go looking for it in the water. It is not in the water.

*Answer:* Out it goes. Back it comes. It is not the same water.

## The second drawing, of the Nine

> Nine hands went after what had run away.
> They drove the repairs home the way a boatwright drives a treenail,
> and the ninth repair is out in the deep water and is not finished.
> Name them by their number. No dipper here has seen their houses.
> One went. One is still abroad. The net cannot tell which.

*Answer:* Out it goes. Back it comes. It is not the same water.

## The third drawing, of the draw

> The draw runs seaward. It is blessed at dawn and taxed at noon.
> One in twelve, as the notice says, printed, read twice,
> once for the Council and once for those who came in on the tide too late.
> The Court books an ember at five marks. No boat in this port has traded one.
> Count embers, not tides. The slate does not care which way the water ran.
> There is a shape of salt and cold water that takes a boat's store
> and gives no figure. We call it the salt-thing. It is not the draw
> and it is not the Return. Do not pray to it. It answers.

*Answer:* Out it goes. Back it comes. It is not the same water.

## The fourth drawing, of the pouring

> We do not buy returns in this country and we do not house the returned.
> What comes back here comes back to the water and not to a cradle.
> Elsewhere one measure into one vessel brings the name back with it,
> with the handle of the cup and the price of bread.
> Many into many brings a stranger who goes toward a cup
> before the cup has been shown, and wants a table he never sat at.
> Both are poured in the south. Neither is poured at this pan.
> A man with no account costs less, they say at the Council table.
> We say it to the net and do not ask who paid the difference.

*Answer:* Out it goes. Back it comes. It is not the same water.

## The fifth drawing, of the debt

> A debt written on salt-paper is a debt the rain may make nothing.
> This is called a virtue. I have seen a man weep at a dry contract.
> The sea keeps what it takes. It has no slate and no clerk
> and does not call what it took a kindness.
> Do not ask the net what it cost. The net has a hole in it
> and has never once been known to stop the water.
> Ask the pan, which has kept the salt and let the water go,
> and cannot tell you whose salt it keeps.

*Answer:* Out it goes. Back it comes. It is not the same water.

Here the net comes up. The dippers say the last answer to the head-rope and not to the water:

> Out it goes. Back it comes. We do not ask it to be the same.

# What the Council clerk did

He came down to the pans with ink in two fingers of a cuff gone soft from washing, and wrote on salt-paper while the paper was still dry enough to take a hand. The rain has taken most of it. What is left stands above my words and I have not struck it away.

- At the third drawing, against *the draw runs seaward*, he wrote *the measure is transferred by the Keeping; the deduction arises*. The rain has eaten *arises* and left *the deduction*. It stands there now, which is more than the figure deserved.
- At *no boat in this port has traded one*, he set *the ember is not a circulating medium*, which is true in his room and has never been true at this quay. Both stand.
- At the fourth drawing, against *we do not house the returned*, he wrote *no return entered in this jurisdiction*. The damp has taken *in this jurisdiction*, so that all that remains is *no return entered*, which is the sentence the Council would like to be true everywhere.
- Against the refrain he wrote *evaporation and precipitation are not the same mass*, and underlined it twice. The rain has left the underline and taken the words. A line with nothing under it is the honest part of this leaf.
- In the space after the fifth drawing, where there is no sixth, he put a round nought and wrote *vacancy*. A nought is a figure and can go soft in the rain and be copied again in a dry hand. The space is not a figure. It is the time the net is out of the water.

He asked what a dipper learned by the prayer. I told him the order and the knots. He entered both and went up the Salt Road. A man who cannot help writing on a thing has let it be seen that he wrote.

# What the boat-woman put in the margin

The charcoal is thick and leans, made with a stick and not a pen. She came down from the Quay with a boat-share in her family and salt in the creases of her wrists, and stood until the net was up before she touched the leaf, because a woman who touches a paper in the middle of a quay will be asked what she had to do with it.

At the fourth drawing: *A child came back upriver, thin, and went toward a cup before the cup was shown. No Council mark paid for him and no Council house keeps him. His mother kept him anyway. I said the refrain to her and she said it back to her pot.*

At *a man with no account costs less*: *My mother held the share and I hold it. My brother came aboard and his word is counted at half. The sea does not count halves. Neither does the woman who landed him.*

At the space after the fifth: *This is the time the net is down. I have sat it out on the gunwale. Nobody enters the sitting.*

She would not say which child, and I have not asked twice.

# At the head and in the folds

The white at the head is salt out of the paper itself and not salt off the sea, which is a thing only a person who has kept salt-paper knows the difference of. The tar is off the oar I held down while I wrote, and went into the grain and will not wash out. The brine ring stood from the first drawing to the fifth. The nick at the corner came off a fish-hook set down where no hook has business lying, and the man did not apologize. The green bead was pressed in by a girl of the furnaces with the sweetness still at her mouth; she said nothing and I did not ask, for a bead from the Glaze Cliffs is a thing a dipper can carry and a thing a glass woman must account for. The third word I have not written back, because a drawing with a word gone is a drawing said slowly, and all of ours are said slowly. The three knots are mine and I will not untie them; they hold what will not go into Grathic. The grey print along the foot is not mine and not the Council's. There is no Last Day in a tin, and nothing here is swept up and burnt in the morning and called dealt with.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

> The tide keeps the count. The rain keeps the debt.
> The sea keeps neither and is not called unjust.

Struck once and written again underneath, very small:

> It is not called just, either.

I asked the clerk, the boat-woman and a man come up from the pulled benches who had read the turn from the water instead of the crier, and had three answers and no hand. I have left the struck line where it was struck. The leaf goes back into the tin and the tin among the boat's things. At the turn of the flood tomorrow the second net will go in and I shall say the five drawings to the head-rope, and the clerk's figure will stand soft above mine, and a woman will come down from the Quay with salt on her wrists and sit out the space where the sixth drawing is not.
