I set the worth of a vessel that comes back. The room is off the Weighing Hall in Slate Row of the Hearthseat, and I have kept the figures in it for eleven years. A man dies in a district and a collector draws him and a factor enters the figure; years later the same man dies in another district and another slate comes under my counter with a different number written on it. I set the number the slate gives and, underneath it, the number the last slate gave. I have never added the two. Adding is a College matter, and the last hand in this room that added a column made a total which would not agree with the notice, and the factor at Greybank sent word that the notice was to be copied again in a better hand.

The brown thumb at the head is iron-gall and mine, made by holding a quire open through a Weighing morning. Clerks in this building can tell an assessor by his thumb before he has spoken, which is one of the small mercies of the trade: a man is known by what he has handled.

Twelve copper bits to a silver mark. Twenty silver marks to a gold crown, a Sere coin that comes north with a factor and goes south when he does. The Court books an ember at five marks and I have never once seen a man exchange the one for the other, though I have entered both figures in the same hour. One in twelve is printed on the notice and read in a clean voice at the Weighing. A whole return is one measure poured into one vessel and booked at the figure. A thin return is many measures pooled and divided among cradles by lot, and the figure is entered afterwards. There is no third kind on the notice, and there are three kinds in this book, the third being the kind that comes back to be counted again.

> *In the narrow upright hand of a man come down from the College:* Valuations entered in the ordinary way. *Underneath, in mine:* There is nothing ordinary about a figure I have written twice for the same person with a different hand holding the slate each time. I have put the person back in.

Draws behind this leaf: seven. Figures struck: none in my hand. Columns struck: one. Words scraped away: one.

# The figure that goes down

A remark is not part of the account. The College has never thought to strike the remark column out of this book, which I take for an oversight and not a mercy, and so I keep what I please in it.

The clay-bound hand came to me three times. The first figure stood at fifty-one, the second at thirty-eight, the third at twenty-nine, and the deduction stood at one in twelve between them like a stone that has not moved while the water went down. I have set the three numbers one under another and joined neither. I do not say what it means. An assessor who explains a figure has begun to write a sermon, and sermons get copied.

On the man of Mud End I wrote the word *stair* first as a cause and then scraped it away, which is the bare square in the lower margin, and wrote it again as a likeness, which is all it is. Four figures from one man, each lower than the last, and each time a collector came in off the clay road with grey at the head of the slate and gave me the number before he gave me the name. There are eleven words for a deduction taught in the College and not one for a stair, which is the whole of the difficulty between us.

The glaze-room girl asked after the taste. She had had it once and had come back with a mouth that knew the shape of it and no word to put to it. I entered the figure and not the question, because a question has no column and a woman who asks one at a counter will be told the figure the notice says and go away with it. She set her cup down on the corner of the counter and did not take it up again, and I have not moved it. There is a ring where it stood on an earlier leaf and I have kept the leaf.

The woman of the Weir came back wanting the river. I have put that in the remark because she said it in my hearing and there is nowhere else to put it. A banker would call it a likeness and a physician would call it a thing to be entered in a column the family is not shown. I have called it wanting. I am aware that this is not a figure and cannot be added, which is why it stays where a clerk carrying the leaf will have to read it.

# The likeness column struck by the College

Beside *Return due* I had kept a place with no heading, in which I set down what the vessel did when the figure was read aloud. The girl of the glaze room turned her mouth toward the cup before the cup was shown. The woman of the Weir turned toward the door that leads down to the river road. The child who would not warm reached toward the lamp. The man of Mud End said nothing and put his hand into his sleeve, where a number had once been, and brought it out empty.

The man from the College borrowed the leaf on the morning after the Weighing and struck the column from the first row to the last, and wrote *No such column* above it in his narrow upright hand. I have drawn the column back underneath and left his words standing over it. He had to look at every row before he could write that there were none worth entering, and I watched him do it, standing as he stands, with his cuff gone soft from washing and the ink settled in two fingers of it. A man does not count what he has not looked at.

I have put no heading over the column still. A heading would turn a likeness into an entry and an entry into something a clerk may be called to answer for, and this house has answered for enough this year.

# What the two borrowed hands did

The College man struck the verb out of *the figure went down* and set *the figure was reduced* above it, and above that *the valuation was adjusted*, which is further from the man who died than the first. I have put my words back underneath both. A sentence with nobody in it is a sentence no woman can ask about, and women ask about these figures in the end, always at the counter, always after the slate is shut.

Against the clay-bound hand he struck *with memory* and set *whole* above it, which is the word the notice prints and the word a house upriver will pay twelve hundred marks to hear. Both stand. I have not chosen between them and would thank any man who told me I had to.

The factor's clerk did one thing. At the foot of the leaf, where I had written *figure smaller than the last two*, he wrote *figure corrected upward* and underlined it twice. The figure was not corrected upward. It was made smaller twice, by two collectors who had never met, in two districts that do not share a slate. I have left his line where he put it and mine where I put it. Neither is a lie in the language of its own room, and the man is not in either of them.

# The song they carry up the passage

*Sung low going up the passage from the Weighing Hall to the rooms beside it, never at the reading and never twice in one day. The fourth stroke is the one they hold; I have set the words as they came.*

> Four for the fire behind the iron,
> four for the nails in this Hall;
> a place where the fifth should stand
> and nobody crosses himself.
>
> The figure goes down, the figure goes down,
> the deduction does not go down;
> they read us the sum in a clean voice
> and we carry the rest in the cold.
>
> One slate at the first and one at the third,
> one at the going-out of the breath;
> the hand that wrote the figure is grey
> and the figure is entered as death.
>
> Hold the fourth. Let the fifth stand where it is.
> What comes back is not what went,
> and comes back as it can.

A clerk come up from Sill told me the bell there goes four, four and a gap and that nobody crosses himself. I have kept his telling beside the song and not made it a hearing. A man of the College put a nought in the fifth place and wrote *vacancy* under it. A nought is a figure and can be carried out in a cuff. The fifth is a place and stays.

# At the head and in the gutter

The brown at the head is iron-gall and mine. The bone-pale streak down the gutter is slip off the forearms of the glaze-room girl, who held her slate away from her coat while she waited and set it for a moment against this quire; she did not know she had marked it and I have not told her. The tallow print at the foot came off the lamp I keep turned low so that a woman at the counter will not change her tale for me when I write down what she asked.

The grey in the outer fold came off the sleeve of the woman of the Weir as she leaned to see the columns. It is not mine and it did not come off my cuff, and I have not swept it. They sweep the corners of houses on the Last Day and burn what they find in the morning and call it dealt with. There is no Last Day in an assessor's room. The rolls of the dead are carried forward here and never closed, which is a kind of mercy in a thing nobody finishes.

The pale round ring in the third leaf was made by a collector setting his cold tube down while he reached for a figure out of his cuff. The paper is thinner inside it. The tube had been used an hour before and was not used again that day, and the ring is not a wound, but it is the only mark on this leaf that looks like one. The scorched corner caught a spark off the grating while I held the leaf up to the light. The green bead is lead glaze from a girl who leaned over to see the figures with the sweetness still at her mouth and touched the paper and said nothing. The small bare square is where I scraped the word away and put no other in its place. I have not written it back and have not forgotten it.

# At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

> The bank keeps the figure that went in. This book keeps the figures that came back.
> The space between them has no column and no hand.

I asked the College man, a factor's clerk come up from Greybank, and a woman who stood at the counter about a daughter of hers, and had three answers and no hand. I have left the lines where they were written.

The leaf goes back under the counter. Tomorrow a vessel will come back a fourth time and a collector will come in off the clay road with grey at the head of his slate, and I shall set the new figure above the old ones and put the verbs back where the College struck them out, and leave the likeness column standing where it was drawn. Somewhere in a district a child will turn toward a cup before a cup has been shown to him, and no figure in this book will tell his mother why. The rolls stay open. The book stays in the cover. That is the whole of it, and it goes on.
