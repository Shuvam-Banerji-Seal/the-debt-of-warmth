I keep the firing at the third kiln below the sill, and I have put this song into a tile before it went into the throat, so that a man with a pen could not take it out afterwards. That is the whole reason for the tile. A song written on paper belongs to whoever holds the paper; a song burnt into clay belongs to the kiln that fired it, and a clerk may copy it if he likes, but his copy is not the song.

We say it to the fire. Said to a woman it becomes an argument, and there is always a woman who will argue. Two keep the watch through a firing, one at the coals and one at the damper, and they take the lines turn about, the one at the damper saying the short lines because her hands are not free. Every fourth line is a rest. The rest is the fifth breath and it is not said aloud. A woman who says the fifth aloud has not kept a firing yet and will be told so, kindly, in the morning.

The tile was soft when I pricked it. Dessa set her thumb on the head to hold it and did not know she had marked it, which is how the slip thumb came there; the green in the corner was a bead off a girl’s lip who leaned over to see the writing, and it melted in the firing and stuck fast and is sweet if you put your tongue to it, which I do not advise. The crack around the word went there in the cooling. I did not mend it. A word a crack has gone around is a word that will not be lost in a straight line, which is more than can be said for most things in this yard.

The song, as it is kept at the damper

The banking

Bank it blind and leave it low,

the coal keeps what the flame will spend;

turn it with the back of a broken pot,

and don’t ask who paid to mend it.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

the pit is cold before the sun,

but the cold comes up when you tread, and not before.

The feeding

Feed it on the hour and not the eye,

the fire keeps its own account of the day;

a mouth that opens on the quarter goes shut on the half,

and the man who counts by his belly learns.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

the gull goes off with a coal not cold,

and there’s one warmth less in the shed and none in the slate.

The damper

The damper bites and the damper holds,

the one iron keeps and the one iron takes;

put your finger to it, feel it take the cold,

and be thankful for the hand it leaves you.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

a room shut tight with the damper down

is a room the breath gets out of, not the war.

The sweetness

Up the terrace the glaze breathes sweet,

sweet enough to make a woman still;

a pale line at the gum is the first of it,

and she sings on with the green at her mouth.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

the sweet is the sickness and the sickness is dear,

and the woman who tastes it has not stopped tasting it.

The watch

Watch it, girl, and do not sleep,

the breath that kills comes soft as a mother’s hand;

four and four and a gap in the throat,

and the gap is where it comes.

The river keeps a note under the stone

and the town sets its clock by it and says nothing;

the kiln keeps a count under the floor,

and no man has made the two agree.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

the river’s note comes through the door,

the fire’s count comes up through the boards,

and they were never the same sound.

The drawing

Let it cool. Let the pot speak first.

A whole one answers full under the knuckle;

a cracked one gives a little flat knock

and will not be told what it should have said.

Leave the crack showing. Let the glaze go over

the good part and the bad part alike;

a repair is a scar with a pot around it,

and the pot is the one that gets carried.

Four for the coals and four for the clay,

and one held back where the fifth should lie;

the fire has done what the fire was asked,

and the fifth was the breath it did not take.

I have set the lines as we keep them, with the rests standing where the rests stand. The fourth line of each part is not a line you hurry. A woman who hurries it comes up with a burned sleeve and a pot gone wrong, and the pot is the one that gets carried, as the song says, though nobody carries the sleeve.

The verse they do not sing while a man from Slate Row stands at the gate

There is one more part. They take it up when the factor’s man has gone back over the north bridge with his satchel under his arm, and break off if he turns. I have it from the woman at the coals, with the space left before it, which is the space they leave in the singing:

One in twelve as the notice says,

three of the four and one to the dish;

but the number goes from a fist to a mouth

and arrives at a shoulder and nobody’s ear.

The barrow goes out of the north gate

with the grey heaped a little above the sides,

and nobody in the square looks at it twice,

which is the afternoon’s work and has always been.

The tube comes cold and the hand comes grey,

and the mouth that reads is not the mouth that paid;

what goes into the pot is not what comes out,

and the account stands, and the bowl’s about.

Four and four and a place where the fifth should be;

the fire keeps the count and the yard keeps the rest,

and the eels run whether the priest has blessed them or not.

I asked her what the last line was worth. She said a song was not obliged to be worth a figure because a clerk had written it down, and then she put her coal thumb on the corner of the tile to keep it from the draught, as she had done when it was soft. I have left the space before the verse where they left it. It is the same space the kiln keeps.

What the Court hand did

The clerk of the factor borrowed the copy on a morning when the weigh-slips were out drying. He writes with the cuff turned back twice and the ink on the first joint of two fingers and will not come out, the way a man writes who is paid by the line.

Where I pricked the coal keeps what the flame will spend, he set above it, the measure is retained and the residue expended. Against a room shut tight with the damper down is a room the breath gets out of, not the war, he wrote decease by asphyxia; not attributable to hostilities. At the count he put a round nought in the fifth place and wrote vacancy in the margin, and at the foot of his copy he entered eight, fifth nil. Account stands.

I have let his hand stand above the copy and put our words back underneath, as the fire keeps above and below in one kiln. A nought is a number. The fifth is a place where a woman lets the rope go and the fire draws breath. He has put a figure where a rest should be, which is the whole art of that hand. He could strike at the paper because the paper was his; he could not strike at the tile, which had gone through the throat with the words in it and come out harder than his pen. When he had done, Dessa looked at his vacancy and asked whether a vacancy kept a pot warm, and he told her that a vacancy was not required to keep anything, and went off over the bridge. I have set her question here because it was asked and nobody answered it that was able.

In the margin, in the hand of a man with two fingers gone

There is no word in Grathic for the iron that keeps and the iron that takes. We say damper and show the hand.

He wrote it of the third kiln and would not sign it, being a man who has kept his fingers in the damper since Y.H. 799 and has not got the two back. I have left it where he set it. It is true of every damper in the yard and of one or two other things the yard does not name.

At the head and in the gutter

The slip thumb at the head is Dessa’s; she holds the tile down with the hand she treads with and does not wash before the firing. The coal-black in the gutter came off the tile’s own corner where it lay nearest the coals and worked into the cuts of the letters, so that eighth reads dark and fourth has a notch gone that I have not written in again. The green bead melted into the corner in the throat and is part of the tile now and cannot be picked off; it is sweet and it is lead, and I have not put my tongue to it since the day it stuck and have told the girls not to either, which is a thing the glaze room does not tell them. The burn-pit is where a coal fell off the damper in the second firing and sat until it had eaten a small round place; a woman’s hand fits it exactly, which is how the watchers warm theirs. The grey along the base came off the ridge in the kiln-house where it turns the corner and has not been swept since a man died uncollected in the Long Cold; I touched it with the tile’s edge and it came away, and I have left it there. The crack around the word fifth went there in the cooling and is not a break through. When the next firing comes I shall set the tile back in its niche and let the watchers warm their hands on it, and the word will still be whole inside its crack, and the clerk’s nought will still be where he put it, on the paper.

At the back, in a hand nobody will own

A pot is a breath the fire agreed to hold.

I asked the woman at the coals, the woman at the damper and a carter waiting under the sill, and had three answers and no hand. I have left the line where it was pricked. The tile goes back into its niche tomorrow and the firing goes on, and the watchers will take the short lines turn about and leave the fifth where it was left, and the river will go on keeping its own note under the stone and the kiln its own count under the floor, and nobody will make them one.
