I keep the wine-list at the press in the Ember house and nobody asked me for this. I am north-born and write in charcoal; the house writes in thin gold and would not set a bond-woman’s hand among its contracts. I write on the back of a leaf spoiled at the third hour, because a spoiled paper is a paper nobody will borrow for a column. He spoke to the fire, then to the four bowls, then to the girl whose name-day it was, and last to the company.

The gold at the head came off the clerk’s cuff; the yellow in the fold is the dust of the burning winds and I have not shaken it out. The wine-ring is the third bowl. The myrrh came off a burial cloth whose family bought back the ash and is called generous here. The green shard is from a bowl of my own country, cracked in the first heat after I was brought south.

The manner of the shrine, as the house keeps it

Written small, from watching, not from any order the Council of Bowls has printed.

  • The fire is carried in a clay bowl and set in the wall-niche before the first voice, fed with oil and a little myrrh. No priest touches it; the keeper of the fire does, and she has not been through the door where the numbers are read.
  • Four bowls stand with their lips to the fire. Three go round. The fourth stands where it was set and is carried away full. Do not look into it, and do not ask whose debt it holds. Ask the bowl.
  • Say the sayings to the fire or to the bowl. Said looking at a person, a saying becomes a lawsuit.
  • Open the vowels and throw the ends of sentences into the fire; the listener supplies the last of the thought. A northerner who says the whole of a sentence has said too much.
  • Clap at the debt and at the new writing, softly at the old slate when it burns. A guest who claps only at the burning has not understood.
  • The Sun-Emperor’s act is announced at the third hour in a court three streets away. Stop until it has passed. Begin again after.
  • A slave is given a clay-name by the house and the old name is forbidden. In some houses it is written inside a bowl which the slave is never allowed to look into.

He said the last twice, once to the fire and once to a woman at the lower table given three copper bits for standing. I have not joined the two sayings.

The sermon, as he said it

Of the fire

He put his hand above the clay bowl and warmed it without touching. His cuffs were clean and his fingers full.

“A vessel was made to pour,” he said. “The cup that keeps is the cup that sours. A life hoarded is a life wasted. Say it over the wine. Do not say it over the accounts, where it would be a very different sentence and might cost somebody a province.”

This fire is fed with oil, and the oil was bought, and the myrrh was bought, and somebody chose to spend it on a flame out before the wine is cold. He spoke in the open voice of the courts, with the ends thrown away; the room leaned after him. I wrote the endings in where I heard them, which a Sere clerk would not do.

Margin, in charcoal, mine: He had three rings on the hand he warmed. He said a life hoarded while a woman at the lower table held a bowl she was not permitted to empty. Both stood in the room. I have not put one over the other.

Of the four bowls

He turned the first bowl a quarter, the way a potter shows a buyer the foot of a cup.

“This one is for the house,” he said. “The debt is named and the bowl goes round. The second is for the fire. The third is for the slate. They go round and are poured, and nobody is the poorer, because what is poured was going to be spent.”

He came to the fourth and stopped. It stood full, with the light going along the rim.

“This one is nobody’s,” he said. “It is set out at every feast and carried away full. Nobody pours it and nobody says whose debt it holds. If the house knew, it would pour it. It does not know, which is a mercy of the Spending and not a failure of the house.”

A girl asked what was in it. He turned his head toward the fire. “Ask the bowl,” he said. She asked the bowl; the bowl answered as bowls answer. I have set the fourth in the table below where a figure should stand. The clerk has set a figure beside it. The two have not touched.

Of the name

The girl whose name-day it was stood at the end of the row in a dress gone stiff with gold thread. Seven names had been given her and the house kept one.

“A name is a bowl,” the priest said. “The more names, the more pourings; the more of a person has been used, the more generous the house has been. Do not be sorry for the six. They were spent on you.”

The girl looked at the fourth bowl and then at her mother, who could not go through the door where her numbers were read. Her mother looked at the fire. I have written the order and not what it means.

I have two names. One was given me in the north and is forbidden here, and one is clay and belongs to this house. I wrote the first inside a bowl in my first winter and have never looked into it, which is the courtesy of this country.

Of the burning

“Burn the slate by lot,” he said. “Write it again before the wine is cold.”

This year it was the household of a woman three places down from me. She wept while the slate burned, the room was kind, and then applauded the new writing. The clerk wrote above that an instrument was discharged and a fresh obligation acknowledged, a true thing in a language nobody at the press speaks.

A guest asked whether a debt had been forgiven or a province ruined.

“Yes,” the priest said.

The guest asked which he meant.

“Measure for measure,” the priest said, and went back to his wine.

The guest asked me afterward and I told him I did not read the accounts of this house. He said I was fortunate. I have thought about that word more than about the fire.

Of them that are emptied

The returned woman was brought in after the third bowl. Solene Ember was returned whole here in Y.H. 799, Y.H. 804 and Y.H. 809, and the house speaks of the fourth as a man looks forward to a harvest he has already sold. She turned a cup a quarter, set it back and said nothing, and the company applauded her.

“A beautiful return is proof of a generous house,” the priest said. “A life poured out gloriously comes back beautiful. A life kept in a corner comes back thin, and thin returns are for the north, who buy them.”

A thin northerner stood at the lower table with three copper bits under his palm. I kept my hand on my wine-list and did not look at him.

“If there is a generosity in this world,” the priest said, “it is in the pouring, and the pouring does not stop to ask who brought the wine.” He said it to the fire. I have set it down as it was said and not as it was meant.

Of the door

A woman of this house may own the bowls and the slaves and her husband’s debt, but not go through the door where her numbers are read. The priest called it a courtesy with a door in it: a door is not an argument and will not fight anybody.

I have stood outside with a wine-list in my hand and heard nothing but a man reading figures in a low patient way. The wood is ordinary wood and there is a latch on the inside. On the last name-day a woman put her hand to the latch and took it away without turning it, and went back and clapped at the burning of a slate that was hers. I have not written which woman.

The pouring-song

Sung at the tread of the press, not at the feast. The fourth verse is said to the bowl and not to the company. There is a silence before it, the length of the fourth bowl standing full, and I have left it the length it is sung.

We tread the press and the wine comes warm,

a neck for the house and a neck for the fire;

the slate goes down on a name-day’s lot

and the fourth goes back to the shelf.

We clap for the debt and the slate,

we buy back the ash at the price of the grain;

the year we burned under one we loved

was a year we had meant to spend anyway.

Here the fourth bowl stands. No sound.

Three are poured and carried away;

one stands where it stood at the first of the day.

Ask it what holds it and ask it why

it goes back full when the others go dry.

A girl from the press asked what the silence was for. Nobody answered her. The clerk put a round mark against it later and at Greybank they put a nought.

The four bowls, as the wine-list keeps them

Figures that come in here in a coat go out again in a new slate. These I have set down where they stood.

Bowl — Poured for — What was said over it — What was entered

The first — the house, at the name-day — the debt named aloud — obligation acknowledged, current

The second — the fire in the niche — the act of the Sun-Emperor — act recorded, no figure

The third — the slate — burn the slate by lot; write it again — one discharged, one written

The fourth — nobody — nothing — a reserve retained, no charge against it

The slate burned — a woman three places down — she wept; the room applauded — affect observed, not entered

My mistress can set a season to every feast before this one, and I have not written the seasons, because a row of seasons is a thing a clerk can carry away and a bowl is not. The crown in the fold is one the guest gave the girl for standing, and it is the only coin here nobody has tried to enter.

What the gold hand did

The tally-clerk borrowed the leaf after the third hour, being a man who cannot leave paper alone. He writes in thin gold and takes the verbs out as though a verb were a thing a man could spill from a cuff.

  • At a vessel was made to pour: vessels are made for use, underlined twice.
  • At the cup that keeps is the cup that sours: retention produces diminution, which leaves out the souring and the cup together.
  • At she wept and the room applauded: affect observed, not entered.
  • At measure for measure: equivalence recorded, with the equivalence underlined.
  • Against the fourth bowl: a reserve retained; no charge against it, a true thing in the language of a counting room, with the bowl still standing in it.

I have left his hand above mine and my words underneath, as the new slate lies over the burned one. He takes the verbs out because a thing always true has no need of them; I put them back because there is always a hand.

What the northern hand did

A factor’s clerk at Greybank wrote on the copy in the narrow upright hand. He has never been south.

  • At the fire-shrine: private fire, no public charge.
  • At the fourth bowl: a round nought and vacancy, which is a number and is not a bowl.
  • At a life hoarded is a life wasted: doctrine, not entered. He asked whether the one in twelve of the northern notice was read in this house. I told him it was not, and he wrote nothing, which is the only honest line in his hand.

I have left his hand above both, as a new account is laid over an older one and the older is not asked to stand up.

At the head and in the gutter

The gold will not wash out of the grain: a contract is meant to last and a saying to be carried. The yellow in the fold is the burning wind, and I should rather it stayed, because a woman who shakes a leaf is pretending she has been somewhere else. The wine-ring belongs to the third hour. The myrrh is from a cloth I put my hand on because a woman there put hers there first; I have not washed the smell out. The crown between the third and fourth leaves is the only thing in this book nobody has entered, which is not the same as nobody having paid for it.

At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

Struck through once and written again underneath, very small:

Three were poured and the fourth went back full.

The room applauded the debt and the debt stayed in the room.

A bowl that keeps is called generous and a bowl that pours is called spent.

The fire takes the names and nobody asks who brought the oil.

I asked the girl from the press, the woman who keeps the fire, the clerk and a factor from Greybank, and had four answerings and no hand. I have left the line struck through and standing, the only honest way to keep a sentence you cannot sign.

The leaf goes back into the oiled silk and the silk into the clay bowl. Next name-day three bowls will go round and one will stand, and the clerk will take the verbs out and I shall put them back, and the fourth bowl will go away full as it came, and nobody in this house will ask it what it holds.
