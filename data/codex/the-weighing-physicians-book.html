I am a physician of the Weighing Hall and my name on the rolls is Senn. I have examined the Kept in their rooms at nine Weighings and held the hands of the returned on the third day. Nobody asked me for this book. A woman from the clay yards came up with a child who would not warm, stood with her thumb in tallow against the margin while I wrote, and set a sentence there I have not been able to cross out. A physician of the Court writes in verbs thinned almost to nothing; a woman with a child on her hip puts them back.

I write on the back of a Weighing notice spoiled in the damp. The figure on the other side shows through every remedy: one in twelve, as the notice says. I did not choose the paper and have not cut the figure away. The clerk who corrects this book is of my own College; he takes the verbs out of my directions as though a thing always true had no need of anybody doing it. I have put them back underneath, as the clay-yard woman put hers in the margin. There are three hands in this book and none of them is clean.

The order of the examining room

Written small at the head, with the word for the calm gone over in Ash.

  • Set the returned one’s face toward the grating, not the door. The fire behind the iron has never gone out, and a person who faces it can be seen by everybody. Margin, in tallow: A stone faces the fire too and feels nothing.
  • Ask what is booked against the house before you ask what ails the person. A household that has named its debt will tell you where the hurt is. This is not kindness. It is the order of the Hall.
  • Turn the slate face down before you look at a child. I learned it from a hearth-maid of Low Kiln who would not say why she turned it.
  • Count the hand on the third day and enter the count. A count entered is a count kept. Margin: A count kept is not a hand held.
  • Do not tap a person to hear them. A body is not a banked cup. I did it once, heard nothing, and have not again.
  • When a collector’s fingers go grey at the tube, enter it as age. The rolls want a word and will take age. Margin, pressed hard enough to score the leaf: It’s the draw. I’ve seen a collector grey in one winter, and it was not age.

She would not give me her name. A name in a margin is a name a clerk can carry out of a room.

The remedies, as they are used

Trouble — What is set to it — The manner of it — Who is debited

The beautiful return (the calm of the third day) — A whole return, booked at twelve hundred marks and forty unspent measures; the calm entered as its sign — We say the Hearth kept what was given and the person comes back quiet. On the third day the hand shakes and we call it the price of beauty. I have seen the calm go on and the name come late, and entered the calm. The margin says a woman who does not know her own child is not quiet but gone. Both stand. — The household, in marks; the returned one, in what is not asked for.

The dream of the former house — A phial of quiet ash at sixty marks, in wine at the Weighing — The rich buy it so the former life will stop arriving in dreams. I gave it to nine; six ceased to speak of the dream and three ceased to speak at all. I entered six and not the three. Margin: That’s the quiet you sold them. — The person, who asks no figure.

The greyed hand of a collector — Rest from the iron; a week near the Hearth; the hand booked as age — We say the Hearth warms what the iron took. I have seen a hand grey further in a week at the grating and written age over it. — The collector, who is paid partly out of other people’s heat.

The Kept in their rooms — Nothing. Fewer than twenty are examined at each Weighing and found whole — They do not feel the fire, do not ask for food, do not want. We call this health and pity them for it. The margin says a stone does not feel the fire either and is not called healthy. I have not crossed the line out. — Nobody. This is the row I cannot debit.

The Weighing chill (the cold after the reading) — The account read aloud again; a measure returned to the house — We say a figure read warms the house that hears it. I read it to nine houses and went back in the morning; six were as cold, three colder. Margin: A figure’s a figure. Try a stone. — The house, which hears and cannot be heated.

The fever that cooks from within — Cold iron to the temple; bleed at the elbow to let the head fall — We say a vessel overfilled must be emptied or it cooks its own contents. A physician come up from Low Kiln says bleeding lets the measure out and not the fever, and that the person is quieter after and not better. I watched four; two were quiet and did not wake. I have written both. — The person, in the arm.

The second chill in a rekindled child — Warmth, barley water, a watch to the fourth day; we walk her — It comes after the warming, not before. The physician from Low Kiln says a warmed body spends faster and walking faster still, and that she should be held and counted with. I have walked eleven and held two, and not written which fared worse. Margin: Hold the next one and see if you can write what you saw. — The household, one night’s sleep; sometimes the child.

A child who comes back wanting another house — The old name entered in the second column, which the family is not shown; a bowl of water under the bed — The returned one calls a name not given in this house and reaches for a door that is not in the room. We enter the former name where the family cannot see it. The margin says a name in a column puts no shape back. One went off it and one kept it a year. — Nobody. The house, in a year it cannot spend.

A cut from a cold iron tube at a collection — Tallow, a clean fold, the measure entered — A collection yields a small wound at the way. The clay-yard woman puts nothing on a cut but tallow; the priests here put the grey out of the public bowl on theirs. I have found grey under the skin in three such cuts and could not get it out. — The cut, in a white line it keeps.

The pale line at the gum — Sour wine; the glaze hands sent back to the mix — The sweetness answers sweetness, we say. The physician from Low Kiln says it is lead. I have seen the line go off a girl who left the room and come back on one who stayed. I have not seen the lead. — The glaze room, which cannot stop and does not ask.

Nothing in this Hall is given. It is entered and called kept. I will stand behind that sentence and behind no other in the book.

The full note, and why a person is not a cup

The bankers tap a cup before a pouring and know by the note whether it will hold. Sound answers full; cracked answers flat, a small dead sound that stops at the surface. I borrowed the trick and tried it on a returned woman gone thin after a third pouring. There was nothing there for the knuckle to find. I wrote that the flat was not in her, which is true, and have not written what else might have been. A person is not a vessel of the Court’s kind, and a Kept vessel is not a person, though both hold without leaking. In the margin, two words in tallow: Now you’re saying it.

On the beautiful return

They come back calm. The returned one sits in the good light near the grating, wants nothing, takes the cup when given, and does not ask whose child she is. We write a beautiful return and enter it at twelve hundred marks and forty unspent measures, and the household thanks the Hearth in a voice that does not rise. On the third day the hand shakes, the household asks for the phial, and we say the beauty must be paid for, which lets nobody ask what the first pouring took.

I asked the woman from the clay yards what she called it. She said her mother crossed two fingers and called it the quiet, and that a woman who has ceased to want is not well but gone. I have entered the calm and not her sentence. Her sentence is not a figure and cannot be entered, which is why it stays where the clerk cannot carry it.

A song for the Weighing

Sung low in the Hall at the reading, to the person and not to the account. There is a place after the fourth stroke where the fifth should stand, and no singer has filled it.

Four for the fire behind the iron,

four for the nails in the Hall;

a place where the fifth should stand

and nobody crosses himself.

The measure is banked, the account stands,

the hand goes grey at the tube;

a woman comes back and wants nothing,

and the house calls it a mercy.

Six for the phials in the locked drawer,

six for the rooms where the Kept sit;

a stone does not ask for the fire

and the clerk does not ask who paid.

Sing to the slate, sing to the figure,

sing to the space where the fifth should be;

what comes back is not what went,

and comes back as it can.

I have never heard the bell at the Cross. A clerk come up from Sill told me it goes four, four and a gap and that nobody crosses himself; I have kept his telling beside the song and not made it a hearing. A clerk of my College put a nought in the fifth place and wrote vacancy beneath it. A nought is a figure and can be carried out in a cuff. The fifth is a place and stays.

What the clay-yard woman put in the margin

Against the Kept: A stone does not feel the fire either. Ask it if it’s well.

Against the phial: Six stopped speaking of the dream. I’d want to know what the six are doing now.

Against the greyed hand: It’s the draw. I have seen it taken out of a man in a winter.

Against the beautiful return: A woman who doesn’t know her child isn’t quiet. She’s gone.

She presses her verbs in with the side of her thumb. The slip under her nail has left a brown print in the gutter and I have not washed it. She took the child away before the count was entered and left no name.

What the College clerk did

A clerk of the College borrowed the leaves at the Weighing, when the Hall had no time to mind a physician’s rough paper. The ink sits in two fingers of his cuff and will not come out. He struck out my column headed Who is debited and wrote No cost entered above it, which is a true thing in a dishonest place. Against the Kept he wrote The vessels are retained in health, the most beautiful sentence I have ever read in a hand that has never sat in a room with one. Where I wrote that the Hearth keeps what it is given, he wrote the measure is retained, taking the verbs out, as though a thing always true had no need of anybody doing it. He has eleven words for a deduction and no word for a hand held.

I have put my verbs back underneath his. A clerk of my College corrects me into coldness and I have let him, because a physician who writes as I do will be struck from the rolls by a man who has never held a hand.

At the head and in the gutter

The figure shows through every leaf: one in twelve, as the notice says. The damp took two words clean off it before I was given the back and I have not written them back. A figure that will not read is a figure that cannot be entered, which is the only mercy in this book nobody has paid for. The brown press in the margin is tallow and slip from a woman’s thumb. The grey in the gutter came off the skirting board of a room where a Kept vessel sits; it is the only Ash here that entered itself, and I have not burnt it on the morning after the Last Day, for there is no Last Day off the Weighing Hall. The scorch on the second leaf is a spark off the grating, caught while I wrote and put out under my hand; I have not entered the burn. The pale clip-ring on the third leaf is not mine and I have not asked whose.

At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

The slate keeps the name. The drawer keeps the phial.

The woman between them is kept in no house.

I asked the College clerk, a physician come up from Low Kiln, and a woman with a child on her hip, and had three answers and no hand. I have left the line where it was written. Tomorrow the Weighing will be read and a household will come with a hand that will not hold a cup, and I shall enter the calm as a mercy and turn the slate face down before I look at the child, because a child is not an account and a woman at the Weighing wants a hand more than she wants a correction.
