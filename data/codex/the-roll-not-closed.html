I keep the roll of the dead. It lies in a tin on this counter and it grows every tend and is never once emptied. A name goes in at the drawing, stays in at the pouring, and is still there when the house that gave it is a figure in the granary accounts and nothing else. On the Remembering I read the names in a clean voice, with the Hearth-fire behind the grating and nobody allowed to touch it. The ordinance is in Ledgerhand, with nobody in any of its sentences, which is the manner this Keeping has of making a death read as though it had happened to a roll and not to a person. I put the people back underneath.

At the head, in mine, written small: Copied for the counter and from the roll. The verbs are mine. The absence of them is not. Let both stand.

Title the Ninth, as it stands in the bank

From the Ordinances of the Keeping, Title the Ninth: Of the Roll of the Dead, the Remembering, and the Account Not Closed. Set down in the first years after the Rising of Y.H. 668; sent down to the factor at Greybank in Y.H. 418; added to in Y.H. 782 and in the Thin Year, Y.H. 812.

I. Of the roll

  1. Every vessel drawn is entered by slate-name and not by birth-name. The birth-name is turned face down at the entering and is not spoken at the Remembering. A name turned down is still a name, and the roll does not say so.
  2. An account is never closed. It is carried forward. The name of a vessel already poured remains in the same hand as the names of vessels not yet drawn, and no column provides for an ending.
  3. What is not entered did not happen. A death entered is a death that was collected; a death not collected is not entered, and the roll has no place for it.
  4. Where the vessel is a foundling with no house, the name is written by the hand that writes the slate, and the house is the Keeping. Where the vessel has a house, the house is entered and not the person.
  5. The measure is banked. The account stands. Nothing is lost that we have kept. Your return is recorded. These four sentences stand at the head of the roll and are read at no Weighing.

Margin: A woman stood here with her hand at the corner of her apron and asked whether her mother’s name was still on the roll, eleven years after the house that gave it was empty of people. I found the line and showed it to her, and the line was there. I have not entered what she said when she saw it, for there is no column for a daughter, and she took her hand off the counter before I could set a word down.

II. Of the Remembering

  1. On the fourth day of the Five, being the Remembering, the names of the dead are spoken in a clean voice by an officer of the Keeping or the clerk he sends. The reader does not ask who is in the room.
  2. A name is spoken once and is not explained. Where two names are alike, both are spoken, and the reader does not say which house is which.
  3. Whole returns are announced on the same day. The figure is read in a clean voice and is not inspected. A whole return is entered at twelve hundred marks and forty unspent measures; the purchaser is entered where the Steward keeps his room and not upon this counter.
  4. Where a name is disputed, the figure stands and the name is not changed. Where a name is not disputed, the figure still stands.
  5. No fire is carried into the reading and no priest touches the grating. The reader stands with his back to the fire and his face to the roll.

Margin: I have read eleven Rememberings at this counter and was never once told whether the names are called up or called back. A keeper of the Hearth-fire said to me that the fire does not care which, and would say no more. I have set that at the edge, where a keeper’s sentence has no column.

III. Of the returning

  1. Where one measure is pressed into one newborn vessel, the return is whole and the person comes back with memory. Where many measures are pooled and divided among many, the return is thin, and what comes back is a stranger with a faint interest in a life it cannot name.
  2. A thin return is paid by the district at thirty marks and is paid in labour. The figure is entered to the district and not to the vessel. The vessel is not asked.
  3. A whole return is booked at twelve hundred marks and forty unspent measures. The difference between the two figures is not a tax. It is not entered as a tax.
  4. After the pouring the name is carried forward and not struck out. The vessel that has been poured is the vessel that stands upon the roll, and no second line is begun.

Margin: A woman asked whether a whole return brings back the person or only the shape the cradle can hold. I read her the eleventh article. She said that was not an answer, and I said it was the ordinance. Both are true and I have entered neither. There is no column in this book for the thing a woman wants to know.

IV. Of the death not collected

  1. A house that has a death in it sends for the collector before the pot is broken at the door. The pot is broken after the draw and not before. A pot broken first lets out what the ordinance has not entered.
  2. Where no collector is called, the death is not entered, and the grey that gathers in the corner of that house is gathered in the ordinary course and carried forward like any other grey.
  3. What is held in a vessel from which no draw was made is not metered by the Keeping and is not returned to any account. The house is not asked, and this article is not sent to the factor.
  4. An officer of the Keeping has no house and no kin. The roll is his family. He is not corrected for a name he did not hear.

Margin: A woman broke a pot at her door before I came up the stair in Ash and told me the heat had gone out of him already. I entered the draw and not the pot, for the pot was the earlier thing and this ordinance reaches only the later. She held the broken rim against her apron all the while I wrote, the way a woman holds a cup she has not yet set down.

The roll-song, said at the reading

Said low at the counter on the Remembering, while the roll lies open and the reader stands with his back to the fire. Never at a Weighing, and never twice in one year.

Name on the roll and a crumb on the sill,

the pot at the door and the pail at the well;

they read the name and they leave the face,

the house keeps the figure and not the place.

Whole for the one and thin for the crowd,

forty unspent and a clean voice;

the cradle answers the way it can

and the roll goes on where the house gave out.

Ash in the corner and tallow gone white,

a name turned down and a figure read twice;

the account stands open and the counter stands still,

and the dead go forward because they must.

In the narrow upright hand, against the last line of the second verse: One figure entered; no deduction arises. In mine: There is a cradle in that line and he has numbered it away. A cradle can be answered; a figure cannot. Both stand.

What the two borrowed hands did

The College man came down from the Hearthseat with ink in two fingers of a cuff gone soft from washing. On article two he struck carried forward and set the account stands above it, which is the same thing said by a man who has never had to find a name at the bottom of a leaf. I have put carried forward back underneath. A standing account is a thing a man can argue with; a carried name is a thing somebody has to keep moving, and somebody does.

The other hand is a keeper’s from the Weighing Hall, narrow, with soot at the edge. Against article eight he wrote the purchaser is recorded where I had written that the purchaser is entered in the room the Steward keeps. Both stand. A keeper who says a thing is recorded has never had to go and find where. I have left my line where it is, because my line is the one the next clerk at this counter will read.

At the head and in the gutter

The grey at the head is Ash off my own thumb, turning the leaf toward the lamp while I found a name; it is the colour of a corner swept too late and will not come out of the creases. The white in the gutter is tallow gone hard where a lamp was set down on the leaf at the Remembering and left until morning; it has taken the corner off a line without taking the sense and I have not picked it off. The fish-scale at the second fold came off a herring sent up from Greybank and laid beside the counter while I read; it has flattened into a little pale leaf of its own and has not been brushed away. The iron in the crease came off the tube-wrap an officer laid there and stood longer over than he needed to; there is always a little iron in the fold of this book and it has never once drawn the warmth out of the paper. The crumb at the foot is barley from a boy who held it while I read him his grandmother’s name and forgot to eat it. The damp along the foot is a pail set under the counter through a morning and has taken the corners off two lines. The score in the lower margin is a bone stylus drawn through the word died, with was drawn set above it; I have put died back underneath and left the score standing, the length of the word they took out. There is no Last Day at a bank, and nothing here is swept up and burnt and called dealt with.

At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

The roll keeps the name and the cradle keeps the warmth.

The space between them has no column.

I asked the keeper who reads the fire, a woman who came about her mother’s name, and a clerk come up from Greybank, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the lines where they were written.

The roll goes back into its tin. In a few days the Five will come and the Remembering will be read, and I shall stand with my back to the fire and say sixty-five names in a clean voice, and the roll will be full when I have finished as it was when I began. A woman will come to ask whether her mother is still on it, and I shall find the line and show it to her, and the line will be there. That is my trade: to keep a name moving and not to say where it goes.
