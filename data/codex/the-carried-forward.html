I write the dead into this book. The bank stands on the Keel, over the Hearth, and the room is warm in a way that has nothing in it of kindness; a man comes in off the road with a figure in his mouth, and the figure is entered, and his name is carried forward. There is no closing column. A clerk come down from the College asked me last tend where the closed accounts were kept. I showed him the empty shelf. He wrote no dispositions recorded in his own book and went back up the road, which was true and cost him nothing.

There is a thing about this bank a clerk gets used to and never gets used to. A measure drawn in a district is pressed here into a newborn vessel, and the figure that stood in the dead man’s row comes out in the living one with no mark between to say it travelled. I enter both. I do not enter what it is that travels. A clerk who writes that down is writing a prayer, and prayers are copied by hands that do not sign them.

The brown at the head is iron-gall and mine, made by holding a quire open through a market morning. It will not come out of the creases, which is how the Steward knows I have copied a thing before he asks me for it. What follows is one leaf of the roll, from the last tend of Stone through the Five and into the first tend of Ilmen.

The rates of this bank

Entry — As the bank has it

Copper bit — twelve to a silver mark

Silver mark — twenty to a gold crown, a Sere coin that does not stay in Grath

Ember — five marks, by the Court’s book; no man has exchanged one for the other

Newborn vessel — twelve to fifteen embers

Labourer in good case — forty to sixty embers

One of the Kept — three hundred; fewer than twenty in the rooms near the fire

Deduction — one in twelve, as the notice says

Whole return — one measure poured into one vessel, booked at the figure

Thin return — many measures pooled and divided, figure entered by lot

Dead vessel — carried forward; never closed

An ember is five marks in this room. In the districts a woman trades six eels for five marks or five for six and makes nothing of it, which is a fact about eels and not about embers. I have set both and joined neither.

The leaf, as it stands

Figures in Ledgerhand, set from the district slates. The remarks are mine, in the hand that carries the name forward, and are not part of the account. The last column has no heading; a heading would make it an account.

On the carrying forward

An account is never closed. It is carried forward. The College has eleven words for a deduction and none for a carrying, which is why its clerk puts a nought in the last column and calls the roll balanced. A nought is a figure. The dead are not a figure that has become nothing, though the column is tidier with it, and a tidier column is what the College sends a man down for.

The batch of forty is the row he counts longest. He counts it once at the door and again at the foot before he strikes it, and I know he has looked because a man does not strike what he has not looked at. When he wrote nil against it I asked him what had been done with the forty, and he said a disposition had not been recorded, which is a true thing to say about a room with an empty shelf. He was not unkind. He had a cuff gone soft from washing and ink on two fingers, and he went down the Hearth Road before I had put the verbs back.

The column I keep with no heading holds the vessel born. I set the district and the tend and no name, because a name in that column would let a kinsman walk from a cradle to a coffin with one figure in his hand, and there is no road in Sarn a man should walk with that figure. The College has struck the column twice in four years. It is still here.

What the borrowed hands did

The College clerk struck carried forward wherever it stood and set was transferred above, as though a man could be moved from one column to another by a verbless sentence. Against the widow’s row he set a nought where the district’s figure stood and wrote nothing further arises. At the head of the last column he wrote No such column. I have drawn the column back underneath and left his words above it.

The other hand is tall and leaning and comes from the Steward’s room. Against the batch of forty it wrote the purchaser is not entered here, which is true, and against the newborn vessel not on the roll, which is also true, in the same ink that enters whole returns for persons not in the room. I have not contested either. A hand that writes whole returns knows the difference between a person in a room and a person spoken of in one, and has made a trade of not saying which.

The verse at the counter

Said low at the turning of a tend, while the leaf is carried from the counter to the shelf. It is not sung at the Weighing and has never been read aloud in the Weighing Hall.

Two for the drawing and two for the pressing,

one for the hand that sets the figure down;

carry it, carry it, into the new year,

the slate keeps the number and the cradle the heat.

One in twelve for the fire, the rest for the vessel,

and nobody enters what it cost to keep;

three on the roll and a fourth in the gutter

where the clerk’s nought went when the damp took it.

The second verse is not old. It came in with the Thin Year and I have heard it said twice, both times by men who would not say it while the Steward’s clerk stood at the door. I have copied it as it came.

In the margins

The slate dust in the gutter came off a district slate laid on this counter while a collector come up from the Ferr told me a figure in a voice that had been talking all morning. The tallow print is mine, from holding the lamp low over a row when somebody behind me was saying something I wanted the end of. The grey print at the foot came off a bundle set down where no bundle should lie, from a house three districts away that had wrapped its dead in a cloth and carried them to the road; the Ash came through the cloth and took the shape of a thumb, and I have not swept it. The small square of bare paper is where I scraped a name away with a knife and put no other in its place. It is the only name I have taken out of this book and I have not forgotten the length of it. The corner gone soft is from the sealed-file room, which is colder than the rest of the Hearthseat and always has been; the leaves take the damp when they lie against that wall, and the ink goes first where the figures are.

At the foot, in a hand nobody will own

The roll keeps the figure. The cradle keeps the warmth. Nobody enters the road between.

I asked the College clerk, a man from the Steward’s room and a woman who came to ask after a vessel of Low Kiln, and had three answers and no hand, as I have had before. I have left the line where it was written.

The leaf goes back under the counter. On the Remembering a clean voice will read whole returns for persons not in the room, and the names of the dead will be spoken, and the roll will be carried forward one more tend with no disposition recorded. A kinsman may come in the morning to close an account. He has never come yet. The shelf is empty and the bank is warm, and the warmth is booked in a hand that will not sign it.
