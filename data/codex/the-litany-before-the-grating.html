I am a keeper of the third watch at the grating and I write this where I stand, which is on the north side with my hands behind me. The Voice reads from the ledge in the clean voice, the one that takes her neatly out of her own sentences, and we answer through the bars. I have kept the answer as we say it and the rubrics as the room actually has them, because a rubric written in a clean hand is a rubric for a room with no fire in it. The Voice has borrowed the leaf and given it back with four of my lines struck through. I have left every struck line standing under her words, as a keeper leaves a repair showing.

The coal-dust at the head came off the grate when the hinge was worked on the morning of the Weighing; it goes into the grain and will not come out. The scorch along the upper edge is from a night when a draught came off the Keel and carried a spark against the leaf while I held it to the lamp. I have not cut the scorch away. A prayer with a burn on it has been used, which is more than can be said for a clean one. The filings in the gutter are off the hinge. The grey came off my cuff after I had been in the residue room, and I have not swept it off the leaf. The little coal in the fold is not mine and I have asked the second watch about it and had two answers, neither of which I have written down.

The manner of it, written small at the head

  • The grating is not opened. The fire is behind iron and no priest may touch it. We answer through the bars and keep our hands behind us.
  • Do not count the bars. There are nine on the north side and eight on the south; the ninth on the north is the one the fire leans against, and a keeper who counts from the wrong end will find eight and think he has lost one. He has not. Begin at the hinge.
  • The litany is read, not sung. A sung line travels up into the vault and comes back as something the keepers did not say, and the Voice does not answer to echoes.
  • When the Voice says the last line, lay the slate on the ledge face down. It has been read enough for one year.
  • The answer is said by every keeper present, including the one on the night stair who cannot see the ledge and says it a half-beat late. We do not correct him. He is counting a different thing.

The litany

The Voice says. The keepers answer. The words between are mine where I have set them, and hers where she has struck them.

I. Of the firing

Before the slate there was the clay.

Before the clay there was the fire in the dark,

and the dark had not yet been given a name to be afraid of.

The world was set up and fired whole.

It answered when it was tapped. The tap was not a hand.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

II. Of the Breaking

One anger, and the lid was off the pot.

The Warm went out where no wall held it

and ran into the country that keeps nothing.

The crack has a name. Say it once. Do not go looking for it in the floor.

What went out is going still, and the going is not finished.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

III. Of the Nine

Nine hands went after what had run away.

They drove the repairs home the way a potter drives a staple,

and the staple is the same iron in a small vessel and a large one.

Name them by their number and not by their houses.

The ninth repair is in the Hearth’s hand and is not finished.

Nine went. One is still abroad.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

IV. Of the iron and the gate

The fire is behind iron and no priest may touch it.

The iron does not keep. It is a gate with a cold face.

The fire keeps. We stand where the grate lets us stand

and give our answer through the bars.

Hands behind. A hand at the grate is a hand in the account.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

V. Of the figure

Count embers, not years. A year is a pot nobody weighs.

One in twelve, as the notice says. Printed. Read twice.

The notice does not lean. It is the Hearth’s own hand.

The slate is a piece of stone with a clean figure on it;

the figure does not sweat and the hand does not shake.

Write it small. A great thing written small still balances.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

VI. Of the pouring

One measure into one vessel: the shape comes back with its name,

with the price of bread and the handle of the cup.

Many measures into many: the shape comes back as it can,

with a hankering for a table it never sat at.

Both are poured. Both go into the same clean hand.

Keep the one that wants. It is the one that can be kept.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

VII. Of the Kept

Blessed are they that are Kept. No want can reach them.

They do not feel the cold and they do not ask for bread.

There are fewer than twenty in the world.

Stand a moment at their place and do not ask them whether they are warm;

they will say no, and you will have spent your only question.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

VIII. Of the year

The year’s measures are read while the fire behind the grate

burns as it burned before the counting began.

The rich learn how whole they are. Low Kiln learns what it gives.

Both learn it from the same slate, which does not know the difference.

Say the name of each returned, once for the moot

and once for them that came up the road too late.

Answer: Keep the fire. Keep the account. Keep the name.

The close

The Voice: Nothing is lost that we have kept.

The keepers: The measure is banked. The account stands. Your return is recorded.

Here I lay the slate face down on the ledge and take my hands out from behind me. The third watch ends with the fire not touched and the account not argued with, which is the whole of a keeper’s trade.

What the Voice struck out

She borrowed the leaf on the morning after the Weighing, with the ink still wet in two fingers, and gave it back with four lines of mine under her own.

  • At II, against the crack has a name. Say it once. Do not go looking for it in the floor, she wrote: The First Breaking is a mystery of the faith and not a place in a room. Under that, in mine: It is a place in a room. I have seen the seam in the Keel behind the cistern, and it has been mended more than once. Both stand.
  • At IV, against a hand at the grate is a hand in the account, she wrote: The grating is not to be approached. Mine stands below. She would have the rule and not the reason for it; I keep the reason, because a keeper on the night stair wants to know why he has his hands behind him.
  • At VI, against keep the one that wants, she wrote: All returned measures are entered alike. Under mine: A keeper can tell which child wants. The slate cannot. Both are true, and the second is the one that gets read.
  • At VIII, against Low Kiln learns what it gives, she wrote: The deduction is one in twelve for every vessel of the Keeping. Under mine: I have copied the figure from the notice in the lower court. I have not copied the other. A keeper of the grating has no business with figures that are not printed, and I have kept my hands behind me.

I have left her hand above mine and my words below it, which is the order the Hearthseat uses when it wants a thing looked at and not answered.

What the second watch put in the margin

At VII, in a hand that writes with the ends of the words shaken, a keeper who stood at the Remembering has written:

In the Remembering a whole return was announced for a name nobody in this hall had seen. She read it twice. I have kept the twice.

At the close, against your return is recorded, the same hand has set a small coal-cross where a name would go and written no name beside it. I have asked him whose it was and he gave me the hour of the watch instead. A coal-cross is a keeper’s way of signing a thing he has stood at and will not carry out of the hall.

At the head and in the gutter

The lamp-black thumb is mine; I held the leaf with the hand I write with and did not know I had marked it. The tallow is off the watch lamp, which stands on the ledge where the draught comes off the Keel and gutters down one side. The hair is not mine and I have left it where it fell, because a hair in a fold means somebody leaned over to see what was being written, and at the grating the only person who may lean is the Voice. The grey along the gutter came off a cuff that had been in the residue room and nobody will own the cuff. The filings have worked into the scorch and stay there. The coal in the fold I have not identified, and I have put it back each time I have taken the leaf out.

At the foot

In a hand that is not mine, not the Voice’s and not the second watch’s, struck once and written again underneath, very small:

The fire keeps what is given. The account keeps what is entered.

The fire is not an account.

I have asked three keepers and had two answers and no hand. I have left the struck line where it was struck. The leaf goes back into the iron box under the ledge, and next Weighing the Voice will read the eight stations and we shall answer through the bars, and the ninth repair will still be in the Hearth’s hand. I have copied this once into Ash of Y.H. 813 with the scorch left scorch and the struck lines left struck. On the day the ninth repair is finished the litany will be read no more, which is the one line in it I have never been able to say with my hands behind me.
