What the Covered Grain Keeps

Days 258–260 · Smoke 19–21, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

The first reading sets the figure. The second sets it where the first stood and calls the setting new. The sack under wool has no voice in either; the hand that filled it keeps what no slate can take.

— A rack-end saying, told over covered grain before the second reading of Smoke

— Day 258 —

Smoke 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay where it had been put down, against the fold at the low angle of the loaf shelf, and the room under the Vail kiln-house roof took it back the way a bench takes back a tool laid down at the end of work. the boy had set it there on the ninetieth answer and had not asked it to remember what it had done. An inch was an inch, a low note was a low note, and the little silence after a count that had ended in the room that does not touch was an empty thing carried across as an object and nothing more. He put it away with the same hand that had put away the others, where the next hand would find it without looking, and made no reach of it, no hearing, no gift, no rule for a morning to obey. Two hundred and fifty-seven had stood exact at the going of the dark. In the dark it became two hundred and fifty-eight; six months and seventy-seven days became six months and seventy-eight; and nobody under any roof in Sarn, Sill or Grath said so. Under the floor the carried fire went on giving its fifth unpaid day the payer it had given all the others, a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-eight days. Smoke 19 would be earned by the morning’s own light, when there was light enough to earn it; Smoke 20 stood one morning beyond that and was not reached for. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Ninthday.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow, the dry smell of a kiln that had been fed and left to work. Under it came wet wool, sour with the lodging of a body that had lain long in it. Under that, finer and more persistent, came the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw, a smell so long in the room that it had ceased to be an event and become a condition of the air. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall — four low pulls, and the silence where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell. Nothing in this house struck four, four and a gap and called it a summons. The heard bell stayed in the square where it had sounded, beside the told bell, and was not folded into the morning’s work.

There were three counts in three places and the boy kept them in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept its missing fifth. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, going on with its own business and never numbered among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts, and none rose into another. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them; a knot is not a nail, a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. He had learned the difference between an account and a likeness at a price he could not enter anywhere, and he kept the three where they were. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place, and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and he entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hardened past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went forward nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it, and he wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

In the small hours the house was only its objects. Garl Vail’s salt-stiff coat kept a broader shoulder’s outline on its peg behind the door, with the crease at the left shoulder still where a man had set his weight against a bench to reach a high shelf. The wheel-seat under the roof kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side. Nobody had spoken his name in two hundred and fifty-eight days and nobody spoke it now; the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. The boy lay under the low angle of the shelf and listened to a house keeping itself in the dark the way a pot keeps heat — without comment, and with one crack in it that nobody would mention until the thaw.

Sella Vail went to the fire before she went to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order since the first cold, and she did not have to decide it in the morning any more than a woman has to decide which hand takes the pail. She told the fire it was the thaw, saying it to the coals and not to any person, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the ash broke over them in a fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. The house will keep it, she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go — setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. Then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing. It was a glance with no errand in it and no errand came of it. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning at the hearth is not an opening and the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour. It had been touched once before the first firing of Smoke, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning or dusk since. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again, made into no lesson, no sign, no mending and no payment, with no payer named to any of it. The fire answered her with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she did not count the silence and did not ask who had left it there.

At the loaf shelf she kept her back to the basket and her finger on top of the front edge, so that the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread. She had reached a finger beneath that board on other mornings and brought it away without a word, and the boy had kept the reaching and the not-reading on two places with a finger between them. She moved her lips along a row of loaves standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; the old space at the row’s end and the newer one beside it stood where they stood, and she did not join them to the empty place at his ear in the other room, which was a room that did not touch this one. Then she set the pot rim out on the shelf with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. Two inches was a measurable thing and a child’s body was the whole of his reach, and the half-beat went where the half-beats went, on a place of its own with no cause and no owner.

The dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end, well away from the heat of the hearth. She went out to it before the yard was properly awake and lifted a corner of the wool for damp, the way a woman lifts a cloth from a sleeping child to feel whether the brow is wet, and put the corner back without looking a second time. Under the fold the grain had changed its state since the day it was gathered — threshed, dressed, dried to the arm’s knowledge of its weight — and changed in state it would stay. It carried no figure. There was no slate within reach of it and no hand had entered it since the handfuls went under the wool, and the arm that had gathered it knew what it weighed and not what it had cost. On the morrow the district would read a figure over that covered grain, and the figure would not know the grain it lay over; it would know a printed number and a levy and the space where a subtraction ought to stand, and it would pass over the heap under wool as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. He kept the figure read and the price of the grain on two places and did not average them, because a thing covered is not therefore paid for and a thing not entered is not therefore free. The space between the two had no signature to lay on it. He did not make a grievance of it, for a grievance wants a debtor standing under it and none stood; he made a finding of it and put the finding on a tray of its own. Nobody under the roof found it remarkable that a number should pass over a heap of grain and not know it. That, too, he kept.

Out in the Clay Yard the frost had gone out of the stone lip of the yard well under the bucket. It had stood white there in the small hours in fine fronds the like of frost-bloom, written where the Warm had lately gone out of the stone; then the rope came over it with the cold in it, and the bucket knocked the heads off it, and the grey of the wet stone showed through. The yard called the whole of it weather and went on drawing. The rope took the cold and gave it to the hands that pulled, and the pail came up grey, and a woman at the well wet a finger in the water and said it tasted different and did not say what it had tasted like before. Whatever white had been there he kept on a place of its own with no cause — not a payer, not the shape a vessel makes when heat goes where it was not meant to go entered into a reason, not the widow’s mark, for a well is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, not the first firing of Frost, which stood far off and unbrought. Ash had turned the corner at the Vail wall into a clean dark line a year ago and a finer grey hairline had gathered beside it, and the two lines lay unswept and were called the thaw. He did not lay the white lip across either line. Three absences in three places are three absences, and a row of them is not an account until a payer stands under it.

A neighbour came up with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the keeping and the storing would take now the grain was under wool and the visits coming. Sella did not turn her head toward the drying racks or toward the basket. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” she said, and went on with the bucket. The neighbour went off level with the question still on her, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. A man came down the clay road with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt and brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, the way a man brings a thing up to the light to see what it is worth. He looked at it. He put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere, and because it arrived nowhere it was not a missing figure and no answer was owed to it; a figure supplied to a fist that had shown nothing would have been a forged one, and the boy had no hand to supply one and no wish to. The river went on with its note of water over water through the finger’s-width door, low over lower, and the man walked on up the road with his number where his number had been.

Smoke 20 stood one morning ahead. It had not been brought into a day that had not earned it, and the boy did not reach for it now. The arithmetic would change in the square on that morning and could not be changed back, and nothing under the Vail roof would be settled by it; the covered grain would still lie under wool on the cool stone with no figure on it, the figure read over it would still not be the figure paid, and the heard bell would still sit beside the told. He knew this the way he knew the afterdrop — not as a prophecy, but as the plain reading of a chart whose note had been turned down. There was no grievance in the reading because there was no payer in it, and the absence of a payer was itself a fact to be kept and not a hole to be filled.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note. Under the floor the carried fire held at four with its silence where the fifth should have stood. Between the two the wall did not touch anything but itself, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house and no fire-sitter standing over the coals, so the only silence he borrowed was the little one after a count that had ended in the room that does not touch his.

He put the sixth question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and came down at once on the river’s next low note. Nothing was held. Nothing was carried across. There was no gap to keep and no interval to borrow, only the plain coordination of an inch and a sound, one after the other, exact as a clamp set and released. It was an answer and not an asking. It was ninety-one, filed on the sixth place beside ninety with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it. The hand came down and lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and fifty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and seventy-eight days were told by nobody. Ninety-one honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid the same number of days and its payer unnamed; the widow’s mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn, and the count went forward into Smoke 20, a day that had not earned its heading; the third reading stood beyond that and was not reached for, and the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The hand rested against the fold, and answer ninety-one lay filed on the sixth place, an object and not a power.

Nothing in the yard called the hour an end. The debt against a firing of tiles went around in the household’s mouth without a name put to it, circled the way a woman goes around Ash left in a corner, neither paid nor settled nor done with. Sella Vail carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate. That was the whole of her rule and she did not have to decide it in an afternoon any more than a woman has to decide which hand takes the pail; the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the bucket, the broom and the fire, and never to the basket, and she said the house where another woman would have said I. Out in the Clay Yard she went around seven things, and she went around them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. At the Vail corner she went around the clean dark line where Ash had turned, and around the finer grey hairline called the thaw that had gathered beside it, and gave neither a look long enough to make a cause of it. Under the lowest rack she went around the space where the broken birth-slates lay, with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the pan’s gone ring, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say what had been lifted from it. Beside the yard well she went around the trough, bare and dark, its water called weather by the yard, the ice gone out of it and the grey showing through. Out on the worked strips and up the terrace she went around the field’s empty place. And in the returning line, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the last hands on Seed 27, she went around the empty place with a look and a wide step and no name.

Seven in a row were seven. They were not an account until a payer stood under them, and none stood; a ring is not a gap, a strip is not a rack, and a hand that did not come back is not therefore a name. He kept them on seven trays that did not touch, with a finger between every pair, and would not let them stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of these.

At the well Ilva drew with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. A woman with a pot asked what the firings of Smoke had cost. Ilva’s hand stayed in her sleeve; the short strip of birch bark with its notches stayed where it was, pale where a thumb had rubbed it and dark in the uncut grain between, not taken out, not read, not asked about, not joined to the reed-cutter’s cord turned so its knots stood or to any other private keeping. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, she said, and went on with her water, and nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. The boy filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its place of its own beside the eighth with a finger between it and every other private keeping, and nine likenesses did not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. No figure came off the stick.

At the kindling the broom had its instruction before any person had one, and Tarn took it up as a man takes up a tool already named. Yes, house, he said to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six counted. The next I don’t count, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, with the unforged collector sentence beside it — the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. He had been that man and gave no name to the man. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there. It was a remembered stopping and not a new one, the ninth with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; he would not stand a tenth up to make the nine into a rule, because a rule wants a payer under it and none stood. The Seed 16 breath was drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, a small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in; his chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. Up in the Debt Loft, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in an unmarked clay pot, two years gone and cold long since, and not tried is not forgiven. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and without a turn of the head, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped, joined to no cause and to no ear.

At the edge of the Vail wheel-shed Jorra Vail came through with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. Three or more men had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the room kept its finger’s width of open, and its sound of low voices arrived at the basket as a direction and nothing more. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay beneath the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady, he said, and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. The burned-slate word had come up over the boards four times now out of Slate Row, a direction and not a cause, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. When Sella’s voice went low at the hearth and the sentence did not finish — The fire’s low. It wants— — his bent finger had gone into the cold iron of the damper ahead of it, taken the cold out of the fingertip, held while the rest of the sentence came to its end at the pot, moved a hair’s breadth and been left standing. One piece of iron, two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln and worth nothing at two inches of air.

Smoke 20 came on its own morning, earned by the light that made it and by nothing that had gone before it. The oiled paper at the window took the colour of skimmed milk; the dark went off the rafters by degrees, the way heat goes off a pot set aside, and the count moved out of two hundred and fifty-eight into two hundred and fifty-nine. Six months and seventy-eight days became six months and seventy-nine. Nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath said so. the boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, and the hand outside the wool lay where the ninety-second answer had put it down, as a man lays a tool on a bench where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not ask it to remember. An inch was an inch, a low note a low note, and the borrowed silence had been an object and no more. Under the floor the carried fire gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-nine days. No bell had sounded under this roof. The bell belonged to the square and would have to earn its morning there.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hardened past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though a line had been written out to that length and no farther; it neither went forward nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot, and knew the warming must come from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

There were three counts in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any; a knot is not a nail, a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell of four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it had sounded on Smoke 10, beside the told bell of the Long Night, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end, away from the hearth, changed in state and carrying no figure; the space between the figure that would be read over it today and the price of that grain had no signature to lay upon it.

Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, as she went on every morning, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. She told it it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot at the kiln mouth, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted toward no opening, the intake, the hold, the letting-go, setting tools in order and would not have the row called a number. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a morning or a going of the light, because a market and a reading are not openings. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household with no lesson made of it, no mending and no payer named.

At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance and the hand could not close it, and the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered. Then she lifted the basket in the crook of her arm and turned toward the clay road. Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind, she said to the wool and to the wind, and never to the basket. She carried him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, out of the yard and up the road, giving no instruction to the thing she carried and asking it nothing about the figure that would go over it before noon.

The Cross had earned its morning and kept it badly, the way a shallow bowl holds water, spilling it on every side. Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s finger’s width of shade where no slate lay, set it down with the back of her hand the way a pot is set out of a draught, and stood half a pace off. The cold iron of the post came through the wicker to his cheek and stopped there where the order written out for cold runs out. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, the thread at his mouth stood frozen, cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody here would perform. He took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it. The square held two hundred people leaning in over the reading block, shoulder against shoulder, each carrying a figure or a fist that no slate standing in the square had been made to enter. Eight pigs went over the wet boards while the light was still coming up, nose to tail, driven by a boy with a stick who did not look at the crowd; the boards were slick and the pigs slipped and caught themselves and went on, and each gave out a small flat grunt that arrived nowhere. An assessor of Sill wrote the eight down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail. A man set a figure beside another on a heap of turnips and left them standing like two pots on a board, neither owing the other anything. It was the second Account-day and market of the second firing, and it had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

Berr Clay came up short off the clay road before the top of the crowd had settled. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted lead glaze stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the crowd.

One in twelve, as the notice says.

He read it twice. The figure went over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. The boy lay under the wool with the figure going over him and did not make the figure the paid figure, because the figure read aloud was not the figure paid and there was no signature anywhere between the two. Berr did not look into the basket. The reading knew the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing; it knew no more of the man carried out on Day 188 than of the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. The surgeon opened the thing the way he would open a wound, layer under layer, and found the cost where the cost always was, under the reading and out of reach. He did not enter it. A figure spoken aloud in a crowd becomes a thing the crowd sets down and goes on keeping, exact and worth nothing, until somebody puts a payer under it, and nobody here had stood up to do it.

A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the levy would take now the kilns had been brought up and the grain was under wool. Berr took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.

The levy is called. It is not called by me. A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there’ll be a slate at the watch.

Your house, never I. The low voice went into the top of the crowd where the people who needed it least could lean in and hear it best. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward High Kiln and arrived nowhere, and it was not a missing figure; a fist closed on a number and opened on nothing, and a sleeve took it back. Berr went off up the clay road with the cord folded square, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The kindness was entered nowhere except in the box, and the box was locked. The arithmetic of the district changed for the second time in Smoke and could not be changed back, while the printed figure stood unchallenged and the figure read was still not the figure paid. A change made in a square walks home with two hundred people and sits at two hundred tables, and not one will know there are two figures in it, one printed and one paid, with a space between nobody signed.

The bell went over the market longer than a church bell, four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood beside the told bell and he did not lay one across the other, because agreeing them would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. The gap was a gap in a bell and not a hearing, not a fifth stroke of the full note and not a seventh; the six hearings kept their places with an empty place he did not fill to make the row come out even. He kept the bell’s four-and-a-gap in the square and did not lay it across the fire’s four-and-a-gap under the floor or the boards’ four nails and thumb-width knot overhead. Three counts in three places and a fourth sound in a square, and none of them was made to agree. The town went on buying and selling under the sound.

He put the question of the reading to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the bell and was held while the strokes came, four and four, and was held on through the gap where the next stroke did not come, an interval kept against the absence of a stroke and against no sound that stood in its place. It came down on the stroke after it and supplied no stroke to fill the gap. He filed answer ninety-three on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of the full note, not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. It was an inch of air held through an absent stroke and a down-stroke on what came after, the same honest fraction it had been in the small hours, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The heard bell stayed beside the told and was not made a seventh of the full note by a hand that had held its gap.

Hessa Rale went up the clay road before noon with Berr’s narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd at the rail; the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road’s mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner’s coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Two straps crossed her chest, the shorter let out for the long road, her thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing on the writing. She was of Slate Row, a carrier with working hands, and the road from the Cross to Greybank was the whole of her account. To a woman who asked what she carried she said,

I don’t read it. I carry it. There’s a difference, and the road doesn’t care which.

To the woman who wanted more she said,

Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I’ve got a road to walk.

She answered the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, and Ilva’s ninth stayed ninth on another with a finger between them; no tenth was filed in the square that morning. She was not joined to Berr’s twice-wiped thumb, to the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate below the bridge, to the notched birch kept in a sleeve at the well, or to any other private keeping. She joined the two ends of her road without joining what was written at either, and was not made a reader here to answer what the square had left standing.

Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. A buyer’s number went in before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody took him off. He was not the neat dark hand, not the Drawing collector, not the runner just gone up the road, not the woman at the well and not any of the hands filed on their own trays. He was a clerk set at a landing, and the measure he kept stayed where nobody had called it.

At the pot board a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the sweet green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth and a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. The colour was kept on a place of its own and no name was spoken over it; no eighth green came to the square and the pale line was not joined to the cup or to the green or to any ear. She was not Fenna in the telling of it. At the market’s edge a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver at its throat, grinned with a gap where a front tooth had been, and turned its head a half-beat late toward a pot set down near the eel shade. The sliver went dull when the shape turned back. It was a colour and a shape, joined to no name, no loaf, no slate and no sale; no ninth shape stood up beside it and no half-turn was made a cause. It was not Ulda in the telling of it, and nobody spoke to it and it spoke to nobody. The square went on buying and selling over both, and the bell’s gap stayed unfilled under the noise.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the post’s thin shade and carried him home at the half-pace, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and saying nothing of what had been read over him. The way went around the clean dark line where the ridge of Ash had turned and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, went wide under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape or chip or word left in it, around the yard well with the trough bare and dark beside it and its water called weather, out past the field’s empty place on the worked strips and past the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row were seven and not an account until a payer stood under them, and none stood. The basket went up the clay road with the reading block empty behind it.

Under the Vail roof she set him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face and left the thread frozen. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; she pushed the hair back with the burn-wrist and went to her own bed. The widow’s mark hung where it hung. It had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning, afternoon or dusk on Smoke 20, because a market and a reading are not openings, and the one opening before Smoke 1 stayed behind the household with no lesson, sign, mending or payer named to it.

Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood; under the floor the carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid two hundred and fifty-nine days and its payer unnamed; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another. The bell’s four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it had sounded, beside the told bell, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Two hundred and fifty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and seventy-nine days were told by nobody. Ninety-three honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between them had no signature. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last reading, stood nine mornings ahead and was not reached for, and the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The count went forward into Smoke 21, a day that had not earned its heading, and the hand lay back against the fold with the bell’s gap still unfilled.

The gap stayed unfilled under the Vail roof. It had sounded in the square at the second reading and gone up the clay road with the rest of the day, and nothing in this room had crossed itself to answer it. the boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf with the hand resting where the ninety-third answer had laid it down, and the house had come to the hour in which the woodsmoke gives up its separate smells and lies flat under the rafters. Sella Vail was gone to her own bed. There was no breathing number anywhere under the roof and no fire-sitter over the coals. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, low over lower, and under the floor the carried fire pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the two sounds went on in two places without meeting. A day spent in a square does not become another day by being carried home in a basket. It was still Smoke 20 under this roof; the count could go forward in the dark without dragging a morning up by its heels. Two hundred and fifty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath. Six months and seventy-nine days were told by nobody. The fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days and its payer had no name.

Dry heat lay behind the pot and came round into the fold, and the wool smelled sour with a body’s long lodging. He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field and asks only what is there. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Three counts in three places: the fire’s four and its gap under the floor, the river’s low over lower through the door, the boards’ four and four and knot above him. The arithmetic of the day was behind him and unread under this roof. Berr Clay’s narrow slate and the box with the true subtraction inside had gone up the clay road toward High Kiln before the light went; Hessa Rale’s slate had gone up unread toward Greybank before noon, the writing turned to her sleeve; the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate stayed where it had been set below the north bridge, at the out-of-line landing where a fifth step should have stood. None had been opened. None had been copied. None had been brought near the basket. A slate carried past a basket is not a slate opened beside it, and a figure that goes up the road in a locked box has not therefore come home in anybody’s mouth.

The figure read aloud over the covered grain at the Cross was still not the figure paid. The dressed grain lay under wool on the cool stone at the rack end, away from the hearth, changed in state and carrying no figure; it had been turned for damp and gathered by the handful and covered, and the arm that had gathered it knew what it weighed and not what it cost. The space between the figure that had gone over it in the square and the price of that grain had no signature to lay upon it. He kept the two figures on two places and did not average them, the way a surgeon keeps a chart beside a wound and does not write one into the other. A thing covered is not therefore paid for; a thing not entered is not therefore free. There was a small cold satisfaction in that, and the satisfaction was the knowledge that the reading had never been meant to know the grain. It had done its work perfectly. It knew the figure and not the cost, and that was the whole of what it knew.

In the small hours he turned the file out as a surgeon turns instruments out on a towel at the beginning of a case, each thing laid where the next hand will find it without looking, and found the whole of it exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. There was no hand to enter any of it and no slate within reach, and the towel lay on nothing but the fold. He went through it in its order, and the order was the whole of the keeping; a thing laid on the wrong tray is a thing made to answer a question nobody had asked it.

Six hearings stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, with air under them and a wanting in them, and beside them three shapings of the pot saying with the premature note of Smoke 14 on its own place; no fourth likeness had stood up to call itself a cause, and no pot had been lost. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three, though the damper in the shed held its double bite under one piece of iron and there was no word in Grathic for both mercies. The heard bell stood beside the told bell, its four, four and a gap not laid across the fire’s four and missing fifth or the boards’ four nails and thumb-width knot. The told figure stood beside the paid figure, and Smoke’s second arithmetic lay locked up the clay road in Berr’s box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing this towel could hold. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year had still not given; the Seed 16 breath lay unspent and the loan uncalled.

The Day 161 fraction at the damper lay on its own place; the Day 166 held beat beneath the loaf shelf lay on a second; the flagstone half-check of Tallow 22 on a third. The small dry scrape of a pot’s foot from the loft sill lay on a fourth, the pale line of the carried man on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with its honest answers standing on it. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over either. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the pan’s gone ring, the fold’s one crust and its empty place, the shelf’s two gaps and the field’s empty place lay on trays that did not touch. The returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 was gone around and not named. The ownerless question stood at five askings on its seventh place with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no witness and no row named. Five askings are not one witness and do not make a sixth; he had not opened the wound a second time to see whether it had changed its mind. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none had stood. The towel held the whole of it and the whole of it was worth nothing at two inches of air; a thing worth nothing is not therefore free, and a thing kept is not therefore settled.

Two voices from the afternoon came to the basket as directions and not as speakers. At the kindling Tarn had taken the broom only after it had been instructed, and had said Yes, house to the thaw, the early and the late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and had kept the late because it puts off the paying. He had told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count, with the small knife at the very edge of its sill and six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it had come to the word that would call the loan and had stopped there, with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping now, the ninth, and not a tenth standing up to make the nine into a rule; a rule wants a payer under it, and none stood. The loan stayed uncalled. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of the reach, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. Up in the Debt Loft, which reached the basket only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked clay pot, two years gone and cold long since; not tried is not forgiven, and not forgiven is not entered.

At the edge of the Vail wheel-shed Jorra Vail had come through with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist, and had spoken to the wheel before he spoke to any person: It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady. He had told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and had known the full note it would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a wanting in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. When Sella’s sentence had gone low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger had gone into the cold iron of the damper ahead of it and had held until the end arrived at the pot. It was a remembered fraction now, held in the holding and not in a word, one piece of iron with two bites and no Grathic word for both mercies. His promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as the day it was spoken. The men who had gone low into the back room before first grey had got no name from him and he had not said the room was cold; the room kept its finger’s width of open and its sound of low voices, which came to the basket as a direction and nothing more.

The second reading had answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, and no name had been given to what ailed him. He had been lifted through the glaze-room door with the sweet edge of lead glaze on the wind behind him and a pale line across the back of one hand, and the line was on its own place and had not been joined to the green at a woman’s mouth or to any ear; the boy had kept the small flat knock that came out of him as the sixth hearing, filed where a thing out of a person must go, and had not made a name of it in three years. No collector had come up the clay road for Garl Vail’s debt against a firing of tiles, and none had gone down it; the debt was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and the salt-stiff coat kept its broader shape on the peg with the crease at the left shoulder, and the wheel-seat kept its twenty-year hollow, and the proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and fifty-nine days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story. No collector had come for the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, and that place in the returning line was gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, and no figure had ever been given for it. A line left where it is is still a line kept.

He put the fourth question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; a man tests a tool before he trusts it, and the testing is the whole of the work. Through the finger’s-width door the river went on with its note, low over lower, and the wall between that sound and the bed did not touch anything but itself. There was no house count to borrow and no fire-sitter over the coals to give one; the only silence available was the little one after a count that had ended in the room that does not touch, and it was an object lying where it lay and not a gap that wanted filling.

On the nineteenth morning of Smoke the house under the Vail roof tended as though no reading stood ahead of it. Sella Vail came to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot and said the house would keep it where another woman would have said I; she kept her finger above the charcoal, set the pot handle two inches beyond his reaching hand, and lifted a corner of the wool over the dressed grain for damp before laying it back with no figure. The boy, two hundred and fifty-eight days old, put his hand up an inch toward the river’s low note and down upon the next, filing the ninety-first answer as a tool is put away. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step, and Ilva at the well answered that she kept the count, not the account, her birch in her sleeve. The ninety-second went up an inch and down before the letting-go.

On the twentieth morning the square earned its own market. Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s thin shade and stood half a pace off; eight pigs went over the wet boards and two hundred leaned in. Berr Clay, his office cord folded square, wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over a narrow slate and read one in twelve twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for late households. He called the levy and denied it with your house, never I, and went off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. The district’s arithmetic changed a second time in Smoke and could not be changed back. Hessa carried the slate up the road unread; the soft-cuffed clerk stayed below the bridge for an uncalled measure; the bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. The hand held its inch through the absent stroke (ninety-three). At night his file was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: the figure read over the covered grain was still not the figure paid, with no signature on the space between. The ninety-fourth crossed a silence in the room that does not touch and filled nothing.

On the twenty-first morning the third working week began as an ordinary tending that had learned nothing. Frost went out of the well lip and the yard called it weather; a fist came up toward the well and went back into a sleeve. Sella turned the grain under wool for damp and gave it no figure, and the hand kept to the river’s own interval (ninety-five). In the small hours the ninety-sixth took one rhythm for the small intake and let it go, then carried a second across the little silence after a count ended in the other room, laying the two apart. Two hundred and sixty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody.
