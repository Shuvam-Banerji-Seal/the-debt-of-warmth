A Man Stood at the Yard’s Edge

Days 399–401 · Ilmen 5–7, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

The yard kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, and a space at the row’s end nobody called a number. A man came to the edge and stood with his hands loose. No loaf passed and no slate was shown; the pot said nothing, and the fire kept four pulls and wanted a fifth.

Ilmen 5, Y.H. 814, Fifthday

The hand lay flat on the fold where the dark had left it, and the small hours came on under the roof with no door opened for them and nobody saying what they had crossed. Three hundred and ninety-eight days stood behind that hand exact and unspent, six months and two hundred and eighteen of them told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the day had a working name, Fifthday, and nothing under the roof made a threshold of it or a signing. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, with the dry kiln heat coming to him sideways and woodsmoke and wet wool in the fold of the blanket and, under that, the faint sour tang of Ash lifting out of the Vail corner where no broom had gone in two years. The room held its heat the way a cup holds water, with a hairline in it nobody mentioned and a staple nobody had entered. Outside, the dark went about its ordinary work along the Clay Yard, and nobody under any roof was asking it to mean anything.

The surgeon took the body in the order it arrived, because a field read out of order is a field read wrong. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put again in the dark and not made again, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The keeping at the fold was a finding however many hands found it, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. A face composed by cold is not composure. Pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and a cold patient cannot clot, and the rewarming he would choose is a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways, slow and from the outside, never hurried and never rubbed, and he could not ask for it. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat and the order in which it takes it back, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold; he had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and the knowledge was of the same order and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work.

Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. He judged neither register, which is a kind of triage and not a peace.

Under the boards the four near nails and the four on the next board kept their places, and the thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not kept its place between them. He did not lay the knot across the fire’s missing pull, though both were four and a gap and both wanted a fifth nobody supplied. Two gaps agreeing are two gaps and not one account; agreeing them wants a payer put under both, and none stood. The carried fire breathed four low pulls in its own order and kept the fifth where it had always been kept, the way a man answers a roll-call with four names and leaves the space for a fifth he will not fill. Its giver had been without a name for three hundred and ninety-nine days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it, and nobody asked who had first kept it. Garl Vail’s name had gone unspoken under this roof the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one keeping, for a fifth pull and an unspoken name are two gaps on two places with a finger between. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease still at the left shoulder; the wheel in the shed kept the hollow worn into its seat across twenty years, and nobody filled it.

Twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 stood behind one unsigned figure, twelve and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space between. Their twelve true subtractions rode locked in a box gone up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down and the boy having no key. Twelve are twelve, and a doctrine out of twelve wants a payer standing where none stood. The reset of the tend supplied no payer to the old twelve or to a new one, and Ilmen 10 stood five mornings off with the year’s first arithmetic waiting there, a reset and not a signing, and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. He did not lay his twelve old findings across a new figure to make a row come out even; a row made even by a hand that has no key is a row made even on paper, and paper is not a payer.

The kept things lay where they had been laid and asked nothing of the new day. The keeping at the fold was found where put and stayed a finding; the three coverings and the river’s winter skin were entered nowhere, not a fourth of the three and not spent on a tend-day; the four rooms that hold the same four things kept the same gap with a finger between every pair and no Court room brought under the roof as a fifth. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and the Drawing house’s flat knock outside them on its own place. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and its gap was not folded into the fire’s four and a gap under the floor, the boards’ four and four and a knot, or the twelve behind one figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, found where put. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and he gave it no source, for a source is an account that has found a payer and none stood beyond the door. The widow’s mark hung where it hung against a cord at a woman’s throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay oiled by ten thousand working thumbs, and a morning was not an opening; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a Fifthday.

First light came the way it comes, without consultation, laying a thin grey over the boards and showing the dust standing in the warm places. Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket. “It’s the thaw,” she said to the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the small red eye under the ash showed and shut and showed again. “The house will keep it,” she said to the pot, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, taking a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured, the way a woman stands over a wound she has dressed and will not look at twice. Her finger lay along the top edge of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The sweet edge of it had not come on the wind this morning; the jar stood where jars stand, and she gave it no more than the glance.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot waited with the hairline of the original break showing dark between the older brass staple and the tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, putting her thumb there before the weight came on the handle, and left the hairline showing. “Leave the crack showing,” she said to the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” To the fire she said, “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and repair stayed apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing for anybody who cares to look. Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt; the debt circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled, and neither pot nor fire was asked to answer for it.

She turned the dressed grain under wool on the drying racks with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and no figure went over it and no handful was lifted, and then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the hand’s intention and the hand’s arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket. A morning was a morning, a hearth was a hearth, and the first working week of a new year wore its face without acknowledging it; the house kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a clay jar and in a space at the row’s end nobody called a number, and if the new year had a face under this roof it was the same face the old year had worn, which was no face at all.

He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. The fold was found where put and was still a finding; the keeping had not been made again, and no hand had placed a second wool or named the first or given it to anybody. The crust’s corner stood a hair off the wool. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its giver without a name for four hundred and one days, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not.

The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and knew that it cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the body wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread at the mouth wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and got none of the three, the wanting and the having-not going on being the work the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. He had once left a job unfinished in a white room and had carried the sentence into a country with no word for the room or the job, and the old life went on gathering interest in the register and was not paid tonight any more than it had been paid on the six days behind. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a signing; he kept it edge to edge against a house that kept its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space at the row’s end and could not have told you what day it was by any of them.

Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended. No slate had been opened within reach of the basket, no figure given for the boy, no door drawn across the tend, no payer set under any of the kept things. The face at the yard’s edge stayed a shape on its place gathering interest without being called; the promise under the wheel-shed stayed neither given nor withdrawn; Tarn’s degree stayed a shape and not a rule; the twelve subtractions stayed in a box up the road with the key gone up in a coat pocket, and the first arithmetic of the year waited three mornings off for a hand not his and not yet come. The hand lay flat on the fold with the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool and the thread frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept — forward.

The Chronicle of Chapter 116

Three tending days came under the Vail roof in the new year’s first working week — Ilmen 5, 6 and 7 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: the crust two inches from the ear with its corner a hair off the wool, the fold in the empty place found where put and still a finding, the thread frozen and unwiped because nobody had ever been given an instruction about the thread, cold stopping at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither winning.

On Ilmen 5 Tarn stood at the end of an ended term, six pieces where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence; his hand went over blade and sill and away with no breath held, and he told the wood, Six counted. The next I don’t count. The ear-sentence stopped at the word that would call the loan. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, spoke to the wheel before any person, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence.

On Ilmen 6, before first grey, three or more men unnamed went low into the back room off the wheel-shed, and Jorra came through the finger’s-width door with grey slate dust white in a fist-crease. Then, from the basket’s low angle, a man from Slate Row whose name was on no roll stood at the yard’s edge with his hands loose — the soft hands of a man who sits at a table, the patient face of a man who can wait in a doorway. Nobody asked what he wanted and he did not say; no loaf passed, no slate was shown, no word carried. The boy filed his face beside the older record-first hands, made no tenth of it, called the waiting no sale and the man no witness.

On Ilmen 7 the house kept its days badly, in loaves pushed along a shelf, glances at a clay jar and a space at the row’s end nobody called a number. Sella told the coals it was the thaw, kept the charcoal unread, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. Tarn and Jorra were offstage; their keepings were found where put.

His satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; the infant got none of its three wants.
