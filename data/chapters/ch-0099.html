What the Notice Does Not Know

Days 348–350 · Ash 19–21, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

Eight readings go up the clay road in a locked box and one figure stands on the notice; the eight know what each house paid, the one knows only the notice, and no hand writes between the two.

— A saying of the Cross, in the dark month

— Day 348 —

Ash 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and seventieth answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist worth speaking of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and had since learned the exact weight of a barley crust at two inches. In the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof the boy lay awake on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under the finger’s-width door. Three hundred and forty-seven had stood exact behind him; three hundred and forty-eight had begun and would stand exact at the going of the light, six months and one hundred and sixty-eight days told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for the same number of days. On its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and forty-eight days. Nothing from the sixteenth, seventeenth or eighteenth of Ash crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The word that had come over the boards on the seventeenth had gone up the terrace and stood nowhere to be asked; it bought no lesson under this roof. A day lived is a day set down, and he did not go back into three finished days to fetch a meaning out of them.

Ash kept its nineteenth day without a boundary drawn. There would be no market, no Cross and no bell on the nineteenth, and no collector up or down the clay road; the going of the light would come as an ordinary going of the light, measured in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. One morning ahead stood the twentieth of Ash, the dark month’s second Account-day, named only as a date. He had long since stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it.

The crust lay two inches from his ear in the dry kiln heat, gone leathery and brown-edged, its corner standing a hair off the wool and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; no third had come in the three days behind to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; the Afterdrop waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease that is no ease, the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile. A cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes; and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. They arrived in the old order, the way instruments arrive on a towel, and he took them because they were there.

He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set within reach. Both were true, the second was louder, and neither won. The surgeon’s register took the small hours in its old order; the infant underneath went on being cold and made his wants known without words, which was the whole of his vocabulary and the whole of his power. A man who had reduced a thousand shoulders could not lift a barley crust two inches, could not wipe his own mouth, could not keep a fold of wool at his own chin. He played it for its exactness and not for pity; there was no pity in it worth the name, only the dry joke that lived in the discrepancy, and the joke cost him nothing because he was the only one in the room who knew there was a joke. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict. It was the work, and he did it.

Sella Vail came in before the basket and went to the hearth before she went to him. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and, under them, the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which had not been swept in two years. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot’s back knows how much a coal will bear; the small flat sound of it went across the room and was the first thing either of them said. *The house will keep it,* she said, in the tone of a woman setting a cup where a cup belongs, and another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting small intake, hold and letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure: the coals took breath, held it, let it go, and the account she kept of it was written nowhere and was not an account.

*Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind,* she said to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze on the wall shelf and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light and went out again.

At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. *If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,* she said to the pot; and to the fire, *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.* The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man who had first said them, which did not stop them being true. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing; repair stays apart from restoration, because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing.

Near the hearth the dressed grain lay under wool. She lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the morning’s, turned the heap once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer. She set the pot rim down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed.

He took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand’s intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given. He wanted the rim under his fingers and got the space where it would have been, and the space was not entered. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. A morning, a hearth and a bed are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months — Tallow, Ember, Smoke, Frost, Bone and Ash — are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash without a name and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this morning for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. Seven arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone and Ash’s first — stood behind one printed figure, seven and not one account. The figure stood one in twelve, unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between. Ash’s first rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across the house’s not-doings, the covered grain, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the two small objects of the hand, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt, and the finger went between.

The twentieth of Ash stood one morning ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise. It was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and no eighth arithmetic was supplied early to make a row come out even. Ash’s second would go up the road in a locked box as Ash’s first had gone, and stand behind the figure with the rest; the district’s arithmetic would change where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, but that was a morning away and the boy did not spend it now. A finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty, and no seventh was invented to fill it; the sixth was the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. The told bell of the Long Night stood in its square beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a seventh of the hearings or a fourth of the three counts.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door water over water went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. None of the three was a fourth of anything; an account that joins three counts under one payer wants a name written at the bottom, and nobody under this roof had written one.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note’s own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the nineteenth. Three hundred and forty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and sixty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the nineteenth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road, and there was no market, no Cross and no bell. Ash kept its nineteenth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; and through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. The twentieth of Ash stood one morning ahead, named only as a date and not brought into a day that had not earned it. Forward, not closed.

Sella Vail carried the boy down to the Clay Yard in the afternoon with the basket held level and the instruction going where it always went — to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire that would be banked when they came back — and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool; “it’s the river wind,” to the wind, and there was no I in either sentence and no instruction for the thing in the wicker. The half-pace she kept from every open slate was not slowness but a measurement, a basket balanced the way a tray is balanced across a room where somebody might have set down a slate and expected a figure off it. He went under her arm with his cheek out of the wind, and the cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and stayed frozen; nobody under any roof in the district had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Behind them, under the floor of the house, the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Three hundred and forty-eight days stood behind him exact and unspent, six months and one hundred and sixty-eight days told by nobody, and would stand exact at the going of the light. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted. That was not the same as not wanting it.

The yard lay open under a sky the colour of wet ash, and the afternoon had laid its thin light across the rows of pots standing with their bellies full of it, each holding its small private cold until a hand came to take it. She went around the place as she went around it every day, with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line of the old Ash ridge lay where it lay, and beside it the finer grey hairline that somebody had called the thaw lay where that lay too, unswept both, a line left where it is being a line kept. Under the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay in their own dark and were entered by nobody, and she went wide around them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The gone pan had left a round ring in the dust with no scrape in it, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the trough had a skin on it that would break under a pail and close again; out on the worked strips was the field’s empty place, a space in the row where nothing had been set and nobody had supplied a thing to set there. On the flat stone by the well the silt-clouded plate of ice stood out of the path of feet, and in the returning line, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, the yard went around a place that had no figure and no name and never acquired one. Seven places, gone around with a look and a wide step. He entered none of them, because a thing entered wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Three coverings stayed weather, entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost on the north lip of the well, the skin on the trough that broke and closed, the plate of ice lifting its clouded face a little out of the path. Each was a covering and each was weather, and the three were three, and if a man had made them one thing he would have had to put a name under it, and no name stood.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay under wool away from the hearth. She lifted a corner with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, turned the heap once and gave no figure. Her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer. Whatever arithmetic the morning would put upon the heap would not know what it cost; the dressed grain would stand under a figure that had never felt the weight of it, and the space would have no signature.

The yard’s hunts and repairs came and went as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and never as figures. A man down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came. Two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early, and said it would be late, and Tarn said “Yes, house” to the one and “Yes, house” to the other, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

At the yard well Ilva drew with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Ash cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey dust of the drying racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and people noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A woman waiting her turn, with a pot on her hip and her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Ash’s firings had taken.

Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, to the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf, to the cord, to the knot-string, or to any saying that had arrived nowhere. No figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat in the gap between the looking and the word. “Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.” She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

He took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart, by what was there and by what was not: the hand to the record before the eyes to the person, the eyes before the mouth, the half-beat in the interval where a lesser observer would have put an intention. He filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand in the district, with a finger between it and Hessa Rale’s eighth. He filed no tenth, made no figure of the slate’s nearness, made her no reader of her own keeping and joined her to none. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice; her well gave no witness, no fifth shaping came, and the direction that had gone over the boards on the seventeenth stayed on its own place beside the Ash 5 joining with a finger between, advanced and not paid.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said, and told a pot “You’ll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any High Kiln master who would call it early and make a song of it; the knowing stayed on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies. “He’s not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn; the claim went on gathering interest against it.

The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing. The shut-room tellings stood at two and the row at five askings with no sixth, and the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 direction stayed held apart on places of their own with a finger between every pair. No voice came over the boards this afternoon to make a sixth telling, a third shut-room telling, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping or a witness; the fourth shaping and the Ash 6 turning of the writing stayed where they had been put, and no sixth was made due on a day that had not earned one.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace, wide around the seven places, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She set him on the bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it. At the hearth she told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and to nobody else, and left the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. Near the hearth she turned the dressed grain once under wool with fingers going in and coming out without closing, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. A going of the light and a bed are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months — Tallow, Ember, Smoke, Frost, Bone and Ash — are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash without a name and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this evening for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

He turned out the whole file as a surgeon turns out instruments on a towel, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else, checked and left as it lay. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources, and the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell — four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross with nobody crossing himself — stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between. Eight arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash’s first and Ash’s second — stood behind one unsigned figure, eight and not one account; the figure read was still not the figure paid, both subtractions of Ash rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer.

Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row at five askings with one shaping and no sixth; the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, and the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the fourth shaping and the Ash 6 turning each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair. None was spent, named, joined, promoted into a witness, a sixth telling, a third shut-room telling, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a cause, a payment or a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The finding that a house keeps by its not-doings stood on a place of its own beside his own gaps with a finger between and was not laid across the absence of any man. The far-off hand that would one day lay the district’s private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

Under the floor the carried fire kept four and a gap; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door water over water went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. Nothing in the day had answered the hand that went in on Seed 15, the man carried out on Day 188 with the cost entered nowhere, the dressed grain read over by a figure that did not know what it cost, the carried fire’s missing fifth, the word that had gone up the terrace, or the room shut with the damper closed. Eight readings had changed the district where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and had answered none of those, and no doctrine stood up out of them, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease on its peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, the clean dark line at the Vail corner with its finer grey called the thaw unswept both, the twice-mended pot on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire, the unforged collector sentence lying beside the knife at the kindling sill, and the debt against a firing of tiles neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and fifty days and no lesson had been made of any of it; the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story. The held breath at the sill had belonged to a living man and was not laid across his objects. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in eight entries set down on eight places and not averaged into one account. It was a cold satisfaction and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary going of the light on the twenty-first. He did not spend the eight readings on himself or make them a lesson, a grievance, a sign or a power. They waited.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note’s own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-six: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and fifty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twenty-first had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road, and there was no market, no Cross and no bell. Ash kept its twenty-first day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months — the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days — was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood nine mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing or sixth held object invented to make the row come out even. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-six; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; and through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 30 stood nine mornings ahead and was named only as a date. Forward, not closed.

Those three days of Ash went the way the dark month goes when nobody draws a boundary across it — one ordinary tending day, one Account-day, one ordinary tending day, each lived and none spent to make a lesson of another. On the nineteenth and again on the twenty-first Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She went wide around seven walked-around places with a look and no number, turned the dressed grain once under wool without a handful, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first at the wire and left the hairline showing, and set the rim two inches out with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. At the well Ilva answered Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, hand to sleeve and the notched wood unread. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, and stopped the ear-sentence short of the calling word. Jorra spoke to the wheel before any person and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished.

On the twentieth the dark month’s second Account-day came to the Cross, earned by its own morning. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the sweet green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner, and under the hot clay of him the smell of nothing at all. He wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, read One in twelve, as the notice says twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it — The levy is called. It is not called by me, your house, never I — and went off up the road with the box locked and Ash’s second subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. Eight arithmetics of the year now stand behind one printed figure, eight and not one account; the figure read is still not the figure paid, and no hand writes between the two.

Each night the hand outside the wool went up an inch, took a small intake, held it whole on the river’s finishing and came straight down — answers 271 through 276, exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. Two hundred and seventy-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power.
