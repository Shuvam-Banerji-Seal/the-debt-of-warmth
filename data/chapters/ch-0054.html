The Pot Not Heard From

Days 213–215 · Grain 4–6, Y.H. 813 · Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln

A pot that sang before its time is not heard of after it. Go around the line; it cost enough to come in. The fire keeps its four and wants a fifth, and the river is not asked what it carries. What the house keeps it does not count, and the hand under the fold is not asked to reach.

— A saying of the Vail kiln-house in the first working week of Grain

— Day 213 —

Grain 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)

The hand lay down against the fold and stayed down. In the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln the boy did not go back into the dark to find a meaning in what had been given there in the night. A holding was a holding. It lay where a tool lies where a man has set it down, and picking it up to turn it over would only wear the edge. The night had offered him a coordination and he had taken it and filed it and let it go; it was not a sign, not a growth, not a reaching toward anything, and he would not make it one, because the making of it would have wanted a payer standing behind it and no payer stood. He was two hundred and thirteen days old and six months and thirty-three days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in Sarn with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number went on under the not-expecting of it the way a coal goes on under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it, and the distinction was a small hard object he kept and did not set down.

The day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound, the way days had come to him since he could remember coming to them at all. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season’s name instead of a cause. Beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short, and then the gap came. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered, and this was neither; it was a drawing-in that stopped where it stopped and asked nobody to cross himself. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by a decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and thirteen days with no payer named to it. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be while nobody put a hand to it; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places: the fire’s four and its silence, the river’s drawing and slackening, the boards’ four and four and the knot. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so they lay in their three places and kept their own company.

The body took its own reading before the mind asked it to. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, stiff past the point at which a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its empty name and had not been filled; no third came to stand there. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon’s register took the reading without being asked — surface warm, core not, the order exact — and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back the smell of hot clay and the gap in the drawing of the kiln. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and not enough to spend. Exact and worth nothing at two paces: that was the whole of it, and the child’s body went on being cold while the man’s mind recorded how cold, and neither bought the other a thing.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel — no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline — and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the shed through a finger’s width of door, the district’s slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirteen days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand’s breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on a wrong wind whose saying about a pot singing before its time had stayed a saying about a pot and cost no pot. None had been promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, the third reading gone up the clay road in Berr Clay’s locked box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, Hessa Rale’s slate gone up unread with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood — Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 — objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the seventh year stood at its very end now and had still not given.

Three held changes stood on three places. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 — the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish — lay where it was left, held and never spoken, no payer named to it and no slate entered; it had been held again through the firing month and into Grain as a remembered repetition, not a change made twice over. Beneath the loaf shelf the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; the finger had stayed on top of the front edge through all the days of Grain so far and had not gone beneath it. The flagstone half-check made on Tallow 22 stood on a third place beside the other two, a place in a yard as the others are places in a wall and in a room that does not touch either; it had not been repeated. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named; he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule. A fourth place held the small dry scrape of a pot’s foot set down on stone, heard from the loft on Day 183, with no word spoken over it and no payer named. A fifth held the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand, read at the well and on the carried body, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, not joined to the sweet wind, the dust, the pot that sang early, the sixth green or the foundling’s sliver; neither the third reading nor either of the earlier readings had answered for that man, and no figure, collector or slate knew what he cost. A sixth held the held inch, with its answers filed one beside another and nothing laid across them.

And beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, the burned-slate word: a direction from Slate Row that had passed over boards twice and arrived nowhere, advanced and not paid, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a fourth likeness and not a third telling of the room shut in the war. Two tellings stayed two. The crust’s gap here, the shelf’s two gaps there, the pan’s gone ring on its own tray, the field’s empty place on another — he took them one at a time and drew no finger across them. No third gap came, no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, and nothing was filled in to make a row come out even. The seventh green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape, owed to a market nine mornings off and not come early into the small hours of a working day.

Morning asked the hand. There was no breath counted anywhere in the house to hold it against; the coals had been left to keep themselves and the other room was quiet. There was only the note through the finger’s-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down at once against the fold, down before the note had drawn out, down while the note was still going. A miss. There was no failure in it and no promise in it and no failure hidden under the miss waiting to be found. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The old man’s exactness and the infant’s refusal occupied the same inch and neither bought the other anything; the body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true. He could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The market did not get to read the inch, and nobody under the roof knew there had been a question.

Sella Vail came to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing, and she kept it the way a widow keeps a wound clean: daily, without comment, with a hand that knew exactly how much pressure the thing would bear. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said the house where another woman would have said I, and if the day came when she said I over anything in this room she did not let it come in the morning. At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting go — the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, which stood with two unnamed gaps and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf’s front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the Ash-dark charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a morning and a woman’s back turned were not the same thing as a finger beneath it. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it. The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. A shelf and a morning are not openings; there had been no opening in thirty-three days. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. The private roll stayed unshown, and Dessa’s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

Grain 5, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)

The fifth morning of Grain’s first tend came up under the Vail roof not as an announcement but as a change in the air at the ear. First the smell: hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw, a smell with a season’s name and no cause anybody had been asked to give it. Then the sound, or rather the place where the sound failed: beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting the last go short, and then stopped. Not a bell. A bell is struck and counted and answered, and this drawing stopped where it stopped and asked nobody to cross himself. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and fourteen days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. They lay in their three places and kept their own company, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

The boy was two hundred and fourteen days old and six months and thirty-four days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in Sarn with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number had gone so quiet that he could no longer hear it asking, but it was there all the same, a coal under ash, and he did not rake it for the sake of seeing it glow. Grain’s first working week held no market and no Account-day; the first of either waited nine mornings off on Grain 10, with the seventh green still a colour and the foundling still a shape, owed and not come early. Under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln he lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, where the underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped where his hand stopped, and he did not ask the roof to be longer.

One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place unnamed, and no third came to stand beside it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon’s register took the reading without being asked — surface warm, core not, the order exact — and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back hot clay under tallow, wet wool, the sour corner breath. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces.

At the edge of the Clay Yard a kiln-hand come up from High Kiln on a wrong wind stood with his collar holding the cold out and the sweet edge of lead glaze at the back of his throat. He had come to say a thing about a pot, and he said it to the yard and not to the basket.

“That one that was singing before its time last month,” he said. “Not heard from since.”

The boy lay two paces off and took the saying apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound. He had no knife and no towel, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline, and he laid it out: a direction with no hand at the foot of it, no owner, no room, no cause and no figure. He had learned to ask two questions of every injury and to ask them in order — what has been done, and what is doing still — and he asked them of the saying now. Something had been done to a pot; something was doing still in the silence after it; and between the two there stood no mechanism he could put a finger on and no man who would own to having set it going. The old saying had been general, a proverb about pots that sing early and generally sing themselves out early; it had gone around the yards for weeks and cost no pot. This one had a date in it and a silence. That was the change, a hair’s breadth, from a rule about the behaviour of pots to the report of one pot that had stopped being reported. He turned it over and looked at the underside. Nothing lay there. A saying is a vessel with air in it; get a hand under it and it lifts easily and gives back no weight, and a man who takes that for proof of something has already forged the finding.

He set it on the tray where the near-full answers lay, beside the pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, and beside the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back. He did not promote it into the hearings. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person and no seventh; a silence was not a hearing, and a report of a silence was not a second hearing of the same pot. No seventh came on the fifth morning of Grain and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The knowing gathered interest on the tray nobody under any roof could read, and the interest was not a payment and drew no payer out of the air.

The sweet edge of lead glaze had come up off High Kiln on the wrong wind and touched the fold and gone out again on the wind that brought it. He took it as a direction and not as a second edge paid. A breath of air out of another quarter is a fact with a place and a going; it is not a cause, not a payment, not a second green at a mouth, and no lesson stood up out of it. The wrong wind went on going out of the yard and the sweetness went with it, and the back of his throat kept the taste a moment longer than the wind kept the air.

The Chronicle of Chapter 54

Three days went over the Vail roof in the first working week of Grain, and the house kept them in loaves, glances and the space where a thing had stood. The boy at the low angle of the loaf shelf was two hundred and thirteen days old on the fourth of the month and two hundred and fifteen on the sixth, and nobody said either number, and no slate in Sarn had a place to enter them. His hand went up an inch toward the river’s note and came down: at once on the fourth morning, before the counted breath on the fifth, at once again on the sixth under conditions that had sometimes answered. In the small hours it held for a small intake, kept the river’s low interval with no count to hold it against, and on the last night stayed through the intake and hold of one of the widow’s counted breaths, coming down on the letting go. Each fraction was filed and named no payer.

A kiln-hand come up from High Kiln on a wrong wind told the yard that the pot which had sung before its time last month had not been heard from since. The saying turned by a hair from a proverb into the report of one pot’s silence, and the boy took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: a direction with no hand at the foot of it, no owner, no room, no cause, no figure. He set it beside the nearly-full answers and never among the hearings. Jorra, who knew the note it would give three turns before it left his hands, let the saying stand; no pot was lost. The sweet edge of lead glaze came up off High Kiln and went out on the wind that brought it.

On the sixth morning the burned-slate word went over the boards a third time, from Slate Row on the breath of the barley price and the weather, with no owner, room, flame or reason. It was a third telling of the slate and still not a third telling of the counting room shut in the war. Jorra grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. Cost-questions at the door and the well went to the pot and the weather and away level. Sella kept her back to the basket and her finger above the charcoal, set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, rested a half-beat longer at the setting and did not look toward the bed. Tarn brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped. No collector came, no slate was opened, and the count went forward exact and unspent, spoken by nobody.
