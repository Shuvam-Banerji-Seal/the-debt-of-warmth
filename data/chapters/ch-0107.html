The Empty Place Beside the Crust

Days 372–374 · Stone 13–15, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

The fire keeps what it is given. The shelf keeps what is taken. Nobody under the roof counts the space between.

— A hearth-saying of Low Kiln, told to the coals

— Day 372 —

Stone 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been put at the close of the day before, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-one exact. The boy did not carry it forward by any decision; he carried it the way he carried the room’s small sounds and the crust’s hair-lifted corner, because it was there and because a man’s attention goes where his field is. He lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, in the small hour before light, and this morning had a date that no roof under that sky would speak aloud: Stone 13, the thirteenth day of the deep-winter month. Three hundred and seventy-one days, six months and one hundred and ninety-one days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. The Drawing stood behind the household on its own morning and bought no lesson. Nothing from the tenth, the eleventh or the twelfth of the month had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, and the boy lay under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, as he had been set at every going of the light.

The surgeon took the body in the old order, as a man takes a field before the light is good enough to work in it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and lay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a face composed by cold from a face composed by choice. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that the order in which a body gives heat up is the reverse of the order in which it takes it back. The knowledge was exact and useless at two inches of air.

He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. The surgeon knew the basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the careful rewarming he would choose, slow and from the outside, never hurried and never rubbed, never set too near the fire, and could not ask for it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before the basket, as she had come to it at every morning since the boy came under the roof. “It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It’s the river wind.” She did not look at the basket when she said it, and she did not look at the boy when she carried him. The cord of her widow’s mark lay oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand shutting on nothing, and the mark hung where it hung. A morning and a hearth were not openings; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a day the district had not named.

At the pot board the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack before she let the weight come on the handle, and said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled* and *If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one*, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man said them. Repair stayed apart from restoration, because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and the hairline was the hand.

The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured, turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted and no handful. The empty fingers stood beside the one handful let fall at the rack end on Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer and none stood. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air.

She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift; a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. The infant wanted a hand on the pot handle. The surgeon knew the heat came sideways in that corner and that a thing set level there was a thing being warmed carefully and slowly, and he could not ask for it and could not thank anybody for it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won.

The room went on around the basket in the way a room goes on around a thing put away. Coal settled in the hearth with a small dry sound, and clay ticked along the shelf as the wall gave up the night’s heat by degrees; woodsmoke and wet wool lay over everything, and under them the faint sour tang of the unswept ridge, which nobody in this house had ever swept before a firing and nobody would sweep today. These were the small sounds, and the small sounds do not lie; the large ones are mostly weather. The morning came on without a boundary drawn anywhere, and the household went about its tending as a surgeon goes about a field taken as found and left as found: not gladly, not reluctantly, but with the whole of the attention on what was there.

He had once read a pale line where a ring had been on the back of a strange hand and had filed it among the hands that know what a face lies about, and that hand too stood on a place of its own and had never been made to stand up and call itself a rule.

He filed it as answer three hundred and twenty-four: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the two bites of iron, the river’s winter skin, the widow’s mark, the new keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and twenty-four honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

The crust kept its hair-lifted corner and its empty place with no third, and the wool was tucked into that place and lay there without a name; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and the Afterdrop waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; through the finger’s-width door the river went low over lower under its winter skin, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Count three hundred and seventy-four exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-four days told by nobody, the carried fire’s fifth unpaid and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. Stone kept its fifteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held, and the count went on under the Vail roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. The fold was warm where a hand had been and the place beside the crust had not been filled and had not been named, and the boy lay with the day’s keeping gathered on its own place and not paid, and nothing in the house stood up to call it an account. Forward, not closed.

The Chronicle of Chapter 107

For three days after the Hearth Drawing the house under the Vail roof went about its tending as a surgeon goes about a field taken as found and left as found, and nothing seen two doors down crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, widest around the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Sella Vail came to the coals before she came to the basket, told them it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first, left the old break showing, and gave both proverbs to the pot and the fire, never to a person. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured, the charcoal unread beneath her finger, and the widow’s mark hung untouched, for a hearth and a morning and a bed are not openings.

Ilva drew at the yard well on the thirteenth with the rope stiff in the cold, clay pale in her nail crescents and rack dust in one thumb seam she did not scrub out. To a woman who asked what Stone’s firings had taken she answered, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, her hand to her sleeve before her eyes to the asker and her mouth after both. On the fourteenth Hessa Rale came down the clay road from Greybank with Berr Clay’s slate edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned toward her own sleeve, the second strap let out; she read nothing aloud and did not look into the basket. Four rooms kept the same gap in their count, four likenesses and not one account — an account wanting a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand stood.

On the fifteenth evening, setting the boy on his bed and righting the fold where the cold had ridden it back, Sella tucked the wool into the empty place beside the crust. No word went with it, no third crust was put there, no name spoken over the place, no look toward his face; her hand rested the half-beat longer than the setting needed and went away. The boy took the interval apart by what was there and by what was not and filed it beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring and the field’s empty place — not a joining of rooms, not a third crust, not a gift, not a payment, no payer standing, gathered once and not made again. The crust kept its hair-lifted corner, and now the wool lay in the empty place without a name. Three days went by exact and told by nobody, and the count went on.
