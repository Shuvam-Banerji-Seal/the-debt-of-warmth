A Word Without a Doorway

Days 345–347 · Ash 16–18, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A hand may stay a breath past the check and no house can enter it; a word may come over the boards and stand in no doorway to be asked. The strip stays in the sleeve, the crack stays showing, and the fifth pull is not supplied.

— A yard saying of Low Kiln, in the dark month

— Day 345 —

Ash 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and sixty-fourth answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist worth speaking of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and had since learned the exact weight of a barley crust at two inches. In the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof the boy lay awake on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under the finger’s-width door. The room smelled of wet wool and tallow and, under them, the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which had not been swept in two years. Three hundred and forty-five stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and sixty-five days told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-five days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Nothing from the thirteenth, fourteenth or fifteenth of Ash crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. A breath had been held at a knife-sill and stayed where it was put; a day lived is a day set down, and he did not go back into those three to fetch a meaning out of them.

Ash kept its sixteenth day without a boundary drawn. The older women of the district named the months by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days that had gone into them, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words under the Vail roof; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. There would be no market, no Cross and no bell on the sixteenth, and no collector up or down the clay road; the going of the light would come as an ordinary going of the light, measured in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. The ear gave a man the truth before the eye did, because the eye is always being told what to see and the ear is not; he had learned that in a room where nobody had ever thought to tell him what he was hearing.

The crust lay two inches from his ear in the dry kiln heat, gone stiffer and browner, its corner standing a hair off the wool and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; no third had come in the three days behind to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; the Afterdrop waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease that is no ease, the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile. A cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes; and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set within reach. Both were true, the second was louder, and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, the surgeon’s register taking the small hours in its old order while the infant underneath went on being cold. It was a small humiliation and an exact one, and he played it for its exactness: a man who had reduced a thousand shoulders could not lift a barley crust two inches, could not wipe his own mouth, could not keep a fold of wool at his own chin.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before the basket. She was a broad woman with forearms the colour of dried slip and a burn across the back of her left hand shaped like a little door — two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside it puckered and pale — and she smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool. One grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light as she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-five days, and she did not supply the pull. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure.

Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air; nothing in the jar was named and no figure came off the glaze. When her hands had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child, the days in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, never in a number anybody else would have recognised as a number.

From the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person. They had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man who had first said them, which did not stop them being true. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and here the hand was left showing in the dark hairline between brass and wire. He read the mending as a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, by what the hand had done and by what it had left undone, and kept the two readings apart, for a seam that holds is not a seam that never broke.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth. She lifted a corner with a still lighter touch, turned it once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. Whatever arithmetic the sixteenth might put upon a heap of grain, it would not know what the heap had cost, and the space between that figure and its price would have no signature set into it.

The pot’s rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. He took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand’s intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given. He wanted the rim under his fingers and got the space where it would have been, and the space was not entered.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. A morning and a hearth are not openings, and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the last touch had been at the kiln mouth on Ash 2, before the dark month’s first firing. Six openings in six months — Tallow, Ember, Smoke, Frost, Bone and Ash — are six and not a rule. The sixth stood behind at Ash 2, held without a name and not spent, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this morning for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the man carried out in the clay-heavy rise of the brook; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The whole tray was checked and left as it lay. Seven arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and Ash’s first — stood behind one printed figure, and seven were seven and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. The finding sat where findings sat, on a place of its own with no payer under it, and was not laid across the house’s not-doings, the dressed grain covered under wool, the five held objects, the handful let fall at the rack end on Bone 22, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh at the kindling on Bone 26, the pot-crack resting beside the five, the held breath at the knife-sill of Ash 14, the two small objects of the hand, the sixth opening at the kiln mouth, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping at the well and the turning of the runner’s writing, or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. A finding with no payer can be carried a long way without becoming a rule, and no doctrine stood up out of seven. Ash’s first rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln; Ash’s second stood offstage on Ash 20, named only as a date and not brought into a day that had not earned it.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, and an empty place kept empty is a finding while a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The sixth was the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. The heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a seventh of the hearings or a fourth of the three counts. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space. The full note was left unasked; there was no opening under this roof to which it might have been put, and the boy did not supply one.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody was allowed to fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; and the unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife at the kindling sill with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without being called, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and forty-five days, and no lesson had been made of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door water over water went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them, and the heard bell was not made a seventh of the full note.

First light came through the finger’s-width door and the room took its shape by degrees, the pot board first, then the shelf’s low edge, then the fold at his ear. The morning started anyway, indifferent and welcome, the way a pot is set down where a pot belongs and no speech made over it. The boy lay small and cold under the wool and filed what there was to file, which was all of it and none of it spendable.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note. It did not reach; the inch was not a reach and he had long ago stopped calling it one. It was the distance a hand may move in a field without disturbing the dressing, and he took it the way he had once taken a hand into a cavity, slowly, with the whole of the attention and the knowledge that most of what a hand does in a field is wait. The note under the finger’s-width door went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, and let the intake go on the note’s own finishing. It came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and sixty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, Ilva’s wood nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and sixty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one of them stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary light of the sixteenth.

Three hundred and forty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixty-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the sixteenth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. Ash 20 stood offstage and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Forward, not closed.

In the afternoon the dry kiln heat went on pressing at the roof the way a hand presses at a seam and finds it holding, and Sella Vail carried the basket down under the finger’s-width door into the Clay Yard. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wind, and set the fold right under his chin without a look at his face. She carried him level at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the way a woman carries a pot filled to the brim and does not slop it, and the yard came up around the boy as a field comes up under the eye before the dressing goes on, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry rasp of rope at the well, the settle of clay going on cooling behind a wall, the far small knock of a pot set down somewhere out of sight. Three hundred and forty-five stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and sixty-five days told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. Under the floor of the house the carried fire had given four low pulls in the morning and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-five days; on its peg the salt-stiff coat kept a broader man’s shape the same number of days with no name spoken over it. Nothing from the morning crossed the yard as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The answer taken in the small hours had gone where answers go and had bought nothing at two inches of air, and the yard was not its echo.

The crust kept its two inches from his ear in the dry cold, gone leathery and brown-edged, its corner standing a hair off the wool, and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; no third had come since the morning to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past the stopping for a warming nobody in this house would perform. He had once stood in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal and decided which of twenty bodies would be opened first, and here he could not lift a barley crust to his own mouth. The joke cost him nothing and was not funny.

She went around the yard the way the yard went around itself, with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line lay where it had lain and the finer grey hairline beside it that the district called the thaw lay beside it unswept, and she went wide of both and swept neither. Beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay where they lay and she stepped around them, entering nothing; the round empty ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept its place with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word, and a man stepping over it did not look down. At the yard well the trough kept its skin, and on the flat stone by the well the silt-clouded plate of ice stood out of the path of feet; out on the worked strips the field kept its empty place; and in the returning line, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, she went around the place the way everybody went around it, with no name spoken. Seven places, and no eighth likeness and no cause across two of them and no payer under any. He entered none, because a thing entered is a thing that wants a payer and none stood.

The three coverings stayed weather and were entered nowhere. The frost that had written white on the north lip of the well in the dark month had gone off while the yard looked elsewhere, and it was not a sign now and had not been a sign then; the skin on the trough broke under the knock of a pail and closed again over what it had covered, and nobody lifted a finger to it; the silt-clouded plate stood where no foot went. A covering over a depth, the same covering over another depth, a piece of the depth lifted and set where no foot would go—three things on three places with a finger between every pair, called weather, and no fourth likeness made a cause. The figure in the box up the road had not entered any of them and was not made to.

The hunts and repairs of the afternoon stayed directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. A man come down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and late. Tarn, bent at the shoulders from six years in the slip pit, with Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, said “Yes, house” to each of them, and kept the late because a late thaw put off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The boy read each as a chart left blank where the number should stand and entered none of the blanks; a cost-question answered with the weather was a direction arrived nowhere, and the arriving was the whole of it.

At the kindling Tarn kept his account in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and in the lip beyond it where it could not: six standing, the seventh at the lip beside the unforged collector sentence that Garl Vail had left there with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man could check it and where a man’s hand could also slip it off. “Six counted. The next I don’t count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and cut nothing and counted nothing and called no loan.

He did the small checking a house could see and no more. He set the knife where it could be checked and passed his hand over it the way a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, the fingers going flat over the blade and the wooden sill, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching what little light there was and giving it back. That much the house could have checked and would have found in order. The hand went away when the checking was done. No breath stayed at the top of the reach. The held breath he had left at the sill on the fourteenth was where it had been put: checked on the fifteenth and found, and now checked again and found, and not made again on the sixteenth. He filed it where it had stood since the fourteenth, on a place of its own beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting, with a finger between every pair, expressly not a sixth held object, not a seventh of anything, not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not the calling of the loan, not a habit, a way, a hearing, a mending, a sign, a cause or a payment, with no payer standing under it. He cut nothing. He counted nothing. He did not spend the Seed 16 breath, and the thumb’s resting on the uncut seventh of Bone 26 he checked and found where it had been laid and did not make again.

The boy had been looking at the kindling without appearing to look at it, as he had looked at a hundred knife-sills, taking the field by the small sounds first. He took this one apart by what was there and by what was not. What was there was a hand that had gone up to check and come down from the checking, nothing held at the top of the reach. What was not there was a cut, a count, a word that called the loan, a spent breath, a thumb laid down where a thumb had been laid before. He had watched a surgeon draw a hand from a field before the dressing went on and known the difference between the hand that works and the hand that only rests; this was the hand that checks and goes away, and the difference was exact.

To the woman with the kindling and to the two fence-menders Tarn said “Yes, house” to the early thaw and the late, and kept the late because it puts off the paying. At the flagstones his stroke went over the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed on its own place with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat and the Frost 5 returned beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot above the shed, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. He brought the ear-sentence all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, the ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach and the loan uncalled. He did not say the sentence. The nine stoppings were nine and not a rule, and no tenth came due on a day that had not earned one.

Jorra came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. To the wheel he spoke before he spoke to any person: “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady.” To a pot he said, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the full note it would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and make a song of it.

The boy lay with his face turned a little to the air, the way she set him so the wind went past his ears and not into them, and he took the yard as it arrived, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry rasp of rope at the yard well, the far small knock of a pot set down out of sight, the scrape of a broom going around something it did not enter. Three hundred and forty-seven stood behind him, exact and unspent and spoken by nobody, and under the floor of the house he had left the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for the same number of days. Nothing from the morning crossed into the afternoon as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. A day lived is a day set down, and he did not go back into it to fetch a meaning out of it.

She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay with the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, unswept both, and her look went over them the way a hand goes over a sleeping animal while her step went wide around them without a word. Beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, entered by nobody and asked about by nobody. The gone pan’s round ring held no scrape, crumb, chip, print or word, only the shape the thing had left when it was taken away. The skin on the trough had broken under a pail earlier and closed again, and the flat stone by the well kept its plate of silt-clouded ice stood out of the path of feet. In the field’s empty place on the worked strips the returning line had a gap in it where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and the yard went around that place as it went around the others, with a look and a wide step and no number, no name, no cause, no body, no figure and no payer. Seven places. She made no eighth of them and set no figure on any.

The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost on the north lip of the well, the skin that breaks under a pail and closes, the silt-clouded plate stood out of the path of feet. The yard had called all three weather and entered them in no slate, and no reading had paid them and no opening under any roof had answered for them. He kept them on three places that did not touch. A fourth would have wanted a payer put under all four, and none stood.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth. She lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the morning’s, turned the heap once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. Whatever arithmetic Ash’s second would put upon the heap in two mornings’ time would not know what the heap had cost, and the space between that figure and its price would have no signature set into it. The empty fingers went beside the one object of Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer.

The month’s hunts and repairs went around the yard as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. A man come down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and late. Tarn said *Yes, house* to the early and to the late and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot.

He cut nothing and counted nothing into the seventh.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, and the three or more men who had gone low into the back room before first grey got no name from him and no word that the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. *It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady.* To a pot he said, *You’ll do,* in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and he knew the full note it would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where no kiln-master come up from High Kiln could call it early and make a song of it.

When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and was left standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies.

He grew quieter when the direction went around the boards and up the terrace, with nobody standing anywhere to ask it anything, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order from anybody. The burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, the shut-room tellings at two and the row at five askings with no sixth, and the claim went on gathering interest against the promise he had neither given nor withdrawn. *He’s not Vail* stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

Tarn’s stroke went over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, and the warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in the Debt Loft, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed on the flagstone’s own place beside the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat and the Frost 5 returned beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule; neither the handful nor the thumb’s resting nor the pot-crack resting nor the held breath at the sill had been made a fifth.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace, wide around the seven walked-around places, and set him on the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof and never in the Debt Loft. She righted the fold at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. The crust lay two inches from his ear, gone stiffer in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and the Afterdrop waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. A morning, a hearth, a going of the light, a yard and a bed are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months — Tallow, Ember, Smoke, Frost, Bone and Ash — are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on Ash 2 and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this afternoon for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

At the hearth she told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot’s back knows how much a coal will bear. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and nothing in the jar was named and no figure came off the glaze.

At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. *If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,* she said to the pot; and to the fire, *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.* The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man who had first said them, which did not stop them being true. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire and she left it showing, for repair stays apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. At the rack end the grain lay turned once under wool, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, and the pot’s rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. He took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand’s intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given. He wanted the rim under his fingers and got the space where it would have been, and the space was not entered.

The whole tray was checked and left as it lay, as a surgeon checks a field before the light and sets down every instrument where it was found. Six stood behind the one unsigned figure and stayed held behind it, not spent to make the figure come out even, and the figure did not know what any of them cost: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with the seventh place empty, six openings in six months with no seventh made due, six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip. An empty place kept empty is a finding, while a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The sixth hearing was the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. The heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a seventh of the hearings or a fourth of the three counts. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space, and seven arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone and Ash’s first — stood behind one printed figure, seven and not one account, Ash’s first locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box, Ash’s second two mornings ahead and named only as a date.

The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt, and the finger went between. The finding that a house keeps by the not-doings stood on a place of its own beside his own gaps with a finger between, and was not laid across the absence of any man.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody was allowed to fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; and the unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife at the kindling sill with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and forty-seven days, and no lesson had been made of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the finger’s-width door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note’s own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the eighteenth.

Three hundred and forty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and sixty-seven days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the eighteenth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road, and there was no market, no Cross and no bell. Ash kept its eighteenth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months — the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days — was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; and through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 20 stood two mornings ahead and was named only as a date and not brought into a day that had not earned it. Forward, not closed.

Those three days of Ash went by the way a pot goes around a shelf — without speech, and with a place kept for what was absent. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. The charcoal tally stayed unread under her finger, and the mended pot came up thumb-first with its hairline showing. In the Clay Yard she went wide around seven walked-around places with a look and no number and turned the grain once under wool without lifting a handful. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, said Six counted. The next I don’t count, and stopped the ear-sentence short of the calling word. Jorra spoke to the wheel before any person, withheld the note he knew three turns ahead, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished.

On the seventeenth a voice with no owner came over the boards from the direction of Slate Row — a word of a room shut with the damper closed and a slate burned over a man nobody named, with no room named, no hand at the flame, no figure, no date and nobody standing in any doorway to be asked. The weather answered first and the price of barley after it, and the voice went up the terrace. The boy filed it beside the joining of the fifth of Ash, a further shaping and not a witness, not a sixth telling, not a payment; no payer stood under it. Jorra grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. At the well Ilva answered Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, her hand on her sleeve and the notched wood unread.
