A Hare Across the Shoulders

Days 300–302 · Bone 1–3, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A hare comes down off the ridge across a man’s shoulders and the yard asks what the carrying cost; the man tells it the weather. The kiln takes its coal before the basket is fed and gives four pulls where a man would look for five. Bone is a month of directions and not figures.

— A saying of the Clay Yard, told to the well and the kiln-mouth in Bone

— Day 300 —

Bone 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813

The hand had been laid down against the fold at the low angle of the loaf shelf as a man puts a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and it lay there still in the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof when the first of Bone came on the way cold comes into a banked house, taking the room one board at a time and asking nothing of the child in it. The boy came into his three hundredth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and twenty days were told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; the count had gone from two hundred and ninety-nine to three hundred the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. A hundred and seventy-four honest answers stood behind him, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid three hundred days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

Bone came on without a boundary drawn. The older women named the months by the weather and the slatemen named them by the number of Account-days, and the two habits went on side by side like two hands hauling on one rope, neither set right and neither left wrong in so many words; the month went the way Frost had gone out, asking nothing of the door it came in by. Firstday had no market, no reading at the Cross and no bell, and the district kept the face it turns to the world on any morning when nothing is due: a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the drying racks. Three hundred stood exact and complete and unspent, spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. The round figure was a threshold and not a payment. He noticed it the way a man notices the tenth instrument laid out on a tray and knows the set is whole without putting a hand to it, and no hand under any roof lifted it and spent it or said it over the basket. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted. That was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, giving off nothing anybody could hold.

The small hours smelled of cooled tallow and wet wool laid over tallow-heat, and under that the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house calls the thaw. Heat had gone out of the fold by degrees, as it goes out of a pot lifted off the coals and set on a cold stone: it left his cheek first and his temple next and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back last, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay. He lay where he had been laid and took the inventory in the order it presented itself, because that order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on the bed, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; he had once known it in a white room with a body on a table and knew it now under a roof of wet wool and cooled tallow, and the knowing did not change by one degree the two inches of air between his mouth and the crust. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches on the rim of the pot set beyond his hand. Both facts stood, and the second was louder, and neither bought the lifting of the crust. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter; the older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. A fire-sitter would have had to be invented to stand under the floor, or a nail driven to stand under the door, and neither was there; no house count came to be borrowed and no fifth pull was supplied to make the row come out even. The three stayed where they were, a rough music in three rooms that did not resolve, and the boy let them be three.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the market the morning before with not one hand in two hundred going to a face, stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, a heard thing beside a told thing, not folded into the three counts under the roof and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Frost’s first, second and third arithmetics, with their three true subtractions, rode locked up the clay road in a box toward High Kiln with one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between them had no signature on it. Three readings were three and not one account. He kept them on three places with a finger between every pair, and kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it — not a lesson, not a grievance he could carry up the road and set down at a door, not a mending, for nothing had been mended, and not a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of it. The finding was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he did not call it a virtue and did not spend it.

The dressed grain lay covered under wool at the rack end, turned on other mornings with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure, read over three times by numbers that did not know what it cost, the space between the figure and the price still unsigned. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the burned-slate word stood at four and the shut-room tellings at two; the three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood beside the eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair, three and not a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and nothing of that saying stood anywhere to be asked. A short strip of notched wood stayed unread in a sleeve not under this roof, the ninth hand stayed nine, and nine must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to these. The edges of the keepings had nearly touched on other days; the finger had gone between them and stayed there. He did not make a fourth likeness of the stayed frost, the skinned trough and the clouded plate of ice to call them a cause, and he did not make the house’s not-doings into a story about himself, and nothing in the keepings was brought into this morning as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment.

She came to the hearth before she came to the basket. That was the grammar of the Vail kiln-house, and it held at first light as strictly as at the going of the light or on an Account-day: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. Sella Vail told the coals it was the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. To the fire she gave the house’s old instruction in the words it had had from a man who was not named under this roof — Feed the fire before you feed yourself — and to the pot, Don’t set that near the door. You’ll pay for it in the thaw. Neither sentence was addressed to the basket. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting-go — and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and she did not look toward the bed.

The house kept its dead man the way it kept its heat: in an object and a saying, never in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella would let nobody fill it. His sayings had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and went to the coals again this morning with nothing in them that could be entered. The debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled; no collector had come up the clay road for it and none had gone down. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and the boy took the objects in their order under the fold as he took the body’s inventory, entering none of them.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour and a morning is not an opening. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next stood in Bone’s first firing if the household fired, earned by Bone’s own light, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay. The basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river’s note. From the next room, whose floor does not touch his, a small intake stood whole — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go, kept as one object on one note — and he held it against the kiln’s fourth low pull at the moment that pull gave, without carrying it into the silence where the fifth should have stood. Then he let it go whole on the same note, borrowing no house count and supplying no pull, and brought it into neither the missing fifth under the floor nor the bell’s gap left behind in its square nor the river’s low over lower under the door. The hundred and seventy-fifth answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon’s clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant’s louder want of the crust two inches off at his ear, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. Nothing bought by the checking could reach two inches.

Three hundred stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the first of Bone: no third reading into one account, no figure into a price with a signature under it, no bell’s gap into a count, no fourth likeness made a cause, no tenth stopping and no tenth hand stood up, no payer supplied to the floor. The three arithmetics stayed locked in the box up the road, the dressed grain stayed covered and unfigured, the three shapings stayed unpaid, the charcoal stayed beneath the shelf, the loan stayed uncalled and the promise stayed ungiven. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger’s width and nothing that had moved. The hand came down on the river’s low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.

The light had thinned without ending; it lay on the Clay Yard like tallow poured thin and gone cold, and under it the yard went about its ordinary work of not touching what it had agreed not to touch. Each got the look and the wide step, and Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody — Go around. It cost enough to come in. It’ll cost more to take out — and did not sweep any of them.

Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them. He kept them on three places of his own with a finger between every pair, and made no fourth likeness of them to call a cause, and named no payer to any. They were three, and a joining of any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood. The surgeon’s hand turned a wound to see the hidden part and turned it back; the boy’s hand lay outside the wool and went nowhere. He had once known the weight of a spleen in two hands and had now no hand to lift a corner of wool, and the knowing and the not-lying stood edge to edge with a finger between them and made no doctrine.

At the well a man come down from Cold Acre waited his turn with a hare across his shoulders. It was laid there the way a man lays a coat when his hands are full — the hind legs hanging along one collarbone, the head tucked in against the other, the weight settled where the shoulder could best carry it — and the yard judged the day’s carrying by the arm that had done it and not by any figure. The beast was lean and long-eared and stiff already in the dry cold, with the grey of the hill frost still caught in the fur at its throat. Nobody asked what it weighed. A man who had carried it from the ridge could have told you what it weighed in the only currency the yard used, which was the ache in the carrying arm, and that was a thing a man knew in his muscle and not in a slate.

A woman waiting with a pot at her hip looked at the hare and asked what the carrying cost. The man told her the weather. He did not say a figure and did not shape his mouth around one; he shifted the hare a hand’s breadth higher on his shoulder, as a man shifts a load that has begun to tell on him, and went off toward the north gate road with it, and the woman went on with her pot. Nobody turned twice. No slate was opened, no price was spoken, no name was given to the man or to the beast, and nobody followed him to see where the hare went. The month’s trade came into the yard as a direction and went out of it as a direction, judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and left no account behind it.

The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and took the exchange in its order, the way he took a wound apart by what was there and by what was not. A cost had been asked and answered with the weather; a carrying had been shown and weighed by an arm and not entered; a man had gone off toward a road and no one had made a procession of it. He did not join the hare to the missing hand, or to the covered grain at the rack end, or to the cold foot nobody here would warm, or to the silence where the fifth pull should have stood under the floor. A cost asked as a direction and answered with the weather left no entry; a hare carried across a man’s shoulders was a carrying and not a figure, and the winter’s hunting was a direction in the dark beyond the finger’s-width door and not a destination anyone had invited him to. He had once priced twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen which of them would live, and now the only arithmetic open to him was the two inches of air between his mouth and a crust gone leathery. The hare went over the shoulder and down the road, and nothing in the basket was the richer or the poorer for it.

At the kindling Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not. The small knife lay at the very edge with Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence beside it — the sentence about the man who was sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt — and neither was entered. He had told the wood the same thing every evening of this month and told it again now: Six counted. The next I don’t count, in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the other sentence came up to his teeth as it always did — the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it — and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stood there, with no figure on it and no name. He did not say the word. It was the ninth stopping, remembered, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; he drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it.

Yes, house, he said to the ones who wanted the thaw early, and Yes, house to the ones who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot. He went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and had not moved. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the small interval at the top of each reach showed where the breath should have gone in and had not. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on another day and nobody named it. Up in the Debt Loft, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and said nothing about the ear, and nobody asked him about the room.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door of the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men had come before first grey and gone low into the back room, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound, a door kept a finger’s width open and low voices that went into it and stayed there.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady, he told it, and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; the knowing was advanced and not paid, and he would not say it before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He read one piece of iron with two bites — the holding and the taking — and there was no Grathic word for both mercies, and he filed it joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

The second morning of Bone came into the Vail kiln-house the way the first had, by degrees and without announcement, taking the room one board at a time in the small hours while the boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf and nobody under any roof told him what had come. Three hundred and one stood exact and complete and unspent in the dark, spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody. The count had gone from three hundred to three hundred and one the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the join. Nothing from the yard of the day before came in with it, and nothing stood at the door asking to be taken in as a lesson or a sign. A morning is a room swept into before the day has put anything in it, and the surgeon took it in its own order, checking the small sounds first because the small sounds were the ones that would not lie.

There was the smell before there was the light: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under them the sour mineral breath off the unswept corner that the house called the thaw. The hearth had gone cold under its ash in the night and kept the shape of the coal that had been turned there. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and under the floor the carried fire answered in fours with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Behind the wall the kiln took its own breath in fours and missed one, and neither of them was a bell. Three counts in three places: the fire’s four low pulls and the gap, the river’s water over water, and the four nails on the near board of the boards and four on the next and the thumb-width knot where a fifth nail should have stood. They were three and they did not become one, because agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. The heard bell in its square stayed beside the told, and the told figure stayed beside the paid with the space between them unsigned. He kept the counts in three places with a finger between and let them lie.

The body he was in had come awake before the room had, and he took its inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at his cheek, his temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder, and the wanting went on under the knowing the way a coal goes on under ash.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and one days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. The house kept him in objects and not in a story: the coat on its peg behind the door, salt-stiff at the shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed with its hollow on the left that nobody filled; the clean dark line where Ash had turned its corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it; the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up the clay road and none had gone down it. His fire still missed its fifth under the floor, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it.

A surgeon knows the difference between a vessel opened and a vessel mended, and that the second is not the first with a nicer name put on it. An opening under a roof is a thing done and then left; the heat comes back, the household straightens around it, and the one who paid for the heat is not asked to stand forward. He had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and knew what a reconnection costs when it is done under a clamp, and he knew also that nobody in this room would call what was about to happen a reconnection at all. They would call it the first firing of Bone, and they would go on with their work. That was the whole of the event, and it was not an event, and the not-being was the thing he kept.

Heat comes back to the outside of a vessel before it comes back to the middle, and a room can look mended while the thing in it stays cold; he had watched that lie on a hundred faces in a ward where the lamps were good and the watchers were tired, and the face that seemed easier was often the face that was going. The firing would warm the pot and the boards and the air of the room by a degree or two, and the boy’s cheek would be as cold when it was done as it had been before the coal went under. The afterdrop waited past the warming nobody in this house would perform, and it did not occur to anybody under the roof to perform it. A firing is not a warming of a person, and a room’s heat is not the heat of the thing laid in it, and the two facts stood side by side with a finger between them and made no argument that anybody could hear.

Sella Vail went to the kiln mouth before she came to the basket. It was the first firing of the month, earned by that morning’s own light, and the house had kept itself ready for it the way a house keeps a thing ready without saying what it is ready for. She came across the fire room with her burn hand half closed, the little-door-shaped burn on the back of her left hand shutting on nothing, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light. She did not look toward the bed.

Her hand went to the widow’s mark on its cord at her throat — a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb — and held it one breath, and let go. The breath was the whole of it: one counted breath, no more and no longer, and then the hand came away from the cord and went to the clay lid. She lifted the lid with the back of a hand, not the fingers, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took.

It answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and one days. Nobody praised the opening. Nobody named what it mended, entered a slate or made a lesson of it; nobody raised a hand to a face or said a number over the basket. The household straightened around the sound the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and went on with what it had been doing. Garl’s proverb went to the fire in the house’s improved voice — Feed the fire before you feed yourself. It’ll still be there in the morning. You won’t — and never to a person, and no one heard in it anything that had been paid.

Five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The four behind — at one hundred and fifty, one hundred and eighty, two hundred and forty and two hundred and seventy-one — stood where they stood and were not looked at again as signs, payments or menders. A fifth opening did not make a sixth due, and no hand under any roof counted toward the next one. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour: a shelf, a well, a rack, a basket, a yard, a morning, a hearth, an afternoon and a going of the light were none of them openings, and the mark hung where it hung through all of them. He took the order of her reaching the way he took the order of reaching at a well, by what was there and by what was not — hand to the mark, then hand to the lid, the breath counted between and the coal going under the pot — and filed no tenth hand over it. A woman touching a mark before a kiln is not a hand at a slate, and nothing came off the clay but the firing.

He kept the fifth opening for the far-off day a house might be left where it is and still kept, and did not bring that day nearer. It stood as an act and not a promise. It was not hoped for, not counted toward and not spent; five were five and four were four and neither number stood up and called itself a rule. The house mended what it had to mend by doing it and saying nothing, and the boy set the kept act on a place of its own without putting a name on what it mended, for a name would have wanted a payer and none stood to it.

At the very moment of the opening the boy took a small intake from the next room, whose floor does not touch his. It came through the wall as a thread of sound comes through cloth — a woman’s breath at her own work, the small intake and the hold of it — and he held it against the first low pull of the fire without carrying it into the missing fifth. He borrowed no house count and supplied no pull. The hand went down on the river’s note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap.

The hundred and seventy-seventh answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was a restraint and not a payment, a key, a gift or a rule. He had held one thing whole at the very instant another thing happened and had not laid the two together, and the not-laying was the answer: an intake kept on its own note, a pull counted where it stood and the silence between the two left silent. No payer was named to either. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the coordination; the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand that could close two inches on a pot handle. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument itself was the work, and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing he had done could reach two inches.

He kept the fifth opening on a place of its own with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and did not lay it across the house’s not-doings or across the dressed grain covered under wool at the rack end. A joining would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either. The opening was a kept act and not a settlement; it had mended nothing anybody had named, and the account it might have settled stood where it had stood before the coal went under the pot. He set it beside the four behind it and gave it no figure, and the finding was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air.

The surgeon in him read the opening the way he had once read a vessel taken back into circulation after a clamp: a controlled reconnection, the way opened slowly, the return of heat from outside, the response of the vessel under the hand, the complication held ready for the moment it should arrive. A reconnection is paid for by somebody, and here the warmth that came back into the pot had been paid for by a man nobody in the room would name, three hundred and one days ago and every day since. Knowing that changed nothing about the distance between his mouth and the crust. He had once held pressure on a graft until the vessel took and had known exactly what the taking cost and who bore it; now he lay under a roof where the taking and the cost were both done to him by people who did not know there was a surgeon in the basket. The clamp in this house was a widow’s mark touched for one breath and let go. It was mercy and mutilation in the same iron gesture, and there was no word in Grathic for both mercies, and no slate on which to enter the one without the other.

Once he had had a tray laid out and a room full of people who would put an instrument in his hand before he had finished asking for it, and a field he could open and close with a knowledge that had cost twelve years to buy. Now the only thing that answered him was a hand that could go up an inch and stay for a small intake, and he had learned to take what it gave without asking it to be more than it was. The opening under the roof had happened at the very instant the hand had taken its intake, and the two things stood side by side on two places with a finger between them, not a cause and not a consequence, not a sign and not a cure. He had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen which of them would live; here the whole of his practice was to hold one small sound on one small note and let it go whole, and to call nothing that happened under the roof a lesson written for his benefit.

When the fire had taken she went back to the ordinary work of the house. The house will keep it, she said to the coals where another woman would have said I, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and kept her finger above the charcoal so the tally underneath stayed unread. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay, and gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket. The grey streak at her temple went pale off the low light and was gone back into the coal-black hair when she turned away. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

Three hundred and one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger’s width and nothing that had moved. The fifth opening stood on its own place with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and neither was laid across the other, and the hand came down on the river’s low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap. The clay lid was held in the back of her hand over the broken pot while the fire took in fours, and the fifth stayed a silence under the floor.

The afternoon of the second of Bone thinned without ending, the way heat goes out of a pot lifted off the coals and set upon a cold stone; it did not leave all at once but drew back by degrees, from the lip first, then from the belly, then from the foot where the stone took what was left, till the pot stood where it had stood and was only less warm than it had been. Sella Vail carried the boy down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the fold laid over his ears against the river wind, the instruction going to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on Secondday, and the district kept the face it turns to a day when nothing is due: a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. The morning’s firing lay behind them and was not looked at. It had been a thing done and then left, like a pot mended and set back on its shelf, and nobody had lifted it and carried it into the afternoon to show what it had mended.

She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the Ash ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the skinned water in the trough; the field’s empty place out on the worked strips; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each got its look and its wide step. She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody — Go around. It cost enough to come in. It’ll cost more to take out — and did not sweep any of them, and no figure came off any of them into the afternoon.

On the north lip of the yard well the stayed frost-bloom kept its white fronds long past the hour when it should have gone with the sun; the trough wore its skin from edge to edge; and the plate of ice lifted off the water stood on the flat stone by the well out of the path of feet, clouded with the silt that had risen into it as it froze. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them. He kept them on three places of his own with a finger between every pair, and made no fourth likeness of them to call a cause, and named no payer to any. The stayed frost was a covering laid over a depth; the skinned water was the same covering laid over a different depth; the clouded plate was a piece of the depth lifted up and set where no foot would go. They were three, and a joining of any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood. He did not call the weather a kindness, or a promise, or the face of anything that had been left; it was weather, and out of the path of feet.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth, and she lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning’s, lighter than the day before, and gave it no figure. Three readings had gone over it in the district and not one had known what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and the grain lay where it lay under the wool, changed in state and unfigured. The surgeon’s hand turns a wound to see the hidden part and turns it back; the boy’s hand lay outside the wool and went nowhere. He had once known the weight of a spleen in two hands and now had no hand to lift a corner of wool, and the knowing and the not-lying stood edge to edge with a finger between them and made no doctrine. He did not lay the covered grain across the morning’s opening, or across the house’s not-doings, or across his own kept gaps; a joining would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to any.

Heat had been drawing back from him all afternoon the way it goes out of a dish lifted off the coals and set down on stone. It left his cheek first, then his temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay. He took the inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; the knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the handle of the basket that held him. At the well Ilva was drawing with the pail against her knee. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the drying racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out; her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft. The rope at the well had stiffened in the dry cold and gave a small dry rasp as the bucket came up, and she went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all. She was of the firing work and not of a house.

A woman waiting with a pot asked what the month’s firings had taken, now the household had fired in Bone and the first opening of the month stood behind it. Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of notched birch lay where it lay in the wood, not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted and not joined to the open slate standing on the stone lip a pace away; no figure came off it. Her eyes went to the asker after the hand had settled, and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word. Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, she said, and put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail. Nobody turned twice.

The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and took the order of reaching as he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. Hand toward the record; thumb over the notches; eyes to the asker; and only then the mouth. He had seen that order in a midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before the crowd, in a keeper’s finger beneath a board; and here it came again from the yard’s firing work and not from any house or Court. He filed it as the ninth such hand, on a place of its own beside the eighth with a finger between every pair, and he filed no tenth. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it, and nothing of the well went under the roof as a lesson, a figure, a mending, a cause or a payment. He did not make her a reader of her own keeping, and did not join the strip in her sleeve a pace away to the morning’s opening, or to the three shapings of a well-saying with no owner standing to be asked, or to the charcoal beneath the shelf. Nine hands were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. She did not know she had been read, and went on with her water.

At the kindling Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not. The small knife lay at the very edge with Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence beside it — the sentence about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt — and neither was entered. He told the wood what he told it every evening: Six counted. The next I don’t count, in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the other sentence came up to his teeth as it always did, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it, and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stood there with no figure and no name; he did not say the word. It was the ninth stopping, remembered, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it.

Yes, house, he said to those who wanted the thaw early, and Yes, house to those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot; he went on wanting the late one because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the small interval at the top of each reach showed where the breath should have gone in and had not. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped on another day and nobody named it. Up in the Debt Loft, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the road and none went down it. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said nothing about the ear, and nobody asked him about the room.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door of the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men had come before first grey and gone low into the back room, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound, a door kept a finger’s width open and low voices that went in and stayed. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady. To a pot he said You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid; he would not say it before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He read one piece of iron with two bites — the holding and the taking — and there was no Grathic word for both mercies, and he filed it joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket or the bed, and the boy made no fourth likeness and laid the withheld note across nothing in the yard.

Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the going of the light on the third of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no bell’s gap folded into a count, no fifth pull supplied, no collector on the road, no sign taken from the opening or from the hare already gone into the weather. Bone 4 and Bone 10 stood ahead in the dark beyond the finger’s-width door and were not brought into a day that had not earned them.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved. The river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt on the flat stone out of the path of feet, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.

The Chronicle of Chapter 83

Bone came on without a boundary drawn, as Frost had gone out, and the district wore its face for a morning when nothing is due. Under the Vail kiln-house roof the boy came into his three hundredth day; the count went to three hundred the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock, and nobody told him what it counted. He kept the round figure, a threshold, and the carried fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed.

He took the body in its old order: a stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third to even the row; the thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on the pot handle.

That afternoon a hunter come down from Cold Acre waited at the well with a hare across his shoulders, the weight judged by the carrying arm. A woman asked the cost; he told her the weather and went off toward the north gate road. At the end of the day the hand held a small intake against the kiln’s fourth low pull and let it go whole: the hundred and seventy-fifth answer, worth nothing at two inches of air.

On the second morning Sella Vail went to the kiln mouth before the basket. She touched the widow’s mark at her throat for one breath, lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot. The fire took and answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Nobody praised the opening or made a lesson of it; five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The boy held a woman’s intake against the first low pull and did not carry it into the missing fifth. In the afternoon Ilva, asked at the well what the firings had taken, said, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. Her strip stayed in her sleeve; the boy filed no tenth hand.

On the third day the white went off the north lip of the well while the yard was looking elsewhere; the trough kept its skin and the silt-clouded plate stood out of the path of feet, and the yard called it weather. He set a held interval beside an empty interval and put a finger between; at the very end a small intake stood whole and was let go on the same note. The count closed at three hundred and two, told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid as many days, and Garl’s name unspoken under the roof. The hand laid itself back against the fold, and the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap.
