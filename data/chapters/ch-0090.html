What the Fingers Let Fall

Days 321–323 · Bone 22–24, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A handful lifted and let fall back tells you the grain is dry. It tells you nothing of the hands that sowed it, and nothing of what the season will ask for it.

— A saying of the drying racks, told in the last tend of Bone

— Day 321 —

Bone 22, Secondday, Y.H. 813

The hand came down against the fold and lay there as a man lays a tool on a bench, not because the work was finished but because the next hand would find it without looking. That was all. There was no small sound to mark the putting-down, and the room went on with its own quiet, and the boy let the quiet be. Three hundred and twenty exact stood behind him and three hundred and twenty-one stood in front, and neither was spoken, and six months and one hundred and forty-one days were told by nobody under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and twenty-one days; the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg, and the proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and the name of the man the coat kept had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. Nothing from Bone 20’s reading came in as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment, and the reading was not read again. Bone went on without a boundary drawn. The older women of the district named the month by the weather that had not come and the slatemen named it by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong under the roof; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it.

He took the body as a wound is taken, in its old order, because the order was the only thing in the room that had not changed. One stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool as though a hand had lifted it and set it down again badly; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in the small hours to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the ease that cold puts on a face was not ease but a composition, and that a patient who looks settled at the end of a line may be worse before he is better: a cold vessel cannot clot, and must be warmed from the outside, and slowly, never rubbed, never set too close to the fire. He knew it as exactly as he had once known where the third rib would tear the lung, and the knowing bought him nothing at two inches of air. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won; the argument between them was the work, and it went on in the dark without a verdict and without a witness.

The body humiliated him in small, exact ways that nobody in the house thought to call humiliating. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and he could not lift his own chin; he had packed cavities with both hands and he could not close two inches on the pot handle; he lay in a basket and was carried like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and the discussion of him went on over his head in the third person while he understood every word and could make none of them. The old registers went on working without an audience. A man who had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds now sorted the sounds in a room he could not sit up in, and the sorting was honest and bought nothing, which was the joke the body played and the joke he had learned to find funny without laughing.

Before the light he read the room by its small sounds, the way a bay is read before the patient is touched, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones generally do. The boards above him held their heat badly and gave it back by degrees; somewhere under the floor the fourth pull came and the fifth did not; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. He had learned to let the sounds come in the order they arrived and not to arrange them, because arranging them wanted a hand to put under them and none stood.

Sella Vail went to the hearth before she went to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order before any of the days the boy could count, and she kept it the way another woman might keep a habit of crossing herself. The coals were grey under their blind ash and held a little heat at the heart of them, and she told them it was the thaw, in the flat tone she used for the pot and the broom and the wool, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so that the ash fell where she meant it to fall. The house will keep it, she said to the fire, where another woman would have said I, and the house kept it, and the coals answered four low pulls from under the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted something under her breath — a small intake, a hold, and a letting-go — toward no opening, and she gave neither gap a figure. The grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn.

At the loaf shelf her finger lay on top of the front edge, resting there a little longer than the checking needed, so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps in it, and she figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze on its shelf by the wall, and shut her hand half over nothing, the burn on the back of her left hand — two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside puckered and pale — closing on air the way a little door closes on a room nobody goes into. She did not touch the widow’s mark. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood far off in a later month’s first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one, she said to the pot, and Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, she said to the fire, and never to a person. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them. The crack showed. Repair was one thing and a whole vessel was another, and the house had long ago decided which of the two it would keep.

He read the pot the way he had once read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen: the first staple set honestly, the second wire drawn tighter than it needed and left longer, the hairline of the original break showing dark between them. A vessel that has been mended carries its break and is the truer for showing it, which was the whole of the proverb and was not a lesson anybody had paid for. The man who had first said it had gone into the Long Cold and had not come out, and the sayings had gone on being said to the pot and the fire and had not mended him. Nobody under the roof had made a doctrine of this either, and the boy did not.

She turned the dressed grain beneath the wool at the rack end with a touch lighter than the last time and gave no figure to it, and the boy noted the place and the touch and put them where he put other things that had not yet been asked to mean anything. The grain lay under the wool away from the hearth, dry and faintly rasping, and nothing about it that morning lifted a hand or let anything fall. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat before the setting and no look toward the bed, and went on to the next thing as though the half-beat had not been spent.

Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed for three hundred and twenty-one days; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, and was not numbered among the hearings at all. Three counts in three places. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them, so the three stayed where they were, side by side and not joined.

The surgeon in him knew what a house would have done with three counts in three places: laid two of them together, called the agreement a cause and put a man under it to pay. The district did this at the Cross with figures and called it a reading; the house did it at the shelf with two gaps and left them unfigured. He had spent a life watching institutions make one account out of several hands, and had signed one of those accounts himself, and the small exact keeping of three separate counts was the nearest honest work he had left.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that came out of vessels; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, and the told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between. Five arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure: one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, five readings five and not one account, their true subtractions riding locked up the road in a box nobody under this roof would open, and the finding kept on a place of its own with no payer under it. It was not laid across the house’s not-doings, or across the dressed grain under wool, or across the four held changes on their four places, or across the two small objects of the hand, or across his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause; the finger went between.

The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap, the staying of Bone 15 and the early letting-go of Bone 17 two objects on two places with a finger between them and between them and everything else there, neither made again, neither a habit or a way or a fifth held change, both wanting a payer that did not stand. Two hundred and sixteen honest answers stood on the sixth place when the light came, each an object and not a power, and the number was spoken by nobody.

He turned the file over in his mind the way he had once turned instruments out on a towel at the end of a list, asking nothing of any of it. An honest answer was a thing done and seen and entered where nobody could read it: the hand up an inch, the intake, the interval held whole, the letting-go on the note, the hand down. It was a restraint and not a power, an object and not a gift, and it had no payer and wanted none. The sixth place was not a shelf, a slate or a bank but a space he kept in the dark where no collector could enter and no Court could tax.

First light came into the room without a market, a Cross or a bell, an ordinary Secondday going on about its own work the way water goes over water. No slate was opened within reach of the basket and no figure came near his hand. He had stopped expecting to be told what any number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and he kept the count because attention was the one thing no institution could tax.

At the going of the water, when the river’s note had come down to its lowest under the finger’s-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath held for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He had merely put his small hand into the order of things for the length of one note and taken it out again, and the order went on whether he did it or not. That was the whole of what an answer was here, and the clean exactness of it was the only thing in the room that had not been entered by somebody else. He filed it as answer two hundred and seventeen: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the five arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. It had not exposed anything anybody would have named. The infant underneath saw only a hand that had tested a thing and kept it, and wanted the crust lifted two inches and got no lifting of it.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed for three hundred and twenty-three days; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door the river kept water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was not numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. The bell’s gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The twice-mended pot stood where Sella had set it with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, repair kept apart from a whole vessel and neither called the other by a name.

At the very end of the day, with the yard quiet and the river’s note at its lowest under the finger’s-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath held for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and twenty-two: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the five arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and twenty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took a clean satisfaction in the field left as it was found, and the infant underneath wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped and got neither, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won. Three hundred and twenty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the coat kept was not spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the twenty-fourth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The five arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure; the burned slate waited at five with no sixth; the strip stayed in the sleeve; the grain lay covered and unfigured; the handful stayed one object on its own place. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place still unnamed and no third, and the hand lay back against the fold while the river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door and Bone went on without a boundary drawn, six mornings short of the month’s last reading and not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The Chronicle of Chapter 90

Three days went over the Vail kiln-house in the last tend of Bone and left the house as they found it. The boy came into his three hundred and twenty-first morning with the hand laid down on answer two hundred and sixteen, a tool put away, and went out of the twenty-fourth with answer two hundred and twenty-two laid down in the same way. Between them six small intakes were taken from the room whose floor does not touch his and let go whole on the river’s note, six honest answers entered where nobody could read them, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

Nothing was carried across as a sign. The reading of Bone 20 was not read again; one in twelve stood unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, and five arithmetics of the year rode up the clay road in a locked box under a space nobody had signed. The boy kept the finding on a place of its own.

Sella kept the grammar of the house: fire before basket, The house will keep it where another woman would have said I, the twice-mended pot lifted on the thumb at the crack, the crack left showing, the pot rim set two inches beyond the reaching hand, the widow’s mark untouched because a morning and a hearth are not openings. On the twenty-second, at the rack end, she lifted the wool, took a small handful of dressed grain and let it fall back through her fingers instead of spreading it. She said nothing, gave no figure, and did not do it again. The surgeon knew the lift of a dressing’s edge and the letting of it fall; the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches and got no lifting of it, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won.

In the yard she went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step, and the three coverings at the well stayed weather entered nowhere. At the well on the twenty-third Ilva drew with the pail against her knee, and when a woman asked what the firings of Bone had taken, her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed; the notched strip stayed in wood, and she answered, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. The boy filed her reaching as a ninth hand and no tenth. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, his ear-sentence stopped at the word that would call the loan. Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence. Nobody opened a slate.

Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The handful stayed one object, the stayed inch and the early letting-go stayed two, and three hundred and twenty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody while Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
