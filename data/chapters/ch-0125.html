A Finger’s Width Short

Days 426–428 · Silt 2–4, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

A finger that goes to the shelf and toward the ear is still a finger short of the gap; the gap keeps its own name.

— A saying of the Low Kiln basket-women at the loaf shelf

— Day 426 —

Silt 2, Y.H. 814, Secondday

Small hours held under the Vail roof with nobody to name them, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put down where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and twenty-five exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-five days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on in the dark the way a coal goes on under ash, with nobody turning it. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. It was a new day. Nothing had come into it from the day behind — not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The dark at the finger’s-width door thinned by no degree anybody measured, and above and behind him the loft kept whatever small sound it kept and did not repeat it.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it three nights ago and would not try now. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it.

Under the exactness, louder because it had a body and the exactness had none, the wanting went on. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle — three small imports, three things a man could do with a wrist and a cloth and a reach of two inches, and he had none of the three. The surgeon said what the body was and where the cold had stopped; the infant said what it wanted and would not be argued out of it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. No verdict was looked for and the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in the dark in a body that could not lift a crust two inches to its own mouth.

The horn of the crust and the cold at the wrist brought him one thing from a life nobody under this roof had heard of, and it came without being called and went back the same way, owing nothing. He had once stood in a warm room beside a man on a table whose fingers had gone the colour of old rope while the room insisted the man was warm, because the room was warm and the room could not be wrong. He had known the order of it the way a potter knows the order of a firing: the extremities first, the core holding out longer than the face would suggest, the pink returning at the surface last of all and lying about what lay beneath. A cold patient cannot clot; a man who is cooling cannot be hurried back, and rubbing a frozen foot does more harm than leaving it alone. None of it had ever been said or asked under this roof; he kept it where he kept the rest, a finding with no payer standing under it.

Above and behind him the loft kept its sound. He did not put an ear to the floor; a hearing is not the same as a sound, and six hearings stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and the Drawing’s flat knock outside it. He did not make a seventh of the small sound over his head. The dark gave way by degrees nobody marked, and the room took on its own shape again — shelf, board, pot, bed — and nobody came to tell the basket what the morning was.

First grey came without anybody opening a door for it. Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as though nothing had turned in the night. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said The house will keep it to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He watched the count the way he had once watched hands in a bay whose doors would not stay shut, reading the work and not the woman, and the reading was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a second day of Silt that had earned only its second. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this morning.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline as he read all honest seams, with the eye of a man who had set a clamp and known the difference between what holds and what only looks joined. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought this absence a payment, a name or a mending.

She put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, turned the dressed grain under wool at the rack end with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and no figure or handful, set each piece where it had lain, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder hung on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody was allowed to fill it; the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey called the thaw beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years; the unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. His name had been unspoken under this roof four hundred and twenty-five days and the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Nobody lifted the boards to supply the pull, and nobody said the man’s name to make the two counts agree.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. In the Clay Yard she went wide around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. No name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost; the wide step was a keeping and not a sign, and a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood.

The small sounds came first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on the flat stone by the well with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger’s width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist, eye, mouth and the half-beat in the gap, and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stands.

At the trough the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and he did not lay it across the loaf’s stopping or the ridge at the Vail corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated and he read it without a figure. The line at the corner had two greys in it and a finger between: the clean dark with the finer grey the yard called the thaw, and the grey of the ridge the yard had called gone and left where it lay. A thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off the river was less cold at one edge where the low note of water over water ran nearer under the winter skin, and the tang came thinner there. He found the softening where put and called it weather — not a sign, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a payment or a custom on a second day of Silt, not looked twice or made again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at the yard well opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price. The dressed grain lay covered under wool at the rack end and a figure read over it would not know what it cost, the ink-neat space between figure and price carrying no signature. Two women at the rail said the rope would take a finger if a body was careless and that cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and went back to their pails; another said the cold had got into the rope and would not come out before the thaw, and another said a rope that held was a rope that held. He gave the women no interior, the words no owner beyond the mouths they came out of and the half-beat no intention.

Nothing from the day behind crossed under the yard’s grammar as a lesson. Tarn’s withheld sentence was found where put and not made a habit, and Jorra Vail’s promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away was the whole of the checking. He cut nothing, counted nothing beyond the six, spent nothing; he said Six counted. The next I don’t count to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended, the Seed 16 breath unspent, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said Yes, house to the fire, and did not turn his head toward the landing. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and came out without the extra holding of Ash 26, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. On a wrong wind the sweetish edge of lead glaze came up from High Kiln and arrived nowhere. He’s not Vail stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

A pail went down on the flat stone by the well with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining as the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger’s width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist, eye, mouth and the half-beat in the gap — the knock before the pail’s settling, the rasp before the rope’s quiet, the fist before the eye, the eye before the mouth — and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stood. A pail is a pail. A rope is a rope. The finger’s width of air between a loaf and where a loaf had been bought no warmth and had no owner, and he wrote no cause across it.

Women came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Fourthday grammar of hand before eye and mouth after both; none looked into the wicker, and none gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights, for a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer.

He went once around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, for a second circuit would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. the returning-line place was widest of all, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back; no name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. At the trough the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and he did not lay it across the loaf’s stopping or the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated, and level is not honest and a level is not a promise.

The line at the Vail corner had two greys in it and a finger between. The clean dark kept its finer hairline called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years; beside it the grey of the ridge lay left in place, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The yard had called the ridge gone in the dark before the year turned and had left it where it lay, and the calling and the leaving stayed on two places with a finger between, the one no mending of the other. The air off the river was less cold at one edge where the low note of water over water ran nearer under the winter skin, and the tang came thinner there. He found the softening where put and called it weather — not a sign, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a payment, not a custom on a fourth day of Silt, not looked twice or made again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it. There was no market, no Cross, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its fourth; the three readings and the two sightings stayed behind where they stood and did not come under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and Silt 10 waited six mornings off as a date and was not brought.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at the yard well opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price, and carried the note off to the pot where it belonged. The dressed grain lay covered under wool at the rack end and a figure read over it would not know what it cost, the ink-neat space between figure and price carrying no signature. He took the three apart by their order — fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap; errand, note, pot, no price under the note; figure, space, no hand at the signature — and filed each on a place of its own. An arriving nowhere is not a refusal and not a payment; a note is not a price; a figure that does not know what the grain cost is a figure with a hole in it, and a hole buys no warmth.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder hung on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody was allowed to fill it; the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years; the twice-mended pot on the pot board kept the hairline of the original break between the older brass staple and the tighter wire; the unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. His name had been unspoken under this roof four hundred and twenty-eight days and the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Neither hairline nor sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending, and nobody lifted the boards to supply the pull.

Tarn and Jorra were offstage and found where put. Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones with six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, his sentence about an ear stopped at the word that would call the loan, the loan uncalled in a term already ended and the Seed 16 breath unspent; his valuable ear was exposure and not safety, and the fears he had were his own and not a lesson for any basket. Jorra Vail was at the Vail wheel-shed and the cold the damper with meal-fine grey dust past his bent finger and men unnamed in the back room, the promise to the men who burn slates neither given nor withdrawn; he had known the full note a pot would give three turns ahead and had kept the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early. The two quiet men stayed unjoined, and their silences were maintenance and not a compact, and nothing under the shed or at the kindling had been mended by the seeing.

Ilva was offstage with her count still in wood, the strip of notched birch in her sleeve unread and her hand ninth with no tenth; the offered figure and its return kept their place beside her hand and the fifth shaping of the well-saying, not offered again, not a custom, not a reader made and not joined to anything. Hessa Rale’s narrow slate stayed unread up the clay road with her eighth hand eighth, and the soft-cuffed clerk’s entered figure stayed a report brought up the yard from the out-of-line landing below the north bridge, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. Berr Clay was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his your house and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed.

The first man’s face, the second man’s direction and turned slate, the asking answered with the weather, the three likenesses of Slate Row, the two market sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either, the green at a pot board and the shape at the market’s edge with no eighth, the softening at the river’s skin kept as weather, and the neighbour’s near-reaching at the shelf filed on its own place: each kept its place with a finger between every pair, not repeated on a day that had earned only its fourth, not made a custom, a sixth shaping, a witness, a reader or a payer. The burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions stood where put, gathering interest against a promise neither given nor withdrawn. No tenth hand, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic, eighth opening, sixth hearing, signature, payer, witness or reader stood on this afternoon, no loan was called, and no slate was opened within reach of the basket.

The going of the light came on without a bell and without a reading, as a going of the light comes under a roof that has kept its hours in loaves and glances. Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said The house will keep it to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had once worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie; if the number came back wrong somebody looked under the drape. Here a woman counted spoons and nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Fourthday that had earned only its fourth. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this evening.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, because a line that looks joined can still be a line that will not hold; and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. Tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool went along under everything, and under them the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, carried the way she carried the mark, without comment.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, horn-hard in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.
