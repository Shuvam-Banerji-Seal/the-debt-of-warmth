What the Roof Kept

Days 204–206 · Ember 25–27, Y.H. 813 · The Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln

The coat keeps a man’s shape and the seat keeps his weight; the debt against the tiles keeps his name without ever saying it. Go around all three with a broom, and leave the crack showing.

— A saying of the Vail kiln-house in the kiln month

— Day 204 —

Ember 25, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)

He lay where the fold had put him down, in the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, and the day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound. The smell was hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that the house called the thaw, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season’s name instead of a cause. The kiln drew in fours beyond the wall. Then the gap. The carried fire under the floor gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by any decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible; a bell is rung and counted and answered, but the space where a fifth pull should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and four days, and no payer had been named to it. He had learned to take the gap as the sound and the four as the thing that led up to it. The tend had turned four mornings back and nobody under this roof had drawn a line under the turning; a household that keeps its days in loaves and glances has no use for a boundary a slate would call a week.

He was two hundred and four days old and six months and twenty-four days, and nobody said so. Nobody under any roof knew that a number had been reached, or that there was a thing in the world which a number could reach. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it: the wanting had gone quiet, the way a door left unlatched goes quiet in a wind that has stopped blowing, and the door was still unlatched all the same. Two hundred and four stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, with nothing done to it and nothing taken from it; the carried fire’s fifth had had no payer for two hundred and four days, and the two counts lay in him side by side without touching, one kept by a man who could not say it and one kept by a house that did not know it kept anything.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be, for a thing counted becomes a thing claimed and a thing claimed wants a name at the foot of it. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Under the floor, four and a silence; through the door, water going over water; above his face, four, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; the boy had spent two hundred and four days declining to supply one, which was not a power, not a finding and not a conclusion, but only the order in which he laid the things down and refused to stack them.

The one brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month, and its empty place beside it was still without a name. No third had come to lie with them. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the cold and was unwiped, because nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried to save and cannot: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. All of that, exact and complete, lay two paces from a pot handle he could not close his hand upon. The body humiliated him and he let it, filing the humiliation with the rest, worth nothing at two paces and worth everything to the man who had no other instrument.

Before the grey he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel — no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger’s width of door, the district’s slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, from a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh came in two hundred and four days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, and the second reading of Ember had gone up the clay road in Berr Clay’s locked box with the true subtraction inside and was still not the figure paid, while Hessa Rale’s slate lay unread at Greybank with the writing turned to her sleeve and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called.

Nine stoppings stood on their widened tray, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned. The Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach and the loan that can hear was uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161 in the iron, the beat that stayed under the loaf shelf, the flagstone half-check made in Tallow 22; the small dry scrape from the Debt Loft stood on a fourth, the pale line at the gum on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth. The crust’s gap, the shelf’s two unnamed gaps, the pan’s gone round ring and the field’s empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap come, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawing the line. The charcoal lay unread beneath the shelf, the warm stone cold and unreconciled in its loft pot, the widow’s mark where it had hung, and the cost of the man carried out entered nowhere. Exact, complete, worth nothing. He put the file away in the same order, which was the whole of the discipline, and went on lying still.

In the morning Sella Vail carried him out from under the low roof, and the keeping of Garl Vail went past them in objects before either of them reached the yard. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door and held a broader man’s shape, the salt still printed at the shoulders where a working man had leaned into wet clay year after year; nobody took it down and nobody put another coat in its place. The seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year leftward lean, hollow on one side as a bowl is hollow where a thumb has rested, and Jorra had spent three years trying to fill the hollow with his own weight and had not filled it. Along the skirting board the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay beside a new hairline of grey, finer than the old, and the house called the new one the thaw and swept neither; a steward with a broom would have made one line of the two and called it dealt with, but this house went around them with a wide step and no number, and had done so since the winter Garl died. His sayings had gone to the pot and stayed there: feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled. None of it was made a lesson. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it had lain since the Long Cold, circled without a name, worse for being uncalled because it could neither be paid nor settled nor stopped. No collector came up the clay road to call it, and no collector was called to it; Garl was not collected, and the house kept him the way a pot keeps the hand that threw it, by holding the shape after the hand has gone.

❦

At the hearth she gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and the running water and the river wind, and never once to the basket under her arm. She said the house where another woman would say I, and kept her half-pace, and when she glanced at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on anything that came within reach of it. Then the hand went back to its work. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She fed him barley gruel from the house’s own dish, two fingers behind his ear, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not his mouth, so the thread at his mouth kept its frozen place from the small hours into the morning and past it. She moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row’s end and the palm’s breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on top of the shelf’s front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and whatever lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting go — and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every morning of the boy’s life. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. She did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, afternoon or dusk since; a morning was not an opening, and there had been no opening for twenty-four days. The instruction went to the fire and the wool and never to the child lying under the roof receiving it, and the boy, who had spent a life in rooms where a man is named before he is treated, filed the order without approving it and without calling it a slight. It was the household’s grammar. A pot was told what it wanted; a child was told nothing, and the warmth arrived all the same.

At the kindling the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. Seven years had been measured out on that sill; six were counted and entered, the seventh stood at the lip with its edge over the dark and nothing under it, nearer its end than it had ever stood and not given. Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said Yes, house to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it at the same time is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road that morning. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

Three or four unnamed men went low into the finger’s-width-open the back room before first grey and got no name from anybody, and the two tellings of a counting room shut in a war stayed two with no third; the boy did not join the men to the tellings, or the tellings to the kiln, or any of them to the room behind the wheel-shed door he could not enter. At the wheel-shed Jorra Vail came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel and kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they’re steady.” He told a pot, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld it from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of lead glaze came up off High Kiln on that wrong wind and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. When Sella’s voice went low and unfinished at the hearth — The fire’s low. It wants— — his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached feeding and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in Grathic that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Asked in the morning, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger’s-width door and came down at once against the fold. The asking was not always answered; the answer this morning was an inch let go early and no failure in it, an object on its own tray beside the other answers the body had given on other mornings and in other small hours, filed on the sixth place with nothing laid across it — not a hearing, water over water remaining a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule, with no payer named to it. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number anybody would have believed. The market did not get to read the inch; no slate in the district would take it, and no hand under this roof had seen it go up.

The morning went on around the small refusal. The salt coat kept its broader shape on the peg and the seat below it kept a hollow no living man had filled. Two hundred and four stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody; the fire’s fifth had had no payer for two hundred and four days, the crust had its empty place still unnamed, the charcoal lay unread, the mark hung where it hung, the stone lay cold in its loft pot and the loan that could hear was uncalled. The river went on over water without noticing that an inch had been offered to it and taken back, and the fold took the hand without a word. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward.

The afternoon thinned without ending. It did not go the way a slate goes, clean across with a sound, but wore away at the edges, the light leaving the high air first and settling what was left of it into the low places, so that the room kept its warmth in the hollow beneath the loaf shelf long after the rafters had gone grey. The boy lay at the low angle where the shelf’s underside becomes a roof, and that roof stopped at the edge of his reach, as if the heat too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it and would go no further, however much he wanted it. The last warmth of the day came down the underside in a slow slant and stopped where the roof stopped, and the small bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back knew the stopping before his mind could file it. Nothing under this roof had ever told the warmth to come an inch closer, and the warmth had never come.

He set the coat, the seat, the two lines, the proverb and the uncalled debt on separate places and laid no finger across them, because five likenesses standing up together will call themselves a rule, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

A neighbour came to the door in the afternoon with a cost-question about the firing, the last tend being in, and held a pot against her hip while she asked what the kiln would want before the kiln month was out. Sella did not take the question into her hands. She told the woman to ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there, and when the woman would have gone on she gave her the weather and the river, and the question went away level, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered. The woman went off with her pot and her question still whole in it, and the boy, lying at the low angle with the fold at his ear, watched the shape of the asking and the shape of the answer and found them the same shape the house used for everything: a thing arriving as a direction, going off as a direction, and arriving nowhere. This was the household’s whole method for staying warm. It was not honest and it was not a lie; it was the grammar the pot was spoken in, and a child who had once been paid to make such grammars agree knew better than to ask who was owing.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water, so that the day ended between two smells he knew and neither of which had ever asked his name. She went wide around the pan’s gone round ring against the wall, the bare dark trough whose water was called weather, the rack space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, the empty place in the returning line and the place the carriers had crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Nothing was swept up and nothing was entered, and the yard kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and spaces nobody called figures.

The carrying arm was a record in muscle. It knew the weight of the basket and the weight of the boy in it as a surgeon’s hand knows the weight of a retractor before the eye goes to the field, and it remembered the one and not the other, for the arm keeps weight and not cost. She had carried him down and up this road so many days that the distance had gone into the shoulder and stayed there, a thing recorded in flesh without anybody writing it, and she would no more have entered it in a slate than she would have told him whose warmth had paid for the miles. The boy felt the half-pace in his ear before he felt it in his ribs, and filed it as a keeping and not as a kindness, because a kindness is a thing a man can be asked to pay back and this could not be.

Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, and the second reading had gone up the road in Berr Clay’s locked box with the true subtraction inside and was still not the figure paid, while Hessa Rale’s slate lay unread at Greybank with the writing to her sleeve and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. Three held changes stood on three places, the fraction of Day 161, the beat under the board on Day 166 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; the loft scrape stood on a fourth and the pale line of the carried man on a fifth. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned. The crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the pan’s gone ring and the field’s empty place lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap, no fourth likeness, no finger of his drawn across them. The carried man’s cost was entered nowhere. The charcoal lay unread beneath the shelf, the warm stone cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in the loft, the sixth green a colour and the foundling a shape, the burned slate a direction and not a cause. The held inch lay on a sixth place beside the others with all its answers, the misses and the short holdings and the long ones, and nothing was laid across it. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the whole of it was exact, complete and worth nothing.

Then he put the question to his hand.

It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger’s-width door. It had gone up an inch on other days and come down at once, or come down before the counted breath had finished, or held for the length of the small intake only, or stayed through the whole of a breath counted at Sella’s fire in the room that does not touch his and come down with the breath; and once it had kept the height past the end of her count and come down on the river’s next low note. These were the answers the body had given and he had filed each on its sixth place and laid nothing across them. Now there was no count at that fire to hold against. She had gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and the room that did not touch his had no breathing number going in it. There was only the rhythm that came through the door, water over water, with its own low interval and no hand at it and no number on it and nobody counting it.

The hand stayed up for the length of that interval. It rose to the inch and held while the low note drew out and slackened, and it came down on the next low note when the river gave it, not when a breath in another room told it to and not when he willed it, because there was no other count in the house to will it against. It was a coordination with the one rhythm through the door. It was not a longer reach, not a stronger hand, not a hearing, for water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named to it and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He entered it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true; the inch was worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row’s end nobody calls a number. The market would not get to read it and no slate in the district would take it. The answer had come wearing a rhythm nobody gave him and nobody counted, and that was the whole of its value and the whole of its smallness. The river did not stop for it. It went on over water without noticing that an inch had ridden one of its low notes for the length of one interval.

The hand came down against the fold and the fold took it without a word. Two hundred and six stood exact in the dark, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire’s fifth had had no payer for two hundred and six days; the mark had been touched once, before the kiln on Ember 1, and at no other hour; the stone lay cold in its loft pot; the charcoal lay unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and nothing was now. The coat kept its shape on the peg and the seat kept its hollow and the clean dark line and the finer grey kept their places unswept, and the count went forward.

The Chronicle of Chapter 51

Three mornings came up over Low Kiln as the last working week ran on, and the house kept its dead man without saying his name. The boy came into his two hundred and fourth day, then the fifth, then the sixth, and nobody told him. The carried fire missed its fifth; the river kept water over water; the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places, none becoming the others, for agreeing them would have wanted a payer and none stood to any. The salt coat held a broader shape, the seat its hollow, the clean line and the finer grey lay unswept, the proverb went to the pot, the tile debt unnamed.

Sella Vail came to the fire before the basket, gave the instruction to wool, fire, pot, broom, running water and river wind, never to the child, and said the house where another woman would say I. She fed him from her own dish with the spoon on her sleeve, set the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, moved her lips along a two-gapped row, kept her finger from beneath the charcoal, and carried him home around the pan’s gone ring. Cost-questions went to the pot and the weather and went away level. The widow’s mark had not been touched since before the kiln on Ember 1.

On the second morning a word came up from Slate Row, spoken over the boards and not to the basket: a slate had burned in the night. Nobody said whose, or why. He filed it beside Jorra’s dust and the two shut-room tellings, a hand between the three, and made no cause of it. Jorra grew quieter, withheld a note from a wrong wind whose saying about a pot singing early cost no pot, and held his bent finger in the damper until the sentence reached the pot --- remembered, not made again. Tarn told the kindling six counted and the next he did not count, brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year was earned, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, no collector came.

Five times he questioned his hand: down at once, one small intake, one small intake, a whole breath down with the breath, down before the breath finished. Then, in the small hours of the last day, with no count at Sella’s fire to hold against, it went up an inch toward the river’s note and stayed for the river’s own low interval, coming down on the next low note --- a coordination with the one rhythm through the door. He filed it on its sixth place, no hearing, seventh, stopping, payment, key, power or rule, no payer named. He still could not close two inches or lift the crust. Two hundred and six stood exact, unspent, spoken by nobody; the river went on over water without noticing the inch.
