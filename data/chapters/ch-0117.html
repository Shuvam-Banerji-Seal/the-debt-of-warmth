The First Figure of the Year

Days 402–404 · Ilmen 8–10, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

A clean slate is not a clean account. One figure read twice is one figure; the grain under the wool has no voice to answer it.

— A saying of the Cross, told on the first market of Ilmen

— Day 402 —

Ilmen 8, Y.H. 814, Eighthday

The hand lay where it had been laid, flat against the fold, and the small hours came on under the roof the way cold comes into a room that has no door for it — not arriving, only being there, the boards giving up their last thin warmth and the dark settling into the wool as though it had been asked and had answered. Four hundred and one days stood behind that hand exact and unspent, six months and two hundred and twenty-one of them told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The day had a working name, Eighthday, and nothing under the Vail kiln-house made a threshold of it or a signing. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen, and the dry kiln heat had gone into the blanket with woodsmoke and tallow, and under that the faint sour tang of Ash lifting out of the Vail corner where no broom had gone in two years. Under the boards the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it. The room held its warmth the way a pot holds water, with a hairline in it nobody mentioned and a staple nobody had entered. Outside, the dark went about its ordinary work along the Clay Yard, and nobody under any roof was asking it to mean anything.

The surgeon took the body in the order it arrived, because a field read out of order is a field read wrong. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put again in the dark and not made again, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The keeping at the fold was a finding however many hands found it, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. A face composed by cold is not composure. Pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and a cold patient cannot clot, and the rewarming he would choose is a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways, slow and from the outside, never hurried and never rubbed, and he could not ask for it. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat and the order in which it takes it back, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold; he had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and the knowledge was of the same order and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work.

Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped away and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. He judged neither register, which is a kind of triage and not a peace.

He kept four hundred and two exact against a house that kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a cold clay jar and in a space at the row’s end nobody called a number, and he did not stand the figure up as a threshold or a signing. Twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 stood behind one unsigned figure, twelve and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space between. Their twelve true subtractions rode locked in a box gone up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down and the boy having no key. The reset of the tend supplied no payer to the old twelve or to a new one, and the year’s first arithmetic waited two mornings off, a reset and not a signing, and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. He did not lay his twelve old findings across a figure that had not yet been read to make a row come out even; a row made even by a hand that has no key is made even on paper, and paper is not a payer. The kept things lay where they had been laid and asked nothing of the new day: the three coverings and the river’s winter skin entered nowhere, not a fourth of the three and not spent on a tend-day; the four rooms that hold the same four things kept the same gap with a finger between every pair and no Court room brought under the roof as a fifth; six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and the Drawing house’s flat knock outside on its own place. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, and the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and he gave it no source, for a source is an account that has found a payer and none stood beyond the door.

First light came the way it comes, without consultation, laying a thin grey over the boards and showing the dust standing in the warm places. It found Sella Vail already at the hearth, for she came to the fire before she came to the basket, and had done so at the going of every dark since the year turned and before that. “It’s the thaw,” she said to the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the small red eye under the ash showed and shut and showed again. “The house will keep it,” she said to the pot, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, taking a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured, the way a woman stands over a wound she has dressed and will not look at twice. Her finger lay along the top edge of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The sweet edge of it had not come on the wind this morning; the jar stood where jars stand, and she gave it no more than the glance.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot waited with the hairline of the original break showing dark between the older brass staple and the tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, putting her thumb there before the weight came on the handle, and left the hairline showing. “Leave the crack showing,” she said to the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” To the fire she said, “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and repair stayed apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing for anybody who cares to look. She turned the dressed grain under wool on the drying racks with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and no figure went over it and no handful was lifted, and then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the hand’s intention and the hand’s arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. She kept the wide step as a keeping and not a sign. The neighbours went about an ordinary Eighthday in the Clay Yard and kept the house’s arithmetic badly for her: a woman pushed a loaf along a shelf and did not count the loaves behind it, a man set a pail down where he always set it and said nothing about the pail, two voices passed the price of barley from one end of the wall to the other without ever making a figure of it, and the space at the end of the loaf row stayed a space. The clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey hairline beside it, called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; a neighbour with a rake looked at the ridge and looked away, and nothing was said of it, and the ridge stayed where it lay. He read the yard by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the pail on stone, a latch, a loaf shifted along a board. Out of those he built no account and put no figure. He did not make a witness of a voice in a yard, and a pause between voices was not a person. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and could not lift his own hand to his own chin, and the two facts stood side by side without becoming a lesson.

Sella set him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, righted the fold at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, and the river’s winter skin lay where the cold had put it, found where put and not spent. At her throat the widow’s mark hung where it hung, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay oiled by ten thousand working thumbs, and a morning was not an opening; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on an Eighthday. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread at his mouth wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and got none of the three, the wanting and the having-not going on being the work.

At the kindling Tarn kept six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled; neither pot nor fire was asked to answer for it, and the stack kept its own arithmetic of four and four and a space, the space not a fifth and not filled. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. The coming away was the whole of the checking. He cut nothing, counted nothing toward the house’s keeping, spent nothing, and said to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” He did not turn his head toward the out-of-line landing where the soft-cuffed clerk was set below the north bridge with his slate turned inward for a measure nobody called; the degree his head had once gone toward it and back to the broom stayed a shape found where put, not repeated as a habit, a way or a rule. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth stood up, no figure and no name. The Seed 16 breath was drawn and left unspent at the top of the reach; the seventh year had run to its very end and the term was already ended, and a term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom but leaves a man more exposed than it found him. The fear that he would be entered again for vagrancy stayed his without a figure, and so did the want he did not voice — a slate with his own name on it and no mark against it, a thing a man could hold up and be called by. He owns nothing and knows where every small thing is.

At the Vail wheel-shed edge Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken slates under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before any person — “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady” — and told a pot “You’ll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and was left standing so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no Grathic word covered both mercies; he took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, and neither looked at the other nor said what the iron had been asked to carry. “He’s not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name under the wheel-shed, made to men who burn slates, stayed neither given nor withdrawn, going on gathering interest against the burned-slate word at five and the four old directions with a finger between every pair.

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. It was rough from the wash and smelled of tallow and the low smoke that gets into cloth when a house has no chimney worth the name, and it lay under the resting hand with the weight of a thing put down for the next hand to find. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. The crust’s gap stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool. The shelf kept its two gaps, and the gone pan’s round ring lay on the board where a pan had stood and a pan no longer stood, a round place and not a hole. The fold was found where put in the empty place and was still a finding; however many hands found it, it remained a keeping found and not made, for a second placing would make a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer standing where none stood. The told bell’s gap lay in its square beside the bell he had heard from the Cross, four, four and a gap, its gap not folded into the fire’s four and a gap under the floor, into the boards’ four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the twelve behind one figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. The silence after the name spoken over the yard on the Remembering sat on the gap tray beside a clean voice’s missing name and the empty name the neighbours had left in their story, each with a finger between every pair, none filled, none made a witness or a reader. The neighbour’s called-gone grey lay beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner, two states on two places with a finger between, and neither was laid across the other to make a row come out even. The face of the man from Slate Row at the yard’s edge lay on a place of its own beside the older record-first hands, a shape seen from a low angle with no name and no interior, no loaf passed, no slate shown, no word that carried; it was filed and not counted. Every one lay where it lay, advanced and not paid, and none stood up and called itself a rule. Counting was an old-life habit and not a gift: it gave him no key, no slate with his own name, no hand to lift a crust two inches, only the exactness of the field and the cost of it, which was nothing. A man may count his own breaths and learn nothing from the count but the counting, and go on counting because it is the only instrument left him.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note under the winter skin. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the river’s note’s own finishing under the covering, let it go as the note ended — neither early nor stayed — and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer three hundred and seventy-nine came out and the hand came down. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction, the clerk’s inward slate, the face at the yard’s edge nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, three hundred and seventy-four through three hundred and seventy-nine, and not one of them was a doctrine. The count came to four hundred and two exact, six months and two hundred and twenty-two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate lay open within reach of the basket, no figure was given for the boy, no door was drawn across the tend. The crust’s corner stood a hair off the wool and the thread was frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept — forward.

The wool went back over the grain with the same light touch, and the space stayed as it was.

Then the pot went down on the board again with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and there was a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between an intention and an arrest: her hand had meant to set the pot nearer and had stopped where it stopped, and the distance cost her nothing and bought the boy nothing, and neither of them called it a gift. Under the roof nobody instructs a basket, and nobody instructs a pot about a hand.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. The seven walked-around places were gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place, and she set him under the roof and never into the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold to the crust’s empty place. The keeping at the fold was found where it had been put, and was still a finding and not a placing: no third crust, no name spoken over the place, no gift, no payment and no payer. A fold put down once and found again on the mornings and goings of the light of many days does not become a habit, for a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer standing where none stood.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung through the carrying and the setting and the righting of the wool. Her hand went to it before the opening of a kiln and at no other hour, and a hearth and a going of the light are no opening. He kept the not-touching as a finding beside the left-in-place ridge and the called-gone grey, two states on two places with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness a cause.

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and he went over the field by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. The crust’s gap stood two inches from the ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the shelf’s two gaps stood in a room that does not touch this one; the gone pan’s round ring lay where a pan had been lifted and not where a pan was; the field’s empty place held the shape of a thing taken out and not a hole dug; the keeping at the fold was found where put and still a finding, with no name over it and no payer under it. The told bell’s gap of the Long Night stood in its square beside the bell he had heard from the Cross, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, its gap not folded into the fire’s four and a gap under the floor, into the boards’ four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the twelve readings behind one unsigned figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. On the gap tray three names had been emptied out and the emptiness was kept with a finger between it and the clean voice’s missing name and the story’s empty name, none filled, none made a witness or a hearing. The neighbour’s called-gone grey stood beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner, two states on two places, with a finger between. Tarn’s ear-sentence had come all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, in a term that had run to its very end and was ended; a term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom, but leaves a man more exposed than it found him. Jorra’s promise under the wheel-shed stayed neither given nor withdrawn. Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt, and his name was unspoken the same number of days as the fire’s fifth unpaid, the two counts side by side and never one. The soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate stayed at the out-of-line landing, reported and not witnessed; Tarn’s degree toward it stayed a shape found where put and not repeated. Berr’s low saying about kindness entered lay on its own place beside neither the twelve findings nor the new figure. The man from Slate Row’s face lay on its tray, filed and not counted among the nine hands, no loaf, no slate and no sale passing under any name.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and the Drawing house’s small flat knock outside on its own place, and neither the knock nor any told gap was made a seventh. Nine record-first hands stayed nine with no tenth, and nine stoppings stayed nine with no tenth. No eighth opening stood, no seventh hearing, no thirteenth arithmetic, no signature, no payer, no witness, no reader and no payment; Hessa’s slate stayed unread up the road toward Greybank, Ilva’s strip stayed in her sleeve, the clerk’s slate stayed inward below the north bridge, and the face stayed on its tray. The four rooms that keep the same gap stayed four with a finger between every pair, and no Court room was brought under the roof as a fifth likeness. The Ash 5 joining of the burned-slate word to the counting room shut in the war, the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions and the fourth shaping stood where put, advanced and not paid, and did not repeat on a day that had earned its own arithmetic. No ownerless voice came over the boards.

The first arithmetic of the year stood gone up the road in Berr’s locked box with the key gone up in the coat pocket that never comes back down and no key in any hand under this roof. It was one reading and not an account; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and the district’s arithmetic had begun again and could not be undone. He did not lay twelve old findings across it to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer, nor did he make a doctrine of one. Nothing in the square had addressed the boy it would carry.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the winter skin. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering — neither early nor stayed — and let it go as the note ended. Then the hand came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer three hundred and eighty-four came out and the hand came down. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the new figure, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction, the clerk’s inward slate, the green at the pot board, the shape at the market’s edge, Berr’s low saying nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, three hundred and seventy-nine through three hundred and eighty-four, and not one of them was a doctrine. The count came to four hundred and four exact, six months and two hundred and twenty-four days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate lay open within reach of the basket, no figure was given for the boy, no door was drawn across the tend, and Ilmen 20 stood ten mornings off and was not brought into a day that had earned only its tenth.

The surgeon’s small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and got none of the three. The crust’s corner stood a hair off the wool and the thread was frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied; the first arithmetic of the year was up the road in a locked box with no key in his hand, and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept — forward.

The Chronicle of Chapter 117

Three days came under the Vail roof — Ilmen 8, 9 and 10 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: crust two inches off with its corner hair-lifted, the fold found where put and still a finding, the thread stiff in the dry heat and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither won.

Sella went to the hearth before the basket each day, told the coals it was the thaw, said The house will keep it, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, gave both proverbs to pot and fire, turned the dressed grain under wool, set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and kept the charcoal unread and the widow’s mark untouched. Tarn kept six where the house could check, the seventh at the lip, told the wood, Six counted. The next I don’t count, and passed his hand over blade and sill and away.

Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, spoke to the wheel before any person, withheld the full note, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence. On the ninth a wrong wind off High Kiln carried the sweet edge of glaze over the boards and out again, no hand or name under it, and the boy filed it as weather.

On the tenth, the year’s first Account-day, Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s shade and stood half a pace off. Berr Clay came off the clay road, red cord folded, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, passed a finger over the figure through his sleeve and read low, One in twelve, as the notice says. He sent it twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it with your house, never I, said low to the block that kindness was entered, and went off with the box locked, believing he had done a kindness. The district’s arithmetic began again and could not be undone; nobody addressed the boy it would carry.

He took the reading apart as a surgeon takes a field: first of the year, not a thirteenth, one reading not an account, no payer in the reset, the figure read not the figure paid, twelve old findings not laid across it, for paper is not a payer. Hessa went up the road before noon carrying what she would not read; the bell went four, four and a gap. Six answers were filed, 379 through 384; the count stood at four hundred and four exact, told by nobody, the fire’s fifth unpaid and Garl’s name unspoken. In the small hours the hand came down flat on the fold, and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down and the fire kept.
