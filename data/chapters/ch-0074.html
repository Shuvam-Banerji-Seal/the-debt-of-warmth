The Week of Going Around

Days 273–275 · Frost 4–6, Y.H. 813 · The Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln

A firing opens the kiln. A week of tending settles nothing. The hand goes to the mark before the fire, and to no shelf, no bed, no basket after it.

— A saying of the drying racks in Low Kiln, told after the first firing of a month

— Day 273 —

Frost 4, Fourthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Outside the wool the small hours went on at their own pace, and the boy came into his two hundred and seventy-third day carried, awake and silent, under the roof of the Vail kiln-house in Low Kiln. Two hundred and seventy-two had stood exact at the going of the dark; two hundred and seventy-three stood exact now, and six months and ninety-three days were told by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath. The count crossed without a sound, the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-three days, and the lean quarter of its warmth stayed where it was with nobody to lift the floor and nobody to supply the pull.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sill had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-three days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the fire answered in fours and withheld its fifth, not a bell; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on the last market-day of Smoke, stayed in the square where it struck beside the told bell of the Long Night; it was not brought under this roof on a Fourthday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it.

Frost 4 came on its own light, earned by that morning and not brought into a day that had not earned it. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Fourthday, and the working week began the way a pot begins on a banked fire, by degrees and without announcement. Frost 10, the month’s first Account-day, stood six mornings ahead and was not brought early into the dark of this one.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket and gave no instruction to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go — and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. At the hearth the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed; nothing was said at any hour to the basket. Instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the thing carried in it.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. On Frost 2, before the kiln, she had put her hand to it for one breath and let go, and lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot; the fire had taken, and nobody had praised the opening or named what it mended, and no slate had been entered. At a morning and a hearth the mark hung where it hung, for neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, no sign, no mending anybody would name and no payer. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule, for a rule wants a payer standing under it; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for.

Out on the Clay Yard the dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at the rack end, away from the hearth, and had been turned once beneath the wool for damp in the first of the light and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it in the days to come did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there.

The yard went around its seven places the way a woman carries a full pail: with a look, a wide step and no number. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, the bare dark the trough beside the yard well with its water called weather, the field’s empty place out on the worked strips, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf’s two gaps, the fold’s one crust or the dressed grain under wool.

Garl Vail’s debt against a firing of tiles went around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-three days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the Vail wheel-shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it, and the proverb said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out went on being said to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. It was a saying about honest repairs said over a pot with no crack in it, and it cost more now than it had when he was alive to say it. Garl was kept in objects and not in a story, a keeping that was maintenance and not an argument, and no lesson was made of any of it. No collector had come up the clay road on Frost 1, Frost 2 or Frost 3, and none had gone down it; the loan stood in the ruts where it had stood, and Tarn’s seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

Tarn took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said Yes, house to those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts. At the kindling the small knife lay at the very lip of its sill with six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the edge where it could not, beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping, with no figure and no name — a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach stayed unspent, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped.

Before first grey men whose names the house did not take went low through the finger’s-width-open the back room off the shed, and Jorra Vail came through with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked no question that would join them to a cause, parting the two states as a surgeon parts clot from vessel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. His bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence, moving the tongue a hair’s breadth and leaving it standing so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence; the boy read one iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness and no row named, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Under the roof the morning went on doing what it had always done, and the boy lay at the low angle of the shelf in the room whose floor does not touch the room of the fire, carried and told by nobody what any of it counted. Sella had carried him past the open slates at the half-pace and come back to the pot; Tarn had taken the broom when it was named; Jorra had gone to the wheel; the grain lay under wool and the fire missed its fifth and the mark hung where it hung. None of it stood up and called itself a lesson. He kept the not-doings on a place of its own with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and did not lay one across the other, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. There was a woman’s low counting in the next room — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go — a sound with a direction and no name, and he took from it only what the hand could take and left the rest where it was. The body offered him two inches of air and a crust he could not lift; the house offered him nothing at all and kept itself whole by not entering what it did. Between the two there was the one thing he could do, and it was small enough to be honest.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note through the finger’s-width door. It took the small intake of a count from the room whose floor does not touch his, held it, and came down on the river’s next low note. It borrowed no house count and supplied no fifth to the kiln; it crossed no silence that did not belong to the river and took nothing from the two-gapped row on the shelf.

One hundred and twenty-one, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift; the restraint was the work and he did not promote it.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Behind the wall the fire kept four low pulls and withheld its fifth under a floor nobody would lift, the lean quarter of its warmth staying where it was with its payer unnamed. Two hundred and seventy-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety-three days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days. The widow’s mark hung where it hung. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the figure read not the figure paid; the heard bell stood beside the told, the seventh year stood at its very end and still ungiven, and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Fourthday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and the river’s low note going quiet after it. Frost went on toward its fifth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months’ openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month’s first firing.

High Kiln stood up over the district with the wrong wind coming off it, and Frost wrote white fronds on the stone lip of the well where the Warm had lately gone out. On a turn of the same wrong wind the sweet edge of lead glaze came over the boards, touched the fold at his ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it, a direction arrived nowhere with no second edge paid and no fourth likeness made a cause.

A woman come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool and the visits were coming. Sella sent her to the pot and the weather. Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it, she said, and the woman went off level with her question still on her, nothing priced, refused or entered. A man come down the clay road with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere. The boy had learned to read a closed fist the way he had once read a chart turned face-down: the shape of the paper was not the writing under it, and a figure that arrives nowhere has not therefore been hidden from a man who wants it. He supplied no missing figure, because a supplied figure is a forged one and the space where it would have stood was part of the keeping. A figure read is still not the figure paid.

The change came at the shelf, in the middle of a morning that had announced nothing. She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the two-gapped row the way a woman counts spoons and does not say what the counting is for. On Day 166 the finger had gone beneath that edge and stayed for the length of one of the breaths she counted under her breath, and had come away with no word and no going back. This time it stayed for two.

The boy lay at the low angle of the shelf and read the two breaths as a surgeon reads a small change in a field: the same hand, the same place, the same silence after, and one more held interval laid into it. Two hundred and seventy-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety-five days were told by nobody, and the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The letting-go in the next room went on where it was, entered nowhere, and Frost went toward its seventh morning without a boundary drawn.

Heat went out of the afternoon the way it goes out of a pot set off the coals: not all at once and with no boundary drawn in the going, but by degrees, drawing back from the rim and sitting down behind the pot where it had always been. The light went with it off the boards and out of the Clay Yard, and Frost went out of the day without a line drawn under it, and neither of the district’s two habits of naming the months was set right or left wrong by anybody under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. Four openings stood behind the household as four and not a rule, for a rule wants a payer standing under it and none stood; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for. Nothing had been made of the week’s small movements, and nothing would be.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sarn had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-five days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order — crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting — and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story, his name unspoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-five days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder held a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and no one had been allowed to fill it; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now to the pot and the fire and never to a person; the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. A debt uncalled can be gone around every day of a man’s life and never be nearer done, which is the cruellest arithmetic in Low Kiln and the one nobody there will enter. No collector came up the clay road on Frost 4, 5 or 6 and none went down it.

In the yard the seven walked-around places were gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, the way a woman carries a full pail past a child who might knock it: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the Ash ridge had turned and the finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the space beneath the lowest rack at the drying racks where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape or crumb or print or chip or word to say where it had been; the trough beside the yard well, bare and dark, its water called weather by everybody who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it; the field’s empty place out on the worked strips; and the returning-line place where the hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf’s two gaps, the fold’s one crust or the dressed grain under wool at the rack end. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The boy went around them with his eyes and kept them on seven places that did not touch.

At the going of the light Sella Vail carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, the way a pot is set where it will not be knocked and not where it is most convenient to reach. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. She went to the hearth before the basket and told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the pot’s foot gave its small flat sound on stone and went out into the room’s ordinary noise with no lesson made of it. The house will keep it, she said, where another woman would have said I. She had said it on every morning of the week and would say it again tomorrow, and the saying had not grown truer by repetition and had not grown less true either; it was the grammar the house used to keep a thing without entering it.

At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed where it was, unread, uncopied and unfigured, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. On the Fifthday the finger had gone beneath that edge and stayed two counted breaths and come away without a word; tonight it stayed on top, and the two breaths kept the place they had been given and did not stand up and call themselves a rule. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. At no hour of this Sixthday did she give an instruction to the basket. The instruction went to the fire and the wool and the pot and the broom and the running water, and never to the thing lying in the basket, which was a keeping and not a conversation.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it. She had touched it once before the kiln on Frost 2, held it one breath, let go, and did not go back; she had not touched it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a yard, a morning, a hearth, an afternoon, a dusk or a going of the light on Frost 4, 5 or 6, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, sign, mending anybody would name or payer, and the boy did not look at it again. He had learned that an opening is an opening and that four are four, and had been careful not to let the learning become a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

In the small hours the file was turned out on the towel as instruments are turned out for a man who will never be called to use them, each thing laid in its own place with room between, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The surgeon who had once laid out a tray for a night’s trauma and known every instrument by its weight found the tray complete and the reach two inches short, and made nothing of the finding.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; no seventh stood in the empty place, and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell’s four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell of the Long Night, not folded into the three counts under this roof and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The premature pot note from Smoke 14 lay on its own place apart from the three sayings about a pot, and no fourth likeness had been made a cause, no pot lost, no name given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188.

The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the sayings about a pot stood at three. The question about the row stood at five askings with one further shaping beside them and a finger between, no witness anywhere to be asked and no row named; no sixth asking had come over the boards on Frost 4, 5 or 6, and the silence was a keeping and not a sixth witness. Tarn’s sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it had come to the word that would call the loan and stopped there on each of the three days, a remembered ninth stopping and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of every reach, and the loan lay uncalled in the ruts.

Nine record-first hands stood on nine places with a finger between every pair: the midwife’s slate before the fold, the thane’s finger over the figure, the keeper’s finger beneath the board, the runner’s strap and wrist, the clerk’s number before the eyes found the buyer, Berr’s twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward entry, Hessa’s edge-on slate, and Ilva’s thumb-nail crossing the notched birch at the well, her strip unread in her sleeve and no figure coming off it. No tenth hand had been filed and the nine were not a rule. The seventh green stood a colour at a silent woman’s mouth and the seventh shape a shape in cast-off clothes at a market’s edge, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over either. Smoke’s third arithmetic sat locked in Berr’s box up the clay road, unread under this roof; the printed figure stood where it had been printed and the figure read was still not the figure paid; the dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end, turned once more with a lighter touch for damp and given no figure, and the arithmetic that would read over it in four mornings did not know what it cost.

The finding that the house keeps itself by its not-doings stayed on its own place with the boy’s own kept gaps on another and a finger between, and he had not laid the finding across the coat, the uncalled loan, the unread charcoal, the missing fifth or the crust in the fold, for to lay any two together would want a payer and there was no payer under any of them. The week had added small movements to the file — a finger under a board for two breaths on the Fifthday, two intakes held apart on that same day, an early ending on this Sixthday — and each stayed where it was laid as a held change on its own place beside the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 held beat and the Tallow 22 flagstone half-check, with no fourth likeness standing up and no doctrine made of three. The kiln-master’s saying about pots that sing early was a saying, the wrong wind’s sweet glaze a direction arrived nowhere, and the white the frost had written on the well lip was weather by noon and a white thing kept on a place of its own by the boy; none of these had been joined, and the week had not taught him a lesson he could spend.

The held interval and the empty interval lay side by side on the sixth place with a finger between, and neither had been made to agree with the fifth pull missing under the floor, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to either. The shelf’s held beat returned at two breaths on the Fifthday was a held change on its own place, not a fourth likeness, not a key, not a doctrine; the two intakes held apart stayed apart; the early ending stayed an ending and not a freedom. He had spent the week refusing to promote what he could not use, and the refusing cost him nothing and gained him nothing, which was the only honest work a body two hundred and seventy-five days old could do. A man who cannot lift a crust two inches can still decide where a holding stops, and the temptation to call that a gift had come and gone each time without his giving it a name.

In the next room whose floor does not touch his a woman’s low counting came through the wall, flattened by work and given nobody’s face, the small intake, the hold, the letting-go, and the little silence after it. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note through the finger’s-width door, water over water, low over lower. It took the small intake of one of those counted breaths and the hold of it, and stayed past the letting-go and through the little silence after it, and set the held interval and the empty interval side by side with a finger between, borrowing no house count to carry the inch across and supplying no fifth pull to the carried fire under the floor. It took nothing from the row with two gaps in the other room and nothing from the charcoal under the shelf and nothing from the covered grain at the rack end. It came down on the river’s next low note with the woman’s letting-go left where it had been, outside the holding and entered nowhere.

One hundred and twenty-six was filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-five, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything; the restraint was the work and he did not promote it. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and seventy-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety-five days were told by nobody, and the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Sixthday; the first firing of Frost stood behind the household without a lesson, the next opening stood far off in a later month’s first firing, and Frost went on toward its seventh morning without a boundary drawn. The river’s low note went quiet after the hand came down, and the boy lay with the crust two inches off and the empty place beside it unnamed, carried forward and unresolved.

The Chronicle of Chapter 74

Frost came on in its own light, and the boy came into it two hundred and seventy-three days old, carried and told by nobody what a number counted. The working week began by degrees and without announcement, with no market, Cross or bell. A brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed; the thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat and was left unwiped; cold came to cheek and temple and the bare wrist, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He took the inventory as a surgeon takes a field. He wanted the crust lifted two inches. That was the louder truth.

Three counts stood in three places: four low pulls under the floor with the fifth withheld, water over water at the door, four nails and four and a thumb-width knot overhead. None rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both, and none stood. Sella went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, said The house will keep it, and carried him at the half-pace, going wide around seven places with a look and no number, among them the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The grain lay under wool at the rack end, turned for damp and given no figure. Tarn kept six and a seventh at the knife’s lip and stopped the ear-sentence at the word that would call the loan. Ilva kept her count in wood; Garl was kept in objects, his debt circling without a name.

On the Fifthday Sella’s finger went beneath the shelf’s front edge and stayed for two counted breaths where on Day 166 it had stayed for one, then came away without a word. The charcoal was not read, and the two gaps above were given no number. The boy filed the returned beat on the shelf’s own place, beside the fraction at the damper and the older held beat, and would not let it stand up as a fourth likeness, a gift or a rule. A house could change by two held breaths and say nothing.

Six times the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note --- for the small intake, for a holding carried across the little silence, for a held beat returned, for two intakes kept apart, for an ending made early --- and came down, borrowing no house count and supplying no fifth. At the close the held interval and the empty interval lay side by side with a finger between, neither made to agree with the missing fifth. Two hundred and seventy-five stood exact, spoken by nobody.
