Where the Stroke Stopped

Days 170–172 · Tallow 21–23, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln and the Clay Yard, Sill

The stone keeps the place where the broom stopped, and the iron keeps the finger that waits in it. The fire keeps its fifth where nobody has looked for a hundred days. What is kept in three places does not add up to an account.

— A saying of the Clay Yard, spoken at the last tend of Tallow

— Day 170 —

Tallow 21, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)

Hot clay came into the room before the light did, under tallow and wet wool and the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years; it was the smell of the yard giving up its water to the dry cold, and it was the first thing the boy knew of the last working week. Under the smell came a sound that was not a bell. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, and there was a gap where the fifth breath should have been, the way a sentence can carry a hole in it that everybody hears and nobody fills. He lay on the bed under the Vail roof in Low Kiln, one hundred and seventy days old and five months and twenty days, and nobody under any roof said so, because there was no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number.

The fold was at his ear and under his chin, wool gone damp where his breath had settled and frozen again in the dry heat. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery since the planting, with an empty place beside it that had no name, had never needed one, and would not call a third to keep it company. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and there was no instruction for that sound either. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water beyond the finger’s-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. He had counted them in the dark. He would count them again.

Cold had come in its known order in the night, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want had come up small and importunate behind it, for the crust two inches off and for a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and had got neither. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted; that was not the same as not wanting it. The man who had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and known the exact order in which a body fails lay in a fold of wool and could not lift his own head, and the two of them argued in the small room without a word: the surgeon’s dry register, the infant’s hunger. The hunger was louder.

Sella Vail came in with the light and put the day in order without once putting it into words. She gave the wool its instruction, and the fire its instruction, and the pot its instruction, and the broom its instruction, and the running water at the yard well its instruction; she did not give the basket one. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It’s the river wind.” To the pot: “Don’t set that near the door. You’ll pay for it in the thaw.” To the fire, later, when she had turned the coals with the back of a broken pot: “It was the thaw. The house will keep it.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and the boy where another would have said my son, and had not said either in all his days. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing as she reached for the wool and shut half over the fold of it when she smoothed it, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals where she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with that wrist.

She stood a half-pace from every open slate, though there was no slate open under the roof, and went around the clean dark line in the corner as she went around a pail set where a child might knock it. The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go; then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was the only thing in the room that admitted anything. She did not look toward the bed.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for one hundred and seventy days. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its own gap, and through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and had never been invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places — the fire through the floor, the kiln behind the brick, the river through the door — and overhead the boards kept their four nails, four, and the knot. None of them rose up and became the others. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

Carried at the half-pace past the kiln mouth, he watched the yard go around what it would not enter. The women went wide around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that was called the thaw, and around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went around it with a look and a wide step and no number, as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. At the well the trough stood bare and dark, its skin of ice gone, and the water in it was called weather. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze; Sella glanced at it once and did not touch it. The shallow clay pan that had stood mouth-up against the wall through Reed was gone these many mornings, and its round empty ring kept the exact width of its absence on a tray of its own, not laid across the shelf’s two gaps, the crust’s gap, or the field’s empty place. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

Tarn took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. He came up over the flagstones with his chest tight from the dark, his narrow shoulders bent, and Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, so that no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. “Yes, house,” he said to the early thaw and again to the late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark on the sill at the kindling where the house could check; the seventh stood at the very lip where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge of its sill with Garl’s unforged collector sentence beside it. He had brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it all the way to his teeth on other mornings and it had gone no further; the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of each reach, and the sentence was not said. In the clay pot in the loft above the Vail wheel-shed the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold. He could no more reconcile the stone with the debt than the house’s kindness with the account he kept of it, and had stopped trying, which was not forgiving.

His stroke went over the flagstones and past the shallow bright patch where an older stroke had stopped, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came. Nine stoppings stood on the tray where they had stood, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine, and the seventh year had not given. He told the kindling what he told it every morning: “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” The broom went on.

The danger of the firing month gathered in the dust ground small as meal on the wrong wind and in the sweetish edge of lead glaze carried up from High Kiln. At the racks a woman set a pot down on the boards and went on with the pot in her other hand; the pot gave a high thin note before its time, a small bright sound with air under it, the note of a vessel with a hairline gone all the way through or a wall too thin for the heat it would be asked to hold. The woman did not turn. Another woman near her turned her head a degree and went back to her work, and nobody said what the sound was or made a lesson of it. The boy filed it as a danger and not as a fourth near-full answer, and no room was changed by it and no pot was lost that morning. A note early was a note spent before its time; a note known and held back was a different thing on a different tray, and he kept the two apart.

He read the firing the way he had once read a room where heat was kept and counted behind a screen while he stood at the gate with a towel in his hands: a theatre he could not enter, with heat held behind a wall and counted in fours, a gate of two bites, and a cost paid by a man whose name was not spoken under any roof. The heat was kept. The cost was kept. The keeping and the cost stood in different places and did not add up to an account that any man would read aloud. His file was exact and worth nothing at two paces. An inventory is not a key. A list is not a hand. He was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it, with a child’s wrist and a crust two inches out of reach.

The fraction from the morning of Tallow 12, when a hand had waited at the cold the damper for a sentence to finish, stood where it had been left. The held beat from beneath the loaf shelf stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it. He did not repeat either, did not lay one across the other, did not let the two of them stand up and call themselves a pair, still less a doctrine. Two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell. Nothing under the roof said Grathic over any of it; the surgeon’s register coloured the noticing and kept out of the way of it, as a man keeps a hand off a field he cannot enter. Three counts in three places, and none became the others.

At mid-morning the broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and went over it again, without once turning along it. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its gap; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its fifth low note, never counted among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire missed its fifth, its payer unnamed for one hundred and seventy days. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. One hundred and seventy stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof.

At midday the well had the yard’s wet work in it. The rope came up dark to the last strand and laid itself on the stone lip with a small slap, and the women spoke to the bucket and not to one another, because a bucket can be told a thing and go on being a bucket. the boy lay in the basket at the edge of the light with a fold of wool at his ear and the crust two inches off, and the note of water going over water came through the finger’s-width door behind him and went on coming, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings of the full note. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down against the fold. It could go no further. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb, and he would count them again when the light went.

A neighbour came up with a wet pot held level in both hands, water in the hollow of it slopping at nothing and going still as she stopped. She set it on the stone lip where the rope had lain and looked at Sella Vail with the look of a woman who has already decided what the answer will be and means to have it said aloud.

“What does a firing cost, set down in this house?” she said. “I’ve a pot come out of the tubs and it wants the kiln before the week’s out.”

She said it again, louder, to the wet pot rather than to the woman. “What’ll a firing want, set down here?” The pot gave back its own small wooden sound and nothing else. The boy had once watched a man put a question to a chart and wait for the margin to answer; the chart had answered in its own neat hand, which was to say not at all. This was that again, in a yard that smelled of clay giving up its water to the dry cold.

Sella did not answer the woman. Her eye went once to the clay jar on the shelf by the wall, cold under its glaze, and came away; her hand, which had started toward it, shut half over nothing and went back to the pot in her own hand, the little-door burn closing on the rim and then on air. She had not touched the jar. She had not needed to. The hand had gone to the record before the hand went to the person, as it goes in other rooms before a face is looked at, and the gesture arrived nowhere and left no entry. The glance had gone to the jar because the jar was the house’s own cold record of what a wet thing set down in the wrong place will cost by the thaw; the hand did not touch it because a record is not opened by a woman who means to keep it. That was his reading of it, and it was exact, and at two paces it bought him nothing.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

The neighbour stood a moment with her mouth open on the next part, then took up her pot by both handles and carried it off level as she had brought it, her elbows tight to her sides, nothing slopping, nothing priced, nothing refused. The question went with her and arrived where such questions arrive, which was in the air over the ruts and not in any slate the boy could see. The surgeon’s register in him read it as a direction sent to an instrument and brought back without a figure: an order given where a number had been asked, and the number neither entered nor denied. He had known charts closed over a finding with the neat margin left showing; this was the same grammar in a clay yard, with wet rope for ink.

Sella’s arm had carried him a hundred and seventy mornings and kept the weight in muscle and not the cost. That was the whole of the record it kept. She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Go around,” she said to it. “It cost enough to come in; it’ll cost more to take out.”

Tarn took it up as a man takes up a tool already named. “Yes, house,” he said to the ones who wanted the thaw early and again to the ones who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at the kindling where the house could check; the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge with Garl’s unforged collector sentence beside it. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the flagstones and past the shallow bright patch where an older stroke had stopped, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came.

A kiln-hand come up on the wrong wind let the sweetish edge of lead glaze go past him from High Kiln and said to the boards that the kilns were at their heat and a pot set down wrong would sing before its time. Nobody made a lesson of it. The boy filed the saying as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer, and kept the early note on its own place. The danger gathered without a hand to spend it, as heat gathers behind a screen while a man stands at the gate with a towel he will not use.

Carried past the drying racks at the half-pace, he watched the yard go around what it would not enter. The women went wide around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that was called the thaw, and around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went around with a look and a wide step and no number, as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. Dry slip dust lay in the rack shadows and came up under the women’s nails and went back to the boards with their pots. The space under the lowest rack kept its broken slates and entered nobody; the field’s empty place kept its shape on a place of its own and was not laid across the shelf’s two gaps, the crust’s gap, or the round ring the shallow pan had left when it went off the wall. A yard that goes around an absence is a yard that keeps it without entering it, which is an account kept in feet rather than ink. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

At the loaf shelf she set the basket low, near the bench leg, at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof. From that low angle he could see the front edge of the loaf shelf and the dark waiting under it where the charcoal marks lay. They waited there as a record waits in a language nobody has asked him to read. He could have read a chart in a room he remembered; he could not read six strokes of charcoal under a board because nobody had told him whose strokes they were. A mark is only a record when a man knows what it is a record of. Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along her own row, and the row stood with two gaps in it: the old space at the end where a loaf had once stood, and the palm’s breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. A rack woman with dry slip dust in the creases of her wrists reached past the bare board with her hand before her eyes found the basket and asked what the row would want now the kilns were hot; Sella told the pot not to be set near the door and said it would pay for that in the thaw, and the woman went off with her pot and no figure.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The two shelf gaps stayed in one room and the crust’s gap stayed in another, and the pan’s gone ring stayed on a tray of its own, and the field’s empty place stayed out in the yard, and none of them touched another. He did not lay them together and call them one absence, because a man who lays four absences together has already chosen which hand to believe. He did not read the charcoal, did not count it, did not copy it. His finger stayed inside the wool. The marks waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. Sella’s finger did not go beneath the board this day; the held beat from beneath it stood where it had been left, on a place of its own beside the fraction at the cold the damper, and the two did not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to either. The told bell stood beside the heard bell, and the kiln’s gap under the wall was not laid across either.

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without a word as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the place where the shallow pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, around the bare dark the trough at the well, and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is carried level and not told why it turns. He had no name for the turns and did not ask for one.

Under the Vail roof she set him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and she had never told anybody. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. In the other room her own bed waited, and the two rooms did not touch.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy days. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its own gap; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places, and none became the others. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and the knot the width of a thumb.

One hundred and seventy stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. He lay with the crust two inches off and the empty place beside it unnamed, and wanted the crust and the breast and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got none of them, which was not the same as not wanting them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. The pot rim stood out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand; the fire missed its fifth under the floor; the two rooms did not touch.

— Day 171 —

Tallow 22, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)

Before first grey the men came up the clay road with their collars high and went low into the finger’s-width-open the back room off the Vail wheel-shed. There were three of them, or four; the boy lay in the basket at the edge of the shed’s shadow and did not count them, because a number given to men who had been given no name would be a number with no place to stand, and he had learned to leave such numbers where they lay. They went in stooping, the way a man goes into a room the door has promised to be higher than it is, and the door kept its finger’s width of open after them, as it had kept it for three years. Sella Vail carried past at the half-pace with the basket level and the little-door burn shut on the handle, and turned no head toward the door, and did not say the room was cold. She had said it once, years back, to nobody in particular, and the saying had gone into the yard and come back without an answer, and she had not said it since. The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning was not an opening. Two tellings of a room shut up in the war with the men inside dead of the breath stood side by side in the boy’s file, one from a man with road-mud on his boots, one from a second man with a bag, each eighteen leagues east and each dated, and no third came this morning to stand beside them.

Under the broken pot the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been; its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-one days. Behind the wall the kiln drew breath in fours with its own gap, and through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings of the full note and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb; none of them rose up and became the others. The sweetish edge of lead glaze came up from High Kiln on the wrong wind and went over the yard like a hand passed over the rim of a bowl, and under it the clay gave up its water to the dry cold. That was the month’s danger and it carried no lesson. A pot set down wrong would sing before its time in a dry heat like this, and the woman who set it down would not hear it; the boy filed the saying as a danger of the yard and not as a fourth near-full answer, and the early note stayed on a place of its own.

Jorra Vail came through from the shed before the light had settled where it meant to fall. Fine grey slate dust was ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; it had come off something scraped or burned, and it had been worked in by the same hands that would presently take up wet clay. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray in the boy’s file, two states of the same morning, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack, where the yard went around them and entered nobody; a whole slate has a place, and a broken one has a place that is gone around, and nobody under this roof had ever told him which was which. He wanted to know. He had wanted to know for a hundred and seventy-one days and could not ask, which was its own way of keeping a thing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands,” he said to it. “It cares if they’re steady.” The wheel gave back its small wooden complaint under his palm, and he set a pot up and told it, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. His hands went in with the bent finger a little ahead of them, and the clay came up damp and obedient on the edge of the wheel. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands; the knowing stood in the boy’s ear as a fact and went onto the near-full tray, beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

The boy watched those hands from under the wool and read them the way he had once read a resident’s hands at a bedside: not what they said but what they had been taught to do before the thinking caught up with them. The bent little finger led because a splinter at eleven had taught it to lead; the dust lay past the bend because the dust had gone on working after the finger had stopped. Two facts a forearms’ width apart, and neither the cause of the other. He had learned to keep facts in separate pockets, the way an instrument goes back into its own hollow on the towel with nothing resting across it. If he laid the dust and the bent finger and the withheld knowing together and called them a finding, he would have a man he could enter, and a man entered in that fashion is a man who can be spent. He kept the three apart. The wheel went on turning and the dust went on lying where it lay.

A kiln-master come up from High Kiln on the wrong wind stopped with Ash on his cuffs and listened to the pot under Jorra’s hands. “That one’s singing before its time,” he said. “A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early.”

Jorra’s eyes went to the door and came back without reporting. He said nothing. The kiln-master took the silence for the agreement men take silence for and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost in the yard that morning. The boy kept the withheld knowing where it was, on the tray with the other near-full answers, and made no fourth out of it and no hearing out of the early note.

Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to the man. “Go around,” she said to the broom. “Take the road last.” Then to the fire, with her voice going down the way it went down when a sentence wanted a word she would not hand it: “The fire’s low. It wants—”

Jorra’s bent finger went to the cold iron of the damper and stopped. It went in ahead of his hand as it had gone in on Tallow 12 and again on Tallow 15, but this time it did not move the tongue at once. The iron took the warmth out of the fingertip and gave a little back in the reverse of the core, the two bites of it shut about nothing. The boy read it as he had read it a hundred mornings: one piece of iron with a bite that holds a vessel whole and a bite that can take the hand off a man, and no word in Grathic that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. He filed it as an object joined to no kiln. Jorra held the iron while Sella finished her sentence.

“—feeding,” she said, and gave the end of it to the pot instead of to him.

Then he moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. He gave nobody a word about the waiting. Nobody under the roof spoke of it. No payer stood to the fraction and no slate was entered.

Tarn had come up the yard with the broom after it had been given its instruction, and had started his stroke on the flagstones before the sentence was ended. The bristles went over the shallow bright patch the stones had kept since Seed 22, the place where an older stroke had stopped and that nobody had entered. Behind the standing finger, with the sentence just finished and the iron still holding, the stroke checked for half a beat. It was not a stop. A stop would have been a thing that anybody could see; this was the small hesitation a man makes when his hand remembers a place his head has told him to forget. Then the broom went on toward the road without a word and without a turn of the head. The stones kept the shallow bright patch where this stroke too had stopped.

Neither man named the other’s silence. Jorra did not look up from the wheel; Tarn did not look toward the damper. The household straightened around the two silences the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and no word went into the air because there was no instruction under any roof for saying it.

To the surgeon’s register the half-beat had a shape and a length: the small distance between a hand’s intention and a hand’s arrest. He measured it without a clock, as he had once measured a pulse against his own breath, one interval and no figure written beside it. It was the sort of thing a man notes in the margin of a chart and never carries into the body of the note, because writing it there would make somebody answer for it. Here nobody had been given an instruction to answer, so the margin stayed a margin and the note stayed clean.

The boy felt the thing before he understood it. He had seen the half-check once, on Seed 22, and it had not come back in all the days between; and now it had come back changed, in the very breath of a sentence allowed to finish and a finger standing in the iron, with both men looking elsewhere. It was not a reconciliation. It was not a hearing and not a payment. No slate was entered, the loan that can hear was uncalled, and the nine stoppings stood where they had stood, nine objects on the interval tray and no tenth among them. He had known a chart to close over a finding with the neat margin showing, and had learned that the margin could be right and the finding worth nothing at two paces. This was that again, in a yard that smelled of hot clay and wet rope. What had widened was not the account but the room the account could not enter. Two things had been held at once under one roof without either man asking to be told what the other held, and the holding was the whole of it.

Beside the nine lay the damper as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln — the bite that holds a vessel whole on its way to the fire, and the bite that can take the hand off a man shut in a room with the breath going out of him, both in the same tongue of iron with no word in Grathic that would carry the two mercies in one mouthful. He had known a clamp that did the same work in a room whose walls were cleaner, and it had been a clamp and not a doctrine there too; here the word carried only the holding, and the taking went nameless, because a house gets to name one of its mercies and not both, and a thing with no name is a thing nobody can be made to answer for.

The morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left — the hand that had waited for a sentence to finish before it moved the iron — held and never spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered; and the repetition of it had not made it a change twice over. Beneath the loaf shelf the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own and not upon the first, a hand that goes under without looking knowing the board by heart or having been told not to look, the reason standing between the two and never settled. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper’s bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man’s broom checked half a beat behind it on the flagstones and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any of them and no payer named: he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The shallow bright patch on the flagstones where the stroke had stopped was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either. Three places, and he kept them with his hands full and unspent.

The two tellings of the room shut in the war stood side by side with no third — breath through the nose and breath in the throat, both saying it was not the war’s hand, one carried downriver with a date eighteen leagues east and taken by the yard for history, the other kept under this roof and spoken around — and the told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself, the telling from the Long Night in its own place and never added to the hearings of the full note.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. the loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row’s end and the palm’s breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under the front edge for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. The shallow pan’s gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own, and the field’s empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was where the yard had gone around it; neither was laid across the shelf’s two gaps or the fold’s one crust and its gap, and the two rooms did not touch. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, kept offstage and unspent. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew a line between any two of them. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and seventy-two days taking them down again, one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-two days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line, and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, and neither was swept at this hour, and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about either. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction, the extremities going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast that had never come under this roof, the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. He got none of them. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the two things stayed in their own hollows as everything else did. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The surgeon’s register read the whole of it as an inventory that was not a key and a list that was not a hand; the reading was exact and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it, with no case to begin and no instrument he could lift. That was the whole finding, and it went into its own hollow and stayed.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger’s-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent; the payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and seventy-two days; and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.

The Chronicle of Chapter 41

The last working week opened with the kilns brought up to heat and the boy one hundred and seventy days old under a roof where nobody counted him. Hot clay came in before the light, under tallow and wet wool, and the kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours with a gap. Under the floor the carried fire answered with four low pulls and the same silence; its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy days. Sella Vail gave her instruction to wool, fire, pot, broom and running water, never to the basket, said the house where another woman would say I, kept her half-pace and left the widow’s mark where it hung, for a morning and a shelf are not openings. She set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed.

Costs went where costs go in that house. A neighbour with a wet pot who asked what a firing cost was sent to the pot with The house has said it; she carried the question away level, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused. At the racks the yard went around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the broken birth-slates and the empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15, with a look and a wide step and no number. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze; Sella glanced at it once and did not touch it.

On the second morning men went low into the finger’s-width-open back room and got no name. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust ground small as meal on both forearms, told a pot it would do, knew its full note and withheld it from a High Kiln kiln-master who said a pot singing early would sing itself out early; no pot was lost. Then, while Sella’s unfinished sentence waited and Jorra’s finger stood in the cold iron of the damper, Tarn’s broom checked half a beat behind it on the flagstones and went on without a word or a turn of the head. He set the movement beside the fraction at the damper and the held beat beneath the loaf shelf --- three held changes and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer.

On the third day Tarn told the kindling Six counted. The next I don’t count, kept the seventh where the house could not check it and held the sentence about an ear under that roof at his teeth without saying the word that would call the loan. No collector came. In the small hours the file lay exact and worthless: six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings with no payer named. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent. The fire missed its fifth; the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
