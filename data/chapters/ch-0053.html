The Unmarked Turning

Days 210–212 · Grain 1–3, Y.H. 813 · Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln

The month goes out as a fire goes out — by degrees, and with nobody calling the hour. What the kiln took in Ember it does not give back in Grain. The coat on the peg keeps the shape it was left, and the seat keeps the hollow. Go around the line. Leave the crack showing. The river is not asked, and the fifth pull has no name.

— A saying of the Vail kiln-house at the turning into Grain

— Day 210 —

Grain 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)

He lay where the fold had taken him and left him, in the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. The hand outside the wool was down against the fold and had been down since the night before, and he did not go back into the dark to find a meaning in it. A miss was a miss. It lay where a tool lies where a man has set it down, and picking it up to turn it over would only wear the edge. The day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound, as days had come to him ever since he could remember coming to them at all. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season’s name instead of a cause. The kiln drew in fours beyond the wall, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short; then the gap. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered, and this was neither. The carried fire under the floor gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by a decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and ten days with no payer named to it. Grain came on beyond the finger’s-width door as Ember had come on before it, in the dark and without a boundary, and nobody under the roof drew a line at the turning.

He was two hundred and ten days old and six months and thirty days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in Sarn with a place to enter it. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. Two hundred and ten stood exact, complete and unspent, as the days before it stood, a count kept in a body that could not lift its own head; the fire’s fifth had had no payer for the whole of that count. The surgeon’s register took the number in and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for, and the room gave back the smell of hot clay and the gap in the drawing of the kiln. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and not enough to spend.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings because it came from water and not from a vessel, drawing out and slackening and coming again when it came. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its silence. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He did not join them. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; the river was not asked, the fire had no name over it, and the nails had been driven by a hand long dead and entered in nothing. So the three lay where they lay, separate as three tools laid out on a board, and he went over them in the order they came and did not lay one across another. The two rooms did not touch. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

Then the inventory of his own small body, taken in the order a surgeon takes it when the light is poor and the chart is absent. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the loaf shelf’s fold, stiff past the point where a child would still eat it, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third coming to stand in it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of a body taking its heat back and could not alter one step of it. The reading was exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. That was the terms of the work here: a man reduced a thousand shoulders and could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the two facts sat in him side by side and neither paid for the other.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel — no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and ten days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot beyond a wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand’s breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing a wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on a wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself at either. The told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid; Ember’s third true reading had gone up the clay road in Berr Clay’s locked box and was not brought back, and a figure read aloud was still not the figure that anyone had paid.

On the widened tray nine stoppings stood as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, with no payer named to any and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned, which now stood at its very end and had still not given. Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction held at the iron for a sentence to finish, the beat held under the loaf shelf’s front edge, and the half-check made once on the flagstones. The small scrape of a pot’s foot set down on a loft sill stood on a fourth place, the pale line read on a carried body on a fifth, and the held inch with all its answers on a sixth. Beside the dust and the two tellings of the room shut in the war, with a hand between the three, the burned-slate word lay on a place of its own — a direction come from a place, gone over boards and arrived nowhere, with no owner, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason, and no word in Grathic that would carry it into a chart a clerk keeps. The crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the shallow pan’s gone round ring and the field’s empty place lay on trays that did not touch. He had no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause, and no finger of his drawn across any of them. Nothing had been filled in to make a row come out even. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket. She had not looked into the basket on any day of his life. The instruction went out to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said the house where another woman would have said I, and the words went to the room and not to the boy in it. She told the fire that the thaw was come and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting go — and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. The widow’s mark hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, afternoon or dusk since. A morning under the roof was not an opening. There had been no opening in thirty days. Her private roll stayed unshown and another woman’s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

Standing with her back to the basket, she moved her lips along the loaf row, which stood with two unnamed gaps, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf’s front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied; the beat she had held under it on an older day stayed where it had been left. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. Then she fed him gruel from the house’s own dish, two fingers behind his ear, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and his mouth not wiped. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

A neighbour came to the door with a cost-question about what the new month’s firing would want now that the kiln month was done. Sella did not give her a figure and did not refuse her one. She sent the question to the pot and the weather: ask what a wet thing costs set down there, the house has said it. The neighbour took the answer away level, with nothing slopped out of it, and nothing was priced, refused or entered, and no slate in the house had a line for the question to lie on. The boy lay at the low angle and took the shape of it off the boards — a cost asked as a direction and answered with a pot is a cost that never arrives at a figure — and filed it where such things go and wrote no cause across it. It was the first morning of a new month and the district’s arithmetic went on in loaves, glances, arms and weather, and nobody corrected either of the two names by which the district called the time.

At the kindling the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside it lay Garl’s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since an older binding, held what light there was and gave it back. He said Yes, house to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, the word waiting on the far side of his teeth. The breath he should have spent at the top of the reach stayed unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath and did not spend it and went on sweeping. No collector came up or went down the clay road in the morning. The warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in the Debt Loft, and the small dry scrape of a pot’s foot set down on a sill stayed on a place of its own — not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not laid across the three held changes and not made into a cause, for a fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause and a cause would want a payer.

The old coordination took it through the small intake, rode the hold, and brought it down on the letting go, down with the breath rather than before it, as it had done once before in this house. It was remembered, not a change made again. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The asking is not always answered and the answer here had been given; both are objects on their own trays and neither is a sign.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle set out for him, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true. Grain had come on without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two month-names had been corrected; two hundred and twelve stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, the carried fire’s fifth had had no payer for two hundred and twelve days, the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given, and the count went forward into Day 213, Grain 4, Fourthday, with Grain 10 held as the first Account-day and market of the new month, where the seventh green and the seventh sighting were owed and must not come early. The seventh green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape, with no loaf, no slate and no sale.

The hand came down with the breath counted at Sella’s fire in the room that does not touch his. The thread at his mouth was still frozen and unwiped, one stiff leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the fire went on in the room that warmed him while the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

The Chronicle of Chapter 53

Ember gave way to Grain at the Vail kiln-house with no boundary drawn and neither month-name put right. The boy in the fold was two hundred and ten days old on the first morning and two hundred and twelve on the third, and no slate had a place to enter it. The carried fire missed its fifth for two hundred and twelve days; the river kept its note of water over water, never counted among the hearings; the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot. Three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing them wanted a payer and none stood to any.

Sella Vail gave her instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She said the house where another woman would have said I; she moved her lips along a two-gapped row and gave nobody a figure, kept her finger above the charcoal, and shut her hand half over nothing at the cold clay jar. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals. The widow’s mark went untouched: no opening in thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two days. The house kept Garl in objects, not in a story --- the salt coat, the hollow seat, the unswept line, the proverb gone to the pot and the tile debt without a name.

The firing thinned into a year’s ordinary tending; the yard went around the broken birth-slates, the pan’s gone ring, the bare trough whose water was called weather and the field’s empty place, each with a look and a wide step. Cost-questions went to the pot and the weather; a man’s number went back into his coat. Berr’s box stayed locked and Hessa’s slate unread. At the kindling Tarn told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count, brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped, kept nine stoppings and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and no collector came. Jorra came through with slate dust, kept his forearms apart, told a pot You’ll do, withheld its note from a wrong wind, and held his bent finger in the damper until Sella’s sentence reached the pot.

In the small hours the file was exact, complete and worth nothing. He asked his hand six times and was answered as the body answered: down before the counted breath, held for the small intake, down at once, held to the river’s low interval, held again for the small intake, and at the last held through a whole breath counted at Sella’s fire in the room that does not touch his. No payer was named; he could not close two inches to the pot handle or speak a number. The count went forward unspent, the fire in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, neither stopping for the ear.
