The Fourth Figure

Days 432–434 · Silt 8–10, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

The basket has heard four figures and holds none of them. The road carries the slate up and comes back without a word on it. A number said four times is four numbers; the cleanest part of the slate is the space where the signature would stand.

— A saying of the Low Kiln bucket-round, in the month of Silt

— Day 432 —

Silt 8, Y.H. 814, Eighthday

The small hours of the eighth day came down under the Vail roof the way silt comes down through still water — not in a fall but in a settling, gathering first at the skirting and lying longest where nothing stirred it, finding the thin places along the boards before it found the middle of the room. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and thirty-one exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. This was an eighth morning, and an eighth is only an eighth. Nothing had crossed into it from the days behind — not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The yard had said what the yard said and the weather had been weather, and both had gone where they went without leaving an instruction under this roof. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water under its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer, and nobody in the room gave the softness a name.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it six nights ago and would not try now. He could not lift it. He could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

He had once put a crust within reach of a man whose hand had stopped obeying him, thinking it a kindness, and watched the fingers come up an inch and fail; the crust had gone hard through the night and nobody in that room had thought to take it away. There had been a chart there and a thermometer and a man who would have known what a number counted. Here there was a woman who counted spoons. The two facts stood where they stood and neither bought the other, and the surgeon’s register went on taking the body in its order and wrote down nothing that a Court could read.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping; all three were true and the wanting went on all the same. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on in the dark — the old man’s exactness with its instrument laid down, the small animal body with its whole unspeakable impatience, and no verdict between them, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He could not speak a word of Grathic and had no way to ask for any of the three; the wanting was therefore complete and therefore useless, and that was the whole of the work.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as a table straightens when a weight is lifted off it. She told the coals it was the thaw and did not tell the basket, and said it in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on an Eighthday that had earned only its eighth. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side. Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. The pot stood where she set it, and the warmth in it went the way warmth goes in a cold room — out, slowly, by the whole of its surface, and faster where the metal met the air.

The small sounds came first, and he took them first because they did not lie. A pail went down on the flat stone by the well with a flat knock that had no note in it and wanted none. The rope at the yard well settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it, going quiet when the weight was taken; the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger’s width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist and eye and mouth and the half-beat in the gap. The fist was the part that arrived; the eye was the part that checked; the mouth was the part that said or held its peace. Between them stood the half-beat, a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist and put it back into a sleeve, the number arriving nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The figure read over the covered grain did not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between figure and price carried no signature. Walk-ons came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Eighthday grammar, and none looked into the wicker or gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights; a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. There was no market, no Cross, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its eighth.

His chest had been tight since the dark. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking.

Six counted. The next I don’t count, he said to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not made a habit, a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; a second would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood. Yes, house, he said to the fire, and did not turn his head toward the landing. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was; he took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and the sixth piece stood where the house could check it.

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

There was no Grathic word for the two bites of one piece of iron and he gave the house neither. He did not look at Sella while she spoke and moved before she had finished, which in that house was how a son answered a mother and a keeper answered a fire. The two quiet men kept their places under one roof a fifth time, their silences maintenance and not a compact; the boy made no rule and no joining of what he saw. He’s not Vail stayed sealed and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, gathering against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions without being called or paid.

The going of the light came on without a boundary drawn. It came the way cold comes down a chimney — not in a fall but in a settling, laying itself first on the iron and lying longest where nobody had stirred it. Sella Vail came to him and carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate, up the nineteen steps and under the roof and never into the Debt Loft. She said nothing to the basket of what had been read over it, and nothing of the figures left standing at the turnip heap, and nothing of the green and the shape, and nothing of the fourth subtraction gone up the road in a locked box. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; she did not tell the basket what the day had been and did not tell it to be still. A basket carried home is a pot carried home and set down, and no woman instructs the pot about the road. Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbers — and came back to the hairline at the wire. The boy watched the counting from under the shelf’s low angle and did not make a figure of it. A woman who counts spoons is not entering an account; she is keeping her hands occupied so that they will not do something else, and that is a thing a surgeon knows the shape of without being told. He had seen residents keep their hands busy in a room where somebody was dying, and it had meant nothing and everything, and he had never written it down.

Under the Vail kiln-house roof the whole grammar came back at both ends of the day as though it had never been interrupted, which is the way a house has of being tender. Sella went to the hearth before the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw and did not tell the basket, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the dry scrape of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, she said to the pot; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one, to the fire, and never to a person. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side. At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it lay and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A morning, a shelf, a pot, a yard, a market, a reading, a basket, a bed and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and an Account-day did not make an eighth come due. The house took up its grammar as though nothing had turned, and the boy lay at the low angle of the shelf and watched it take it up.

She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping at the fold lay in its empty place, found where put, still a finding and not a placing, with no third crust, no name, no gift and no payer. The crust stood horn-hard two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth was stiff in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

Garl Vail was in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door; the hollow wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody was allowed to fill; the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash; the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline showing between staple and wire; the unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. His name had gone unspoken four hundred and thirty-four days and the carried fire’s fifth had gone unpaid the same four hundred and thirty-four days, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Neither the debt nor the fifth pull was called, paid, forgiven or made to answer the other, and no lesson was made of any of it.

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing and kept every gap a gap. Eight pigs, a heap of turnips with three figures standing on it, a fist that went toward the watch and came back, a note that was not a price, a cup turned a quarter and set down, a small shape that turned its head too late, a hand that stopped two inches off a handle: these he set out in the order they had come, and the order was the finding, not the sum. He did not add them. A surgeon who adds findings before the last one is in has already made up his answer, and the answer is usually the one he wanted before he cut.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and the Drawing house’s small flat knock outside on its own place; neither the knock nor the bell’s told gap was made a seventh hearing. The heard bell stood beside the told bell of the Long Night with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three — the heard bell’s four and a gap, the boards’ four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, the carried fire’s four low pulls under the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The offered figure and its return, the well-asking answered with the weather, the second man’s turned slate and direction, the clerk’s entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the three likenesses of Slate Row, the neighbour’s near-reaching at the shelf, the reported second slate of the sixth, the hair-thin thaw of the seventh, the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions — each stayed on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, and none was stood up as a rule, a witness, a tenth hand, a sixth shaping, a doctrine or a payer. A doctrine is an account that has found a payer; none stood.

Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure. Four were four and not one account. The first was not made a thirteenth by the three that followed; the second supplied no signature; the third no doctrine; the fourth no payer. The twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 he did not lay across the four to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand with no key is made even on paper and paper is not a payer. The figure read was still not the figure paid. Berr’s kindness said low to the block and his your house, never I, stood on their own trays, believed and not owed; the Kindling collector’s reason, the clean voice’s missing name and the Long Night story’s empty name went on gathering toward the far-off accounting and were not paid. Tarn’s valuable ear stood more exposed in an ended term with the loan uncalled; Jorra Vail’s promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn against the burned slate at five. The surgeon’s unsent call to Nina stayed where it had been put, in a pocket that holds nothing, and was not paid again.

Answer four hundred and forty-four was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment. Six honest answers stood there, four hundred and thirty-nine through four hundred and forty-four, two to each of the three days, and not one was a doctrine. Four hundred and thirty-four exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-four days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire’s fifth went unpaid and Garl’s name went unspoken the same number, the man and the fire side by side and not one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. He stood no number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it and the tang thinner; the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it — the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body’s small assent to a sound — took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room.

He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into an eleventh day not yet lived and not brought into this one.

The eighth and ninth of Silt were given over to the small honest work of a Vail household, and a chronicler of that yard would have set down nothing but pails and rope and grain. Under the loaf shelf the boy lay in the basket at the low angle of the heat, taking the field in the surgeon’s old order: the crust gone into old horn two inches from his ear, the keeping at the fold found where put, the thread stiff at his mouth and unwiped. Cold had stopped at cheek and wrist as at the end of a written line. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle; the surgeon knew the wants useless and the infant wanted them all the same, and the quarrel went on without a verdict. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket at both ends of each day, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot’s back, kept the charcoal unread beneath her finger, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, and set the rim two inches beyond his hand. Tarn kept six pieces where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, and his sentence about an ear worth more than the debt stopped at the word that would call the loan. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel and withheld the full note from any master who would sing a pot before its time.
