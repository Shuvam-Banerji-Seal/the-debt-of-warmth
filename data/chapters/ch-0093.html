What the Fire Keeps

Days 330–332 · Ash 1–3, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

The fire keeps its own count and tells nobody; the hand that feeds it keeps another and has no one to tell it to.

— A proverb of the Low Kiln kiln-houses, told at the coming of Ash

— Day 330 —

Ash 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool had come down and laid itself back against the fold, and the room went on the way a room goes on after a man puts away a tool: nothing announced, nothing altered, only the shape of the thing left in the place where the next hand will find it without looking. It was the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, and the dry kiln heat was shut up in the dark the way a fist shuts over a coal, taking the water out of everything and giving nothing back. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf and had not moved, because there was no part of him that could move and change anything. Three hundred and twenty-nine had stood exact at the going of the light; three hundred and thirty stood now, six months and one hundred and fifty days, told by nobody, exact, complete, unspent, and of no use to anybody who might have wanted it. Under the board at his back the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and that silence had stood three hundred and thirty days with no payer named under it. Garl Vail stayed where he had been kept, in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed keeping the hollow twenty years of another man’s weight had worn into it and that nobody filled, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both, the twice-mended pot with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and thirty days, and the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with and was worse for being uncalled.

Ash had come on the way dark comes on, without a boundary drawn across it. The older women named the month by the weather that had not yet done what it promised and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. The dark was named and not explained, and the thinning Warm was called weather and entered nowhere. Nothing that had happened on the last day of the month gone out came in under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. Six arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space between, six readings six and not one account; the true subtractions rode locked up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it.

He took the room in as a field is taken in before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones generally do. The boards gave back heat in their own order, the near board and the next; the river went low over lower under the finger’s-width door, a sound he had learned to place nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; somewhere in the wall a coal settled and was quiet. None of these sounds had been sent to him and none had been kept for him. They arrived in the order they arrived and he filed them in that order, which was the only order he had ever been given and the only one he trusted.

The inventory came next, in the old order, because an inventory taken out of order is no inventory. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder through the dry heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself, and no third had come in during the night to make the row come out even; a third would have made a row, and a row of three would have wanted a reason, and there was no reason standing under it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond the three stops, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew what the ease on a face meant. The surgeon in him knew it before the infant could be hushed: the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a body that is losing its heat, the grimace that looks like a smile. A cold vessel cannot clot. A cold patient will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He had once known where to put his hands on a man in a room full of bright lights and loud machines; here he could not lift one hand two inches to his own mouth. The joke of it had gone grey with the telling and was still exact, which was worse than if it had stopped being funny. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle and bring it within reach. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the argument between them went on under the wool without verdict and without witness, and the argument was the work.

Sella Vail came to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order of the house for three hundred and thirty days, and she kept it the way she kept every other thing worth keeping, without explaining it to anybody who might have wanted to enter it. The room smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and, under them, the faint tallow of the wheel-shed that no amount of sweeping took out of a clay house. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed first on nothing and then on the back of a broken pot.

“It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, in the tone of a woman stating what the weather had already decided. She turned them with the back of the pot, not with a poker, and let them answer in their own way. Four low pulls came up out of them and the place where a fifth should have stood stayed as it had stood, and she did not supply the fifth and did not name who had taken it.

“The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot still in her hand, counting something under her breath: a small intake, a hold, a letting-go, all directed toward no opening and none of it arriving anywhere. Neither gap was figured. The boy put her three small acts on a place of their own with a finger between them and his three, for two people keeping the same sound under one roof are not one sound, and two likenesses are not a cause, and a cause wants a payer standing under it, and none stood.

“Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind,” she told the fold, and told the wind nothing, for the wind in this district had never once asked anybody to be told anything. Her finger lay on the top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps, and neither gap was given a figure. Once, and only once, her eyes went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze on the shelf by the wall; her hand went halfway over nothing and shut, and the burn on its back closed on air — two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, with the pale puckered skin inside it. The widow’s mark hung where it hung at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it and the cord oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. She did not touch it. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The sixth stood one morning off at Ash 2, a date and not a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot waited with the hairline of its original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle.

“Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she said to the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” She said the second to the fire and the first to the pot, and neither proverb went to a person, and the crack was left showing.

The dressed grain under its wool was turned with a touch lighter than the day before, and no figure was taken off it, and no handful was lifted. Her fingers went in and came out and did not close. The handful let fall at the rack end on the twenty-second of the month gone out stayed one object on its own place and was not made again, and the empty fingers of this morning went beside it as a keeping and not as a second, for two would have been a habit and three a way, and a habit and a way are both accounts that have found somebody to pay, and nobody had.

She set the pot down with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. There was a half-beat between the set-down and her letting go of the handle, and she did not look toward the bed in it. The note the pot gave as it settled was not quite full and not quite flat, and he filed it where a near-full thing must go and gave it no seventh place.

Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four and withheld its fifth, its payer without a name for three hundred and thirty days. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings. None of the three rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. The gap the bell had left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five of them from four sources and the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. The seventh place stayed empty, and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between.

The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The staying at the inch of the fifteenth and the early letting-go of the seventeenth, the handful let fall on the twenty-second and the thumb’s resting on the twenty-sixth sat each on its own place on the sixth with a finger between every pair and between them and everything else there — not a habit, not a way, not a fifth held change, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a gift and not a rule, and none made again on the first of Ash. Four held changes kept four places: the returned half-check with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat, the Frost 5 returned beat and the fourth on its own place. Four were four and not a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The two small objects of the hand stood as two and not as a habit, and the sixth place kept its room for all of them without asking any of them to move over.

Six arithmetics of the year rode locked up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln: one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space, six readings six and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, and was not laid across the house’s not-doings, or across the dressed grain covered under wool and read over by a number that did not know its cost, or across the four held changes, or across the handful, or across the thumb’s resting, or across the two small objects of the hand, or across his own gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause; the finger went between. He had once taken a hand out of a field before the dressing went on, and this was the same motion done with nothing but attention for a tool. Ash 1 was an ordinary tending day. The month’s first Account-day and market stood nine mornings off and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it.

The first light came into the room without asking for it, and found the pot’s crack before it found the fold. It was an ordinary Firstday. No market came, no Cross, no bell; the district went about its own accounting at a distance that reached this roof only as the small dry rasp of the river wind against the wool. Sella had gone to the fire as she always went first, and the instruction had gone to the coals and the wool and the pot and never to the basket, and the house had kept what the house kept without saying whose house it was. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and had not been touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and five openings in five months are five and not a rule.

In the last of the small hours, when the light had come and the note had gone down to its lowest under the finger’s-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach, the intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He had put his small hand into the order of things for the length of one note and taken it out again, and the order went on whether he did it or not.

He filed it as answer two hundred and thirty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and thirty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without a verdict in the small hours under the roof. No comfort was given and none was taken.

Three hundred and thirty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the first had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. Ash had come on without a boundary drawn and neither habit of naming the months was set right; the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, and it had bought nothing at two inches of air and was not for sale, and it went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and thirty-five; and the river’s low note came on under the finger’s-width door while Ash 1 stood one morning short of the sixth opening and was not brought toward it.

Every object stayed on its own place with a finger between, and nothing was joined, no cause laid across two and no payer put under any of it.

The finding that six are six and not one account sat on a place of its own with no payer under it. It was not laid across the house’s not-doings, the dressed grain covered under wool and read over by a number that did not know its cost, the four held changes, the handful let fall at the rack end, the thumb’s resting at the kindling, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. No doctrine stood up out of any of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of it. He had once signed a chart that made one account out of several hands, and the small exact keeping of things apart was the nearest honest work he had left.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and never once went into the Debt Loft. She went wide around the seven walked-around places, and the house came back around them the way a pot comes back around the hand that set it down. She said nothing of where they had been and gave the instruction to the fire, the wool and the pot and never to the basket. The house will keep it, she said where another woman would have said I. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because a going of the light and a bed are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months are six and not a rule.

She went to the hearth before the basket. It’s the thaw, she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-two days. A small intake, a hold and a letting-go went toward no opening, and neither gap was figured. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and gave neither gap a number; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The twice-mended pot came up thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack. If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one, she said to the pot, and to the fire, Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, and never to a person. The hairline of the original break stood between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and the rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The dressed grain lay turned under wool with a lighter touch and no figure and no handful; her fingers went in and came out without closing, and the grain did not know what any figure would have cost.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door water over water went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell’s gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth; the heard bell stayed beside the told bell of the Long Night and the told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between.

The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The staying at the inch of Bone 15, the early letting-go of Bone 17, the handful let fall at the rack end on Bone 22 and the thumb’s resting on the uncut seventh at the kindling on Bone 26 sat each on its own place with a finger between every pair; none was made again on the first, the second or the third of Ash, none was a habit, a way, a fifth held change, a hearing, a gift or a rule, and no payer stood under any. Four held changes stood on four places and were not a rule. The sixth opening had gone on a sixth place of its own on the second, with the same finger’s width around it, and no seventh place had been made ready and nobody stood who could have called it. Six arithmetics of the year rode locked up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space between, six readings six and not one account; the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across any of these. Ash’s first arithmetic belonged to its own morning, and a figure read before that morning would have been a figure carried forward and stood over a field that had not yet been worked.

In the small hours, with the yard quiet and the river’s note at its lowest under the finger’s-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and forty: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and forty honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it. The infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without a verdict in the small hours under the roof. No comfort was given and none was taken.

Three hundred and thirty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-two days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the third of Ash had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. Ash went out without a boundary drawn and neither habit of naming the months was set right; the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and forty; and the river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door while Ash went out without a boundary drawn. Ash came on as a dark room comes on, without a boundary drawn, and neither habit of naming its months was set right. On the first morning the boy came into his three hundred and thirtieth day, taking the room as a surgeon takes a field, by the small sounds first. One crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed, the thread at his mouth frozen, unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. In the yard she went wide of seven places with a look and a step and no number.

On the second morning the kilns were brought up for the dark month’s first firing. At the kiln mouth, before any instruction went to the basket and before a word, Sella stood over him with her hands still and put her left hand to the widow’s mark at her throat, the cord oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. She held it one breath, let go, lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot. The fire took, answering four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule; nobody entered a slate and no seventh was made due. He made no lesson or payment of it, noting only that the fifth pull was still missing.

The day after was an ordinary tending day. At the well Ilva told a woman what Ash had cost: Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, her hand flat on the sleeve where the strip lay unread. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside a collector sentence never forged; Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence.

Each morning and close the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note, took a small intake and let it go on the same low note, coming down as a man puts away a tool.
