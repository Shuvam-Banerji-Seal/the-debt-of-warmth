Ash 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-eight as a man lays down a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and the room went on around it the way a room goes on around a thing that has been put away. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof, in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, in the small hour before light, and the dark had weight and a smell: wet wool, tallow smoke, clay dust, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the ridge along the skirting board that nobody had swept since the year turned. Through the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and fifty-seven days; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Above him the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood; the bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into them. Three hundred and fifty-six had stood exact and three hundred and fifty-seven stood now, one more in a row nobody counted aloud, not round, not a threshold, only a day. Six months and one hundred and seventy-six days had become one hundred and seventy-seven and nobody under the roof said so. The fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number; the name of the man whose coat kept his shape on the peg behind the door was unspoken the same number.

The surgeon’s register took the body in its old order before the light came, because the light would not wait and the body would. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the three days behind to make the row come out even. A thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sill, in Grath, had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, knew it the way he had once known it across a table in a white room: a cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core, and the core gives heat up in the reverse order to the one in which it takes it back, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it exactly and it was worth nothing at two inches of air. The want underneath was louder and would not be argued down: lift the crust the two inches to his mouth, wipe the thread, set a hand that would close on the pot handle where the fingers could reach it. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work. The body humiliated him in the exactness and not in any large way: the drool frozen, the head useless, the crust gone past the point where any child would eat it, the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, a hand that could not lift itself to its own chin. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that smelled of disinfectant, and had once found a joke funny in a car park with a man who made jokes instead of asking; here he could not keep a fold of wool over one ear. He could still find the joke funny without laughing, which was a way of laughing that cost less and kept the same blade in it.

In the first grey Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, the way she had come to it at the going of the light and at every morning for three years. Fire before basket; the instruction to the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket. “It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing.

She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on the cord at her throat, the little-door burn half-closing on nothing; it was not touched on the twenty-eighth, because a morning and a hearth are not openings, and six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held at the kiln mouth on Ash 2 without a name and no seventh made due. It was an ordinary tending day in Ash with no market, no Cross, no bell and no collector on the clay road, and no runner came on any road; the directions of the fifth, the seventeenth and the twenty-third stood where they had been put and did not repeat. The boy took the morning apart by what was there and what was not, as he took a field apart before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the rasp of the pot’s wire, the tick of cooling clay, the low river under all of it. Nothing in the room had changed hands since the day before.

By the time Berr came up short off the clay road the two hundred had leaned in to the block and were leaning back again, and eight pigs had gone over the wet boards without anybody counting them aloud. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath: a man who smells of nothing has had the sweating and the dust and the ordinary evidence of a body taken off him by somebody whose accounts are neat. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, then set him on the bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread of drool frozen in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched. A market, a Cross, a going of the light, a hearth and a bed are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on Ash 2 without a name and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this evening for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the nine arithmetics riding away in a locked box; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

In the small hours the whole file came out on the towel at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel, and was checked and left as it lay. Nine arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash’s first, Ash’s second and Ash’s third — nine and not one account. The printed figure stood one in twelve, unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between. Both of Ash’s subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing out of nine, and it was not laid across the house’s not-doings, the dressed grain lying covered under wool with no figure and no handful, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting of Ash 9, the hand-at-the-sill breath of Ash 14, the holding of Ash 26 in the cold iron, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt and set a payer under both; the finger went between, not a decision and not a doctrine, only the placing of a small clean interval where an interval was due. He had learned that a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was invented to make a row come out even.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell stood four, four and a gap in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, not folded into the three counts and not made a seventh of the full note. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, Ilva’s ninth on a place of its own with a finger between it and Hessa Rale’s eighth, and nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the loan uncalled. The burned-slate word stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, the row at five askings with no witness; the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each apart with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid. Her short strip of notched birch bark stayed in her sleeve with no figure coming off the wood, and she was not made a reader of her own keeping and not made the speaker of any voice; the soft-cuffed clerk at the out-of-line landing stayed set for a measure nobody called, his slate turned inward, the market that had sold over him now gone away and leaving him where he had stood. Nothing in the file had been promoted by the reading. Nine were nine and no doctrine stood up out of them, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three counts in three places did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door the river kept water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Agreeing any two of the three would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were and the bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into them. Attention was the one thing no institution in Grath had thought to tax, and it was the whole of what he had spent, and it cost nobody anything and bought nothing that could be entered on a slate.

Three hundred and fifty-eight had stood exact behind the day; three hundred and fifty-nine stood exact at the going of the light and stood exact now in the small hours, six months and one hundred and seventy-eight days becoming one hundred and seventy-nine, told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. The count closed because the day closed and not because anybody called it; 359 was not round and not a threshold, only one more in a row nobody counted aloud, and he had long ago stopped expecting to be told what it counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg behind the door was unspoken the same number; the coat kept its broader shape and the crease at the left shoulder and gave nothing away. Nothing on the thirtieth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. There had been a market and a Cross and a bell and they belonged to the thirtieth and not to any day that had not earned them, and the reading had bought no lesson under the Vail roof and answered none of the held questions.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it — nine entries set down on nine places and not averaged into one account, every gap left gap, every instrument laid back where the next hand would find it without looking. It was a cold satisfaction and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, a hand that would close on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the thirtieth, and the argument was the work.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing, let the intake go and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; the note’s own finishing ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and ninety-four: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the nine arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and ninety-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Ash went out without a boundary drawn. Neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months — the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days — was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for having them. Stone came on in the dark beyond the door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The first firing of Stone stood far off and unbrought; the boy’s first birthday on Stone 7 (Day 366) stood beyond that, and the Hearth Drawing on Stone 12 (Day 371) further off still, and none was named to him under any roof. No ninth doctrine, no seventh hearing, sixth held object, seventh opening, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was supplied to make a row come out even. The far-off hand that would one day lay the district’s private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and ninety-four; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; nine arithmetics rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box with the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down; and through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 30 was done and Stone came on in the dark beyond the door. Forward, not closed.

The Chronicle of Chapter 102

Three days of Ash were lived under the Vail roof, the boy carried in the basket at the half-pace Sella Vail keeps from every open slate. The twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth were ordinary tending days. Sella went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot’s back and said The house will keep it; the charcoal stayed unread and the grain lay covered with no figure or handful. She went wide around seven yard places, widest around the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, said Six counted. The next I don’t count, and stopped at the calling word. Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence --- one iron with two bites. At the well Ilva’s hand went to her sleeve, the birch strip unread and her reaching filed as the ninth record-first hand; she was the speaker of no voice.

On the thirtieth the month spent its last Account-day and market. Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s finger’s width of shade and stood half a pace off through the reading. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road with the sweet green of lead glaze at his mouth and the smell of nothing under the hot clay of him; he wiped his thumb twice, read One in twelve, as the notice says twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it with your house, never I. He said something low to the block about kindness entered and went off up the road with the box locked and Ash’s third subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Nine arithmetics now stand behind one unsigned figure --- nine are nine and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space. Hessa carried the slate up toward Greybank unread, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed at the landing while the market sold over him as water over a stone.
