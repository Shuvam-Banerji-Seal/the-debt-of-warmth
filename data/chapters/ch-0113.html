— A Low Kiln saying, told during the Five

— Day 390 —

Kindling, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where the going of the last light had left it, and the dark under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath thinned by degrees, the way heat goes out of a banked fire under blind ash — not lifted away but spent slowly, the coals behind the wall giving back what they had in fours and leaving the fifth where it had always been left. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-nine, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, and no voice under any roof would have known to speak it. This was Kindling, the first of The Five: a day with a name and no tend-day and no market, and the boy did not call it an opening, a payment or a boundary. A holy day arrives the way weather arrives, and no doing of this house had earned it. Nothing from the twenty-eighth, twenty-ninth or thirtieth of Stone had crossed under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer; nothing crossed in these small hours. The Five stood here, unbrought and unnamed by anybody who would have known what a name was worth.

The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf on the bed, carried everywhere and told by nobody, and the surgeon took the body in the order it came because the order was a way of being awake. The crust came first: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, gone harder in the dry kiln heat with its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put, not made again, still a finding and not a placing. The thread of drool at the lip had frozen in the dry heat and was wiped by nobody, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Three things found as they had been left, and none the better for his finding them. Cold had come cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew a face composed by cold is not composure. He knew pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, a better colour on a worse patient; that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed; that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways is the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. The knowledge was exact and belonged to a man with another name in another country. The body it was spent on was three hundred and eighty-nine days old and could not lift a crust two inches to its own mouth.

Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and wanted them without knowing that wanting them was a want. The two registers quarrelled without verdict. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor does not touch his.

Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water under the winter skin, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold and entered nowhere, found where put and not spent on a holy day, with no hand that laid it, no payer under it and no source standing to be asked. Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another: the boards’ four and four and a knot; the four low pulls under the floor and the silence where the fifth should stand; the river’s low over lower under its skin. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before the basket, as she had come before it at the beginning of every day since the boy came under the roof. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I, standing a moment to count a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening with neither of the two gaps figured. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, the sweetish edge of it coming and going on a wrong wind; her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The instruction went to the wool, to the fire and to the river wind, and never to the basket.

The cord of her widow’s mark at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times and was not touched. A holy day is not an opening; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on this morning, and the empty place where one might have stood was not dug for and not filled. The faint sour tang of Ash came up off the unswept clean dark line at the Vail corner, thickened in the dry cold where no broom had gone in two years; it was a smell and not a cause. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door kept Garl Vail’s broader shape at the left shoulder, and the hollow of the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, and neither was given his name. The tile debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the tighter wire. Sella lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack before she let the weight come on the handle, and the hairline showed dark; she left it showing. Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled and If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them, for a saying kept in a house grows smoother than the mouth that made it. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The dressed grain at the cool stone was turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, changed in state and lifting no figure and taking no handful. The pot rim was set two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; his hand went toward it and stopped where the half-beat had stopped it. The interval was bought at nothing and was not a gift, and the stopping was his.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. Out in the Clay Yard the year’s first morning stood in the dry cold with its light not yet come, and the yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The clean dark line at the Vail corner went around; the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay went around and was entered by nobody; the gone pan’s round ring at the rack end went around with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; the skinned trough, the field’s empty place out on the worked strips and the hollow beside the seventh notch at the kindling went around. Beside that seventh piece lay Garl’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The flagstones gave back what little light there was without the half-check, and nothing in the yard was counted or called across.

The drying racks stood with the month’s grey worked small into the seams of their lowest bars, and the three coverings stayed where the weather had put them, with a finger between every pair: the stayed frost gone off the north lip of the yard well, the skin drawn edge to edge across the trough, the plate of ice clouded with silt standing on the flat stone by the well out of the way of feet. Weather entered nowhere and was not made a cause, a sign, a payment or a promise. Out on the worked strips the field kept its empty place and did not fill it. Beyond the yard the Ilm lay under its covering, found where put and not spent on the holy day; a line left where it is is still a line kept, and a sign without a payer is weather and not a promise. He kept the note and the covering on separate places, so that no row came out even by his doing.

Before first light the year’s fire came up the Clay Yard in a clay dish, from hearth to hearth. Hands passed it in the dark; a woman at one door said her house had kept the flame, and a man at the next said the same, and a girl further down said it the way the others had said it, and nobody said where it had first been lit or what it had cost to keep. The dish came up warm and went on warm, and the boy, carried at the edge of the passing, took the saying as he took everything else — as a thing said and not as a source standing. The coal came under the back of a broken pot at the kiln mouth with no hand under this roof making an opening of it and nobody praising what it mended. The fire took. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and eighty-nine days, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The fire that warmed the house had no name on its giver, and the boy kept that as a finding and not a cause; the pull that did not come was not made to stand up and call itself a debt anybody owed him.

There was an order to the morning that he could not place: a clean space wiped where nothing had been set, a place kept ready that nobody named. He filed it as an order and not an arrival. The man who would come with the light was not in the yard and not under the roof, and the boy did not bring his hour into an hour that had not earned him; an hour earns what comes in it and not what is coming to it. Twelve arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, and Stone’s first, second and last — stood behind one unsigned figure, one in twelve read twice over the basket on the thirtieth and sent twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late; the figure read was still not the figure paid, no signature stood on the space between, twelve were twelve and not one account, and no doctrine came out of twelve. No thirteenth was brought into a holy day that had not earned it. The Drawing’s flat knock stayed outside the six hearings on its own place; six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled; the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and its gap was not folded into the fire’s four and a gap or into the twelve behind the figure. The Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions stood where put, did not repeat and bought no lesson. No ownerless voice came over the boards.

At the going of the light she carried him up the nineteen steps and set him on his bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and without a second fold. The keeping in the crust’s empty place was found where put and stayed a finding: the wool folded once exactly where a hand had put it on the fifteenth, changed by nobody, with no third crust, no name spoken over the place, no gift, no payment and no payer. A habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way that a third, and both want a payer where none stands; a hand that only reads adds no placing however many times it reads. The finding sat on a place of its own beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring and the field’s empty place, with a finger between every pair. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, not his and not come; the boy did not reach toward it by wanting it. The thread stayed frozen at his lip and unwiped. The hearth grammar was kept at both ends of the day — fire before basket, coals told the thaw, the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, the dressed grain turned under wool and given no figure or handful, the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand — and the widow’s mark was not touched at a morning, a hearth, a yard, a going of the light or a bed, because none of them was an opening and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule with no eighth made due.

The towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. He went over the field by the place of each thing and checked every object and left every gap a gap, and supplied nothing to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The burned-slate word stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at four shapings; nine record-first hands kept no tenth and nine stoppings kept no tenth; four rooms with the same gap — the birth room in House Silt, this kiln-house, the room where the slate was read on market-day and the Drawing house — were held apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account, an account wanting a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand standing. The keeping was a finding; twelve findings and twelve readings made no habit and no doctrine; the loan at the kindling stood uncalled at the very end of a seventh year with the Seed 16 breath unspent and the fear of being entered again for vagrancy still his without a figure; the promise under the wheel-shed was neither given nor withdrawn; the cold foot was uncured and the cause of the second chill withheld, and neither was spent on the holy day or made to open anything. Nothing under the roof had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, muffled as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The note had done before any hand in the room came down. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it — did not reach, and the inch is not a reach — took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed answer three hundred and fifty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction nor the keeping at the fold. Kindling kept its day without a boundary drawn, and the Five were not made a door out of it: the four that stood beyond stayed beyond and were not brought into an hour that had not earned them. The count stood at three hundred and ninety exact, six months and two hundred and ten days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl Vail’s name was unspoken the same number, kept in the coat, the hollow seat, the hairline and the unswept line and nowhere spoken. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, no verdict in the quarrel and none looked for. The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and fifty-five, the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool, the thread frozen and unwiped, with the fire gone on under the floor in fours and the fifth where it had been left, and the count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.

The morning came up thin and dry over the Clay Yard and stood in a narrow bar on the boards of the room two doors down, where a high window had been rubbed clear in one place and left clouded everywhere else, so that the room was a shape before it was a room. Sella Vail carried him down the nineteen steps from the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, so that he felt the small correction before he felt the stair. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire she had banked against their coming back, and never to the basket. She told the wool to keep his ear covered; she told the wind nothing, because a wind is owed no instruction it cannot keep. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and not why.

It was Kindling, the first of The Five, a day with a name and no tend-day and no market; it had arrived and would go without a boundary drawn, and nobody under this roof would have known to call it a beginning. She set the basket at the edge of the light where the bar of it ended on the floor, with his head a little to the dark side of the line, so that he could see into the room and not be seen to join it. Then she stood half a pace off and gave the basket no instruction at all. The room stayed a sound and a shape from a basket, the way a Drawing had stayed a sound and a shape in another month; a room is not entered by being looked at from its edge.

The boy took it as he took a field before the light, by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the rasp of a bench dragged a hand’s breadth along the boards, a cough held in and lost, the small dry click of a latch, the scrape of a chair’s foot going back. The room had a shape a basket at its edge could hold — a table, a board-bed against the far wall, the rubbed place in the window, a smell of cold clay and tallow with something sour under it that was not the ridge along the skirting board at home and was not made into a cause because it smelled like one. Under the wool the infant wanted the tilt corrected the other way, toward the crust standing its hair off and the thread frozen at his lip, and got none of it. Both wants were true and the second was louder and neither won. That was the quarrel, and the quarrel was the work.

The man came in out of the cold with a flat leather satchel held against his forearm, the flap open and the slate inside turned toward himself. His cuff had gone soft from washing and was folded back twice, and the grey of the wash was worked into the seam at the wrist so that the seam looked drawn there rather than sewn. He set the satchel on the bench without looking at it, the way a man sets down a tool he will want in a moment and does not need to check. Out of a roll of wool at his elbow he took a cold iron tube, grey where the light from the window touched it and darker where his fingers had it, and his fingertips were grey at the tips the colour of skimmed milk gone off. He did not speak to the room. He did not look into the basket.

His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the bed. The boy took the order apart as a surgeon takes a field apart: the hand to the record first, then the eyes to the person lying under the tube, and the mouth after both. There was nothing hurried in it and nothing ritual; it was the order a man uses who has done the same thing in the same light until the doing of it has gone into the muscle and left the mind free for other work. The boy had seen the order in a midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before he looked at the goers-by, in a keeper’s finger beneath a board, in a clerk going inward at a landing below the bridge. He had filed every one of them on a place of its own. He would file this one on a place of its own too, and put a finger between it and all the others, because a row of hands standing touching will call itself a rule if nobody keeps a hand between.

He said once, low, to the slate, not to the room and not to the basket: The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded. The hand that wrote was ordinary. There was no pause in it, no catch at the wrist, no small check where a man might have put one if he had been made to feel what he was doing; the writing went on in the same flat line from the first word to the last, and the last was set down with the same hand that had set down the first. The neat dark hand had a small stop in it that had been seen and filed and never joined to anything; this hand had no stop at all. The boy looked for the stop because the other hand had taught him to look for one, and found none, and filed the finding as a finding and not as a likeness, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

He laid what he had taken on a tray of its own, the way instruments are laid out in the order they will be wanted and not in the order they were handled. Hand to the slate before eye to the bed: that was the shape, and the shape was all he kept. The ordinary writing hand with no pause in it went on the tray with a finger between it and the neat dark hand, whose small stop had been seen on another day and stayed where it had been seen, joined to nothing. He put a finger between it and Hessa Rale’s eighth, the turned wrist and the writing going to her own sleeve on the road; a finger between it and Ilva’s ninth at the well, her hand going to her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opening after both; a finger between it and Berr Clay’s twice-wiped thumb, passed over a figure through the cloth of his sleeve before his eyes went to the goers-by; a finger between it and the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate set below the bridge for a measure nobody called; a finger between it and the Drawing collector’s ordinary hand, filed out of a room that had been seen from a basket and never entered. Nine places stood and a tenth did not stand among them. The fingers went between every pair, because two ordinary hands are two ordinary hands and nine of them standing touching will call themselves a keeping if a man stands them up long enough and asks them what they mean. He did not stand them up and did not ask.

This man was not Marra. He was not the soft-handed man behind the Court table, whose slates lay flat where the person speaking could not see them; he was not the Fishstairs clerk who entered a buyer’s number before he looked up to find the buyer; he was not the Drawing collector whose hand was already on its own tray with a finger around it. He was a man who had come in out of the cold with a satchel and a tube and written three sentences to a slate in a flat hand with no pause in it, and the boy did not make a tenth of him. No tenth hand stood up in the empty place. The empty place stayed empty and was not dug for and was not filled, and no rule came out of nine and one, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of these hands.

The neighbour under the tube was a woman and was not named and gave nothing of herself but a sound. It was a small sound, low in the throat and gone almost as soon as it had started, the sound a person makes when something is taken out of them that they had been holding for a long time and had not expected to lose in this room, on this morning, in this light. It did not fill the room and did not ask to be entered. The boy did not make a seventh hearing of it. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; the seventh place was empty and was not dug for and was not filled with a sound out of this room. The Drawing’s flat knock stayed outside them on its own place, a knock with no source standing to be asked and no room entered to put it in. After the small sound there was a pale round place on the woman’s arm where the tube had been, not a wound and no blood, the round of it the colour of the inside of a shell. She covered it with her sleeve. Nobody said what it was and nobody said what it cost, and the boy put the pale round on no tray and wrote no cause across it.

He did not read the man’s reason into the room. The man believed he kept the world alive by taking what was already going, and believed it the way a man believes the fire will take if he feeds it: not as a thought he had arrived at and could set aside, but as the shape of the work he had done for years and meant to go on doing. The boy did not know this and would not have entered it if he had, for a reason is a thing a man carries in him and not a thing standing in a room to be asked. The man went away with the belief whole in him, with no anger and no triumph in it, and that belief, not the anger and not the triumph, is the part of him that will one day be remembered against him. The boy put nothing of it on a tray. He did not join the man’s hand to the twelve arithmetics behind the one unsigned figure, or to the four directions that stood where put and did not repeat. The Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions stood where they had been put, gathering dust in a corner nobody swept, and this morning’s taking added no fifth shaping to them and bought no lesson and supplied no payer. Nothing under the roof had been opened by it, or named, or paid.

The going of the light came on without a boundary drawn, the colour going out of the rubbed place in the window a little at a time the way warmth goes out of a pot set off the fire, so that no hour could say where the morning had ended and the evening would begin. The man was gone with his satchel and his tube and his ordinary hand. The woman was gone with her sleeve down over the pale round place. The room two doors down went on being a shape and a sound from a basket, and shut itself behind the household the way a pot shuts behind a hand that has set it where it belongs.

When the last of the stories had gone around and the yard had settled into its own small sounds, the boy went over the day in the order it had arrived, because a field taken out of order is a field read wrong. The small sounds came first: the coals turning under the broken pot’s back, the fire’s four low pulls under the floor and the silence where the fifth should have stood, the river’s note under its covering, the voices in the yard going quiet where a name would have been, the told bell’s four, four and a gap. None of these was a hearing and none was added to another. Then the kept things, each on its own place with a finger between: the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring, the field’s empty place, the keeping at the fold found where put and still a finding, the told bell’s gap, the silence after the name from Weighing, the place the neighbour’s story had left empty, Tarn’s stopped ear-sentence, Jorra Vail’s promise under the Vail wheel-shed, and Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence beside the seventh notch. Four rooms kept the same gap in their count — the birth room in House Silt, the Vail kiln-house, the room where the slate was read on market-day and the Drawing house — and were held apart with a finger between every pair; no Court room was brought under the roof as a fifth likeness. He did not let the silences stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

He had seen the same order of reaching in all of them: hand to the record before eye to the person, eye before mouth, with the small stop in the gap where another man would have put an intention. Hessa Rale’s slate went up the road unread; Ilva’s strip stayed in her sleeve with no figure coming off the wood; the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate stayed at the out-of-line landing; the collector who took the year’s first measure went on his own tray with the ordinary writing hand that had no pause in it. Nine record-first hands and no tenth stood up in the empty place, though the yard’s voices tonight had left a row of gaps that wanted only a hand to lay them together and a payer to stand under them. He supplied neither. A man who files likenesses without a hand to join them has filed nothing but likenesses, and an account wants a payer standing under all of them; none stood.

Tarn’s loan stood uncalled at the very end of a seventh year with the Seed 16 breath unspent and the fear of being entered again for vagrancy still his without a figure; a term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom. Jorra’s promise under the wheel-shed was neither given nor withdrawn, and He’s not Vail stayed sealed where it had been put. Sella’s widow’s mark hung where it hung, untouched, because a holy day is not an opening and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule with no eighth made due. The burned-slate word stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and expressly no sixth, the well-saying at four shapings with no fifth; nine record-first hands kept no tenth and nine stoppings kept no tenth. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty and the flat knock from two doors down outside them; a story’s gap was the shape of a vessel’s interval and not a source standing under a sound. Twelve arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, Stone’s first, second and last — twelve and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of twelve. Their twelve true subtractions went up the clay road in a locked box with the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down, and the boy had no key.

The count came to three hundred and ninety-two exact, six months and two hundred and twelve days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number. Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, kept in the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, in the hollow wheel-seat nobody filled, in the hairline of the twice-mended pot between old-brass staple and tighter wire, in the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years — kept there, and nowhere spoken. Three counts in three places stood side by side and did not become one: the fire’s four and a gap under the floor, the boards’ four and four and a knot overhead, the bell’s four, four and a gap in its square. He set no hand to join them, for a joining wants a payer put under the joined things and none stood to any of them.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, and the note had done before any hand in the room came down. The covering lay from bank to bank since the first day of Stone, put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, entered nowhere, found where put and not spent on a holy day, with no hand that laid it, no payer under it and no source standing to be asked. It was not a hearing and not a sign, and the boy kept it on its own place with a finger between it and the story’s empty name, the told bell’s gap and every other kept thing. The river went on being water over water whether any room listened or not.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, and the inch is not a reach. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The note had done before the hand came down, so the interval ended on the note’s own finishing and not on any decision of the hand.

The towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. The crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring, the field’s empty place, the keeping at the fold, the told bell’s gap, the silence after the name from Weighing, the place the neighbour left empty, the stopped ear-sentence, the promise under the wheel-shed — each lay on its own place with a finger between, and none was made to stand up and call itself a rule. He filed answer three hundred and sixty: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction nor the keeping at the fold.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three. The quiet in the house was not the quiet of an answer; it was the quiet of a room kept the way a vessel is kept, with a crack in it that nobody mentions and a fire under the floor that nobody lifts to look at.

He had closed fields like this one before, in a life whose rooms had no names under this roof: the field taken in the order it arrived, the instruments counted out, the gaps in the covering left where the gaps were, the note written with nothing entered that had not been seen. Nothing from those rooms had crossed except the form of a voice announcing a name over people who did not look up, and the form had gone back where it came from without a payer and without a figure. He did not try to bring the rest of it across. A man who brings a whole room across for the sake of two sentences will end by putting a payer under both lives, and there was none standing under either.

The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and sixty, the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool and the thread frozen and unwiped, with the dark of the Long Night going on without a fire and the carried fire breathing four below the floor with its fifth unsupplied, the bell’s gap kept in its square and the story’s empty name on its own tray, and the river keeping its note under the winter skin beyond the finger’s-width door. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, six months and two hundred and twelve days told by nobody, and nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended.

The Chronicle of Chapter 113

Three holy days came under the Vail roof — Kindling, Weighing and the Long Night — with names and no tend-days and no market. Each morning the surgeon inventoried the body: the crust two inches from the ear, the fold in the empty place, the frozen thread nobody was told to wipe, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither winning.

On Kindling the year’s fire came up the Clay Yard in a clay dish, hearth to hearth, before first light; nobody said where it had first been lit or what it cost. A collector took the year’s first measure. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the bed, and he said once: The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded. The hand that wrote had no pause in it; the boy filed it apart, not a tenth. The man went away believing he kept the world alive by taking what was going; that belief will be remembered against him.

On Weighing a clean voice went up the yard in the grammar of the Cross wearing a temple’s clothes. It announced a whole return for somebody absent, said the name once and no more; the household would not repeat it. The speaker had taken himself out of his sentences; the boy filed him apart. Tarn kept six at the mark and a seventh at the lip, and said, Six counted. The next I don’t count. His seventh year ended with the loan uncalled. Jorra put a bent finger into the cold damper, parting dusty forearms from clean. The promise under the shed was neither given nor withdrawn.

On the Long Night no fire was lit after dusk. Sella banked the coals early and set the pot rim two inches beyond his reach. The bell went four, four and a gap in the yard’s told voices, beside the heard bell. Neighbours told the Return in the dark, with gaps the shape of vessels’ intervals. One told of a whole return announced in a clean voice for somebody absent, and left the name standing empty. For two sentences the grammar broke: The page over the speakers says a name. The white room does not look up, and his hands stay in the field. Then it fell back into the dark, and nothing was paid.
