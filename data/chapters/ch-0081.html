The Last Tend of Frost

Days 294–296 · Frost 25–27, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A stick keeps what a slate would tax. A shelf keeps what a stick cannot say. The month keeps neither, and goes.

— A saying of the firing women at the yard well in Low Kiln, told in Frost

— Day 294 —

Frost 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813

The hand had been laid down against the fold at the low angle of the loaf shelf as a man puts a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and it lay there still in the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof while Frost the twenty-fifth came on by degrees and from the outside, the way cold comes into a banked house and takes the room one board at a time. The boy came into his two hundred and ninety-fourth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and fourteen days were told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; the count had gone from two hundred and ninety-three to two hundred and ninety-four the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. A hundred and sixty-two honest answers stood behind him, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-four days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Frost 25 had come on its own ordinary Fifthday, with no market, no reading at the Cross and no bell, and it asked nothing of the boy under the fold except that he lie there and be warm where he could.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on the bed, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease at all. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and fourteen days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order — crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting — and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. It was an old habit, this standing ready with a fact nobody would ask for, and he kept it the way a house keeps its charcoal under a finger and does not look. He had signed a chart once without reading the note in the margin and carried that shape into a country where notes are turned face-down toward floorboards; here he read every note he was not given and entered none of them. The dry heat had drawn the water out of the thread at his mouth and left it stiff as wire drawn through tallow; he could not lift a hand to wipe it, and no instruction had ever been given to anybody to wipe it, and the two facts stood side by side without either becoming a complaint. A man who has spent his life putting things right in rooms where somebody always called for him finds it a strange discipline to lie under a shelf and call nothing. He had been learning it since his first morning under this roof and it had not got easier.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety-four days; at the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10 and again at the market on Frost 20, stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night; no bell had come on this morning and he would not lend an ear to one that had not earned it.

The told figure stood beside the paid and the space between them had no signature. Frost’s first arithmetic and Frost’s second rode locked up the clay road in 's box toward High Kiln, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; two readings were two sayings and not one account, and neither had closed the hand that went in on Seed 15 or the man carried out on Day 188. The dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth at the rack end, turned with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure, so that any number travelling over it would not know what it cost. The Frost 14 saying stood on a place of its own with its first, second and third shapings, a finger between every pair, beside 's eighth record-first hand with a finger between that and the nearest shaping; not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining and not paid, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of the three stood anywhere to be asked. Neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words — the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost 30, the month’s third and last Account-day, stood five mornings ahead, and the boy made nothing of the nearness and did not let it stand up as a sign, for a sign wants somebody to read it and nobody under this roof had been given the office.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, as she had gone every morning of her life in this house, and gave the instruction to the fire and never to the basket. She told it the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. The small hours had laid a film of grey over everything, and she moved through it with her hands already knowing what they had to do: the wool told to keep the river wind off his ears, the pot told its word, the broom told to go around, and never a word spoken to the child in the fold who could not have answered. When she came to the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it was unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. At the rack end she lifted a corner of the wool over the dressed grain to feel for damp and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not look toward the bed.

The boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had learned to watch a surgeon’s hand leave a field — for what it did not touch — and filed the not-touching on its own place, maintenance and not a message. Instructions went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water; the basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He wanted the thread wiped and the crust lifted and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and he got none of the three and could not ask for any of them. There was no bitterness in the not-asking either, which was the second small betrayal of the morning, and he did not give it a place to stand.

Tarn was at the kindling with the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count in the tone of a man stating the weather, and when a woman went by the door wanting the thaw early and another wanting it late he said Yes, house to each so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of the clay road and had stood there since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name — a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of the Debt Loft, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. The boy, who had once counted the exits in a room without deciding to, kept the seventh where Tarn had set it and entered no figure against it.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door at the edge of the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady. To a pot on the wheel he said You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the boy took it as a direction and a sound. When Sella’s sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no Grathic word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set the iron on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; he’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. Jorra did not look toward the bed, and the boy did not ask him to.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-four days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; Frost’s first arithmetic and Frost’s second rode locked in Berr Clay’s box up the road toward High Kiln, and the keeping of a man by not-doings stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady. To a pot on the wheel he said You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound, and no more a room than the loft was. When Sella’s sentence came low and unfinished his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no Grathic word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set the iron on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-six days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; the keeping of him by not-doings stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on the bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. When she came to the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath was unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The next opening stands in a later month’s first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward; four openings in four months are four and not a rule. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with the handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed.

The boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had learned to watch a surgeon’s hand leave a field — for what it did not touch — and filed the not-touching on its own place, maintenance and not a message. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. He wanted the thread wiped and the crust lifted and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and he got none of the three and could not ask for any of them. There was no bitterness in the not-asking either, which was the second small betrayal of the day, and he did not give it a place to stand.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-six days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. In the small hours he turned the file out on the towel at the head of the bed as a surgeon turns instruments out on a tray, each in its place, none of them a cure. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell stood beside the told with its four, four and a gap, and no bell had come on this day and none was lent an ear it had not earned. The told figure stood beside the paid with the space between them unsigned; Frost’s two arithmetics were locked up the road and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and two sayings were two sayings and not one account.

The three shapings of the Frost 14 saying stood on a place of their own beside Hessa Rale’s eighth record-first hand, with a finger between every pair. Not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a payment, and not joined to Ilva’s strip in a sleeve that was a pace off yesterday and is not under this roof tonight, nor to Berr’s twice-wiped thumb, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate at the out-of-line landing, nor to the charcoal beneath the shelf. A finding with no throat is not a witness however often it is said, and a shaping is not an opening of a slate nor a figure put in a runner’s hand. Ilva’s ninth hand stayed nine with the notched birch strip unread and no figure coming off the stick, and he filed no tenth hand; the count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the burned-slate word at four, the shut-room tellings at two. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, and no eighth came to either. The house’s not-doings stayed on a place apart from his own gaps with a finger between them, for want of a payer under either. Nothing stood up and called itself a rule.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the next room. At the very end of the day a small intake came from the room whose floor does not touch his — the breath of a woman counting what she will not say aloud — and he took it at its top and let it go on the same note, whole and unjoined. He did not carry it into the carried fire’s missing fifth, did not cross the room’s letting-go with the river’s low note of water over water, did not borrow a house count, did not supply a pull under the floor. The hand came down on the river’s note as it always did, and the gap stayed a gap. The hundred and sixty-eighth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule. The clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant’s plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. One hundred and sixty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing. Two hundred and ninety-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixteen days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended; no thread was opened and none was closed. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger’s width and nothing that had moved. The river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door after the hand came down. The crust lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed, and out on the flat stone by the well, out of the path of feet, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt, and Frost went on without a boundary drawn.

The Chronicle of Chapter 81

Frost went out as it had come in, without a boundary drawn. Its three mornings came each on its own, with no market, no reading at the Cross and no bell, taking the house one board at a time. Nobody under any roof told the boy that his days had come to two hundred and ninety-six. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed.

He took the body: a stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third to even the row; the thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. The grain turned beneath the wool with a lighter touch each time and named no cost; the pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not touch the widow’s mark, for a morning is not an opening. The yard went wide around its seven places.

On the twenty-sixth Ilva drew at the yard well, clay pale in her nails. Asked what the month’s firings had taken, her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there; the notched birch strip was not taken out, read or joined to the open slate lying near. Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. The boy laid the order of the reaching --- hand to record, eyes to asker, mouth after both --- on the ninth hand’s own place; he filed no tenth.

Tarn at the kindling kept six where the house could check and a seventh at the lip, stopped the ear-sentence at the word that would call the loan as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the loan stayed uncalled and no collector came on the clay road. Jorra Vail spoke to the wheel before any person, put his bent finger into the cold damper and withheld the note he knew three turns ahead; he’s not Vail stayed sealed. Garl stayed in objects, his name unspoken two hundred and ninety-six days.

The file lay out on the towel at the close: six hearings with the seventh place empty, the told figure beside the paid, nine hands and nine stoppings with no tenth. The hand filed the hundred and sixty-third through the hundred and sixty-eighth answers, each worth nothing at two inches of air. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The river’s low note went quiet under the door, and the plate of ice stood clouded with silt.
