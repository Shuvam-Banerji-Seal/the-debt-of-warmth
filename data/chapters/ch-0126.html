What the Road Carries

Days 429–431 · Silt 5–7, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

Two slates under one arm, both turned to the sleeve; the road carries and reads neither. A hair-thin thaw where no broom goes; the river softens at one edge under its skin. Both are weather, and weather keeps no account.

— A saying of the Low Kiln bucket-round, in the month of Silt

— Day 429 —

Silt 5, Y.H. 814, Fifthday

The small hours came down under the Vail roof without a hand to name them, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and twenty-eight exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-eight days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on in the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. It was a fifth morning. Nothing had come into it from the days behind — not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The dark at the finger’s-width door thinned by no degree anybody measured, and under the river the low note of water over water went on beneath its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer, and nobody in the room gave the softness a name.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it four nights ago and would not try now. He could not lift it. He could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

He had once stood in a room kept warm by a number and watched a man on a table go cold from the fingers and the toes inward while the room insisted he was warm. The air had been right about itself and wrong about the man, and the two facts had stood side by side without either paying for the other. He had known the afterdrop then as he knew it now: the core giving up its heat to the surface, the surface pink and lying, the cold travelling the wrong way. That room was in another country and another time and none of it came under this roof; the surgeon’s register went on taking the body in its order and wrote down nothing that a Court could read.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping; all three were true and the wanting went on all the same. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on in the dark — the old man’s exactness with its instrument laid down, the small animal body with its whole unspeakable impatience, and no verdict between them, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He could not speak a word of Grathic and had no way to ask for any of the three; the wanting was therefore complete and therefore useless, and that was the whole of the work.

First grey came without a bell. It came the way light comes into a room whose shutter has been set against a cold wind — by degrees nobody marked, settling on the backs of chairs before it reached the floor. Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

It’s the thaw, she told the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. The house will keep it, she said to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Fifthday that had earned only its fifth. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this morning.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, because a line that looks joined can still be a line that will not hold; and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; a basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. In the Clay Yard she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and the yard went on about its small honest work. At the well a rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining as the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken; the basket was not told what the sound was and was not told to be still.

Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said Yes, house to the fire and did not turn his head toward the landing. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking. Six counted. The next I don’t count, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. He cut nothing, counted nothing beyond the six, spent nothing. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. He’s not Vail stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn. The two quiet men stayed unjoined, their silences maintenance and not a compact, and the boy found both where put on places of his own with a finger between, making no custom and no rule of what he had twice seen.

In the Clay Yard the small sounds came first, and he took them first because they did not lie. He took each apart by fist and eye and mouth and the half-beat in the gap. The fist was the part that arrived. The eye was the part that checked. The mouth was the part that said or held its peace. Between them stood the half-beat, and the half-beat was a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. A man might have called the gap a hesitation and the hesitation a meaning; the surgeon had seen too many gaps in too many rooms to put a meaning into one simply because it had the shape of a pause. He gave it no intention, and it lay where it lay, exact and complete and worth nothing.

One wide circuit went around the seven walked-around places, once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it. The space beneath the lowest rack kept its broken birth-slates entered by nobody. The gone pan kept its shallow round ring. The trough kept its water. The field’s empty place lay out on the worked strips with nobody standing in it. And the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out, was gone around widest of all. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The step was a keeping and not a sign. He had learned, in a country that had no word for what he had lost, to walk around an empty place without putting a name in it; the wide step was the whole of what could be done and was therefore done exactly.

At the trough the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf’s stopping and not laid across the ridge at the corner. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated. Level is not honest. A level is not a promise. He had read a great many straight lines in his life and knew that a straight line drawn in a vessel says only where the water came to rest, and says nothing at all about the hand that set the vessel down or the cold that made the water hold still.

At the Vail corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey called the thaw beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. Beside it the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place, and a finger’s width stood between the two greys. A thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off the river was less cold at one edge, where the low note of water over water ran nearer under the winter skin and the tang came thinner; he found it where put, called it weather, and did not look twice. It was not a fourth covering, not a sign, not a payment and not a custom. Nobody swept it, nobody owned the day, and no hand stood under it to make it mean anything.

Costs arrived as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at the well opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The figure read over the covered grain did not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between the figure and the price carried no signature. He did not write a cause across any of them. A direction is a direction, and weather is weather, and a space with no name in it stays a space.

The small hours of the sixth day came down through the house the way cold comes down a stairwell — not in a fall but in a settling, gathering first in the corners, lying longest where nothing stirred it, finding the thin places along the boards before it found the middle of the room. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and twenty-nine exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-nine days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. This was a sixth morning, and a sixth is only a sixth. Nothing had crossed into it from the fifth day behind — not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The yard had said what the yard said and the weather had been weather, and both had gone where they went without leaving an instruction under this roof.

Through the finger’s-width door the dark thinned by no degree anybody measured. Under the river the low note of water over water went on beneath its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer, and nobody in the room gave the softness a name. He had found it where it lay on the day behind and had called it weather, and calling it weather had cost him nothing and bought him nothing, and he did not call it anything again this morning. A finding repeated is still a finding. A name given twice wants a hand that named twice, and a rule wants a third, and both want a payer standing under them where none stood.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would once have tried to eat it and would not try now, and the not-trying was not a lesson in anything. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. All three were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on in the dark — the old man’s exactness with its instrument laid down, the small animal body with its whole unspeakable impatience, and no verdict between them, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He could not speak a word of Grathic and had no way to ask for any of the three; the wanting was therefore complete and therefore useless, and that was the whole of the work.

There had been a room in the other life where a man lay under a sheet and the chart at the foot of the bed said one thing and the man’s fingers said another, and the surgeon had read the chart by what it left out rather than by what it set down, because a neat hand can enter a thing neatly and still not have looked at it. He had learned to read a house the same way — not by what it would not survive, which was the reading any fool could make of a cold room and a banked fire, but by what it would not say. This house would not say the man’s name. This house would not say what the fifth pull under the floor was owed to. It would not say why a fold lay in an empty place or where the hand went that had put it there. The refusals stood in the room like objects, exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he filed them where they lay without putting a cause across any of them. That room was in another country and another time and none of it came under this roof; the surgeon’s register went on taking the body in its order and wrote down nothing a Court could read.

First grey came without a bell. It came the way light comes into a room whose shutter has been set against a cold wind — by degrees nobody marked, settling on the backs of chairs before it reached the floor, showing the edges of things before it showed their faces. Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

It’s the thaw, she told the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that has done this work before and expects to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. The house will keep it, she said to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither of them has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Sixthday that had earned only its sixth. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this morning. There was a sixth in the counting of the kindling and a sixth in the answer last filed and a sixth in the day, and the three sixes stood on three places with a finger between every pair; he did not lay them across one another to make a row come out even, for a row that comes out even is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, because a line that looks joined can still be a line that will not hold; and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A fist of it was taken up from the board, closed, turned once in the palm to feel its weight, judged by the hand that carried it and set back where it had lain; no figure went over that either. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. The pot stood where she set it, and the warmth in it went the way warmth goes in a cold room — out, slowly, by the whole of its surface, and faster where the metal met the air.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; a basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. In the Clay Yard she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and the yard went on about its small honest work. At the yard well a rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken; the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. The basket was not told what the sound was and was not told to be still.

Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said Yes, house to the fire and did not turn his head toward the landing. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking. Six counted. The next I don’t count, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. The sentence was found where put and was not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not a hearing and not a rule.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. His chest had been tight since the dark. He cut nothing, counted nothing beyond the six, spent nothing; and the boy, who had twice seen a hand withdraw from a field before the dressing went on, found the withdrawal where put on a place of its own and did not make a custom of it on a morning that had earned only its sixth.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. There was no Grathic word for the two bites of one piece of iron, and he gave the house neither. He’s not Vail stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, gathering where it lay against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions without being called or paid. The two quiet men kept their places under one roof a third time, and the boy found both where put with a finger between, their silences maintenance and not a compact, no rule stood up out of what he had seen and no joining made that was not his to make.

The morning kept its grammar and asked nothing for it. A loaf was pushed a finger’s width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. Women came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Sixthday grammar of hand before eye and mouth after both; none looked into the wicker, and none gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights, for a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. There was no market, no Cross, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its sixth. The softening at the river’s skin stayed where put and was called weather; the neighbour’s near-reaching at the shelf stayed on its own place from the day behind and was not told again or made a custom; the two sightings stayed on separate trays with no eighth of either. Nothing from the fifth crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

Beside the work, on places of their own with a finger between every pair, stood the things already found: the offered figure and its return, not offered again; the asking at the well answered with the weather; the second man’s turned slate and the direction he went; the first man’s face; the clerk’s entered figure brought up the yard from the out-of-line landing below the north bridge as a report and never as a sighting; the fifth shaping of the well-saying; the three likenesses of Slate Row. Ilva was offstage with her count still in wood, her strip in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth. Hessa Rale’s narrow slate stayed unread up the clay road, her eighth hand eighth and joined to none. Berr Clay was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his your house and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed. None of these was told again. None was made a witness, a reader, a sixth shaping, a custom or a rule. None was joined to another on a day that had earned only its sixth, for a joining wants a hand to make it and the only hand that would have made it lay outside the wool and could not close two inches on the rim of the basket.

One wide circuit went around the seven walked-around places, once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number; widest around the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. At the trough the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf’s stopping and not laid across the ridge at the Vail corner. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated; level is not honest, and a level is not a promise. At the corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years, and the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between; a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. He found both where put and did not look twice, and went back to the small sounds because the small sounds did not lie.

Answer four hundred and thirty-five came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road behind one unsigned figure, the printed one in twelve, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk’s entered figure, the two market sightings, the neighbour’s near-reaching, the widow’s mark nor the keeping at the fold. It was the third of six that this chapter would hold, two to each of its days, and not one a doctrine.

Four hundred and thirty exact, six months and two hundred and fifty days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail’s name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; his name had not crossed a threshold under this roof in four hundred and thirty days and no account had gone up the road to call his debt, and neither the count of his absence nor the count of the missing pull had been laid across the other. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth, and a number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer where none stood. Silt 10 waited four mornings off as a date and was not brought into this morning.

Sella carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place. In the small hours the towel would come out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and the file would turn out exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The surgeon’s exactness and the infant’s want stood side by side, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the quarrel the work. He finished on his plate what was put before him and could not reach the plate. The hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and thirty-five, as a man puts away a tool where a next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. Forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.

Between them stood the half-beat, a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Into this went a word, and no body stood under it.

It came at the well from mouth to mouth among the bucket-round. A woman at the rail, with her eyes on her pail and her hand already going back to the rope, said low into the next woman’s sleeve that the woman who carries the slate had taken up a second. The next said it to the next while the cold-stiffened rope complained on its knot, and the third added the part about the writing, and the fourth had nothing to add and passed it on anyway. She’s carrying a second, one said. A narrower one, said another. Edge under her arm, the writing turned the same way in. Nobody asked whose writing. Nobody said whose slate. The word went around the well the way a coal goes from hand to hand in a dark room — passed on because the hand that had it wanted to be rid of it, not because any of them had seen it warm. When it came back to the woman who had first said it, she was drawing water and did not say it again.

No speaker stood under the word. No name was spoken over either slate. Berr Clay was up the clay road with the box locked and the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down, and his slate was in the keeping of a woman who was not here to be asked; the second slate belonged to the same mouth’s saying and to no body in the yard. One of the mouths gave the order of it, and gave it as though somebody had stood near enough to see: her hand went to the slates before her eye went to the yard, and her mouth went after both. Between hand and eye stood the half-beat, and in the telling it stood as a place and not a person, the interval where a lesser observer would have put an intention. There was no intention in it. An intention wants a hand under it, and the only hands at the well were busy with pails.

No room was entered in the telling. No threshold was crossed. No slate was opened within reach of the basket, and no figure came off either slate, narrow or wide. The woman who carried the second thing was not made a reader of it by a word at a well; she was a shape in a mouth and no witness stood under the shape. A report is not a sighting. A saying that has gone from mouth to mouth is a saying that has been carried, and carrying is not reading, and nobody in the yard had done the one thing that would have made the second slate an account.

He had worked in a building where a thing said in one room arrived three doors down with the first speaker gone clean out of it, and the chart at the end of the corridor carried no line for it, because a chart is a vessel for what somebody has put into it and an unentered thing has nowhere to stand. He had learned to read such reports by the order of the mouths and not by their heat. This one came in the order hand, eye, mouth, with the half-beat standing between hand and eye, and he took it apart the way he took apart a wound — by what lay where, not by any meaning it offered.

The two greys at the corner kept their finger between them, the called-gone beside the left-in-place, and he did not lay one across the other to make a row come out even. In the other life he had stood at a ward window and watched frost leave the glass in two lines that met at a corner, and a nurse had called it the weather and gone on with her count; he had learned to read such lines by the place where they stopped, not by the name anybody gave them. These lines stopped where they stopped. He wrote nothing down that any Court could read.

The word that had gone around the well on the sixth day was not told again. The woman who carried the second slate was not standing in the yard to be asked, and the second slate stayed a shape in a mouth with no figure off it, narrow or wide, on a place of its own. Nobody made a reader of her out of a saying, and nobody made a witness of the half-beat in the gap. Silt 7 had its own work and had not earned a telling of the sixth. Nothing crossed under the roof from the corner or the well as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and the house took up its grammar as though nothing had turned.

The going of the light came without anyone calling it. Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order.

It’s the thaw, she told the coals, and did not tell the basket. She said it in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted — spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered — and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Seventhday that had earned only its seventh. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. The pot stood where she set it and the warmth in it went the way warmth goes in a cold room — out, slowly, by the whole of its surface, and faster where the metal met the air.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf, at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping at the fold in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. He could not speak a word of Grathic and had no way to ask for any of the three things the body wanted, and the wanting was complete and therefore useless. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and thirty-one days; nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap: the pail’s flat knock on the stone, the rope’s cold-stiffened rasp, barley wall to wall without a number, the loaf stopped a finger’s width; the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest at the returning-line place; the trough’s round print on its own place; the two unswept lines at the corner with a finger between; the softening at the river’s skin found where put and called weather; the fist arriving nowhere, the note that was not a price, the figure that did not know what the covered grain cost with the ink-neat space unsigned. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and the Drawing’s flat knock outside; the heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Beside all these, on places of their own with a finger between every pair, stood the things kept but not spent. The reported second slate of the sixth stood near, but did not touch, the eighth record-first hand that had gone up the road with Hessa Rale and her first slate unread; near, but not touching, the offered figure that had come out of a shut fist, looked at the light and gone back into its sleeve, arriving nowhere, and its return; near, but not touching, Ilva’s ninth hand with her strip of notched birch bark unread in its fold and the fifth shaping of the well-saying. The well-asking answered with the weather, the second man’s turned slate and direction, the first man’s face, the soft-cuffed clerk’s entered figure brought up the yard from the out-of-line landing below the north bridge as a report and never as a sighting, the three likenesses of Slate Row, the neighbour’s near-joining of shelf and fold from Silt 3 stopped a finger’s width short on its own place, the green and the shape of the two sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either, the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions. Tarn was offstage at the kindling with six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence and the tile debt circling without a name; Jorra Vail was offstage at the Vail wheel-shed, his bent finger’s cold taken out of the damper ahead of a sentence nobody finished. Neither was made a rule or a compact. None of the kept things was told again, made a custom, a witness, a reader, a tenth hand, a payment, a calling of the loan or a paying. The hand that will one day lay the district’s private keepings together is far off and is not his.

The surgeon’s exactness and the infant’s want stood side by side, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the quarrel the work. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle where the warmth lived; he knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, the thread a small dry nothing, the pot handle a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. He finished on his plate what was put before him and could not reach the plate.

The answer was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, laid across neither the reported second slate nor the thaw at the corner, the eighth hand, the offered figure and its return, the clerk’s entered figure, the three likenesses, the twelve arithmetics, the three readings, the fifth shaping, the neighbour’s near-reaching, the two sightings, the widow’s mark nor the keeping at the fold.

Four hundred and thirty-one exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-one days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail’s name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; his name had not crossed a threshold under this roof in four hundred and thirty-one days and no account had gone up the road to call his debt. He did not stand the number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth, and a number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer where none stood.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it, the tang thinner there. The note came from under the covering and not from the covering, and ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it — the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body’s small assent to a sound — took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. Three days came under the Vail roof and the house kept them in loaves pushed a finger’s width. No market, Cross, bell or runner came. The boy carried four hundred and twenty-nine, four hundred and thirty and four hundred and thirty-one.
