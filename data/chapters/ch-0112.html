The Twelfth Arithmetic

Days 387–389 · Stone 28–30, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

Twelve times the figure went over the basket and twelve times it went up the road in a locked box. The pot kept its crack and the year kept its cold, and the hand that wrote went home believing well of itself.

— A Low Kiln saying, told at the last market of the year

— Day 387 —

Stone 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813

The hand had lain flat on the fold since the going of the last light, and the dark went out of the room under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath by degrees, the way a fire goes out under blind ash — not lifted away but thinned, the coals behind the wall giving back their heat in fours and leaving the fifth where it had been left since the night a boy came under this roof to stay. The boy took the field as it came, in the order it came, as a surgeon reads a case off a towel before the light is good enough to trust: the crust first, stiff and leathery-brown at the edge, two inches from the ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it; the thread of drool frozen at the lip in the dry kiln heat and wiped by nobody, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Three things found as they had been left, and none the better for his finding them. Dry heat had taken the moisture out of the room overnight and left the crust harder and browner than it had been at the going of the light, a small tough thing with a burnt edge; the wool lay folded once in the empty place and had not been folded again by any hand in the dark.

He was carried everywhere and told by nobody, and the telling he did himself was in the only register he had: three hundred and forty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, with the three hundred and forty-ninth not yet begun, and three hundred and eighty-six days behind him, six months and two hundred and six, with the twenty-eighth of Stone to be lived before the light went. The year’s last reading stood two mornings off. It did not stand in this room. Nothing that had been read over a basket on the twentieth of this month had crossed under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and nothing crossed now; the eleven arithmetics standing behind one unsigned figure kept their distance from a morning that had not earned a twelfth. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that smelled of antiseptic and hot metal, with a resident holding the light and a nurse calling the time; he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the fact had long ago stopped being funny without ever quite stopping being true.

Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face that cold composes is not composure, and that pink returning is the surface warm over a core that has not warmed at all; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, the way one turns a body toward a fire and waits for the fingers before trusting the colour. A basket set level out of the draught where the heat came to a body sideways was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. Under the wool the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both registers were true. The second was louder. Neither won. The quarrel was the work, and he had stopped looking for a verdict in it, which was not the same thing as having found one.

First grey came thin through the finger’s-width door and laid no boundary on anything. Sella Vail went to the hearth before she went to the basket, as she had gone at both ends of every day for three years. “It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so that the ash lifted and the red showed and went under again. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither of the two gaps a figure; a thing counted aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I, and the sentence went to the fire and not to the wicker. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, with the sweetish edge of it coming and going on a wrong wind, and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She did not touch the widow’s mark at her throat, the cord oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times: a morning and a hearth are no openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot was lifted thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, before the weight came on the handle, so that the hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Garl Vail’s two proverbs went with the lifting, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them: “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” to the pot; “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” to the fire. They went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, because a thing said to a person becomes a thing a Court can enter. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The dressed grain she turned once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, lifting no figure and taking no handful; it lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end and did not know what it cost. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. Inside the wool his hand went toward the rim and stopped where the half-beat had stopped it. The interval was bought at nothing and was not a gift, and the stopping was his.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she had kept from every open slate for three years, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, so that he felt the small correction before he felt the stair. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep his ear covered,” she told the wool. To the wind she said nothing, because a wind is owed no instruction it cannot keep. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and not why. He took the descent as a wound is taken apart, by the small things first: the scrape of a heel on the second step, the draught that came up at the fifth and went out again at the sixth, the particular hollow the basket made against her hip when she shifted her weight at the turn. Under the wool the infant wanted the tilt corrected the other way, toward the crust standing its hair off two inches from his ear, and wanted the thread at his lip wiped, and got neither. Both wants were true and the second was louder and neither won. That was the quarrel, and the quarrel was the work.

Out in the Clay Yard the morning went about its ordinary tending and left its accounts where they lay. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner with its finer grey hairline that the house called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the month’s dry cold, carrying the faint sour tang of Ash up off it where no broom had gone in two years; the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; the round ring at the rack end where the shallow clay pan had once stood, keeping no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the field’s empty place out on the worked strips; the trough with its skin drawn edge to edge; the silt-clouded plate of ice on the flat stone by the well, set a little out of the path of feet; and, widest of all, the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The going-around there was so wide that her elbow cut the corner and the basket canted the other way to balance it, and she righted it without looking down. Once. Not twice. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it and none stood, so the step was taken and not entered. The satisfaction of a field gone around as found and left as found cost nothing, which was how the boy knew it was not peace.

Beside the last of the seven lay Garl Vail in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat at the Vail wheel-shed that nobody was allowed to fill, the clean dark line that had been his ash-marker, the twice-mended pot’s hairline, the unforged collector sentence beside the knife at the kindling, and the tile debt circling without a name and worse for being uncalled. He was a big man with clay in the creases of his hands and a laugh that ended in a cough, who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him; his name had not been spoken under this roof for three hundred and eighty-six days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story. Nothing in the yard stood up and called any of it a lesson.

The three coverings stayed where the weather had put them, with a finger between every pair. The white frost was gone off the north lip of the yard well; the skin lay drawn edge to edge across the trough; the plate of ice clouded with silt stood on its flat stone out of the way of feet. Weather entered nowhere and was not made a cause, a sign, a payment or a promise. Out on the worked strips the field kept its empty place and did not fill it. Beyond the yard the river kept its low note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over it from bank to bank since the afternoon of Stone 1 lay the winter skin, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold and entered nowhere, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this morning and not made again, not spent on the week’s work or on the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. No hand had laid the skin, no payer stood under it and no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is, is weather. He kept the note and the covering on separate places with a finger between them, so that no row came out even by his doing.

The keeping in the crust’s empty place was found where put and was not made again: the wool folded once, exactly where a hand had put it on the fifteenth, changed by nobody in the night, with no second fold, no third crust, no name spoken over the place, no gift, no payment and no payer. It stayed a finding. A habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way that a third, and both want a payer where none stands; a hand that only reads adds no placing however many times it reads. The finding sat on a place of its own beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring and the field’s empty place, with a finger between every pair, and was not a joining of rooms, not a sixth held object, not a sign, a hearing, a mending, a cause or a rule. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, not his, and not come.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Behind the wall the kiln mouth kept its last firing long gone cold and answered nothing. The boards fronting the shelf kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Agreeing any two of the three would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to any of them. The Drawing’s small flat knock out of the neighbour on the pallet stayed outside them on its own place, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place, for a hearing wants a source standing and that room had been seen from a basket and not entered.

Eleven arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, Stone’s first and Stone’s second — stood behind one unsigned figure: one in twelve, read twice over the basket on the twentieth and sent twice, once for the moot and once for the late households. The figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between, no doctrine came out of eleven, and eleven readings were eleven and not one account. Their eleven true subtractions rode locked in Berr Clay’s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down and the boy having no key. No twelfth arithmetic was brought into a morning that had not earned it, and none of the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions repeated or bought a lesson; they stood where put. No ownerless voice came over the boards.

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, with every gap left gap. He went over the field by the place of each thing and checked every object and left every gap a gap, and supplied nothing to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eighth opening, thirteenth arithmetic, witness, reader or payment was supplied; the burned-slate word stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at four shapings, nine record-first hands and nine stoppings with no tenth, four rooms with the same gap held apart with a finger between every pair — the birth room in House Silt, this kiln-house, the room where the slate was read on market-day and the Drawing house seen two doors down — four likenesses and not one account, an account wanting a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand standing and no such payer.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water under the winter skin, muffled as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it — did not reach, and the inch is not a reach — took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down: the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed answer three hundred and forty-nine: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction nor the keeping at the fold. Stone kept its twenty-eighth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Grathic Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. Two mornings stood between this day and the month’s last reading and were not brought into it, and The Five stood beyond that. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-seven exact, six months and two hundred and seven days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number, its payer unnamed, and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, no verdict in the quarrel and none looked for. The keeping is a finding and not a rule; eleven arithmetics are eleven and not one account; four directions are four and not a witness. The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and forty-nine, the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool, and the count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.

The day’s costs arrived as directions and went away as weather. A man down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders and answered the question of its price with the weather which had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed and no slate opened. A man with a cracked jar in a rope sling was sent to a pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. To a woman with a basket of kindling who asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and to two fence-menders who said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, Tarn said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. No collector came up or went down the clay road, and no bell came on any road.

At the kindling the account stood in its old exactness. Six pieces lay in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The tile debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could check it and where a hand could also slip it off. Tarn passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs; the hand went up and came away, and the coming away was the whole of the checking. He held no breath at the top of the reach. The boy read the movement the way he had once read a hand drawn from a field before the dressing went on — neither the hand that works nor the hand that only rests, but the hand that checks and goes away.

“Six counted. The next I don’t count,” he told the wood, and cut nothing, counted nothing, spent nothing.

He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, with the seventh year now at its very end. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. He did not look along the clay road. Two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called, with nobody standing to enter it. A term ending is not a payment, not a forgiveness, not a freedom. A man with no house stands where the road runs, and the fear that he will be entered again for vagrancy stays his, kept without a figure and without asking anybody to carry it. This was the tenth day of the month’s last tend, the year’s third Account-day and its last market; the twelfth arithmetic stood in this morning and had been earned by it, and nothing from the twenty-eighth or the twenty-ninth had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. Eleven were eleven and not one account. Twelve would be twelve before the light went and not one account either. The boy lay in the dark with the morning waiting on him the way an uncalled case waits on a surgeon — known in outline, unwanted in particular, certain to arrive.

The boy took his body in its old order, as a surgeon takes a field off a towel before the light is good enough to trust: the crust first, stiff and leathery-brown at the edge, two inches from the ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it, holding the fold of wool put there on the fifteenth and found where put on every morning since, not made again, still a finding and not a placing; the thread of drool frozen at the lip in the dry kiln heat and wiped by nobody, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Three things found as they had been left, and none the better for his finding them. Cold had come cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew a face composed by cold is not composure; that pink returning is the surface still warm over a core still cold; that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the careful rewarming he would choose for such a patient and could not ask for. The knowledge was exact and belonged to a man with a name in another country. The body it was spent on was three hundred and eighty-eight days old and could not lift its own head.

Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, and a hand on the pot handle, and wanted them without knowing that wanting them was a want. The two registers quarrelled without verdict. Both were true. The second was louder. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, as she had at both ends of every day since the boy came under the roof. The instruction went to the wool, to the fire and to the river wind, and never to the basket.

Before first light she carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold and entered nowhere, found where put and not made again, not spent on the reading or the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson. It was muffled in the going as a voice is muffled by a shut door, and had no hand that laid it, no payer under it and no source standing to be asked. The note had done before any hand in the room came down.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, did not reach, took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

Stone kept its twenty-eighth, twenty-ninth and thirtieth days without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it: older women called it by the weather and slatemen by Grathic Account-days, and neither habit was set right or set left in so many words under any roof. The year went out as it had come in, without a line across it. The reading at the Cross had changed the district’s arithmetic for the twelfth time and it could not be changed back; twelve readings stood behind one unsigned figure with no signature on the space and no doctrine out of twelve, and nothing under the Vail roof stood up and called itself an account. The surgeon’s only victory was a refusal, and it cost him nothing: twelve were twelve, and the hand that would lay them together and the payer standing under them did not stand, and no row came out even by his doing.

The Five stood beyond the last of Stone and were not brought into a dark that had earned only the year’s thirtieth day. They belonged to no month and had no name under this roof, and the boy did not reach toward them by wanting them or make a boundary out of the year’s going that would be a door to them. The loan at the kindling stood uncalled at the very end of the seventh year, the fear of being entered again for vagrancy still his without a figure; the promise under the wheel-shed was neither given nor withdrawn and went on gathering interest against the burned slate at five and the four directions standing where put, buying no payment and naming no room. The cold foot was uncured and the cause of the second chill was withheld, and neither was spent on the year’s going or made to open anything; they crossed into the days beyond as they had crossed every day of the year, unentered and unspent. Nothing under the roof had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended.

The count stood at three hundred and eighty-nine exact, six months and two hundred and nine days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl Vail’s name was unspoken the same number, kept in the coat and the hollow seat and the hairline and the unswept line and nowhere spoken. Three counts that stood side by side and did not become one, and the boy set no hand to join them, for a joining wants a payer standing and none stood. He took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three. The Chronicle of Chapter 112

Three days ran out the year under the Vail roof, and the boy kept a count no roof could read. Stone twenty-eight, twenty-nine and thirty came without a boundary drawn. Each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: barley crust two inches from the ear; the empty place holding the fold; the thread frozen and unwiped, for nobody was ever told about the thread; cold stopping at cheek, temple and bare wrist-joint; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed slowly from outside, never rubbed. Under the wool the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true, the second louder, neither winning.

On the twenty-eighth the fold was found where put, a finding and not a placing. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and set the pot rim two inches beyond his reach. Tarn kept six at the kindling and the seventh at the lip, told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count, and brought his ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped. Jorra Vail parted dusty from clean forearms as clot from vessel and put his bent finger into the cold damper. Answers 349 and 350; the count stood at 387.
