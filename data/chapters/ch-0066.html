The Figure Over the Wool

Days 249–251 · Smoke 10–12, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

— Day 249 —

Smoke 10, Tenthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay where it had been laid, against the fold at his ear, and the boy did not move it. The seventy-second answer had gone down on the sixth place with no payer named and stayed there, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He had set the hand down as a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of a shift, where the next hand will find it without looking, and there was no ceremony in the setting and no meaning in it beyond the setting. It was not a sign and it was not a gift; it was a hand laid down. He had once held a clamp in that place and known from the resistance under his fingers when a vessel would take and when it would tear, and the knowing had cost him nothing at the time and everything afterwards. Now the hand was small and soft-nailed and lay outside the wool on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. The surgeon took the inventory of the two hands and entered neither anywhere. The file lay open on the towel, every edge where a hand would look for it first, and he did not close it, because a file left open is not an invitation and a file closed is not an ending. There was no towel, no instruments and no case; there was only the order of the laying, sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt, and the boy did not ask the order to mean anything more than the order meant.

Two hundred and forty-eight had come down out of the close, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and sixty-eight days told by nobody. He did not hold the number as a man holds a thing he expects to be given back. He held it the way an instrument is held in its case, sharp by sharp, and now the case opened on a new day and the number went forward into two hundred and forty-nine, six months and sixty-nine days, and nobody under any roof said so. The house kept its days badly, in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a clay jar, in a space at the row’s end that nobody called a number, and he kept this one exactly, edge to edge, against all of them. The wanting of a number went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under ash. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on being a small animal thing in a body that could not lift its own head. It was the small hours and the dark had not yet thinned; first grey was a thing the day would earn and had not earned yet.

The smells came in their old order, the near before the far: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw. The oiled paper at the window hung slack in its frame and let the cold come through in a slow sheet that found the wall and went no further. The first sound was not a bell. It was the kiln drawing in fours under the floor, pull, pull, pull, pull, and the silence where the fifth should have stood, a silence with the shape of a held breath in it and no chest under it to hold. Smoke 10 was that morning’s work and the market had not been brought into the small hours; the bell that would go later at the Cross, four, four and a gap, was not heard here and was not borrowed. Smoke 20 stood eight mornings beyond the close, a date and not a day, and was not brought early into a stretch of dark that had not earned its heading. A day is a small vessel, and a day not yet lived will not be poured into one that is.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had always been kept, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-nine days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing going over a lower thing, never counted among the hearings and never missing from them. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The fire’s fifth had been owing since the first carried coal went under a broken pot; the river’s note had no payer because it was water doing what water does; the boards’ knot was a knot in wood and owed nobody anything. To lay the three together would be to make a fourth thing of them, and a fourth thing made out of three would stand up and ask who had paid for the agreement. He left the trays where they lay. It was a discipline and not a belief; a discipline is a thing a hand uses, and a belief is an account that has found somebody to stand under it.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness entered in a figure is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though the line it followed had been written out to that length and no longer; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the severe face cold wears and the face a man wears when he is warmed too fast, knew that a cold patient cannot clot, knew that the grimace of severe cold looks like a smile and that you do not rub a frozen foot, knew that the warming must come from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it, and the knowing was an instrument in a body that could not use it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child’s hunger keeps no hours while the register goes on being exact inside a hungry body. The surgeon took the inventory in the order the facts arrived, crust, gap, thread, cold, afterdrop, and entered none of it anywhere. He had once been the man who decided which bodies got the warmth and which did not; here he was the body, and the warmth was two inches away across a gap he could not close, and there was no one in the room to decide for him.

Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, as she went every day, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. She told it it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot at the kiln mouth, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting-go, a count she had never offered to any ear under this roof and did not offer now. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak showed pale off the coals. The burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work, the lintel and the threshold of it going slack when there was nothing held. The fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument. Feed it, she said to the coals, in the voice she used to the pot and the broom and the running water, and not once to the basket under the fold at the bed’s low angle.

the widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and she did not touch it in the small hours or at the shelf or at the basket. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon and a dusk are none of them openings. She had touched it once, before the first firing of Smoke on Smoke 1, held one breath, let go, tipped the carried coal under a broken pot and let the fire take, still missing its fifth. That one opening lay behind the household and she did not look at it again, and no lesson was made of it and no payer named. The mark was a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and it was not brought out to be looked at, which was the whole of its keeping. He did not read the rule as a mending performed or a payment made, because a rule that stands up and calls itself a mercy wants a payer under it and none stood. The cord lay flat against her collarbone where her hand had left it, and her hands went on with the coals.

The dressed grain lay near the hearth under its fold of wool and she turned it for damp in the order the work had taught her: wool back, grain felt to the knuckle, spread thin on the flat stone to finish, turned, gathered by the handful, and the fold laid flat again. It changed state and gained no figure. It was entered on no slate and laid across none of the absences, and when a reading spoke a figure over it later that morning the figure would not know what the grain had cost, because a figure read over a covered thing knows the cover and not the thing. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by an arm that knew weight and not cost, the forearm going down into the heap and coming up with the amount the hand already knew before the eyes got to it. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf’s front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; a mark nobody reads is still a mark kept, and a mark read wants a figure put under it, and a figure wants a payer. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall and her hand shut half over nothing and came away; she did not touch the jar or say what it held. The pot rim went out and its handle came round two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting that was not a look toward the bed and was not a look away from it either. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance, and the hand could not close it, and the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the keeping would take now the grain was under wool. She did not price it, refuse it or enter it. Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it, Sella said, and carried the question off level in her own words so nothing slopped, and the woman went off with her pot and the question still on her. A man come down the clay road with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the yard well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere, and it was not a missing figure. The boy opened the shape of the fist as he would open a chart laid face-down: the shape of the paper, not the writing under it; a keeping, not a loss; a hand shut, not a hand that had given. He entered neither fact, and he did not join the neighbour’s cost to the man’s number, because a payer put under both would be a payer invented to make a row come out even, and an invented payer is the beginning of an account that nobody in this yard had asked to open.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man’s shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf, and nobody straightened it or took it down. The wheel-seat kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and had not been filled. The ridge of Ash had turned the corner and become the clean dark line, and beside it the finer hairline of grey had gathered, called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, a saying about honest repairs said over a place where the repair had been hidden. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-nine days. No collector came up the clay road on the morning that was coming and none went down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, not a story, and the boy did not turn the objects into a cause or the debt into a lesson.

He put the first question of the day to his hand. It was not a question in words and it wanted no answer in words. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger’s-width door, and he let it come down at once on the river’s next note, down before the small intake could be held out into a count, down while the sound was still going and before the letting-go. It was down at once. He filed answer seventy-three on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No seventh came in the small hours and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. It was an inch of air and a down-stroke, the same honest fraction it had been on the day before and the day before that, seventy-three objects now on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

The market was coming; it had not entered the basket. The arithmetic of the district was about to be read in a square where two hundred people stood, and nothing under this roof would be settled by it: the grain would stay covered and unfigured, the mark would hang where it hung, the debt would stay uncalled and the fifth would stay missing. That was a keeping and not a failure to act. Sella lifted the basket at the going of the small-hours light and turned toward the clay road, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind, she said, and did not look into the fold and said nothing of what would be read over it. The hand outside the wool had come down at once on the river’s next low note, answer seventy-three lay on the sixth place with no payer named, and the file lay open on the towel, exact, complete and worth nothing.

Sella Vail set the basket down the way she would set a pot down out of a draught, with the back of her hand and without looking into it, and gave it neither an instruction nor a reason. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind that came up off the boards, found the shade and went around it. Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind, she said, and stood half a pace off. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a thing left owes nobody an account of itself.

Under the shade the warm side of him was going thin; over the square the sun was on two hundred backs at once, and the square held its heat badly, the way a shallow bowl holds water, spilling it on every side.

Eight pigs went over the wet boards while the light was still coming up. They came nose to tail, driven by a boy with a stick who did not look at the crowd; the boards were slick and the pigs slipped and caught themselves and went on, and each gave out a small flat grunt that arrived nowhere. Two hundred people leaned in over the reading block, shoulder against shoulder, each carrying a figure or a fist that no slate standing in that square had been made to enter. An assessor of Sill wrote the eight down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail, and the pigs went on over the boards and the figures went into the slate and the two things did not agree because nobody had asked them to. All morning the square stood with every withholding in the district gathered under the sun and not one of them read. A man came out of the press with a figure in his mouth and set it beside a figure a neighbour had left on the turnip heap, and the two stood there side by side the way two pots stand on a board, each whole, neither owing the other anything, and the crowd went on leaning. It was the first Account-day of the second firing, and it had not been brought into the small hours; it had earned its morning and come into it. The boy knew the shape of a thing brought to be counted and left uncounted: an instrument laid on a towel and not used, a chart turned face-down, a number spoken into a room and carried home to a shelf. A figure spoken aloud in a crowd becomes a thing the crowd will set down and go on keeping, exact and worth nothing, until somebody puts a payer under it.

Berr Clay came up short off the clay road before the top of the crowd had settled. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted lead glaze stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the crowd.

One in twelve, as the notice says.

He read it twice. The figure went over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and the second reading was for people who had not yet turned their faces toward the block. The boy lay under the wool with the figure going over him and did not make the figure the paid figure, because the figure read aloud was not the figure paid and there was no signature anywhere between the two. Berr did not look into the basket. The boy thought of the man carried out on Day 188, whose cost no figure, collector or slate had entered, and of the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come back; the reading knew neither, and knew the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing. The surgeon opened the thing the way he would open a wound, layer under layer, and found the cost where the cost always was, under the reading and out of reach.

A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the levy would take now the kilns had been brought up. Berr took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.

A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there’ll be a slate at the watch.

Your house, never I. He said the levy was called and that it was not called by him, and the low voice went into the top of the crowd where the people who needed it least could lean in and hear it best. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward High Kiln and arrived nowhere, and it was not a missing figure. He went off up the clay road with the cord folded square and the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The kindness was entered nowhere except in the box, and the box was locked, and the arithmetic of the district had changed for the first time in Smoke and could not be changed back.

The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb and the finger passed over a figure on a tray of their own. He did not make a tenth shape of them and did not join them to the assessor’s hand going to the slate before the pigs, or to any other order of reaching in the square; a shape is a shape, and two shapes laid together do not make a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them. Berr’s proverb walked in his mouth with the Court’s passive grammar braided through it, and the boy heard the two voices in the one sentence as he heard the two hand-orders in the one morning: the notice, the figure, the levy, your house, and never I. The man had taken himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the sentence stood where he had left it with nobody in it at all.
