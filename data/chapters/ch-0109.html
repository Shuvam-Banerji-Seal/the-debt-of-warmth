Days 378–380 · Stone 19–21, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

— Day 378 —

Stone 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been put at the close of the day before, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-seven exact. The boy had not carried it forward by any act of will; a man’s attention goes where his field is, and his field was two inches of air above a barley crust and the small sounds of a house waking in its sleep. He lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, and this morning had a date that no roof under that sky would speak aloud: Stone 19, the nineteenth day of the deep-winter month, six months and one hundred and ninety-seven days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. The file had come out on the towel at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel when a case is done and before the next is called, and he went over it by the place of each thing with every gap left gap.

There were a hundred things in the room he could not change and one he could only read. He took the body in the old order, because a field is checked in a sequence and a surgeon does not trust an impression that has not been made to stand in order. First the crust: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. It had stood two inches off for three hundred and seventy-seven days and would stand two inches off this morning also; the distance was not a wish and had no giver. Beside it the wool lay folded once in the empty place, exactly where a hand had put it on the fifteenth, and the place kept both its fold and its emptiness the way a wound keeps the edge the dressing left and the gap the dressing did not close. Then the cold. It had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and it had stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

The room smelled of tallow and wet wool and the dry breath of the kiln standing cool behind its wall since the firing of Stone 5, which had bought no word and no lesson under any roof. He knew the face that cold composes is not the face of composure. A still face can be a face that has gone past caring, and the pink that comes back to it first is the surface still cold while the core is colder; warmth returns in the reverse of the order in which it left, extremity before the heart, and a man who takes the first pink for the turn will call a patient saved an hour before he loses him. A cold patient cannot clot. These things were known and were useless here, for there was no patient and no hands, only a basket with the surgeon’s register behind its eyes. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped from his lip, a hand on the pot handle standing just beyond the reach of his own. Both registers were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work; he had stopped expecting it to end, which was not the same as being used to it.

He found the keeping where it had been put. The wool lay folded once in the crust’s empty place, unchanged by the night; the night had added nothing to it and taken nothing from it. There was no second fold, no third crust, no name spoken over the place, no look toward his face, no instruction to the basket. For three mornings a small dread had gone before him to the bed, the dread that a fourth finding might stand up and call itself a habit; this morning the dread found no hand to stand on. A fourth finding makes no fourth placing. A hand that finds is not a hand that places, and a habit wants a hand that placed twice where no hand stood. Nothing in the room had been put there again. The only hand that had touched the place had put the fold there once, on the fifteenth, and had not come back to it.

He filed it on a place of its own beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the gone pan’s round ring at the rack end, and the field’s empty place out on the worked strips, with a finger between it and the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper. It was not a joining of rooms, not a gift (a gift wants a giver and nobody gave), not a payment, with no payer, not a sixth held object, not a seventh or an eighth, not a sign, a hearing, a mending, a cause or a rule. No fourth likeness was made a cause. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come. The satisfaction of the finding was clean and cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace; underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and neither verdict was given.

Under the roof Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, as she had at both ends of every day for three years. “It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I, and the sentence went to the fire and not to the wicker that held him. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure, so that the count went into the room and not onto a slate. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person — “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” and “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” They were improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and lifted no figure and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift.

The cord of her widow’s mark was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung, untouched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule and no eighth was made due. The salt-stiff coat kept the crease at its left shoulder on its peg behind the door and the hollow wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow that nobody was allowed to fill; the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey hairline beside it, the thing the house called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where it had not been swept in two years. All of it was kept in objects and not in a story, and no name was spoken over any of it.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and seventy-eight days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

Ten arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash’s first, Ash’s second, Ash’s third and Stone’s first — the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of ten. Their ten true subtractions rode in Berr Clay’s box up the clay road locked toward High Kiln, unread under this roof; the key had gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down and the boy had no key. The finding that ten are not one account sat on a place of its own and was not laid across the keeping at the fold or any other kept thing. Stone 20’s eleventh arithmetic stood one morning ahead and was not brought into this day.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for or filled, and the flat knock of the Drawing stood on its own place outside them. The heard bell of the Long Night stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into any of the three counts. Above and behind, in the Debt Loft, the warm loft stone stood cold in its unmarked pot and no foot went up to it; nothing came over the boards and nothing was owed to a voice that had not spoken.

In the small hours the river kept its note beyond the finger’s-width door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Over it from bank to bank lay the winter skin that the cold had put on and that the cold would take off, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this night and not made again, with no hand that laid it, no payer under it and no source standing to be asked. A line left where it is is still a line kept, and a sign without a payer is weather and not a promise. He kept the covering on its own place beside water over water and put a finger between the covering and the note it muffled, so that no row came out even by his doing.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and thirty-one honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

The wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the crust gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had reached the end of its own instruction. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The thread stood frozen and unwiped. The hand lay flat against the fold on answer three hundred and thirty-one, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-eight exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-eight days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. Stone kept its nineteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held both its names, and the count went on under the Vail roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.

In the afternoon Sella Vail carried him down the nineteen steps from under the Vail kiln-house roof into the Clay Yard, in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. The basket went level as a pot that must not be slopped and out of the draught, and the whole of her attention was on the keeping of that level, so that at every turn of the landing she corrected the tilt before the tilt had time to become a habit in the wicker. The boy felt the small correction before his body felt the stair. It was the correction a hand makes under a tray, exact and unremarked; the infant under the wool wanted it to go another way, toward the crust two inches off and the frozen thread at his lip, and got no such correction at all. Nineteen steps, each at the half-pace, and nobody counted them out loud, and the afternoon went down with the basket.

She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner she went around the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called the thaw, and swept neither, because a line left where it is is still a line kept and a thaw goes when it goes. The dry cold had thickened it; the faint sour tang of Ash came up off it where it had not been swept in two years, and she did not turn her head toward the smell, having settled long ago what the corner was and what it was not. Under the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody, and she went around the space above them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. At the rack end the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the pan had gone out of the house and the ring stayed, which is how a vessel is kept after it is gone. Out on the worked strips she went around the field’s empty place, and around the trough, and around the silt-clouded plate of ice on the flat stone by the well that stood a little out of the path of feet. Widest of all she went around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, so wide that her elbow cut the corner of the yard and the basket tilted the other way to balance it, and she righted it without looking down.

He took the wide step apart the way he took a field apart, by what was there and by what was not. Seven places gone around with a look and no figure; the widest gone around by nothing measurable, and once and not twice. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it, and none stood. The yard kept its places by walking around them; the keeping cost a step each time and nobody entered the steps, and he did not enter them either. The clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it unswept, thickened in the month’s dry cold, and the broken slates stayed entered by nobody, and the round ring at the rack end kept its emptiness without a word written into it.
