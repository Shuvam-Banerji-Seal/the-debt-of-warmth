— Day 396 —

Ilmen 2, Y.H. 814, Secondday

The hand lay flat on the fold where the dark had left it, and the small hours came on under the roof the way weather comes, with no door opened for them and nobody saying what they had crossed. Three hundred and ninety-five days stood behind that hand exact and unspent, six months and two hundred and fifteen of them told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the day had a working name, Secondday, and nothing under the roof made a threshold of it or a signing. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had always been kept, and the boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, with the dry kiln heat coming to him sideways and the smell of woodsmoke and wet wool in the fold and, under that, the faint sour tang of Ash lifting out of the Vail corner where no broom had gone in two years. The room held its heat the way a cup holds water, with a crack in it nobody mentioned. Outside, the dark was going about its ordinary work along the Clay Yard, and nobody under any roof was asking it to mean anything.

The surgeon took the body in the order it arrived, because a field read out of order is a field read wrong. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool; beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put again in the dark and not made again, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. A keeping found is a keeping still however many hands find it, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. A face composed by cold is not composure. Pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and a cold patient cannot clot, and the rewarming he would choose is a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways, slow and from the outside, never hurried and never rubbed, and he could not ask for it. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat and the order in which it takes it back, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold; he had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and the knowledge was of the same order and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work. Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, as she had come to it every morning of his life, and gave the coals their instruction first. “It’s the thaw,” she said, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and the low red eye under the back of the broken pot answered her with a small breathing that was not an answer to anything anybody had asked. She turned the coals with the back of the pot and stood a moment with her hands not still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every morning of his life, with neither gap figured and neither made to stand for a number. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, with the sweetish edge of a wrong wind coming and going over it, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the new binding, and she said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, and the other about a thing worth mending being worth mending twice, the second time being the real one, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said it. She kept repair apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The pot gave back a small flat note as she set it down — not the full clear note of a sound vessel and not the dead note of one cracked through, but the note of a thing that carries its break and goes on holding — and she had heard it every morning of his life and had never once called it mended. A strand of coal-black hair with one grey streak at the temple came loose and she did not push it back. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, with no figure over it and no handful lifted, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. There was a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. He filed the half-beat as the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift; it was the same interval he had once bought with a clamp and called by another name, and the other name bought nothing here either.

The house went back to its grammar as though nothing had been asked of it. The instruction went to the wool, to the pot, to the fire and the banked fire and to the river wind, and never to the basket. What she said low went to the pot and the fire, and when she spoke of the boy it was the boy, and when she spoke of what she meant to do it was the house, a grammar that had outlasted a turning of the year without losing a word. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord of working thumbs and she did not touch it; a morning, a shelf and a basket were not openings, seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a Secondday. A rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor the carried fire breathed four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its giver without a name and its fifth unpaid three hundred and ninety-six days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it. Garl Vail’s salt-stiff coat kept its crease at the left shoulder on the peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed kept the hollow twenty years had worn on that side and nobody filled it, the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey beside it called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years, the twice-mended pot kept the hairline showing between staple and wire, his unforged collector sentence lay at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford, and the tile debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. His name was not spoken under this roof on this morning, and the fire and the man stood side by side in the house’s keeping without becoming one keeping. No collector came up the clay road and no runner came down it; no market and no bell, and no reading at the Cross. The Ash 5 joining of the burned-slate word to the counting room shut in the war and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions from the direction of Slate Row stood where they had been put, did not repeat and bought no lesson, and the boy gave no fifth shaping to the four.

He carried twelve arithmetics across into this morning and found them unchanged. The three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, and Stone’s first, second and last: twelve readings of one small printed figure, twelve and not one account. The printed figure stood at one in twelve, unchallenged, and twelve true changes to the district’s figures had not moved that one mark. The figure read was still not the figure paid; the space between them had no signature on it and no doctrine came out of twelve, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Their twelve true subtractions rode locked in Berr Clay’s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down and the boy with no key to anything. The reset of the tend had supplied no payer to the old twelve and none to a new one, and it was a fact of the Court’s counting and not an act of anybody under this roof. He did not lay his twelve old findings across a new figure to make a row come out even. Ilmen 10 stood eight mornings off with the year’s first arithmetic waiting there, a reset and not a signing, and he did not bring it into a morning that had not earned it. No thirteenth reading stood behind the figure and no signature stood on the space where the year had gone over like a pot set down in the same place on a board, the board not changed under it.

Sella carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, and widest around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; the look stayed the length of a step and then went off the place. She set him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. The three coverings stood weather entered nowhere with a finger between every pair, and the river kept its note of water over water under the winter skin beyond the finger’s-width door, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, found where put and not spent on an ordinary morning. The thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

Out in the yard a woman set a pail down where she always set it, and a man counted kindling into a stack and did not count the stack, and two voices passed the price of barley from one end of the wall to the other without either voice making a figure of it. Nothing in the yard arrived anywhere as a number and no hand came under the roof to enter what had not been entered. The keeping at the fold was found where put on the morning and was still a finding, not a placing; he did not give the place a name or the finding a second hand, and the warmth of it, such as it was, went on being kept at cost to nobody who could have been asked.

He turned out the file in the hush after the yard’s ordinary sounds had gone past, as instruments come out on the towel between cases and before the next is called, and went over the field by the place of each thing. It was not put away for good; another case would come and the towel would come out again and the hand would find the place of every instrument without looking. The crust’s gap first: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool, the empty place beside it holding the fold found where put and not made again, and the keeping at the fold a finding still. The shelf’s two gaps stayed where the loaves had not been set; the gone pan’s round ring stayed on the board where a pan had stood and stood no longer; the field’s empty place kept its shape out beyond the door. The told bell’s gap sat in its square beside the heard bell from the Cross, four, four and a gap in two places and a gap in a third, three counts in three places staying three because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. The silence after the name from Weighing lay on the gap tray with the clean voice’s missing name and the empty name the neighbours had left in the story of the Remembering, each with a finger between, none filled, none made a witness, a reader or a payer. The neighbour’s called-gone grey lay on its own place beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner, and a thing called finished was not finished for being called so. The stopped ear-sentence stood at the word that would call the loan, at the very end of a term already ended, with no figure on it and no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford; the promise under the Vail wheel-shed stood neither given nor withdrawn; Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch. Each lay on its own place with a finger between and none stood up to call itself a rule.

Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, each on a tray of its own, and he did not make a tenth of the hand that had gone to the coals before the basket or of any hand that would come up the road before noon to measure what nobody had called. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and not dug for, and the Drawing house’s flat knock outside them on its own place. The four directions stayed four with a finger between every pair; the coverings and the river’s skin stayed found where put and unspent; the four rooms with the same gap stayed four with no Court room brought under the roof as a fifth; the second chill kept its withheld cause and the cold foot below the sill stayed uncured and unnamed; green was a colour and the foundling a shape with no eighth of either. Every one advanced and none paid, and no hand under the roof drew a boundary across the morning to make them stand as a doctrine.

Answer three hundred and sixty-seven was filed exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place from the days behind and three hundred and sixty-seven stood since the first, not one a doctrine.

The count came to three hundred and ninety-six exact, six months and two hundred and sixteen days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, and the two stood side by side without becoming one. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Peace in the old life had always come with something spent — a shift ended, hands washed, a chart signed; this cost nothing because nobody had been asked to pay. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread at his mouth wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and got none of the three, and the wanting and the having-not went on being the work the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it.

Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended. No slate had been opened within reach of the basket, no figure given for the boy, no door drawn across the tend, no payer set under any of the kept things, and the year’s first arithmetic waited eight mornings off where it had been left. Beyond the finger’s-width door the river went on under its skin, water over water, and the hand outside the wool came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The crust’s corner stood a hair off the wool, the thread was frozen and unwiped, the count stood at three hundred and ninety-six exact and told by nobody, and four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept, forward.

The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice. Tarn stood at the kindling at the very end of a term already ended. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it, where the house could not check and where it could be taken, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. A woman come for an armful of kindling asked what the house would want of him now his time was out, and he said “Yes, house” to the question and to the thing behind the question, agreeing so thoroughly that the question had nowhere left to put a foot, and went on stacking with the small tools of the house going into his pocket one after another and coming out again where they belonged. He had owned nothing for six years and he knew where every small thing was.

The boy read the stopping as a surgeon reads a clamp left on a vessel: the instrument did not close anything, and the not-closing was a state of the field and not an omission by any hand. A term ending was not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom. It left a man more exposed than it had found him, because a man with no house has no wall between his body and the people who would measure it, and the exposure was carried on this afternoon and not spent. Two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called; the boy set the three on one place with a finger between each and no payer standing under any of them.

No collector came up and no runner came down. The patch kept its own place on the stones and was joined to no cause and no ear.

Ilmen 3, Y.H. 814, Thirdday

The small hours came on under the Vail kiln-house roof the way a coal comes on under ash, without a flare and without a hand at it, and the boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, where the dry heat arrived sideways and went no farther, and where nobody under any roof had ever thought to give an instruction to the basket. Three hundred and ninety-six days stood behind him exact, six months and two hundred and sixteen of them told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; answer three hundred and sixty-eight had been laid flat on the fold at the going of the light and had bought nothing with its exactness, and the new morning asked nothing of him in return. Under the floor the carried fire breathed four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had always been kept, and the bed held him level out of the draught in the place Sella Vail had chosen and not in the one he would have chosen. Outside, the dark went about its ordinary work along the Clay Yard, and no door was opened for it and no name given to what crossed.

The surgeon took the body in the order it arrived, because a field read out of order is a field read wrong. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put again in the dark and not made again, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. A keeping found is a keeping still however many hands find it, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. The thread at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody had ever been told about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. A face composed by cold is not composure. A cold patient cannot clot, and pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and the rewarming he would choose is a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways, slow and from the outside, never hurried and never rubbed, and he could not ask for it. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold, and the knowledge was of the same order as the knowledge of a chart signed past a note nobody read, and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put either to work. Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work.

Sella came to the hearth before the basket and gave the coals their instruction first. “It’s the thaw,” she said, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured and neither made to stand for a number, and kept her finger along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. Garl Vail was in the room as he was in every room of the house, in the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on the peg behind the door, in the hollow wheel-seat nobody was allowed to fill, in the clean dark line at the Vail corner with its finer grey beside it called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash where no broom had gone in two years. His name was unspoken three hundred and ninety-six days and the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number, and the two stood side by side in the house’s keeping without becoming one keeping. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, left the hairline showing between the old-brass staple and the new binding, and said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person. Repair was kept apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and with no figure over it and no handful lifted, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The instruction went to the wool and the pot and the fire and the banked fire and the river wind, and never to the basket. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning, a shelf and a basket were not openings, and nothing under this roof made a threshold of a Thirdday.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and set him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. The fold went right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and without a second fold. Out in the yard the morning went about its grammar: a woman set a pail where she always set it, a man counted kindling into a stack and did not count the stack, and the price of barley passed from one end of the wall to the other without standing up as a figure. Then a wind came up wrong off High Kiln, the way a wind comes that has taken the wrong side of the terrace, and brought the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards and past the pot board, where the clay jar stood cold under its glaze. It was a direction and not a second edge paid; it came across the yard, touched the wool at his ear and went out again on the wind that brought it. Sella’s hand went toward the jar and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air, and she said nothing to the woman at the pail and nothing to the pot. The sweetish edge went and came and went, and the jar stood where it stood, and the boy filed the edge as weather and gave it no source, for a source is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

A man come down the clay road from High Kiln stood at the yard well a while later with the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth, and spoke to the weather about the pots. “They’ll take a glaze this weather if you let them,” he said, and wiped the back of his wrist across his mouth, and went off toward the drying racks with the green still at the corner of it. He was a glaze-hand and nothing more under this roof: no name went with him, no slate, no figure, no room entered and no errand that brought him down the road. The boy filed the green as a colour on a place of its own, beside Berr Clay’s green at the reading and the greens that had come to mouths at past markets, with a finger between every pair and no joining. Berr’s green was a colour and those were colours and the colour at this man’s mouth was a colour; greens were greens and not one account, and no eighth of them was made due. He did not call the colour by the name of any woman, and no woman was given a name under this roof. It was not a hearing, not a cause, not a name and not a payer; it gathered where it stood and was not spent.

Costs came to the yard as directions and went away as weather. A man brought a number up out of a fist toward the pail at the well, looked at it where it stood in the air between his knuckles and the water, and put it back into his sleeve; it arrived nowhere and entered nothing. A woman with clay pale in her nail crescents asked what the day’s work had cost and was sent to the pot with “the house has said it,” and the pot, being a pot, said nothing at all. The boy filed the order of reaching as he had filed it before — hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both — and made no tenth hand of the man with the fist, the woman at the pail, the glaze-hand at the racks or any hand that had gone before them. Nine hands stood with no tenth and the place where a tenth might have stood was left empty. He gave no figure to the number in the fist and no source to the silence of the pot; a cost that arrives nowhere has no payer under it, and he did not put one there.

Word came up the yard before noon that a runner had gone up the clay road earlier with the writing turned to her own sleeve and unread. It was a shape reported in a place, not a sighting from the basket and not a room entered; the boy took it as he took the weather, and gave no interior to the voice that carried it. Hessa’s eighth hand stayed eighth on its own tray, and she was not made a reader by the carrying of a slate, for a runner is not a reader of what she carries and no figure came off the narrow slate going up the road. Ilva’s notched strip of birch bark stayed in her sleeve with the count kept in wood and no figure coming off the wood, and her ninth hand stayed ninth, not a reader and not a tenth. Berr Clay’s twelve true subtractions stayed locked in his box up the road with the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down, and the boy had no key. Twelve arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure with no signature on the space and no doctrine out of twelve, and the reset of the tend had supplied no payer to any of them. No tenth hand was filed and no reader stood under any roof; the word went up the road and arrived where words go when nobody asks them to stop.

The thread was left frozen and unwiped, because nobody had given an instruction about the thread.

The morning had gone the way mornings go when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept, with every kept thing left where it lay.

The boy lay in the finger’s width of shade Sella Vail had chosen, at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, where the heat came sideways and stopped at his cheek as at the end of a written line. The boy kept the step as a keeping and not as a sign, for a sign wants a reader standing at the end of it and none stood; the yard turned around the places the way a woman turns a scorching loaf, and the turning was the whole of it.

Tarn stood at the kindling at the very end of a term already ended. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it, where the house could not check and a hand could take it, beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The next I don’t count.” He cut nothing. He counted nothing. He spent nothing. The seventh lay at the lip and was not lifted.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it was found where it had stopped, at the word that would call the loan: a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth stood up, no figure on it and no name on the man. The breath drawn on Seed 16 was still unspent at the top of the reach, and the loan that could hear had not been called. The fear of being entered again for vagrancy stayed his without a figure, and so did the want he had not voiced: a slate with his own name on it and no mark against it. He did not look along the clay road. The boy read the stopping as a surgeon reads a clamp left on a vessel: the instrument closed nothing, and the not-closing was a state of the field and not an omission by any hand. A term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom. It leaves a man more exposed than it found him, for a man with no house has no wall between his body and the people who would measure it. Two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called: the boy set the three on one place with a finger between and no payer under any.

Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the Vail corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist gave back what little light there was, and his chest had been tight since the dark. The held breath of Ash 14 lay at the knife-sill and the thumb’s resting of Bone 26 where that had been put, found where put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction, the clerk’s inward slate nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, three hundred and sixty-seven through three hundred and seventy-two, and not one of them was a doctrine. The count came to three hundred and ninety-eight exact, six months and two hundred and eighteen days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl’s name was unspoken the same number, and the two stood side by side without becoming one.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread at his mouth wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and got none of the three, the wanting and the having-not going on being the work the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate lay open within reach of the basket, no figure was given for the boy, no door was drawn across the tend and no payer was set under any of the kept things. The soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the landing with his slate turned inward for a measure nobody called, and the degree Tarn’s head had gone toward it stayed a shape on its own place gathering interest without being called, neither a rule repeated nor a danger come due.

The hand lay flat on the fold with the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool and the thread frozen and unwiped; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody here would perform; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The dressed grain lay covered under wool with no figure over it, and the pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept — not settled, not ended, only forward.

The Chronicle of Chapter 115

Three tending days came under the Vail roof after the turning of the year — Ilmen 2, 3 and 4 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: the crust two inches from the ear, the fold in the empty place found where put, the thread frozen and unwiped, cold stopping at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither winning.

On Ilmen 2 the house went back to its grammar as though nothing had turned. Sella told the coals it was the thaw, kept the charcoal unread and set the twice-mended pot’s rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. At the kindling Tarn stood at the end of an ended term, six pieces where the house could check and the seventh at the lip; his hand went over blade and sill and away with no breath held, and he told the wood, Six counted. The next I don’t count. The ear-sentence stopped at the word that would call the loan.

On Ilmen 3 a wrong wind off High Kiln brought the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards. A glaze-hand down the clay road had the green at one corner of his mouth; Sella’s hand went toward the cold jar and shut half over nothing. Costs came and went as weather: a number rose out of a fist and went back into a sleeve, and a woman sent to the pot got no figure from a pot. Word came up that a runner had gone up the road with the writing turned to her sleeve and unread.

Then word came up that the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at the out-of-line landing, his slate turned inward for a measure nobody had called. He was a shape seen by nobody under the roof, and no witness stood. The boy filed the order of reaching on a place of its own and made no tenth hand of him. Tarn’s head went a degree toward the landing and back to the broom without a word, and the stroke went on over the flagstones. The danger came as news and one small movement, and gathered interest without being called.
