— Day 282 —

Frost 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Outside the wool the small hours went on at their own pace under the roof of the Vail kiln-house in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, and the boy lay carried, awake and silent, with the hundred and thirty-eighth answer put down behind him the way a pot is set on a shelf where nobody will knock it. Two hundred and eighty-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn; six months and one hundred and one days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The hand had gone up an inch, taken the held interval and the empty interval from the file, set them side by side with a finger between, borrowed no house count and supplied no fifth to the kiln, and come down on the river’s next low note. That was done. It was not recalled as growth, a gift, a reaching or a sign; it was a thing put away in its place, and the place was the sixth, and the sixth was a place and not a power. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the lean quarter of its warmth stayed where it was with nobody to lift the floor and nobody to supply the pull.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and one days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order — crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting — and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

The room smelled of hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called the thaw. Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. No fire-sitter stood over the coals and no house count came to be borrowed. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The heard bell’s four, four and a gap stayed in the Cross beside the told bell of the Long Night, not brought under this roof on a Thirdday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note.

Then the count crossed from two hundred and eighty-one to two hundred and eighty-two the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. Two hundred and eighty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and one hundred and two days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The house kept its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, and gave nobody a number to carry. Even the crossing made no sound anybody could have entered. The small hours went on, and the room whose floor does not touch his gave its low counting without a word addressed to the basket — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go — and the boy took only what the hand could take and left the letting-go where it was.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to anything else, as she had come to it on the mornings behind and would come to it on the mornings ahead. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing as she reached the coals, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light. She told them it was the thaw. You’ll go when you go, she told them, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. Her instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, and her hand did not go to it at a morning and in a basket, because neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with its face turned away; she did not look at it again to make a lesson of it, and four openings were four and not a rule, and the next stood in the first firing of a later month, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. She set the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, left the thread frozen where it lay, and stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. She gave no instruction at any hour to the basket. He was set under the roof and never in the Debt Loft; this was a keeping and not a kindness anybody had named. The finding that a house keeps itself by not-doings stayed on a place of its own, with the boy’s gaps on another and a finger between; no third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up as a cause.

Garl Vail stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the Vail wheel-shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, which nobody filled; the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey hairline beside it, unswept both. The debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled, and his proverb had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-two days. No collector had come up the clay road on the mornings behind and none had gone down it, and the household went around the debt as Sella went around Ash, without a figure and without a name.

Beyond the finger’s-width door the stayed frost-bloom lay white on the north lip of the yard well, where the yard had left it white on Frost 8 and had gone on calling it weather on Frost 9, and the plate of ice that Sella had lifted off the skin on the trough stood on the flat stone by the well out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. The rope had stiffened; the trough kept its skin; nobody had given either a figure and nobody had carried either away. The surgeon’s reading of a skin laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, with a finger between; he made no fourth likeness of the three and no cause of any two, and the first light coming up over the Clay Yard did not pay any of it. The finer grey dust of the drying racks sat in the seams of the flagstones beyond the door, a direction and not a hand, entered nowhere; nobody scrubbed it out and nobody turned it into a figure.

Out at the rack end the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone, turned once in each of the two days behind with a lighter touch each time, and given no figure at any turning. Berr’s box had gone up the clay road toward High Kiln with Frost’s first true subtraction locked inside, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between the figure that would be read over the covered grain and what that grain had cost had no signature on it. Nothing under this roof had opened the box or copied the slate, and nothing under this roof would bring any of the reading back as a lesson, a figure, a mending or a payment.

Frost 13 came on in its own light and was not brought into the day behind it. Frost 20 (Day 289), the month’s second Account-day, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. The light went first along the well’s white lip, then across the flagstones, then in under the shelf where the boy lay, thin and cold and exact as a blade laid on the towel. The kiln answered in fours with its gap and not a bell. He had counted the day before without being told what the count was for, and he counted this morning without asking anybody to tell him; the wanting of a number spoken went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under ash, and there was nobody in the room to hear it and nobody who would have known what a number counted if it had been spoken.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note. It took the small intake of the river’s own going through the finger’s-width door, carried it across the little silence left by one of the counted breaths in the next room — a house rhythm carried across the river’s interval on an ordinary morning — and came down on the next low note. No house count was borrowed for it and no fifth was supplied to the kiln. It was an honest coordination on the sixth place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a held change, not a key, not a power, not a doctrine and not a rule. One hundred and thirty-nine stood beside one hundred and thirty-eight. The surgeon’s clean satisfaction at carrying one rhythm across another was checked by the infant’s plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work, and he did not call it a virtue. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Beyond the finger’s-width door the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and Frost 20 (Day 289), the month’s second Account-day, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into this day. The ordinary first light came up on a morning with no market in it. The river’s low note went quiet after the hand came down.
