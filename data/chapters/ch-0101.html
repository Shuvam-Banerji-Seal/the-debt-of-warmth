The Grain Under Wool

Days 354–356 · Ash 25–27, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

The pot keeps its crack, the grain keeps its cost, and the yard goes around the place where the hand did not come back. Ask any of them what a thing is worth and it will tell you the weather.

— A saying of Low Kiln, in the last days of Ash

— Day 354 —

Ash 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It had come up an inch a moment before, done its small work and gone, and now it rested with the backs of the fingers against the wool and the thumb a little apart, the way a hand rests after a probe has been set down into its tray and let go. In the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof nothing had changed hands. Two hundred and eighty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and the two hundred and eighty-third had not yet been entered, because the note under the door had not yet finished. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf and listened to a house keeping itself by its not-doings. The room had its night sounds: coals settling under their ash, the wool breathing at his ear, a dry tick from cooling clay, and under all of them the low river. The large sounds were mostly weather, and the small ones did not lie, so he read the room as a trauma bay is read before the light, by the small sounds first. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

Nothing from the twenty-second, the twenty-third or the twenty-fourth had crossed under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The word that had gone over the boards on the twenty-third stood nowhere to be asked and bought no lesson here; the direction it had shaped stood where it had been put and did not repeat. Eight arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and the first two of Ash — and both of the dark month’s subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. One in twelve stood printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Eight were eight and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The widow’s mark had not been touched since the sixth opening at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at four shapings, Hessa Rale’s writing still turned toward herself and unread, Ilva’s notched strip still in her sleeve with no tenth, the breath held at the knife-sill still where it had been set and not made again. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid, none of them a sixth telling, a third telling of the shut room, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a witness, a name, a reader, a cause or a payment. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the loan was uncalled. Five held objects lay on their own places, the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that, with a finger between every pair and no sixth. Ash 30 stood offstage as a date only, five mornings ahead, and he did not bring it into a day that had not earned it.

Three hundred and fifty-three had stood exact at the going of the light yesterday; three hundred and fifty-four stood exact now, in the small hours of the twenty-fifth, and told by nobody under this roof. Six months and one hundred and seventy-three days had become six months and one hundred and seventy-four. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid three hundred and fifty-four days and its payer had no name. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door kept the broader shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and fifty-four days, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. Three hundred and fifty-four was not a round number and not a threshold. It was one more in a row nobody counted aloud. He had long ago stopped expecting to be told what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it.

The surgeon’s register took the body in its old order, as a wound is taken in the order the layers lie. The crust came first: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself and no third had come in three days to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Low Kiln, in Sill, in Grath, had ever been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure. He knew the grimace of deep cold that looks like a smile, and the pink that returns to a surface while the core is still cold; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it, and he could not lift his own head.

Under the knowing the infant wanted, and the wanting was louder. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. He wanted the thread wiped. He wanted a hand that would close on the pot handle set two inches beyond the reaching fingers. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the argument between them was the work. The body humiliated him in its exact small ways: the drool frozen with no thumb to wipe it, the head useless on the fold, the crust leathery past the point any child would eat it, the pot handle two inches beyond the hand that would have closed on it in another life. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and he could not turn over. He had once held pressure in a cavity with both hands and now he could not keep a blanket on. The dry joke lived in that and cost him nothing and was not, in the end, funny.

Before first light Sella Vail came to the hearth, and the fire came before the basket. She did not look toward the bed. Her forearms were the colour of dried slip, and the grey streak at her temple showed where the lamplight touched it; she smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under that the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which she had not swept in two years and did not intend to sweep. Her hand went to the coals first. She told them it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and the fire and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool; “it’s the river wind,” to the wind. To the pot she gave another practical sentence and to the coals another, and to the thing in the wicker she gave nothing at all.

She stood a moment over the coals counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, the cord of her widow’s mark oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted. She left the hairline showing, so that repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Near the hearth she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave.

The boy took that setting apart by what was there and by what was not: a hand, a pot, a rim, a pause; no figure spoken, no look toward the bed, no instruction to the basket. He filed the pause where it belonged and made no more of it. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and the widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash without a name and not spent, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected. None of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-four, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It had come up an inch a moment before toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, done its small work and gone, and now it rested with the backs of the fingers against the wool and the thumb a little apart, the way a hand rests after a probe has been set down in its tray and let go. In the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof nothing had changed hands. Two hundred and eighty-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and the two hundred and eighty-fifth had not yet been entered, because the note under the door had not yet finished. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf and listened to a house keeping itself by its not-doings. The room had its night sounds: coals settling under their ash, the wool breathing at his ear, a dry tick from cooling clay, and under all of them the low river. The large sounds were mostly weather, and the small ones did not lie, so he read the room as a trauma bay is read before the light, by the small sounds first. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

Nothing from the days behind had crossed under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment, and the day before this one had been kept exactly and left exactly. Eight arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and the first two of Ash — and both of the dark month’s subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. One in twelve stood printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Eight were eight and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The widow’s mark had not been touched since the sixth opening at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty; the heard bell four, four and a gap in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night; nine record-first hands with no tenth; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled. The burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at four shapings, Hessa Rale’s writing still turned toward herself and unread, Ilva’s notched strip still in her sleeve, and the breath held at the knife-sill still where it had been set and not made again. Five held objects sat with the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-sill breath beside that, none a sixth and none a seventh. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid, none a sixth telling, a third telling of the shut room, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a witness, a name, a reader, a cause or a payment. The promise under the wheel-shed stood neither given nor withdrawn, and the far-off hand that would one day lay the district’s private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

Three hundred and fifty-four had stood exact at the going of the light yesterday; three hundred and fifty-five stood exact now, in the small hours of the twenty-sixth, and told by nobody under this roof. Six months and one hundred and seventy-four days had become six months and one hundred and seventy-five. The carried fire’s fifth was unpaid three hundred and fifty-five days and its payer had no name. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door kept the broader shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and fifty-five days, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. Three hundred and fifty-five was not a round number and not a threshold. It was one more in a row nobody counted aloud. He had long ago stopped expecting to be told what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it.

The surgeon’s register took the body in its old order, as a wound is taken in the order the layers lie. The crust came first: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself and no third had come in these days to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Low Kiln, in Sill, in Grath, had ever been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure. He knew the grimace of deep cold that looks like a smile, and the pink that returns to a surface while the core is still cold; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it, and he could not lift his own head.

Under the knowing the infant wanted, and the wanting was louder. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. He wanted the thread wiped. He wanted a hand that would close on the pot handle set two inches beyond the reaching fingers. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the argument between them was the work. The body humiliated him in its exact small ways: the drool frozen with no thumb to wipe it, the head useless on the fold, the crust leathery past the point any child would eat it, the pot handle two inches beyond the hand that would have closed on it in another life. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and he could not turn over. He had once held pressure in a cavity with both hands and now he could not keep a blanket on. The dry joke lived in that and cost him nothing and was not, in the end, funny.

Before first light Sella Vail came to the hearth, and the fire came before the basket. She did not look toward the bed. Her forearms were the colour of dried slip, and the grey streak at her temple showed where the lamplight touched it; she smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under that the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which she had not swept in two years and did not intend to sweep. Her hand went to the coals first. She told them it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and the fire and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool; “it’s the river wind,” to the wind. To the pot she gave another practical sentence and to the coals another, and to the thing in the wicker she gave nothing at all.

She stood a moment over the coals counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, the cord of her widow’s mark oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted. She left the hairline showing, so that repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Near the hearth she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave.

The boy took that setting apart by what was there and by what was not: a hand, a pot, a rim, a pause; no figure spoken, no look toward the bed, no instruction to the basket. He filed the pause where it belonged and made no more of it. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and the widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash without a name and not spent, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire’s missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected. None of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The dark month was at its last tend, and the cold had a harder edge at the finger’s-width door than it had had a day ago. Out in the yard the skin on the trough had thickened until it would have held a poured spoon; the silt-clouded plate of ice stood lifted a little out of the path of feet, and the gone white frost on the well’s north lip had not come back. Under the door the river’s note sat lower, and under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood. The kiln stood cold behind the wall and nobody went out to look at it. Three coverings the cold had laid on three different objects, and between the three a finger, because likeness without a payer is only a likeness and not a cause; and the trough’s skin and the plate and the gone frost were filed where they lay and left there. Ash 30 stood four mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and its nearness was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. No ninth arithmetic was supplied early, and no seventh opening, sixth hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was invented to make the row come out even.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and eighty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the pot-crack resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and eighty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The day had offered no new thing, and that was itself an exact result entered nowhere; the keeping went on because there was nothing else under this roof to do with the attention, and attention was the one thing no institution here had ever thought to tax. The infant underneath took none of the satisfaction and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-sixth. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Nothing on this day had yet been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended.

Three hundred and fifty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Ash kept its twenty-sixth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months — the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days — was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood four mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-five; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; and through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. Forward, not closed.

The afternoon came into the Clay Yard the colour of wet ash, light lying flat across the racks so that the pots stood bellies full of a short day and gave nothing back. Sella Vail carried the boy down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, to the river wind and to the fire to be banked against their coming back, never to the basket. She had no need to tell a pot where it was going. A carried thing is told by the hand that carries it. Under the fold his ear took the yard by its small sounds first, as a field is taken before the light: rope dragging stiff, the dry small rasp of it, a pot set down a shade too near the draught and shifted, the scrape of a broom going around something instead of over it, and under all of them the river, low and going about its own work. The large sounds were mostly weather. The small ones did not lie.

She went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at the Vail corner lay where the Ash ridge had turned, with the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called the thaw, unswept both. Beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay in their own dark and were entered by nobody. The shallow pan’s gone ring held its round outline on the stone with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word inside it. the trough kept its skin, thick enough now to have held a poured spoon. Out on the worked strips the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out was gone around as it had been gone around every day of the dark month, with a look and a wide step and nothing said. Seven places, entered none. A thing entered wants a payer standing, and none stood.

Three coverings the cold had laid on three different objects stayed weather entered nowhere, and between every pair a finger, because likeness without a payer is only a likeness and not a cause. The gone white frost on the well’s north lip had not come back; the skin on the trough broke under a pail and closed behind it; the silt-clouded plate of ice stood lifted a little out of the path of feet on the flat stone by the well. He filed the three where they lay and made no fourth of them. At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool and Sella turned it once with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing. No figure came off it. No handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22, a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three would be a way, and both want a payer.

A man come down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and late. Tarn said “Yes, house” to each of them, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Hunts and repairs were directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. No runner came on any road and no collector went up or down the clay road; there was no market, no Cross and no bell, and no voice came over the boards.

Ilva was at the yard well drawing with the pail against her knee. The rope was stiff in the Ash cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up; clay stood dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. People noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A slate lay open a pace away on the stone lip with the writing turned toward whoever had left it, and nobody was reading it aloud.

A woman waiting her turn, with a pot on her hip and her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Ash’s firings had taken.

Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark lay pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away, or to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, or to the cord, or to any saying that had arrived nowhere. No figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word, and then she answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart — hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention — and filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand in the district, with a finger between it and Hessa Rale’s eighth and no tenth. He made no reader of her own keeping and joined her to none. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice; there was no voice at this well, no fifth shaping came of the saying, and the directions of the fifth, the seventeenth and the twenty-third stood where they had been put and did not repeat. Her count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The edges of the finding came near to other edges and the finger went between.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said, and to a pot on the bench he said, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the the full note that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any kiln-master who would call it early.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire’s low, Jorra, and the damper —”

His bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence. It took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no Grathic word for both mercies.

Then, after the tongue had been moved and the sentence finished and the household straightened — after the work was done, not before — the bent finger stayed in the cold iron a breath longer than the work required. It took the cold out of the fingertip and kept it there. He made no song of it. He did not name it. He did not look toward the basket. He gave no name to the men in the back room and did not say the room was cold. The extra interval had no figure, no witness and no payer standing under it; the hand went away from the iron as a hand goes away from a tool put back where it belongs, and the yard went on about its work.

The boy took that interval apart by what was there and by what was not: a finger, a piece of iron, a tongue moved a hair’s breadth, a sentence finished, a household straightened, and then a breath that the work had not asked for. He filed it on a place of its own with a finger between it and the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath and his own gaps. It was a private keeping gathered once and not spent. It was not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not an eighth of anything, not a habit, not a way, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a mending, not a cause, not a payment and not a rule, and no payer stood under it. It gathered toward the far-off hand that will one day lay the district’s private keepings together, and that hand was not his and was still far off.

He did not join the new interval to Jorra’s dust, to the damper’s double bite, to the uncalled debt, to the covered grain, to the charcoal, to Ilva’s strip or to the Ash 23 direction. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between. He’s not Vail stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, while the claim went on gathering interest against it. Nothing under this roof was made a lesson of the extra breath, and nobody under this roof noticed that a breath had been held at all.

Eight arithmetics of the year — the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and the first two of Ash — stood behind one unsigned figure, one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, eight readings eight and not one account; both of the dark month’s subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled. The row stood at five askings with no witness, the burned slate at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, and the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid. Beside the five held objects, the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and his own gaps, with a finger between every pair, lay the holding of Ash 26: gathered once, not spent, not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not an eighth of anything, not a habit, not a way, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a mending, not a cause, not a payment and not a rule, with no payer standing under it. The far-off hand that will one day lay the district’s private keepings together was still far off and was not his. No voice had come over the boards on the twenty-seventh to make a sixth telling, a third shut-room telling, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a tenth hand or a tenth stopping, and none had been supplied to make a row come out even.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace, going wide around the seven places with a look and a step and no number, and set him on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay, frozen in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Low Kiln, in Sill, in Grath had ever been given an instruction about the thread. At the hearth the fire came before the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched — a going of the light and a bed are not openings; six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash without a name and not spent, and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the finger’s-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. He filed it as answer two hundred and eighty-eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the new holding in the iron, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and eighty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond the reaching fingers. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-seventh. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Three hundred and fifty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-six days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twenty-seventh had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. Ash kept its twenty-seventh day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months — the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days — was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood three mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. No ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping invented to make a row come out even. Three counts in three places — the carried fire at four and a gap under the floor, the boards four, four and a knot overhead, the river low over lower under the door — did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood; the bell’s gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution here had ever thought to tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-eight; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; and the extra interval stayed where nobody would enter it, a keeping gathered once and not made again. Through the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 30 stood three mornings ahead and was named only as a date. Forward, not closed.

Ash kept its last three tending days without a boundary drawn, no market, no Cross or bell and no collector on the clay road. Under the Vail roof Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot’s back and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She went wide around the yard’s seven walked-around places with a look and no number, turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure or handful, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack and left the hairline showing, and set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The trough’s skin grew thick enough to hold a spoon and the river’s note sat lower under the door.

Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence, said Six counted. The next I don’t count, checked the thumb’s resting of Bone 26 and the held breath of Ash 14 and found each where put and not made again, and brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped. Jorra Vail spoke to the wheel before any person, parted dusty from clean as clot from vessel, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence --- one iron with two bites, the holding and the taking.

On the twenty-sixth, after the sentence was finished and the household had straightened, that finger stayed in the cold iron a breath longer than the work required. The boy filed it on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other keeping: gathered once and not spent, not a sixth held object, not a habit, not a way, not a rule, with no payer under it. On the twenty-seventh Jorra took his finger out and left the interval where it had been put, for a second would be a habit and a third a way. At the well Ilva, her hand to her sleeve before eyes to the asker and mouth after both, answered Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, the notched wood unread; it was the ninth record-first hand, with no tenth, and she was the speaker of no voice.

Each night the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note, took a small intake, held it whole on the note’s own finishing and came straight down --- answers 283 through 288, exact and worth nothing at two inches of air.
