The Figure Without a Signature

Days 297–299 · Frost 28–30, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

Three readings make a month and none of them knows what the grain cost. The bell keeps its gap, the slate keeps its figure, and the price stands where nobody set it down.

— A saying at the reading block in the Cross of Sill, told in Frost

— Day 297 —

Frost 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813

The hand had been laid down against the fold at the low angle of the loaf shelf as a man puts a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and it lay there still in the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof while the twenty-eighth of Frost came on the way cold comes into a banked house, taking the room one board at a time and asking nothing of the child in it. The boy came into his two hundred and ninety-seventh day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and seventeen days were told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; the count had gone from two hundred and ninety-six to two hundred and ninety-seven the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. A hundred and sixty-eight honest answers stood behind him, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-seven days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Frost 28 had come on its own ordinary Eighthday, with no market, no reading at the Cross and no bell, and it asked nothing of the boy under the fold except that he lie there and be warm where he could.

The room had its morning smell before it had its morning light: wet wool lifting from the fold, tallow gone cold in its dish, a thread of clay dust in the crease of the bedding, and under all of it the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house calls the thaw. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note of water over water, a thing over a thing, and behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with a gap where the fifth should have stood. First light came in as a narrow bar through the door and did not move; it found the dust in the air and then the rim of the pot on its board, and went no further into the room than the fold. The boy lay at the angle where the shelf becomes a roof and took what came as it came, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. Counting was an old habit and not a gift; he had counted the exits of rooms before he had ever counted the nails of a floor, and nobody here had asked him to count anything.

He took the body first. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on the bed, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that the ease a man sees on a face at that stage is not ease, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and seventeen days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order — crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting — and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. The dry heat had drawn the water out of the thread and left it stiff as wire drawn through tallow; he could not lift a hand to wipe it, and no instruction had ever been given to anybody to wipe it, and the two facts stood side by side without either becoming a complaint.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body that could be warmed. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal. He had signed a chart once without reading the note in the margin and carried that shape into a country where notes are turned face-down toward floorboards; here he read every note he was not given and entered none of them. It was an old habit, this standing ready with a fact nobody would ask for, and he kept it the way a house keeps its charcoal under a finger and does not look. A man who has spent his life putting things right in rooms where somebody always called for him finds it a strange discipline to lie under a shelf and call nothing. He had been learning it since his first morning under this roof and it had not got easier.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety-seven days; at the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The knot had the width of a thumb and was not a nail; a row made even by calling it one would have been a row made even by a lie.

In the small hours he turned the file out on the towel as a surgeon turns instruments out on a tray, each in its place, none of them a cure. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the market on Frost 20 and held since, stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night; no bell had come on this morning and he would not lend an ear to one that had not earned it. The heard and the told lay side by side with a finger between, two sayings of one sound and not one hearing borrowed to stand under the other.

The told figure stood beside the paid and the space between them had no signature. Frost’s first arithmetic and Frost’s second rode locked up the clay road in 's box toward High Kiln, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; two readings were two sayings and not one account, and neither had closed the hand that went in on Seed 15 or the man carried out on Day 188. The dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth at the rack end, turned with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure, so that any number travelling over it would not know what it cost. A figure that does not know what a thing cost can pass over it twice and go away clean; the boy had seen a man do that with a chart and a good reason, and the good reason had not made the number true.

The Frost 14 saying stood on a place of its own with its first, second and third shapings, a finger between every pair, beside 's eighth record-first hand with a finger between that and the nearest shaping; not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining and not paid, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of the three stood anywhere to be asked. Ilva’s ninth hand stayed nine with the notched birch strip unread in her sleeve and no figure coming off the stick, and he filed no tenth hand; the count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the burned-slate word stood at four, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the shut-room tellings stood at two and had not become three; three pot sayings stood together with the Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The seventh green stood a colour and the seventh shape a shape, and no eighth came to either. Nine hands were nine and not a rule, and nine stoppings were nine and not a payment; a tenth supplied to make either stand up as a doctrine would have wanted a payer and none stood.

Garl Vail stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and nobody in the house let it be filled. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-seven days. Tarn’s loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up or down the clay road; Jorra Vail’s promise stood ungiven and he’s not Vail stayed sealed; from the Debt Loft, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The house’s keepings by not-doings stayed on a place apart from the boy’s own gaps with a finger between them, for want of a payer under either. Nothing stood up and called itself a rule, and the keeping of a man by omission was maintenance and not a story.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, as she had gone every morning of her life in this house, and gave the instruction to the fire and never to the basket. She told it the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. The small hours had laid a film of grey over everything, and she moved through it with her hands already knowing what they had to do: the wool told to keep the river wind off his ears, the pot told its word, the broom told to go around, and never a word spoken to the child in the fold who could not have answered. She carried no number into the room and took none out; what she counted she counted with her lips and kept where nobody was shown. When she came to the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath was unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, the little door of it closing on nothing, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not look toward the bed.

At the rack end she lifted a corner of the wool over the dressed grain to feel for damp and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. The wool went down again over the grain the way a fold goes down over a face that is not to be looked at. Out in the worked strips the field kept an empty place the planting had not filled; beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them; on the stone lip of the yard well the round ring where the shallow pan had stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word to say what had been set there; the trough beside it had a skin over its water from edge to edge; and on the flat stone by the well, set out of the path of feet, the lifted plate of ice stood clouded with silt. The stayed frost, the skinned water and the clouded plate were three coverings in three places, and the boy kept them with a finger between every pair. He made no fourth likeness of them and no cause; a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood. The three were weather, entered nowhere, and no figure came off any of them. She did not bring any of it under the roof as a lesson or a mending, and the boy did not make a sign of any of it.

When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. The boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had learned to watch a surgeon’s hand leave a field — for what it did not touch — and filed the not-touching on its own place, maintenance and not a message. Instructions went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water; the basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He wanted the thread wiped and the crust lifted and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and he got none of the three and could not ask for any of them. There was no bitterness in the not-asking either, which was the second small betrayal of the morning, and he did not give it a place to stand.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the next room. At the end of the morning a small intake came from the room whose floor does not touch his — the breath of a woman counting what she will not say aloud — and he took it at its top and let it go on the same note, whole and unjoined. He did not carry it into the carried fire’s missing fifth, did not cross the room’s letting-go with the river’s low note of water over water, did not borrow a house count, did not supply a beat under the floor to make the row come out even, for a fifth supplied to please a total is a finding forged and a payer invented to stand under it. The letting-go went on belonging to the woman who let it go and was not crossed or borrowed; it was her breath and her count and not his. The hand came down on the river’s note as it always did, and the gap stayed a gap.

The hundred and sixty-ninth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant’s plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue; nothing bought by the checking could reach two inches of air. He had once spent a life finding the one error that makes a set of numbers agree, and here the finding was to leave them as they stood.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. One hundred and sixty-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing. Two hundred and ninety-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and seventeen days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Frost 30 stood two mornings off and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it or made a sign of anything. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended; no thread was opened and none was closed. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger’s width and nothing that had moved. The river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door after the hand came down. The crust lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed, and nothing that had been counted under the roof had bought the lifting of it.

Sella Vail carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the half-pace was not haste and not fear but the distance a woman keeps from a thing that any man with a slate in his hand could enter. She gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the wind, Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind, to the wool and not to the child who could not have answered, and the boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had once watched a surgeon’s hand leave a field — for what it did not touch. The yard had the look of a room somebody had swept around a sleeping animal: a wide border left where the feet went, and the animal undisturbed in the middle of it. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the seven stayed seven and did not stand up and call themselves an account, for an account wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood under any of them.

At the Vail corner the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay beside the finer grey hairline the house calls the thaw, and she swept neither, giving the broom its instruction before anybody — Go around. It cost enough to come in; it’ll cost more to take out. Beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates kept their place in the dust and the yard went around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no hand picked up a piece and no slate was entered. On the stone at the rack end the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; a pan was gone from it years ago and the ring had outlasted every account that might have said what became of it. The trough beside the yard well had a skin over its water from edge to edge, and out on the worked strips the field’s empty place lay where the planting had not filled it, and in the returning line another place lay where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The boy carried the missing hand the way he carried the crust in the fold and the man carried out on Day 188: as an object on its own place, not named, not paid, not joined to anything that stood near it.

At the well the white frost-bloom stayed on the north lip where it had stayed since Frost 8, past noon and past the going of the light and onto days that had no business keeping it; the rope gave its dry note as a woman drew, and the water in the trough held its skin. On the flat stone by the well, set where no foot went, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt, lifted out of the trough on Frost 9 and told where to stand. The yard called all of it weather, and weather was entered nowhere. The boy kept the white lip, the trough’s skin and the plate on three places of their own with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness between them and no cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood. Three coverings laid over three depths were three coverings; a fourth likeness would have been an account with no payer, which is the kind of account this house has learned to leave where it lies.

At the rack end she lifted one corner of the wool from the dressed grain to feel for damp, and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. The arithmetic standing two mornings off would read over a thing whose price it did not know, and the space between the figure and what the grain had cost would stay unsigned. He had learned to recognize that space the way another man recognizes a familiar face across a room, and he did not greet it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the yard’s failing light, and she did not look toward the basket.

A wrong wind came up off High Kiln and brought the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards, and it touched the fold at his ear and went out again on the wind that brought it — a direction arrived nowhere, not a second edge paid, not a sign, not a mending. He kept it on a place of its own and did not lay it across the house’s not-doings or his own gaps, for want of a payer under either. The keeping stayed a finding and not a rule.

At the kindling Tarn had the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He said Six counted. The next I don’t count to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, and said Yes, house to a woman who wanted the thaw early and Yes, house to a man who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of the clay road and had stood there since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name — a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of the Debt Loft, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it.

At the edge of the Vail wheel-shed Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella’s sentence came low and unfinished his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no Grathic word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set it on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. At the well Ilva drew with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft, and the district would not have turned its head twice for her; she was of the yard in the way the rack and the trough were of the yard. A neighbour with a pot, falling in beside her at the bucket-round, asked what the month’s firings had taken now the month was nearly out, and an open slate lay on the stone lip with the writing turned toward its owner and nobody reading it aloud.

Her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The flat of it came down on the place where the strip lay as a woman lays a hand flat on a thing she has no mind to take up; the notches were not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted, not asked about and not joined to the open slate a pace away, and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the woman with the pot after the hand had settled and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat between, and she answered the question put and not the one behind it: Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. She put the flat of her hand down once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned to look at her twice.

The boy, lying at the edge of the work where no slate lay, took the order of her reaching apart the way he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not: hand to the record, thumb over the notches, eyes to the asker, mouth after both. He filed it as the ninth record-first hand on a place of its own beside the eighth, with a finger between every pair, and filed no tenth, and made no figure of the slate’s nearness and kept that nearness as an absence rather than a joining. He made her no reader of her own keeping, no witness and no party, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of this stood anywhere to be asked. Nine were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. She did not look toward the basket and did not know she had been read, and nothing of the exchange came under the roof as lesson, figure, mending, cause or payment.

The light went out of the yard without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost 28 went the way Frost 1 had come, without a lesson left on the step. She carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on the bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. The thread at his mouth lay frozen where it had lain since morning and was left there; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a going of the light is not an opening. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. At the rack end she lifted a corner of the wool from the dressed grain to feel for damp and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it; three numbers had gone over it that month without knowing what it cost, and the space between the figure and the price stayed unsigned. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay. The basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

At the kindling Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with the worn thumb-mark almost worn flat, six standing in it and the seventh at the lip beside Garl Vail’s unforged collector sentence about the man who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. He told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count in the tone of a man stating the weather, and the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping, with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping, the ninth, and he did not make a tenth of it before the seventh year at its very end. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the small interval showing where it should have gone, and the breath from Seed 16 stayed unspent with it. His chest had been tight since the dark.

Yes, house he said to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped and joined to no cause and to no ear. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. Up in the Debt Loft, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Frost 30, and nobody said the omission had a name.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door of the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. He did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the bed or the basket. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two shut-room tellings stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift, and the promise of the foundling’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not ask about Tarn’s ear and Tarn did not ask about the room.

Garl Vail stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and nobody in the house let it be filled. The clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey beside it and neither had been swept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-nine days, and no collector had come up the clay road for the debt and none had gone down it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of it.

The small keepings stayed small through the going of the light. Ilva’s short strip of notched birch lay in her sleeve out at the yard well, unread, unfigured, not taken out for any slate, and no tenth record-first hand was filed to stand beside the ninth and make a rule of nine. The three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood on a place of their own beside Hessa Rale’s eighth hand with a finger between every pair, three and not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining and not paid, and no fourth came to stand up and be asked. The runner’s slate went up the road unread and no shaping made her a reader, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and nothing of that saying stood anywhere to be asked. The ownerless question about the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no sixth, and no witness stood and no row was named. At the pot board the seventh green was a colour and at the market’s edge the seventh shape was a shape, each on a place that did not touch the other, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over either and no joining to loaf, slate, sale, hearing, cause or romance. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-nine days with its payer unnamed, gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river’s note. There, at the very end of the day, a small intake stood whole — breath from the room whose floor did not touch his, the small intake, the hold, the letting-go, all kept as one object on one note — and he let it go on the same note without carrying it into the fire’s missing fifth, the bell’s gap left behind in the square, or the river’s low over lower. The hundred and seventy-fourth answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon’s clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant’s louder want of the crust two inches off at his ear, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. Nothing bought by the checking could reach two inches. The hand came down on the river’s note as it always did and the gap stayed a gap, and he did not fill the gap to make the row come out even.

Two hundred and ninety-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and nineteen days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. One hundred and seventy-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the going of the light: no third reading into one account, no figure into a price with a signature under it, no bell’s gap into a count, no green into a name, no shape into a story, no boy’s kept gap into the house’s not-doings, no fourth likeness made a cause and no tenth hand or stopping stood up. No thread was opened and none was closed. The three arithmetics stayed locked in the box up the road, the dressed grain stayed covered and unfigured at the rack end, the three shapings stayed unpaid, the charcoal stayed beneath the shelf, the loan stayed uncalled and the promise stayed ungiven.

Bone stood a direction in the dark beyond the finger’s-width door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. It would come on its own morning, the hunting-and-repair month with a hunter’s carrying arm, and the next opening stood in Bone’s first firing if the household fired, earned by Bone’s own light, five if it fired and not a rule. The boy did not hope for it and did not count toward it; a day that has not been lived has not earned the things that will be read over it, and he had long ago stopped bringing tomorrow’s arithmetic into tonight’s fold to see what it would weigh.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger’s width and nothing that had moved. The river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door, and out on the flat stone by the well the plate of ice stood clouded with silt out of the path of feet, and Frost went out without a boundary drawn.

Frost went out as it had come in, without a boundary drawn. Three days came each on its own: two tending days with no market, Cross or bell, and a Tenthday that was the month’s third and last Account-day. Nobody told the boy his days had come to two hundred and ninety-nine. Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days.

He took the body: one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third to even the row; the thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. The grain turned beneath the wool with a lighter touch and named no cost; the pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not touch the widow’s mark, for a reading is not an opening.

On the twenty-eighth Ilva drew at the yard well; asked the cost, her hand went to her sleeve and stayed there, the strip not taken out or read. Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. The boy filed her reaching as the ninth hand and no tenth. Tarn stopped the ear-sentence at the word that would call the loan; Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper and withheld the note he knew three turns ahead. Garl stayed in objects, his name unspoken two hundred and ninety-nine days.

On the thirtieth Berr Clay came up short off the clay road. He wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve into the two hundred and sent the figure over the basket twice. He called the levy and denied it with your house, never I, taking himself out of his sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field, and went off with the box locked and Frost’s third subtraction inside. The arithmetic changed a third time and could not be changed back; the figure read was still not the figure paid. The bell went four, four and a gap.

Three readings stood on three places, three and not one account. The finding went on a place of its own with no payer: not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending and not a doctrine. The hand filed the hundred and sixty-ninth through the hundred and seventy-fourth answers, each worth nothing at two inches of air. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open with every gap left gap, and the plate of ice stood clouded with silt out of the path of feet.
