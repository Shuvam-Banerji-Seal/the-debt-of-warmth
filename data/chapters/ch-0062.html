The Basket Heard the Figure Twice

Days 237–239 · Grain 28–30, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A reading is not a payment and the end of a month is not a door. The basket hears the figure twice and carries home the weight of neither; the row stands where it stood.

— A saying of the Clay Yard, told at the going out of Grain

— Day 237 —

Grain 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay against the fold where it had come down on the river’s next low note, and the boy set the coordination down where a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of the work. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close the two inches to the pot handle, and both were true and neither was a sign; the two rhythms held one against the other across a wall that does not touch went onto the sixth place with the other answers, an object laid with objects, exact, complete, worth nothing. Under the Vail kiln-house roof fire went on in one room and river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

He was two hundred and thirty-seven days old and six months and fifty-seven days, and nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-six to two hundred and thirty-seven with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. Hot clay came first under the roof in Low Kiln, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw. The day’s first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. There was no market on an Eighthday and no Cross and no bell; Grain 30 stood two mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-seven days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. The surgeon’s register took the three in at a glance and filed them apart, the way he had once filed three wounds from one blow that had no single instrument behind them.

Sella Vail had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The two rooms did not touch. In the room that was not his the fire kept its own time under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, and in the room that was not hers the river kept its low note through the gap, and between them a wall stood that had never been given a door. Nothing in his room held a count for him to borrow. He had learned to keep a rhythm without the sound, as a man keeps the step of a road he has walked a thousand times, but he borrowed nothing from the room that does not touch his and did not try. The dark went on being the dark, and the cold went on stopping where it had stopped, and he lay with the fold at his ear and listened to three things that would not become one.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from that ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account, and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. Nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back. It had come to the end of an order written out for it and had neither a command to go further nor a command to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in the small hours the second was the louder, because there was nothing in the dark to set the eye other things to count. The adult who had once stood at a warming bed and known the number by the patient’s skin took the same reading now from the inside of his own wrist and entered it nowhere. There was no hand to carry the finding to a slate, and no slate under this roof would have known what it counted.

A body read at two paces is exact and worth nothing without a hand to carry it; a body read from the inside is the same finding with the same worthless exactness. He had spent a life learning what a cold patient’s skin means and had no way to spend the knowledge on himself. The child in him did not care about the worth of it. The child in him wanted the crust, and wanted the thread wiped, and wanted a hand that could close two inches, and got none of the three, and went on wanting them with a patience the adult had long ago stopped calling patience and had instead entered as a fact.

Nothing from the old life broke through into the small hours. No voice came with the grammar of a different country, no lit rectangle with a name on it, no file in a white room; the surgeon was present only as a way of noticing, and the noticing was enough. He parted a colour from a hearing the way he had once parted clot from vessel, and a shape from a seventh of a note, and the figure read over a covered basket from the figure paid, and let each lie on the place it had earned. It was a dry and unsentimental business, and it cost him something that he could not name and did not try to name. He had a joke about it that he did not laugh at, because a laugh costs more than a man in his position can spare. The child in him wanted the crust and the adult kept the trays, and the two went on arguing without a word between them.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirty-seven days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule; The burned-slate word stood at four on a place of its own with a hand between it and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and the bell of Grain 30 was not lent to a day that had not earned it. The told figure lay beside the paid: one in twelve read low into the crowd on Grain 10 and again on Grain 20, the figure going twice over the basket each time, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and still not the figure paid. Grain 20’s arithmetic had been changed and could not be changed back, and its tarnish was kept on its own place, held apart from the arithmetic actually paid, the way a chart’s contradiction is held apart from the signature that would close it. The true subtraction stayed locked in Berr Clay’s box beyond the roof and answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him, no offstage death entered.

Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, nine objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the carried man’s pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, gathered at Grain’s first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since; no eighth of either had come, no second look was joined, no name was spoken over either, no loaf passed, no slate came near and no sale was made. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured near the hearth. The returning-line place had gone around with a look and no number.

On a seventh place of its own, with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the sayings, the tellings, the hearings and the count, the question stood with three askings and nobody to own them. Three askings are not one witness; a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. He did not make a fourth of them to fill the silence. A question about a row asks for a place, a place wants a witness, and a witness wants a day when somebody stands somewhere and can be asked; he kept the want where it was and put nobody under it.

Likenesses were taken down one at a time before they could stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer, and he had put no payer under any of these. The three pot sayings stayed sayings. The three near-full answers stayed advanced and unpaid. The burned-slate word stayed a direction over the boards and not a fourth saying, and the counting room stayed a room shut in the war and not a third telling. He had learned here that a row is not an account until somebody stands under it, and that a man who leaves a row standing long enough to want an answer will put somebody under it before he knows what he has done. He left the rows standing and put nobody under them. He had kept nothing and spent nothing and joined nothing, and the file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing at two paces of air.

Before first grey the house began its ordinary work, and the work went to everything in it except the basket. Sella came to the fire before she came to him. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. She said the house where another woman would have said I, told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said. The fire answered four low pulls behind the wall and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-seven days, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing at all, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The wall stood between that count and his, and he took its length only as a man takes the length of a shift he has worked a thousand times; nothing crossed from that room that could be entered. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At the loaf shelf she moved her lips along the two-gapped row without a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch. Then she kept her finger on top of the shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required and not looking toward the bed. The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-seven days, because a morning, a shelf, a yard, a basket and a pot are not openings.

At the hearth she turned the dressed grain under its wool for damp. Her fingers went into it to the knuckle and came out dry, and she spread it thin on the flat stone to finish and gathered it back by the handful, and set the fold flat over it again. It changed state and gained no figure. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm’s weight, which knew weight and not cost, and entered it on no slate and laid it across none of the absences. She had carried it past the racks and the well on the same mornings she had carried him, and the arm kept both weights and the price of neither.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. “Ask the pot,” Sella said. “The weather knows the rest.” She carried the unanswered question away level, the way a woman carries a full dish across a room, so that nothing slopped and nothing was entered. Later, coming down the clay road in the morning’s thin light, a man brought a number up out of a fist toward the yard well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so the figure arrived nowhere. The trough stood bare and dark beside it and its water was called weather. In the Clay Yard the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it lay unswept, and she went around both without a broom. The arithmetic that had been read over the covered grain on Grain 20 kept its tarnish on its own place; the arithmetic paid kept its place beside it; and the true subtraction stayed beyond the roof in a box nobody under this roof had opened.

At the low angle of the shelf she set the basket down, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, and went to the morning’s work with her burn shut on nothing. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note. The first smell under the roof was hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, sour Ash under all three; the first sound was the kiln in fours with its gap. No bell had gone and none would go before Grain 30.

She had carried the cost-question away level toward the pot and nothing in it had been given a figure. He put the first question of the chapter to his hand under the conditions that had sometimes answered. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger’s-width door and stayed for the small intake, and came down at once against the fold. The forty-ninth answer went onto the sixth place with no payer named. It was not spent and it was not explained: not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Forty-nine answers stood there now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand came down at once against the fold while Sella carried the unanswered cost-question away level toward the pot, and the count went forward without a voice.

The afternoon thinned without ending. It lost weight the way a banked fire loses light and keeps its heat, and under the low angle of the loaf shelf what was left of it settled behind the pot and came into the fold where the boy lay. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account, and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it. It had neither a command to go further nor a command to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He read the three points from the inside the way he had once read a patient’s skin under a light too dim for the rest of the room, and entered the reading nowhere. The adult who had stood at a warming bed and known the number by the slow return of colour took the same measure now off his own wrist, and the child in him did not care what the number was. The child wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand that could close on the pot handle. He got none of the three and went on wanting them. Both registers were true, and as the light went off the wall the want was the louder of the two, because there was nothing left in the afternoon to set the eye to counting. A man can be exact about a thing and still want it, and the wanting does not make the exactness a lie. He had learned that in a white room with a sheet over a body and had carried it here without ever saying it aloud.

Everything in the room had its own way of being kept, and none of them was a story. Garl Vail was kept in objects. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held a broader man’s shape with a crease at the left shoulder that no hand had smoothed out in two years; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on the left, which nobody filled; the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner lay unswept, and beside it the new, finer hairline of grey that Sella called the thaw had been stood over with a broom and not swept. One of his proverbs had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, *leave the crack showing*, and was still said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out; the joke had outlived the thing it was true about and cost more now there was nobody to say it in his own voice. The debt against a firing of tiles circled the house’s talk without ever being named — gone around the way a woman goes around a pot left on the floor in the dark, a careful circle and no word for the thing circled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and thirty-seven days. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of any of it; a line left where it was is still a line kept. No collector had come up the clay road for the tiles on Grain 28, and none had gone down it.

He set these beside the things he kept himself and did not lay one across another. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with no seventh; the coat and the seat and the clean dark line stood in the room and had never asked to be heard. A house that kept a man in a coat and a boy who kept a month’s arithmetic in the order of instruments laid on a towel were doing one motion in the dark, each without knowing the other kept anything at all. He did not call it an equality and did not put a finger between them, because the finger would have been the beginning of an account. He had learned what a row of likenesses will do to a man who wants an answer, and he left the rows standing and put nobody under them.

At the kindling the afternoon went on in the same thin key. Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody — go around what the water left, it had cost enough to come in and would cost more to take out — and Tarn took it up the way a man takes up a tool that has already been named, saying “Yes, house.” He took nothing on his own authority: not the broom, not the small knife, not even the breath he had been holding since Seed 16. When he spoke to the wood it was in the tone of a man stating the weather. “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” And after a moment, to no one: “I owe six.”

The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. Six years stood in a thumb-mark worn almost flat by use, where the house could check them; the seventh stood at the lip where it could not, and that was the one entry in this house the house could not check. Beside the knife lay the sentence Garl had never forged, about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford — the man had been Garl himself, and Garl had given no name to him. Tarn had never entered the sentence and never thrown it away. It lay where a tool lies that a man may one day need and has decided he will not use. He put the knife back with the flat of his hand and went through the motion of checking it with his eyes, because a man who owns nothing should know where the small things are.

His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line along the skirting board, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed the small sound of the loft settling came to the boy as a direction and nothing more. In its unmarked clay pot in the Debt Loft lay the warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold long since, and he could no more reconcile it with the debt than the house’s kindness with the account he kept of it. He had stopped trying, which was not forgiving. The stone reached the boy only as that sound with a direction: a place, a pot, a weight settling on a board, and nothing that could be carried down. Fire had gone out of the stone and the debt had not gone anywhere.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to Tarn’s teeth as he swept. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, said “Yes, house” to the fire, and went on with the broom. The stopping was a remembered stopping and not a new one: nine of them stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, nine objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent. The loan was not called, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Grain 28. He agreed so entirely with those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check it had once made there, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning to look along it. The stone kept the place where the stroke had stopped and the boy kept the unchecking stroke on a tray of its own, as a surgeon keeps a finding that has not yet been repeated. Two ears kept their silence under one roof and neither man put a word between them. Tarn did not look toward the basket and the boy did not ask about the loft; the keeping went on in both directions, each man sure he was the only one doing it.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width-open door off the Vail wheel-shed in the middle of the afternoon with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened. It was not bone-coloured slip. He parted the two as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, keeping dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the room off the shed before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings and no rule; The burned-slate word stood at four on a place of its own, a direction over the boards from Slate Row that had arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and did not become three. No fifth saying stood up, no third telling of the shut room, no fourth likeness made a cause, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. No pot was lost in the yard.

He grew quieter as the afternoon went on, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. The quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella’s sentence at the hearth went low and unfinished — “It wants —” — his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair’s breadth and was left standing. It was a remembered repetition and not a change made again, and the boy filed it on its own place, paired with no other fraction of the kind. He read the iron again as one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies and joined to no kiln. “He’s not Vail” stayed sealed where it had been sealed on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed or the basket, and the boy did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined.

Grain 29, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

The small hours came on without a boundary drawn anywhere under the roof. They arrived the way heat arrives in a banked fire: not as a thing that began but as a thing that had been going on longer than the ear had been listening. the boy came into his two hundred and thirty-eighth day with nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath saying either that or the other thing, six months and fifty-eight days, and the count went forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-seven with no hand to enter it. “Which row did the pot go quiet on?”

No owner stood behind it. No room, no hand at the kiln, no cause. It did not come from the fire or the pot or the wool or the running water or the river wind, all of which had their instructions and went on keeping them; it did not come into the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. First the skin of it, which was what it asked. A row. It did not ask how the pot had gone quiet, or why, or who had carried out the man on Day 188, or what ailed him; it asked on which row, and a row is a place to stand. Under that lay where it stood. Over the boards, in the air, with nothing behind it and nothing under it. A thing that stands nowhere cannot be walked up to and cannot be asked a second question, which is a surgeon’s way of saying that it is not a witness. Under that lay what it would want before any man could answer it. Somebody who had been there, who stood somewhere, who had seen which row and could be asked to say so and could be held to the saying. A witness. Under that lay the last layer, the one a man comes to only after he has opened the others and found them empty: who would have to stand under the answer to pay for it. Nobody stood. No owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, nobody to stand under the saying and nobody to be asked. The true subtraction sat locked beyond this roof in a box gone up the clay road, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him and no offstage death. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places — the fraction at the damper, the held beat beneath the shelf, the half-beat on the flagstones — with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line read off the carried man at the well on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth.

Beside the hearings but never among them, the seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape stayed a shape. Across the square at the pot board a silent woman had clay under her nails in the small crescents, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long, the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip; she had turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood. At the market’s edge a shape in cast-offs had carried a man’s shoulders on a child’s frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull; its head had gone toward a set-down pot a half-beat late and stopped in the middle of the turn. No eighth green had come and no eighth shape had come; no second look had been joined to the first, no name had been spoken over either, no loaf had passed, no slate had come near and no sale had been made. The colour had not been laid across the pale line at the well or the line on the carried man, and the shape had not been joined to the boy’s own turning. The returning-line place was still gone around with a look and no number.

And on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the sayings, the tellings, the hearings and the count, the question stood with four askings and no witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. Four askings are still not one witness; they are four holes in a silence and do not stand up and call themselves a mouth. No fifth asking had come over the boards in the going of the light, and the row stood where it stood.

He put the question to his hand the sixth way of the chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger’s-width door and stayed. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house now to borrow, no fire-sitter standing over the coals and counting, nothing in his room to hold a rhythm against; he took the inch through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended, and brought it down on the river’s next low note. Two rhythms crossed in the inch and nowhere else, one held against the other across a wall that does not touch. It was remembered, not a change made again. He filed the coordination on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true and neither stood up and said what the other was. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to a pot handle, lift a hand to his own chin or lift a crust two inches to his mouth. Fifty-four answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a sign.

Two hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and fifty-nine days told by nobody; the fire’s fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow’s mark untouched with no opening in fifty-nine days; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Grain’s third and last reading had gone up the clay road behind them and was unread under this roof, and the figure had gone twice over the basket and neither telling had come home with them. Smoke 1 stood three mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing; the hand came down on the river’s next low note, and the count went forward, exact and unspent, into a day that had not yet earned its heading.

Three days came under the Vail roof. The kiln drew in fours with the fifth missing, the river kept its note, and the boards held four nails, four and a knot; he kept the counts apart; agreeing them wanted a payer and none stood to any. He came into his two hundred and thirty-seventh day, then the eighth, then the ninth. A brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear; the thread froze unwiped; each small hours the file lay exact and worth nothing.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, instructing everything but the basket. She turned the dressed grain under wool, spread and gathered it by the handful; it changed state and gained no figure, counted in glances and by an arm that knew weight, not cost. Cost-questions went to the pot and the weather; a number out of a fist went back into its sleeve. The seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape a shape; the widow’s mark hung untouched for fifty-seven, fifty-eight, fifty-nine days.

At the kindling Tarn took the broom only when instructed, told the wood six were counted and the next he did not count, and set the knife where six could be checked and the seventh could not. The ear-sentence reached the calling word and stopped; nine stoppings stood, the Seed 16 breath was unspent, no collector came. Jorra Vail parted dust from slip, withheld the note he knew three turns ahead, kept the burned slate at four, and left he’s not Vail sealed.

On the afternoon of Grain 29 an ownerless voice asked over the boards which row the pot had gone quiet on. Weather and barley answered, and the voice went up the terrace while the row stood. The boy opened it as a surgeon opens a wound and found no witness. Four askings are not one witness. He set the fourth telling beside the first three with a finger between, and made no fifth.

On Grain 30, the third and last Account-day, Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s thin shade. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve twice over the basket, said the levy was called and not by him, spoke of your house and never I, and went off with the box locked. The district’s arithmetic changed a third time and could not be changed back; Hessa Rale carried the slate up the road unread. A figure read aloud was still not the figure paid.

Six times the hand answered, the forty-ninth through the fifty-fourth, each filed with no payer named. At the close it went up an inch, rode one of Sella’s counted breaths from the room that does not touch his, kept past the silence after her count and came down on the river’s next low note --- two rhythms in one inch, remembered, not a change made again. The file lay open; the count went forward. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
