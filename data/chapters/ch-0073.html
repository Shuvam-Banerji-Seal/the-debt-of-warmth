The Mark Before the Kiln

Days 270–272 · Frost 1–3, Y.H. 813 · The Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln

The month turns on no spoken word. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln, holds one breath, and touches nothing else that day.

— A kiln-house saying in Low Kiln, told at the turning into Frost

— Day 270 —

Frost 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The boy had taken it up on the hundred and fourteenth answer and set it down again with no more ceremony than that: up an inch toward the river’s note, held for the small intake and for the hold and the letting-go of a breath counted in the room that does not touch, kept at that small height through the little silence after the count had ended, then down on the river’s second low note. It was placed and it was done. He did not recall it as a sign, or as growth, or as a reaching, or as the first thing of a month; a tool laid down under a fold is a tool laid down, and the next hand comes to it without looking and without thanks.

Under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln the house kept itself the way a pot keeps heat, by not opening. The count had crossed in the dark from two hundred and sixty-nine to two hundred and seventy, and two hundred and seventy stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety days were told by nobody. The carried fire under the floor had given its fifth unpaid two hundred and seventy days and its payer had no name. None of this was written. The boy had stopped expecting to be told what a number counted, which was not the same thing as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, neither going forward nor going away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on the last market-day, stayed in the square beside the told bell of the Long Night; it was not brought under this roof and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Frost came on in its own morning and was earned by that light. It had not been brought into a day that had not earned it; Smoke had gone out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two names for the months had been set right or left wrong. There was no market, no Cross and no bell under this roof on a Firstday. The first firing of Frost stood one morning ahead and was not reached for and not made into a promise, a lesson or a sign; a month’s turning is not an opening and the mark belonged to the kiln, not to the turning of a page. Smoke’s third arithmetic lay behind and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay’s locked box, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end, and the space between the figure that would go over it and what it had cost had no signature on it.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket and before any instruction was given. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, fed them and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening — the small intake, the hold, the letting-go — and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. The burn was shaped like a little door closed on nothing. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing. The pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. Every instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot and the running water, and never to the basket. She carried at the half-pace from every open slate, set him on the bed at the low angle of the shelf and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. The two rooms did not touch, and nothing crossed between them but the half-beat at the pot’s rim.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, hearth or bed since, because a morning and a hearth and a basket and a bed are not openings. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The one touch that would come on the morrow’s kiln belonged to a day that had not yet earned it and was not brought early into this one; it was not made a rehearsal or a sign now, any more than the first firing was made a lesson afterward. Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease on its peg, the wheel-seat keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack and said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, the debt against a firing of tiles going around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy days, and no collector had come up the clay road on this morning or gone down it.

First light came up the yard and laid a thin bar along the boards and along the fold at his ear. The room smelled of hot clay under tallow and of wet wool, and under those the sour breath of the unswept corner called the thaw, which she had told the coals and left standing where it stood. He was carried and awake and silent, and the morning went on around him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. Nothing had been called and nothing had been paid; the household went on and his exactness went on with it, and he did not let the one stand up as a lesson about the other.

In the small hours he turned the file out on the towel and found the whole of it exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; three sayings about a pot stood with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the told bell stood beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid, with Smoke’s third arithmetic gone up the road in a locked box and unread under this roof. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth; three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth and the pale line of a carried man on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with a hundred and fourteen answers on it. Nine record-first hands stood with Ilva’s ninth on its own tray and her strip unread in her sleeve, and the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth. The row question stood at five askings with one further shaping, no witness under any and no row named. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three.

The shallow pan’s gone ring, the shelf’s two gaps and the fold’s one crust with its unnamed place beside it lay on trays that did not touch. The house’s not-doings stood beside his own kept gaps with a finger between and no joining, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. And the finding of the third reading stood on a place of its own: a figure read a third time over the same basket was no nearer the covered thing than a chart laid over a covered body is nearer the wound under it, and three readings were three and not one account. It was not laid across the house’s not-doings or across his own kept gaps, and it was not a lesson, a grievance or a doctrine. The file lay open with every tray parted and every gap left gap, and he made nothing of any of it and would not let any of it stand up as a gift.

In the room that does not touch, a woman had counted breaths while the house was dark: the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. The count had ended some time before and a little silence stood after it, whole and empty on a place of its own. He did not cross it and did not fill it and did not borrow its rhythm, and he did not lay it across the fire’s missing fifth under the floor, for that would want a payer and none stood. Below, the fire kept four with its gap; through the door the river kept low over lower; overhead the boards kept four, four and a knot. Three places, three counts, and the empty interval kept on a fourth with a finger between it and each.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note. It held for the small intake and came down on the next low note. It borrowed no house count and supplied no fifth, held the interval neither through the counted breath in the other room nor past the little silence after it, and crossed nothing. It was one hundred and fifteen, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and fourteen with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Under the floor the fire kept four with its gap; through the finger’s-width door the river went low over lower; the first morning of Frost stood unspent beside the basket. Two hundred and seventy stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and ninety days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid that many days with its payer unnamed. The mark hung where it hung, the grain lay covered and unfigured, the heard bell stood beside the told, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended; only the hand was placed down, forward and unresolved, and the river’s low note went quiet after it.

Sella Vail carried him into the Clay Yard in the afternoon with the basket held as a pot filled is held that must not be slopped, at the half-pace from every open slate. She went around seven things with a look and a wide step and no number for any of them. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the Ash ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the house calls the thaw, and swept neither. She went around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, which held no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. At the yard well she went around the trough standing bare and dark with its water called weather. Out on the worked strips she went around the field’s empty place, and in the returning line she went around the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, carrying that with her as she carried the rest and giving it no name.

Seven in a row were seven and not an account. An account wants a payer standing under it, and none stood to any of them. He did not join the dark line to the grey hairline, the rack space to the pan’s ring, the trough to the field, or the field to the hand. The hand was carried and not named, not paid, not joined to the shelf’s two gaps in the other room, to the fold’s one crust with its unnamed place beside it or to the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end. Seven went around and seven stayed seven. The space between the figure that would go over the grain and what the grain had cost had no signature on it, and the looking at it made none.

His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy days. No collector had come up the clay road on this first morning of Frost and none had gone down it; the road lay in the afternoon light with nobody on it who wanted anything from this house. He kept the objects on a tray of their own and made no story of them, and he did not lay the uncalled debt beside any other keeping, for a joining would want a payer under both and none stood.

At the yard well Ilva drew water with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. A woman with a pot asked what the month’s firings had taken. Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there; the short strip of birch bark notched along one edge did not come out, no thumb passed over it and no figure came off it. Her eyes went to the asker before her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. She went on with her water and nobody turned to look at her twice. He filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, and nine objects did not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The birch stayed in her sleeve, not read, not asked about, not joined to the reed-cutter’s cord, the knots, the six pages, the tins, the charcoal under the shelf, the missing hand or the pale line.

At the kindling Tarn had the small knife at the very lip of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man who turned back at the frozen ford with no name on him and no figure on the debt. To the yard voice that wanted the thaw early and the one that wanted it late he said Yes, house to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. He told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name — a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the warm stone in its unmarked loft pot lay cold and unreconciled where he had put it two winters ago. The loan stayed uncalled, and he did not say that a man who owns nothing has still kept one thing exactly.

At the edge of the Vail wheel-shed Jorra Vail stood with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Before first grey three or more men had gone low into the finger’s-width-open the back room, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound with a door left a finger’s width open, and nothing more. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, with no pot lost. When Sella’s sentence about the fire came short of its end, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her hand, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one iron with two bites — the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man — with no word in Grathic for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln. The burned-slate word stood at four, a direction over the boards from Slate Row and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; five askings of the row question stood with one further shaping beside them, no witness under any and no row named. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.
