Days 381–383 · Stone 22–24, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

A thing left lying has two keepers: the hand that left it and the place that held it. The place keeps no account.

— A Low Kiln saying, told over a pot set down and forgotten

Stone 22, Secondday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid down at the close of the day before, and the count stood at three hundred and eighty exact. The boy did not carry it forward by any act of choosing; a man’s attention goes where his field is, and his field was two inches of air above a barley crust and the small sounds of a house waking in its sleep. He lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, in the last full working week before the year’s last reading, and this morning had a date that no roof under that sky would speak aloud: Stone 22, the twenty-second day of the deep-winter month, six months and two hundred and one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Nothing from the nineteenth, twentieth or twenty-first of the month had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer; the eleventh arithmetic stood behind the household without teaching it anything, and the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed as instruments lie out when a case is done and before the next is called, with every gap left gap.

He took the body in the old order, because a field is checked in a sequence and a surgeon does not trust an impression that has not first been set in order. First the crust: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. It had stood two inches off for three hundred and eighty days and would stand two inches off this morning also; the distance was not a wish and had no giver. Beside it the wool lay folded once in the empty place, exactly where a hand had put it on the fifteenth, and the place kept both its fold and its emptiness the way a wound keeps the edge the dressing left and the gap the dressing did not close. Then the cold. It had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen in the dry kiln heat and lay unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

He knew a face composed by cold from a face composed by choice. The first was a mask the weather had set and the weather would take off; the second was a man’s own work and stayed when the room warmed. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, that such a patient will seem better before he is worse, and that heat returns to the extremities before it returns to the core, which is why a surgeon warms from the outside and slowly, never rubbing, never hurrying, never setting the body too near the fire. The knowledge was exact and useless at two inches of air. It had once been his trade to put a hand into a cavity and know by feel which vessel had given way; now he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the afterdrop went on doing its patient work in a body that had no surgeon in it.

The infant under the wool wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. The wanting went on under the exactness as water goes on under ice, with nowhere to go and no leave to stop. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. He had stopped looking for a verdict in the quarrel, which was not the same as having found one; the quarrel itself was the work, and this morning it began again on the same two notes as every morning since the first.

The seventh finding came before the light, as the others had. The wool lay folded once in the crust’s empty place, unchanged by the night, exactly where a hand had put it on the fifteenth. He found it. He did not place it. No second fold had come in the dark, no third crust stood against the two gaps to make a row come out even, no name had been spoken over the place and no gift had been left in it. A hand that finds is not a hand that places. Six findings from a hand that only read had added no seventh placing, and this seventh added none either: a habit wants a hand that placed twice and none stood, a way wants a third and none stood, and both want a payer where no payer stood.

He filed it on a place of its own beside the crust’s gap, the two gaps in the shelf in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the gone pan’s round ring at the rack end and the field’s empty place out on the worked strips, with a finger between it and every other kept thing so that no row came out even by his doing. It was not a joining of rooms, not a sixth held object, not a sign, not a hearing, not a mending, not a cause and not a rule. It was not laid across the eleven arithmetics standing behind the household and not laid across the four rooms with the same gap held apart. It had gathered interest all night without being spent, as dust gathers in a corner nobody sweeps, and a corner is kept by nobody’s hand all the same. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come. An account wants a hand to lay the places together and a payer standing under all of them, and no hand stood and no payer stood.

The small satisfaction of a field found as it had been left and left as it had been found cost him nothing. That was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, as she had at both ends of every day for three years. The room smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which she had not swept in two years and did not intend to sweep. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the back of the pot going down into the bed of them and lifting a small breath of heat that came up under her forearm and died into the room.

“The house will keep it,” she said to the coals, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither of the two gaps a figure. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted, the breaths of a sleeping child, the nails in a board, the strokes of a pot’s handle going round. Here they had work, and the work went to the fire first, because a fire that has been kept does not know whether anybody is sorry and only knows whether it has been fed.

Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row of the shelf’s front edge and figured neither gap, the way a woman might move her lips over a row of nails without counting them. Once she glanced at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold with the skin inside them puckered and pale. The cord of her widow’s mark hung at her throat, oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and she did not touch it now: a morning and a hearth are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, the thumb going to the fault before the weight came on the handle at either end, and left the hairline showing.

“Leave the crack showing,” she told the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.”

“If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. The proverbs had been improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had thought them when he first said them, and they went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, because a thing said to a person becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Repair stayed apart from restoration in her hands: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. What had been mended in this house had been mended twice, and the old-brass staple and the tighter wire crossed over the old fault like two men holding a gate that had once fallen and would fall again if either let go. The boy read the crossing as he would have read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, and kept the knowing to himself.

The dressed grain she turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, turning it once and lifting no figure from it and taking no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; the half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. Inside the wool the boy’s hand went toward the rim and stopped where the half-beat had stopped it, and the infant’s want of that hand on the pot handle went on without an answer. Nothing crossed to him from the hearth. No instruction had ever gone to the basket, and none went now.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and eighty-one days. Nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull and nobody named the payer. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for. The Drawing’s small flat knock out of the neighbour on the pallet stayed on its own place outside them, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place, for a hearing wants a source standing and that room had been seen from a basket and not entered. The heard bell of the Long Night stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into any of the three counts.

Nine record-first hands kept no tenth; nine stoppings kept no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the loan stayed uncalled; the burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and the well-saying at four shapings. Eleven arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of eleven, and the eleven had bought no teaching under this roof. The three directions that had come over the boards in Ash stood where they had been put, did not repeat and bought no lesson. The fourth of nothing had not come and no row was made even by his doing.

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. Three hundred and thirty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place and the three hundred and thirty-seventh was to come. The five held objects stood with the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper beside them, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair; the keeping at the fold stood beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring and the field’s empty place and was not laid across any of them. The finding that eleven are not one account sat on a place of its own too, with the finger between it and every other kept thing. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, tenth hand, tenth stopping or twelfth arithmetic was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, muffled under the winter skin as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The skin the cold had put on and the cold would take off was found where put, not made again, not spent on the coming week’s work or on a lesson under any roof; no hand had laid it, no payer stood under it and no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is is weather and not a promise.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-seven: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-one exact, six months and two hundred and one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. The twenty-second came on without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. The year’s last reading stood beyond the week and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and the Five stood beyond that. The hand lay flat on the fold. The afternoon came on without a boundary drawn, the light going thin over the roofs of Low Kiln in Sill, Grath, and Sella Vail carried the boy down from under the Vail kiln-house roof at the half-pace she had kept from every open slate for three years. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep his ear covered. The wind will have it else,” she said to the wool. She said nothing to the thing that held him, because a basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and not why.

There were nineteen steps between the door and the flagstones. She corrected the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, so that he felt the small correction before he felt the stair, and the whole of the carry was that correction, made nineteen times and never once named. He took the descent as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small things first, because the small things do not lie: the scrape of a heel on the second step, the draught that came up at the fifth and went out again at the sixth, the particular hollow the basket made against her hip when she shifted her weight at the turn. The infant under the wool wanted the correction to go the other way, toward the crust two inches off and the thread frozen at his lip, and got none of it. Both wants were true and the second was louder and neither won. That was the quarrel, and the quarrel was the work.

Out in the Clay Yard the afternoon went about its ordinary work and left its accounts where they lay. Nothing from the eleventh arithmetic standing behind the household had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and nothing crossed out here either. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the month’s dry cold, carrying the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; the round ring at the rack end where the shallow clay pan had once stood, keeping no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the field’s empty place out on the worked strips; the trough; the silt-clouded plate of ice on the flat stone by the well, set a little out of the path of feet; and, widest of all, the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The going-around there was so wide that her elbow cut the corner and the basket tilted the other way to balance it, and she righted it without looking down. Once. Not twice.

He took the wide step apart by what was there and by what was not. Seven places gone around with a look and no figure. The widest gone around by nothing measurable at all, by the turn of an elbow and the counterweight of a wicker basket, which are not measures anybody keeps. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The keeping cost a step each time and nobody entered the steps and he did not enter them either. The satisfaction of a field taken as found and left as found cost him nothing, and because it cost him nothing he knew it was not peace.

The three coverings stayed where the weather had put them, with a finger between every pair. The white frost was gone off the well’s north lip; the skin was drawn edge to edge across the trough; the plate of ice clouded with silt lay on its flat stone out of the way of feet. Weather entered nowhere and was not made a cause, a sign, a payment or a promise. Beyond the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and over it from bank to bank lay the winter skin, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this afternoon, not made again, not spent on the week’s work or on the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. No hand had laid the skin, no payer stood under it, no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is, is weather.

Ilva was at the yard well once, drawing with the pail against her knee. The rope stood stiff in the Stone cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up, and the water went dark and moving below without a sound. Clay stood pale in the crescents of her nails; the finer grey rack dust was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub it out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. The people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred.

A woman waiting her turn, with her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Stone’s firings had taken.

Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker, and her mouth opened after both, with the half-beat lying in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches, dark in the uncut grain between. It was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, nor to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, nor to the cord, nor to the knot-string, and no figure came off the wood.

“Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.”

She had answered the question put and not the one behind it. She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice. She had not looked into the basket and had no notion she had been read.

The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart: hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap between the looking and the word. He filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand, with a finger between it and Hessa Rale’s eighth. He filed no tenth, made no reader of her keeping, joined her to none and made no figure of the slate’s nearness. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it, and the notches gathered interest toward a far-off hand that was not his and had not come.

The day’s directions arrived nowhere. A man with a cracked jar in a rope sling was sent to a pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. No collector came up or went down the clay road, and no bell came on any road.

He lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, in the last full working week before the year’s last reading, and this morning had a date no roof under that sky would speak aloud: Stone 23, the twenty-third day of the deep-winter month, six months and two hundred and two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire’s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail’s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. The eleventh arithmetic stood behind the household and had bought it no teaching; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed as instruments lie out when a case is done and before the next is called, with every gap left gap. No voice had come over the boards in the night, and the three directions that had come in the dark month stood where they had been put without repeating; the fourth of nothing had not come, and no row was made even by his doing.

He took the body in the old order, because a field is checked in a sequence and an impression that has not first been set in order is an impression only. The crust came first: one stiff leathery barley crust with a brown edge on it, lying two inches from the ear, hardened another night in the dry kiln heat, its corner lifted a hair off the wool. Two inches had been its distance for three hundred and eighty-one days and two inches would be its distance this morning; the width was not a wish and had no giver standing behind it. Beside it the wool lay folded once in the empty place, where a hand had put it on the fifteenth and where no hand had been since, and the place held its fold and its emptiness together the way a wound holds both the edge the dressing left and the gap the dressing did not close. Then the cold, coming cheek, temple, the bare little joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopping there as if a line had been written out for it with no order to go farther and no order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a rewarming nobody under this roof would perform. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen stiff in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

He knew what a basket set level out of the draught, where the heat came to a body sideways, would be: the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, that such a patient will seem better before he is worse, and that heat returns to the extremities before it returns to the core, which is why a surgeon warms from the outside and slowly, never rubbing, never hurrying, never setting the body too near the fire. The knowing was exact and useless at two inches of air. It had once been his trade to put a hand into a cavity and know by feel which vessel had given way. Now he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the afterdrop went on doing its patient work in a body that had no surgeon in it.

The infant under the wool wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. The want went on under the exactness as water goes on under ice, with nowhere to go and no leave to stop. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. He had long since stopped looking for a verdict in the quarrel, which was not the same as having found one; the quarrel itself was the work, and this morning it began again on the same two notes as every morning since the first.

The eighth finding came before the light, as the others had. The wool lay folded once in the crust’s empty place, unchanged by the night, exactly where it had been put on the fifteenth. He found it. He did not place it. No second fold had come in the dark, no third crust stood against the two gaps to make a row come out even, no name had been spoken over the place, no gift left in it. A hand that finds is not a hand that places. Seven findings from a hand that only read had added no seventh placing, and this eighth added none either: a habit wants a hand that placed twice and none stood, a way wants a third and none stood, and both want a payer where no payer stood.

He filed it on a place of its own beside the crust’s gap, the two gaps in the shelf in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the gone pan’s round ring at the rack end and the field’s empty place out on the worked strips, with a finger between it and every other kept thing so that no row came out even by his doing. It was not a joining of rooms, not a tenth held object, not a sign, not a hearing, not a mending, not a cause and not a rule. It was not joined to the Ash 5 joining or to the Ash 17 or Ash 23 direction, each standing on its own place, nor to any fourth that had not yet stood; it was not laid across the eleven arithmetics standing behind the household or the four rooms with the same gap held apart. It had gathered interest all night without being spent, as dust gathers in a corner nobody sweeps, and a corner is kept by nobody’s hand all the same. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come. An account wants a hand to lay the places together and a payer standing under all of them, and no hand stood and no payer stood.

The small satisfaction of a field found as it had been left and left as it had been found cost him nothing. That was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket, as she had at both ends of every day for three years. The room carried woodsmoke and wet wool, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which no broom had crossed in two years and which she did not intend to cross now. She told the coals it was the thaw, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the pot’s back going down into the bed and bringing up a small breath of heat that came under her forearm and died into the room.

“The house will keep it,” she said to the coals, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment on a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps. Her hands were never still; with nothing to do they counted breaths, nails, the strokes of a handle going round. Here the work went to the fire first, for a fire that has been kept does not know whether anyone is sorry, only whether it has been fed.

Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row of the shelf’s edge and gave neither gap a number, the way a woman might move her lips along a row of nails without counting them. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze, and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air — two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside puckered and pale. The cord of her widow’s mark hung oiled at her throat where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and she did not touch it now: a morning and a hearth are no openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, the thumb reaching the fault before the weight came on the handle, and left the hairline where it was.

“Leave the crack showing,” she told the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.”

“If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. The proverbs had been improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had thought them when he first said them; they went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, because a thing said to a person becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Repair stayed apart from restoration in her hands. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and the old-brass staple and the tighter wire crossed over the old fault like two men holding a gate that had fallen once and would fall again if either let go. The boy read the crossing as he would have read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen and kept the knowing to himself.

The dressed grain she turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, turning it once, lifting no figure and taking no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed — the half-beat the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. Inside the wool his hand went toward the rim and stopped where the half-beat had stopped it. The infant’s want of that hand on the pot handle went on without an answer. Nothing crossed to him from the hearth; no instruction had ever gone to the basket, and none went now.

Before noon Sella carried the boy down the nineteen steps and set the basket level out of the draught at the edge of the Clay Yard, where the upper stretch of the clay road showed between the walls. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. From the low angle of the wicker the road was a band of thin winter light and pale scurf, and a person going up it was seen first as a stride and then as a coat.

Hessa Rale came up that road toward Greybank with Berr Clay’s narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned toward her sleeve. The thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was — not concealment, but the manner of carrying a slate so the face of it is not rubbed and no goer-by takes a figure off it without a hand to put there. The second strap was let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist came first, her hand arriving at the slate before her eyes arrived at the yard, and no figure came off the face of it.

“What do you carry?” a woman by the path asked, though the question she meant was another one.

“I don’t read it. I carry it,” Hessa said. “There’s a difference, and the road doesn’t care which.”

The woman wanted more. “Then who set the figure on it?”

Hessa settled the second strap again, not for the woman and not for the road, with the small flat correction of a person settling a burden that has become part of the walk. “Ask the road. It knows the way and I know the weight. That’s the whole of what either of us knows.”

She read nothing aloud, gave no figure and did not look into the basket, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. Nothing in her road wore a name, a date or a figure for the boy to enter. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the women and her eyes went to the women before her mouth opened, and that order was the whole of what he had to go on.

The boy took the turning of the writing toward herself apart as a surgeon takes a field apart and filed it on a place of its own beside the Ash 6 sighting, with a finger between. A runner going up with the writing turned to her own sleeve is not a voice standing in a doorway and not a saying fulfilled. He made no fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying, no reader of her, no witness standing and no payment, and joined her neither to Ilva’s strip in its sleeve nor to Berr’s twice-wiped thumb, nor to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk’s inward slate nor to any other private keeping. Her eighth stayed eight; he filed no tenth hand; eleven arithmetics going up the road behind her in a locked box whose key did not come back down did not make her a reader of what she carried. She was a hand and stayed a hand, and the figure went up the road unread and would come down at somebody else’s hour.

Three hundred and thirty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place and the three hundred and thirty-ninth was to come. The five held objects stood with the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper beside them, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair; the keeping at the fold stood beside the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the gone pan’s round ring and the field’s empty place and was not laid across any of them. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, tenth hand, tenth stopping or twelfth arithmetic was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Nothing she carried stood up here and called itself an account.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, muffled under the winter skin as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The skin the cold had put on and the cold would take off was found where put, not made again, not spent on the week’s work or on a lesson under any roof; no hand had laid it, no payer stood under it and no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is is weather and not a promise.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-nine: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-two exact, six months and two hundred and two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. The twenty-third came on without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. The year’s last reading stood beyond the week and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and the Five stood beyond that. The hand lay flat on the fold. The runner was gone up the clay road with the writing turned to her own sleeve. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.

The afternoon came on as a field taken at rest, and a field taken at rest is read by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. The boy was carried down the nineteen steps at the half-pace Sella Vail keeps from every open slate, the tilt corrected at each turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back and never to the basket. She set the basket level out of the draught at the edge of the Clay Yard, where the heat would come to it sideways, and went about her work without a look toward it. No runner came up the clay road that afternoon; no collector went up it or down it; there was no market, no Cross and no bell. Nothing from the eleventh reading, nothing from the keeping at the fold, nothing from the road taken before noon crossed into the afternoon as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. A house keeps its days in loaves and glances and in the space where a loaf has stood; a yard keeps its work in hands and weights and weather; and the two keepings did not meet and were not one, because a figure raised between them would want a payer standing under it and none stood.

The boards went on behind him the way boards go on, talk arriving along their length and going off it without a taker. A woman set a pot down and told the pot what it had cost rather than telling anybody standing near. Two men came up off the clay road with a number held shut between them and let it out only as far as the weather. The price of barley went over the boards on the same breath as everything else, and after the price of barley came the weather, and after the weather came nothing at all. It was the grammar of the district and had been for as long as anyone under the fold could remember: nothing in it required a taker, and nothing in it supplied one. The boy took it in as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the part that can be checked first, and left the rest lying where it lay.

The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey hairline beside it, the hairline called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had crossed in two years. Under the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody. At the rack end the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept its emptiness without a scrape, a crumb, a print, a chip or a word. Out on the worked strips the field’s empty place lay where it had lain since the hands came in and one hand did not come back with the rest. The trough held its skin, and the silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone a little out of the path of feet. Widest of all was the returning-line place, gone around so wide that the elbow cut the corner and the basket tilted the other way to balance it, righted without looking down. Once and not twice. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it and none stood, so the keeping cost one step and no more and nobody entered the step and he did not enter it either.

The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair: the white frost gone off the north lip of the yard well, the skin drawn edge to edge across the trough, the clouded plate lifted up and set where no foot would go. The river kept water over water below them, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and over both, bank to bank since the afternoon of Stone 1, lay the winter skin, found where put and not made again, not spent on the week’s work or the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. No hand had laid the skin, no payer stood under it, no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is is weather and not a promise. He kept the covering on its own place beside the note and put a finger between the two so that no row came out even by his doing.

It was into this ordinary going-on, with the boards already talking and the yard going around its places, that the word came.

It came up from the direction of Slate Row, which the yard spoke of as a direction and not as a place anybody meant to go that afternoon. There was no owner standing under it. It went along the boards where the neighbours stood with their baskets, spoken over the boards and not into any basket, on the same breath as the price of barley and the weather behind that. A voice with nobody to it said that a room had been shut against the cold, and that a slate had been burned so a count would not go up, and that the cold and the count had gone out together through the same gap. It named no room. It put no hand at any flame. It gave no figure, no date, and no name to the man it would have been about, and there was nobody standing in any doorway to be asked. It was a direction and not a visit. It went on to the price of barley as a man goes on to the price of barley when he has said something else entirely, and then to the weather, and then it went off up the yard and was gone while the work went on around it. Nobody leaned in to take the answer down. Nobody turned twice.

The boy opened it as a surgeon opens a wound, by what was there and by what was not. What was there: a room shut, a slate burned, a count kept down, a gap that let cold and figure out by the same opening. What was not: a witness standing somewhere and able to be asked, a room named, a hand at the flame, a date, a figure, a payer, a name under it. He laid what was there beside what was not and found no seam between them that any clerk could close. The voice had arrived from a quarter league off and had not lowered itself for the child in the basket; a word spoken over a thing is not a word spoken to it. It had said a shape of things and not a fact about them. He set the shape on a place of its own beside the Ash 5 joining of the burned-slate word to the counting room shut in the war, and beside the Ash 17 direction from Slate Row, and beside the Ash 23 direction one shape further, and put a finger between it and each of the three, so that the four should not touch and make a row come out even.

It was a fourth direction and he would not let it stand up as anything more. It was expressly not a sixth telling of the burned slate, which stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four. It was not a third telling of the shut room, which stood at two. It was not a fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying, which stood at four, and not a sixth asking of the row, which stood at five with no witness. It was not a hearing of the full note; the six hearings stood beside and never among and the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for. It was not a seventh of anything, not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a reader, not a joining to the promise neither given nor withdrawn, and not a joining to the keeping at the fold, which lay where it had been put and had gathered no hand to place it a second time. It was not a payment and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this. He made nothing of its being there and looked for no fifth beside it, because a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

The shape went on gathering interest as dust gathers in a corner nobody sweeps, and a corner is kept by nobody’s hand all the same. It gathered toward the far-off hand that would one day lay the district’s private keepings together and call the joining an account; that hand was not his and had not come. An account wants a hand to lay the places together and a payer standing under all of them, and no hand stood and no payer stood. The yard’s barley price went on over the boards as though nothing had been said into it, which was the truth of the matter, and the boards lay where they had laid, four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not.

His chest had been tight since the dark. The fourth direction over the boards called no loan and supplied no tenth stopping, and the boy did not carry it down to the sill.

Another with a cracked jar in a rope sling asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. To each of them Tarn said, “Yes, house,” and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. No sixth asking of the row came over the boards that afternoon, no tenth record-first hand stood up in the empty place, and the fourth direction stood where it had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said to it, and told a pot going past on a bench, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He grew quieter as the afternoon went on, the way he did when a direction went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order from anybody. Sella gave the fire its instruction and Tarn gave the broom its, and the two went on as if the quiet were weather they had agreed to work in. The boy did not take the quiet for an answer. It had been found where put on the mornings and at the goings of the light of Stone 22, 23 and 24 — a seventh, an eighth and a ninth finding — and nine findings from a hand that only read had added no ninth placing to the first. A second placing would make a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stands. No name had been spoken over the place, no third crust stood against the two gaps to make a row come out even, and the far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come.

Nine record-first hands kept no tenth; nine stoppings kept no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the burned-slate word stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and expressly no sixth, the well-saying at four shapings with no fifth. Four rooms kept the same gap in their count — the birth room in House Silt, the Vail kiln-house, the room where the slate was read on market-day and the Drawing house seen two doors down — held apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account, an account wanting a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand standing and no such payer. The fourth direction of Stone 23 stood on its own place beside the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 and Ash 23 directions, with a finger between every pair. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand, tenth stopping or twelfth arithmetic was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The finding that eleven are not one account sat on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other kept thing, and the keeping at the fold and the fourth direction sat beside all of these and were not laid across any of them.

Eleven arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, Stone’s first and Stone’s second. The figure had been read and was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between, and no doctrine had come out of eleven. Their eleven true subtractions rode locked in Berr Clay’s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down and the boy having no key. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for. The Drawing’s small flat knock out of the neighbour on the pallet stayed on its own place outside them, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place, for a hearing wants a source standing and that room had been seen from a basket and not entered. The heard bell of the Long Night stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into any of the counts.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. The quarrel was the work and he had stopped looking for a verdict in it, which was not the same as having found one. Stone kept its twenty-second, twenty-third and twenty-fourth days without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-three exact, six months and two hundred and three days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire’s fifth unpaid the same number and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. The wool lay folded once in the crust’s empty place beside the crust gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner a hair off the wool, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place, and the thread was frozen and unwiped. Eleven arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure with no signature on the space and no doctrine out of eleven, and the keeping at the fold was not one of them and had not been laid across any of them. The Five stood beyond, and the year’s last reading stood six mornings beyond the week and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its low note under its winter skin. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it, as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and forty-two: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction nor the keeping at the fold. The surgeon’s exactness was not comfort. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three. The hand lay flat on the fold with the crust’s corner standing a hair off the wool, and the count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.

The Chronicle of Chapter 110

Three days the house under the Vail roof kept its shape, and the boy kept a count no roof could read. Stone twenty-two, twenty-three and twenty-four came without a boundary drawn, the last full working week of the year. Each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: barley crust two inches from the ear, corner a hair off the wool; wool folded in the empty place where a hand put it on the fifteenth; thread frozen and unwiped; cold stopping at cheek, temple and bare wrist-joint; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true, the second louder, neither winning; the quarrel was the work.

On the twenty-second the fold was found where put a seventh time and still not placed. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, said the house would keep it where another woman would say I, carried him down the nineteen steps, and set the pot rim two inches beyond his reach with a half-beat. Ilva at the well answered: Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account — ninth record-first hand, no tenth. Tarn told the wood Six counted. The next I don’t count; Jorra’s bent finger went into the cold damper ahead of an unfinished sentence. Answers 337 and 338; the count stood at 381.

On the twenty-third the fold was found an eighth time. Hessa Rale went up the clay road before noon with Berr Clay’s narrow slate edge-on and the writing turned to her own sleeve, telling the woman who asked that she carried the slate and did not read it. In the afternoon a voice with nobody standing under it came over the boards from Slate Row: a room shut against the cold, a slate burned so a count would not go up, the cold and the count gone out through the same gap. The boy opened it as a wound and filed it beside the older joinings — a fourth direction, not a sixth telling, not a witness, not a rule — gathering toward a hand far off and not his. Answers 339 and 340; the count stood at 382.

On the twenty-fourth the fold was found a ninth time, and nine findings added no ninth placing. The yard went around its seven places, widest around the returning-line place, once and not twice; Tarn’s ear-sentence stopped at the calling word, the seventh year at its end; Jorra’s finger came out of the iron without the extra holding. Answers 341 and 342; the count stood at 383 exact, six months and two hundred and three days told by nobody, the fire’s fifth unpaid and Garl’s name unspoken the same number. The surgeon’s satisfaction in a field taken as found cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace.
