— Two sayings of the Clay Yard, told in the second firing of Smoke

Smoke 4, Fourthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay against the fold where it had come down on the river’s next low note, and the boy set the coordination down the way a man lays a tool on a bench at the end of work and leaves it where the next hand will look for it. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. The sixtieth answer had crossed two rhythms in one inch of air across a wall that does not touch, and now the inch was finished and the hand was only a hand again, small, soft-nailed, outside the wool, with the fold at the ear and one crust lying two inches from it. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift that crust to his mouth; both were true, and neither stood up to call itself a sign. Under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln one room kept fire and the other kept river, and neither stopped for the ear between them. The file lay open on the towel where he had left it, exact, complete and worth nothing, six hearings standing beside the full note and never among, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. There was no towel, no instruments and no case, only the order of the laying, and that was the whole of the discipline: the sharp thing by the sharp thing, the blunt by the blunt, every edge where a hand would look for it first.

He was two hundred and forty-three days old and six months and sixty-three days, and nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and forty-two to two hundred and forty-three with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. Smoke went on the way a firing goes, without a boundary drawn across it, no stroke to mark the hour, neither of the district’s two names for the months set right or left wrong in the telling. There was no market on a Fourthday and no Cross and no bell. Smoke 10 stood six mornings ahead, the first Account-day and market of the second firing, and it stood beyond this day and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

Hot clay came first under the roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw. The day’s first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. He took the smells in the order they arrived and the sound in the order it did not, the way he had once taken a room in the dark: what was under the hand first, what was behind it second, and what the ear wanted to hear last. A man who had learned to find the bleeder before the bleed knew that the ear lies and the hand does not, and he had learned it here again in a house where the ear was told nothing and the hand was given everything it could not use.

In the small hours the coals kept themselves and no breathing number stood anywhere in the house. The fire’s keeper had gone to her own bed before the dark settled and left the fire to its four low pulls, and there was no fire-sitter over the coals and no count kept aloud under this roof. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, awake and silent, with the fold at his ear and the room that does not touch his keeping its own quiet on the other side of the wall. The oiled paper at the window hung slack in its frame in the dry heat and lifted for nothing, and the little light there was came from the banked coals and went no further than the hand. He borrowed no rhythm from the room that does not touch his; he had not borrowed one on any of the three days behind this one, and he did not borrow one now.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-three days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. Three instruments that will not read against one another are still three instruments, and an instrument is a thing you use, not a thing you believe. The boards were overhead and the river was through the door and the fire was under the floor, and a man could stand under the three of them all his days without their ever adding up to a voice.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from that ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to lie down there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, and would stay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He had watched men come up out of the cold and go worse before they went better, and knew the shape of it: the blood leaving the hands for the middle, the shaking, the slow mind, the warmth that must be put in from the outside and slowly, and the fact that nobody under this roof would put it in. A cold patient cannot clot and the face of severe cold wears the look of a smile; he had seen both, and had seen what comes of rubbing a man and setting him too near a fire. Here there was no fire to set him near and no hand to do the rubbing, and the warming was put off onto a far side of the house where nobody stood. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child’s hunger keeps no hours at all and the adult’s register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon’s register took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

The dressed grain lay under its fold of wool near the hearth, covered and unfigured. In the morning she had lifted it for damp, felt it to the knuckle, spread it thin on the flat stone to finish, gathered it back by the handful and set the fold flat; it had changed state and gained no figure, and now it lay as it lay, counted in glances and handfuls and by the heft of an arm that knew the weight and not the cost. A muscle remembers a load and not what the load was worth; he had spent his life learning to tell the two apart and had no word here in which to enter either. He had dressed a hundred wounds and knew the difference between a fold laid flat to keep and a fold laid flat to hide, and this was the first kind and was entered nowhere. The figure that had gone over this grain in the square at Grain’s last market was not a figure this room had entered, and the two were left apart and not averaged. No slate came near the fold and no figure was laid across any of the absences.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the wheel-seat in the shed with its twenty-year hollow, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned the corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, both left unswept. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-three days. No collector had come up or gone down the clay road for the tile debt on Smoke 1, 2 or 3 and none had gone down now on Smoke 4; the debt circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to the basket — leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and went still to a pot with no crack in it, over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would. A line left where it is is still a line kept.

The end of the sentence arrived at the pot. The household straightened around his silence without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. He had held the iron first on Day 161 and on the days after, and this was the holding remembered and not a change made again, paired with no other fraction and not joined to the damper fraction of Garl’s year or the held beat under the shelf or the half-beat on the flagstones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in Grathic for both mercies. He read it as an object worth nothing and joined it to no kiln. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light the boy turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments and no case, only the order of the laying, sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt with every edge where a hand would look for it first; the discipline was the whole of it, and a discipline is a thing a hand uses, not a thing a man believes.

Six hearings stood beside the full note and never among, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings. The fourth burned-slate telling stood on its own place and the two tellings of the room shut in the war stood at two with no third. The told bell lay beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid, and Grain’s third subtraction stayed locked in Berr Clay’s box, going up and down the clay road and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the three held changes — the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 held beat, the Tallow 22 half-check — lay on their own places with the loft scrape, the pale line and the held inch beside them, six places and no joining across any of them.

The ninth record-first hand-shape lay on a place of its own with a finger between it and the eighth and fingers between it and every other private keeping. Nine record-first hands were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. What the notches held was a count of firings, plain as a firing is plain, kept in wood because a slate would want a figure put on it; nothing on the stick waited to be read and no figure came off it that a clerk could enter. He did not read it, had not asked the woman about it and had not joined the order of reaching to the cords, the knots, the pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line. The seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth and no name spoken over either. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the pan’s gone ring lay on its own tray; the returning-line place had been gone around with a look and no number; and the ownerless question stood on its seventh place with four askings and no witness standing. He drew no finger between the crust’s gap, the shelf’s two gaps, the empty place in the field and the returning-line place, and he made no fourth likeness a cause. Nothing in the file had moved, and nothing in it had been completed to make the row come out even.

Then he put the question to his hand the sixth way of the chapter.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger’s-width door. In the room that does not touch his, Sella’s count had come to its end: small intake, hold, letting go, and the little silence standing after it that she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear. He took the whole of it — the intake and the hold and the letting-go, and the silence after the count had ended — and crossed it with the river’s next low note in the inch and nowhere else, across a wall that does not touch. Two rhythms crossed in the inch, one borrowed from the other side and one kept on this, and the crossing was remembered and not a change made again. He did not make a longer reach of it or enter a miss as a finding, did not hear a seventh of the full note, did not stand a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, did not pay, did not hold a change, did not make a key, a power or a rule. Sixty-six answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had crossed a trauma bay at a run with a clamp in his fist, and now two inches of air lay between his hand and a crust he could not lift to his mouth; the two facts stood on one tray and neither was promoted into a finding. He filed the answer with no payer named and went back to the crust, which was where he had been all afternoon and where he would be at the close of it.

The hand came down on the river’s next low note and lay against the fold like a tool put away. The file lay open on the towel, exact, complete and worth nothing; under the floor the fire stopped at four, and the silence where the fifth should have stood stood where it stood. Two hundred and forty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and sixty-five days were told by nobody; the fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow’s mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk on Smoke 4, 5 or 6, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went out without a boundary drawn in the telling and neither of the district’s two names for the months was set right or left wrong; there was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Sixthday, and Smoke 10 (Day 249), the first Account-day and market of the second firing, stood beyond the close and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The count went forward into Smoke 7, a day that had not yet earned its heading.

The Chronicle of Chapter 64

Three days came under the Vail roof and went without a boundary drawn. He came into his two hundred and forty-third day, then the two after, and nobody said any number over him. He kept the count like an instrument: exact, complete, unspent, worth nothing at two inches of air. Fire under the floor missed its fifth; river through the door went over water; the boards held four nails, four and a knot. He kept them on three trays; agreeing would want a payer and none stood to any.

One leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third. The thread froze unwiped; cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three. He knew the shape of a man going cold and wanted the crust. He kept the tray.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, said The house will keep it, and counted toward no opening. The grain came out from under wool for damp, was spread thin and gathered by the handful; it changed state and gained no figure. Cost-questions went to pot and weather; a man’s number went back into his sleeve. The widow’s mark hung where it hung, for the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour.

At the kindling Tarn put six where the house could check and the seventh where it could not. The ear-sentence reached the calling word and stopped; nine stoppings stood, no collector on the clay road. Jorra parted dust from slip and withheld the note he knew three turns ahead; the burned slate stood at four, the shut-room tellings at two. Garl was kept in objects, his name unspoken two hundred and forty-five days.

At the yard well on the fifth morning Ilva of the firing work passed a thumb-nail across notched birch bark before her eyes went to the woman who asked and before her mouth answered: Five to this morning. The boy took the order of reaching and nothing else: he set a ninth record-first hand beside the eighth, a finger between, and would not let nine likenesses call themselves a rule. Next morning a wrong wind carried the sweet edge of glaze over the boards, touched the fold and went out on the wind that brought it; one pot saying stood again, and no fourth likeness was made a cause.

Six times he put the question to his hand, the sixty-first through the sixty-sixth, and filed each on the sixth place: down at once, down before the letting-go, held to the river’s interval, two rhythms crossed in the inch, one rhythm held through Sella’s silence, crossed again. None was a reach, a hearing or a power. At the close the hand lay against the fold like a tool put away and the file lay open, exact and worth nothing; the count went forward into a day unearned.
