What the Wood Did Not Say

Days 438–440 · Silt 14–16, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

The wood keeps the word it was told and the word it was not; the corner keeps its grey after the broom has gone the other way. A day nobody numbers is a day kept all the same.

— A saying of the Low Kiln yard, told over the kindling in Silt

— Day 438 —

Silt 14, Y.H. 814, Fourthday

The small hours of the fourteenth day came down under the Vail roof without a boundary drawn, the way Ash comes down in a room where no broom has gone — not in a fall but in a settling, finer along the skirting than in the middle of the floor, finding the crack in the boards before it found the warmth. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where the going of the light had left it, on answer four hundred and fifty, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and thirty-seven exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-seven days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under Ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and thirty-seven days; Garl’s name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-seven, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road, four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature set on the space; all four rode locked in Berr Clay’s box behind the twelve of Y.H. 813, with the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. None of it had crossed under the roof in the night as sign or lesson, mending or cause, payment or payer. The second asking of the twelfth stood on a place of its own, and the face at the yard’s edge of the thirteenth stood on another, with a finger between and no word spent on either, for a morning that had earned only its fourteenth did not tell them again. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept water over water under the winter skin, a low thing over a lower thing, ending on its own finishing; nobody in the room gave the softness at one edge a name.

He took the body in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it weeks ago and would not try now, and he could not lift it, and could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and goings of the light behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose, and could not ask for it; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

Three things found as they had been left, and none the better for the finding. The surgeon read them in their order and put no figure on them. The infant under the same skin wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, wanted the thread wiped from his lip, wanted a hand on the handle of the pot that would bring a cup within reach. The surgeon knew all three wants were useless and filed them without verdict; the infant wanted them all the same. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a settlement, because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the small one outside the wool that could not close two inches of air. He had once stood at the head of a table and laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted, and had known the difference between a finding and a want without having to think which was which. That was another room and another pair of hands. It paid nothing here. The wanting was not a weakness in the register and the register was not a denial of the wanting; they were two entries on the same page, and neither had been signed.

First grey came up the clay road and into the yard without asking, and Sella Vail came to the basket with the dark still in her sleeves. She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool, the river wind, the pot and the fire and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was, or what the hour was, or told to be still. A basket carried down is a pot carried down and set where the heat comes to it, and no woman explains the hour to a pot. The Clay Yard was wet ash and old brick dust under the thin light, with barley wall to wall and no number on it, and she went around what she went around with a look and a wide step and no count; a fourth morning of Silt was a fourth morning of Silt and not a reading, and nothing from the tenth had come down the road into it.

Under the roof she went to the hearth before the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw, in the voice another woman would have used to a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone that went into the boy’s ear ahead of the warmth. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps; they were gaps and wanted no figure put under them, for a figured gap wants a payer standing in it and none stood. Her finger went along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count and wanted no answer. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand — two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold — closed on air and went back to the pot.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side. Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, she said to the pot. If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; the pot was mended and was not made whole, and the crack was left where a man could see it had been handled. At the rack end she drew the wool over the dressed grain with the flat of her palm, turning a corner of it for damp with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and gave it no figure and no handful, though a fist of barley weighed in the hand knows what it weighs. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; the half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest, and she did not measure it and did not name it.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung. She touched it before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open; a Fourthday morning was not an opening, and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its fourteenth. Under the Vail corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years, unswept and called weather. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping at the fold was found where put and stayed a finding. Nothing under the roof had been mended by anything that had stood on its own place through the night, and nothing had been made a lesson of it.

A man who owns nothing knows where every small thing is; a man who has counted six years knows what a seventh is worth when nobody comes to call it. The boy took the stopping apart by the place of each part — what was there, what was not, and no payer standing under either — and filed the silence on a place of its own beside Garl’s unforged sentence and the older stopped sentence. It was an object and not a doctrine; a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood. He did not put a tomorrow into it.

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her unfinished sentence. Nothing from the twelfth’s asking, the thirteenth’s face or the tenth’s reading had come under the shed as sign, mending, cause, payment or payer, and the boy made no joining of what he saw.

She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, a Sixthday is not an opening, and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration; a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. Garl Vail’s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf, at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping at the fold in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry nights with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody under this roof would perform. He could not speak a word of Grathic and had no way to ask for any of the three things the body wanted, and the wanting was complete and therefore useless. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and forty days; nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.
