Days 435–437 · Silt 11–13, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

— Day 435 —

Silt 11, Y.H. 814, Firstday

The small hours of the eleventh day came down under the Vail roof the way silt comes down through a standing well — not in a fall but in a settling, laying itself first along the skirting and lying longest where nothing had stirred it, finding the thin places in the boards before it found the middle of the room. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where the going of the light had left it, on answer four hundred and forty-four, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and thirty-four exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-four days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and thirty-four days; Garl’s name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-four, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road, four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid; all four rode locked in Berr Clay’s box with the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. None of it had crossed under the roof in the night as sign or lesson, mending or cause, payment or payer. An eleventh morning was only an eleventh morning. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept the low note of water over water under the winter skin, ending on its own finishing, and nobody in the room gave the softness at one edge a name.

The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. He took the body in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it six nights ago and would not try now. He could not lift it. He could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put on the mornings and goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose, and could not ask for it; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

Three things found as they had been left, and none the better for the finding. The surgeon read them in their order and put no figure on them. The infant under the same skin wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, wanted the thread wiped from his lip, wanted a hand on the handle of the pot that would bring a cup within reach. The surgeon knew all three wants were useless and filed them without verdict; the infant wanted them all the same. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a settlement, because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the small one outside the wool that could not close two inches of air. He had once stood at the head of a table and laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted, and had known the difference between a finding and a want without having to think which was which. That was another room and another pair of hands. It paid nothing here. The wanting was not a weakness in the register and the register was not a denial of the wanting; they were two entries on the same page, and neither had been signed.

First grey came the way a lamp is turned down rather than lit, a thinning of the dark that showed the shape of things before it showed their colour. Sella Vail came to him and carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. She did not tell the basket what the day was and did not tell it to be still. A basket carried down is a pot carried down and set where the heat comes to it, and no woman instructs the pot about the hour. She set him under the roof and not in the loft, at the low angle of the shelf where the sideways heat found him, and went to the hearth before the basket.

She told the coals it was the thaw, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and did not tell it to the basket. She turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone that had done this work before and expected to do it again. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and moved her lips on a two-gapped row that was neither a prayer nor a count and wanted no answer. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand — two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold — closed on air and went back to the pot.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side. Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled, she said to the pot; If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure. At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, lifted each piece for damp, turned it, laid it back where it lay and drew the wool over with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

The widow’s mark hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened the kiln mouth and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A morning, a shelf, a pot, a hearth and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and a Firstday was not made an eighth by the reading that had gone up the road on the tenth. Under the Vail corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years, and the boy lay with his eyes at the level of it and looked at it once and not twice.

Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones with the morning’s quiet already worked into him. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence, with no name under the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt circling against a firing of tiles that would never come. The stack kept its own arithmetic of four, four and a space, and the space was not a fifth and was not filled. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach; the coming away was the whole of the checking. Six counted. The next I don’t count, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, cutting and counting and spending nothing. The sentence was found where put and not made a habit, a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; a second would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stands.

The other sentence, about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it, came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered stopping that was not made a tenth. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said Yes, house to the fire and went on over the flagstones without turning his head toward the landing. A man who owns nothing knows where every small thing is; a man who has counted six years knows what a seventh is worth when nobody comes to call it. The boy took the stopping apart by the place of each part — what was there, what was not, and no payer standing under either — and filed the silence on a place of its own. It was an object and not a doctrine; a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood.

At the Vail wheel-shed edge Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and got no name from him and no word that the room was cold, while the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her unfinished sentence. One bite of the iron held and one took; there was no Grathic word covering both mercies. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. The household straightened around the waiting without a word from anybody. The boy lay under the shelf and kept he’s not Vail sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years; the promise to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn against the burned slate standing at five, and the two quiet men under one roof kept their places on trays of their own, their silences maintenance and not a compact. Nothing from the reading of the tenth had come under the shed as sign, mending, cause, payment or payer, and the boy made no joining of what he saw.

The morning went on at the work of a morning. Sella’s hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbers, and came back to the hairline at the wire. The barley lay wall to wall without a number. Tarn’s seven stood where the house could check and could not. Jorra’s dust stayed white in a fist-crease that was not opened twice. Each thing kept its own place with a finger between it and the next, and the boy added none of them together, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up his answer, and the answer is usually the one he wanted before he cut. He kept four hundred and thirty-five exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-five days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire’s fifth went unpaid and Garl’s name went unspoken the same number, and the two counts stood side by side without becoming one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth.

Answer four hundred and forty-five was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment. He had learned to read a room from a low angle in the other life, from the floor beside a gurney where the sheeting hid everything but the hands; a face can lie for a whole shift and the hand at the edge of the field cannot. He took the small sounds first because they do not lie. A pail came down on the flat stone by the well with a flat knock, the stone and the wood and the hand that set it down, and no fourth thing standing behind to be asked. Barley lay wall to wall along the rack without a number on it. A loaf went a finger’s width along a board and stopped, with nobody calling the stopping a figure. He took each apart by the place of each part: the fist that came down, the eye that followed, the mouth that did not open, the half-beat lying in the gap between the setting-down and the stopping. The gap was a gap. He gave it no intention, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. A surgeon who writes the name of a cause over a gap has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. The pail’s knock was one sound and then no sound; it did not go on speaking after the hand left it. The rope’s rasp ended with the settling of the knot. The loaf stopped a finger’s width from where a thumb had put it and stayed stopped until another hand moved it. He had worked in a room where a nurse said a number twice and the chart carried it once, and he had learned to ask which of the two was the finding and which the telling. Here there was no chart and no nurse; there was a hand, a knock, an eye that went to the pail and a mouth that stayed shut, and the half-beat between, and he let the half-beat stand as a half-beat and wrote nothing over it.

That was one room and one set of hands, and it paid nothing here. The boy’s hand outside the wool lay where the fold had put it and could not close two inches of air. Neither bought the other, and the file went on with the case open.

On the thirteenth a man from Slate Row stood at the yard’s edge with loose hands and a patient face. Nobody asked what he wanted and he said nothing. The boy filed the face on a place of its own.

Six answers stood on the sixth place, two to each day and not one a doctrine. The count was four hundred and thirty-seven exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-seven days told by nobody. The river kept its low note under its winter skin; the hand came up an inch, took a small intake, held the interval and came down flat on the fold. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust.
