Days 228–230 · Grain 19–21, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

— Day 228 —

Grain 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

Hot clay came first under the Vail kiln-house roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf with the hand outside the wool lying down again against the fold. It had come down in the small hours just gone on the river’s next low note, after being carried through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of a breath counted in a room that does not touch this one, kept through the little silence after that count had ended; two rhythms held against each other across a wall, remembered, not a change made again. Then the hand lay down. He set the coordination down where a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of the work — not watched, not named, not asked to stand up and say what it was. It was not a sign, not a growth, not a reaching, not a gift, not a power. The room that warmed him and the room he could not enter kept their old wall between them, and the fire went on in the one and the river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

He was two hundred and twenty-eight days old and six months and forty-eight days, and nobody said either number in his hearing or over his head; a house that kept its days in loaves and glances and the space at a row’s end had no use for a figure belonging to a child it had taken in without a slate. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and twenty-seven to two hundred and twenty-eight with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. The day’s first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood, with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-eight days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. An empty place kept empty is a finding; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and he had set that down on other days without its needing to be said again. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and he knew that a vessel which has cooled cannot clot and must not be rubbed too fast; neither knowing bought him the use of his own hands. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in a body of two hundred and twenty-eight days the wanting was the louder. He could lift his chin two inches and lift the crust two inches and put the one to the other, and he could do neither. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now the whole reach of him was two inches of air and the fold’s warm, yeasty smell.

With the light he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel — no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline — and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through a finger’s width of door, the district’s slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. Each sat on its own square of the towel with its object beside it — the cup’s thin lip, the pot’s side under the knuckle, the slate’s upper line, the small flat sound out of a body lifted wet — and no object had grown a handle that a child could close two inches on. No seventh had come in two hundred and twenty-eight days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand’s breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which wrong-wind voices had let stand again on the last morning. None had been promoted into the hearings, no cause stood up, no lesson was made, no name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, no pot had been lost in this yard.

The burned-slate word stood on a place of its own with a hand between the three sayings and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket on Grain 10 and still not the figure paid, with the true subtraction locked in Berr Clay’s box going up toward High Kiln and Hessa Rale’s narrow slate gone up unread toward Greybank, the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called. Dessa’s cost stood unentered and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere. Grain 20 stood two mornings ahead with the second Account-day and the market and was not brought early into a day that had its own work. The nine stoppings stood where they had stood, on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places — the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 beat beneath the shelf, the Tallow 22 half-check on the flagstones — with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the carried man’s pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. No payer was named to any of them, and nothing stood up out of them to call itself a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a key, a power or a rule.

He put the question to his hand the first way of this chapter. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger’s-width door and stayed a moment, then came down at once under conditions that had sometimes answered — a plain coming-down, neither held past the intake nor carried through the counted breath, only the inch and the letting-go. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named and did not spend it and did not explain it. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in a small hand and both are true and neither is a sign; once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to the pot handle or lift a crust to his mouth or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. Thirty-one answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power. The hand lay down again against the fold.

Sella Vail came to the fire before the basket. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the broom and the running water and the fire, and to no basket: Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind. It was the same instruction she had given on the first morning and had not varied in two hundred and twenty-eight days, and she said the house where another woman would have said I. At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row the way a woman moves her lips over the days of a week without saying them. The row stood with two gaps: the old space at the end and the palm’s breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word. She gave nobody a figure. When her hands had nothing to do they counted: spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child, and here the row’s two gaps took the place of spoons. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf’s front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman’s back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

The dressed grain lay under its covering near the hearth, dressed and stored, and the work of it was finished three days gone. It had come off the strips as cut stalks, had gone under wool as a bundle, had been beaten out on the flagstones and winnowed on the river wind, had lain thin to finish drying, and now lay covered again with the fold set flat; three changes of state and no figure over it anywhere. He took it apart in the order he took everything within two paces: what it was, grain; where it had stood, in the worked strips east of Low Kiln; who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof; what it asked of him, nothing. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight and not what the grain would have cost to buy back, and the yard had counted it in glances and handfuls and in the space a hand’s scoop left in the fold, and no slate had been opened and no hand entered it. When a woman at the door asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool, Sella sent the question to the pot and the weather: Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it, and carried the answer away level, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. Three changes of posture make no number, and a keeping is a keeping by being covered or by being spread, and the two postures of one grain do not add to a figure. That the grain had been out of its covering and back again three times and was not a figure still stood, and the surgeon’s register set the finding down without triumph: the bundle had been a keeping, the spread grain a keeping, the covered grain a keeping, and keeping is not the same act as counting and has no figure to enter.

The yard would go around its places as it had gone around them every morning since the grain came in. It would go around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called the thaw, neither swept; around the rack space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood; around the bare dark the trough whose water was called weather; around the field’s empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each would get its look and its wide step and no number: no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. The absence gathered interest the way a pot gathers heat, by being left where it was, and he kept it on the place where it lay and did not lay it across the dressed grain or the shelf’s two gaps or the pan’s gone ring. The dressed grain and the returning-line place were two keepings, and he drew no finger between them. Grain 20 stood two mornings ahead and was not brought early into a day that had its own keeping to do.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The house will keep it, she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting go — the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy’s life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in the same motion, an interval and an interval kept, not a promise, not a key, not an opening. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and the day’s ordinary work began over the covered grain with no figure anywhere under the roof.

It pooled there the way warmth pools in the hollow of a seat that has been sat in for twenty years, and he had nothing to do with the making of it and could not have told it from any other warmth. Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man’s shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder that a wheel-keeper had leaned into for twenty years; the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left that nobody filled; the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay along the skirting board and the new finer hairline beside it was called the thaw and neither was swept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, a saying about honest repairs that had outlived the thing it was true about, and cost more for being said to a pot that had no crack to leave showing. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, a space in a sentence shaped like the hollow of the wheel-seat. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and twenty-eight days, and no lesson had been made of any of it, and no collector had come up the clay road on Grain 19.

The boy took the objects apart the way he took everything within two paces, in the order of what they were and where they had stood and what they asked of him. A coat was a coat kept stiff with salt; a seat was a hollow worn by a weight; a line on a wall was a line left where it was, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. At the kindling Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said. To the wood he said, “Six counted. The next I don’t count,” in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that the house could not check. Beside it lay the unforged collector sentence — a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford — and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man, and his own name was not spoken with the sentence either.

Then the ear-sentence came to his teeth as it had come on other afternoons: an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name; a remembered stopping and not a new one. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the ninth stopping stood at nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year at its very end had still not given. The loan was not called. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it on Grain 19. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed, in the Debt Loft that reached him only as a sound with a direction, an unmarked clay pot held the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter the house’s man died, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving.

He said “Yes, house” to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and something must give at the end of the seventh year, and it had not given here.

He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they’re steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said, “You’ll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three shapings of the pot saying stood on their own place and stayed three sayings about a pot: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. Grain 20, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The one saying that the wrong wind had let stand over the boards — a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw — he let stand again as a saying and no more, taking it apart and setting it back where it stood. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No lesson came out of it. No name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot was lost in this yard. The burned-slate word stood at a fourth telling, a direction come over the boards from Slate Row and arrived nowhere; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two, and he joined them neither to the dust nor to the pot nor to the men.

He grew quieter as the burned-slate word went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off one corner; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella’s voice went low and unfinished at the hearth, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence and stood there. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back. He held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left the finger standing. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies.

At the going of the light she carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. At every open slate she kept the half-pace and went wide. She went wide around the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it called the thaw, neither swept; around the rack space beneath the lowest of the drying racks where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; around the trough, bare and dark, whose water was called weather; around the field’s empty place out on the worked strips; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number — no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no figure and no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under them, and none did. The absence went on gathering its interest on a place of its own, advanced and unpaid, and he did not lay it across the covered grain or the crust’s empty place.

She set him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen. Then she went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house: nothing in his room to hold a rhythm against, and he borrowed none from the room that does not touch his. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof; the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its fifth low thing; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot.

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel — no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline — and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through a finger’s width of door, the district’s slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirty days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand’s breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood the three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month has not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. The burned-slate word stood on its own place at a fourth telling, a direction over the boards from Slate Row with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood beside it with a hand between the three and stayed two. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid, the true subtraction gone up the clay road in a box that answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 and nothing for the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come back. The nine stoppings stood at nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the Seed 16 breath was unspent. Three held changes stood on three places — the fraction in a wall, the beat beneath a shelf, the half-check in a yard — with the small dry scrape from the loft sill fourth, the pale line at a gum fifth, and the held inch sixth. And on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between it and the hearings and the sayings and the tellings and the count, lay the seed of the question about the row: no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no name and no payer, waiting on the far-off day a witness stood somewhere and could be asked.

Nothing had been joined, named, opened, spent or ended. Exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.

Then, with no breathing number anywhere in the house, he put the sixth question of the chapter to his hand. The conditions were the conditions that had sometimes answered: an inch of air between the hand and the note of water over water through the finger’s-width door, a room with no count in it to borrow, and the river keeping its own low interval on the far side of the wall. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed. He took it through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended and brought it down on the river’s next low note. Two rhythms, one held against the other across a wall that does not touch, remembered, not a change made again. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now he could not close two inches on the pot handle set out for him, and the two facts lay in the same small hand without either buying the other.

It was the sixth way of the chapter and the thirty-sixth answer, filed on the sixth place with no payer named to it — not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Thirty-six answers stood on that place, each exact and worth nothing. The surgeon’s register laid the whole of it down at two paces and asked the dark what it was for; the dark gave back the river’s low note and nothing that could be entered.

Two hundred and thirty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath. In Low Kiln, under the Vail roof, the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty days; the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered at Grain’s first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over them. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with no figure over it, and the place in the returning line went around with a look and a wide step and still no number. The question about the row stood over the boards with nobody to own it — no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, a direction arrived nowhere with a witness it would need and no witness standing yet. Grain 30, Day 239, the third and last Account-day and market, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought early into this night.

The hand came down on the river’s next low note, and the count went forward exact and unspent into Day 231, Grain 22, Secondday, while the question stood over the boards with nobody to own it and the dressed grain lay covered with no figure over it.

Ninthday came under the Vail roof without a bell, only the kiln drawing in fours and stopping where the fifth should have stood. Hot clay came first under tallow, wet wool under that, and beneath all three the sour breath of the corner the house called the thaw. The boy lay at the loaf shelf’s low angle, two hundred and twenty-eight days old by no slate’s reckoning, with one crust two inches from his ear. Fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places; agreeing them would want a payer and none stood to any. His file was exact, complete, worth nothing.

On Grain’s tenth day Sella set the basket in the moot-post’s thin shade. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road, wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over the figure and read one in twelve low into the crowd, the figure going twice over the basket; the levy was called, and not by him. He went off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Hessa carried the slate up before noon unread, and the bell went four, four and a gap. The district’s arithmetic changed for the second time in Grain and could not be changed back: the figure read was not the figure paid.

Back under the roof cost-questions went to the pot and the weather, and a fist’s number went back into a sleeve. A wrong wind off High Kiln carried the sweet edge of lead glaze over the fold and out again, and a voice let a pot saying stand; he took it apart and set it back, a saying and no cause.
