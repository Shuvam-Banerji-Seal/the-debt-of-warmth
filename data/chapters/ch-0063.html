The hand lay against the fold where it had come down on the river’s next low note, and the boy set the coordination down the way a man lays a tool on a bench at the end of the work. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. Two rhythms had crossed in one inch of air across a wall that does not touch, and now the inch was finished and the hand was only a hand again, small, soft-nailed, outside the wool, with the fold at the ear and one crust lying two inches from it. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift that crust to his mouth; both were true, and neither stood up to call itself a sign. Under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln one room kept fire and the other kept river, and neither stopped for the ear between them.

He was two hundred and forty days old and six months and sixty days, and nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-nine to two hundred and forty with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. Smoke came on the way a pot goes out of the sun: Grain gave way without a boundary drawn across it, no stroke marked the hour, and neither of the district’s two names for the months was set right or left wrong in the telling. There was no market on a Firstday and no Cross and no bell; Smoke 10 stood nine mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

Hot clay came first under the roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner the house called the thaw. The day’s first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. He took the smells in the order they arrived and the sound in the order it did not, the way he had once taken a room in the dark: what was under the hand first, what was behind it second, and what the ear wanted to hear last.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from that ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to sit there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child’s hunger keeps no hours at all and the adult’s register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon’s register took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments and no case; there was only the order of the laying, and that was the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources — a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger’s width of door, the district’s slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and forty days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand’s breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule — a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which wrong-wind voices let stand again. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer, and none had been named to any of them.

the burned-slate word stood at four on its own place and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve printed and unchallenged and read twice over the basket in the square, and still not the figure paid. Grain’s third arithmetic had gone up the clay road and was unread under this roof, and the space between the figure read over the basket and the figure over the dressed grain at home had no signature on it and was left apart, not averaged. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man’s shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf; the seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and no one had straightened it. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty days. No collector had come up the clay road for that debt and none had gone down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

For the first firing of Smoke she went to the kiln mouth before anything else, before the basket and before the instruction to the wool. the widow’s mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze, with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she put her hand to it. She held it one breath. Then she let go. Sixty days of going around ended in that one held breath, and nothing in the house stood up to say that they had ended. She lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand, the burn-side, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took. It answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Nobody praised the opening and nobody saw what it mended; no slate was entered, no lesson made, no bond changed hands and no payer named. It was maintenance on a vessel nobody would name, done the way a widow keeps a wound clean, and she put it behind the household without a word.

The heat was kept behind a screen and let through a gate of two bites; one bite kept it in and the other let it out, and there was no word in Grathic for both mercies. She did not explain the gate to the fire and the fire did not ask. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals when she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and she did not look toward the bed.

Afterward she went to the hearth as she had gone on every morning, and the house took up its ordinary grammar. At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, moved her lips along a row with two gaps without giving the gaps a figure, and glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, shutting her hand half over nothing. When she set the pot rim out she put the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

She did not touch the mark again at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon or a dusk, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the rule held exactly as on Tallow 1 and Ember 1.

At the wheel-shed Jorra Vail parted dust from slip, told a pot it would do, knew its note three turns ahead and withheld it; the burned slate stood at four, the shut-room tellings at two, and he’s not Vail stayed sealed.

Six times the boy put the question to his hand, the fifty-fifth through the sixtieth, each filed with no payer named. At the close the hand went up an inch, rode one of Sella’s counted breaths from the room that does not touch his, kept through the little silence after her count and came down on the river’s next low note — two rhythms in one inch, remembered, not a change made again. Sixty answers stood, exact and worth nothing. The file lay open. Smoke went out; nothing was joined, named, opened, spent or ended.
