What the Figure Does Not Know

Days 327–329 · Bone 28–30, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

Six readings stand behind one figure and do not know one another. The figure was never asked what it read over, and the space where the price should stand has kept its silence.

— A saying of the Cross, told at the going of Bone

— Day 327 —

Bone 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813

The hand outside the wool had come down and laid itself back against the fold, and the room went on as a room goes on after a man puts away a tool: nothing announced, nothing altered, only the shape of the thing left in the place where the next hand will find it without looking. It was the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, and the dark had the dry kiln heat shut up in it, the heat that takes the water out of things and gives nothing back. The boy lay on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf and had not moved, because there was no part of him that could move and change anything. Under the board at his back the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and that silence had stood there three hundred and twenty-seven days with no payer named under it. Garl Vail stayed where he had been kept, in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed keeping the hollow twenty years of another man’s weight had worn into it and that nobody filled, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both, the twice-mended pot with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and twenty-seven days, and the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with and was worse for being uncalled. Three hundred and twenty-six had stood exact at the going of the light; three hundred and twenty-seven stood now, six months and one hundred and forty-seven days, told by nobody, exact, complete, unspent, and of no use to anybody who might have wanted it. Nothing that had happened on the twenty-seventh came in under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment, and the thumb that had rested on the uncut seventh notch at the kindling stayed one object on its own place and was not made again. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, as it had come on without one, and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women called it by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days, and the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it.

He took the room in as a field is taken in before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones generally do. The boards gave back heat in their own order, the near board and the next; the river went low over lower under the finger’s-width door, a sound he had learned to place nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; somewhere in the wall a coal settled and was quiet. None of these sounds had been sent to him and none had been kept for him. They arrived in the order they arrived and he filed them in that order, which was the only order he had ever been given and the only one he trusted.

The inventory came next, in the old order, because an inventory taken out of order is no inventory. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder through the dry heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself, and no third had come in during the night to make the row come out even; a third would have made a row, and a row of three would have wanted a reason, and there was no reason standing under it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The Afterdrop waited beyond the three stops, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew what the ease on a face meant. The surgeon in him knew it before the infant could be hushed: the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a body that is losing its heat, the grimace that looks like a smile. A cold vessel cannot clot. A cold patient will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He had once known where to put his hands on a man in a room full of bright lights and loud machines; here he could not lift one hand two inches to his own mouth. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle and bring it within reach. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the argument between them went on under the wool without verdict and without witness, and the argument was the work.

Sella Vail came to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order of the house for three hundred and twenty-seven days, and she kept it the way she kept every other thing worth keeping, without explaining it to anybody who might have wanted to enter it. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed first on nothing and then on the back of a broken pot.

“It’s the thaw,” she told the coals, in the tone of a woman stating what the weather had already decided. She turned them with the back of the pot, not with a poker, and let them answer in their own way. Four low pulls came up out of them and the place where a fifth should have stood stayed as it had stood, and she did not supply the fifth and did not name who had taken it.

“The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot still in her hand, counting something under her breath: a small intake, a hold, a letting-go, all directed toward no opening and none of it arriving anywhere. Neither gap was figured. The boy put her three small acts on a place of their own with a finger between them and his three, for two people keeping the same sound under one roof are not one sound, and two likenesses are not a cause and a cause wants a payer standing under it, and none stood.

Her finger lay on the top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps, and neither gap was given a figure. Once, and only once, her eyes went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze on the shelf by the wall; her hand went halfway over nothing and shut, and the burn on its back closed on air — two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, with the pale puckered skin inside it. She did not touch the mark at her throat. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the sixth stood far off at Ash 2 in the first firing and was not brought near.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot waited with the hairline of its original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle.

“Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she said to the pot. “If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” She said the second to the fire and the first to the pot, and neither proverb went to a person, and the crack was left showing.

The dressed grain under its wool was turned with a touch lighter than the day before, and no figure was taken off it, and no handful was lifted. Her fingers went in and came out and did not close. The handful let fall at the rack end on the twenty-second of Bone stayed one object on its own place and was not made again, and the empty fingers of this morning went beside it as a keeping and not as a second, for two would have been a habit and three a way, and a habit and a way are both accounts that have found somebody to pay, and nobody had.

She set the pot down with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. There was a half-beat between the set-down and her letting go of the handle, and she did not look toward the bed in it. The note the pot gave as it settled was not quite full and not quite flat, and he filed it where a near-full thing must go and gave it no seventh place.

The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause; the finger went between. He had once taken a hand out of a field before the dressing went on, and this was the same motion done with nothing but attention for a tool. Bone 30 (Day 329), the month’s third and last reading, stood two mornings ahead and was not brought into this one; the Cross and the reading block were a direction and no more.

The first light came into the room the way light comes into a room that has not asked for it, touching the pot’s crack before it touched the fold. It was an ordinary Eighthday. No market came, no Cross, no bell; the district went about its own accounting at a distance that reached this roof only as the small dry rasp of the river wind against the wool. Sella had gone to the fire as she always went first, and the instruction had gone to the coals and the wool and the pot and never to the basket, and the house had kept what the house kept without saying whose house it was.

In the last of the small hours, when the first light had come and the note had gone down to its lowest under the finger’s-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach, the intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He had put his small hand into the order of things for the length of one note and taken it out again, and the order went on whether he did it or not.

He filed it as answer two hundred and twenty-nine: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the five arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and twenty-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and twenty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-seven days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the coat kept was not spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the twenty-eighth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, and it had bought nothing at two inches of air and was not for sale, and it went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and twenty-nine; and the river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door while Bone went on without a boundary drawn, two mornings short of the month’s third and last reading and not brought into a day that had not earned it.

Five openings in five months stood with the sixth far off at Ash 2 and not brought near. Seven green stood a colour and seven shape a shape with no eighth. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, and no collector came up or went down the clay road.

Garl stayed where he had been kept, in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat kept the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody filled it; the clean dark line and the finer grey kept their places unswept. The twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and both proverbs went to the pot and to the fire and never to a person, so that the repair stayed apart from restoration. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and twenty-nine days, and the carried fire’s fifth had been unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; no lesson had been made of any of it.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed at the going of the light with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot is parted from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not say it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and make a song of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her unfinished sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no Grathic word for both mercies. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up from the yard at the half-pace from every open slate, went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and set him on the bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. There was a sound from above and behind only, a foot on a stair that did not come down, and no interior was entered. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. She said nothing about where they had been or what had been read over the basket, and the instruction went to the fire, the wool and the pot and never to the basket. The house will keep it, she said where another woman would have said I. The widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because a going of the light and a bed are not openings and five openings in five months are five and not a rule; the sixth stood far off at Ash 2 and was not brought near. She went to the hearth before she went to the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and twenty-nine days. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The twice-mended pot came up with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire with the crack left showing. The dressed grain lay turned under wool on the cool stone with no figure and no handful lifted; her fingers went in and came out and did not close. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through the finger’s-width door water over water went on as a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, not numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell’s gap left in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told and the told figure beside the paid with no signature between.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and thirty-four: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and thirty-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without a verdict in the small hours under the roof. Three hundred and twenty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the thirtieth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. Bone went out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months was set right, and the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do; it had bought nothing at two inches of air and was not for sale, and it went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and thirty-four; and the river’s low note went quiet under the finger’s-width door while Bone went out without a boundary drawn. Ash 1 stood one morning ahead and Ash 2, the sixth opening, two mornings ahead, and neither was brought into a day that had not earned it.

On the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, left both proverbs with pot and fire, turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure, and set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number; the Cold Acre hare was answered with the weather and the cracked jar sent to a pot that said nothing. At the well on the twenty-ninth Ilva told a woman who asked what the firings had taken, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account, her hand flat on the sleeve where the notched strip lay unread.

On the thirtieth the month’s third and last reading came to the Cross. Berr Clay came up off the clay road with the red cord of his office folded under his coat and the sweet green of glaze at one mouth-corner, wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, read One in twelve, as the notice says twice over the basket --- once for the moot and once for the late households --- called the levy and denied it with your house, never I, and went off up the road with the box locked, believing he had done a kindness. Six arithmetics now stood behind one unsigned figure, six and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer. Hessa Rale carried the slate up toward Greybank before noon and would not read what she bore: I don’t read it. I carry it. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself; no collector came up the road or went down it.

Each day gave two answers: a small intake let go whole on the river’s note, the hand coming straight down as a tool put away where the next hand will find it. Six were entered, two hundred and twenty-nine through two hundred and thirty-four, each worth nothing at two inches of air.
