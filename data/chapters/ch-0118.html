A Word at the Well

Days 405–407 · Ilmen 11–13, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

A figure goes up the clay road in a locked box; a word comes up from the well and stops on the stone lip. Neither carries a name under it, and the yard keeps both.

— A saying of the Clay Yard, told on the twelfth morning of Ilmen

— Day 405 —

Ilmen 11, Y.H. 814, Firstday

The hand lay flat where it had been laid, and the small hours came on under the roof without being let in, the way cold comes into a room that has no door for it — not arriving, only being there, the boards giving up their last thin warmth and the dark settling into the wool as though it had been asked and had answered. Four hundred and four days stood behind that hand exact and unspent, six months and two hundred and twenty-four of them told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, and answer three hundred and eighty-four lay under it, a small intake held whole on the river’s note’s own finishing and let go as the note ended, laid down as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Firstday was the working name of this morning, and nothing under the Vail kiln-house made a threshold of it or a signing. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen, in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, and dry kiln heat had gone into the wool with elm-bark smoke and tallow, and under that the faint sour tang of Ash lifting out of the Vail corner where the clean dark line kept its finer grey hairline beside it, called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold and left unswept two years. Under the boards the carried fire gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it. Through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, low under the winter skin, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, found where put and not spent on a tend-day. Outside, the dark went about its ordinary work along the Clay Yard, and nobody under any roof was asking it to mean anything.

The surgeon took the body in the order it arrived, because a field read out of order is a field read wrong. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust, gone harder through the dry nights, stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; beside it the empty place held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put again in the dark and not made again, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The keeping at the fold was a finding however many hands found it, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. A face composed by cold is not composure. Pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The careful rewarming he would have chosen was a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came to him sideways, and it was done under this roof and he could not ask for it. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat and the order in which it takes it back, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold; he had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and the knowledge was of the same order and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work.

Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot board’s handle, and wanted them without knowing that wanting them was a want. The two registers quarrelled without verdict. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor does not touch his.

He kept four hundred and five exact against a house that kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a clay jar and in a space at the row’s end nobody called a number, and he did not stand the figure up as a threshold, a signing or a doctrine. The year’s first arithmetic had gone up the clay road in Berr’s locked box toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down and no key in his hand, and nothing had come back down the road addressed to the basket. It was one reading and not an account, not a signature, not a payer and not a thirteenth; the figure read was still not the figure paid. Twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 stood unchanged behind one unsigned figure, and he did not lay twelve old findings across the new one to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand that has no key is made even on paper, and paper is not a payer. The box went up the road with the first true subtraction of the year inside it and with a man believing he had done a kindness, and the believing stayed up there with the box and did not come under this roof as lesson or cause. The kept things lay where they had been laid and asked nothing of the new day: the three coverings and the river’s winter skin entered nowhere, not a fourth of the three and not spent on a tend-day; the four rooms that hold the same four things kept the same gap with a finger between every pair and no Court room brought under the roof as a fifth; six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and the Drawing house’s flat knock outside on its own place. The month’s second Account-day stood beyond the days this house had earned and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it.

First light came up over the yard the way a pail comes up out of a well — practical, wet, and asking nothing of the hand that lifted it. Along the wall the neighbours went about the ordinary grammar of a Firstday, and the grammar was the only thing in the morning that could be relied on to be exactly itself. A woman set a pail on a stone where a pail always stood and did not look down to see whether it stood where it had stood before. A loaf went a finger’s width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure, because a figure wants a hand to put it there and no hand had. Two voices passed the price of barley from one end of the wall to the other and arrived at the far end without ever having been a number, and a neighbour with a rake raised it at the ridge, looked at the clean dark line and its finer grey, and set the rake down again with nothing done and nothing said. The ridge lay where it lay. There was a word in the yard for grey put down and another for grey gone, and the two words were kept on two places with a finger between them, for a thing called gone is a thing somebody has agreed to stop looking at, and no such agreement had been made here. He took the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: a pail set on stone, a rake’s rasp on the ridge, a rope settling, the river’s low over lower under its covering. Three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before the basket, as she had come at both ends of every day since he came under this roof. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I, standing a moment with a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening and figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread; her lips moved on a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. One glance went to the clay jar cold under lead glaze beyond the board, and the hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and went back to the pot. At her throat the widow’s mark hung where it hung and was not touched. A morning was not an opening; a hearth, a shelf, a yard and a going of the light were not openings; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a day that had earned no more than its first light.

She lifted the twice-mended pot from the pot board thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side of it. Garl Vail was in that hairline and in the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on its peg behind the door and in the hollow wheel-seat in the shed that nobody filled, and not in a story anybody told; his name had not been spoken under the roof for four hundred and five days, and the two counts — his name and the fire’s fifth — stood side by side without becoming one. She said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled and If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves the hand showing, and a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure for the same reason a potter leaves a staple in the light. The tile debt against a firing of tiles went on circling without a name and worse for being uncalled, and no collector came up the road to call it on a Firstday.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone away from the hearth. She lifted a corner and turned what was under it with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, piece by piece, setting each down where it had lain, and took no figure from it and no handful for herself. The arithmetic gone up the road over grain covered at home did not know what the grain cost; it had gone up with the rest of them, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. She, who could not enter a figure anywhere and had no hand to lift a crust two inches, set none against it. The wool went back over the grain with the same light touch, and the space stayed as it was. Then the pot went down on the board again with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and there was a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between an intention and an arrest: her hand had meant to set the pot nearer and had stopped where it stopped, and the distance cost her nothing and bought the boy nothing, and neither of them called it a gift. Under the roof nobody instructs a basket, and nobody instructs a pot about a hand.

She carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. The seven walked-around places were gone around with a look from the basket’s low angle and a wide step that belonged to somebody else, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back, and the wide step was kept as a keeping and not made a sign. Beyond the last house the field’s empty place held the shape of a thing taken out of a field and not the shape of a hole anybody had dug; she did not lay that shape across the loaf’s stopping or the ridge or the two words for grey. Likenesses stood up want a man put under every one of them, and no man stood. She set him under the roof and never into the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold to the crust’s empty place, and went back to her fire saying nothing at all.

At the kindling Tarn kept six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking. He cut nothing, counted nothing, spent nothing, and told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, Six counted. The next I don’t count. He took the broom only after the flagstones had been given their instruction and said Yes, house to the question and to the thing behind it, agreeing so thoroughly the question had nowhere left to put a foot, and went over the flagstones without a word and without a turn of the head toward the landing. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, an object and not a rule, an interval and not a payment; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled at the very end of a term already ended, and a term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom but leaves a man more exposed than it found him. Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was, his chest tight since the dark. He was helpful when he was frightened and on this morning he was not frightened, and the household read the quiet of him the way it read the quiet of Sella. The fear of being entered again for vagrancy stayed his without a figure, along with a want he did not voice for a slate with his own name and no mark against it.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door off the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and got no name from him and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person — It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady — and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, knowing the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withholding the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth and was left standing so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. He took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. He’s not Vail stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name under the shed was neither given nor withdrawn while the burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, and the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions apart and unspent. He grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order from anybody, and the boy kept the quiet on its own place and made no cause of it.

The small hours put the day away the way a surgeon puts instruments away when a case is done and before the next is called. The towel came out at the head of the bed, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and he went over the field by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. The crust’s gap stood two inches from the ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the shelf’s two gaps stood in a room that does not touch this one; the gone pan’s round ring lay where a pan had been lifted and not where a pan was; the field’s empty place held the shape of a thing taken out; the keeping at the fold was found where put and still a finding, with no name over it and no payer under it. The told bell’s gap stood in its square beside the bell he had heard from the Cross, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, its gap not folded into the fire’s four and a gap under the floor, into the boards’ four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the twelve readings behind one unsigned figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. On the gap tray three names had been emptied out and the emptiness was kept with a finger between it and the clean voice’s missing name and the story’s empty name, none filled, none made a witness or a hearing. The neighbour’s called-gone grey stood beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner, two states on two places with a finger between, and the four shapings of the well-saying stood where put and did not repeat and bought no fifth. Tarn’s ear-sentence had come all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there in a term already ended; Jorra’s promise stayed neither given nor withdrawn; Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch with no name under it, and his name had gone unspoken four hundred and five days. The Ash 5 joining of the burned-slate word to the counting room shut in the war and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions stood where put, advanced and not paid, and did not repeat on a day that had earned only its own morning. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and the Drawing house’s small flat knock outside on its own place; nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings stood with no tenth. No eighth opening stood, no seventh hearing, no sixth shaping, no thirteenth arithmetic, no signature, no payer, no witness, no reader and no payment. Counting was an old-life habit and not a gift: it gave him no key, no slate with his own name, no hand to lift a crust two inches, only the exactness of the field and the cost of it, which was nothing.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river’s note under the winter skin. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note’s own finishing under the covering — neither early nor stayed — and let it go as the note ended. Answer three hundred and eighty-five came out and the hand came down. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the new figure, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the child’s grey fingertips, the mother’s cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river’s skin, the widow’s mark, the fourth direction, the clerk’s inward slate, the green at the pot board, the shape at the market’s edge, Berr’s low saying nor the keeping at the fold.
