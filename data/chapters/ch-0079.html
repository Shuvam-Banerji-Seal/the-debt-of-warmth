What the Slate Carried

Days 288–290 · Frost 19–21, Y.H. 813 · Low Kiln, Sill

— A saying of the women at the yard well in Low Kiln, told on a morning of Frost

— Day 288 —

Frost 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813

The hand lay where it had been laid, against the fold at the low angle of the loaf shelf, as a man puts a tool down where the next hand will find it without looking; and the small hours came on over the Vail kiln-house the way cold comes into a house whose fire has been banked, by degrees and from the outside, so that a sleeper would feel no change until the change was finished. The boy came into his two hundred and eighty-eighth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. A round number of answers stood behind him, and a hundred and fifty hand-movings had gone into the making of them, and neither the number nor the movements stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Two hundred and eighty-seven had gone to two hundred and eighty-eight the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam; six months and one hundred and eight days were told by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath. Under the floor the carried fire’s fifth stood unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had ever promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on the bed, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, and that the face cold puts on is a face of ease which is not ease at all. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and eight days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order — crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting — and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had any authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. He had learned, in a room of white light and moving hands, that a dying man will often seem to rally just before he goes, and that the rally is a bargain the body makes with itself and keeps badly; here the seeming came without the dying, or with so little of it that no watcher could have measured the loss, and he held the knowledge ready for a day that might never call for it. It was an old habit, this standing ready with a fact nobody would ask for. He kept it anyway, the way a house keeps its charcoal under a finger and does not look.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-eight days; at the finger’s-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell’s four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10, stayed in the square beside the told bell of the Long Night, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note, but a heard bell beside a told bell. No fire-sitter stood over the coals and no house count came to be borrowed. Through the finger’s-width door the Clay Yard lay under its own thin light, and the river went low over lower under it, and the boards went on keeping their four and four and a knot as though the keeping of them were the whole work of the night.

Water over water was not a vessel’s note and he had never entered it as one; it was the river going under its own skin, a low thing laid over a lower thing, and the ear at the door took it as weather takes a sound, without asking who had set it there. The kiln’s four pulls came up through the floor as a man’s breathing comes through a wall, and the silence after them had the shape of a held hand. Three counts, three places, no agreement; the first instinct of the man he had been in another room was to find which two things agreed and call the third the error, and here the error would have been to agree at all, because agreement wanted a payer and no payer stood. He let the three be three.

On the towel at the head of the bed the file lay turned out as instruments are turned out for a man who will never be called to use them, each thing in its own place with room between, known by weight before the eye fell on it. The surgeon’s hand went over it in the dark and found every object where it had been laid. Six hearings lay first with the empty seventh place beside them kept empty; the heard bell lay beside the told in its square; three pot sayings lay together with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place apart and no fourth likeness made a cause; the burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the ownerless question lay with five askings and one shaping beside them, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named. The nine stoppings stood with no tenth, and the nine record-first hands stood with Ilva’s ninth in her sleeve unread and no tenth, the count kept in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. Frost’s first arithmetic stayed locked in Berr Clay’s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the printed one in twelve unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, while the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end with the arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 still not knowing what it cost. The space between figure and price kept its unsigned edge and no hand in this house had signature enough to close it.

Then he set the Frost 14 well-saying on a place of its own beside Hessa Rale’s eighth record-first hand with a finger between: one saying, no second shaping, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. A woman waiting her turn at the well had said it to the boards and the well-roof three days back, that the runner who carried Berr Clay’s slate once carried a figure that named a person she knew; she had said it the way the yard says the weather and gone on with her bucket, and no owner of the voice stood anywhere to be asked. It did not make Hessa a reader of what she had carried for years, and it was not joined to the strip in a sleeve a pace away, nor to a thane’s twice-wiped thumb, nor to a clerk’s inward slate, nor to the charcoal under the shelf. He forbade it to stand up with the three held changes — the fraction of the damper held on Day 161, the beat held beneath the shelf on Day 166, the half-check of the flagstone stroke on Day 171 — or with the small dry scrape of the loft sill, the carried man’s pale line, his own held inch, or the held and empty intervals, into a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stands to any of them. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the saying was one saying and not a doctrine; and nothing in the file was given a name that would let it be called.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said The house will keep it where another woman would have said I. With her back to the basket she kept her finger on top of the shelf’s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread, uncopied and unfigured, moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. In the unswept corner the grey ridge called the thaw lay finer than the clean dark line beside it, and the Ash in it had not been lifted in two years; she had not swept it and did not intend to sweep it, and on a Ninthday morning the hand at her throat went to the kiln-lid and not to the widow’s mark, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. At the cool stone she turned the dressed grain beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the day before and gave it no figure. To the wool she said Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind in the old proverb-form, and to the broom she gave its instruction before anybody else could, Go around, and the broom went around. She did not say whose boy, whose fire, whose hundred; a thing said aloud becomes a thing a slate can enter, and she had long ago decided that no slate should enter it. She stood the pot rim on the pot board two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, and gave every instruction to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never once to the basket. At the hearth the fire answered four low pulls and left the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, and went away without looking toward the bed. The house kept its number the way it kept everything, in loaves and glances and the unnamed space where a loaf should have stood, and it kept the boy by not asking what he was and by not entering the warmth.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river’s note and took the small intake of the fire’s silence across the interval to the going of the first morning under this roof, and filed the hundred and fifty-first answer as an object worth nothing at two inches of air. The surgeon’s clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant’s louder want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. The satisfaction was small and clean and he did not spend it; the wanting was large and damp and would not be answered by any counting. Two inches of air stood between the hand and the crust as they had stood for two hundred and eighty-eight mornings, and the space had no signature and no payer and no instruction written in it. He had once called such spaces trivial because they could be measured; he no longer called them trivial, and he still could not close them. Neither the intake nor the interval was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule; one hundred and fifty-one honest answers stood beside one hundred and fifty, exact, complete and unspent, and a longer row did not stand up as a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Frost the twentieth (Day 289), the month’s second Account-day and market, stood one morning ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The boy made nothing of the nearness and did not let it stand up as a sign. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line lay where the Ash ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it, called the thaw, lay finer still; neither had been swept, and the broom went around both as a woman goes around a sleeping animal. Beneath the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay where they had been laid, and she gave the space under the rack the same wide berth she gave a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, without looking at the slates and without entering them. On the flat ground near the drying racks the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word; a pan gone is known by the ring it leaves, and the ring was not a message and had no hand to send it. Beside the well the trough had skinned over from edge to edge and its water was called weather by everybody who passed it, and the weather was not asked what it cost. Out on the worked strips the field’s empty place stood where it had stood since the planting, and the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, was gone around with a look and no number.

Seven were seven and not an account. She did not count them under her breath and he did not count them for her; the missing hand was carried, not named, not paid and not joined to the one crust in the fold, or to the dressed grain covered and unfigured under wool at the rack end, or to the man carried out on Day 188. A house that keeps by going around things keeps its accounts in the feet, and a man who reads such a house learns to count the steps and to call no total. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; here he could not turn his head far enough to see the space under the rack and still keep the crust in view, and the two things stood on different places with a finger between. The yard’s goings-around went on over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and he was the stone and not the water.

The stayed frost lay white on the lip of the yard well where the Warm had lately gone out, and the rope stiffened as the bucket came up and gave its small dry rasp. On the flat stone by the well, set out of the path of feet where nobody would knock it, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt where the silt had risen into it, a covering laid over a depth that the yard called weather and asked nothing of. The boy kept the white lip on a place of its own and the plate on another, and he made no fourth likeness between them and the trough’s skin and the frost on the boards, and he named no payer to any of them. A cause is a thing that wants a payer standing under it, and nobody stood. The bucket went down and came up; the rope gave its dry note; the weather went on being weather, entered nowhere.

Garl Vail was present in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat kept a broader man’s shape on its peg behind the door, with the crease at the left shoulder where a collector’s hand had once rested or where a man had leaned; the boy had never decided which, and the not-deciding kept the coat honest. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person — feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled — and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and eighty-eight days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; the first true subtraction of Frost rode locked in a box toward High Kiln with the printed figure unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the keeping of a man by not-doings stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story. Tarn was at the kindling with the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where the house could not; Garl’s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood Six counted. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady. To a pot on the wheel he said You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.
