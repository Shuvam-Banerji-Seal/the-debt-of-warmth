The Figure That Went Back to the Sleeve

Days 420–422 · Ilmen 26–28, Y.H. 814 · Low Kiln, Sill

Offer a figure to a stick and the stick keeps its notch; the figure goes back into the fist and the fist into the sleeve. Neither has paid, and the wood has not learned a name.

— A saying of the firing women at the Low Kiln wells

— Day 420 —

Ilmen 26, Y.H. 814, Sixthday

Small hours had come and gone without anybody under the roof naming them. The towel lay at the head of the bed, rough from the drying and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid at the last answer of the day gone by. Four hundred and fourteen exact, and the count standing at four hundred and nineteen, and nobody under any roof knowing what a count was. It was a new day. Nothing had crossed into it from the one before — not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The boy lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen; the room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. A sour mineral tang came up off the corner where no broom had gone in two years. He was awake, as he had been awake every morning since the first one, and there was nobody in the room to tell what the morning was.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it three nights ago and would not try now. Beside it the empty place held the keeping at the fold, found where put in the mornings and the goings of the light of the three days behind him, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it.

The infant wanted three things without knowing that wanting them was a want. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, and a hand on the handle of the pot. Both registers were true; the second was louder; neither won. It was not a verdict but the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor did not touch his.

Under the flat hand the file kept its old shape. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for, and the Drawing house’s flat knock outside them on its own ground. The bell heard from the Cross kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and its gap was not folded into the carried fire’s four and a gap below the boards, or the boards’ four and four and a thumb-width knot, or the two readings of the year behind one unsigned figure beside the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 — three counts in three places staying three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings with no tenth; Hessa’s narrow slate stayed unread up the clay road, and Ilva’s short strip of notched birch stayed unread in a sleeve in the yard, her hand ninth with no tenth made of it. Garl Vail’s name had gone unspoken four hundred and nineteen days and the fifth pull below the floor had gone unpaid the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one. The two readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure beside the twelve old arithmetics, two and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the second, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of two; Berr Clay’s box had gone up the clay road locked and the key had gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down, and paper is not a payer.

The clerk’s entered figure stayed reported at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge, a figure entered before the eyes went up, on a place of its own with a finger between it and Tarn’s degree toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard’s edge — a report brought up the yard and never a sighting, not the calling of the loan, not a tenth hand and not a rule. The asking that had come up once at the well stayed beside the second man’s direction down the bridge road and his narrow slate with its writing turned to the sleeve, with a finger between it and the first man’s face, the clerk’s figure and the fifth shaping of the well-saying; it was not told again on a day that had earned only this morning and was not made a door. The fifth shaping sat on its own tray between Hessa’s eighth hand and the saying of the road from the Cross to Greybank, with a third finger between it and the four ownerless directions. Tarn’s sentence was found where put and was not made a habit; the softening at the river’s skin was kept as weather; the promise under the wheel-shed was neither given nor withdrawn. First light came thin through the finger’s-width door, the kind that shows dust rather than warms it. The river kept its note of water over water under the winter skin, a low thing over a lower thing, and the boy found it where put and called it weather. The count became four hundred and twenty exact, six months and two hundred and forty days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. He did not stand it up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure, and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics across the two readings of the new year to make a row come out even, for a row made even on paper is not a payment and no key came under any roof. A round-sounding number is only a number with air around it; it bought no warmth and borrowed no payer.

Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay, and the coals gave off the dry smell of a fire banked and not forgotten. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. The house will keep it went to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch the widow’s mark. A morning, a hearth, a shelf, a yard and a basket are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its twenty-sixth.

She said to the fire, in the tone of a woman who has told the same thing to the same fire for three winters, The fire doesn’t know you’re sorry. Feed it, and put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, and did not look to see whether the basket had heard. Her hands went on counting when they had nothing to do — three spoons, a nail, the small breath a child makes that nobody numbers — and came back to the hairline at the wire as hands come back to a wound they have dressed. At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool. She turned it piece by piece with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece down where it had lain, took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand’s work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled and If it’s worth mending it’s worth mending twice. The second time is the real one. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

At the carrying-down she went nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. In the Clay Yard she went around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number: the stone lip, the worn boards, the strip of hard mud, the place where a wheel had stood, the two narrow gaps between the stacked pots, and the returning-line place, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign, for a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. She did not look twice; looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there. She set him under the roof at the yard’s edge where the light came sideways and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. The thread stayed stiff and unwiped.

Tarn was at the kindling and the flagstones when the basket came down, and the household went on around him as it always went when he was doing what he was told. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beside Garl’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the tile debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking. In the tone of a man stating the weather he told the wood, Six counted. The next I don’t count, cutting nothing, counting nothing, spending nothing. The sentence was found where put and was not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; it did not gather a second silence on an unearned day, for a second silence would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. His chest had been tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was, his stroke over the flagstones going without the half-check. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction and said Yes, house to the fire. He did not turn his head toward the landing; the degree stayed a shape found where put and was not made a second time. His seventh year had run to its end with the term ended and the loan uncalled, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the fear of being entered again for vagrancy still his without a figure, and the want he did not voice still there for a slate with his own name and no mark against it. The boy filed the sentence on a place of its own beside the unforged collector sentence and Jorra’s silence, and laid none of them across the clerk’s figure, the degree or the well-asking, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Jorra Vail came through the finger’s-width door from the Vail wheel-shed with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened — not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person, It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady, and told a pot You’ll do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time, for a pot called early is a pot whose note has been spent before it was earned. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence. One bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies; he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair’s breadth, and was left standing so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting. He took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. He’s not Vail stayed sealed and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

The morning work at the yard well went the way morning work goes. A pail went down on its stone with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set, and the rope settled into its knot with the stiff dry rasp that cold puts into hemp, a sound without any grain of intention in it. At the flat stone by the well a woman waiting her turn shifted a pot from one hip to the other and said to nobody in particular that the rope would take a finger if a body was careless, and another at the rail said that cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and went on about her business. The boy took the order apart by the place of each part: the hand on the rope, the eye on the pail, the mouth after both, and the half-beat lying in the gap where a name would have stood. There was no name in the gap. He gave the women no interior and the words no owner beyond the mouths they came out of, for a voice in a yard is a voice in a yard and the cold takes a finger and takes another.

Ilva stood once at the well drawing with the pail against her knee. The rope was stiff in the dry cold and gave its small rasp as the bucket came up; clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. People noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. The short strip of notched birch lay in its fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between.

The woman waiting her turn, with her pot against her hip and her shawl drawn tight against the river wind, looked at the pail and then at the sleeve, which is to say she looked where people look when they want a number and do not want to ask for it twice. “What’s Ilmen taken?” she said. “I’ll put a figure to it. I can do that much.” She brought a number up out of a shut fist and held it toward the light, the way a woman at a market board holds a weight in her palm before the weigher calls it, and put the fist toward the strip in Ilva’s sleeve.

Ilva’s hand went flat over the fold before her eyes went to the asker, and her mouth opened after both, the half-beat lying in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. She answered the question put and not the one behind it. “Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.”

The woman held the figure out a breath longer, looking at the flat hand and at the light coming up over the yard, and then shut her fist upon it again and put it back into her sleeve, where it arrived nowhere. “Then it’s a stick,” she said, and went back to her place in the round, and nobody turned twice. No slate was opened within reach of the basket, no figure came off the wood, no reader was made and no name was spoken over the notches. The offered figure went from the fist toward the wood, looked at the light and went back into the sleeve; the strip kept its notches and the count stayed in wood where no clerk’s thumb could take a figure off it. Ilva put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail. She was not made the woman who explains what the stick means.

The boy took the order apart by the place of each part as a surgeon takes a field apart — fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat lying in the gap — and gave the fist no figure and the sleeve no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. He filed the offer and its return on a place of its own beside her ninth record-first hand and the fifth shaping of the well-saying, with a finger between every pair. It was not a sale, not a reader made, not a tenth hand, not a payer, not an account, not a sixth shaping, not the calling of the loan and not joined to the well-asking, the clerk’s entered figure, the second man’s turned slate or the face at the yard’s edge. It was a reaching gathering interest toward the far-off hand that will one day lay the district’s private keepings together, and it was not that hand. Her fear was advanced and left unpaid. Nothing under any roof had been paid, named or ended, and the yard went on around the pail.

The trough stood bare and dark. The thin skin that had lain on it through the turn was gone and the rim kept the line where the water had been, a lower country marked on the side of a vessel. The gone pan held its shallow round print on a place of its own, and the boy did not lay that ring across the loaf’s stopping or across the ridge at the corner. At the Vail corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold, with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. The called-gone grey stood beside the left-in-place ridge with a finger between. No agreement had been made to stop looking at the ridge, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The tang was thinner in this morning’s air and the grey lay a hair less distinct along one stretch; he found the softening where put and did not look twice, and did not make it a sign, a fourth covering or a payment on a day that had earned only its twenty-sixth.

Costs came to the yard as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man come down the clay road brought a number up out of a shut fist toward the well, held it where the light could see it, looked at it and put it back into his sleeve; it arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, and no signature stood between the figure and the price. The offered figure was not laid across any of these; two arrivals that reached nobody do not become one arrival, and no name, owner, answer carried, witness, reader, sale, tenth hand or paying stood under any of them.

Answer four hundred and fifteen came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the two of the new count, the printed figure, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk’s figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the bell’s gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the winter skin, the widow’s mark nor the keeping at the fold. Seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, 409 through 415, one to this morning and two to each of the three days behind it, and not one was a doctrine. The woman’s fist was shut again on the offered figure and Ilva’s flat hand lay on the fold in her sleeve; the boy’s hand came down and lay flat on the fold in the basket. The crust stood two inches off with its corner a hair above the wool and the thread unwiped, and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept — forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.

The afternoon came on without anybody under the roof giving it a name, the way the dry half of the year comes on: not as a threshold crossed, not as a thing the morning had owed and paid, but as light sliding off the near boards and up the far wall of the shed, so slowly that a man watching would have sworn the wall had grown lighter of its own accord. Nothing in the Clay Yard had been told what the day was. The boy had not been told to be still. The basket sat where Sella Vail had set it, under the eave where the heat came sideways, and the yard went on at the scale the yard keeps — pail, rope, board, stone — and not at the scale of the number he carried under his tongue.

He took the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. A pail went down on the flat stone by the well with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set, and the sound told him the stone was dry and the pail was empty and the arm that set it down had not been in a hurry. The rope settled into its knot with the stiff dry rasp that cold puts into hemp, a sound with no grain of intention in it, and he let it be a rope and nothing more. Somewhere a loaf was pushed a finger’s width along a board and stopped — stopped by a hand, but nobody called the stopping a figure, and nobody set a second loaf beside the first to see which had travelled the farther. Barley went from wall to wall in a woman’s two hands and never once became a number. The yard kept its accounts in weight and distance and in the warm places on a stone, and none of them wanted a figure put against them.

The large sounds were mostly weather. The river worried at its skin under the finger’s-width door; the wind came down off the sill and went around the stacked pots; a shutter knocked once somewhere and was answered by nothing. He had learned to leave the weather where it lay. A man who makes a hearing out of weather ends by putting a payer beneath it, and there was no payer in the wind. At the rail two women went on with the bucket-round as if the morning’s small commerce had never stopped, one saying the rope would take a finger off a careless hand and the other saying cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and both going back to their pails. He gave the words no owner beyond the mouths they came out of and the cold no motive. A voice in a yard is a voice in a yard, and the taking of a finger is not an argument.

He went around the seven walked-around places once and not twice. He had gone around them in the morning when the pail went down; he would go around them again at the going of the light when Sella carried him up; and between the two he went around them now in the afternoon with a look and a wide step and no number. That was the whole of it — a look, a wide step, and no number. The stone lip of the well, worn smooth on the side where knees had rested; the boards worn hollow before the kiln; the strip of hard mud that kept the print of a wheel; the place where the wheel itself had stood for twenty years with nobody filling it; the two narrow gaps between the stacked pots, each gap gone around with the same wide step; and the returning-line place, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. The wide step at that one was wider than at the others, and the widening was a keeping and not a sign. A figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. He did not look twice. Looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there, and there was no such hand to be found in the afternoon light.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty, and the Drawing’s flat knock stayed outside them on its own ground; no hearing was made of the river’s note, and the offered figure was not made a seventh of anything.
