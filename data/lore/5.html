The factions of Sarn

An account for the reader of the Compendium: the powers that teach the Warm, the powers that keep it, the powers that buy it, and the powers that have decided what should be done with it. Every faction is given its public face and its private one. Where a leader is named, the name is the one that appears on the slate.

Sarn has no dark lord. Its most frightening institution is a reasonable body of men and women with a ledger and a good reason. The most dangerous faction in the world wants to save it by making every vessel unbreakable, and it has been called mad for eight hundred years.

❦

I. The powers that keep and teach

The Warm Court — the Keeping

Kind. Bank, church and government in one; the realm’s central power. Seat. The Hearthseat on the Keel; its nearest physical presence in Grath is the factor at Greybank. Named leaders. Steward Derren, head of the Keeping; Assessor-Marshal Pell Varr, who put down the Ash Rising in Y.H. 668; Collector Marra, who writes measures and has begun to go grey at the fingertips. Goals. To keep the world alive by banking warmth faster than it leaks into the Waste, and to record every measure so that nothing is lost that has been kept. Methods. Assessment, collection, banking, deduction, the Return. Its violence is paperwork: the slate is read aloud, the account stands, and a man who refuses is clip-held by the wrist until he consents. Resources. The Hearth, the Nine Wells (five sealed), the banks at the Hearthseat and Greybank, the Kept vessels, the rolls of the dead kept open forever, and the loyalty of officers who have no family but their account. Enemies. The Waste. The Unbanked, publicly described as mad. A hot trace that will not cool. A man who looks at a dying body and says, plainly, leave it. Hidden agenda. To preserve the institution, which it has learned to identify with the world. The Court does not believe the world can be saved; it believes the counting can be continued. Those are different things, and the difference is the whole of its policy.

The College of Assessors

Kind. Academy and gatekeeper; the school where the value of a life is taught. Seat. Slate Row in the Hearthseat, with an assessor’s house in Sill built in Y.H. 776. Named leaders. Provost Wessen, who teaches Ledgerhand and the eleven words for a deduction; the Sill assessor, unnamed on the local slate. Goals. To value lives accurately and to keep the valuation method in the hands of the College alone. Methods. A three-year apprenticeship in Ledgerhand; the passive voice; the annual Weighing; a slate-name entered on the rolls in place of a birth-name. Graduates are given no house and no kin. Resources. The rolls, the Weighing notices, the sealed files, and the exclusive right to write a lawful deduction. Enemies. The Vorr, who recognise the numbers in the oldest slates and will not translate them. The Deep Hand. Anyone who can add. Hidden agenda. The College knows the printed figure of one in twelve is false. It teaches the true method to its own and prints the false one for everyone else. The calculation that a poor district can be spent is taught as an exercise.

The Order of the Tenth Clamp

Kind. Martial order and masters’ lineage; the gatekeeper of the art of Cutting. Seat. The First Seam beneath Keld Keep, with a duelling floor cut into the stone. Named leaders. Hald Iron, master of the floor; Siv of the First Seam, who keeps the crossed-out survey of Y.H. 588. Goals. To hold the passes, to keep the method of opening a way in the forearm within Keldish hands, and to make sure no lowlander learns it for free. Methods. Duels with iron clamps, a measure stated before a name, the tattoo of the number at thirteen, and a trial of hot iron for honesty which has produced a great many honest men with dead hands. Resources. The deep shafts, the warm-stone workings, Keldish mercenaries, and the only reliable teaching lineage for the first way in the world. Enemies. The Court’s assessors, whom they distrust without ever arguing with them. The Warm-stone grub. The Sweating. Hidden agenda. The Order has kept a copy of Haldre’s suppressed paper since Y.H. 710 and reads it annually. They do not intend to use it. They intend to be the only people who know it exists.

The Kiln-Masters of Sill

Kind. Craft guild; the gatekeeper of Holding in Grath. Seat. The Cross and the drying racks of the Clay Yard, Low Kiln. Named leaders. Berr Clay, the Thane of Clay, confirmed by the Court’s factor; Sella Vail, who keeps the Vail fire and has a widow’s mark. Goals. To keep the kilns firing, the First Firing meaningful, and the knowledge of the full note within the families that have earned it. Methods. Apprenticeship, the knock of the knuckle on a pot, the levy of clay and labour, and a long local memory for which household owes which. Resources. Three kiln-yards, the slip pits, the clay roads, and the Vail household’s standing across four generations. Enemies. Nine Pots, in a feud over the road that outlives the men who started it. The cupworm. The Court’s levy of warmth, which is heavier than the levy of clay. Hidden agenda. The masters know that a child with a clinical ear is worth more than a child with a good hand, and they have quietly watched Corin since the First Firing. They do not know what he is. They know what he can hear.

The Hearth-Maids

Kind. Female order of midwives, collectors’ assistants and keepers of the birth-slates. Seat. Scattered through every Grathic village; the birth-slate book is kept in the hearth-maid’s own house. Named leaders. Alda Reed, who attended Corin’s birth, crossed herself at the birth-flower, and told nobody what she had seen. Goals. To deliver children, record births within three days, and keep the small domestic knowledge of the afterdrop and the warmed stone alive. Methods. Practical instruction in proverbs and half-sentences; the stone against the belly; the slate; the crossed sign at a birth-flower. Resources. Access to every newborn vessel in Grath, the trust of households that would not let an officer into the room, and the memory of the second chill before the Court struck its name from the record. Enemies. The collectors, who arrive with a tube and take the mother’s attention. The College, which tried in Y.H. 782 to make every birth a Court entry and succeeded by paying them. Hidden agenda. The maids have kept a second set of notes since Y.H. 651: every infant who grew worse on the third day and spoke words in an unknown language. The notes are not in the Court’s file. Alda Reed’s is the fullest.

The Vorr of the Listening Stones

Kind. Ancient people and keepers of numbers; a lineage rather than a school. Seat. The Listening Stones, five leagues above Keld Keep; a few seats at the Court’s survey table. Named leaders. Ao of the Third Seam, Iyn of the Listening Stone, Eru Who Counts. Goals. To sit until the stones give up their numbers and to keep the record of the repairs without selling it. Methods. The Long Sitting at forty; a life’s patience; answers of one sentence, usually the numbers are correct, which is not a translation. Resources. The Deep Hand’s numerals, a memory of the unnumbered ages, and four thousand people who cannot be banked in the ordinary way. Enemies. The Court, which employs them and watches them. Anyone impatient. The age of the world, which is longer than any Vorr’s attention. Hidden agenda. They know the oldest repairs are not clay and have known for centuries. They will not say what they are, because the last surveyor who wrote it down was removed from the shaft in Y.H. 717.

The Dippers of Vhalen

Kind. Salt priesthood and practical school of tide-lore. Seat. The salt pans outside Vhal, and the Council’s tar-smelling room in bad weather. Named leaders. Neri Sail, chief dipper; Kett of the Quay, who keeps the tide-tallies. Goals. To bless the nets, read the Sallow draw, and keep Vhalen’s warmth from going out with the cold tides. Methods. Trailing a net in the sea at dawn; contracts on salt-paper that rot in the rain, which is considered a virtue; a law that lasts too long is a law that has stopped earning. Resources. The pans, the boats, the glass of the Glaze Cliffs, and a people who can turn a wet document into a fortune. Enemies. The Court’s assessors with their lists of deductions. The salt-thing. The winter. Hidden agenda. The dippers know that Vhalen’s Sallow has no separate Hearth, whatever the priests say. They have measured the draw. They tell the Council only what is profitable.

II. Houses, churches and powers

House Vail of Low Kiln

Kind. Kiln-house; the closest thing Grath has to a noble family. Seat. The Vail kiln-house in the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, beside the Slip Brook. Named leaders. Sella Vail, keeper of the fire; Jorra Vail, her son; the household’s dead, whose names are kept on the roll. Goals. To hold the fire, keep the wheel, and see that a Vail name is written on the birth-slate of every child born under the Vail roof. Methods. A widow’s mark, four generations of standing, inheritances settled by who has the stronger hands, and a practical refusal to discuss anything that matters. Resources. One kiln, the slip pits, a house of clay-daubed rooms, and the attention of the whole district. Enemies. The Court, which levy one in three. Nine Pots. The cupworm. The Ash ridge that grows along the skirting board in a bad year. Hidden agenda. Sella took Corin in as a foundling because a slate-name with no house can be used by anyone. She has kept the boy close and has never once asked where his memory came from. She is waiting to see what he will become.

The Sunbowl houses of Sere

Kind. Great slave-owning houses; the nobility of the southern empire. Seat. The Bowl of Sere, three hundred leagues south of Sill; provincial bowls throughout the empire. Named leaders. Alessa Sunbowl, Rial of the Ten Bowls, Ilyra Gilded. Goals. To empty vessels gloriously, to own beautiful returned slaves as proof of generosity, and to keep the northern measures flowing south at a price no poor household can refuse. Methods. The public Bowl feast, the burning of debt-slates by lot, open chattel slavery, and a law in which measure for measure means whatever the speaker has already decided. Resources. Grain, wine, oil, gold crowns, the largest slave population in the world, and the warmest funerals in Sarn, which can ruin a house. Enemies. The Court’s deduction on their grain. The Fenmarch at the Peat Line. Any slave who remembers enough to want something. Hidden agenda. The houses buy poor measures to make beautiful whole returns for their own exhibits, which means a Sere household is a machine for turning a district of thin people into one decorative person. The order is intentional and is never spoken of in the counting room, where a woman’s numbers must be read by a man.

The Temple of the Return

Kind. Public church of the Keeping; the faith taught to children. Seat. Temples in every town; the Weighing Hall in the Hearthseat is its altar. Named leaders. Hesta Clay, Voice of the Return, with the keepers of the Hearth-fire beneath her. Goals. To teach that the world was made whole, cracked once, and is being mended by the mercy of the Hearth; to make the Return the first thing a child learns and the last thing a dying person hears. Methods. Temple walls showing the single breaking; the Nine Who Kept as figures of pity and reverence; the phrase nothing is lost that we have kept; the fire behind a grating that no priest is allowed to touch. Resources. Every childhood in Sarn, the Five Cold Days, and an institution that has never needed an argument because it got there first. Enemies. The surveyors of Keld, who have seen the layers in the rim-seam. The Deep Hand. Anyone who reads the oldest repairs as language. Hidden agenda. The founding story is an invoice. The temple’s own oldest slate shows the repairs laid one over another, and the oldest of all are not the work of any god that can be named. The temple keeps the slate face down.

The Marsh Mother

Kind. Peat cult of the Fenmarch; a church of the mud. Seat. The Standing Post and the peat-dries; rites performed face down in peat. Named leaders. Bogga of the Reed, Mudge Peat, who sells the Ash the Court will not license. Goals. To keep warmth in the mud and to take it back when the household needs it; to sell cheap thin returns and ask no questions about the source. Methods. Lying face down in peat until the cold becomes ordinary; knot-sticks instead of writing; a child named by its mother before the collector arrives, which is the closest thing the Fenmarch has to a declaration of war. Resources. The peat-dries, the smugglers’ paths, cheap returns at a third of the Court’s price, and a population that can identify a collector by his boots before it can name its own children. Enemies. The draining of the fens. The Court’s lists. A winter in which the peat does not dry. Hidden agenda. The cult knows where the Ash goes, because it sells the Ash. It does not care what the Ash is, which is why it has survived where more squeamish institutions have not.

The Spending

Kind. Fire church of Sere; the doctrine of glorious emptying. Seat. Open courts with a fire in the middle, in every bowl of the empire. Named leaders. Valen Openhand, the Bowl-Priest. Goals. To teach that a vessel was made to be emptied and that a life hoarded is a life wasted. Methods. Feasting, the emptying of a public bowl of wine to announce an act of state, and the burning of one household’s debt-slates by lot at the end of a feast, after which new ones are made. Resources. Wine, music, public fires, and an audience that applauds a debt carried as heartily as a debt forgiven. Enemies. The northern habit of calling Sere spendthrifts. The Court’s tax on funeral ash. Hidden agenda. The Spending teaches that emptiness is generous so that the poor will volunteer to be emptied. The priests who teach it are among the fullest vessels in the empire.

The Council of Tides

Kind. Merchant republic; the foreign power of the Sallow coast. Seat. Vhal, in a room that smells of tar and wet rope. Named leaders. Avis Wake, Mav Hull, Dolf Brine, elected for a year at a time by ship-owners and salt-masters. Goals. To keep the salt and glass trades moving, to avoid being taxed into the Keeping, and to make sure no law lasts long enough to stop earning. Methods. Contracts written on salt-paper that rot in damp, which is considered a virtue; the pulled benches for condemned criminals; a slave who can be bought in Vhal but cannot be returned there. Resources. Ships, salt, glass, rope, dried fish, and a council that can turn a wet document into a fortune or the loss of one. Enemies. The Court’s assessors. The winter. The salt-thing. Sere’s buyers when they arrive with gold. Hidden agenda. The Council has never paid for a thin return and never will, which is why returned slaves are not housed in Vhalen. It has calculated that a person with no account is cheaper than a person with one, and calls this civility.

III. Criminals, rebels and secret societies

The Ash-runners

Kind. Criminal network; smugglers of warmth and quiet ash. Seat. The Fenmarch boardwalks and the Waiting Room at Sill, where a clerk can be paid to look away. Named leaders. Slog Eel, Hobb Mud, and a collector’s clerk in Slate Row whose name is not on any roll. Goals. To move Warm where the Court would not allow it and to sell forgetting to anyone who can pay. Methods. Cheap thin returns with no questions; quiet ash by the thimble; bone-hound ash from the shallow graves; a slate corrected in a second hand. Resources. The Fen paths, the peat-dries, a market in forgotten lives, and the complicity of people whose accounts are already damaged beyond repair. Enemies. The Court, which punishes the unlicensed sale of quiet ash with a greyed hand. The collectors, who are sometimes customers. Hidden agenda. One of their customers in Y.H. 812 paid for a whole return with the warmth of forty children of Low Kiln, and the runner who carried the slate has never been able to spell the buyer’s name. The name was Corin.

The Sere factors

Kind. Slavers and buyers of measures; the empire’s advance guard in the north. Seat. A counting house in Greybank and a shipping quay in Vhal; the Bowl of Sere behind them. Named leaders. Ilyra Gilded, Rial of the Ten Bowls, with agents in Low Kiln who never give their names. Goals. To buy poor measures at prices no household can refuse and ship them south as beautiful whole returns for the great houses. Methods. Credit, the kindness of advances, the purchase of a debt before it is due, and the quiet conversion of a district into one decorated person. Resources. Gold crowns, grain, and the cooperation of households that have already sold next year’s warmth. Enemies. Grath, which hates Sere traders and sells to them anyway. The Court, which fought a thirty-one-year War of Deductions over the same trade. Hidden agenda. A factor does not want the poor person’s warmth for its own sake. He wants the shape of it, because a beautiful whole return must remember enough to be exhibited, and only certain lives make a good show.

The Broken Slates

Kind. Rebel network in Low Kiln; descendants of the Ash Rising. Seat. The back rooms of the Vail kiln-house and the Fishstairs in Sill. Named leaders. Ennit of Low Kiln, whose family was taken in the levy of Y.H. 668; Jorra Vail, who has never said whether he is with them. Goals. To destroy the slates, stop the levy, and make it impossible to bank a person who does not consent. Methods. Broken birth-slates, a year in the clay yard for the offender, ash on the doorstep of an assessor, and a refusal that is just short of an unclosed account. Resources. The memory of ninety children taken in one levy, the silence of the hearth-maids, and the fact that a slate can be destroyed in a kiln in four minutes. Enemies. The Court, Pell Varr’s list, and the collectors who come with a slate already written. Hidden agenda. They want the Return abolished, which in Sarn means the end of the economy. They have not worked out what comes after. Their best argument is that nothing could be worse, and the Court’s sealed file suggests that they may be right.

The Unbanked

Kind. Secret faction inside the Keeping, descended from the Ash Rising and named in a Court file of Y.H. 675 as persons of the sealed opinion. Seat. No fixed seat. A locked drawer in Rusk’s room above the Fishstairs; a sealed file in the Hearthseat; the margin of a slate in Greybank signed M. Named leaders. Rusk of the Weir, who teaches a strange boy to listen to vessels; Collector Marra, who pays for a whole return with her own fingers; Haldre, whose paper The Sealed Vessel was read by nine people in Y.H. 702 and who disappeared in Y.H. 704. Goals. The Final Banking: make every vessel perfectly retentive, so that nothing leaks, nothing is owed, and no one can ever give anything away. A sealed world is an unbreakable world. Methods. Recruitment through apprenticeship, the forgery of assessments, the purchase of whole returns with memory intact, and the long cultivation of a man who can look at a dying body and decide without prayer. Resources. A collector inside the Keeping, a teacher inside the Clay Yard, a cold iron tube, a locked drawer, and a hot trace that crossed between worlds. Enemies. The Court’s public answer is that they are mad. The Court’s private answer is in the sealed file. Their real enemy is want itself, which they mean to abolish. Hidden agenda. They did not make the blizzard and they did not put the minivan on the ice. They wanted a vessel that gave at the moment of breaking, and found one in a country they could not have named. They paid to have the memories left in, which is the most expensive single purchase a banker in Sarn can make. Rusk taught Corin to make him the instrument. Marra has been paying for it ever since.

The Deep Hand

Kind. Secret society of scholars; a reading of the oldest slates. Seat. A back table in the Keld survey shaft, and one room in the Hearthseat whose key is held by an assessor. Named leaders. Ludo of the Old Kiln, a Grathic scholar who found a slate in the ash throat; Derren the Younger, an officer’s son with access to the sealed file. Goals. To read the Deep Hand and learn what the oldest repairs were made of. Methods. Copying slates that are not meant to leave the shaft; comparing the Keld translation Ash with the Court’s translation language; asking the Vorr the wrong question and recording that the numbers are correct. Resources. One slate from the Old Kiln, a crossed-out page from Y.H. 588, and the observation that a pot mended many times is not the pot it was. Enemies. The College of Assessors. The Court, which removed a surveyor from the shaft in Y.H. 717 for writing words that hold. Hidden agenda. They believe the oldest repairs are made of promises that were never kept, made solid. If this is true then the world is being held together by the very thing the Court’s economy produces, and the Keeping cannot be abolished without finding out what holds the walls up.

IV. Foreign powers at the border

Keld — the Warden of the Rim

Kind. Foreign power; mountain kingdom of iron, lead, coal and warm-stone. Seat. Keld Keep, with the First Seam beneath it and the Cold Pass ten leagues beyond. Named leaders. Karric VII, Warden of the Rim, who shut the Cold Pass in Y.H. 741; the mine-reeves beneath him. Goals. To hold the passes, keep the true Hearth claim alive, and sell iron and warm-stone to whoever pays without becoming anyone’s subject. Methods. Mercenaries, the Descent at thirteen, a measure stated before a name, and a treaty enforced at the point of a surveyor’s slate. Resources. Iron, lead, coal, warm-stone, the survey shaft, and a population that can hold a hot clamp against its palm without flinching. Enemies. The Court, which has never accepted the Hearth claim and has never bothered to argue it. The Sweating. Grathish clay-men levied into a war they did not choose. Hidden agenda. The Warden knows the true Hearth is not under his mountain. He keeps the claim because it keeps the Court out of the shafts, and because a kingdom that believes it owns the source can charge more for its stone.

Sere — the Sun-Emperor

Kind. Foreign power; hot empire of grain, wine, bowls and open slavery. Seat. The Bowl of Sere, three hundred leagues south of Sill. Named leaders. The Sun-Emperor, whose acts are announced by the emptying of a public bowl of wine; the Council of Bowls beneath him. Goals. To grow north by purchase rather than conquest, to buy measures cheaply, and to keep the Peat Line from becoming a border it has to defend. Methods. Agents with credit, the Bowl feast, a public burning of debt-slates followed by the making of new ones, and a phrase measure for measure that means whatever the emperor has already decided. Resources. Gold crowns, grain, oil, wine, fine glazed ware, and the largest slave population in the world. Enemies. The Court’s deduction on grain. The Fenmarch. The northern habit of calling Sere spendthrifts. Hidden agenda. The empire does not want territory in Grath. It wants the poor. A northern district that has been bought is more useful than a northern district that has been conquered, because nobody in a conquered district signs a contract.

Vhalen — the Council of Tides (as foreign power)

Kind. Foreign power and trading republic; the gate between Grath and the Sallow Sea. Seat. Vhal, eighty leagues north-east of Sill by the Salt Road. Named leaders. Avis Wake, Mav Hull, Dolf Brine, rotating yearly. Goals. To keep the Salt Road open, to tax nothing that can be avoided, and to make the Court’s assessors feel poor in a room full of salt-paper. Methods. Contracts that rot in damp, a welcome for any traveller who pays, and the pulled benches for those who do not. Resources. Ships, salt, glass, rope, dried fish, and a legal system in which a wet document is no document at all. Enemies. The Court’s lists. The salt-thing. A hard winter that closes the coast pass. Hidden agenda. Half the port’s fortunes have turned on a contract going damp at the right moment. The Council’s real doctrine is not the Sallow Hearth but the convenient disappearance of evidence, which it has elevated into an art and a foreign policy.
