# The long road — arc roadmap for *The Debt of Warmth* (private)

*This is the author's working map, not the reader's. It may be revised freely. The numbers, times and chapter spans are provisional; the discoveries and the losses are the spine and should be moved only with care. Early arcs cover weeks and months because every day is lived: a passage chapter carries at most seven lived days, each with its own dated entry, and a normal chapter carries one to three; childhood is not skipped but sat through. Chapter 15 closes on Day 74, Silt 15, Y.H. 813, with the boy seventy-four days old, two months and fourteen days, one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed, the flood up and down and gone into mud, the weak board gone or trodden flat with no accident made of it, the honeyed ice, the stopping hairlines and the early Singing Sill note renamed past tense as the thaw that was, the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it both unswept, six hearings kept beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, a child's cold foot called the cold by one woman and weather by the yard and filed as the seed of a beast years away (T093), Alda's sixth note still unread, the pewter tin above the empty bed unopened, both cords and the knot-string unread, the broken birth-slates under the rack, the carried fire at four, four and a gap, the clerk still set at the Fishstairs, and the Greybank saying filed on its own tray with no cause written across it --- nothing joined, paid, explained or ended; Chapter 16 opens on Day 75, Silt 16, in the mud the flood left, with the boy carried to the edge of the yard's sweeping at a half-pace from every open slate and the count going on unspent and exact against the house's bad arithmetic. Chapter 11 closed on Day 47 with a coal gone upriver with a brine-gull and the shape of the forty-first knot legible without its name; Chapter 10 closed on Day 44 with the knot tied and the word *forty* spoken into the basket. The first twelve arcs take Corin from a cracked, unranked infant to a man the Court can no longer place in any ledger. The saga does not end there.*

**Pacing note.** The provisional `first_appearance` chapter numbers on the character sheets are planting markers, not promises. As each arc is planned into chapters, those markers are to be reconciled with the day-count in `bible/calendar.md` and updated on the sheets. Nothing in this file overrides canon in `bible/` or `memory/places.md`; where the roadmap and the bible disagree, the bible wins and this file is corrected.

The nine named ranks are trades, not prizes: Thin, Whole, Opened, Held, Drawn, Banked, Welled, Hearthward, Kept. Corin begins below Thin. He never reaches Kept: to do so would be to win the world's highest rank and lose the willingness to look at a body and say *leave it*.

## Arc 1 — The Cold Stone
*Subtitle: a warm stone, already going cold*

**Approximate in-world time.** Days 1–about 93 (Stone 7, Y.H. 812 through the Five Cold Days, the first thaw and the Fish Blessing on Ilmen 15, Day 44, the year's flood of Silt and the first planting week of Seed, Y.H. 813). Roughly three months, every day dated. The Silt flood of Chapter 15 is the year's flood, not the great Breaking of Arc 3 years hence, when the low houses are taken and the town's thin place is named.

**Central conflict.** An infant with a trauma surgeon's memories must survive the first weeks of his own cold, cracked, low-capacity vessel while a household already hollowed by loss decides whether to keep him. Beneath that: someone has written his name on a slate before seeing his face, and forty children have been spent to pay for it.

**Major beats.**
1. Birth on Stone 7. Alda Reed delivers a child with a branching red flower across his ribs and forearm, crosses herself, and says nothing. The stone pressed to his belly is already cooling. Dan's last Chicago moment — orange light on snow, a grey coat at the edge of the flare light writing without stopping, a phone lit under a sheet with a name he will not look at — breaks into the birth through the present-tense seam.
2. The double consciousness: he counts the nails in the floorboards (four, four, a knot the width of a thumb), knows the afterdrop when he sees it, wants the breast. The surgeon's judgment and the infant's hunger quarrel on the page, and the body wins, loudly.
3. Day 3. Sella Vail carries a covered bundle up the Clay Yard. Alda tells Dessa Silt her child went in the levy, among the Forty. The lie is told for mercy and will curdle for years.
4. Jorra says *he's not Vail* and then says nothing about it for years. Tarn is given the loft while the boy is given a bed. Dan notices, with the part of him that was a surgeon, that a poor household has just performed triage on its own members and called it kindness.
5. Garl's objects: the wheel-seat worn hollow on the left, the salt-stained coat on the peg behind the door, the Ash ridge Sella will not sweep. The first story of a man who was left where he lay and whose world did not end.
6. Account-day at the Cross, Stone 10. Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron before touching the slate and says *one in twelve, as the notice says* in a voice that stops the shouting; the figure is read twice and accepted, and nobody addresses the boy it will carry. Ostrowski's remembered voice corrects a full name in a room that is becoming a clay yard, and Vesci's grammar goes outward in Berr's *kindness is entered*. The bell at the Cross strikes on through the market, longer than a church bell, and nobody explains it; a barrow of grey goes out toward the Bone Orchard unread.
7. The Hearth Drawing, Stone 12 (written). A collection witnessed from a basket: the cold iron tube, the small flat knock of a vessel giving way --- the fifth hearing and the first from a person --- a rekindled child worse on the third day. The midwives call it the second chill. Something in Dan recognises a second cause and cannot name it. The collector has grey fingertips and a reason, and is neither Marra nor the Cross clerk (T080); the death-house is the same room as the birth room and the kiln-house, with the same gap in the count (T081).
8. The Five Cold Days. Kindling, Weighing, a whole return announced in a clean voice for somebody else, the Long Night, the Remembering, bread baked on the Last Day and the Ash left in the corners. Dan reaches for a pocket with no phone in it.
9. Dawn at the Fishstairs. Mira Silt sells eels and rumours, flicks her eel-bones, keeps a knot-string, will not sit where a slate is open. Dessa's cough is a vessel going thin and everyone calls it the pits.
10. The Fish Blessing at the Weir, Ilmen 15. Orra Weir ties a forty-first knot she cannot account for, says the word *forty* within the boy's hearing, and watches what he does with it. He is too young to answer. He does not forget the shape of the cord.

**Introduced.** Sella Vail, Alda Reed, Dessa Silt, Jorra Vail, Tarn, House Vail, Berr Clay, Mira Silt, Ulda, Orra Weir, the Fishstairs and the Weir. The Vail house's private roll and Tarn's unspoken ear enter with it on Day 2, before either can be understood; the charred slate lies in the kiln ash from the first scene. The first life enters as voices and light only: Nina's lit phone, Des's laugh, Ostrowski's full name, Vesci's warm *butcher*, Tasha's chart, the crooked casket.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Alda's mercy-lie to Dessa is the first betrayal and the pattern of the book. Dessa dies uncollected in the clay-heavy Slip Brook on Day 38; the child is held on the bank and hears the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through; the collector's clerk is at the Fishstairs and Alda arrives after the vessel has given. Love arrives as maintenance: a stone warmed for a cot, a coat held against a draught, a fire banked when there is no reason to bank it.

**The twist.** The reader is allowed to believe, with the district, that the boy was one of the Forty. In retrospect the birth scene reveals the other story: the woman in the grey coat in a country she cannot name, writing his name before she had seen his face, and a warmth that would not cool.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Unranked and cracked. The first actual hearings of the flat note come on Days 2–3 --- a cup across the birth room, then a pot in the Vail house --- and on Day 4 the district's own reading slate answers flat twice; on Days 5–6 a Vail pot through the wall and a dying neighbour under the collector's tube at the Hearth Drawing bring the count to five hearings from four sources, the fifth the first from a person, and Tarn's broom stops at the pot without anyone naming the sound. He cannot reach any of them or make anyone tap one; he counts without deciding to and recognises the afterdrop. He hears the full note and the flat before anyone taps. Cost: infancy. The body cannot act on any of it; he drools, cannot roll, cannot reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin; the stone at his belly goes cold and is left behind in House Silt, and nobody notices but him.

**Chapter 5 revision.** The first nine days close with the ear witnessed: Tarn sees the boy turn toward a pot before it is set down and keeps silent, so two ears now live under one roof without a word between them --- the loan that can hear is known to be able to hear, which raises its price years before Ulda sells it. A note expected and not given becomes the first object in the private file that never happened, planting the interval that will one day be the nearly-full note of the third thing. The Fishstairs and the market's edge add a second kind of keeper, one whose accounts are carried in grey wool and eel-bones and in a second cord never taken to Nine Pots, and put the boy among the bits in Dessa's count without anyone knowing it is an account. Nothing is joined; the gap stays the part nobody has thought to enter.

**Chapter 6 revision.** The first tend of Stone closes with the boy fourteen days old and the household's arithmetic made visible in objects: a kindling stack measured four, four and an empty fifth, loaves arranged so the days to Kindling are a shape no collector can take up, a seventh year held back, and a fist closed around the number none will say. The ear gains no sixth hearing but a second interval, filed beside the first on a separate tray; the woman in the grey coat's pause is seen a fourth time at the Court's table and lies unjoined beside the other three, while the Drawing collector's hand with no pause stays in its own place. Alda writes a second note and turns it face-down toward a floorboard, and crosses the sign at his wrist without knowing it, covered with a date nobody asked for. Tarn goes still for two breaths at a kindness he cannot enter; Sella names the kiln's breath as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End calls a death from it merciful. A hairline in the river ice is held up to the light and read as weather while the yard argues about an early or late thaw because the levy falls in it. A neighbour back from the Court's room describes bench, pot, draught and gap, and the boy keeps the room in four separate places rather than write a cause where he has only noticed a likeness. The crust goes from warm to cold in the fold and stays where it was put. Nothing is joined, paid or closed; the Five are eleven days off and the boy is the only person in Low Kiln keeping the number exactly.

**Chapter 7 revision.** The last tend of Stone is counted and the year's last market closes with nothing explained. The pause in the neat dark hand comes a fifth time at the market's edge, in one untagged beat of orange flare light on wet snow, and changes the count and nothing else. Sella measures the ridge by thumb against the death-house ridge, lets Berr go around Garl's uncalled debt, and sets the boy on his bed under the roof with both crusts in the fold and the fire keeping four, four and a gap. Kindling is tomorrow. Two threads are planted toward the day a crust goes missing (T086) and the sound that will come from a person on the Slip Brook (T087); no thread is closed.

**Chapter 8 revision.** The Five Cold Days are lived one dated day at a time and the year turns on Ilmen 1 without anything being explained. The year's fire comes up the Clay Yard in a clay dish, passed from hearth to hearth, and enters the house from a source nobody names or prices --- a new plant, T088, toward the far-off naming of the Hearth as a wound. The boy extends the count to its light with a hypothetical fifth that is not a sixth hearing, and at the Weighing hears the Cross grammar wearing a temple's clothes in an officer's clean voice, a whole return announced for somebody absent and a name the household will not repeat; he files the silence after the name among the gaps and sets the Court's room, heard as a voice through a doorway with bench, pot, draught and a missing line, beside the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house as a fourth likeness without writing a cause. The Long Night gives him stories whose gaps have the shape of vessels' intervals and a bell told four, four and a gap which he keeps apart from the bell he heard, while cold comes at cheek, temple and wrist and neither breast nor crust is given. On the Remembering Garl's name is not spoken and Alda lays dates where a name should stand, crossing two fingers at the birth-flower without knowing it; the pocket-place holds wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain and the boat promise comes in past tense, untagged. On the Last Day no third crust enters the fold; Mira shows the second cord twisted twice with its knots uncounted, and Sella measures both ridges with one thumb and leaves the Ash where it lies, then burns it on Ilmen 1 and lets a neighbour call it dealt with while she says nothing. Because the coals are still banked the mark stays untouched across the turn of the year, one in twelve stays printed and unchallenged, and the boy carries his count --- five hearings from four sources and no sixth, five unjoined pauses, the told bell beside the heard bell and the empty fifth --- into Y.H. 813. No power moved, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, and nothing joined or closed. The story has not grown in a new direction; the carried fire and the clean voice are two more ways for an institution and a household to keep a warmth nobody is allowed to price.

**Chapter 9 revision.** The first seven days of Ilmen are lived one dated entry at a time and the thaw comes on in small things the district refuses to name. At the well a woman says the water tastes different and a man says the ice is honeyed under the mud; the warm spell becomes weather by the time the bucket goes around again, and the boy keeps the shape of the unnamed thing because keeping a shape is the only work available to him. **Dangers.** Cold, the kiln's breath, ice-mice in the thatch, the second chill, thin ice on the Slip Brook, cupworm in the drying racks, and above all the collector who comes with a slate already written. The truest danger is being seen.

**How it changes him.** He learns that competence without power is only a more humiliating kind of helplessness, and that institutions and households alike call their cruellest arithmetic kindness. He begins to keep the only record nobody can tax: attention.

## Arc 2 — The First Firing
*Subtitle: a child makes a pot, and the pot answers*

**Approximate in-world time.** Roughly twelve years of lived days, from the thaw of Y.H. 813 to the First Firing. These are the saga's densest passage chapters: every day has its dated entry, and childhood is not summarised.

**Central conflict.** A cracked, low-capacity vessel must learn to stop leaking while every adult who notices his ear is weighing whether he is worth more than he costs.

**Major beats.**
1. At the kiln he finds the charred edge of a slate Garl burnt, with a spiral written on the back by a man who thought he was going mad and a woman who thought she was burning rubbish. He cannot read it. He copies it.
2. At the Cross, Fenna Clay taps a cup with her knuckle. He has already tapped it and says it will crack at the handle in a season. It answers flat. It is the first time anyone else has heard what he hears.
3. The kiln-masters notice the ear. Berr Clay offers him a place among them, in good faith, in exchange for a silence about what he hears. He refuses; Berr enters the refusal on a slate rather than taking offence.
4. Ulda sells his first small secret to a Waiting Room clerk for a loaf: the Vail foundling hears the full note before the pot is tapped. The clerk writes it down. Ulda enters the loaf nowhere.
5. Rusk begins teaching at the Fishstairs. *What do you hear? No. Listen again. What do you hear now that you've stopped trying to be clever?* Every lesson is true and every lesson is a lever.
6. He learns the banked breath by watching potters turn the drying racks away from the fire, and stops his own leak by brute, painful attention. The flaw remains under the repair. The note is nearly full and never quite.
7. Mira keeps barley and Orra keeps knots: two records of the Forty in materials no Court can confiscate. He learns that a district keeps its own accounts in knot-string and silence.
8. Ash-mites in the skirting board drink the thin edge of a sleeper's measure. He tries to drain a corner, seal it, keep a room clean. The Ash returns in a single night, heavier. His first experiment is a failure and somebody else sleeps in the room.
9. He tries to grow something in a dark cup against a wound that has turned. Sometimes it kills the rot; sometimes it kills the patient. He does not yet know why, and records both outcomes.
10. The First Firing: he makes a pot in the Vail yard and knocks it with a knuckle. The household holds a feast. He is still Corin, a foundling with no house-name, and the ear has made him valuable and therefore unsafe.

**Introduced.** Fenna Clay and the Glaze Room, Rusk of the Weir in full, the Kiln-Masters of Sill, the Broken Slates, Ulda's Waiting Room clerk, the habit of the second record.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Ulda's first sale; Jorra handing a slate with Corin's name to people who burn slates; Sella's private roll entering the boy as *the fire's, not the house's*. Love is the shared ear with Fenna — a river between two districts and a class between two lives, neither said aloud.

**The twist.** The gift everyone envies is not a gift but a surgical habit, twenty years of finding the thin place under the skin. And the boy who cannot hold warmth is the one who will one day refuse to let the world decide who is beyond keeping.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Thin, holding. He stops his own leak and answers a pot's note correctly. Cost: a childhood in the clay yard; the Vail household's patience; Ulda's first treason; the permanent knowledge that he is worth more than he costs and that someone has decided what to do about it.

**Dangers.** Cupworm, kiln's breath, lead-rats, sweet-sickness in High Kiln, the Broken Slates, Berr's good offer, the clerk's slate, the Ash-mites, a mould he cannot control.

**How it changes him.** Attention becomes a form of love and a record becomes an act of refusal. He also learns to be frightened of being valued, which is the beginning of his suspicion of every kindness in Sarn.

## Arc 3 — The Weir
*Subtitle: the river takes what the Court doesn't*

**Approximate in-world time.** About two years, ages twelve to fourteen.

**Central conflict.** Can a vessel hold under ordinary strain, and can a boy read another vessel by touch without a master's permission — in a district where the collector can arrive with a slate already written?

**Major beats.**
1. He walks the Weir at dusk and learns to listen to water and ice the way Rusk reads a weir: where the water slows, what has been chewed, the note a thing makes when tapped.
2. Rusk's questions about the locked drawer; the lesson that a way once cut stays cut; the boy's first deliberate refusal to answer a question he knows is a lever.
3. Pia of the Quay comes up the Salt Road with salt and glass, sells him the same herring twice, asks what a cup sounds like when it holds, and keeps the answer on a slate-rubbing in a dry place.
4. The Singing Sill gives its low note under the water in Ilmen. He hears a second note beneath the first: the town itself, held together by streets and water, with Low Kiln as its crack.
5. Orra ties and reties the forty-first knot and watches him with the word. He cannot answer. The watching does not stop.
6. The Breaking: the Ilm comes up over the Fishstairs and takes the low houses, silt waist-deep. He hears the note of a whole district under strain and understands that a place is a vessel with an enormous capacity and a slow, certain failure.
7. A silt-leach fastens on a child wading below the sill; he finds it by the cold in one foot that does not go away, and learns where a beast's ways run.
8. He holds under ordinary strain for the first time and can read a graft by touch. Whole. The Vail household does not notice for a season.
9. Berr renews his offer in good faith; the boy refuses again; Berr enters the second refusal on a slate and goes on being reasonable.
10. Sella's silence starts to look like a wall he will spend years climbing. He begins to understand that she knew his mother.

**Introduced.** Pia of the Quay and the Salt Road, the Singing Sill and the Breaking as physical events, the town as a vessel, the full grammar of Rusk's teaching.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Pia is the first door to the wider world, and the twice-sold herring is the first contract. Sella's kindness curdles in retrospect when he learns she paid Dessa in barley to stay away. The love of a teacher begins to feel like a debt with no date on it.

**The twist.** Low Kiln is the flaw in Sill that the rest of the town agrees not to mention. Cooling the quarter cools the whole account, and the gateway house does not notice until a season has gone.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Whole. Hold under ordinary strain; read another vessel by touch. Cost: the First Firing itself, twelve years of being watched, and the knowledge that the household's silence about his origin is a debt he cannot enter in any account.

**Dangers.** Reed-wolves in the marsh, ice-mice, silt-leach, thin ice, the Breaking, Rusk's drawer, the Court's factor at Greybank, the collector's schedule.

**How it changes him.** He learns his first political fact through his hands: the poor quarter is the crack in the town, and a repair that ignores the crack is a lie told in the language of mercy.

## Arc 4 — The Clamp and the Graft
*Subtitle: a tourniquet with a different name*

**Approximate in-world time.** About one year; the journey to Keld by the Rim Road and back.

**Central conflict.** To cut a way and move Warm deliberately within the self, in a world where the only reliable teaching lineage keeps the method as a guild secret and states a man's measure before his name.

**Major beats.**
1. The Rim Road with a warm-stone; the first sight of the Rim and the Cold Pass; the mountains swelling in the deep mines.
2. Keld Keep: a man states his measure before his name. The lowlander gives a number, is checked, and is found interesting because he gave the wrong one with confidence.
3. Hald Iron on the duelling floor: *Number first. Then the name.* He offers to cut the first way and names the honest price — a fortnight of uselessness, a permanent mark, a way that never closes — and withholds the other price.
4. The cut: a way opened in the forearm by cutting across a surface way and keeping it open while it scars. Pain, a mark, a fortnight in which the arm is useless. He watches the old man's fingers grey at the tips.
5. Siv of the First Seam listens to the wall before he looks at the visitor, and gives a number where the boy expected an answer.
6. The crossed-out survey of Y.H. 588: a returned man with a branching red mark on forearm and ribs, the entry struck through by a surveyor who thought better of writing the shape. The boy does not know what it means. The ground shifts anyway.
7. A clamp-duel witnessed: a way opened in the forearm, a man down, the long ugly interval of dragging and breathing and trying to stop the bleeding. He notes exactly where the ways run close to the skin.
8. Haldre's paper in a tin lined with warm-stone, read once on the Last Day; eight copies burned, the ninth kept. Hald reads it and intends never to use it.
9. A Sweating in the deep seam: the smell of hot lead, warm-stone grubs in the working, men who cannot climb out fast enough. He survives by being further down and by knowing there were minutes, not hours.
10. Home. He can move Warm within himself. He sets a clamp across a dying man's forearm and watches the limb below go the colour of old rope, and says nothing, because here they call stopping a flow mercy.

**Introduced.** Hald Iron, Siv of the First Seam, the Order of the Tenth Clamp, the First Seam, the Vorr as numbers, the Sweating, the seam-serpent as a change in the numbers.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Hald's honest partial price is a kindness with a blade: truth enough to consent, not enough to refuse. Siv's crossed-out page is the first thing a lifelong keeper has given away. Neither man says what he thinks of the boy's mark.

**The twist.** The first way is not an achievement but a permanent wound, and the Order has kept Haldre's paper for a century precisely so it will never have to be used. A method kept unused is not knowledge; it is a debt.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Opened. Cut and use a way in the forearm; move Warm within the self. Cost: a permanent scar; a fortnight of uselessness; Rusk's attention sharpened by the new scar; the first memory of Chicago noticeably thinner, though he cannot say which one.

**Dangers.** Clamp-duels, the Sweating, seam-serpents drinking through the stone, warm-stone grubs, Keldish suspicion, the cold of the Rim Road, and a way that once cut is never closed.

**How it changes him.** He learns that every method is a debt and that a guild's secrecy is a form of accounting. The boxer's chin earns its keep: he learns to take one punch and wait, and never becomes a fighter.

## Arc 5 — Low Kiln
*Subtitle: forty measures, one return*

**Approximate in-world time.** About one year.

**Central conflict.** Can he clamp, graft and carry beyond his natural measure — and can he refuse a collection in a world where refusal is the crime of an unclosed account?

**Major beats.**
1. Marra at the collector's table in Slate Row: cuffs buttoned to the wrist, the collector's habit of touching the slate before the person, fingertips the colour of wet ash, an apology that breaks off in an unfinished clause.
2. Rennick tapping chalk against the slate frame, offering a correct figure as a gift to a household that will pay for it. He keeps coming back to forty: why forty unspent measures for one intact one?
3. The Forty of Low Kiln named as a single fact: Orra's cord, Mira's barley, Alda's lie, Berr's signed slate, Marra's forged assessment with the deduction entered as zero, Veno's unspellable purchaser, Derren's sealed entry. The boy who was pitied as one of the Forty learns he was the reason for them.
4. Ulda sells the mark under the sleeve to the Waiting Room clerk. Collectors come for Corin. Ulda discovers that he has not protected the boy at all, and begins the long business of trying to be useful to the person he betrayed.
5. Tarn refuses a whole return — the first refusal of the Court's best gift in Low Kiln, years before Dan's more famous one, and therefore almost remembered by nobody.
6. Dan sets a graft on a dying neighbour, holds pressure until the vessel takes, and greys at the fingertips. The donor's measure was shaped differently; the recipient starts to want a life that was not hers.
7. He refuses a collection once. Nothing happens. The world does not end. That nothing is the crack in the whole structure.
8. The district's distrust hardens: he is now the boy who can hear and therefore the boy who can be spent. Berr watches him with a kiln-master's eye and calls it an investment.
9. Sella shows him the second household roll, in which Garl and Enna are entered and he is entered as *the fire's, not the house's*. The wall cracks an inch.
10. Alda's tin under the floorboard is nearly found; the second notes are nearly read; he stops himself, not yet able to bear what a woman who lied for mercy wrote down.

**Introduced.** Collector Marra in the flesh, Rennick, the Waiting Room, the Forty as a completed fact, the crime of the unclosed account.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Ulda's betrayal lands and does not land as a speech, only as an affectionate sentence Dan finishes for him years later. Tarn's refusal is the moral plant. Marra's fingers are entered nowhere, including by her.

**The twist.** The most expensive single purchase in Sarn was made with the warmth of forty children, and the assessment was forged by the woman who wrote the collector's reports the College most admires. A whole return costs more than a poor district's yearly warmth; the difference is the district.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Held. Clamp, graft, carry beyond his measure; refuse a collection. Cost: grey fingertips; the Forty of Low Kiln; a district's distrust; the first understanding that his gift is an instrument someone sent for.

**Dangers.** The collector's tube, clip-holding by the wrist, the Broken Slates, Rennick's arithmetic, Marra's exactness, Ash-wraiths after a levy, a mismatched graft and the want it leaves behind.

**How it changes him.** He discovers that the world's founding mercy is optional, and that *leave it* is the most dangerous sentence in Sarn because it is the one sentence with no passive form. He begins to understand that a no with no alternative is not yet an answer.

## Arc 6 — Slate Row
*Subtitle: eleven words for a deduction and none for sorry*

**Approximate in-world time.** About two years.

**Central conflict.** To learn to read a Sarnish ledger and drive a flow outward — and to learn what the printed figure is built to hide.

**Major beats.**
1. Wessen turns a slate face-down before he speaks to a boy, so the boy understands he is not being entered. He teaches Ledgerhand. The price is a slate-name where a birth-name was, and one memory burned thin in the Weighing Hall. He will not say which memory.
2. Dan cannot add, but he can hear a cracked vessel through a wall. Wessen is interested in the disagreement between the two facts, which is how he becomes a teacher rather than an examiner.
3. Rennick's rivalry: admiration curdling into a need to verify. *You heard the flat note before I tapped it. I'd like to know how you did it. Not to take it. I want to know whether it's a method.*
4. The printed one in twelve against the roll's one in three. The difference is not an error but a method, and the College teaches the true method only to those who will never print it.
5. The Weighing Hall: Hearth-fire behind cold iron, the Weighing read aloud in a clean voice. A memory burns thin — a memory of Chicago he will not know he has lost, so that when he tries to picture his mother's kitchen there is a door-shaped gap where the oregano jar was.
6. The outward way: he opens a way and pushes a flow out of the body at short range. Range a few paces; the caster is cold afterwards; somebody must pay to rewarm him. He is careful whose heat pays.
7. He takes Marra's wrist to check a pulse and finds the colour underneath. *I don't remember my fingers.* He understands that she means *I do*, and that she has been paying for him with them for twelve years.
8. Berr says *the account stands* and means it; Dan hears Vesci's warm *that's what a chart is for* inside the sentence and understands they are the same sentence spoken in two rooms.
9. He reads his own return: the deduction entered as zero, the name written before the face, a whole return booked at twelve hundred marks and forty unspent measures, purchaser not recorded.
10. He copies the true figure, burns the copy, and then keeps a second copy. He has learned the habit of the district: a record the institution cannot own.

**Introduced.** Wessen, the College of Assessors, the Weighing Hall, the Hearth-fire, the true deduction, the shape of a method that cannot be taught.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Wessen's teaching is a gift that takes a self; Marra's fingers confessed in a physical detail rather than a confession; Rennick's gift of a correct figure to a household that will pay for it.

**The twist.** The deduction is not a tax. It is the machinery by which the poor are spent, and the printed notice is a founding invoice dressed as mercy. The passive voice is not a protection but a tool, like the clamp and the cold iron tube.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Drawn. Direct a flow outward; read a ledger; understand the deduction. Cost: a memory burned thin in the Weighing Hall (one of the last clean Chicago memories), a slate-name in place of a name, and the knowledge that the institution's most ordinary sentence is a threat.

**Dangers.** The College, the penalty of a false measure (a struck hand), Rennick's arithmetic, the Hearth-tongue reaching back at a drawer, the greyed hand for unlicensed quiet ash.

**How it changes him.** He begins to keep second records on purpose and to hear the grammar of his own first life in the grammar of the Keeping. The surgeon who once let a chart close a matter now reads every chart against the roll.

## Arc 7 — The War of Measures
*Subtitle: a district cold for a season*

**Approximate in-world time.** Two to three years; a war fought over who may be banked and whose measures may be bought.

**Central conflict.** Can he store Warm outside his body and move it through clay and iron while nations decide, with perfect courtesy, who gets to be whole?

**Major beats.**
1. Veno Brine in the Greybank counting house: the damp document, the kindness with a figure attached, the private drawer of dry documents he never touches in company.
2. Sere factors buy Low Kiln's measures with credit, the kindness of advances, the purchase of a debt before it is due. Households sell next year's warmth and call it a gift from the Hearth.
3. Solene Ember glimpsed in a room with good light, removing a ring one at a time while she tells a visitor which death she means. *A beautiful return is a crack covered with lead. You've seen it in your pots. I'm the pot.*
4. The war of collectors and lists: no great battles, but burned kilns, poisoned wells, a district made thin by drinking from the Ferr, households entered and re-entered. Dan triages a field of the wounded and is sickened by how good he is at it.
5. He makes a cup-bank in clay and learns that the store leaks unless tended, and that every ember in it is somebody's warmth.
6. A great banking leaves a district cold for a season. He hears the note of a whole street fall flat under his knuckle and cannot un-hear it. The bill is named, and the name is not his.
7. A hand he cannot save: a graft set from the wrong donor, a mismatched measure that wants a life not its own. The Second arrives in the Afterdrop and hesitates at the wrong moment.
8. He stores Warm outside the body in clay and iron and spends it through another. Banked. He lives off an account that is not his, which is the condition of every Banked vessel in Sarn.
9. Marra's slate and Veno's damp copy of the purchaser's name come within a hand's breadth of each other in a counting house; Veno cannot spell the name and lets it go.
10. He counts the exits in every room he enters. The surgeon's tic has become a strategy, and he no longer tries to stop it.

**Introduced.** Veno Brine, Solene Ember as a glimpse, the Sere factors, the War of Measures, the cup-bank and the cost of a great working.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** A hand lost to a mismatched graft; a district's season of cold; Solene as the first adult love who arrives already wounded and already older in every way that matters.

**The twist.** The war is not about territory. The empire wants the poor, not the land: a district that has been bought is more useful than one conquered, because nobody in a conquered district signs a contract. A beautiful whole return is one decorated person built out of a district of thin ones.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Banked. Store Warm outside the body; move it through clay and iron. Cost: a district cold for a season; a hand he could not save; the first time he is responsible for a bill he cannot pay and must carry.

**Dangers.** Sere buyers, Keldish mercenaries, the salt-thing on the Sallow, the bank's leak, the Second, assessors with lists, the kiln's breath in a closed counting room.

**How it changes him.** He learns that scale defeats him: one tired man with a clinical ear cannot out-work a bank. From here on he stops trying to win the account and starts trying to change what an account is.

## Arc 8 — Three Times Returned
*Subtitle: a beautiful return is a crack covered with lead*

**Approximate in-world time.** About two years.

**Central conflict.** To draw on the stores the world already holds — and to love a woman who may be poured a fourth time into a body that will not know him.

**Major beats.**
1. Solene's three deaths told in a warm Sere voice, naming which return each belongs to. She asks everyone what debt they intend to carry; in the north they think it is small talk.
2. The romance begins in adulthood: restraint, the rings removed one at a time, the shift of weight, the thing said quietly about a crack. She cannot admit a gift because Vhaleni and Sere both have taught her to enter everything, and he cannot offer one because he has no house.
3. Fenna Clay tells him the composition of the Court's fine ware and what lead does to a glaze that hides a flaw. It is the first physical answer he has ever had to a political question, and the answer he most trusts.
4. Solene's hands unsteady on the third day after a feast: the lead in the cup, not the price of beauty. She has known for nine years and has said nothing, because saying it would cost her the room with good light.
5. He follows a way out of his body into the world and finds a source that answers — a sealed spring on the Keel. The level drops for everyone. The key is not his.
6. Welled. He draws on stores held in the world itself without owning them. The Vorr say he has borrowed from a debt that was not his, which is not a translation and is exact.
7. Overreach: he tries to correct the current, to draw more than the world can spare. The level drops in a district; the walls begin to sing; he cracks with a small flat knock and is hollow for a time.
8. Most of the arc is spent recovering: grey fingers, a tremor, a chill food does not fix, a click in a knee that was not there before, sleep that thins the memory further.
9. He recognises the Second Want in Solene — the shape of a former owner trying to resume itself in the Afterdrop — and understands that he cannot promise her a whole fourth return. The one thing he most wants to mend is the one thing he cannot.
10. He learns that a person returned three times is not less real than a person returned once, which is the same argument he will one day make about the world.

**Introduced.** Solene in full, the Nine Wells and their keys, the Vorr's one-sentence answers, the Bowl of Sere as an idea, the true nature of a beautiful return.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Veno intends to sell Solene's fourth return to a house that wants a beautiful calm exhibit that remembers nothing. The love he cannot fix by mending is the lesson he most needs and least wants.

**The twist.** The calm of a beautiful return is a burn. Memory thins first, then appetite, then the sense of self, then the walls; the rich pay to lose less, and call the result whole.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Welled, then a setback. Draw on the world's stores; recognise the Second Want in the woman he loves. Cost: overreach cracks him; most of the arc is recovery; the level drops in a district that never agreed to pay, and he must learn the payer's name.

**Dangers.** The sealed springs and their keys, the Vorr's silence, the Hearth-tongue, the cold when the draw goes wrong, mire-fever in the Fenmarch, the Fourth Return.

**How it changes him.** He learns that taking from the world is the same act as taking from a person, and that a love he cannot repair is still a love. The habit of counting what each working costs becomes a moral practice rather than a professional one.

## Arc 9 — The Teacher's Debt
*Subtitle: did I teach you what to do with what you heard?*

**Approximate in-world time.** About one year; the middle war and its aftermath.

**Central conflict.** To hold the well-draw without taking more than the world can spare — and to survive learning that the teacher who made him is the man who intends to seal the world.

**Major beats.**
1. He holds the well-draw so that the level no longer drops, and for the first time glimpses a repair that takes nothing from anyone: a clamp that stops a flow without stopping the want.
2. Rusk becomes gentle, which is the most alarming thing about him. *Don't thank me. Thank the boy you'll be when you've decided whether to say leave it out loud.*
3. The locked drawer opened too late: Haldre's *The Sealed Vessel*, a list of nine names with eight struck out, and a rubbing of a branching red mark taken from a forearm in the First Seam in Y.H. 588.
4. The betrayal. In the middle war Rusk hands Corin to the people who intend to seal the world and believes until the last moment that he is doing it for the boy's own good.
5. Rusk asks one last question and answers it himself, badly, as he has always done. He does not become a monster. He becomes a tired man who has decided a sealed world is worth one more spent life.
6. Marra's fingers understood at last; her apology broken off at the same place; Dan supplies the words she cannot, in the plainest terms, and the world does not end.
7. Siv's crossed-out page and Rusk's rubbing and the flower on his own ribs: the same shape in three hands. He is not an accident.
8. The names of the others begin to surface: a mender who made the repair beautiful and lied, one who made it honest and let the world break, one who became the Court.
9. Hald Iron says *that will hold* and then watches it not hold, and does not comfort him, because comfort is a lowland habit and the floor has no use for it.
10. He comes out of the war with the well-draw held and with a teacher who is now an enemy who loves him, which is the hardest fact in the book to put down.

**Introduced.** The Unbanked as a named fact, Haldre and his paper, the nine names, the concept of the Final Banking in plain words.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Rusk's betrayal is the central wound. Marra's unfinished apology is completed by the person she spent. The love of a teacher is proved to be a lever, which does not make it untrue.

**The twist.** The man who taught him to decide without prayer is the man who wants him to decide that the whole world is better sealed. Every lesson was true and every lesson was a lever toward that ending.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Welled, restored. Hold the well-draw without taking more than the world can spare. Cost: Rusk's betrayal; the drawer opened too late; the loss of the last illusion that his gifts were his alone or that anyone who loves him is safe to trust.

**Dangers.** The Unbanked, the quiet seal, a Welled overreach that takes a district, the people who intend to spend him, and his own increasing coldness, which feels to him like competence.

**How it changes him.** He learns that a teacher can love a student and ruin him in the same sentence. He begins to build a moral code that does not depend on anyone's approval, which is the beginning of the third thing.

## Arc 10 — The Sealed Opinion
*Subtitle: nothing is lost that we have kept*

**Approximate in-world time.** About two years.

**Central conflict.** To draw from the Hearth without becoming one of the polite, empty people who can no longer remember why they came into a room — and to read the sealed file.

**Major beats.**
1. Steward Derren in a room gone cold, writing down a boy's refusal while the boy is still saying it. *You have said a thing that cannot be entered. I have entered it anyway. That is my kindness.*
2. Derren's grammar: one in twelve printed, one in three done; *the world cannot be saved; the counting can be continued.* Dan hears Vesci in every sentence.
3. He reaches the Keel and the Weighing Hall; the Hearth-tongue behind cold iron; a way cut that reaches the Hearth's head. A memory each drawing.
4. He is never cold, and learns that this is a kind of death. A Hearthward practitioner is a library burning itself to light a room.
5. The names of the previous menders: one made the repair beautiful and lied; one made it honest and let the world break; one became the Court. The branching flower appears on men who have never met each other.
6. The sealed file read: the Hearth is a wound, the Keeping is salvage from a failing repair, the Ash is what is left of a self and also the oldest repair under the world.
7. Haldre's paper in Derren's drawer and Hald's tin, both read on the Last Day when the ash in the corners is not swept. Both men are right and neither acts.

9. He hears Derren say *the account stands* and Rusk say *a sealed world* in the same week and understands that they are the same sentence spoken in two rooms.
10. He draws from the Hearth and pays with a shape of Nina — the boat promise beginning to go — and cannot be sure afterwards whether he has lost the daughter or only the memory of her.

**Introduced.** Steward Derren in person, the sealed file, the Hearth as wound, the previous menders, the Kept as a condition rather than a rank to envy.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Marra's confession in clauses she cannot finish; Derren's interest, which is more dangerous than anger; Rusk's paper arriving as a handbook for the enemy; the fear that each memory paid to the Hearth is another piece of Nina or Chicago.

**The twist.** The Court and the Unbanked are not opposites. They are two methods toward one ending, and that ending is the paradise the temple has described since Y.H. 1: a world of unbreakable vessels, which is a world of the walking dead.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Hearthward. Draw from the Hearth; never be cold. Cost: a memory each drawing; the names of the previous menders; the first real fear that he is becoming the thing he was sent to prevent.

**Dangers.** The Hearth-tongue reaching back, the Kept, the sealed wells and their keys, Derren's kindness, the quiet seal, a memory-loss that cannot be distinguished from becoming a different person.

**How it changes him.** He learns that his *no* still has no alternative, and a no with no alternative is just another way of letting the world break. He decides he must make a third thing, and that making it will cost him the standing of every teacher he has had.

## Arc 11 — The Third Thing
*Subtitle: leave the crack showing*

**Approximate in-world time.** About two years.

**Central conflict.** To build a repair that leaves the crack visible and the Warm free to move — and to refuse the quiet seal when every authority, friendly and hostile, recommends it as mercy.

**Major beats.**
1. He declines the next rank. The Court cannot place him in any ledger that matters; the Unbanked cannot call him an instrument any more. He is, for the first time, unclassifiable.
2. He takes a clamp and a staple apart and understands that neither is the repair. Old Garl's proverb returns: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*
3. Fenna's glaze: a good glaze hides the crack, and that is its whole purpose and its whole defect. She puts less lead in the Vail order, a small treason against her household's price and the first honest work she has done.
4. The first working that takes nothing from anyone: a vessel that admits its crack and still holds, nearly full and never quite. The full note, honestly given.
5. The emptiness of the Kept glimpsed from inside — a room with a good fire that nobody in it can enjoy. He understands what the Court calls blessing.
6. Solene refuses a fourth return. Tarn's earlier refusal is remembered at last and the two link: the first time a returned slave in the north refused the Court's best product, and the first time a clay-bound hand did.
7. The Unbanked offer him the sealing; the Court offers him a Kept vessel; both offers made in the same soft voice. Derren writes his answer down while he is still giving it.
8. He builds the third thing in secret with charcoal on slate, is wrong five times, and each wrong answer is carried by somebody else: a graft that wants, a bank that leaks, a clamp that stops the want with the flow, a memory burned for a lesson that was wrong, a district cooled for a season that achieved nothing.
9. The second records converge as physical proof: Alda's tin, Wessen's second file, Orra's cord, Mira's knots, Garl's burnt slate, Siv's crossed-out page, Veno's dry document. None of them could have been entered. Together they are the only complete account of the Forty.
10. He makes the repair and it holds — nearly full, never quite — and the world does not end, and nobody cheers. A made thing has to be lived in.

**Introduced.** The third thing; the Kept from inside; the convergence of the second records; Rusk broken, helping at the last.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** Rusk helps because he has finally stopped being right. Marra's fingers finish paying. Fenna, who cannot be booked, and Solene, who may be poured, both choose the crack over the glaze.

**The twist.** The highest rank in Sarn is a numbness. Refusing it is the only way to keep the one thing he came with: the willingness to look at a body and decide, and then to stay in the room afterwards.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Hearthward, refusing the next rank. Build a repair that leaves the crack visible; decline the quiet seal. Cost: almost everything he has — his standing, his teacher, any chance of a whole return, the last clean memory of the old life, and the comfort of believing that being right is the same as being useful.

**Dangers.** The Unbanked and the Court in the same room; the quiet seal; the use of Solene as an exhibit; the loss of his own shape through memory after memory paid to the Hearth; the temptation of a cleaner answer.

**How it changes him.** He learns that repair is not restoration and that a debt can be carried instead of settled. He stops trying to close the account and begins the long work of living inside the repair.

## Arc 12 — The Long Account
*Subtitle: the account is carried*

**Approximate in-world time.** About three years.

**Central conflict.** To give Warm without taxing it — to make a life inside the third thing, and to decide what to do with the knowledge that the way home is real and cannot be paid.

**Major beats.**
1. He gives Warm without a slate. It is the first time anyone in Sarn has done it, and the first time the word *gift* is used in its plain sense in a district that has entered every kindness as a debt.
2. The mechanism of return is found: the hot trace that brought him across can be followed back, because he died giving and the warmth stayed hot and tangled with an unpaid promise.
3. The price is understood: going back means drawing the warmth out of the woman the child in the minivan became. She is not Nina. Two women he never named carry his last warmth and his last unsent words separately.
4. The last account cannot be settled by dying again. He decides to carry it. The unsent call is finally spoken aloud in Sarn, to nobody who can hear it, and it is enough.
5. The Forty named one by one from Orra's cord — Kess first, whose mother has a knot and no return. What follows is not a return but a remembering, which is the only kind the Court cannot book.
6. Low Kiln rebuilt: the quarter holds a note nearly full; the Ash ridge at the Vail kiln-house is swept at last, on the morning after the Last Day, and Garl's peg and coat stay where they are.
7. A feast with bread and kiln apples burnt at the outside and the browned crusts given to the children. The ordinary, stubborn domestic life that has survived eight hundred years of accounting.
8. Derren survives his own accounting and is respected for it. Berr still says *the account stands*. Rennick adds correctly and cannot stop, which is the most dangerous thing in the Hearthseat now.
9. Marra's fingers are done; she asks him a question and this time he supplies the words. Rusk sits outside the room and asks one last question of nobody and answers it, for once, almost well.
10. He is no longer in any ledger that matters to the Court. The saga continues: a made thing has to be lived in, and a debt that cannot be paid must still be carried, day by dated day.

**Introduced.** The mechanism home as a moral fact; the minivan child as a woman still warm on Earth; the life of the third thing.

**Betrayal / fall / death / love.** The final choice not to go is the last betrayal of the old life and the first fidelity to it. The loves that stay and the loves that cannot are held at the same weight. No death is undone.

**The twist.** The way home is real. The account cannot be paid. He stays, and staying is the harder act — for a man whose whole life was spent trying to get back to a daughter who did not need him to.

**Power milestone — and its cost.** Hearthward, unranked by the Court's scale. Give Warm without taxing it; carry the debt instead of paying it. Cost: he is outside every ledger that matters; the saga continues; the last thing he wants is the one thing he must not buy.

**Dangers.** The Waste still creeping inward; the Court's grammar going on without him; the temptation of the mechanism; the ordinary cold that no repair abolishes.

**How it changes him.** He becomes a man who can look at a dying body and decide, and then stay in the room. The surgeon at last learns the second duty: not to decide and walk away, but to carry. He does not become a god. He stays a large tired man gone soft in the middle, with a click in one knee, surgeon's hands on a man's wrists, and the habit of counting the exits in every room.

# Beyond arc 12 — the distant shape

*Private, coarse and revisable. These eras are not arcs; they are the weather of the thousands of chapters after the first twelve. No ending is written anywhere in this file.*

**The Settlement era (after arc 12).** The third thing is lived in, not celebrated. Low Kiln keeps a note nearly full; the Court goes on keeping accounts in a district that no longer believes the printed figure. The first conflicts are domestic and administrative: who tends a repair that leaks by design, who pays for the heat, whether a crack left visible can be lived with by people who were taught to fear it. Dan's authority is moral and exhausted; he is the oldest person in most rooms and still not the most powerful.

**The War of the Two Keepings.** The Keeping does not collapse. It splits: one wing, led from the sealed files, argues that the third thing is just a slower leak and that the quiet seal must be imposed before the Waste comes in; the other, including officers who can add, argues that an institution which cannot admit a crack has already failed. Rennick's correct arithmetic is the opening shot — the printed one in twelve published against the roll's one in three, and a generation raised on a figure that breaks in their hands. Keldish mercenaries, Vhaleni contracts that rot in the rain, Sere buyers who want the poor and not the land. Dan fights this war with records and with his ear, not with power, and is often on the losing side.

**The Opening of the Slates.** The Deep Hand is translated — not by Dan, whose great limit is that he cannot read the first account, but by a Vorr who finally sits long enough and a scholar from the Old Kiln who copies what the stone says. The founding invoice is made public: Sarn was never fired whole, the first Court sold the world's heat to buy a thousand years of whole returns, and the oldest repairs under the world are unkept promises made solid. The temple's story survives anyway, because a story that got there first does not have to be true to keep a room warm. The Empty Cantons are named at last.

**The Long Returning.** Other people find the mechanism Dan found. Some go back, and the price is always drawn from a woman or man still warm on Earth. A fugitive industry grows up around the crossing: brokers, forged traces, people who pay in warmth they do not own. Dan, who refused the passage, becomes the authority on a road he will not travel — and must decide whether to stop others from paying a price he himself declined. The woman the minivan child became is reached but never used; she appears at the edge of the saga as a life lived fully on another planet, carrying a warmth she cannot account for.

**The age of the earlier menders.** The chain of broken worlds opens. The three previous menders are encountered through their works and, once, in person: the one who made the repair beautiful and lied (a world of perfect glaze over a total crack); the one who made it honest and let the world break (a people who died cold and uncollected, and were right); and the one who became the Court (Steward Derren's predecessor, whose last act was to found the institution Dan has spent his life opposing). Dan does not defeat them. He learns which part of each of them is also in him: the liar's love of a clean note, the honest one's contempt for compromise, the founder's inability to see a person inside an account.

**The last revelation.** Sarn was made cracked, and the work is not to close the crack but to keep a hand on it. The Ash is returned, name by name, to the people it was made from — a long, unglamorous restitution carried out by hearth-maids and clerks who can add. The Hearth is reopened as a wound that is allowed to leak, and the Weighing becomes a remembering rather than an account. The third thing is a practice, not a monument. It has to be mended twice; the second time is the real one.

**What never happens.** No final battle, no coronation, no ascension to Kept, no restoration of the first life, no closing of the last account. Dan never becomes a god and never goes home. He grows old in a world that is still cracked, with a bad knee, a shelf of slates nobody can confiscate, and the habit of looking at a body and noticing the one small thing that should be otherwise. New menders are recalled in other worlds, and some of them fail differently. The account is carried forward. The saga continues.
