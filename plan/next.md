## Chapter 2 — He's Not Vail

**Days covered.** Days 2–3 — Stone 8 and Stone 9, Y.H. 812, Eighthday and Ninthday. On the third day Sella takes the child up the Clay Yard and Alda tells Dessa the lie.

**Opening moment.** Open inside the Vail kiln-house on the morning of Day 2, before anyone has spoken a line of dialogue. Begin with the breath: the low intake, the pause, the long letting-out he counted through the wall in the last chapter, only now he is inside the thing that makes it. Let the house be known by its objects before it is known by its people --- the wheel-seat worn hollow on the left, the salt-stained coat on the peg behind the door, the grey Ash ridge along the skirting board that Sella will not let anyone sweep. Tarn is already sweeping when anyone wakes; he pockets the small bench knife. Garl is present only as the shape his objects keep. Jorra stands at the wheel with hands like a gate hinge. Then the line: *he's not Vail.* Sella does not answer it. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, and *the boy* where another would say *my son*; she touches the widow's mark at her throat before she opens the kiln and at no other time. Garl's proverb, in the house's voice: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 2).** The kiln-house as above. The flat note of a cup across the room before anyone taps it, and he cannot reach the cup. Des's laugh breaks through in memory --- ten minutes of bad pizza and silence in a car park, one minute timed on the radio --- and vanishes. Do not tag it; let it arrive and go.
- **Scene 2 (Day 2).** Jorra and Sella at the wheel and the fire. *I haven't said.* Jorra never looks at Sella while obeying her. Tarn's deference has a nail in it; he calls everyone *house* and says *I owe*. Sella's instruction is to the pot and the fire, never a finished feeling. The boy reads the hollow seat and the salt-stained coat with the four-four-and-a-knot count he brought from the birth room.
- **Scene 3 (Day 3).** Sella carries the covered bundle up the Clay Yard without looking back. Dessa stands at the end of the yard, counts nineteen steps and does not follow; her pulse is in her throat and the scar under her thumb. Alda tells her the boy went in the levy, among the Forty, and becomes exact about dates she was not asked for. Close on the boy given a bed in the house and Tarn given the loft, with Dan noticing that a poor household has just performed triage on its own members and called it kindness --- and unable to say a word about it.

**Characters on stage and their voices.** Sella Vail (proverbs, instruction to the pot and the fire, never a finished feeling), Jorra Vail (short declarative; *he's not Vail*; *I haven't said*), Tarn (deferential with a nail in it; *house*; *I owe*), Alda Reed (dates instead of comfort), Dessa Silt (the smiling-when-angry *door shut*), Garl Vail as absence, Deshawn Carter as one remembered laugh. All five have Chapter Log lines from Chapter 1; Jorra and Tarn get their first here.

## Chapter 3 — One in Twelve

**Days covered.** Day 4 — Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday. Account-day and the last market before the Five Cold Days.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1.** The Cross at market: two hundred people, eight pigs, one assessor. Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate. He never says *I*; he says *the district* and *the account*. *One in twelve, as the notice says.* The shouting stops because the crowd has to lean in to hear him. Dan, in a basket, hears the note of Berr's slate as a flat thing --- a comparison he has no words for yet and would not share if he had.
- **Scene 2.** Ostrowski's voice breaks through the market noise: `\memory{Marchetti. You're back on in ten.}` and the sentence about the first duty and the second. The clay yard, the pigs and the trauma bay occupy the same paragraph for a breath, then the market returns. Do not explain the shift.
- **Scene 3.** A collector's clerk at the edge of the market handles a slate with a name in a strange, neat hand; the name was written before the face and nobody in the market remarks on it. Berr speaks to Sella about her widow's mark as a kindness that is entered. The uncalled debt Garl left against a shipment of tiles hangs in the room without being named. Chapter closes on Ash carried out to the Bone Orchard, or on the slate read a second time --- forward motion into the collection of the Hearth Drawing.

**Characters on stage.** Berr Clay (kiln-master's proverb braided with Court grammar; apologises in the form of an instruction), Sella Vail, Alda Reed, Mira Silt in the background, Ostrowski as a remembered voice, the unnamed assessor.

## Chapter 4 — The Hearth Drawing

**Days covered.** Days 5–6 — Stone 11 and Stone 12, Y.H. 812, Firstday and Secondday. Stone 12 is the Hearth Drawing, first collection of the year.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 5).** A quiet day in the Vail house: the banked coals, Sella touching the mark before she opens the kiln, the boy's fifth day of failing to keep a blanket on. He hears the full note of a Vail pot through the wall of the shed and is corrected by nobody, because nobody else hears it. A neighbour's child is brought to Alda with the second chill.
- **Scene 2 (Day 6, the Drawing).** A collection witnessed from a basket at a neighbour's house. The cold iron tube against a way; the collector's fingers; the small flat knock of a vessel that has cracked all the way through, which Dan recognises as a sound a body makes when it gives. The measure is booked. The account stands. Keep the collector's hands exact and the collector's voice in the soft passive; he is an officer, not a monster, and has a reason.
- **Scene 3.** The rekindled infant worse on the third day of a return. The midwives call it the second chill and explain it as a graft settling; Dan knows the afterdrop from rewarming patients and is corrected by every woman in the room. He says nothing. He reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. Close on the Ash ridge in the corner of the death-house, thicker by a finger's width, and the simple fact that the year's first measure has been taken.

**Characters on stage.** Alda Reed (instruction, dates, the tin touched once inside her bodice), the collector (a walk-on with one function; one line in the Court's grammar and a name in `characters/_minor.md`), Sella, Dessa in the background with the cough, the unnamed neighbour family.

**Threads planted.** T012 (the Second Want, struck from the record), T013 (the Ash thickens), T009 (a death left where it is), T004 (the phone-pocket reach), T061 (the flat note), T007 (Alda's tin).

**Indexing.** `\lore{the Hearth Drawing}`, `\lore{the Afterdrop}`, `\lore{the Second Want}` (do not use the term in narration yet --- index the concept only when a character says the midwife's word), `\person{Alda Reed}`, `\place{Low Kiln}`, `\lore{cold iron}`, `\lore{collection}`.

## Chapter 5 — Nineteen Steps

**Days covered.** Days 7–9 — Stone 13, Stone 14 and Stone 15, Y.H. 812. Day 7 is Thirdday; dawn at the Fishstairs comes on Day 8. Fill Day 7 so the reckoning has no gap between the Drawing and the market.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 7).** The Vail household at first light: Tarn sweeping the yard a second time, the small knife back in his pocket; Jorra at the wheel; Sella feeding somebody else's child from her own dish. The boy turns his head toward a pot before anyone taps it. Tarn sees and says nothing, because a clay-bound hand who can hear the full note is worth more than his debt --- and because a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called.
- **Scene 2 (Day 8).** Dawn at the Fishstairs below the north bridge. Eels, salted perch, rumours sold at noon but already in the air at dawn. Mira Silt with slime dried on her sleeves, the missing top of her left ear explained by a hook that the scar contradicts, the eel-bones flicked against her thumb. She short-weights Berr Clay's order by two eels and has been doing it for six years; he has paid her two marks short for the same six years. She will not sit at a table where a slate is open and answers every question with a question and then her own answer. *He's warm. That's all I'll say on a slate, and I'm saying it off the slate.*
- **Scene 3 (Day 9).** Dessa at the edge of the market with the cough that is a vessel going thin, mistaken for the pits; the pale scar rubbed; the nineteen steps counted because counting keeps something larger out of the room. Mira's knot-string is glimpsed --- a cord of grey wool touched when a number is spoken, a separate cord for the child in Nine Pots she has never knocked on. Mira tells Dan nothing and gives him a crust quickly, briskly, before anyone can make a speech about it. Close with the boy watching the cord and the eel-bones and understanding that this woman keeps accounts in a material no Court can confiscate, though he has no words for that yet.

**Characters on stage.** Mira Silt (the full idiolect, fastest speaker in the book), Dessa Silt, Tarn, Sella (briefly), Berr Clay (a short market exchange), gulls and eel-sellers as walk-ons.

**Threads planted.** T025 (Mira's knots and barley), T059 (the child in Nine Pots), T008 (Dessa's lie and the Forty, still unspoken), T036 (Tarn's ear witnessed), T060 (brisk kindness), T050/T051 seeded only if Fenna is glimpsed across the market --- keep her to a silhouette with green at the mouth and no dialogue in this chapter.

## Chapter 6 — Counting What There Is

**Days covered.** Days 10–14 — Stone 16 to Stone 20, Y.H. 812, Sixthday through Tenthday. Day 14 is Account-day. The Five are eleven days off and the household begins to count what it has.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 10).** A household counting fuel and bread against the days before Kindling. Sella touches the widow's mark before she opens the kiln and at no other time; the banked coals under the ash are the condition on which she holds the house. Alda comes to see the boy and touches the tin inside her bodice once, and writes something on her own slate she does not leave lying about. The boy's count extends from the boards to the stack of kindling --- four, four, a gap where the fifth should be --- and the gap is what he notices.
- **Scene 2 (Day 14, Account-day).** The Cross again, in deep cold. Berr Clay reads the account in the district's grammar; a collector's clerk with a soft passive voice handles a slate whose upper line is in the neat dark hand, and the boy catches the pause a second time. Sella's uncalled debt against the tiles is spoken around, never named. The crowd leans in. Nothing is proved and the boy knows he has seen the hand twice.
- **Scene 3.** Back at the Vail house at dusk: the ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack; a woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and is contradicted by everybody who wants it to come late. Dan reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. Close on the Ash ridge thicker by another finger's width and on the day-count the household keeps badly and he keeps exactly, with the Five still ahead. Forward momentum: a second sighting of the hand, a crack in the ice, a coming that cannot be hurried.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed, Berr Clay (short), the collector's clerk (a different walk-on from Chapter 4's collector; one line and one gesture, name him in `characters/_minor.md` if he speaks twice), the unnamed market voices.

## Continuity rules for these five chapters

1. **Days 2–14 only**, in order, with no gaps: Ch 2 = 2–3, Ch 3 = 4, Ch 4 = 5–6, Ch 5 = 7–9, Ch 6 = 10–14. After Chapter 6 the reckoning continues at Day 15 (Stone 21) and must not skip the approach to the Five or the Five themselves (Days 25–29), each with a dated entry.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-Hearth scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only** --- Ostrowski's correction, Des's laugh, a half-line of a call. Never for a general recollection.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the blanket, the hand that cannot reach the cup. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The stone was warmed at Dessa's fire at the Vail hearth and the crust came out of that house's meal; neither is entered. When somebody pays in Chapter 2 or 6, let the reader be able to name who.
8. **The two hands stay separate in his mind.** He has set the pause at the flare beside the pause on the slate and has not joined them. Do not do the joining for him; it is paid off in Slate Row years away.
9. When a chapter closes, update `memory/state.md`, add one line per day to `memory/timeline.md`, append to `memory/story-so-far.md`, give every character who appeared a Chapter Log line, move planted threads from `open` to `planted` in `plan/threads.md`, and add any new coined terms to `bible/glossary.md`.
