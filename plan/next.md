# Next — intentions for chapters 1–5 (private)

*Working notes for the author, to be read with `memory/state.md` before any scene is written, and with `bible/voice.md` and `design/STYLE-REFERENCE.md` open beside them. Days follow `bible/calendar.md` exactly: Day 1 is Stone 7, Y.H. 812. Do not recap. Do not explain the Warm. Let the reader be as lost as Dan is.*

**The governing image of the opening move:** a warm stone. The last thing Dan knows is fire arriving from every direction at once and the cold leaving his hands; the first thing he knows in Sarn is a small river stone, warmed against a newborn's belly, already giving up its heat. Everything the saga argues about — who paid, who is owed, whether warmth can be left where it is — is inside that stone. Keep it in chapter one as a physical object, never as a symbol.

## Chapter 1 — The Stone Going Cold

**Days covered.** Day 1 only — Stone 7, Y.H. 812, a Seventhday. The Ilm is under ice. One chapter, one day, lived from the inside of an infant's body.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (drop capital).** The birth room in Low Kiln. Open on the stone. *The stone had been warm when the midwife put it against him and was warm no longer.* Alda Reed's hands, the slate within reach of her hand, Dessa Silt counting under her breath — nineteen steps from the slip pit to the drying rack, counted now for a different reason. The room smells of wet wool, tallow, clay dust and the sour mineral edge of the Ash ridge. Dan's first perceptions are surgical and useless: he registers the angle of Dessa's wrist, the rhythm of her breathing, the exact moment the afterdrop would begin if this were a rewarming patient. He wants the breast. Both are true and the second is louder.
- **Scene 2.** The branching red flower. Alda crosses herself with two fingers at the corner of the birth-mark, notices she has done it in front of a stranger, and says it is a birth-flower. The tense tightens and Chicago breaks through for a beat: the orange tunnels of sodium light on wet snow, the wipers, the hiss of fuel, a phone lit under a sheet with a name he will not look at, a woman in a coat the colour of wet ash standing at the edge of the flare light with a slate, writing; the writing does not stop. `\memory{...}` only for remembered *words*, never for this scene — the memory itself is written straight into the prose, present tense for a sentence or two, then back to past.
- **Scene 3.** Stone Row or the doorway: Mira Silt is in the room and says almost nothing; the shape of a name written on a collector's slate is glimpsed and not understood. The stone goes cold. A woman's hand — Sella's — reaches for the covered bundle but does not take it yet. Close on the cold stone and the counting: four nails on the near board, four on the next, a knot the width of a thumb. Forward momentum, not an ending: someone will come on the third day.

**Characters on stage and their voices.**
- **Dan/Corin** — no dialogue possible. Interior register dry, exact, counting; infant body immediate, hungry, humiliated. Never explain the old life to anyone.
- **Alda Reed** — practical instruction addressed to the mother, the stone, the child, then the room. *Warm the stone first. A cold stone is a stone, not a kindness.* When lying she becomes unusually exact. The crossed sign is involuntary.
- **Dessa Silt** — short, practical, the joke that costs her; calls the child *the little hot one*, never by a name. Counts under her breath. Rubs the pale scar on her forearm.
- **Mira Silt** — answers a question with a question and then her own answer; flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie; will not sit where a slate is open. She is standing; she stays standing.
- **The grey-coated woman and Nina** — only as the death-scene image and the lit phone. Name neither in the narration of Sarn.

**Threads planted.** T001 (birth-flower), T002 (grey coat), T003 (name before the face), T004 (lit phone), T006 (stone), T007 (crossed sign), T008 (the lie, seeded), T013 (Ash), T071 (counting exits — here just counting), T072 (attention).

**Indexing for the first mentions in this chapter.** `\person{Daniel Marchetti}` / `\person{Corin}` as the narration requires; `\person{Alda Reed}`, `\person{Dessa Silt}`, `\person{Mira Silt}`, `\person{Nina Marchetti}` (on the lit screen), `\place{Low Kiln}`, `\place{Sill}`, `\place{Grath}`, `\place{Chicago}`, `\place{the expressway}`, `\lore{the Warm}`, `\lore{the Return}`, `\lore{the Afterdrop}`, `\lore{the Ash}`. **What is withheld.** The word *collector* is not attached to the grey coat. The number forty is not spoken. Marra is not named. The reader is allowed to think the woman at the crash caused the fire; she did not.

## Chapter 2 — He's Not Vail

**Days covered.** Days 2–3 — Stone 8 and Stone 9, Y.H. 812. On the third day Sella takes the child up the Clay Yard and Alda tells Dessa the lie.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1.** The Vail kiln-house in the Clay Yard, before the household knows it will keep him. Tarn is already sweeping when anyone wakes; he pockets the small bench knife. Garl is known only by objects: the wheel-seat worn hollow on the left, the salt-stained coat on the peg, the Ash ridge along the skirting board that Sella will not let anyone sweep. Dan hears the flat note of a cup across the room before anyone taps it and cannot reach the cup. Des's laugh breaks through in memory — ten minutes of bad pizza and silence in a car park — and vanishes.
- **Scene 2.** Jorra at the wheel, sixteen, hands like a gate hinge. *He's not Vail.* Sella does not answer. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I* and *the boy* where another would say *my son*. She touches the widow's mark at her throat before she opens the kiln and at no other time. Garl's proverb, remembered in the house's voice: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*
- **Scene 3.** Day 3. Sella carries the covered bundle up the Clay Yard without looking back. Dessa stands at the end of the yard and counts nineteen steps and does not follow. Alda tells her the boy went in the levy, among the Forty, and becomes exact about dates she was not asked for. The chapter closes on the boy being given a bed in the house and Tarn being given the loft, with Dan noticing the shape of that decision and unable to say anything about it.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail (proverbs, instruction to the pot and the fire, never a finished feeling), Jorra Vail (short declarative; *I haven't said*; never looks at Sella while obeying her), Tarn (deferential with a nail in it; calls everyone *house*; *I owe*), Alda Reed (dates instead of comfort), Dessa Silt (the smiling-when-angry *door shut*), Garl Vail as absence, Des as one remembered laugh.

**Threads planted.** T006, T008, T009, T031 (burnt slate, seeded as the kiln's ash), T035 (Jorra's *he's not Vail* and the broken slates), T036 (Tarn's knife and ear), T048 (Des), T057 (Sella's second roll, seeded), T058 (the first wall), T060 (love as maintenance).

**Indexing.** `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Jorra Vail}`, `\person{Tarn}`, `\person{Garl Vail}`, `\person{Deshawn Carter}`, `\place{the Clay Yard}`, `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`, `\place{the Vail wheel-shed}`, `\place{the Debt Loft}`, `\lore{the full note}`, `\lore{the Ash}`, `\lore{widow's mark}`. Canonical form is `Tarn`, not `Tarn Vail`, in running text.

**What is withheld.** Do not say why Sella took him. Do not say what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version. Tarn's kept knife is a physical fact, not a clue the narration points at.

## Chapter 3 — One in Twelve

**Days covered.** Day 4 — Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday. Account-day and the last market before the Five Cold Days.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1.** The Cross at market: two hundred people, eight pigs, one assessor. Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate. He never says *I*; he says *the district* and *the account*. *One in twelve, as the notice says.* The shouting stops because the crowd has to lean in to hear him. Dan, in a basket, hears the note of Berr's slate as a flat thing — a comparison he has no words for yet and would not share if he had.
- **Scene 2.** Ostrowski's voice breaks through the market noise: `\memory{Marchetti. You're back on in ten.}` and the sentence about the first duty and the second. The clay yard, the pigs and the trauma bay occupy the same paragraph for a breath, then the market returns. Do not explain the shift.
- **Scene 3.** A collector's clerk at the edge of the market handles a slate with a name in a strange, neat hand; the name was written before the face and nobody in the market remarks on it. Berr speaks to Sella about her widow's mark as a kindness that is entered. The uncalled debt Garl left against a shipment of tiles hangs in the room without being named. Chapter closes with Ash carried out to the Bone Orchard or with the slate read a second time — forward motion into the collection of the Hearth Drawing.

**Characters on stage.** Berr Clay (kiln-master's proverb braided with Court grammar; apologises in the form of an instruction), Sella Vail, Alda Reed, Mira Silt in the background, Ostrowski as a remembered voice, the unnamed assessor.

**Threads planted.** T015 (the deduction), T041 (Berr's locked box, not yet shown), T008 (the levy's language), T046 (Ostrowski's two duties), T047 (Vesci's grammar seeded in Berr's), T044 (the call as an absence), T061 (the flat note), T071 (counting the room).

**Indexing.** `\person{Berr Clay}`, `\person{Lena Ostrowski}`, `\place{the Cross}`, `\place{Slate Row}`, `\place{the Bone Orchard}`, `\lore{Account-day}`, `\lore{the deduction}`, `\lore{the Keeping}`.

## Chapter 4 — The Hearth Drawing

**Days covered.** Days 5–6 — Stone 11 and Stone 12, Y.H. 812. Stone 12 is the Hearth Drawing, the first collection of the year.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 5).** A quiet day in the Vail house: the banked coals, Sella touching the mark before she opens the kiln, the boy's fourth day of failing to keep a blanket on. He hears the full note of a Vail pot through the wall of the shed and is corrected by nobody, because nobody else hears it. A neighbour's child is brought to Alda with the second chill.
- **Scene 2 (Day 6, the Drawing).** A collection witnessed from a basket at a neighbour's house. The cold iron tube against a way; the collector's fingers; the small flat knock of a vessel that has cracked all the way through, which Dan recognises as a sound a body makes when it gives. The measure is booked. The account stands. Keep the collector's hands exact and the collector's voice in the soft passive; he is an officer, not a monster, and has a reason.
- **Scene 3.** The rekindled infant worse on the third day of a return. The midwives call it the second chill and explain it as a graft settling; Dan knows the afterdrop from rewarming patients and is corrected by every woman in the room. He says nothing. He reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. Close on the Ash ridge in the corner of the death-house, thicker by a finger's width, and the simple fact that the year's first measure has been taken.

**Characters on stage.** Alda Reed (instruction, dates, the tin touched once inside her bodice), the collector (a walk-on with one function; give him a line in the Court's grammar and a name in `characters/_minor.md`), Sella, Dessa in the background with the cough, the unnamed neighbour family.

**Threads planted.** T012 (the Second Want, struck from the record), T013 (the Ash thickens), T009 (a death left where it is), T004 (the phone-pocket reach), T061 (the flat note), T007 (Alda's tin).

**Indexing.** `\lore{the Hearth Drawing}`, `\lore{the Afterdrop}`, `\lore{the Second Want}` (do not use the term in narration yet — index the concept only when a character says the midwife's word), `\person{Alda Reed}`, `\place{Low Kiln}`, `\lore{cold iron}`, `\lore{collection}`.

**What is withheld.** No experiment yet, no clamp, no heresy. The second cause of the second chill is a fact the narration does not know how to state. Do not let anyone explain the Return.

## Chapter 5 — Nineteen Steps

**Days covered.** Days 8–9 — Stone 14 and Stone 15, Y.H. 812. Dawn at the Fishstairs on the second day.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1.** The Vail household at first light: Tarn sweeping the yard a second time, the small knife back in his pocket, Jorra at the wheel, Sella feeding somebody else's child from her own dish. The boy turns his head toward a pot before anyone taps it. Tarn sees and says nothing, because a clay-bound hand who can hear the full note is worth more than his debt.
- **Scene 2.** Dawn at the Fishstairs below the north bridge. Eels, salted perch, rumours sold at noon but already in the air at dawn. Mira Silt with slime dried on her sleeves, the missing top of her left ear explained by a hook that the scar contradicts, the eel-bones flicked against her thumb. She short-weights Berr Clay's order by two eels and has been doing it for six years; he has paid her two marks short for the same six years. She will not sit at a table where a slate is open and answers every question with a question and then her own answer. *He's warm. That's all I'll say on a slate, and I'm saying it off the slate.*
- **Scene 3.** Dessa at the edge of the market with the cough that is a vessel going thin, mistaken for the pits; the pale scar rubbed; the nineteen steps counted because counting keeps something larger out of the room. Mira's knot-string is glimpsed — a cord of grey wool touched when a number is spoken, a separate cord for the child in Nine Pots she has never knocked on. Mira tells Dan nothing and gives him a crust quickly, briskly, before anyone can make a speech about it. Close with the boy watching the cord and the eel-bones and understanding that this woman keeps accounts in a material no Court can confiscate, though he has no words for that yet.

**Characters on stage.** Mira Silt (the full idiolect, fastest speaker in the book), Dessa Silt, Tarn, Sella (briefly), Berr Clay (a short market exchange), gulls and eel-sellers as walk-ons.

**Threads planted.** T025 (Mira's knots and barley), T059 (the child in Nine Pots), T008 (Dessa's lie and the Forty, still unspoken), T060 (brisk kindness), T050/T051 seeded only if Fenna is glimpsed across the market — keep her to a silhouette with green at the mouth and no dialogue in this chapter.

**Indexing.** `\person{Mira Silt}`, `\person{Dessa Silt}`, `\place{the Fishstairs}`, `\place{the Weir}`, `\place{Nine Pots}`, `\lore{eel-bones}`, `\lore{knot-string}`.

**What is withheld.** Mira does not say she knows Corin is Dessa's son. Dessa's cough is not diagnosed. The word *forty* is not spoken in this chapter; it comes at the Fish Blessing when Orra arrives.

## Continuity rules for these five chapters

1. **Days 1–9 only**, in order, with no gaps. After chapter 5 the timeline continues at Day 10 (Stone 16) and must not skip to the Five before their dated entries are written.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-Hearth scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only** — Ostrowski's correction, Des's laugh, a half-line of a call. Never for a general recollection.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the blanket, the hand that cannot reach the cup. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The warmed stone was warmed at somebody's fire; the eel crust was taken out of someone's meal. Let the reader be able to name who.
8. When a chapter closes, update `memory/state.md`, add one line per day to `memory/timeline.md`, append to `memory/story-so-far.md`, give every character who appeared a Chapter Log line, and move planted threads from `open` to `planted` in `plan/threads.md`.
