**Where Chapter 36 left the hand.** Small hours of Day 149, Reed 30, under the Vail kiln-house roof. The boy, one hundred and forty-nine days old and four months and twenty-nine days, told by nobody, lay alone on his bed and not in the Debt Loft; the brown-edged crust lay stiff two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third, the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door and coming down against the fold. Sella had carried him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, around the pan set out to dry and the trough with its skin of ice and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, set him on his bed, put the fold right without looking at his face, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said *The house will keep it*, counted under her breath toward no opening, set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, left the widow's mark untouched because a dusk is not an opening, and gone to her own bed without explaining the count; the two rooms do not touch. His file held six hearings of the full note and no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, **nine** stoppings of the near-saying — Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 — objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end and still held; the damper's double bite joined to no kiln, the two shut-room tellings with no third, the told bell beside the heard, a sixth green that is still only a colour, a sixth silent half-turn at the market's edge that is still only a shape, the two shelf gaps in a room the fold does not touch, the charcoal under the shelf unread, and every private keeping unread. The reed month went out without a boundary drawn and the count crossed into Smoke exact and unspent, spoken by nobody. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear and the number of his days spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither and could not ask.

## Chapter 37 — Smoke begins

**Days covered.** Days 150–152 — Smoke 1–3, Y.H. 813, Firstday to Thirdday. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each opened by its own `\daymark` and lived in full. Smoke 10 is the first Account-day of Smoke and belongs to Chapter 38. *The firing month has begun: the kilns go into the year's last working season and the district's withholdings go on being kept by hands that cannot read them.*

**Opening moment of Chapter 37.** Open on Day 150, Smoke 1, Firstday, with the boy one hundred and fifty days old and exactly five months, and nobody under any roof saying so. The reed month is over; the last bundles and the last market have gone, and the count crosses into the firing month with nine held words, a sixth colour, a sixth shape and the loan uncalled. The kilns are being brought up for the season's last work: the clay jar is cold under its glaze, the pan set out to dry is gone from its place, and the trough by the well has lost its skin of ice. Let the first smell of the chapter be hot clay and tallow and the first sound the kiln's breath in fours with its gap, not a market bell. Sella carries him past the kiln mouth at the half-pace from every open slate and, for the first firing of Smoke, touches the widow's mark before the opening and at no other hour; she gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket. Carry the hand going up an inch toward the river's note of water over water and coming down against the fold. Do not recap Chapter 36. Forward, not closed.
