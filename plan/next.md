## Chapter 7 — The Number They Give Out

**Days covered.** Days 15–24 --- Stone 21 to Stone 30, Y.H. 812, Firstday through Tenthday. Day 24 is Account-day and the last market of the year. This is a passage chapter: **every day gets its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, ten entries, no day poured into another. The Five (Days 25–29) are held for Chapter 8 and must each be dated there. Day 20, Stone 26, is **not** an Account-day.

**Opening moment.** Open on Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday, at first light under the Vail roof, not on a thought and not on a recap: the coals holding under their blind ash, the stack of kindling still four, four and an empty fifth, the pot turned a little toward the light on the bench, and the crust cold in the fold. Sella goes to the fire before she goes to anything else and touches \lore{the widow's mark} before she lifts the lid and at no other hour. Somebody has been out to look at the ice and come back with a number they will not give; it arrives under the roof as an absence, carried in a fist and a glance. Do not explain the loan that can hear, the debt, the mark or the missing note. Nobody has spoken to the basket.

**Dated spine, one entry each.**
- **Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday.** The household counts fuel and bread into the last tend before the Five, in loaves and glances and not in figures; the boy's private count can go no further into it than it did on Stone 16 --- four, four and the empty fifth. Somebody come back from the ice has a number they will not give. Sella touches the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.
- **Day 16, Stone 22, Secondday.** Tarn has one year of his seven left and will not count it. He is sent for a collector for a neighbour and goes; on the way back a flat note comes past the door on a vessel somebody is carrying, and his head turns a degree. The reader sees it; nobody in the house does. Do not yet say that a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called.
- **Day 17, Stone 23, Thirdday.** Jorra comes in from the back room with slate dust on him and says nothing about where he has been; the boy reads the not-looking the way he once read a resident who would not meet the senior's eye. Plant the Broken Slates without naming them. Sella gets quieter and the yard straightens.
- **Day 18, Stone 24, Fourthday.** Alda's tin is touched once inside her bodice and a second note is written that she does not leave lying about (T023, seeded toward the floorboard). The exact dates come faster than the question needs them.
- **Day 19, Stone 25, Fifthday.** The cold deepens. A pot in the yard cracks with a sound Dan knows and nobody calls a sound; he cannot turn his head far enough toward it. The hearing count stays five from four sources.
- **Day 20, Stone 26, Sixthday.** An ordinary day, and not an Account-day. The number for the Five changes in the telling from three to two to whatever the district decides to give out that morning (T077). The boy hears figures he cannot spend and goes on keeping his own.
- **Day 21, Stone 27, Seventhday.** A woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and is contradicted by everybody who wants it late. Ice-mice in the thatch; the kiln breathes four, four and a gap.
- **Day 22, Stone 28, Eighthday.** Mira Silt passes the yard with eels and touches a knot when a number is spoken; she gives the boy a crust briskly, before anyone can make a speech of it, and the separate cord for the thin child in Nine Pots is glimpsed again (T059). Dessa coughs from the pits and calls it weather.
- **Day 23, Stone 29, Ninthday.** The yard prepares for the Last Day sweeping. The Ash ridge in the Vail house is measured against another house by the width of a thumb; Garl's coat stays on its peg and nobody takes it down.
- **Day 24, Stone 30, Tenthday.** Account-day and the last market of the year. The household's stores are counted and the Five are spoken of as three days off by a district that has counted what it has and does not want to say it. At the edge of the market a collector's clerk handles a slate with a name in the neat dark hand a **fifth** time; the boy sees the pause and still cannot read it, and the fifth does not join the other four. Close on the Vail kiln-house door, the banked coals and the fire keeping its four and four and its gap --- forward motion into Kindling, when the year's fire is lit from the Hearth and the first measure is taken.

## Chapter 8 — The Five Cold Days

**Days covered.** Days 25–30 --- Kindling, Weighing, the Long Night, the Remembering and the Last Day (Days 25–29, Y.H. 812), then Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813 (Day 30, Firstday, the Opening of the Slates). Give **each of the five holy days its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, and Day 30 its own entry too. No day is poured into another, and no childhood is summarised. Check `bible/calendar.md` before writing; the Five have names, not tend-days, and no market is held during them.

**Dated spine, one entry each.**
- **Day 25, Kindling.** Every hearth in Sill lit from the Hearth-flame. The boy watching a fire come into the house from somewhere he cannot see and cannot reach; the count extended to the light it makes. Sella touches the widow's mark before the kiln and at no other time.
- **Day 26, Weighing.** The year's measures read aloud in a clean voice. Somebody else's whole return announced for somebody who is not in the room; the grammar Berr used at the Cross now wearing a temple's clothes (T047 advance). The boy hears a figure and says nothing. Plant the reading of a name the household will not speak.
- **Day 27, the Long Night.** No fire after dusk. Stories of the Return told in the dark; the boy listens to the voices instead of the flames and counts the gaps between them. The bell's four-four-and-a-gap comes back in the telling of the year.
- **Day 28, the Remembering.** Names of the dead spoken. Garl's name is not spoken in the Vail house; the coat stays on the peg and the ridge stays unswept. The boy counts the silence where the name should be and files it with the other gaps.
- **Day 29, the Last Day.** Bread baked; the grey Ash in the corners left where it lies to be burned next morning and called dealt with. Sella will not let anyone sweep early. The boy watches the ridge measured by a thumb's width and understands that a thing can be left in place and still be accounted for.
- **Day 30, Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813.** The new year opens with the banked coals still banked and the Ash burned and called gone. The count goes on into a year the district has already entered on its slates. Forward motion into the thaw and the work of the slip pits, with the stone still unmentioned in House Silt and the figure still one in twelve.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed (in the Remembering and the second notes), Dessa Silt (the cough worse, still called weather), Mira Silt (a crust, briskly), the household and market voices. **Threads advanced.** T077 (the number the district gives out, now the year's measure), T013 (the Ash left and burned and called dealt with), T009 (what was left where it lies), T031 (the charred slate still in the kiln ash, untouched), T071/T072 (the count carried into a new year), T060 (love as maintenance through the Five), T073 (the name not spoken in the Remembering), T036 (Tarn's term, one year now gone), T081 (the Weighing Hall planted as another room with a bench, a draught and a gap --- not recognised as such yet), T082 (the gaps in the stories counted like intervals).

## Chapter 9 — The River Under the Ice

**Days covered.** Days 31–44 --- Ilmen 2 to Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813, Secondday to Fifthday. A passage chapter: **every day gets its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, fourteen entries, no day poured into another. The Fish Blessing falls on Day 44. Dessa dies on Day 38 and stays dead.

**Dated spine, one entry each.**
- **Day 31, Ilmen 2, Secondday.** The thaw begins in the little things: water in \place{the Slip Brook}, oiled paper that no longer lifts quite so hard. Mira touches a cord when a number is spoken at the yard. The boy is a month old tomorrow and still carried everywhere.
- **Day 32, Ilmen 3, Thirdday.** Tarn has one year of the seven left and will not count it; he agrees with everything so thoroughly that there is nowhere for an argument to go. The boy's hand outside the fold goes up an inch toward the broom and comes down.
- **Day 33, Ilmen 4, Fourthday.** Jorra comes in from the back room with slate dust and says nothing. The men whose names are not spoken are not named; the Broken Slates are planted toward Day ~160 without being called by that name. Tarn's ear is not spoken of.
- **Day 34, Ilmen 5, Fifthday.** Alda touches the tin once and writes what she turns down; a third hidden note goes with the other two, still unread. The exact dates come faster than the question needs them.
- **Day 35, Ilmen 6, Sixthday.** A pot in the yard answers nearly full under nobody's knuckle and the boy hears the difference between nearly and full. No power used, no rank change, no hand to tap it. This is the first earthly answer to the interval he filed on Stone 13 (T082 advanced, not paid).
- **Day 36, Ilmen 7, Seventhday.** The ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack. Somebody comes back from looking at it with a number they do not care to give (T077, now a habit of the district rather than a mystery to be explained; T085 advanced toward the Breaking).
- **Day 37, Ilmen 8, Eighthday.** Dessa's cough is worse and is still weather. Her thumb goes to the sieve-wire scar, the nineteen steps go into the thumb, the door-shut smile comes down. The boy cannot name what he sees in her face.
- **Day 38, Ilmen 9, Ninthday.** Dessa drowns in the clay-heavy rise of \place{the Slip Brook}, uncollected. The boy, thirty-eight days old, is held on the bank and hears the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through --- the sixth hearing and the second from a person. The collector's clerk is at the \place{Fishstairs}; Alda arrives after the vessel has given. He cannot speak, cannot move, cannot look away. Do not let him understand what the sound was; he only files it.
- **Day 39, Ilmen 10, Tenthday.** Account-day at the Cross. The market goes on; the boy is carried through it. No recap of the figure, no new joining of the pauses. The count goes on because there is nothing else that is his.
- **Day 40, Ilmen 11, Firstday.** Sella does not send for a collector for the woman in the brook, and nobody remarks on it. The Vail ridge is measured against another house's by the width of a thumb and left where it lies. Plant the first heresy toward ~150: warmth left where it is, and the world does not end.
- **Day 41, Ilmen 12, Secondday.** Mira gives the boy a crust briskly, before anyone can make a speech of it, and touches a knot when a figure is spoken. The separate cord for the thin child in Nine Pots is glimpsed again (T059).
- **Day 42, Ilmen 13, Thirdday.** A tin above Dessa's bed holds a scrap of cloth from the birth; the boy notices the object without knowing what it is and does not enter it anywhere. Sella's private roll remains unshown.
- **Day 43, Ilmen 14, Fourthday.** The \place{Weir} is prepared for the Fish Blessing. Orra Weir's cords are in the \place{Eel House}; Fenna Clay is glimpsed again across the market with green at her mouth and no dialogue. Alda and Orra do not discuss the third day.
- **Day 44, Ilmen 15, Fifthday.** The Fish Blessing at the Weir. Orra Weir ties a forty-first knot she cannot account for, says the word *forty* within the boy's hearing and watches what he does with it. He is too young to answer. He does not forget the shape of the cord. Payoff of the withheld number; opens T024 toward ~300 and ~1400.

## Continuity rules for these five chapters

1. **Days 15–59 only**, in order, with no gaps: Ch 7 = 15–24 with ten dated entries, Ch 8 = 25–30 with six dated entries, Ch 9 = 31–44 with fourteen dated entries, Ch 10 = 45–47, Ch 11 = 48–59 with twelve dated entries. Day 14 is finished and must not be revisited as a new scene. Check `bible/calendar.md` for every tend-day: Day 20 is Stone 26 and is not Account-day; Day 24 is Stone 30 and is; Day 49 and Day 59 are Account-days; Day 60 is Silt 1 and opens Chapter 12. No day is skipped and no childhood is summarised.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-collection scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation. Ch 6 used it for a chart at the well and flare light at the Cross; do not make it a habit, and never tag a `\memory{}` to it.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only** --- Ostrowski's two duties in Ch 3, a half-line of a call. Chapters 4–6 tagged none; let the market voice and the hospital stand on their own until a remembered line earns the tag.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the fold, the hand that cannot reach the cup or lift to its own chin. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The stone, the gruel, the eels and the crust came from somebody's fire and were entered nowhere; Sella paid twice and has taken a measure from the district. Mira's short eels and Berr's short marks are the same arithmetic at a different stall. When somebody pays in Chapters 7–11, let the reader be able to name who. Dessa's death is a taking nobody enters and the boy cannot refuse.
8. **The hands stay separate in his mind.** He has set the pause at the flare beside the pause on the birth-slate, the Cross of Stone 10 and the Court's table of Stone 20, and has not joined them. Ch 7 gives a fifth sighting; do not do the joining for him. The Drawing collector's neat hand has no pause at all and stays in its own place. It is paid off in Slate Row years away.
9. **The ear keeps its objects in order.** Cup (Ch 2), pot (Ch 2), Vail pot through the wall (Ch 4), dying man at the Drawing (Ch 4), district's slate twice (Ch 3), the district's slate again (Ch 6 and Ch 7), a pot cracking in the yard (Ch 7), a nearly full pot (Ch 9), Dessa on the bank (Ch 9). Five hearings from four sources stand at the close of Ch 6; Stone 13 and Stone 17 added intervals, not hearings. Tarn hears some of them and says nothing; he has now seen the boy's ear and kept that too. Each is useless and none is named in Grathic.
10. **When a chapter closes, update `memory/state.md`, add one line per day to `memory/timeline.md`, append to `memory/story-so-far.md`, give every character who appeared a Chapter Log line, move planted threads from `open` to `planted` in `plan/threads.md`, and add any new coined terms to `bible/glossary.md`.
