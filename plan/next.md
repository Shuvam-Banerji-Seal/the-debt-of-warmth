## Chapter 5 — Nineteen Steps

**Days covered.** Days 7–9 --- Stone 13, 14 and 15, Y.H. 812, Thirdday to Fifthday. Day 7 must be filled so the reckoning has no gap after the Drawing; dawn at the Fishstairs comes on Day 8.

**Opening moment.** Open on Day 7 at first light inside the Vail kiln-house, not on a thought: the coals still banked under their ash, the door at its finger's width, Sella's hand going to \lore{the widow's mark} before she opens the kiln and at no other time, the boy six days old under a fold she puts right without looking, a pot waiting on the bench in the shed. The boy turns his head toward the pot *before* anyone sets it down --- the expectation of a note, not the hearing of one --- and Tarn, coming up the yard with his broom, sees it and says nothing, the small knife already back on the sill where it can be checked. Jorra is at the wheel with bone-coloured slip on his forearms; no slate dust today. The reader is shown the second ear a third time and nobody under the roof sees it. Do not explain the loan that can hear, the debt or the mark. Forward, not closed.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 7).** The household at first light after the Drawing. Sella feeds somebody else's child from her own dish; the bed-under-the-roof and loft-above-the-shed arithmetic is performed again without anyone calling it triage. The boy's count extends to the death-house bench --- four, four, a knot the width of a thumb --- and he keeps the interval where the pot's note did not come today. Tarn's lowered head, the knife back in its place, and Jorra's not-looking at the boy are read the way he once read a resident who would not meet a senior's eye. No explanation of what was taken yesterday; nobody in the yard has said it.
- **Scene 2 (Day 8).** Dawn at \place{the Fishstairs} below the north bridge. Eels, salted perch, rumours sold at noon but already in the air at dawn. \person{Mira Silt} in her own element after her background turns: slime dried on her sleeves, the missing top of her left ear explained by a hook the scar contradicts, eel-bones flicked against her thumb. She short-weights Berr Clay's order by two eels and has done it for six years; he has paid her two marks short for the same six years. She will not sit where a slate is open and answers every question with a question and then her own answer: *He's warm. That's all I'll say on a slate, and I'm saying it off the slate.* A cord is touched when a number is spoken (T025), but the knot-string itself is not shown plainly until Day 9. Do not let her say she knows Corin is Dessa's son.
- **Scene 3 (Day 9).** Dessa at the edge of the market with the cough that is a vessel going thin, mistaken for the pits; the pale scar rubbed; the nineteen steps counted because counting keeps something larger out of the room. Mira's knot-string is glimpsed --- a cord of grey wool touched when a number is spoken, a separate cord for the child in \place{Nine Pots} she has never knocked on (T059). She gives the boy a crust quickly, briskly, before anyone can make a speech about it. Close on the boy watching the cord and the eel-bones and understanding that this woman keeps accounts in a material no Court can confiscate, though he has no words for that yet. \person{Fenna Clay} is planted only as a silhouette across the market with green at her mouth and no dialogue (T050/T051).

**Characters on stage.** Mira Silt (the full idiolect, fastest speaker in the book), Dessa Silt, Tarn, Sella (briefly), Berr Clay (a short market exchange), gulls and eel-sellers as walk-ons. Jorra stays in the wheel-shed. Ostrowski stays offstage.

## Chapter 6 — Counting What There Is

**Days covered.** Days 10–14 --- Stone 16 to Stone 20, Y.H. 812, Sixthday through Tenthday. Day 14 is Account-day. The Five are eleven days off and the household begins to count what it has.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 10).** A household counting fuel and bread against the days before Kindling. Sella touches the widow's mark before she opens the kiln and at no other time; the banked coals under the ash are the condition on which she holds the house. Alda comes to see the boy, touches the tin inside her bodice once, writes something on her own slate that she does not leave lying about, and goes on giving exact dates after the question has stopped (T023). The boy's count extends from the boards to the stack of kindling --- four, four, a gap where the fifth should be --- and the gap is what he notices.
- **Scene 2 (Day 14, Account-day).** The Cross again, in deep cold. Berr Clay reads the account in the district's grammar; a collector's clerk with a soft passive voice handles a slate whose upper line is in the neat dark hand, and the boy catches the pause a **fourth** time --- once at the flare, once on the birth-slate, once at the Cross on Stone 10, and now here --- still unread, still unjoined. Keep the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in it (T003) filed separately; do not let the fourth sighting join the three. Sella's uncalled debt against the tiles is spoken around, never named. The crowd leans in. Nothing is proved. If the clerk is a different walk-on from Chapter 3's, say so in `characters/_minor.md`; give him a sheet only if he speaks twice. He is not Marra.
- **Scene 3.** Back at the Vail house at dusk: the ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack; a woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and is contradicted by everybody who wants it to come late. Dan reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. He notices the Court's room is another version of the death-house and the birth room --- bench, door, draught, the same gap --- and makes nothing of the repetition (T081 advance, not a recognition). Close on the Ash ridge thicker by another finger's width and on the day-count the household keeps badly and he keeps exactly, with the Five still ahead. Forward motion: a fourth sighting of the hand, a crack in the ice, a coming that cannot be hurried.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed, Berr Clay (short), a collector's clerk, the unnamed market voices. Dessa unwell but offstage.

## Chapter 8 — The Five Cold Days

**Days covered.** Days 25–30 --- Kindling, Weighing, the Long Night, the Remembering and the Last Day (Days 25–29, Y.H. 812), then Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813 (Day 30, Firstday, the Opening of the Slates). Give **each of the five holy days its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, and Day 30 its own entry too. No day is poured into another, and no childhood is summarised. Check `bible/calendar.md` before writing; the Five have names, not tend-days, and no market is held during them.

**Dated spine, one entry each.**
- **Day 25, Kindling.** Every hearth in Sill lit from the Hearth-flame. The boy watching a fire come into the house from somewhere he cannot see and cannot reach; the count extended to the light it makes. Sella touches the widow's mark before the kiln and at no other time.
- **Day 26, Weighing.** The year's measures read aloud in a clean voice. Somebody else's whole return announced for somebody who is not in the room; the grammar Berr used at the Cross now wearing a temple's clothes (T047 advance). The boy hears a figure and says nothing. Plant the reading of a name the household will not speak.
- **Day 27, the Long Night.** No fire after dusk. Stories of the Return told in the dark; the boy listens to the voices instead of the flames and counts the gaps between them. The bell's four-four-and-a-gap comes back in the telling of the year.
- **Day 28, the Remembering.** Names of the dead spoken. Garl's name is not spoken in the Vail house; the coat stays on the peg and the ridge stays unswept. The boy counts the silence where the name should be and files it with the other gaps.
- **Day 29, the Last Day.** Bread baked; the grey Ash in the corners left where it lies to be burned next morning and called dealt with. Sella will not let anyone sweep early. The boy watches the ridge measured by a thumb's width and understands that a thing can be left in place and still be accounted for.
- **Day 30, Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813.** The new year opens with the banked coals still banked and the Ash burned and called gone. The count goes on into a year the district has already entered on its slates. Forward motion into the thaw and the work of the slip pits, with the stone still unmentioned in House Silt and the figure still one in twelve.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed (in the Remembering and the second notes), Dessa Silt (the cough worse, still called weather), Mira Silt (a crust, briskly), the household and market voices. Berr Clay may be glimpsed at the Weighing but does not explain anything.

## Chapter 9 — The River Under the Ice

**Days covered.** Days 31–44 --- Ilmen 2 to Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813, Secondday to Fifthday. A passage chapter: **every day gets its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, fourteen entries, no day poured into another. The Fish Blessing falls on Day 44. Dessa dies on Day 38 and stays dead.

- **Day 32, Ilmen 3, Thirdday.** Tarn has one year of the seven left and will not count it; he agrees with everything so thoroughly that there is nowhere for an argument to go. The boy's hand outside the fold goes up an inch toward the broom and comes down.
- **Day 33, Ilmen 4, Fourthday.** Jorra comes in from the back room with slate dust and says nothing. The men whose names are not spoken are not named; the Broken Slates are planted toward Day ~160 without being called by that name. Tarn's ear is not spoken of.

- **Day 36, Ilmen 7, Seventhday.** The ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack. Somebody comes back from looking at it with a number they do not care to give (T077, now a habit of the district rather than a mystery to be explained).
- **Day 37, Ilmen 8, Eighthday.** Dessa's cough is worse and is still weather. Her thumb goes to the sieve-wire scar, the nineteen steps go into the thumb, the door-shut smile comes down. The boy cannot name what he sees in her face.
- **Day 38, Ilmen 9, Ninthday.** Dessa drowns in the clay-heavy rise of the Slip Brook, uncollected. The boy, one month and a day old, is held on the bank and hears the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through --- the sixth hearing and the second from a person. The collector's clerk is at the Fishstairs; Alda arrives after the vessel has given. He cannot speak, cannot move, cannot look away. He will remember the exact shape of her hand on the basket handle. - **Day 40, Ilmen 11, Firstday.** Sella does not send for a collector for the woman in the brook, and nobody remarks on it. - **Day 42, Ilmen 13, Thirdday.** A tin above Dessa's bed holds a scrap of cloth from the birth; the boy notices the object without knowing what it is and does not enter it anywhere. Sella's private roll remains unshown.

## Continuity rules for these five chapters

1. **Days 7–44 only**, in order, with no gaps: Ch 5 = 7–9, Ch 6 = 10–14, Ch 7 = 15–24 with ten dated entries, Ch 8 = 25–30 with six dated entries, Ch 9 = 31–44 with fourteen dated entries. Day 6 is finished and must not be revisited as a new scene. Check `bible/calendar.md` for every tend-day: Day 42 is **Ilmen 13, Thirdday**; Day 44 is Ilmen 15, Fifthday. No day is skipped and no childhood is summarised.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-collection scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation. Ch 4 used it for the Afterdrop and the turned chart-note; do not make it a habit.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only** --- Ostrowski's two duties in Ch 3, a half-line of a call. Chapter 4 tagged none; let the market voice and the bay stand on their own until a remembered line earns the tag.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the blanket, the hand that cannot reach the cup or lift to its own chin. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The stone and the crust came from the Vail hearth and were entered nowhere, and the house that paid twice has now taken a measure from the district. When somebody pays in Chapters 6, 7 or 9, let the reader be able to name who. Dessa's death is a taking nobody enters and the boy cannot refuse.
8. **The two hands stay separate in his mind.** He has set the pause at the flare beside the pause on the birth-slate and the pause on the clerk's slate, and has not joined them. Ch 6 gives a fourth sighting and Ch 7 a fifth; do not do the joining for him. The Drawing collector's neat hand has no pause at all and stays in its own place. It is paid off in Slate Row years away.
9. **The ear keeps its objects in order.** Cup (Ch 2), pot (Ch 2), Vail pot through the wall (Ch 4), dying man at the Drawing (Ch 4), district's slate twice (Ch 3), the district's slate again (Ch 6 and Ch 7), a pot cracking in the yard (Ch 7), a nearly full pot (Ch 9), Dessa on the bank (Ch 9). Five hearings from four sources stand at the close of Ch 4; Tarn hears some of them and says nothing. Each is useless and none is named in Grathic.
10. **When a chapter closes, update `memory/state.md`, add one line per day to `memory/timeline.md`, append to `memory/story-so-far.md`, give every character who appeared a Chapter Log line, move planted threads from `open` to `planted` in `plan/threads.md`, and add any new coined terms to `bible/glossary.md`. Record any new place in `memory/places.md` before it appears on a map.** Walk-ons go in `characters/_minor.md`, and `meta.json` "characters" lists only slugs that have sheets. Orra Weir needs a full sheet before Ch 9; the Drawing collector, the neighbour mother, the rekindled child, the death-house women and the neighbour at the Drawing are walk-ons already logged.
11. **Keep the engines turning.** Alda's lie curdles toward Dessa's death; Mira's knot-string and Sella's silence are both second records; Tarn's ear makes him valuable and therefore unsafe; Berr's box stays locked and his reason stays good; Jorra's dust and the unnameable back room point at the Broken Slates; the Drawing collector's grey fingertips wait to be read beside Marra's (T080); the death-house, the birth room and the Weighing Hall are one room repeated (T081); Fenna's green at the mouth is planted without dialogue. Twists, betrayals and romances must be in the ground before they are harvested, and every one of them is logged in `plan/threads.md`.
