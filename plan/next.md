## Continuity rules for these chapters

1. **Days 129 onward only**, in order, with no gaps and no day poured into another: Ch 30 = 129–131 (Reed 10 an Account-day), Ch 31 = 132–134, Ch 32 = 135–137, Ch 33 = 138–140 (Reed 20 an Account-day), Ch 34 = 141–143. Days 120–128 are finished and must not be revisited as new scenes. Day 129 is Reed 10 and the first Account-day of Reed, Day 139 is Reed 20 and the second. A passage chapter never exceeds seven lived days.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-collection scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation and never more than once in a chapter. Chapter 29 paid none and wrote no `\memory{}` tag; let the surgeon's register carry the old life until a remembered line earns a break. Do not tag a `\memory{}` to it.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only.** Chapters 4–29 tagged none; let the market voice and the hospital stand on their own until a remembered line earns the tag.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture. An arm carrying a bundle may be read like the surgeon's ninety-second sorting; that is a comparison inside his own head, never a spoken speech to a neighbour.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the fold, the hand that cannot reach the cup or lift to its own chin, the frozen thread at the mouth nobody wipes, cold at cheek--temple--wrist with the wool ridden back. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The carried fire, the stone, the gruel, the eels and the crusts came from somebody's fire and were entered nowhere; Sella paid twice and has taken a measure from the district. Mira's short eels and Berr's short marks are the same arithmetic at a different stall. Dessa's death is a taking nobody enters and the boy cannot refuse. The coal the gull took, the smoky crust that went out of the fold and the pale river stone that went out of House Silt are three more subtractions entered in no account, and the boy keeps all three. The flood counted in loaves and glances is a fourth; the warm barley loaf from a man from Slate Row is a fifth, with no slate between giver and receiver. The hands going into the planting strips are a sixth; the two empty places on the shelf and the empty place in the fold are a seventh, an eighth and a ninth, held apart in two rooms. A loaf carried out under a woman's arm on Seed 6 is a tenth; the hand that does not come back out of the strips in Ch 26 is an eleventh; the reed-cutter's tally at the Weir in Ch 27 is a twelfth; the charcoal tally under the Vail shelf planted in Ch 30 is a thirteenth; the near-saying held at the teeth in Ch 28 and stopped a second time in Ch 29 is a fourteenth, a kept thing that is owed with no payer named. Each should gather interest without being paid in Chapters 30–34.
8. **The hands stay separate in his mind.** The five pauses in the neat dark hand must not be joined; the Drawing collector's hand has no pause and stays in its own place; the officer's clean voice is a third thing filed apart; the twice-wiped thumb of Berr stays on its own tray. The neighbour at the loaf shelf's hand, which went to the board before her eyes went to the boy, is a seventh shape on its own place (T097), and her sayings and the other neighbours' are tellings on that place, still not paid; **no seventh shaping came in Ch 29** and the two sayings carried into Reed stay on two places that do not touch. The watcher's late turn at the market on Seed 19 is a third shape beside the two tellings, joined to neither (T097/T034). Do not allow a fourth likeness to make a cause.
