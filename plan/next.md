## Chapter 45 — the middle of Ember, and the small edge

**Days covered.** Days 186–188, Ember 7–9, Seventhday to Ninthday.

**Opening moment of Chapter 45.** Open on Day 186, Ember 7, Seventhday, before first grey under the Vail kiln-house roof, with the boy one hundred and eighty-six days old and six months and six days, and nobody under any roof saying so. Do not recap the close of Chapter 44. The first smell is hot clay under tallow and wet wool with the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner; the first sound is the kiln drawing breath in fours with a gap, not a bell. Under the floor the carried fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and eighty-six days; through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; overhead the boards keep four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb — three counts in three places that do not become one. The crust lies stiff and leathery two inches from his ear, gone a little harder in the dry kiln heat, its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread of drool is frozen and unwiped; his hand goes up an inch toward the river's note and comes down against the fold. Sella gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket, says *the house*, keeps her half-pace, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze without touching it, sets the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand with her hand resting a half-beat longer at the setting, and leaves the widow's mark where it hung, because a morning and a shelf are not openings and the one opening was before the kiln on Ember 1. The stone lies back in its pot in the loft, cold and unreconciled; the boy does not remember the holding as a holding made again. Forward, not closed.

**Intentions.** This is the chapter where the gathered danger of Tallow and the first working week can at last be paid in a small, non-fatal edge: a burned hand, a pot that sings itself out early under a hand that set it down wrong, a glaze-room hand carried out of the kiln with the sweet-sickness on his breath, a room nobody enters twice. Choose one edge, not a catalogue, and make it physical and particular; pay it without a lesson, without a payer named to the keeping and without an entry in any slate. Keep it small: no major character dies, no thread is closed, no doctrine is made. Let the boy read the body exactly — the pale line at the gum, the burn's edge, the way a hand is held — and be unable to reach any of it or speak any of it. The dust ground small as meal, the sweetish lead edge riding a wrong wind, the pot giving its note before its time and Jorra's withheld knowing are the things that gather into the edge; nothing in the chapter may turn them into a fourth near-full answer. Keep the first day free of any recap of an Account-day: there is no Cross, no bell, no Berr, no Hessa, no clerk, no green-mouthed woman and no foundling on stage in these three days. Keep the count exact at one hundred and eighty-six, seven and eight; the fire's payer unnamed; the mark untouched; the nine stoppings nine; the Seed 16 breath unspent; the loan uncalled; the charcoal unread. Tarn's ear-sentence may come to his teeth once more and stop short of the word, an interval and not a tenth stopping, or hold short of the threshold as on Tallow 18; in neither case is a tenth stood up before the seventh year is earned.

**Characters on stage.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Corin and Jorra Vail as the shed, the wheel, the damper and the work require. If the edge is paid in the yard, let the hurt hand be a walk-on and give it a line in `_minor.md`, not a sheet; if a glaze-room figure is carried out, keep him unnamed, given the pale line at the gum and the sweet breath and no speech. Berr Clay, Hessa Rale and the soft-cuffed clerk stay offstage until the Account-day; Mira Silt, Orra Weir, Alda Reed, Marra and the reed-cutter offstage; Dessa dead and unnamed; the foundling offstage with his sixth glimpse unspent. No new named character may speak without a full sheet first.
