## Chapter 3 — One in Twelve

**Days covered.** Day 4 --- Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday. Account-day and the last market before the Five Cold Days.

**Opening moment.** Open on the last steps of the walk from the \place{Clay Yard} to the \place{Cross}: the boy in a basket under a fold of wool, the kiln's breath going out behind him and the market arriving in layers --- pigs, clay dust, the moot shouting, a bell that is not a church bell. He counts the steps to a new number. Then \person{Berr Clay} wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate and says *one in twelve, as the notice says*, and the shouting stops because the crowd has to lean in to hear him. In the basket Dan hears the slate give back a flat thing, the same dead sound the cup gave across the birth room two days ago, now the whole district's account under a knuckle he cannot lift. Do not say that the two sounds are the same in Grathic; do not let anyone tap a slate for him.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1.** The Cross at market: two hundred people, eight pigs, one assessor. Berr never says *I*; he says *the district* and *the account*. He apologises in the form of an instruction. Sella has brought the boy in a basket and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*; she does not set the basket where a slate is open. The crowd leans in. The flat note of the slate is a physical fact in the scene, not a clue the narration points at.
- **Scene 2.** \person{Lena Ostrowski}'s voice breaks through the market noise: \memory{Marchetti. You're back on in ten.}, and the sentence about the first duty and the second. The clay yard, the pigs and the trauma bay occupy the same paragraph for one breath, then the market returns. Do not explain the shift or tag the memory.
- **Scene 3.** A collector's clerk at the edge of the market handles a slate with a name in a strange neat hand; the name was written before the face and nobody in the market remarks on it. Berr speaks to Sella about her widow's mark as a kindness that is entered. The uncalled debt Garl left against a shipment of tiles hangs in the room without being named. Close on Ash carried out toward the \place{Bone Orchard}, or on the slate read a second time --- forward motion into the collection of the Hearth Drawing.

**Characters on stage and their voices.** Berr Clay (kiln-master's proverb braided with Court grammar; apologises in the form of an instruction), Sella Vail (instruction to the pot, never a finished feeling), Alda Reed (dates instead of comfort), Mira Silt in the background (question, then her own answer), Ostrowski as a remembered voice, the unnamed assessor and a collector's clerk (one line, one gesture; name him in `characters/_minor.md` if he speaks twice). This is Tarn and Jorra's first chapter offstage; keep them out of it.

**Threads planted / advanced.** T015 (the deduction), T041 (Berr's locked box, not yet shown), T008 (the levy as the language in which a child's life is priced), T046 (Ostrowski's two duties), T047 (the first outward echo in Berr's grammar, after the interior echo of Ch 2), T044 (the call as an absence), T061 (the flat note carried into the market), T071 (counting the room), T005 (the dark hand seen a second time), T077 (the number the district gives out).

## Chapter 4 — The Hearth Drawing

**Days covered.** Days 5–6 --- Stone 11 and Stone 12, Y.H. 812, Firstday and Secondday. Stone 12 is the Hearth Drawing, first collection of the year.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 5).** A quiet day in the Vail house: the banked coals, Sella touching the widow's mark before she opens the kiln and at no other time, the boy's fifth day of failing to keep a blanket on. He hears the full note of a Vail pot through the wall of the shed and is corrected by nobody, because nobody else says what it is. Tarn's broom stops at it too and he says nothing (T076); a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. A neighbour's child is brought to Alda with the second chill.
- **Scene 2 (Day 6, the Drawing).** A collection witnessed from a basket at a neighbour's house. The cold iron tube against a way; the collector's fingers; the small flat knock of a vessel that has cracked all the way through, which Dan recognises as the sound a body makes when it gives --- a sound he will hear again on the bank of the Slip Brook. The measure is booked. The account stands. Keep the collector's hands exact and the collector's voice in the soft passive; he is an officer, not a monster, and has a reason.
- **Scene 3.** The rekindled infant worse on the third day of a return. The midwives call it the second chill and explain it as a graft settling; Dan knows the afterdrop from rewarming patients and is corrected by every woman in the room. He says nothing. He reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. Close on the Ash ridge in the corner of the death-house, thicker by a finger's width, and the simple fact that the year's first measure has been taken.

**Characters on stage.** Alda Reed (instruction, dates, the tin touched once inside her bodice), the collector (a walk-on with one function; one line in the Court's grammar and a name in `characters/_minor.md` if he speaks twice), Sella Vail, Tarn (offstage but heard), Dessa in the background with the cough, the unnamed neighbour family.

**Threads planted.** T012 (the Second Want, struck from the record), T013 (the Ash thickens), T009 (a death left where it is), T004 (the phone-pocket reach), T061 (the flat note), T007 (Alda's tin), T076 (the second ear in the house witnessed only by the reader).

**Indexing.** `\lore{the Hearth Drawing}`, `\lore{the Afterdrop}`, `\lore{the Second Want}` (index the concept only when a character says the midwife's word; do not use the Court's term in narration yet), `\person{Alda Reed}`, `\place{Low Kiln}`, `\lore{cold iron}`, `\lore{collection}`.

**What is withheld.** No experiment yet, no clamp, no heresy. The second cause of the second chill is a fact the narration does not know how to state. Do not let anyone explain the Return.

## Chapter 5 — Nineteen Steps

**Days covered.** Days 7–9 --- Stone 13, Stone 14 and Stone 15, Y.H. 812. Day 7 is Thirdday; dawn at the Fishstairs comes on Day 8. Fill Day 7 so the reckoning has no gap between the Drawing and the market.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 7).** The Vail household at first light: Tarn sweeping the yard a second time, the small knife back in his pocket; Jorra at the wheel; Sella feeding somebody else's child from her own dish. The boy turns his head toward a pot before anyone taps it. Tarn sees and says nothing, because a clay-bound hand who can hear the full note is worth more than his debt --- and because a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. Sella touches the widow's mark before the kiln and at no other time.
- **Scene 2 (Day 8).** Dawn at the \place{Fishstairs} below the north bridge. Eels, salted perch, rumours sold at noon but already in the air at dawn. \person{Mira Silt} with slime dried on her sleeves, the missing top of her left ear explained by a hook that the scar contradicts, the eel-bones flicked against her thumb. She short-weights Berr Clay's order by two eels and has done it for six years; he has paid her two marks short for the same six years. She will not sit at a table where a slate is open and answers every question with a question and then her own answer. *He's warm. That's all I'll say on a slate, and I'm saying it off the slate.*
- **Scene 3 (Day 9).** Dessa at the edge of the market with the cough that is a vessel going thin, mistaken for the pits; the pale scar rubbed; the nineteen steps counted because counting keeps something larger out of the room. Mira's knot-string is glimpsed --- a cord of grey wool touched when a number is spoken, a separate cord for the child in \place{Nine Pots} she has never knocked on. Mira tells Dan nothing and gives him a crust quickly, briskly, before anyone can make a speech about it. Close with the boy watching the cord and the eel-bones and understanding that this woman keeps accounts in a material no Court can confiscate, though he has no words for that yet.

**Characters on stage.** Mira Silt (the full idiolect, fastest speaker in the book), Dessa Silt, Tarn, Sella (briefly), Berr Clay (a short market exchange), gulls and eel-sellers as walk-ons.

**Threads planted.** T025 (Mira's knots and barley), T059 (the child in Nine Pots), T008 (Dessa's lie and the Forty, still unspoken), T036 (Tarn's ear witnessed), T060 (brisk kindness), T050/T051 seeded only if Fenna is glimpsed across the market --- keep her to a silhouette with green at the mouth and no dialogue in this chapter.

**Indexing.** `\person{Mira Silt}`, `\person{Dessa Silt}`, `\place{the Fishstairs}`, `\place{the Weir}`, `\place{Nine Pots}`, `\lore{eel-bones}`, `\lore{knot-string}`.

## Chapter 6 — Counting What There Is

**Days covered.** Days 10–14 --- Stone 16 to Stone 20, Y.H. 812, Sixthday through Tenthday. Day 14 is Account-day. The Five are eleven days off and the household begins to count what it has.

**Working scenes.**
- **Scene 1 (Day 10).** A household counting fuel and bread against the days before Kindling. Sella touches the widow's mark before she opens the kiln and at no other time; the banked coals under the ash are the condition on which she holds the house. Alda comes to see the boy, touches the tin inside her bodice once, and writes something on her own slate that she does not leave lying about. The boy's count extends from the boards to the stack of kindling --- four, four, a gap where the fifth should be --- and the gap is what he notices.
- **Scene 2 (Day 14, Account-day).** The Cross again, in deep cold. Berr Clay reads the account in the district's grammar; a collector's clerk with a soft passive voice handles a slate whose upper line is in the neat dark hand, and the boy catches the pause a second time. Sella's uncalled debt against the tiles is spoken around, never named. The crowd leans in. Nothing is proved and the boy knows he has seen the hand twice.
- **Scene 3.** Back at the Vail house at dusk: the ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack; a woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and is contradicted by everybody who wants it to come late. Dan reaches for a pocket with no phone in it. Close on the Ash ridge thicker by another finger's width and on the day-count the household keeps badly and he keeps exactly, with the Five still ahead. Forward momentum: a second sighting of the hand, a crack in the ice, a coming that cannot be hurried.

## Chapter 7 — The Number They Give Out

**Days covered.** Days 15–24 --- Stone 21 to Stone 30, Y.H. 812, Firstday through Tenthday. Day 24 is Account-day and the last market of the year. This is a passage chapter: **every day gets its own dated entry of at least a paragraph**, ten entries, no day poured into another. The Five (Days 25–29) are held for Chapter 8 and must each be dated there.

**Dated spine, one entry each.**
- **Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday.** The household counts fuel and bread into the tend. Sella touches the widow's mark before the kiln and at no other time. The boy's count extends to the stack of kindling: four, four, a gap. Somebody has been out to look at the ice and come back with a number they will not give.
- **Day 16, Stone 22, Secondday.** Tarn has one year of his term left and will not count it. He is sent for a collector for a neighbour and goes; a flat note comes past the door on a vessel somebody is carrying, and he turns his head a degree.
- **Day 17, Stone 23, Thirdday.** Jorra comes in from the back room with slate dust on him and says nothing about where he has been. Sella gets quieter; the boy reads the not-looking the way he once read a resident who would not meet the senior's eye.
- **Day 18, Stone 24, Fourthday.** Alda's tin is touched once inside her bodice and a second note is written that she does not leave lying about (T023, seeded toward the floorboard).
- **Day 19, Stone 25, Fifthday.** The cold deepens. A pot in the yard cracks with a sound Dan knows and nobody calls a sound. He cannot turn his head far enough toward it.
- **Day 20, Stone 26, Sixthday.** Account-day. Berr reads the account at the Cross. The boy hears a flat note a third time --- from the district's own slate --- and keeps it with the other two without joining them.
- **Day 21, Stone 27, Seventhday.** A woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and is contradicted by everybody who wants it to come late. The number for the Five changes in the telling from three to two to whatever the district decides to give out that morning (T077).
- **Day 22, Stone 28, Eighthday.** Mira Silt passes the yard with eels and touches a knot when a number is spoken; she gives the boy a crust briskly, before anyone can make a speech of it. Dessa coughs from the pits and calls it weather.
- **Day 23, Stone 29, Ninthday.** The yard prepares for the Last Day sweeping. The Ash ridge in the Vail house is measured against another house by the width of a thumb; Garl's coat stays on its peg and nobody takes it down.
- **Day 24, Stone 30, Tenthday.** Account-day and the last market of the year. The household's stores are counted and the Five are spoken of as three days off by a district that has counted what it has and does not want to say it. At the edge of the market a collector's clerk handles a slate with a name in the neat dark hand a third time; the boy sees the pause and still cannot read it. Close on the Vail kiln-house door, the banked coals, and the fire keeping its four and four and its gap --- forward motion into Kindling, when the year's fire is lit from the Hearth and the first measure is taken.

## Continuity rules for these five chapters

1. **Days 4–24 only**, in order, with no gaps: Ch 3 = 4, Ch 4 = 5–6, Ch 5 = 7–9, Ch 6 = 10–14, Ch 7 = 15–24 with ten dated entries. After Chapter 7 the Five (Days 25–29) must each have a dated entry in Chapter 8, and Day 30 is Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813. No day is skipped and no childhood is summarised.
2. **Point of view stays with Dan.** No Court scene, no inside-the-Hearth scene, not yet. The first such departure is far off.
3. **Present tense only for Chicago breaking through**, one or two sentences at a time, never for anticipation.
4. **`\memory{...}` for remembered words only** --- Des's line, Ostrowski's correction, a half-line of a call. Never for a general recollection.
5. **No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.** A face is not like a screen here. It may be like one in Chicago, where the screen is part of the texture.
6. **The body humiliates him** in every chapter: drool, the useless head, the blanket, the hand that cannot reach the cup. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
7. **Every cost has a payer.** The stone and the crust came from the Vail hearth and were entered nowhere, and the house that paid twice has now taken the boy. When somebody pays in Chapters 6 or 7, let the reader be able to name who.
8. **The two hands stay separate in his mind.** He has set the pause at the flare beside the pause on the slate and has not joined them. Do not do the joining for him; it is paid off in Slate Row years away.
9. **The ear keeps its objects.** Cup (Ch 2), pot (Ch 2), Vail pot through the wall (Ch 4), cracked vessel at the Drawing (Ch 4), Berr's slate (Ch 3), the district's slate again (Ch 6 and 7). Three flat notes by the end of Chapter 7, each useless, none named in Grathic. Tarn hears two of them and says nothing.
