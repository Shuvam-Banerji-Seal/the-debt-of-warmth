The boy, one hundred and forty-six days old and four months and twenty-six days, told by nobody, lay on his bed and never in the Debt Loft; Sella had carried him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, gone around a pan set out to dry and a trough with a skin of ice, set him on his bed, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it went away; she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said *The house will keep it*, counted under her breath toward no opening, pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, neither looked toward the bed nor touched the widow's mark, and went to her own bed without explaining the count.
