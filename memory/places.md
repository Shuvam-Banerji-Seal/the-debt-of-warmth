# Gazetteer — every place named so far

*Private working file. Canonical names are given exactly as they must be written with `\place{...}` in the scenes. Distances are in leagues (a league is three Sarnish miles, a little under three kilometres), and direction is measured from **Sill** unless the entry says otherwise. Where a place lies inside another place, the containing place is given first in the relations.*

---

## I. The world and the great powers

### Sarn
**Kind:** the world. **Region:** all.
The bowl of warm land between the Hearth beneath and the Waste beyond the Rim; every vessel in it is a small failing dam.
**Relations:** The Hearth lies beneath the Keel to the south-east; the Waste begins beyond the Rim to the west; the Sallow Sea fills the north-east.

### The Hearth
**Kind:** source, chamber of stone. **Region:** beneath the Hearthseat, on the Keel.
The great source of the Warm, said to hold what the world had when it was made; its fire is kept behind iron in the Weighing Hall.
**Relations:** Beneath the Hearthseat, about 150 leagues south-east of Sill by the Clay and Hearth Roads; the Nine Wells lie within twenty leagues of it.

### The Waste
**Kind:** waste, cold country outside the world. **Region:** beyond the Rim.
The great sink where everything unheld goes; travellers who go far into it come back older and thinner.
**Relations:** Begins beyond the Rim, roughly 200 leagues west of Sill; no Court collectors go there.

### The Rim
**Kind:** mountain chain. **Region:** west and north of the bowl.
The cold mountains that ring Sarn, broken by a few passes; the Listening Stones stand in its high places.
**Relations:** Nearest spur about 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill; the Cold Pass cuts it at 130 leagues; Keld Keep lies against it.

### The Keel
**Kind:** low ridge. **Region:** centre-south-east of the bowl.
A long low ridge of stone over the Hearth, mildly warm in winter and therefore crowded.
**Relations:** About 140 leagues south-east of Sill; the Hearthseat stands on it; the Nine Wells rise in it.

### The Sallow Sea
**Kind:** inland sea. **Region:** north-east.
A cold inland sea the colour of skimmed milk, whose tides run against the wind and are said to follow the Warm rather than the moon.
**Relations:** Its shore lies about 70 leagues north-east of Sill; the Ilm drains into it; Vhal stands on its coast at 80 leagues; the Glaze Cliffs are on the same shore.

### The Warm Court
**Kind:** realm, institution, church. **Region:** everywhere in theory; the Hearthseat in fact.
The Keeping: assessors, collectors and bankers who own the Hearth, the Returns and the slates, and speak of theft as accounts.
**Relations:** Seated in the Hearthseat, 150 leagues south-east of Sill; its factor in Greybank is 18 leagues east of Sill and is the Court's nearest physical presence to Grath.

### The Hearthseat
**Kind:** city. **Region:** the Keel.
The Court's city, built over the Hearth, where the Weighing Hall, the sealed files and fewer than twenty Kept vessels are kept.
**Relations:** 150 leagues south-east of Sill: 18 leagues east by the Clay Road to Greybank, then 140 leagues south-east by the Hearth Road; the Keel under it; the Nine Wells within twenty leagues.

### The Weighing Hall
**Kind:** hall, temple and counting room. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The room where the year's measures are read at the Weighing and where the Hearth-fire burns behind a grating of cold iron.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, above the Hearth chamber.

### The College of Assessors
**Kind:** academy, counting house. **Region:** the Hearthseat, on the Keel.
The school where the value of a life is taught and Ledgerhand written; it holds the only right to enter a lawful deduction and keeps a second file of names removed from the rolls. Provost Wessen teaches here.
**Relations:** In Slate Row in the Hearthseat, 150 leagues south-east of Sill; an assessor's house stands in Sill's own Slate Row, built in Y.H. 776.

*The close ground of the Court city drawn on `atlas/the-hearthseat`: the rooms a surveyor of the Keeping could put a name to. Direction is from the Weighing Hall.*

### Slate Row of the Hearthseat
**Kind:** court quarter, street. **Region:** the Hearthseat, on the Keel.
The Hearthseat's own Court quarter, holding the College of Assessors and the officers' counting rooms; the year's measures are read aloud here at the Weighing. On a chart it is lettered simply *Slate Row*, but in scenes it must be told apart from Sill's quarter of the same name: `\place[Slate Row of the Hearthseat]{Slate Row}`.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, 150 leagues south-east of Sill; the College of Assessors stands here.

### The Hearth-fire
**Kind:** flame, wonder. **Region:** the Weighing Hall, the Hearthseat.
The never-out flame behind a grating of cold iron, said to be a tongue of the Hearth drawn up through the Keel; it has never gone out and no priest has ever been allowed to touch it. Even the Kept are not asked to stand near it.
**Relations:** In the Weighing Hall, above the Hearth chamber; 150 leagues south-east of Sill.

### The Weighing notice
**Kind:** printed notice, board. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The notice set up by the Weighing Hall on which one part in twelve is printed as the deduction; it is the figure the districts read and the figure the College teaches its own to write against.
**Relations:** Beside the Weighing Hall in the Hearthseat.

### The Steward's room
**Kind:** room, office. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The room of the head of the Keeping, off the Weighing Hall, where a slate is kept flat under the fingertips while a person is still speaking and where the sealed file is read.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, beside the Weighing Hall.

### The sealed files
**Kind:** archive, strong-room. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The Court's locked archive, kept by the Steward of the Hearth. It holds the copy of the Keld survey's crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588, the private authorisation of a whole return of forty unspent measures with the purchaser left unrecorded, and the Court's private answer to the Unbanked. Haldre's suppressed paper is kept in a drawer beside it.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, near the Weighing Hall.

### The bank at the Hearthseat
**Kind:** bank, hall. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The first of the Keeping's two banks, where measures drawn in the districts are pressed into newborn vessels; the second stands at Greybank. The rolls of the dead are carried forward here and never closed.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat on the Keel; the bank at Greybank is 150 leagues west-north-west by road.

### The rooms of the Kept
**Kind:** chambers. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
Fewer than twenty rooms kept near the fire for the Kept vessels, who hold three hundred measures and feel nothing at all.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, on the Keel.

### The back rooms of the Hearthseat
**Kind:** work rooms. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
Plain rooms behind the College where blent-work is said to be done — condemned in law and spoken of only in the back rooms. The law gives a blent person the measure of the vessel its donor had, which in practice means no measure at all.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, behind the College of Assessors in Slate Row.

### Vhalen
**Kind:** realm, merchant republic. **Region:** Sallow coast.
A ring of port towns governed by a Council of Tides, living on salt, glass, rope, ships and contracts written on paper that rots.
**Relations:** North-east of Sill, 80 leagues by the Salt Road; capital Vhal on the Sallow shore; the Glaze Cliffs on the same coast.

### Vhal
**Kind:** port city. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow coast.
The principal Vhaleni port, seat of the Council of Tides, with salt pans, glass furnaces and the tar-smelling council room.
**Relations:** 80 leagues north-east of Sill; at the northern end of the Salt Road; the Glaze Cliffs about five leagues along the coast.

### The Quay
**Kind:** district, waterfront. **Region:** Vhal, Vhalen.
The working waterfront of Vhal where boat-shares are held and pass through mothers; a man who marries into a boat-owning family is said to be come aboard and his opinion on the boat is counted at half. Pia of the Quay's mother holds a share here.
**Relations:** Inside Vhal, on the Sallow shore, 80 leagues north-east of Sill.

### The Salt Pans
**Kind:** works, salt flats. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow coast.
Shallow pans where sea water is left to sun and wind; the dippers bless the nets here at dawn and read the Sallow draw. Contracts written on salt-paper rot in the damp, which is considered a virtue.
**Relations:** Outside Vhal on the Sallow shore; the Glaze Cliffs lie a few leagues along the same coast.

### The Council room
**Kind:** council chamber, hall. **Region:** Vhal, Vhalen.
The Council of Tides meets in a room that smells of tar and wet rope; its decrees are written on salt-paper and rot in damp weather, which is held to be a virtue.
**Relations:** Inside Vhal, on the Quay, beside the Sallow shore, 80 leagues north-east of Sill.

### The tide-tally house
**Kind:** counting house. **Region:** the Quay, Vhal.
The house on the Quay where the tide-tallies are kept and where Kett of the Quay reads the draws against the salt-paper contracts; a sheet left in the damp is a debt made nothing.
**Relations:** On the Quay in Vhal, a short way from the Council room.

### The glass furnaces
**Kind:** works, furnaces. **Region:** Vhal, Vhalen.
The furnaces of Vhal where sand and lead from the Glaze Cliffs are melted into glass for sale as far as Sere; the men who work them revolted in Y.H. 626.
**Relations:** Inside Vhal, on the shore side east of the Quay; the Glaze Cliffs lie about five leagues along the coast.

### The pulled benches
**Kind:** galley benches, quay. **Region:** the Quay, Vhal.
The benches of the salt galleys where condemned criminals row a term of seven years, or until their hands give out; a Vhaleni hull is never long from the oars.
**Relations:** At the Quay in Vhal, beside the moored salt boats.

### The coast pass
**Kind:** pass, road. **Region:** between Greybank and Vhal, Sallow coast.
The low pass on the Salt Road where the clay country gives way to the salt coast; the road from Sill reaches Vhal by Greybank and this pass in eighty leagues.
**Relations:** On the Salt Road between Greybank and Vhal, roughly a day's walk south-west of Vhal.

### Keld
**Kind:** realm, mountain kingdom. **Region:** the Rim.
A high kingdom of iron, lead, coal and warm-stone, ruled by the Warden of the Rim, whose surveyors keep the record of the rim-seam.
**Relations:** 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill by the Rim Road; capital Keld Keep; the Cold Pass beyond it at 130 leagues.

### Keld Keep
**Kind:** fortress city. **Region:** Keld, the Rim.
The Warden's seat, where a man must state his measure before his name and where the deep survey shaft begins.
**Relations:** 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill; the First Seam beneath it; the Listening Stones about five leagues higher into the Rim; the Cold Pass ten leagues beyond.

*The Keep above and the deep survey beneath it drawn on `atlas/keld`: the walled city, the Warden's hall and the shaft mouth; the Cold Pass and the Listening Stones; the First Seam with its galleries, Duelling Floor, survey room and Ninth Gallery. Direction is from the Warden's hall.*

### The Warden's hall
**Kind:** hall, seat. **Region:** Keld Keep, Keld.
The Warden's hall inside the walls of Keld Keep, marked by a standard, where the Warden of the Rim holds his court and a man states his measure before his name.
**Relations:** Inside Keld Keep, near the mine-reeve's room and the shaft mouth.

### The mine-reeve's room
**Kind:** counting room. **Region:** Keld Keep, Keld.
The room in Keld Keep where the mine-reeve checks a man who has lied about his measure and counts every ember of iron and every life that goes down.
**Relations:** Inside Keld Keep, beside the Warden's hall.

### The shaft mouth
**Kind:** entrance, timbered head of a mine. **Region:** Keld Keep, Keld.
The timbered opening within Keld Keep where the rope goes down into the First Seam and where a child's Descent begins.
**Relations:** Inside Keld Keep, below the Warden's hall; the First Seam descends from it.

### The First Seam
**Kind:** mine, survey shaft. **Region:** Keld, beneath Keld Keep.
The deep working where the Keldish survey the oldest repairs and where a crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588 records a man with a branching red mark. Siv of the First Seam keeps the survey here.
**Relations:** Beneath Keld Keep, 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Duelling Floor
**Kind:** duelling floor, chamber. **Region:** the First Seam, Keld Keep.
A floor cut into the stone of the deep shaft where the Order of the Tenth Clamp fights with iron clamps and where a young Keldish person first opens a way in the forearm; Hald Iron is master of the floor. A surviving copy of Haldre's suppressed paper is kept nearby in a tin lined with warm-stone.
**Relations:** Inside the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep, 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Listening Stones
**Kind:** standing stones, survey site. **Region:** high Rim, Keld.
Old stones against which the Vorr sit for days and come away with numbers; the Keldish surveyors use them to read the rim-seam.
**Relations:** About five leagues higher into the Rim from Keld Keep, some 125 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Cold Pass
**Kind:** mountain pass, border. **Region:** the Rim, between Keld and Grath.
The pass closed by Karric VII in Y.H. 741 and fixed as the northern border by the agreement of Y.H. 747.
**Relations:** About 130 leagues west-north-west of Sill; ten leagues beyond Keld Keep on the Rim Road.

### The survey post
**Kind:** survey post, marker. **Region:** the Cold Pass, the Rim.
The survey post at the Cold Pass, set where the border was fixed in Y.H. 747; there is a post and no gate, and the Warden's surveyors do not promise to bring a man back.
**Relations:** At the Cold Pass, ten leagues beyond Keld Keep on the Rim Road.

### The Descent
**Kind:** passage, initiation way. **Region:** the First Seam, Keld Keep.
The way down the First Seam that every Keldish child takes at thirteen, when the measure is cut into the forearm and a warm-stone is given after the knife; a man who returns thin has the old numbers cut out and new ones put in.
**Relations:** From the shaft mouth in Keld Keep into the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep.

### The Ninth Gallery
**Kind:** mine gallery. **Region:** the First Seam, Keld Keep.
A deep gallery of the First Seam where the warm-stone is worked and where a leech of the gallery kept his remedies on the backs of survey sheets; the rock here sweats and the smell of hot lead gives the only warning.
**Relations:** Inside the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep, deeper than the Duelling Floor.

### The survey room
**Kind:** survey room, counting room. **Region:** the First Seam, Keld Keep.
The room off the Duelling Floor where the surveyors of the rim-seam keep their tally and where the crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588, with its branching red mark, is shown to those allowed to read it.
**Relations:** Off the Duelling Floor in the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep.

### The tin lined with warm-stone
**Kind:** tin, holding. **Region:** the survey room, the First Seam, Keld Keep.
The tin lined with warm-stone, kept in the survey room off the Duelling Floor, holding a surviving copy of Haldre's suppressed paper. The Order of the Tenth Clamp reads it once a year and does not act on it.
**Relations:** Inside the survey room, off the Duelling Floor in the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep.

### Sere
**Kind:** empire. **Region:** the far south.
A hot empire of grain, wine, bowls and open chattel slavery, whose people teach that a vessel was made to be emptied gloriously.
**Relations:** 300 leagues south of Sill; its northern reach stops at the Peat Line; the capital is the Bowl of Sere.

### The Bowl of Sere
**Kind:** imperial city. **Region:** Sere.
The capital, a city of open courts and public fires where debt-slates are burnt by lot and returned slaves are exhibited.
**Relations:** About 300 leagues south of Sill; the Peat Line is roughly 200 leagues north of it.

### The Ember bowl-house
**Kind:** great house. **Region:** the Bowl of Sere.
A house of the Ten Bowls that buys poor northern measures to make one beautiful whole return for display at feasts; Solene Ember was returned whole here in Y.H. 799, Y.H. 804 and Y.H. 809.
**Relations:** Inside the Bowl of Sere, about 300 leagues south of Sill; its northern interests are represented by the factor at Greybank.

### The Fenmarch
**Kind:** realm, reed confederacy. **Region:** south-west.
Peat and boardwalk villages governed by reeves chosen for standing longest in a bog; the market for cheap thin returns.
**Relations:** 90 leagues south-west of Sill; the Standing Post is its meeting place; the Empty Cantons lie between it and Grath; the Peat Line is its southern limit.

### The Standing Post
**Kind:** meeting place, village. **Region:** the Fenmarch.
A raised boardwalk and a post carved with reeves' marks where the Fen reeves settle fines and feuds.
**Relations:** About 90 leagues south-west of Sill; the only Fen place outsiders are shown.

### The Peat Line
**Kind:** border, wetland. **Region:** between the Fenmarch and Sere.
The wet frontier where Sere's northern advance stopped in Y.H. 634 and where the two powers trade, raid and buy measures.
**Relations:** Roughly 100 leagues south-west of Sill; about 200 leagues north of the Bowl of Sere.

## II. Grath — the birth country

### Grath
**Kind:** country, region. **Region:** north-west of the bowl.
A soft country of clay towns, peat bog and river valleys, under a Thane of Clay confirmed by the Court's factor at Greybank.
**Relations:** The Rim lies to the west and north-west; the Sallow Sea to the north-east; Sill is its principal town; Greybank its eastern gate.

### Sill
**Kind:** town. **Region:** Grath, on the Ilm.
A town of just over four thousand souls where the Ilm drops four feet over a ledge of hard stone; the birthplace of Corin.
**Relations:** On the Ilm; 18 leagues upriver (west) of Greybank; 1 league east of the Weir; 1 league north of the Old Kiln; at the meeting of the Clay Road and the river road.

### Low Kiln
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Sill, south bank below the sill.
The poor quarter of kilns, trodden-ash floors and thick grey Ash in the corners; Corin is born here and the Vail kiln-house stands here.
**Relations:** Inside Sill, on the south bank below the stone sill; High Kiln is about 200 paces up the terrace to the north; the Clay Yard is within the district, beside the Slip Brook.

### High Kiln
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Sill, north terrace.
The master potters' quarter on the steep street above Low Kiln, where the glaze is mixed and the lead smell hangs.
**Relations:** About 200 paces north and uphill of Low Kiln; the Cross is a short way west.

On Tallow 22, Y.H. 813 a kiln-master come up from this terrace on the wrong wind, with Ash on his cuffs and the sweetish lead edge of \lore{lead glaze} riding the air, stopped at the Clay Yard and listened to a pot under Jorra's hands; he said *That one's singing before its time. A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early*, took Jorra's silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost. On Tallow 21 a pot set down at the racks had already given a high thin note before its time with nobody making a lesson of it, and the boy filed both as dangers of the month, not as a fourth near-full answer. Ch 41.

### The Glaze Room
**Kind:** workshop. **Region:** High Kiln, Sill.
The room where the glaze is mixed and tasted by the glaze-mixers of House Clay; the lead in the mix causes the sweet-sickness, whose first sign is a pale line at the gum. Fenna Clay works here.
**Relations:** Inside High Kiln, on the terrace above Low Kiln; the Vail kiln-house buys its glaze here.

### The Cross
On Reed 18, Y.H. 813 Berr Clay came up short off the clay road with the red cord folded under his coat, wiped his thumb twice, paid two marks short for two pair of eels and said *A number goes into a slate, not a bundle* and *your house*, never *I*; the true subtraction stayed in the locked box up the clay road and the low sentence about one in twelve was spent on the boards with nobody leaning in. No fifth green came to a silent woman's mouth at the pot board in these three days. Ch 32.

**Kind:** market square. **Region:** Sill.
The meeting of the Clay Road and the river road, where the moot shouts, the slate is read and the pigs are weighed on market-day. On Account-day two hundred people lean in to hear the figure while eight pigs are driven over the wet boards; the reading is given twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. A bell strikes through the morning and afternoon, longer than a church bell, and nobody crosses himself at it; the boy counts its strokes four, four and a gap. The square has four ways out --- the road down to the south bridge and Slate Row, the steep street up to High Kiln, the north bridge, and the road toward the Bone Orchard --- and a Court table set at its Slate Row fringe where business is done in the open air. People bring figures to a heap of turnips and leave them standing side by side without making them agree. On Stone 30, Y.H. 812 the year's last market was held here: one in twelve was read to the crowd, the Five were spoken of as three days off, and the pause in the neat dark hand was seen a fifth time at the Court's table at the square's Slate Row fringe.
On Ilmen 10, Y.H. 813 Account-day resumed here the morning after a woman was taken by the brook and lay uncollected: eight pigs went over the weighing board, the levy was called for the thaw, one in twelve was read twice and not challenged, and the number for the thaw went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and was never given. The bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; Sella set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade and carried it home saying nothing about what had been read over it. On Ilmen 20, Y.H. 813, Account-day, Ch 12, Berr Clay came to the block with the red cord folded under his coat and green of glaze at one corner of his mouth, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing and read one in twelve over the basket twice; the true subtraction went unspoken, the number for the thaw went from fist to mouth to shoulder and was given to nobody, and the bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The clerk at the Court's table and the clerk set at the Fishstairs both put hand to slate before eye to person, and the boy kept the two orders apart.

On Silt 10, Y.H. 813, Account-day held itself on the dry step above the water while the second step stood under an inch of brown water gone flat and glossy. Berr Clay read one in twelve twice over the water and the basket, the true subtraction went over it unspoken, the number for the flood's cost went from fist to mouth to shoulder and reached nobody, and the bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the two hundred leaned in and went back to the pigs. Ch 15.

On Silt 20, Y.H. 813, Account-day, the crowd was thinner than at the flood's reading, the water and the lean week having taken bodies out of the market; Berr Clay read one in twelve over the fewer bodies, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, took himself out of his own sentence and went off toward High Kiln with the box locked. The printed figure stood unchallenged over the thinner crowd and a figure read aloud was not the figure paid. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself; Sella set the basket in the moot-post's thin shade with his cheek to the cold iron and did not look into it. Ch 17.

Sella set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with the cold iron at his cheek and carried him home at dusk saying nothing about what had been read over him. Ch 18.

### Slate Row
**Kind:** Court quarter. **Region:** Sill.
A narrow street holding the Court's room, the assessor's house, the collector's table and the Waiting Room. **Relations:** 8 leagues west of Sill; Stonemouth about 3 leagues east; the Rim Road passes north of it.

### Greybank
**Kind:** granary town. **Region:** Grath, eastern gate.
The Court's granary and factor's house, where the Clay Road ends and the Hearth Road begins; the first place a Sill man sees the Court at full size. On Ilmen 26, Y.H. 813 a man come downriver from Greybank, road-mud on his boots, told the bucket-round at Low Kiln's yard well that in the last war a counting room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside died of the breath; he said it as a curiosity with a date, eighteen leagues east, and went back down the road he came. The yard took it for history and nobody joined the sealed room to any kiln in Sill (T065). On Silt 25, Y.H. 813 a second man come downriver from Greybank with road-mud on his boots and a bag repeated the saying at the same well as a curiosity with a date; the yard took it as history again and nobody joined it to any kiln, while the boy filed the second telling beside the first without letting the two touch, keeping breath through the nose against breath in the throat and both saying *not the war's hand* (T095).
**Relations:** 18 leagues east downriver of Sill; Mud End 15 leagues west; the Hearth Road runs 140 leagues south-east to the Hearthseat.

### The Counting House
**Kind:** counting house. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The Vhaleni factor's counting house in Greybank where glass, grain, quiet ash and whole returns are brokered and where documents on salt-paper are left in the damp; Veno Brine keeps his private drawer of dry documents here.
**Relations:** Inside Greybank, 18 leagues east downriver of Sill, near the Court's factor's house and the end of the Clay Road.

### The Court's factor's house
**Kind:** house, counting room, seat of the Court's factor. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The factor's dwelling and tally-room beside the end of the Clay Road, where the Court's collector's clerk keeps the tally for the Sill district in a tin on a shelf; the officer makes the draw and the clerk sets the figure down in Ledgerhand. Sella Vail's widow's mark was confirmed here and entered as a kindness, and the factor showed Berr Clay the name of the purchaser of the forty children's warmth, which Berr has forgotten on purpose. In the first weeks of Stone, Y.H. 812 the slate for that private whole return was carried from this house to the collector in Sill.
**Relations:** Inside Greybank, 18 leagues east downriver of Sill, on the north bank of the Ilm; near the Counting House and above the Greybank quay; the Clay Road ends here.

### The Court's granaries
**Kind:** granaries, storehouses. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The Court's grain stores at Greybank: long roofed houses with vent slits and wide grain doors where the levy grain is held and the factor's account is kept in kind. The town's name is read as often in these banks of stored warmth as in the grey silt bank of the Ilm on which it stands.
**Relations:** Inside Greybank on the north bank of the Ilm, east of the Court's factor's house; the Hearth Road runs south-east from them toward the Hearthseat, 140 leagues.

### The Greybank quay
**Kind:** quay, landing, wharf. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The timber landing on the north bank of the Ilm where the Clay Road ends among barrels, grain sacks, salt and iron; coarse pots, tile, peat and eels go downriver from it and salt and iron come back up, and river barges lie against it. An eel skin costs a mark here. In the Long Cold of Y.H. 811 the Ilm lay under ice for ninety-four days and no collector could cross from this quay.
**Relations:** On the north bank of the Ilm inside Greybank, below the Court's factor's house and the Counting House; Sill is 18 leagues upriver.

### The bank at Greybank
**Kind:** bank, hall, place of banking. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The Court's banking hall at Greybank, one of the two banks named by the Keeping alongside the bank at the Hearthseat, where measures drawn in the district are pressed into vessels and the rolls of the dead are carried forward rather than closed.
**Relations:** Inside Greybank, beside the Court's factor's house on the north bank; the Ilm runs below it.

### The Greybank burial grounds
**Kind:** burial ground. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The graves south of the town, where thin returns are laid; the bone-hound is said to follow the smell of Ash here rather than the smell of decay.
**Relations:** South of Greybank, beyond the Ilm, a short walk from the Greybank quay.

### The Greybank market
**Kind:** market square. **Region:** Greybank, south bank.
The stalls of the south bank where grain, salt, iron, eels and coarse pots are weighed and a slate-post carries the factor's notices; the district's figures go over the top of the baskets here as they do at the Cross in Sill.
**Relations:** On the south bank of the Ilm inside Greybank, across from the Greybank quay.

### The winter ford
**Kind:** ford, frozen crossing. **Region:** Greybank, on the Ilm.
The shallow crossing of the Ilm at Greybank, marked on the chart by a dotted double line; in the Long Cold of Y.H. 811 the river bore ice for ninety-four days and no collector could cross from the quay.
**Relations:** Across the Ilm below the Greybank quay, between the quay street and the road down to the south bank.

### The Old Kiln
**Kind:** ruin. **Region:** Grath, south of Sill.
A collapsed kiln with grey clay that will not take a glaze and an ash throat deeper than a man.
**Relations:** 1 league south of Sill; a slate in the Vail kiln-house came from here.

## III. Rivers and roads

### The Ilm
**Kind:** river. **Region:** Grath to the Sallow Sea.
The great silty river of Grath, frozen from Stone into Ilmen and breaking in the Silt flood called the Breaking. On Stone 19, Y.H. 812 a man come up from the river held a palm's breadth of its ice up to the tallow light in the Clay Yard: a dark hair ran in from one edge and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the other, and he said the water under it had gone different. On Stone 21 a man come back from looking at it brought a number he would not give, shut in a fist with the thumb tucked under, and said it had come a hair further. On Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813 the ice was called honeyed under the mud and the water at the well tasted different; on Ilmen 7 the same hairline came up into the open skin of the river, longer than a man could lift, and a man come back from looking said it had come a hair further with a figure shut in his fist that he thought better of giving. The boy set it beside the missing step at the Fishstairs and the gap in the told bell and kept the three apart. A woman from High Kiln said the thaw would come early; the yard wanted it late, because a late thaw puts off the levy, and Sella called the crack weather. The boy filed the hairline as a shape, not a cause, beside the other gaps. On Ilmen 19 and Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813 more hairlines ran into the ice and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge; the yard called them weather, and the boy kept them beside the frost-bloom on the well-lip, the honeyed ice of a man's saying, the early low note of the Singing Sill and the high clay-heavy brook in places that did not touch.
On Silt 8, Y.H. 813 it ran under a skin so thin the dark current showed through in veins, the ice the colour of grey slip-water in a shallow dish and honeyed all through rather than only at the edge; more hairlines had run in and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the open water. The boy kept the veins, the honeyed saying, the widening hairlines and the early Sill note in places that did not touch. Ch 14.
By Seed 5–11, Y.H. 813 the river ran low under the finger's-width-open door of the Vail kiln-house, and at the going of the light on Seed 11 its low muttered note of water going over water came through the gap while the fire's four, four and a gap came up through the floor, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred days. The boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down; nothing was joined, paid, explained or ended. Ch 20.

**Relations:** Rises in the Rim about 120 leagues west of Sill; passes Stonemouth, the Weir and Sill; runs east through Greybank and reaches the Sallow Sea some 80 leagues below Sill.

### The Ferr
**Kind:** river, tributary. **Region:** upper Grath.
A colder tributary carrying lead dust from the western workings; its water is said to thin a man who drinks it through a season.
**Relations:** About 40 leagues long; joins the Ilm at Stonemouth, 5 leagues west of Sill; the Lead Run enters it a few leagues upstream.

### The Slip Brook
**Kind:** brook. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
A narrow clay-coloured stream through the Clay Yard whose colour the children of Low Kiln read as weather. On Stone 25, Y.H. 812 it carried a skin the colour of skimmed milk gone grey that held under the wind without breaking, and on Stone 27 it still carried it; both were read as weather. On Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813 it had lost its stiff skin and moved under a thin grey film that broke and closed again around the stones; by Ilmen 8 it ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should, under a film that no longer held the wind, and a child who went near the bank was called back by the middle of the yard. The boy set the higher brook beside the honeyed ice under the mud at the Ilm and the hairline that had come up out of the thaw, three objects in three places, and made no cause of them. On Ilmen 19–25, Y.H. 813 it still ran clay-heavy a little higher than it should, the colour of a pot washed out into the water, and the women at the tubs said the pits had been worked early and called it the colour of the water; on Ilmen 25 the boy kept it as the fourth of four witnesses to the thaw, beside the frost-bloom, the honeyed ice and the stopping hairlines, and none of the four touched.
On Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813 its clay-heavy rise came up behind the drying racks in the night and took Dessa Silt from the end of nineteen steps; the yard lifted her out with their hands and no words, no collector was called, and the boy was held on the bank and heard the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through. Through Ilmen 10–15 it still ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should under a film that no longer held the wind, and the yard called it weather. On Ilmen 18, Y.H. 813 it still ran clay-heavy a little higher than it should behind the drying racks, the colour of a pot washed into a bucket and left to settle, and the Singing Sill gave its early low note through the boards under it all; the yard called both weather.

Through Days 61–67, Y.H. 813 it ran clay-heavy and higher behind the drying racks, going over itself with a low note the district had no word for; the boy filed water-over-water on a place of its own and the yard called it the thaw, with the year's flood four days off at the close of Chapter 14.
**Relations:** Runs behind the Vail kiln-house and enters the Ilm at Sill.

### The Clay Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath.
Packed clay over corduroy, five days by cart in Grain and six in Silt; surfaced as a Court road by the treaty of Y.H. 418.
**Relations:** Runs east from the Cross at Sill to Greybank, 18 leagues; the Salt Road continues from Greybank.

### The Salt Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath to Vhalen.
The trade route from the clay towns to the Sallow ports, by Greybank and the coast pass.
**Relations:** Begins at Sill, runs east to Greybank (18 leagues) and then north-east to Vhal (a further 62 leagues); total 80 leagues.

### The Rim Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath to Keld.
The improving road and worsening weather between Sill and the Rim, travelled with a warm-stone.
**Relations:** Runs west-north-west from Sill to Keld Keep, 120 leagues; reaches the Cold Pass at 130 leagues.

### The Hearth Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Greybank to the Hearthseat.
The merchants' road to the Court, spoken of in Grath as if it were a fever.
**Relations:** Runs south-east from Greybank to the Hearthseat, 140 leagues; Greybank is 18 leagues east of Sill.

## IV. Wonders, wastes and buried places

### The Nine Wells
**Kind:** hot springs. **Region:** the Keel.
Nine springs where the ground gives back more than it takes; four sealed by the Court in Y.H. 1 to 120, a fifth in Y.H. 618, and the keys kept.
**Relations:** On the Keel within twenty leagues of the Hearthseat and the Hearth beneath it.

### The Glaze Cliffs
**Kind:** coastal cliffs. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow shore.
Blue-green cliffs stained by old lead workings, sweet-smelling in the heat; children are warned not to lick them.
**Relations:** On the Sallow coast about five leagues from Vhal, roughly 85 leagues north-east of Sill.

### The Empty Cantons
**Kind:** depopulated province. **Region:** south of Grath.
Roads, wells and doorways with no people; the ledger's empty squares after Y.H. 40, paid for by the first whole returns.
**Relations:** About 60 leagues south of Sill, between Grath and the Fenmarch; the Peat Line lies further south-west.

### The Singing Sill
**Kind:** river ledge, wonder. **Region:** Sill, the Ilm.
The hard stone under the falls that gives a low note when the Warm rises in Ilmen; the town takes its name from it. On Ilmen 7, Y.H. 813 it gave its low note a little earlier than it should, coming up through the boards of the well-platform, and the yard called it weather while the neighbours argued an early thaw against a late one. On Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813 a low note came early through the boards of the Weir; on Ilmen 25 it gave that note earlier than it should once more, coming up through the boards of the Vail kiln-house and spoken of from the Weir, and the yard called it weather, keeping it apart from the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's own missing breath.
On Silt 7, Y.H. 813 it gave its low note a week ahead of its hour, felt in the roots of the teeth and against the roof of the mouth before the ear placed it out in the water; a woman at the tubs said the water ran fast under the ice and then said weather and went home warm on it, and the boy set the early note beside the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's gap in four places joined by nobody. Ch 14.
**Relations:** Under the Ilm at Sill, below the Fishstairs and the north bridge.

## V. The first life — Earth

*These are not places in Sarn and carry no distance from Sill. They are entered here because they are named in the past-life memories and must be written with `\place{...}` when a scene breaks into Chicago. The canonical names below are exact. Only what Dan's life touched is listed; the rest of Earth is not in the book.*

### Chicago
**Kind:** city. **Region:** Earth, Illinois, the 2020s.
The city of the first life, on the shore of a great fresh-water lake; a place of elevated trains, trauma bays and blizzards. It has no Hearth, no collectors and no word for the Warm, which is why Dan can never explain it to anyone in Sarn.
**Relations:** Contains Hermosa, Elmwood Park, Evanston, Cook County Hospital, Lane Tech, the University of Illinois Chicago and the hospital car park; Lake Michigan lies along its eastern edge.

### Hermosa
**Kind:** neighbourhood. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The block of two-storey brick bungalows where Dan grew up, with the el running high and iron over Pulaski and the summer smelling of charcoal and wet pavement. Site of the Marchetti family house, where love was shown by fixing things and never discussed.
**Relations:** Inside Chicago; a short drive to Lane Tech and to Cook County Hospital.

### Elmwood Park
**Kind:** neighbourhood. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan and Elena Rossi were married in a church with too many relatives and a photographer who cried; also where Nina grew up.
**Relations:** Inside Chicago, near Hermosa.

### Evanston
**Kind:** city. **Region:** Earth, Illinois.
The lakeside city where Paul Vesci was raised and where he keeps a private practice with good light.
**Relations:** Just north of Chicago on Lake Michigan.

### Cook County Hospital
**Kind:** hospital, workplace. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The county trauma centre where Dan trained, became an attending and worked nights; a large room with ceiling-mounted lights where a team moves with the rehearsed speed of people who have done the same thing four thousand times. The place that made him and the place that ate his marriage.
**Relations:** In Chicago; near the apartment Dan kept after the divorce and the car park where he and Des sat; site of Tasha Broussard's review.

### The hospital car park
**Kind:** place, ritual site. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan and Des ate bad pizza in silence for ten minutes after a bad shift, and where Dan learned of his father's death at sunrise. The friendship's whole architecture. Remembered in Chapter 2 in the present tense: wet slush under the lamps, the dashboard clock past three, cold pizza in a paper bag on the console, the lemon-and-diesel smell of the rig, Des's gold tooth catching the light, the laugh that filled the space where the eleventh minute would have been, and the same laugh running on the dispatcher's channel for exactly one minute after the expressway.
**Relations:** Beside Cook County Hospital.

### Lane Tech
**Kind:** school. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The technical high school Dan attended because his mother insisted, where he took things apart on the shop floor and first learned that he liked a right answer under his hands.
**Relations:** In Chicago, not far from Hermosa.

### The University of Illinois Chicago
**Kind:** university. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan found the human body in the cadaver lab the way other boys find engines — by being forbidden and doing it anyway.
**Relations:** In Chicago.

### Lake Michigan
**Kind:** lake. **Region:** Earth, beside Chicago.
The great cold lake at the edge of Dan's life, and the subject of the boat he promised Nina for nine years and never bought. The photograph on his wall shows Nina at six squinting in its summer light.
**Relations:** Along Chicago's eastern shore; the boat promise and the unsent call are bound to it.

### The expressway
**Kind:** road, site of the crash. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The northbound expressway near the split, where a pileup formed in a blizzard at 2:17 in the morning and Dan died pulling a child from a minivan before the fire took them both. The woman in the grey coat stood at the edge of the flare light here with a slate.
**Relations:** In Chicago, out toward the northern suburbs; the hinge between the two lives.

### The apartment near the hospital
**Kind:** dwelling. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Dan's small flat after the divorce: a bed, a chair, a television, a fridge with four beers and food he never ate. On the wall, Nina at six; in a drawer, an unsent letter to Elena with one honest sentence; beside the bed, a half-read book and a cold coffee.
**Relations:** Near Cook County Hospital.

### The nursing home
**Kind:** workplace. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Theresa Marchetti worked part-time as a nurse's aide, coming home with her uniform smelling of bleach, old people and lilac soap. It is never named more precisely in the book.
**Relations:** In Chicago.
