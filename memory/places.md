# Gazetteer of Named Places

*Canonical names are given first and must be used in the indexing macros. Distances are in the world's own units (see `bible/world.md`): a league is about a long day's walk, a mile five furlongs, a chain forty paces. Places of the other shore are kept at the end and have no relation to Cindermere distances.*

---

## The world and its seams

### Cindermere
**Kind:** world. **Region:** the whole. A cracked vessel of a world, saucer-shaped, with a salt lake at its centre and a riveted horizon; the name is used for both the world and the old survey that first measured it. **Relations:** contains every place below except those of the other shore.

### the Great Seam
**Kind:** cosmological feature. **Region:** the horizon. The line where the sky-lid is riveted to the earth-body, visible at dawn as the Star Seam; its rivets are the stars. **Relations:** encircles Cindermere; measured from the north silt shelf of the Pale Water in AR 312 and again in AR 421.

### the Under-Seam
**Kind:** cosmological feature. **Region:** below the world. The seam beneath the earth, where the unswept Owed is said to gather. **Relations:** below every land; nearest spoken of in the Salt Marches and the House of Seams.

### the Star Seam
**Kind:** cosmological feature / wonder. **Region:** the horizon at dawn. The Great Seam seen in the first light, when the rivets are said to cool audibly on the longest night. **Relations:** seen principally from the Salt Marches.

### the Pale Water
**Kind:** salt lake. **Region:** the centre of the world. A shallow bruise-coloured lake, sixty leagues north to south and thirty across, that never freezes and hums at night; the source of salt, silt and the salt-sickness. **Relations:** Ond on its eastern shore; the Salt Marches on its south and west; the Silt Basin to the south-east; the Salt Waste around its whole rim.

### the Singing Staple
**Kind:** wonder / monument. **Region:** the Salt Marches. A cathedral-tall brass staple driven into the flat ground, older than any record, which gives out one pure note at dawn; a chapel is built at its foot and drilling it is forbidden. **Relations:** twenty-two leagues south-west of Ond; about thirty leagues south-east of Saltham.

### the Glasswold
**Kind:** wonder / ruin-forest. **Region:** the Rift Marches. A stand of trees turned to green glass by an ancient firing, each trunk keeping the shape of bark; its slivers cut and do not heal cleanly. **Relations:** eighteen leagues east of Ond, beyond the border forts; the Ash Waste lies further south.

### the Bone Orchard
**Kind:** wonder / burial ground. **Region:** the Gloam Weald. A grove where pale shapes hang from the boughs like fruit; the Weald-born say they are Names waiting to be called. **Relations:** deep in the Weald, several leagues north of Wealdhollow; no mapped road reaches it.

## The ring of lands

### the Clay March
**Kind:** region. **Region:** north-west of the Pale Water. Low wet ochre ground of clay pits, kiln-yards, flax and washing, watered by the Gyle and the Vey; the birth region of Wren Sedge. **Relations:** north of the Salt Marches; west of the Vey and Ond; east of the Gloam Weald; south of the Wold.

### the Wold
**Kind:** region / kingdom. **Region:** north. Cold highland forest of iron, pine and frost, ruled from Harrowstead; the only realm that rejects the Mending. **Relations:** north of the Clay March and the Gyle headwaters; the Frost Weald is its northern reach.

### the Gloam Weald
**Kind:** region / forest. **Region:** west of the Clay March. Ancient dim forest older than the survey, home of the Gloam-mothers and gloamcraft; iron is left at its boundary. **Relations:** west of Kilnwick by some twenty-five leagues; its council sits at Wealdhollow.

### the Salt Marches
**Kind:** region. **Region:** south and west of the Pale Water. Grey salt flats of the nine cantons, given over to salt, silt, forgetting and rumour. **Relations:** south of the Clay March by the Salt Track; west and south of Ond; includes Crye's Flat, the Low Shelf and the Grey Reach.

### the Rift Marches
**Kind:** region / borderland. **Region:** east of Ond. Broken high country of old wars, ruins, scrap and free companies, with eleven forts and no lasting border. **Relations:** east of Ond and the Pale Water; north of the Ash Waste; contains the Glasswold.

### the Coast of Nine Harbours
**Kind:** region / merchant league. **Region:** the Cinder Sea, south-east. A warm seaboard of nine rich cities held together by weights, credit and the Articles of Verran. **Relations:** south-east of the Rift Marches by sea; its chief city Verran is about a hundred and twenty leagues by sea from Ond.

### the Ashen Waste
**Kind:** region / waste. **Region:** the far south. Salt and cinder, unsurveyed; nothing grows and little returns. **Relations:** south of the Rift Marches and the Silt Basin; east of the Salt Waste.

### the Salt Waste
**Kind:** waste. **Region:** the rim of the Pale Water. Barren alkali shelf-lands, grey and salt-crusted. **Relations:** surrounds the Pale Water; where it meets the Clay March, the Salt Track begins.

### the Ash Waste
**Kind:** waste. **Region:** south of the Rift Marches. Deep volcanic cinder, yielding scrap iron and coins of mints no realm admits. **Relations:** south of the Glasswold; north of the Ashen Waste.

### the Silt Basin
**Kind:** waste / bog. **Region:** south-east of the Pale Water. A grey ooze into which carts vanish; the source of much Owed used in Ond. **Relations:** between the lake's south shore and the Ashen Strait; east of the Salt Marches.

### the Frost Weald
**Kind:** waste / forest. **Region:** the far north. Trees frozen in winter that do not rot when they fall. **Relations:** the northern reach of the Wold, beyond Harrowstead.

### the Cinder Sea
**Kind:** sea. **Region:** south-east. Warm, bright with shipping, the highway of the Nine Harbours. **Relations:** east and south of the Rift Marches; joined to the Ashen Strait.

### the Gloamwater
**Kind:** sea. **Region:** the far west. Cold, seldom crossed, the boundary of the Weald-born world. **Relations:** west of the Gloam Weald.

### the Ashen Strait
**Kind:** strait / channel. **Region:** the south-east. The tidal passage joining the Cinder Sea to the Pale Reach, passable at slack water only; the old charts warn that passage is never free. **Relations:** between the Cinder Sea and the salt flats at the lake's south-east; north of the Silt Basin.

### the Pale Reach
**Kind:** estuary. **Region:** the south-east shore of the Pale Water. A tidal channel that carries the sea's rise up into the salt flats and the lake's hem. **Relations:** the inner end of the Ashen Strait; east of Saltham.

## Ond and the House of Seams

### Ond
**Kind:** city. **Region:** the eastern shore of the Pale Water, at the mouth of the Vey. The riveter city, walled in brass-stained stone, population about ninety thousand; the seat of the House of Seams and the Mending industry. **Relations:** twenty-six leagues downriver from Veyford; about forty leagues by river from Kilnwick; the Singing Staple lies twenty-two leagues south-west.

### the House of Seams
**Kind:** building / institution. **Region:** the centre of Ond. The great kiln-cathedral and ledgers where the Mending is performed and the world's accounts are kept; over its door are carved four words in the sharp script: *What is broken is owed.* **Relations:** at the heart of Ond, beside the Ledger Quarter.

### the Seamward
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Ond. The rich quarter of fine Mendings, smooth-palmed families and houses with no visible seam-marks. **Relations:** next to the House of Seams; above the Ledger Quarter.

### the Ledger Quarter
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Ond. The clerks' quarter of counting rooms and narrow stairs, where arrears are entered in a clean hand. **Relations:** between the Seamward and the Cinder Quays.

### the Cinder Quays
**Kind:** district / works. **Region:** Ond, on the Pale Water. Salt works, seam-metal workshops and the labour quays where debtors are worked. **Relations:** on the east shore of the Pale Water, below the House; the Cull Warrens are built beneath it.

### the Cull Warrens
**Kind:** district / labour warrens. **Region:** Ond, below the Cinder Quays. The underground rooms where over-mended souls are housed and set to work; first built in AR 264. **Relations:** beneath the Cinder Quays; reached by a stair from the salt yard.

### the Synod vault
**Kind:** archive. **Region:** Ond, within the House of Seams. The room holding the First Ledger, the rolls of Riveters and the closed files, including the file stamped *wait*. **Relations:** inside the House of Seams; its copy of the Rift scrap was sent from the Glasswold in AR 362.

## The Clay March — Kilnwick and its neighbours

### Kilnwick
**Kind:** village. **Region:** the Clay March, west bank of the Gyle. The birthplace of Wren Sedge: about three hundred and forty souls, clay pits to the west, three cone kilns to the south, a wash-house on the bank, the Hearthrite chapel under an ash tree, and a reeve's house with a leaded window. **Relations:** fourteen leagues upriver from Veyford; three leagues downriver from Clayholt; two leagues west of Barrow Wick; four leagues upriver from Eelhook; nine leagues north of Saltham; about forty leagues by river from Ond.

### Clay Lane
**Kind:** lane. **Region:** Kilnwick. The village lane from the clay pits down to the kiln-yard; clay dust lies on it all year. **Relations:** runs west from the ash tree to the pits.

### the Wash Bank
**Kind:** lane / bank. **Region:** Kilnwick. The river bank where the washing is done and sheets snap in the wind; the wash-house stands here. **Relations:** runs along the Gyle below the reeve's house.

### the Hearthrite chapel
**Kind:** chapel. **Region:** Kilnwick. A small chapel under the ash tree at the village centre, with leaded windows and a Mending post in its yard; the Riveter comes every nine weeks. **Relations:** at the centre of Kilnwick, between Clay Lane and the Wash Bank.

### the reeve's house
**Kind:** house. **Region:** Kilnwick. A clay house with a leaded window the village is quietly proud of, and an oven where the best bread is baked. **Relations:** above the Wash Bank, a short walk from the chapel.

### the Gyle
**Kind:** river. **Region:** the Clay March. A slow brown river, thirty paces wide at Kilnwick, deep under the bluff, rising in Brook and Silt and again in Rot; a tributary of the Vey. **Relations:** runs past Kilnwick; joins the Vey at Veyford, fourteen leagues downriver.

### the Vey
**Kind:** river. **Region:** the Clay March and the Pale basin. The broad silt-heavy river that drains the basin to the lake and carries the March's clay to Ond. **Relations:** begins at Veyford where the Gyle joins it; runs twenty-six leagues downriver to Ond.

### the river road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** the Clay March. The hauling path along the Gyle and the Vey to Ond; mud for most of the year, dust for six weeks in high summer; each household owes three days a year to keep it passable. **Relations:** Kilnwick to Veyford to Ond; the Old Kiln Way is its overland alternative.

### the Old Kiln Way
**Kind:** road / track. **Region:** the Clay March. An overland track used when the river is high or the toll is hated. **Relations:** from Kilnwick through Clayholt and Barrow Wick to Veyford, eighteen leagues overland.

### the Salt Track
**Kind:** track. **Region:** the Clay March. The southward track down which salt comes back in sacks from the first flats. **Relations:** nine leagues south from Kilnwick to Saltham.

### Clayholt
**Kind:** village. **Region:** the Clay March. A smaller potting village with a better clay seam and worse water; it supplies bricks for Kilnwick's kilns. **Relations:** three leagues upriver from Kilnwick; on the Old Kiln Way.

### Mire End
**Kind:** hamlet. **Region:** the Clay March, in the wet flats. Eels, rushes and a bridge that washes away twice a decade; the people are said to hold their breath when crossing. **Relations:** six leagues downriver from Kilnwick, between Kilnwick and Veyford.

### Mire End bridge
**Kind:** bridge. **Region:** Mire End. A wooden bridge over the Gyle flats, carried away in the Silt flood of AR 431. **Relations:** at Mire End, six leagues downriver of Kilnwick.

### Barrow Wick
**Kind:** hamlet. **Region:** the Clay March. Built around an old burial mound dug into every winter, from which nothing is ever found — considered a good sign. **Relations:** two leagues east of Kilnwick over the rise, on the Old Kiln Way.

### Eelhook
**Kind:** hamlet. **Region:** the Clay March. A fishing hamlet of eleven households on a bend of the Gyle; its salted eels are sold as far as Veyford. **Relations:** four leagues downriver from Kilnwick, short of Mire End.

### Veyford
**Kind:** town. **Region:** the Clay March, at the joining of Gyle and Vey. A town of about nine hundred with a toll bridge, the district Riveter's post and the only market where a March pot brings coin. **Relations:** fourteen leagues downriver from Kilnwick; twenty-six leagues upriver from Ond; reached overland by the Old Kiln Way in eighteen leagues.

### the Veyford toll bridge
**Kind:** bridge. **Region:** Veyford. The bridge over the Vey whose toll is confirmed to Ond by the Treaty of the Iron Ring (AR 251). **Relations:** at Veyford, at the junction of the two rivers.

### Saltham
**Kind:** hamlet / salt post. **Region:** the northern edge of the Salt Marches. The first flats reached from the March; salt sacks come back from it along the Salt Track. **Relations:** nine leagues south of Kilnwick; the Singing Staple lies roughly thirty leagues south-east.

## The Salt Marches and the cantons

### Crye's Flat
**Kind:** salt flat / settlement. **Region:** the Salt Cantons. A canton flat and the birth-place of the Crye clan; names are spoken here at low water. **Relations:** about five leagues west of Saltham.

### the Low Shelf
**Kind:** salt flat / settlement. **Region:** the Salt Cantons. A low grey shelf where the cantons sell thimbles of Pale Water for forgetting. **Relations:** about eight leagues south of Saltham.

### the Grey Reach
**Kind:** salt flat / settlement. **Region:** the Salt Cantons. An outer flat of grey silt ropes where the shapes at low water are most often seen. **Relations:** about twelve leagues south-west of Saltham.

---

## The Wold

### Harrowstead
**Kind:** capital / fortress town. **Region:** the Wold. The seat of the Harrow crown and its thanes, where the king cannot tax without consent and the weregild is kept in iron. **Relations:** about sixty leagues north of Kilnwick; about ninety leagues north of Ond; the Frost Weald lies beyond it.

### the border forts
**Kind:** fortifications (eleven). **Region:** the Wold–Ond frontier. Forts raised after the Treaty of the Iron Ring in AR 251; nine stand, two are not spoken of. **Relations:** along the Gyle headwaters and the upper Vey, north of the Clay March.

---

## The Gloam Weald

### Wealdhollow
**Kind:** council seat. **Region:** the Gloam Weald. The gathering place of the Gloam-mothers, where decisions are made by refusing things and no iron is allowed inside the boundary. **Relations:** about twenty-five leagues west of Kilnwick; the Bone Orchard lies several leagues north of it.

## The Rift Marches

### the old print-house
**Kind:** ruin. **Region:** the Rift Marches. A ruined house where a fragment of typeset paper in the unreadable script was found in AR 362 and sent to Ond. **Relations:** in the Rift Marches near the Glasswold; the scrap is now in the Synod vault.

### the chapel of the petition
**Kind:** chapel. **Region:** the Rift Marches. A chapel holding the one surviving copy of the Culls' petition of AR 414. **Relations:** among the Rift forts, east of Ond; its exact location is not given in the survey.

## The Nine Harbours and the south

### Verran
**Kind:** city / chief harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours, on the Cinder Sea. The wealthiest of the nine, where the Factors meet yearly to set weights, peace and the price of passage; the gold seal is minted here. **Relations:** about a hundred and twenty leagues by sea south-east of Ond; the Articles of Verran were signed here in AR 349.

### Correy
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A harbour of warehouses and glass furnaces, house of the Correy family. **Relations:** along the coast west of Verran.

### Brekk
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A fishing and shipbuilding port, house of the Brekk family. **Relations:** along the coast west of Correy.

### Marche
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A port that handles smuggled seam-metal in wine casks, house of the Marche family. **Relations:** along the coast east of Verran.

### Isen
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A small harbour known for its bronze work and its exact weights. **Relations:** along the coast east of Marche.

### Dorne
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A harbour of credit and Receipts, house of the Dorne family. **Relations:** along the coast east of Isen.

### Fenn
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. A galley port, house of the Fenn family. **Relations:** along the coast east of Dorne.

### Orewell
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. The easternmost harbour, nearest the Ashen Strait. **Relations:** at the coast's eastern end, by the strait.

### Cinderhaven
**Kind:** city / harbour. **Region:** the Coast of Nine Harbours. The ninth harbour, a dye and grain port facing the warm Cinder Sea. **Relations:** south of the Rift Marches, across the water from the Ash Waste.

## The other shore (the past life)

*These places have no relation to Cindermere distances. They are kept here because they have been named and must stay named. Distances in this section are in that world's miles.*

### Cauldwick
**Kind:** port town. **Region:** the other shore, in cold northern latitudes. A town of brickworks, lead-glaze potteries, rope-walks, a glue factory and two printshops; Nell Rill lived and died here in 1883. **Relations:** on the river Wreak, about six miles from the sea; Silt Lane lies within smell of the tide.

### the Wreak
**Kind:** tidal river. **Region:** the other shore. A broad brown river rising twice a day, which has drowned the lower quay many times and flooded Nell's workshop cellar in October 1883. **Relations:** runs through Cauldwick to the sea.

### Silt Lane
**Kind:** street. **Region:** Cauldwick. The street of two rooms above a cooperage where Nell lived; Mr. Vetch owned half of it. **Relations:** within smell of the Wreak's tide.

### Nell's workshop
**Kind:** workshop with cellar. **Region:** Cauldwick, on Silt Lane. The ground-floor mending workshop with a cellar holding clay, glaze powders, finished work and the box of twelve wedding cups. **Relations:** the stairwell filled with river water on the night of the flood.

### the cooperage
**Kind:** building. **Region:** Cauldwick, Silt Lane. The building above which Nell's two rooms were rented. **Relations:** on Silt Lane, over the workshop.

### Riga
**Kind:** port city. **Region:** the other shore, on the Baltic. The Baltic timber port to which Kit Rill sailed on the *Marrow* at sixteen; his letters came for a year and then stopped. **Relations:** across the Baltic from Cauldwick.

### the Baltic
**Kind:** sea. **Region:** the other shore. The cold sea beyond which Kit went and from which his returned letter came unopened. **Relations:** east of Cauldwick.
