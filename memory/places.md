# Gazetteer — every place named so far

*Private working file. Canonical names are given exactly as they must be written with `\place{...}` in the scenes. Distances are in leagues (a league is three Sarnish miles, a little under three kilometres), and direction is measured from **Sill** unless the entry says otherwise. Where a place lies inside another place, the containing place is given first in the relations.*

---

## I. The world and the great powers

### Sarn
**Kind:** the world. **Region:** all.
The bowl of warm land between the Hearth beneath and the Waste beyond the Rim; every vessel in it is a small failing dam.
**Relations:** The Hearth lies beneath the Keel to the south-east; the Waste begins beyond the Rim to the west; the Sallow Sea fills the north-east.

### The Hearth
**Kind:** source, chamber of stone. **Region:** beneath the Hearthseat, on the Keel.
The great source of the Warm, said to hold what the world had when it was made; its fire is kept behind iron in the Weighing Hall.
**Relations:** Beneath the Hearthseat, about 150 leagues south-east of Sill by the Clay and Hearth Roads; the Nine Wells lie within twenty leagues of it.

### The Waste
**Kind:** waste, cold country outside the world. **Region:** beyond the Rim.
The great sink where everything unheld goes; travellers who go far into it come back older and thinner.
**Relations:** Begins beyond the Rim, roughly 200 leagues west of Sill; no Court collectors go there.

### The Rim
**Kind:** mountain chain. **Region:** west and north of the bowl.
The cold mountains that ring Sarn, broken by a few passes; the Listening Stones stand in its high places.
**Relations:** Nearest spur about 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill; the Cold Pass cuts it at 130 leagues; Keld Keep lies against it.

### The Keel
**Kind:** low ridge. **Region:** centre-south-east of the bowl.
A long low ridge of stone over the Hearth, mildly warm in winter and therefore crowded.
**Relations:** About 140 leagues south-east of Sill; the Hearthseat stands on it; the Nine Wells rise in it.

### The Sallow Sea
**Kind:** inland sea. **Region:** north-east.
A cold inland sea the colour of skimmed milk, whose tides run against the wind and are said to follow the Warm rather than the moon.
**Relations:** Its shore lies about 70 leagues north-east of Sill; the Ilm drains into it; Vhal stands on its coast at 80 leagues; the Glaze Cliffs are on the same shore.

### The Warm Court
**Kind:** realm, institution, church. **Region:** everywhere in theory; the Hearthseat in fact.
The Keeping: assessors, collectors and bankers who own the Hearth, the Returns and the slates, and speak of theft as accounts.
**Relations:** Seated in the Hearthseat, 150 leagues south-east of Sill; its factor in Greybank is 18 leagues east of Sill and is the Court's nearest physical presence to Grath.

### The Hearthseat
**Kind:** city. **Region:** the Keel.
The Court's city, built over the Hearth, where the Weighing Hall, the sealed files and fewer than twenty Kept vessels are kept.
**Relations:** 150 leagues south-east of Sill: 18 leagues east by the Clay Road to Greybank, then 140 leagues south-east by the Hearth Road; the Keel under it; the Nine Wells within twenty leagues.

### The Weighing Hall
**Kind:** hall, temple and counting room. **Region:** the Hearthseat.
The room where the year's measures are read at the Weighing and where the Hearth-fire burns behind a grating of cold iron.
**Relations:** Inside the Hearthseat, above the Hearth chamber.

### The College of Assessors
**Kind:** academy, counting house. **Region:** the Hearthseat, on the Keel.
The school where the value of a life is taught and Ledgerhand written; it holds the only right to enter a lawful deduction and keeps a second file of names removed from the rolls. Provost Wessen teaches here.
**Relations:** In Slate Row in the Hearthseat, 150 leagues south-east of Sill; an assessor's house stands in Sill's own Slate Row, built in Y.H. 776.

### Vhalen
**Kind:** realm, merchant republic. **Region:** Sallow coast.
A ring of port towns governed by a Council of Tides, living on salt, glass, rope, ships and contracts written on paper that rots.
**Relations:** North-east of Sill, 80 leagues by the Salt Road; capital Vhal on the Sallow shore; the Glaze Cliffs on the same coast.

### Vhal
**Kind:** port city. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow coast.
The principal Vhaleni port, seat of the Council of Tides, with salt pans, glass furnaces and the tar-smelling council room.
**Relations:** 80 leagues north-east of Sill; at the northern end of the Salt Road; the Glaze Cliffs about five leagues along the coast.

### The Quay
**Kind:** district, waterfront. **Region:** Vhal, Vhalen.
The working waterfront of Vhal where boat-shares are held and pass through mothers; a man who marries into a boat-owning family is said to be come aboard and his opinion on the boat is counted at half. Pia of the Quay's mother holds a share here.
**Relations:** Inside Vhal, on the Sallow shore, 80 leagues north-east of Sill.

### The Salt Pans
**Kind:** works, salt flats. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow coast.
Shallow pans where sea water is left to sun and wind; the dippers bless the nets here at dawn and read the Sallow draw. Contracts written on salt-paper rot in the damp, which is considered a virtue.
**Relations:** Outside Vhal on the Sallow shore; the Glaze Cliffs lie a few leagues along the same coast.

### Keld
**Kind:** realm, mountain kingdom. **Region:** the Rim.
A high kingdom of iron, lead, coal and warm-stone, ruled by the Warden of the Rim, whose surveyors keep the record of the rim-seam.
**Relations:** 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill by the Rim Road; capital Keld Keep; the Cold Pass beyond it at 130 leagues.

### Keld Keep
**Kind:** fortress city. **Region:** Keld, the Rim.
The Warden's seat, where a man must state his measure before his name and where the deep survey shaft begins.
**Relations:** 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill; the First Seam beneath it; the Listening Stones about five leagues higher into the Rim; the Cold Pass ten leagues beyond.

### The First Seam
**Kind:** mine, survey shaft. **Region:** Keld, beneath Keld Keep.
The deep working where the Keldish survey the oldest repairs and where a crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588 records a man with a branching red mark. Siv of the First Seam keeps the survey here.
**Relations:** Beneath Keld Keep, 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Duelling Floor
**Kind:** duelling floor, chamber. **Region:** the First Seam, Keld Keep.
A floor cut into the stone of the deep shaft where the Order of the Tenth Clamp fights with iron clamps and where a young Keldish person first opens a way in the forearm; Hald Iron is master of the floor. A surviving copy of Haldre's suppressed paper is kept nearby in a tin lined with warm-stone.
**Relations:** Inside the First Seam, beneath Keld Keep, 120 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Listening Stones
**Kind:** standing stones, survey site. **Region:** high Rim, Keld.
Old stones against which the Vorr sit for days and come away with numbers; the Keldish surveyors use them to read the rim-seam.
**Relations:** About five leagues higher into the Rim from Keld Keep, some 125 leagues west-north-west of Sill.

### The Cold Pass
**Kind:** mountain pass, border. **Region:** the Rim, between Keld and Grath.
The pass closed by Karric VII in Y.H. 741 and fixed as the northern border by the agreement of Y.H. 747.
**Relations:** About 130 leagues west-north-west of Sill; ten leagues beyond Keld Keep on the Rim Road.

### Sere
**Kind:** empire. **Region:** the far south.
A hot empire of grain, wine, bowls and open chattel slavery, whose people teach that a vessel was made to be emptied gloriously.
**Relations:** 300 leagues south of Sill; its northern reach stops at the Peat Line; the capital is the Bowl of Sere.

### The Bowl of Sere
**Kind:** imperial city. **Region:** Sere.
The capital, a city of open courts and public fires where debt-slates are burnt by lot and returned slaves are exhibited.
**Relations:** About 300 leagues south of Sill; the Peat Line is roughly 200 leagues north of it.

### The Ember bowl-house
**Kind:** great house. **Region:** the Bowl of Sere.
A house of the Ten Bowls that buys poor northern measures to make one beautiful whole return for display at feasts; Solene Ember was returned whole here in Y.H. 799, Y.H. 804 and Y.H. 809.
**Relations:** Inside the Bowl of Sere, about 300 leagues south of Sill; its northern interests are represented by the factor at Greybank.

### The Fenmarch
**Kind:** realm, reed confederacy. **Region:** south-west.
Peat and boardwalk villages governed by reeves chosen for standing longest in a bog; the market for cheap thin returns.
**Relations:** 90 leagues south-west of Sill; the Standing Post is its meeting place; the Empty Cantons lie between it and Grath; the Peat Line is its southern limit.

### The Standing Post
**Kind:** meeting place, village. **Region:** the Fenmarch.
A raised boardwalk and a post carved with reeves' marks where the Fen reeves settle fines and feuds.
**Relations:** About 90 leagues south-west of Sill; the only Fen place outsiders are shown.

### The Peat Line
**Kind:** border, wetland. **Region:** between the Fenmarch and Sere.
The wet frontier where Sere's northern advance stopped in Y.H. 634 and where the two powers trade, raid and buy measures.
**Relations:** Roughly 100 leagues south-west of Sill; about 200 leagues north of the Bowl of Sere.

## II. Grath — the birth country

### Grath
**Kind:** country, region. **Region:** north-west of the bowl.
A soft country of clay towns, peat bog and river valleys, under a Thane of Clay confirmed by the Court's factor at Greybank.
**Relations:** The Rim lies to the west and north-west; the Sallow Sea to the north-east; Sill is its principal town; Greybank its eastern gate.

### Sill
**Kind:** town. **Region:** Grath, on the Ilm.
A town of just over four thousand souls where the Ilm drops four feet over a ledge of hard stone; the birthplace of Corin.
**Relations:** On the Ilm; 18 leagues upriver (west) of Greybank; 1 league east of the Weir; 1 league north of the Old Kiln; at the meeting of the Clay Road and the river road.

### Low Kiln
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Sill, south bank below the sill.
The poor quarter of kilns, trodden-ash floors and thick grey Ash in the corners; Corin is born here and the Vail kiln-house stands here.
**Relations:** Inside Sill, on the south bank below the stone sill; High Kiln is about 200 paces up the terrace to the north; the Clay Yard is within the district, beside the Slip Brook.

### High Kiln
**Kind:** district. **Region:** Sill, north terrace.
The master potters' quarter on the steep street above Low Kiln, where the glaze is mixed and the lead smell hangs.
**Relations:** About 200 paces north and uphill of Low Kiln; the Cross is a short way west.

### The Glaze Room
**Kind:** workshop. **Region:** High Kiln, Sill.
The room where the glaze is mixed and tasted by the glaze-mixers of House Clay; the lead in the mix causes the sweet-sickness, whose first sign is a pale line at the gum. Fenna Clay works here.
**Relations:** Inside High Kiln, on the terrace above Low Kiln; the Vail kiln-house buys its glaze here.

### The Cross
**Kind:** market square. **Region:** Sill.
The meeting of the Clay Road and the river road, where the moot shouts, the slate is read and the pigs are weighed on market-day. On Account-day two hundred people lean in to hear the figure while eight pigs are driven over the wet boards; the reading is given twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. A bell strikes through the morning and afternoon, longer than a church bell, and nobody crosses himself at it; the boy counts its strokes four, four and a gap. The square has four ways out --- the road down to the south bridge and Slate Row, the steep street up to High Kiln, the north bridge, and the road toward the Bone Orchard --- and a Court table set at its Slate Row fringe where business is done in the open air. People bring figures to a heap of turnips and leave them standing side by side without making them agree. On Stone 30, Y.H. 812 the year's last market was held here: one in twelve was read to the crowd, the Five were spoken of as three days off, and the pause in the neat dark hand was seen a fifth time at the Court's table at the square's Slate Row fringe.
**Relations:** Central Sill, between the two bridges; Slate Row lies a quarter league east; the Fishstairs below the north bridge; Low Kiln across the river to the south.

### Slate Row
**Kind:** Court quarter. **Region:** Sill.
A narrow street holding the Court's room, the assessor's house, the collector's table and the Waiting Room. On market-days the Court also sets a table at the fringe of the Cross, where a clerk touches the top slate before he looks at the woman waiting and says *it's entered as it stands*; from that end of the square Slate Row is spoken of as a direction, not a place anybody means to go that day.
**Relations:** A quarter league east of the Cross; Low Kiln is across the river, south-west.

### The Waiting Room
**Kind:** room. **Region:** Slate Row, Sill.
The room where the collected are kept overnight before banking, and where the Forty of Low Kiln were held in Y.H. 812.
**Relations:** Inside Slate Row, behind the collector's table.

### The Fishstairs
**Kind:** landing steps. **Region:** Sill, north bank.
The steps below the north bridge where eels are sold at dawn and rumours at noon; Rusk keeps a room above them. The way down counts four steps, four, and a landing where a step should have been; in the Breaking the Ilm comes up over the steps and puts them back a hand's breadth out of line, and the district reads the water as weather and goes on using them.
**Relations:** Immediately below the north bridge at the Cross; the Weir is 1 league upriver; Rusk's room is above the steps.

### The edge of the fish-trade
**Kind:** shaded trading ground. **Region:** the Fishstairs, Sill, north bank.
The boards at the outer edge of the dawn trade, under the north bridge's shadow, where the eel stalls thin out and no Court slate has ever reached. Sella Vail sets the basket here when she wants the boy to see the trade and not be seen into --- the same care she used at the edge of the light at the Drawing. On Stone 15, Y.H. 812 she set him in this shade while Dessa Silt counted nineteen at the head of the steps and Mira Silt put a brown-edged barley crust into the fold. The market bell strikes back toward it from the Cross, four, four and a gap.
**Relations:** At the Fishstairs below the north bridge and within sight of the Cross; the steps run down from it to the landing that is not a step.

### The Bone Orchard
**Kind:** burial ground. **Region:** Grath, outside Sill's north gate.
The ground of pots broken for the dead and of the shallow graves of thin-return children. On market-days a barrow of grey Ash goes out through the north gate toward it, heaped a little above the sides where the load has shifted, and nobody in the square looks at it twice; it is the afternoon's work, the same work that goes out every market-day. the boy puts the grey in the barrow with the ridge under the Vail skirting board and the charred slate in the kiln ash, and nobody explains any of the three. Another barrow went out through the north gate at the last market on Stone 30, Y.H. 812, heaped a little above the sides where the load had shifted; nobody looked twice.
**Relations:** 1 league north of the Cross by the north gate road; Cold Acre is another league beyond it on the ridge.

### The Clay Yard
**Kind:** work yard. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
The slip pits, wheel shed, drying racks and kiln that make up a clay child's whole world. House Silt stands three doors up the yard; Sella Vail's walk from its door to the Vail kiln-house on the third morning is counted by the boy in the bundle as a line of steps, against Dessa's nineteen out and nineteen back. On that morning the sleet of the day before has gone off into a dry cold that lifts the oiled paper at the windows; a woman at a tub looks up as the bundle passes and goes back to her work without appearing to have seen it.
**Relations:** Inside Low Kiln, beside the Slip Brook; the Ilm is a short walk south.

### The Vail kiln-house
**Kind:** house and kiln. **Region:** the Clay Yard, Low Kiln.
A kiln-house held by the Vail family for four generations; Sella Vail keeps the fire and Jorra Vail is her son; Corin is taken in here as a foundling on the third day and laid on a bed of fresh straw under the roof. The house is known by its objects before its people: the kiln breathing in fours with a gap where the fifth breath should be, coals banked under ash, a door that does not quite shut, the hollow wheel-seat, a salt-stiff coat on a peg behind the door, and a proverb in the house's mouth (*leave the crack showing*) said to the pot and the fire and never to a person. The Ash ridge along the skirting board is near two thumbs wide, turns the corner without a break, and has not been swept since Garl Vail died uncollected in the Long Cold; it will be swept on the Last Day and burned next morning. In the kiln ash lies the charred edge of a slate, black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark, the writing gone off the end; the house has swept around it for a year without picking it up. The house keeps a roll of its own, entered in a careful hand and not shown to a man come down from Slate Row with a slate. Through the last tend of Y.H. 812 the boy lay on his bed under this roof with two crusts in the fold, one spent from Stone 15 and one given by Mira Silt on Stone 28, and the kiln took its breath in fours and a gap on every day.
**Relations:** In the Clay Yard, Low Kiln; the Slip Brook runs behind it; the Old Kiln is 1 league south; the wheel-shed and the Debt Loft are within it.

### The Vail wheel-shed
**Kind:** workshop. **Region:** the Vail kiln-house, Clay Yard, Low Kiln.
The shed where Garl Vail's wheel stands with its seat worn hollow on the left side from twenty years of leaning; Sella will not let anyone fill it, though she permits her son to sit there --- a distinction the house has never explained. Jorra Vail works there with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the left little finger bent from a splinter at eleven, and the clay-bound hand Tarn sleeps above it. A child of the house first learns the full note of a pot here. On the third day a pot set down on the bench gives its small flat note; Tarn's broom stops for two strokes and nobody says what the sound was.
**Relations:** Inside the Vail kiln-house; the Debt Loft is above it; the drying racks stand outside to the east.

### The drying racks
**Kind:** racks, work-place. **Region:** the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill.
The open racks outside the Vail wheel-shed to the east, where wet pots stand to dry and the yard does its ordinary work around a basket set down at the edge of the floor. Fine grey slate dust from the room off the shed comes back on a thrower's forearms past the bend of the little finger and is read against bone-coloured slip; under the racks is the space where whole slate should have stood and which the yard goes around without entering. On Stone 25, Y.H. 812 a pot set down at the racks cracked all the way through with the small flat knock and was called a pot gone wrong and swept around; on Stone 29 the Last Day brooms came out among the racks and a woman at the tubs said the grey would be left where it lay until then.
**Relations:** Inside the Clay Yard, east of the Vail wheel-shed; the Slip Brook runs behind them; House Silt stands three doors down the yard.

### The Debt Loft
**Kind:** sleeping loft. **Region:** the Vail kiln-house, Clay Yard, Low Kiln.
The narrow loft above the wheel-shed where the house's clay-bound hands sleep; Tarn has slept here for six of his seven years and keeps his few things in an unmarked clay pot. On the third day the foundling is given a bed under the Vail roof while Tarn is given this loft --- a poor household sorting its own members and calling the sorting kindness. Tarn came down the ladder with a broom when the boy was laid on the straw, and said *He's a foundling. I'm a debt. Don't put us in the same sentence, Sella.*
**Relations:** Above the Vail wheel-shed, inside the Vail kiln-house.

### The back room
**Kind:** work room, kept closed. **Region:** the Vail kiln-house, Clay Yard, Low Kiln.
A room off the wheel-shed which the house does not enter in any roll it shows. Men come into it whose names are not spoken and speak in low voices; Jorra Vail comes out of it with slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms, grey and fine and not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel, and says *the back room's cold* to nobody in particular. The boy watches the dust and wants to know which slate was broken and whether it was a birth-slate, and cannot ask. Sella shifts the damper instead of answering. The broken slates under the drying rack remain unshown.
**Relations:** Inside the Vail kiln-house, off the wheel-shed; the Debt Loft is above the shed.

### The close ground of Sill
*The small features of the birth-town drawn on `atlas/home-settlement`: the land a child of Low Kiln knows by foot. Direction and distance are from the Cross unless the entry says otherwise.*

### The north bridge
**Kind:** bridge. **Region:** Sill, at the Cross.
The upper of Sill's two bridges, by the market square; the Fishstairs lie just below it on the north bank and the Singing Sill a little way downstream.
**Relations:** At the Cross, over the Ilm above the four-foot fall; the south bridge lies downriver, beyond Slate Row.

### The south bridge
**Kind:** bridge. **Region:** Sill.
The lower bridge, carrying the Clay Road and the river road out east of the town toward Greybank.
**Relations:** Downriver of the Cross and Slate Row; the meal mill stands just below it on the south bank.

### House Clay
**Kind:** house, holding. **Region:** High Kiln, Sill.
The kiln-master's house of Berr Clay, Thane of Clay, above the terrace. The red wool cord is folded away in a drawer here, and a locked box kept in the house has never been destroyed.
**Relations:** In High Kiln, above Low Kiln and west of the Glaze Room.

### House Silt
**Kind:** house, holding. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
The two-room clay-daubed house of Dessa and Mira Silt, three doors from the Vail kiln-house; its front room is the birth room of Chapter 1, where Corin is born on Stone 7, Y.H. 812. The floor is trodden ash packed until it has the spring of old bread, and a grey ridge of Ash runs along the skirting board from the bench by the door, under the door and into the next room, left there until the Last Day. The room holds a board-bed with a fold of grey wool, a stool by the wall, a hearth with a slip pot, and the birth-slate propped on the bench within the midwife's right hand, its writing turned under. The door does not shut; a low song from a woman working below comes up through the floor. The slip pits are a walk away, counted in nineteen steps to the drying rack. The stone at the newborn's belly was warmed at the Vail hearth across the yard and carried here in a rag; on the third morning it is left behind in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned bed when Sella carries the boy away, and the cup across the room gives its flat note to the empty bed. Alda sets a cup down twice in this room on the second day and neither time does anyone say what the sound was.
**Relations:** In Low Kiln, east of the slip pits of the Clay Yard; the Vail kiln-house is three doors west, down the yard.

### The Drawing house
**Kind:** dwelling, death-house. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill; two doors down the Clay Yard from the Vail kiln-house.
An unnamed neighbour's clay-daubed house, the same room as a birth room and the Vail kiln-house with its bench by the door, its pot on the bench, its draught under the door and the grey of the Ash in a corner. Sella Vail carried the boy here before the kiln's first breath on Stone 12, Y.H. 812, and set the basket at the edge of the light where he could see the collection and not be part of it, never where a slate lay open. A dying neighbour on a board-bed against the far wall gave under the collector's cold iron tube, left with a pale round place on his arm where the iron had been; a rekindled child on a folded quilt at the other side of the hearth grew worse before the day was out. The Ash ridge along this room's skirting board is thicker by a finger's width than the ridge at Vail and turns the corner by the door without a break. The boy counted the room's nails four, four and a knot the width of a thumb, the same gap as under the Vail roof. On Stone 29, Y.H. 812 Sella walked two doors down and laid her thumb in this ridge after measuring the Vail one; this ridge was a finger's width thicker, and neither was swept.
**Relations:** Two doors down the Clay Yard from the Vail kiln-house; House Silt is three doors down in the other direction.

### House Reed
**Kind:** house, holding. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
The reed-cutters' house of Alda Reed, the hearth-maid, with a tin of second notes hidden under a floorboard. The chart draws the house but not the tin.
**Relations:** In Low Kiln, east of the Clay Yard, near the birth-flower shrine.

### Rusk's room
**Kind:** room, dwelling. **Region:** the Fishstairs, Sill.
A room above the Fishstairs in which an old man keeps a drawer with a small lock. He never sits down indoors.
**Relations:** Above the Fishstairs, below the north bridge.

### The Court's room
**Kind:** hall, court and place of the Return. **Region:** Slate Row, Sill.
The room where the slate is read aloud on market-day and the district's measure is spoken for; in the Keeping, bank and church are one room. On Stone 19, Y.H. 812 a neighbour come back from it described it to the yard: a bench by the door from which the slate is read, a pot set on the bench, a draught under the pot going along the floor to the corner and felt at the ankles before the cold is felt, and a place in the figures where a line should stand and does not, the same every time; the figure is read twice, once for the moot and once for those who come late. The boy set this room beside the birth room in House Silt, the Vail kiln-house and the death-house two doors down, which share the bench, the pot, the draught and the gap, and kept the four likenesses in four separate places rather than write a cause where he had only noticed a likeness.
**Relations:** In Slate Row, a quarter league east of the Cross; a table carrying its business in the open air stands at the Slate Row fringe of the Cross.

### The Court's table at the Cross
**Kind:** table, open-air place of business. **Region:** the Cross, Sill.
A table at the Slate Row fringe of the market where the Court's business is done in the open air before it is carried into Slate Row. The clerk who keeps it has a cuff gone soft from washing and ink in the creases of two fingers; he lays his hand on the top slate before he looks at the woman waiting, then says *it's entered as it stands. There's nothing in it the household need correct.* The upper line of that slate is in a strange neat hand, with a small stop between two clusters that the boy has seen twice before and cannot read. The table is distinct from the collector's table in Slate Row, where the measure is actually drawn. On Stone 30, Y.H. 812 the soft-cuffed clerk laid his hand on the top slate here a third time, and the small stop in the neat dark upper line came a fifth time and lay unjoined beside the other four.
**Relations:** At the Slate Row end of the Cross; Slate Row lies a quarter league east.

### The moot-post
**Kind:** post, notice-board. **Region:** the Cross, Sill.
The post by the block where the slate is read, with a nail in it from which the printed notice hangs --- a square of paper gone soft at one corner, with a row of marks the wind turns over and back. Sella sets the basket down beside it in the late morning, in the post's finger's width of shade, the only shade at that end of the square, and the boy lies with his cheek to its cold iron. She did so again on Stone 30, Y.H. 812, at the last market of the year, with both crusts in the fold at his side.
**Relations:** At the Cross, beside the reading block.

### The assessor's house
**Kind:** house. **Region:** Slate Row, Sill.
The narrow house of Sill's assessor, built in Y.H. 776.
**Relations:** In Slate Row, beside the Court's room.

### The collector's table
**Kind:** table, place of business. **Region:** Slate Row, Sill.
The table at which the collector draws the measure and writes a name; the Waiting Room stands behind it.
**Relations:** In Slate Row, before the Waiting Room.

### The smithy
**Kind:** works, forge. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
A small forge east of the Clay Yard where kiln irons, barrow irons and the collectors' cold-iron tubes are mended. It keeps no kiln-mark and is not a great house.
**Relations:** In Low Kiln, between the Clay Yard and House Silt.

### The meal mill
**Kind:** mill, works. **Region:** Sill, on the Ilm below the south bridge.
A small water-mill grinding the barley baked into the brown-edged loaf of Low Kiln.
**Relations:** On the south bank just below the south bridge, downriver of the Cross.

### The birth-flower shrine
**Kind:** shrine, niche. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
A small hearth-maids' niche where birth-slates are left overnight and the Return is taught to the district's children. A crossed mark is cut above it, which the chart draws but does not explain.
**Relations:** In Low Kiln, beside House Reed, east of the Clay Yard.

### The yard well
**Kind:** well. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
The shared well of the low houses, with a stone lip and a little roof, a short walk from the drying racks. It is not the never-freezing well of Cold Acre.
**Relations:** In Low Kiln, east of the Clay Yard.

### The barley fields
**Kind:** fields. **Region:** Sill.
Strips of barley on the warmer ground east of Low Kiln and on the terrace above; the second firing follows the harvest in Smoke.
**Relations:** East of Low Kiln toward the river road, with a further strip north of High Kiln.

### The turnip grounds
**Kind:** fields. **Region:** Sill, west of Low Kiln.
Small plots of turnips and chard worked by the households of the slip pits.
**Relations:** West of the Clay Yard, toward the river.

### The steep street
**Kind:** street, path. **Region:** Sill.
The steep way from the High Kiln terrace down to the north bank and the north bridge; the potters' children run it barefoot in Ember.
**Relations:** From High Kiln down to the Cross and the north bridge.

### The north gate
**Kind:** gate. **Region:** Sill.
The gate on the north road out of Sill; the Bone Orchard lies a league beyond it and Cold Acre another league past that.
**Relations:** North of the Cross, at the head of the road to the Bone Orchard.

### The reedbeds
**Kind:** marsh, reeds. **Region:** Sill, south and east.
The wet ground at the edge of the settlement where reeds are cut and wolves come in the hunting month of Bone.
**Relations:** South of Low Kiln, toward the Old Kiln and the marsh road to Reedholt.

### The Weir
**Kind:** fishing weir and plank walk. **Region:** Grath, on the Ilm.
Eel traps and a plank walk one league upriver, where the Fish Blessing is held and Rusk walks at dusk. Orra Weir keeps the traps and dries the eels here.
**Relations:** 1 league upriver (west) of Sill; Stonemouth is a further four leagues upriver; the Eel House stands on the plank walk.

### The Eel House
**Kind:** hut and drying rack. **Region:** the Weir, Grath.
Orra Weir's trap-hut on the plank walk, where eels are salted and the cords of knot-accounts are kept. The Fish Blessing is prepared here on Reed 15.
**Relations:** On the Weir, 1 league upriver of Sill.

### The plank walk
**Kind:** walkway. **Region:** the Weir, Grath.
The raised boards that follow the north bank of the Ilm out to the traps and over the water on posts; the only dry way to the eels when the river rises. Rusk walks it at dusk. The Weir chart draws the boards and their posts but not which of them will bear a man's weight in Silt.
**Relations:** Along the north bank at the Weir, 1 league upriver of Sill; the Eel House and the eel racks stand beside it, and a spur turns down onto the weir head.

### The eel traps
**Kind:** fishing traps. **Region:** the Weir, Grath.
Conical baskets set in the gaps of the weir with their mouths upriver, so an eel goes in and cannot find the way out. Orra Weir lifts and salts them; the hearth-maids bless them at the Fish Blessing. A trap-keeper's saying about them is cut in the margin of the Weir chart.
**Relations:** In the Ilm at the Weir, 1 league upriver of Sill; the plank walk serves them.

### The eel racks
**Kind:** drying racks. **Region:** the Weir, Grath.
The poles beside the Eel House where the caught eels are hung to salt. These are not the drying racks of the Vail Clay Yard in Low Kiln, though both are entered in the Atlas with the same careful line.
**Relations:** Beside the Eel House on the plank walk at the Weir, 1 league upriver of Sill.

### The blessing stone
**Kind:** stone, shrine. **Region:** the Weir, Grath.
A flat stone on the north bank where the hearth-maids stand to bless the weir and the traps at the Fish Blessing. A crossed mark is cut into it, which the chart draws but does not explain.
**Relations:** On the north bank at the Weir, beside the plank walk, 1 league upriver of Sill.

### The river road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath, along the Ilm.
The bank way by which Sill reaches the Weir and Stonemouth; it meets the Clay Road at the Cross in Sill. Packed clay, rutted in Silt and passable on foot when the low road is under water.
**Relations:** Runs along the north bank past the Weir, 1 league upriver of Sill, and on to Stonemouth, 4 leagues further up.

### Cold Acre
**Kind:** farm hamlet. **Region:** Grath, north ridge.
Six houses, barley, turnips and one well that never freezes.
**Relations:** 2 leagues north of Sill on the ridge; 1 league beyond the Bone Orchard.

### The Tallow Works
**Kind:** works. **Region:** Grath, north-east of Sill.
A rendering house for sheep fat whose smell carries into Sill on a north wind.
**Relations:** 2 leagues north-east of Sill; Cold Acre about two leagues to the west.

### Mud End
**kind:** peat hamlet. **Region:** Grath, downriver.
Nine houses and peat stacks where the moot meets in a byre and the winter peat decides whether anyone stays warm.
**Relations:** 3 leagues downriver (east) of Sill; Greybank a further 15 leagues east; Reedholt lies in the marsh to the south.

### Reedholt
**Kind:** marsh hamlet. **Region:** Grath, east marsh.
Reed-cutters' huts on the edge of the marsh where wolves come in the reedbeds.
**Relations:** About 4 leagues east of Sill, south of the river road; Mud End about 2 leagues north-east.

### Stonemouth
**Kind:** quarry village. **Region:** Grath, upper Ilm.
The meeting of the Ferr and the Ilm, with lime kilns and coarse sandstone workings.
**Relations:** 5 leagues upriver (west) of Sill; the Weir is 4 leagues downriver; Nine Pots a further 3 leagues west by the Ferr.

### The Lead Run
**Kind:** stream. **Region:** Grath, upper Ilm basin.
A blue-grey stained stream running off an old working nobody will admit to, said to make a man thin if drunk through a season.
**Relations:** About 6 leagues west of Sill; enters the Ferr; Stonemouth a few leagues down the Ferr.

### Nine Pots
**Kind:** pottery hamlet. **Region:** Grath, west.
Four kilns and a shared clay pit, in permanent feud with Sill over the road.
**Relations:** 8 leagues west of Sill; Stonemouth about 3 leagues east; the Rim Road passes north of it.

### Greybank
**Kind:** granary town. **Region:** Grath, eastern gate.
The Court's granary and factor's house, where the Clay Road ends and the Hearth Road begins; the first place a Sill man sees the Court at full size.
**Relations:** 18 leagues east downriver of Sill; Mud End 15 leagues west; the Hearth Road runs 140 leagues south-east to the Hearthseat.

### The Counting House
**Kind:** counting house. **Region:** Greybank, Grath.
The Vhaleni factor's counting house in Greybank where glass, grain, quiet ash and whole returns are brokered and where documents on salt-paper are left in the damp; Veno Brine keeps his private drawer of dry documents here.
**Relations:** Inside Greybank, 18 leagues east downriver of Sill, near the Court's factor's house and the end of the Clay Road.

### The Old Kiln
**Kind:** ruin. **Region:** Grath, south of Sill.
A collapsed kiln with grey clay that will not take a glaze and an ash throat deeper than a man.
**Relations:** 1 league south of Sill; a slate in the Vail kiln-house came from here.

## III. Rivers and roads

### The Ilm
**Kind:** river. **Region:** Grath to the Sallow Sea.
The great silty river of Grath, frozen from Stone into Ilmen and breaking in the Silt flood called the Breaking. On Stone 19, Y.H. 812 a man come up from the river held a palm's breadth of its ice up to the tallow light in the Clay Yard: a dark hair ran in from one edge and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the other, and he said the water under it had gone different. On Stone 21 a man come back from looking at it brought a number he would not give, shut in a fist with the thumb tucked under, and said it had come a hair further. A woman from High Kiln said the thaw would come early; the yard wanted it late, because a late thaw puts off the levy, and Sella called the crack weather. The boy filed the hairline as a shape, not a cause, beside the other gaps.
**Relations:** Rises in the Rim about 120 leagues west of Sill; passes Stonemouth, the Weir and Sill; runs east through Greybank and reaches the Sallow Sea some 80 leagues below Sill.

### The Ferr
**Kind:** river, tributary. **Region:** upper Grath.
A colder tributary carrying lead dust from the western workings; its water is said to thin a man who drinks it through a season.
**Relations:** About 40 leagues long; joins the Ilm at Stonemouth, 5 leagues west of Sill; the Lead Run enters it a few leagues upstream.

### The Slip Brook
**Kind:** brook. **Region:** Low Kiln, Sill.
A narrow clay-coloured stream through the Clay Yard whose colour the children of Low Kiln read as weather. On Stone 25, Y.H. 812 it carried a skin the colour of skimmed milk gone grey that held under the wind without breaking, and on Stone 27 it still carried it; both were read as weather.
**Relations:** Runs behind the Vail kiln-house and enters the Ilm at Sill.

### The Clay Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath.
Packed clay over corduroy, five days by cart in Grain and six in Silt; surfaced as a Court road by the treaty of Y.H. 418.
**Relations:** Runs east from the Cross at Sill to Greybank, 18 leagues; the Salt Road continues from Greybank.

### The Salt Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath to Vhalen.
The trade route from the clay towns to the Sallow ports, by Greybank and the coast pass.
**Relations:** Begins at Sill, runs east to Greybank (18 leagues) and then north-east to Vhal (a further 62 leagues); total 80 leagues.

### The Rim Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Grath to Keld.
The improving road and worsening weather between Sill and the Rim, travelled with a warm-stone.
**Relations:** Runs west-north-west from Sill to Keld Keep, 120 leagues; reaches the Cold Pass at 130 leagues.

### The Hearth Road
**Kind:** road. **Region:** Greybank to the Hearthseat.
The merchants' road to the Court, spoken of in Grath as if it were a fever.
**Relations:** Runs south-east from Greybank to the Hearthseat, 140 leagues; Greybank is 18 leagues east of Sill.

## IV. Wonders, wastes and buried places

### The Nine Wells
**Kind:** hot springs. **Region:** the Keel.
Nine springs where the ground gives back more than it takes; four sealed by the Court in Y.H. 1 to 120, a fifth in Y.H. 618, and the keys kept.
**Relations:** On the Keel within twenty leagues of the Hearthseat and the Hearth beneath it.

### The Glaze Cliffs
**Kind:** coastal cliffs. **Region:** Vhalen, Sallow shore.
Blue-green cliffs stained by old lead workings, sweet-smelling in the heat; children are warned not to lick them.
**Relations:** On the Sallow coast about five leagues from Vhal, roughly 85 leagues north-east of Sill.

### The Empty Cantons
**Kind:** depopulated province. **Region:** south of Grath.
Roads, wells and doorways with no people; the ledger's empty squares after Y.H. 40, paid for by the first whole returns.
**Relations:** About 60 leagues south of Sill, between Grath and the Fenmarch; the Peat Line lies further south-west.

### The Singing Sill
**Kind:** river ledge, wonder. **Region:** Sill, the Ilm.
The hard stone under the falls that gives a low note when the Warm rises in Ilmen; the town takes its name from it.
**Relations:** Under the Ilm at Sill, below the Fishstairs and the north bridge.

## V. The first life — Earth

*These are not places in Sarn and carry no distance from Sill. They are entered here because they are named in the past-life memories and must be written with `\place{...}` when a scene breaks into Chicago. The canonical names below are exact. Only what Dan's life touched is listed; the rest of Earth is not in the book.*

### Chicago
**Kind:** city. **Region:** Earth, Illinois, the 2020s.
The city of the first life, on the shore of a great fresh-water lake; a place of elevated trains, trauma bays and blizzards. It has no Hearth, no collectors and no word for the Warm, which is why Dan can never explain it to anyone in Sarn.
**Relations:** Contains Hermosa, Elmwood Park, Evanston, Cook County Hospital, Lane Tech, the University of Illinois Chicago and the hospital car park; Lake Michigan lies along its eastern edge.

### Hermosa
**Kind:** neighbourhood. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The block of two-storey brick bungalows where Dan grew up, with the el running high and iron over Pulaski and the summer smelling of charcoal and wet pavement. Site of the Marchetti family house, where love was shown by fixing things and never discussed.
**Relations:** Inside Chicago; a short drive to Lane Tech and to Cook County Hospital.

### Elmwood Park
**Kind:** neighbourhood. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan and Elena Rossi were married in a church with too many relatives and a photographer who cried; also where Nina grew up.
**Relations:** Inside Chicago, near Hermosa.

### Evanston
**Kind:** city. **Region:** Earth, Illinois.
The lakeside city where Paul Vesci was raised and where he keeps a private practice with good light.
**Relations:** Just north of Chicago on Lake Michigan.

### Cook County Hospital
**Kind:** hospital, workplace. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The county trauma centre where Dan trained, became an attending and worked nights; a large room with ceiling-mounted lights where a team moves with the rehearsed speed of people who have done the same thing four thousand times. The place that made him and the place that ate his marriage.
**Relations:** In Chicago; near the apartment Dan kept after the divorce and the car park where he and Des sat; site of Tasha Broussard's review.

### The hospital car park
**Kind:** place, ritual site. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan and Des ate bad pizza in silence for ten minutes after a bad shift, and where Dan learned of his father's death at sunrise. The friendship's whole architecture. Remembered in Chapter 2 in the present tense: wet slush under the lamps, the dashboard clock past three, cold pizza in a paper bag on the console, the lemon-and-diesel smell of the rig, Des's gold tooth catching the light, the laugh that filled the space where the eleventh minute would have been, and the same laugh running on the dispatcher's channel for exactly one minute after the expressway.
**Relations:** Beside Cook County Hospital.

### Lane Tech
**Kind:** school. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The technical high school Dan attended because his mother insisted, where he took things apart on the shop floor and first learned that he liked a right answer under his hands.
**Relations:** In Chicago, not far from Hermosa.

### The University of Illinois Chicago
**Kind:** university. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Dan found the human body in the cadaver lab the way other boys find engines — by being forbidden and doing it anyway.
**Relations:** In Chicago.

### Lake Michigan
**Kind:** lake. **Region:** Earth, beside Chicago.
The great cold lake at the edge of Dan's life, and the subject of the boat he promised Nina for nine years and never bought. The photograph on his wall shows Nina at six squinting in its summer light.
**Relations:** Along Chicago's eastern shore; the boat promise and the unsent call are bound to it.

### The expressway
**Kind:** road, site of the crash. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
The northbound expressway near the split, where a pileup formed in a blizzard at 2:17 in the morning and Dan died pulling a child from a minivan before the fire took them both. The woman in the grey coat stood at the edge of the flare light here with a slate.
**Relations:** In Chicago, out toward the northern suburbs; the hinge between the two lives.

### The apartment near the hospital
**Kind:** dwelling. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Dan's small flat after the divorce: a bed, a chair, a television, a fridge with four beers and food he never ate. On the wall, Nina at six; in a drawer, an unsent letter to Elena with one honest sentence; beside the bed, a half-read book and a cold coffee.
**Relations:** Near Cook County Hospital.

### The nursing home
**Kind:** workplace. **Region:** Chicago, Earth.
Where Theresa Marchetti worked part-time as a nurse's aide, coming home with her uniform smelling of bleach, old people and lilac soap. It is never named more precisely in the book.
**Relations:** In Chicago.
