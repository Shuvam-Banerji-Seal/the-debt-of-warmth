# Reckoning of Days

Day 1 | Stone 7, Y.H. 812 (Seventhday) | the birth room off the Clay Yard in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Corin is born two hours old with Daniel Marchetti's whole memory and a branching red flower on his ribs; the stone Dessa warmed at the Vail fire goes cold at his belly while a name is entered in a neat dark hand on Alda's slate before anyone has looked at his face, Mira gives bread and keeps her back to the writing, and Sella's door-burned hand feels for the warmth, hovers over the bundle and withdraws with the third day still to come.
Day 2 | Stone 8, Y.H. 812 (Eighthday) | the Vail kiln-house and wheel-shed, and the birth room of House Silt, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | In the kiln-house the fire keeps its four-and-four-and-a-gap breath around Garl's hollow wheel-seat, salt coat and unswept Ash ridge while Tarn pockets and returns the bench knife and Jorra declares the foundling is not Vail to a Sella who answers the pot instead, and three doors down Corin, two days old, hears the flat note of a cup he cannot reach, feels the warmed stone go past temperature, endures Alda's ordered washing and unasked-for dates and Des's laugh from a Chicago car park, while Dessa calls her cough weather, counts nineteen steps and refuses his name as the room arranges an unspoken question about who pays to keep him.
Day 3 | Stone 9, Y.H. 812 (Ninthday) | the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house with its wheel-shed and Debt Loft, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella carries the covered bundle up the icy yard with the burn-door closing on its weight and the boy counting her steps, while Dessa stands at the end of nineteen steps she does not take with her thumb on the sieve-wire scar and Alda tells her he went in the levy among the children taken for the account and lets unasked-for dates settle over an unspoken figure, leaving the cold stone behind on the abandoned bed, and under the Vail roof the boy is laid on fresh straw with heat returning painfully to his hands and feet, hears Jorra's sentence sealed and Tarn's I'm-a-debt-not-a-foundling and a second flat note from a pot, and sees the charred edge of a slate in the kiln ash that he cannot reach, the market and the Cross still ahead.
Day 4 | Stone 10, Y.H. 812 (Tenthday) | the Clay Yard, the Cross and the Slate Row end of the market, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath; one remembered Cook County trauma bay | Sella Vail carries the boy in a basket under wool from the banked Vail kiln to Account-day at the Cross, where Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron and reads one in twelve, as the notice says, twice to two hundred leaning people while the boy counts a hundred and nine steps, eight pigs and the market bell's four-four-and-a-gap and hears the slate give its flat answer a third and a fourth time; Ostrowski's voice breaks through in the present tense with the two duties and a pocket with no phone in it, a clerk's neat hand with its small stop is seen a third time, Berr confirms Sella's widow's mark at Greybank as a kindness entered while leaving Garl's tile debt uncalled, Alda Reed crosses two fingers at the flower and covers the sign with a date, Mira Silt asks her own eel price without once looking at the basket and shoves a loose board clear, Ash goes out in a barrow toward the north gate and the Bone Orchard, and Sella turns back up the yard with the boy holding numbers he cannot spend and a note he cannot place.
Day 5 | Stone 11, Y.H. 812 (Firstday) | the Vail kiln-house and wheel-shed, the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Under coals that held all night Sella Vail touched the widow's mark before she opened the kiln and at no other time while the five-day-old boy could not keep wool on his chin and she put the fold right without looking, Tarn took the small bench knife, set it back where it could be checked and stopped his broom two strokes at a Vail pot's flat note without turning his head, Jorra spoke to the pots with slip dried to old bone on his forearms, and Alda Reed came under the roof to give a neighbour mother exact dates over a rekindled child going grey and wrote a note she turned writing-down, leaving the boy with cold, hunger and a sound nobody in the house named.
Day 6 | Stone 12, Y.H. 812 (Secondday, the Hearth Drawing) | the Vail kiln-house, a neighbour's death-house two doors down, the threshold of House Silt, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath; two sentences in a remembered Cook County bay | Sella carried the boy in the basket to a neighbour's house and set him at the edge of the light where a collector with a soft cuff took a cold iron tube to a dying man's arm and the man gave with the small flat knock --- fifth hearing of the note and first from a person --- while the figure read at the Cross was entered in a neat ordinary hand as the account stood, and later the rekindled child was worse on the third day of its return, Alda turned a second note down under a milk cup, the death-house Ash ridge measured a finger's width thicker than the ridge at Vail and turned the corner without a break, and the year's first measure had been taken from the dying and the newly born on the same day before Sella banked the coals at dusk, fed the boy from the house's own dish and passed Dessa Silt counting nineteen steps into her thumb at her threshold without a look into the basket.
Day 7 | Stone 13, Y.H. 812 (Thirdday) | the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella Vail touched the widow's mark before she opened the kiln and at no other time and fed the seven-day-old boy barley gruel from the house's own dish while a pot on the shed bench was turned toward before it was set down and gave nothing, the interval kept as an object and the hearing count still five from four sources, while Tarn, the knife already back on its sill, saw the turning and stopped his broom half a stroke at the empty set-down and said nothing, Jorra worked the wheel with bone-pale slip and no slate dust, the number of days to Kindling went around as a closed fist and entered the house as an absence, Tarn's sixth year was counted and his seventh held back, and at dusk Sella banked the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch the mark.
Day 8 | Stone 14, Y.H. 812 (Fourthday) | the Fishstairs below the north bridge, the row up from the Ilm and the walk back to the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath; two remembered sentences in a Cook County cafeteria | Sella carried the eight-day-old boy down before light to buy eels, where Mira Silt worked with pale slime on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone, flicked eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, gave Berr Clay four eels where six should go against his two marks short for six years, called the basket warm off the slate and not on one, and touched the grey cord at her waist when a figure was spoken, while a collector crossed the bridge with a soft cuff and two present-tense sentences of a cafeteria at four in the morning and a paper wrapper eaten to its last crumb broke through and were taken back by the river, and the boy counted four steps down and four and a landing where a step should have been before Sella bought two pair of eels and carried him home with the bucket swinging at her knee and river silt on the wool.
Day 9 | Stone 15, Y.H. 812 (Fifthday) | the edge of the fish-trade in the north bridge's shade, the Fishstairs, and the Vail kiln-house at dusk, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella set the nine-day-old boy in a patch of shade no slate had reached, where Dessa Silt stood at the end of nineteen steps with slip-grey nails, a sieve-wire scar and the cough from the pits called weather, counted four loaves on the seventh of Stone and bits to the woman with a basket, and did not look in, while a green-mouthed girl watched the pots and Mira's grey cord lay at her waist over a second cord never taken to Nine Pots before she put a brown-edged barley crust into the fold --- a crust, not a sermon --- that he could smell and feel but could not lift to his mouth, and at dusk Sella counted her loaves twice, Tarn put the small knife back on the sill and Jorra moved the damper, leaving the boy on his bed under the roof with the warmth at his side and his hand going up an inch and coming down, four, four and a gap.
Day 10 | Stone 16, Y.H. 812 (Sixthday) | the Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Ten days old with the market crust gone cold in the fold, the boy watched Sella Vail touch the widow's mark before she opened the kiln and at no other hour, measured the kindling stack with Tarn as four bundles and four and a space where the fifth should be while Sella arranged her loaves so the days to Kindling stood as a shape no collector could enter, heard Jorra say *you'll do* to a pot in bone-pale slip and move the damper before she had finished, and lay under Alda Reed's hand as she reached for her slate before she reached for him, touched the tin once inside her bodice, crossed two fingers at the red edge of his wrist without knowing it and covered the sign with an unasked-for date before writing a note she turned face-down into her shawl and went on giving dates after the question had stopped.
Day 11 | Stone 17, Y.H. 812 (Seventhday) | the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Tarn swept before light, set the small bench knife back on its sill and announced it to the empty yard, went still for two breaths when Sella said he had done well, then agreed flatly and swept on, while a pot turned toward on the bench gave nothing and his broom stopped half a stroke at the empty set-down and neither he nor the boy named the second interval, and Sella called the kiln's breath the danger a man slept into in Mud End and the district called that death merciful, Tarn held the pale ring at his left wrist up to the light and put the knife back where it could be checked, and Jorra shifted the damper with no slate dust on his forearms and did not say the back room was cold.
Day 12 | Stone 18, Y.H. 812 (Eighthday) | the Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard and the yard well, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The figure of the days went around the yards shut in a fist and arrived under the Vail roof only as an absence, and a neighbour at the threshold spoke around Garl's uncalled tile debt while Sella kept her half-pace and said *the house* where another woman would say I, and at the yard well another neighbour reached for her slate before her child and for two sentences the boy was in a ceiling light at four with a chart's note turned face-down and a hand going past it to a signature, then in a pocket that held wool where a lit phone with a name had lain, and the hand came back with nothing as he filed it beside the other record-first hands and found wool, woodsmoke and the cold crust at the end of the reaching.
Day 13 | Stone 19, Y.H. 812 (Ninthday) | the Vail kiln-house and the yard path toward the Ilm, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | A man come up from the river with water frozen on his legs held a shard of ice to the tallow light in which a dark hair ran and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge, a woman from High Kiln said the thaw would come early and everybody who wanted it late contradicted her because a late thaw puts off what is payable in the thaw, a neighbour back from the Court's room described its bench by the door, pot, draught and gap which the boy set beside the birth room and the death-house without joining them, and Sella measured the Ash by a thumb's width --- near two thumbs here and a finger more two doors down --- leaving both ridges to turn their corners unbroken until the Last Day while the household kept its number badly and he kept it exactly and could spend neither.
Day 14 | Stone 20, Y.H. 812 (Tenthday, Account-day) | the Cross, the Court's table at its Slate Row fringe, and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella carried the fourteen-day-old boy down in the basket and set him in the moot-post's shade a half-pace from every open slate, where the bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself, Berr Clay wiped his thumb and read one in twelve to two hundred leaning people, called the levy for the feast and said it was not called by him, went around the uncalled tile debt and did not look into the basket, and a soft-cuffed clerk at the Court's table laid his hand on the top slate before he looked at the woman waiting and said it stood as entered, while the boy saw the small pause in the neat dark hand a fourth time --- flare, birth-slate, Cross on Stone 10, now here --- unread and unjoined to the other three, with the Drawing collector's ordinary hand kept apart, and at dusk the coals hissed under ash, the fold was put right, the crust stayed cold in the fold and the Five were eleven days off.
Day 15 | Stone 21, Y.H. 812 (Firstday) | the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The last tend opened with Sella Vail touching the widow's mark before she lifted the lid and at no other hour, fuel and bread counted into the days in loaves, glances and one brown-edged loaf set apart while the boy carried his own exact number down to the kindling stack standing four, four and a space where the fifth should be, somebody back from looking at the ice bringing a number in a shut fist that was called a cold and answered with the fire, and Jorra working in bone-coloured slip with no dust, moving the damper early, while the kiln breathed four, four and a gap and the crust lay cold in the fold.
Day 16 | Stone 22, Y.H. 812 (Secondday) | the Vail kiln-house and a neighbour's house two doors down, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Tarn swept before light, set the small knife on its sill, told the empty yard it could be checked and said to the kindling six counted and the next he did not count, then fetched a collector for a neighbour whose cuffs were soft from washing and fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off and who set his hand to the slate before the bed saying the measure was taken and the account stood as entered, while on the way back a carried vessel gave its flat note past the door and Tarn's head turned a degree and under the roof the boy filed the half-heard note with the intervals, not among the five hearings from four sources.
Day 17 | Stone 23, Y.H. 812 (Thirdday) | the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the drying racks, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | After five clean mornings Jorra Vail came in with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of his little finger, said nothing of where he had been, told the wheel it did not care whose hands only if they were steady and went to the damper before Sella had finished, while low talk in the back room and missing slates under the drying racks stood only as shapes with a door kept shut, Sella got quieter and the yard straightened around it, and the boy filed the dust beside the ridge, the charred slate and the barrow of grey and made nothing of the likeness.
Day 18 | Stone 24, Y.H. 812 (Fourthday) | the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Alda Reed came under the roof smelling of soap and warm milk, laid the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turned to the basket, touched the little tin inside her bodice once without opening it, pressed the fold too long, crossed two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing it and covered the sign with a date nobody had asked for, went on giving exact dates after the question had stopped, said warm and breathing which was exact and not true, and wrote on a narrow slate what she did not leave lying about, turning it face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name.
Day 19 | Stone 25, Y.H. 812 (Fifthday) | the Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The cold came down another degree with oiled paper lifting at the windows and a grey skin on the Slip Brook, Sella set the basket facing the kiln wall to keep the river wind off his ears, and two paces from his head a pot cracked all the way through with the small flat knock he knew and nobody called a sound, which he could not turn toward, so a woman called it a pot gone wrong and he filed it as a third object on the interval tray while Tarn's broom checked half a stroke at the place and said nothing, Jorra's forearms were clean of dust, and the warmth crossed into him at the bank and was entered nowhere.
Day 20 | Stone 26, Y.H. 812 (Sixthday) | the Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard and the yard well, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | An ordinary Sixthday and not Account-day passed with mending, drying, sweeping and eating, while the number for the Five went around the yards in mouths and fists as three, then two, then whatever the moot gave out, a neighbour at the well counting it twice and a third saying it would be whatever the moot gave out before the argument ended in weather, Sella rearranged her loaves and fed him gruel thinned with milk with the warmth entered nowhere, Tarn swept twice and replaced the knife on its sill, and the boy kept the one number none of them would give, that one day after tonight was tomorrow.
Day 21 | Stone 27, Y.H. 812 (Seventhday) | the Vail kiln-house and the drying racks, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | A woman from High Kiln with sweetish lead-glaze at one corner of her mouth said the thaw would come early and neighbours who wanted it late contradicted her because the levy fell in the thaw and Tarn said to the thatch that late was his and meant a longer week before the paying, ice-mice spread through the thatch and a numb grey patch on a child's hand was called what the cold did, the boy kept the thaw argument and the hairline in the ice together and drew no line between them, and the kiln breathed four, four and a gap while the district's figure and the fire's figure did not agree.
Day 22 | Stone 28, Y.H. 812 (Eighthday) | the Clay Yard, the yard well and House Silt three doors down, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Mira Silt came up from the eels with silt on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone, answered a question with a question and her own answer, put her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a figure was spoken, gave a fresh brown-edged crust briskly into the fold saying it was a crust not a sermon, and showed a second cord twisted twice under her belt that had never gone upriver to Nine Pots, while three doors down Dessa coughed from the pits at the end of nineteen steps, called it weather, counted nineteen out and nineteen back and four loaves on the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve, smoothed her sieve-wire scar and shut her door-shut smile without looking into the basket, leaving the old crust cold beside the new.
Day 23 | Stone 29, Y.H. 812 (Ninthday) | the Clay Yard, the Vail kiln-house and a neighbour's house two doors down, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The yard made ready for the Last Day with brooms and a date said aloud two or three times, a woman said the grey would be left where it lay until the Last Day and burned the morning after and called dealt with, Sella laid one thumb into the Vail ridge and then the death-house ridge a finger's width thicker without saying which was which, Garl's salt coat kept its shape on its peg and the uncalled tile debt went around in a careful circle, Tarn offered to check the width with his thumb and set the knife on its sill, Jorra went quieter with no dust on his forearms and would not say the full note was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel, and at the bank Sella did not touch the mark.
Day 24 | Stone 30, Y.H. 812 (Tenthday, Account-day) | the Cross, the Court's table at its Slate Row fringe, the market's edge and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella carried the twenty-four-day-old boy down in the basket and set him in the moot-post's shade with cold iron at his cheek and both crusts in the fold, where the bell went four, four and a gap, Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve as the notice says, said the levy was called for the feast and not by him and did not look into the basket, the district gave the Five out as three days off, a soft-cuffed clerk at the Court's table laid his hand on the top slate before looking at the woman waiting and said it stood as entered, and the small pause in the neat dark hand came a fifth time without joining the other four, with one untagged beat of orange flare light on wet snow in which a hand in a grey sleeve found wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain and the name was not looked at, before Sella carried him home at dusk, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch the mark, the fire keeping four, four and a gap and Kindling tomorrow.
Day 25 | Kindling, Y.H. 812 | the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Before first light a coal under ash came up the yard in a clay dish from hearth to hearth with its source and cost unspoken, Sella Vail touched the widow's mark once before tipping it into the kiln and fed the twenty-five-day-old boy from the house's own dish without wiping his mouth, and he counted the new firelight four, four and an empty shelf where a fifth would be while a collector's first measure of the year went into a shut fist and a woman's thought-better-of sentence, the coat, hollow seat, unbroken ridge and charred slate keeping their places and the two crusts lying uneaten in the fold.
Day 26 | Weighing, Y.H. 812 | the outer fringe of the Cross, the doorway of the Court's room and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | An officer read the year's measures in a clean voice wearing a temple's clothes and announced a whole return for somebody not in the yard with a name the household would not repeat, Berr Clay wiped his thumb at the edge of the reading with his red cord folded under his coat and went off with his box still locked, and the boy set the silence after the name among the gaps while the Court's room came to him only as a voice through a doorway with a bench, a pot, a draught and a missing line, which he laid beside three other rooms without writing a cause.
Day 27 | the Long Night, Y.H. 812 | the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | With no fire relit after dusk the house sat in the dark telling of the Return --- a thin child who knew warmth before a face, a man returned with somebody else's hands, Alda Reed's dates standing where wonder should have stood --- while the boy counted the gaps between voices and kept a told bell of four, four and a gap beside but never on the heard bell, cold arriving at cheek, temple and wrist with neither breast nor crust given, Tarn holding his seventh year back and Jorra saying nothing of the back room.
Day 28 | the Remembering, Y.H. 812 | the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Names went from door to door along the yard but not one under the Vail roof, where Sella went around Garl's unspoken name as around ash left in a corner and the boy filed the silence where it should stand with the other gaps, Alda Reed laid birth-slates on the bench, touched her tin unopened, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower without knowing it and covered the sign with a date while in the old register a hand found wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain and the name went unlooked-at, the charred slate and the two crusts staying where they lay.
Day 29 | the Last Day, Y.H. 812 | the Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard and a neighbour's house two doors down, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Bread was baked with no third crust for the fold, Mira Silt came up from the eels to answer a question with a question and her own answer, touched the grey cord under her apron at a figure and showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it without any knot counted, and Sella would not let the ash be swept early, laid one thumb into the Vail ridge and the same thumb into the neighbour's a finger's width thicker, left both turning their corners unbroken, and banked the coals without touching the mark.
Day 30 | Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday) | the Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The grey left where it lay was burned before light and called gone and dealt with while the coals stayed banked and the mark untouched for want of an opening, Slate Row was spoken of as a direction with one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the true deduction unspoken, work turned toward the thaw and the slip pits while the Ilm held under its ice, and the thirty-day-old boy carried his count --- five hearings from four sources and no sixth, the empty fifth, the unspoken name, five pauses unjoined --- into a year the district had already entered, his hand going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

Day 31 | Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813 (Secondday) | the yard well, the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | A woman let down a bucket and said the water tasted different while a man come up from the Ilm said the ice was honeyed under the mud and nobody said the word for it, the oiled paper sagged and the Slip Brook lost its stiff skin behind the racks while a warm spell became the weather, and the thirty-one-day-old boy in the basket kept the shape of what a district could taste and would not name while Mira Silt answered a question with a question and put her thumb to a grey cord with a second twisted twice beneath it, neither counted, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the bucket and came down.
Day 32 | Ilmen 3, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday) | the Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The boy was a month old and nothing marked it while Tarn set the small knife on its sill and told the yard it could be checked with six years of seven counted and the seventh held back, agreed with everything so thoroughly that no argument could stand and wanted the late thaw because a late thaw puts off the paying, Sella fed him gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish and said the house where another woman would say I, his broom checked half a stroke at the place where no note had come and Ash came up the colour of the clean dark line when he scraped his knuckles, the proverb went to the pot and the coals were banked without a touch of the mark.
Day 33 | Ilmen 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday) | the Vail wheel-shed, the drying racks and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Jorra Vail came in from the back room with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases past his bent little finger and said nothing about where he had been, told a pot it wanted thinner slip and the wheel it did not care whose hands only if they were steady and went to the damper before Sella had finished, the men whose names are not spoken stayed unnamed and the space under the rack where whole slate should have stood went around by everybody and entered by nobody while the boy kept grey-dust forearms and clean-slip forearms on separate trays and a pot set on the bench was filed with the objects that were not hearings, the count still five from four sources.
Day 34 | Ilmen 5, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday) | the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Alda Reed came smelling of soap and warm milk and laid the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold, gave a date where a wonder should have stood, touched the tin once inside her bodice without opening it, crossed two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing it and covered the sign with the same unasked-for date before going on with dates after the question had stopped, wrote a fourth hidden note in the second hand and turned it face-down toward a floorboard the boy could not name, and said warm and breathing, which was exact and not true, while three doors down a cough was called weather.
Day 35 | Ilmen 6, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday) | the drying racks, the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | A pot set down out of a woman's shadow answered nearly full under nobody's knuckle with a thumb's breadth of air under the note and a want in it, the first earthly answer to the interval carried since Stone 13, filed on its own tray advanced and not paid and not added to the five hearings from four sources, Tarn's head went a degree toward it and he swept on without naming it, Jorra told the pot it would do and moved the damper early, Sella touched the widow's mark before she opened the kiln and at no other hour, and the boy kept the missing breath, the empty fifth in the carried firelight and the nearly full note in three separate places with a hand outside the wool that went up an inch and came down.
Day 36 | Ilmen 7, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday) | the yard well, the edge of the Ilm and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The ice showed a hairline running in and stopping a thumb's breadth short of the edge, carried into the thaw from the shard held to tallow on Stone 19 and set beside the missing step at the Fishstairs and the told bell in three places, a man came back with a figure in his fist and thought better of giving it so the number went around as a shape nobody gave out, Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, had a number he did not care to give and went off toward High Kiln with the box in his house still locked while the Singing Sill's low note and the thaw itself were called weather, one in twelve stood printed and unchallenged, Tarn agreed with both sides and kept his seventh year held back, and at dusk the coals went under blind ash without a touch of the mark.
Day 37 | Ilmen 8, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday) | House Silt three doors down, the Slip Brook and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella set the basket in the shade of the nineteen steps where Dessa Silt stood with her shoulder against the jamb, slip-grey nails and wet clay at her hem, her cough worse than on Stone 28 and still called weather, put nineteen out and nineteen back into her thumb, counted four loaves on the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve with three to the levy and bits to the woman with a basket, drew her thumb over the sieve-wire scar and did not look into the basket or say the number or the name, Mira passed her sister without looking and brought no crust, the Slip Brook ran clay-heavy a little higher than it should, and when Dessa's thumb went to the wicker for one breath an old hand in a grey sleeve reached for a pocket and found wool with a boat on cold water unsaid, the boy carried up the yard at dusk thirty-seven days old with the two crusts in the fold and his hand going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.
Day 38 | Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday) | the Slip Brook behind the drying racks, the Fishstairs and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The clay-heavy rise came up in the night and took Dessa Silt from the end of nineteen steps, and the yard lifted her out with their hands and no words while the thirty-eight-day-old boy, held on the bank, heard the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through --- the sixth hearing of the full note and the second from a person, beside the five from four sources and not among them --- and kept the paid shape of her thumb on the wicker, Alda Reed coming after the vessel had given to reach for her slate before the body and lay dates where a wonder should have stood, a soft-cuffed clerk set at the Fishstairs for a measure nobody had called for, and the two crusts waiting in the fold at dusk.
Day 39 | Ilmen 10, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday) | the Cross, the moot-post's shade and Slate Row, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Account-day resumed over a woman uncollected in the brook, Berr Clay wiping his thumb twice and reading one in twelve as the notice says before taking himself out of his own sentence while the true subtraction went over the basket, the number for the thaw passing from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and never arriving, a soft-cuffed clerk's hand going to the top slate before his eyes went to the woman waiting, and the boy keeping his exact count of four ways out of the square and the bell's four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the fold put right at his ear without a look at his face.
Day 40 | Ilmen 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday) | the Vail kiln-house, the drying racks and the yard well, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody found a word for the omission, so warmth could be left where it fell and the morning came anyway while the bucket went round without a slate, Sella measured the clean dark line where the Ash turned against the death-house ridge by a thumb and left both where they lay, Tarn set the knife on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back and swept the road to a collector without turning because a late thaw puts off the paying, Jorra's bone-coloured forearms went to the damper ahead of his hand, and Sella fed the boy barley gruel from the house's own dish and touched neither crust.
Day 41 | Ilmen 12, Y.H. 813 (Secondday) | the Fishstairs and the Slip Brook behind the drying racks, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Mira Silt came up from the eels with pale slime on her sleeves and her head held to the right where the top of her left ear had been, answered a question with her own answer, put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was spoken and showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it with neither knot counted nor door lifted, brought no third crust and did not look into the basket, while the weir women's talk of the blessing and its number ended in weather and the Slip Brook ran clay-heavy behind the racks, kept with the honeyed ice and the hairline in three places.
Day 42 | Ilmen 13, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday) | House Silt three doors down and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Sella carried a clay jar three doors down and set the basket just inside a door standing a finger's width open, where the boy saw from the height at which shelves are highest a round pewter tin above the empty bed with a pale fold at its seam and a thumb's bright rub on the lid and entered it nowhere, nobody said the woman's name or the number in her thumb, and Sella opened no slate and turned no fetching of a jar into a walk to a bank, setting the jar on the shelf by the wall at home.
Day 43 | Ilmen 14, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday) | the Cross, the river road and the Weir, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | Preparations for the Fish Blessing went upriver with eels salted and traps lifted and Orra's knot-accounts kept in the Eel House, a woman with green at one corner of her mouth looked at pots across the market and was gone unnamed, Orra Weir came onstage rope-handed and eel-scarred telling of a collector's boat with four oars and giving a date where an answer should stand, Alda touched her unopened tin beside her and neither spoke of the third day, the Ilm ran higher under a thinning skin called weather, and the weak board in the plank walk and the Singing Sill's early note were filed as gaps in a sixth room.
Day 44 | Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday) | the Weir, the Eel House and the blessing stone, and the Vail kiln-house at dusk, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath | The hearth-maids blessed the traps on the flat stone while the eels ran as they always ran, Orra tied a forty-first knot she could not account for over the story of a small warm covered bundle with a flower carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower and said the word forty low into the basket and watched, while the forty-four-day-old boy kept the shape of the knot and the word on separate trays with Mira's two cords still unread, the traps came up full, the river rose under its thinning skin and was called weather, and at dusk he was carried home with both crusts still in the fold and the fire behind him keeping four, four and a gap.
