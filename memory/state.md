# State of things — at the threshold of chapter 1 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing scene 1. It is the moment before Day 1 begins: late on Stone 6, Y.H. 812, in Low Kiln, and — in the other world — 2:17 in the morning of a February night in Chicago, at the instant the fire takes the minivan. The two are the same instant seen from two sides of a crack. Nothing has been written yet.*

---

## The moment

The hinge. A man dies giving: he has pulled a child from under the dashboard and the warmth goes out of him into that child in an act of giving, so it does not cool into a bank. It stays hot, tangled with an unpaid promise, and becomes a trace that crosses. In the same breath a woman in a grey coat at the edge of flare light writes a measure on a slate, and writes a name — **Corin** — before she has seen the face it belongs to. In Low Kiln, a woman in labour has been carrying that face for nine months and has not yet borne it.

Day count: **Day 1 is about to begin.** It will be Stone 7, Y.H. 812, a Seventhday, with the Ilm under ice. No day has been lived in the story yet; the timeline file holds only its heading.

---

## Place

- **Sarn:** Grath, the town of \place{Sill}, the poor quarter of \place{Low Kiln} on the south bank below the sill. The room is a two-room clay-daubed house off the Clay Yard, floor of trodden ash, a grey ridge along the skirting board that nobody will sweep on the Last Day. A stone has been warmed at the Vail hearth and wrapped for a newborn's belly. The Ilm lies under ice; the Five Cold Days are three days away.
- **Earth:** the northbound expressway near the split in \place{Chicago}, in a blizzard, sodium lamps making orange tunnels of the snow. The wipers have stopped. A minivan is burning.

---

## The protagonist's condition

**As Daniel Marchetti, dead.** Forty years old, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County. Large, gone soft in the middle, forearms like a butcher's, a left knee that clicks in the cold, short steady hands with a pale line where a wedding ring had been. He died at 2:17 a.m. in February 2024, going back to the minivan for the last time. The last things he knew, orderless: the hiss of fuel, orange light on snow, the vibrating weight of his phone in his coat with a name on the lit screen he would not look at, the taste of metal, heat arriving from every direction, the cold leaving his hands, and a wordless sentence that was nevertheless complete — *I have left the job unfinished.* His warmth left him in giving and stayed hot. His memories are intact. He is portable because of a debt, not a miracle.

**As Corin, about to be born.** A cracked, low-capacity infant vessel, unranked — below Thin. Twelve-to-fifteen-ember capacity at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. Across ribs and down the left forearm runs a branching red mark like a frost-flower or a burn map: the fire writing itself into the trace. He will drool; he cannot hold up his head; he cannot turn over; he cannot reach a cup. The surgeon's hands are small soft wrists with milky nails. The clicking knee is a soft unformed joint that will click in time. A foundling with no house-name, because the collector wrote only the given name on the birth-slate. No one in the room knows there is a forty-year-old man listening.

**Powers at the threshold.** None that anyone can name. He has a **clinical ear**: he will hear the full note and the flat before anyone taps, because he spent twenty years finding the thin place under the skin. He knows the afterdrop when he sees it. He counts pulses and nails and exits without deciding to. These are habits, not gifts, and he cannot explain them in Grathic. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read a ledger, cannot bargain for food.

**Wounds he carries.** The missed call to Nina (a boat on Lake Michigan, nine years promised, never bought — the weight of it made him portable). Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution called it a systems failure. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle and nobody correcting it. Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one, Ostrowski's full name, Vesci's warm *butcher*. He does not know Sarn's word for any of it. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate.

## Who is where

### In Low Kiln, Sill
- **Dessa Silt**, 24, thin and leaking, in labour in the room off the Clay Yard, counting nineteen steps under her breath, rubbing the pale scar on her forearm from the broken sieve-wire. She believes the child she is carrying will be taken in the levy. She does not know Sella Vail is already waiting to take him. She will die on Day 38, uncollected, in the Slip Brook.
- **Mira Silt**, 27, her sister, in the room, eel slime dried on her sleeves, eel-bones in her pocket, the smile kept on her face like a door on the latch. She knows more than she will say and has not said it for three years — except that the child is not yet born, so she knows only the shape of what is coming. She has a knot for a thin child in Nine Pots on a separate cord and has never shown it to anyone.
- **Alda Reed**, 42, the hearth-maid, arriving with a warmed stone in one hand and a birth-slate in the other, the tin box under her floorboard at home containing Y.H. 651's second notes. She will cross herself at the flower and tell nobody what she saw.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire, at the kiln-house in the Clay Yard, touching the widow's mark at her throat before she opens the kiln. Her husband Garl has been uncollected for a year; her daughter Enna died of the second chill; the Ash ridge in her room is the thickest in Low Kiln. She intends to take the child on the third day and has already decided, without knowing why, not to call a collector for anyone in her house.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16, at the wheel, which he took at thirteen when Garl died. He has broken three birth-slates and buried the pieces under the drying rack, and has told nobody. He has not decided whether he is with the Broken Slates. He will say *he's not Vail* when the boy arrives.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand, six years into a seven-year term, asleep in the Debt Loft above the wheel-shed with his few things in an unmarked clay pot and the small bench knife he has kept for six years in his pocket. He can hear the full note of a pot and has told nobody. He turned back at the frozen ford when sent for a collector for Garl and has carried the unsaid sentence for two years.

### Elsewhere in Sill and Grath
- **Berr Clay**, 48, Thane of Clay, at home in High Kiln, the red wool cord folded in a drawer. He has already signed the slate that takes forty children of Low Kiln for a private account, has deliberately forgotten the purchaser's name, and keeps the true deduction (one in three) in a locked box he has never destroyed.
- **Collector Marra**, not yet in Sill. She is at the edge of the flare light in Chicago, in a coat the colour of wet ash, writing. Her fingertips will go grey between Y.H. 803 and now; she will say it is cold rooms. She has forged the assessment for a whole return with the deduction entered as zero and has been paying for it ever since with her own fingers, booked nowhere.
- **Orra Weir**, 34, at the Weir one league upriver, in the Eel House, with a cord of forty knots and, not yet tied, a forty-first. Her son Kess is one of the Forty. She cannot spell the name on the collector's slate but remembers its shape.
- **Rusk of the Weir**, 70, walking the traps at dusk in a coat lined with ice-mouse fur. He is Unbanked, has been since Y.H. 675, and has known the name on the slate for a year. In the locked drawer in his room above the Fishstairs: Haldre's *The Sealed Vessel*, nine names with eight struck out, and a rubbing of a branching red mark from Y.H. 588. He has not yet seen the boy and has already decided what to make of him.
- **A Waiting Room clerk** in Slate Row whose name is on no roll takes small secrets from children and pays in loaves and ale. Ulda will sell him the boy's ear in a few years and, later, the mark.

### The wider world, asleep or waking
- **Greybank** (18 leagues east): **Veno Brine** in the counting house holds a damp copy of the slate for a whole return and cannot spell the purchaser's name. He has kept it ten years. **Solene Ember**, kept in a room with good light, removes a ring one at a time and does not know the boy exists.
- **The Hearthseat** (150 leagues south-east): **Steward Derren** has authorised a private whole return of forty unspent measures without recording the purchaser. In the sealed file he has written: *The instrument is in place. The account is carried.* **Provost Wessen** has a slate corrected in a second hand in his second file and has entered nothing about it in any report. **Rennick**, 26, posted to Sill in Y.H. 810, is not yet in the town but is already asking why forty unspent measures are needed for one intact one.
- **Keld** (120 leagues west-north-west): **Hald Iron** reads Haldre's paper on the Last Day from a tin lined with warm-stone. **Siv of the First Seam** keeps the crossed-out Y.H. 588 survey and the shape of the mark on the back of his tally. **The Vorr** sit against the Listening Stones and will say only that the numbers are correct.
- **Vhalen** (80 leagues north-east): **Pia of the Quay**, 14, does not yet know the boy. In ten years she will sell him a herring twice and keep the question on a slate-rubbing in a dry place.
- **The Waste** lies beyond the Rim, taking what nobody keeps. The Ash in the Vail corner is the shape of one unkept promise already made in that house.

### On Earth
- **Nina Marchetti**, grown, lives a life organised around not waiting. She called her father on the night of the crash; the missed call is on her phone still and she has never deleted it. She will never learn that the warmth that crossed was not hers, and that the child in the minivan — not she — carries the last of it.
- **Deshawn Carter** took the 3 a.m. call and laughed for exactly one minute before he put the radio down, because that is what the dispatcher needed. He has not yet told Nina her father was working late; he will, and it will be the last lie anyone tells her about him.
- **Lena Ostrowski** is retired. She has a photograph of Dan as a junior and a little book of every patient she lost, with Tasha Broussard's name in Dan's own handwriting. She will send a card that arrives late and say Daniel was a good surgeon.
- **Paul Vesci** practises privately in Evanston in a room with good light, keeps a file on Dan's career with one page about Tasha's chart, and has forgotten the unkind thing he said about Nina.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** Exactly how a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, the small evidence a body carries, and triage — the ninety-second sorting of the wounded into those who will live, those who will live only if he chooses them, and those who will die whatever he does. He knows the smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, the feel of snow going up a sleeve, the orange of streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is somehow in a baby's body. He knows the stone at his belly is losing heat at a rate he could almost measure.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago. He does not know that the way home is real, or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the doctrine he is being taught is a kind of accounting; that the midwives' second chill has a second cause; that the household's silences are arranged around something; that he is being watched. Suspicion, not knowledge — never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the threshold

- **Nina** — love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. Reached only as a lit phone and a photograph.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** — the dead life's voices: the laugh he cannot reach, the full name he hears at every decision, the warm voice that gave his institution the sentence it needed. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* is the grammar the Warm Court will speak in.
- **Sella** — the woman who will keep him and never ask where his memory came from. Kindness as a wall.
- **Dessa** — his mother, three doors away, who believes him dead and will die believing it.
- **Alda** — the woman who will lie to her for his safety, and who has kept a second record since Y.H. 651.
- **Jorra, Tarn** — the house's wound and its debt; both will be given a reason to resent the foundling before he can speak.
- **Marra and Rusk** — the two halves of a purchase he knows nothing about. She pays with her fingers; he pays with his silence. Both are paying for him before he takes his first breath.
