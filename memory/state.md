## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, seventy-seven days old at the close of Day 77, two months and seventeen days, carried everywhere: to the mud of the Clay Yard, the racks and the well, to the yard well again in the afternoon, to the Fishstairs and the shade under the north bridge, three doors down just inside a finger's-width-open door in House Silt, back to the well and the racks, and lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth or the water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from his wrist so the cold finds the place. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; it was not crossed in this chapter. He wants the crust, wants the breast and wants the bucket, and can reach neither: the spent crust lies stiff in the fold and the warmth he most wanted is an empty place in the fold. Dessa Silt is thirty-nine mornings dead at the close and his number for his own days is still given by nobody.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs, Alda going to her slate before the fold and then to the tin in her bodice, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the grey cord while deciding, and the man from Slate Row who handed over a loaf without a slate. This chapter added: that recovery can be another keeping-by-avoidance, a yard that sweeps around what it left as a ward walks around a bed; that the going of a note is as unnameable as its coming; that a figure carried from fist to mouth to shoulder arrives nowhere and a figure read aloud is not the figure paid; that a head can turn half a beat after the knock and the turner not know why, and that a delay after the knock is a different object from one before it; that value can pass between a man and a child with no slate in the middle, and that the shape of such a keeping is not the same as calling it a sale; that a landing a hand's breadth out of line can be walked over carefully by a whole market with nobody making a fall of it; that a woman can keep two accounts in wool, one of what was taken and one of what was kept back, in a material no officer would know to read; that three omissions of one shape --- leaving out to save, leaving out to hide, leaving the speaker out of his own account --- do not touch; that a mercy-lie told for a dying woman has become a fact in a dead woman's face that no return can alter; that the shape of a keeping done by leaving a thing out appears in a man's silence, a woman's date, a proverb said to a pot with no crack and instructions given to a fire, the wool and the bucket; that grey dust can come back after a clean day and a room can stand a finger's width open all morning without being entered; and that a seventh year held back presses toward an end where something must give. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he keeps the half-beat beside but never on his own turning; the seal of the back room beside the kiln's gap; the told bell beside the heard bell, and both beside the early note, the district's note and the kiln's four-four-and-a-gap; six like rooms in six places; the six hearings beside and never among; **three** near-full answers advanced and not paid.

## Open dangers

- The flood has come and gone and the district's habits have not changed. The yard has turned eight days of water into loaves, glances, the thaw that was and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The boy heard the note of the whole district under strain --- many small failures inside one slow certain one, with a thin place nobody would name --- and filed its going beside its coming with no word, no pointing hand and no cause written across either; he knows the anatomy of a vessel going thin and has no Grathic word for the failure in a quarter of a town. The weak board in the plank walk is gone or trodden flat under the silt, seen by nobody and made into no accident, set beside the missing Fishstairs step and the stopping hairlines in three gaps entered in no account; the Fishstairs steps have now come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off. The honeyed ice, the hairlines that stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge and the early Sill note are spoken of as the thaw that was, as though a thing said after the fact were the same as a thing said before it. The district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold; a woman wrapped it in wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing. The cold entered nowhere and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure; **a new thread must be opened for it in part 2**.
- A man described, over a bucket at the well in chapter 13, a room shut in the last war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath, their heat gone out with no way to feed it. The yard received it as history with a date; Sella, who lost a husband to the same danger, heard it and told the fire it was the thaw, and under her own roof she once sat up three nights with a child of hers who cooled while everybody was kind. The boy filed the saying on its own tray beside the told bell, the heard bell, the district's note and the kiln's gap and wrote no cause across them. Planted toward ~650 (T065). The carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the payer of the coal has gone unnamed for seventy-seven days, the coal that went up with the brine-gull on Day 45 is entered nowhere and missed in none.
- No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission; she is thirty-nine mornings gone and still unentered. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument, and strengthened by the water going around a thing the house set down and leaving it standing, and now by a yard sweeping around what the water left without entering it. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.

A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she stood at the flood's edge with her board and string and got no answer. - The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept six likenesses apart on purpose; no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work. Calling a thing over after the fact is a third grammar: a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot. Alda's date laid over the sign is a fourth: a beautiful entry standing where a true one should be, in a dead woman's face that no return can alter.
- The flat note has six hearings --- five from four sources and one from a person --- and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. Two whole vessels have given their notes past the yard door and neither he nor Tarn turned toward them, and a note that is not turned toward has entered nobody's keeping. The nearly full note now has three answers on its own tray, advanced and not paid, and is not a hearing and not the third thing. The river's own note of water going over water, its new note of a district under strain, and the falling water's drag are each fifth things on places of their own and not hearings at all. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, four, four and a gap in the square and in a story told in the dark, kept in two places; the told bell, the heard bell, the early note, the district's note and the kiln's gap stay separate. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling and the firelight, and the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter; the three exact days now lie on a tray of their own beside the three slates set edge to edge.

The word *the Breaking* has not been said in his hearing; the word forty was said once, low, is not explained, and was not repeated.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles; the exact look and sound of a vessel handing its work to the next thing until the skin holds more than it was made to hold and the face arranges itself in the ordinary way; and the anatomy of a room sealed against its own heat. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that a recovery can be a keeping-by-avoidance and a sweep around a thing is a way of not entering it; that the going of a note is as unnameable as its coming; that a figure carried from fist to mouth to shoulder arrives nowhere and a figure read aloud is not the figure paid; that a head turned after the knock is a different object from a head turned before it, and a child may not know why he turned; that value can change hands with no slate between giver and receiver, and the shape of that transaction is not yet the name of it; that a landing out of line can be used carefully by a whole market without anyone making a fall of it; that two cords of wool can hold the most important accounts in a district and no officer would know to look under an apron; that three omissions of one shape --- to save, to hide, to leave the speaker out --- do not touch; that a mercy-lie laid over a sign becomes a fact in a dead woman's face that no living person can alter; that a keeping done by leaving a thing out appears in a man's silence, a woman's date, a proverb said to a pot with no crack and an instruction given to the fire; that dust can return after a clean day and a room can stand a finger's width open all morning without being entered; and that a seventh year held back presses toward an end where something must give. He knows Tarn's hearing and his own are the same object held from two sides, and Jorra's silence a third of the same shape, and that a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day count. He knows the order of reaching and has now seen it in eight hands, and keeps each on its own place because a likeness is not a person.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote six times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the notes go, what goes on in the back room whose door stands a finger's width open, who the two men were who came in since first light on Day 77, why grey slate dust gets worked past Jorra's little finger on some mornings and not others, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know why no collector came for the woman in the brook or that the omission will one day have a name. He has heard the word forty spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and does not know that it is the number of children who paid for him or that he is one of them, nor what the forty-first knot counts, nor that Orra tied it the night a woman carried him up the Clay Yard with a flower on the bundle. He can read the shape of the knot but not its name and has no word to spend it on. He does not know who rubbed the pewter lid bright, who took the crust from the fold, who lifted the stone from the wool or who pays for the coal, and does not know that the man from Slate Row is a Waiting Room clerk or that the warm loaf was payment for anything at all. He does not know why Ulda turned his head toward a pot, or that the child will one day sell his ear and then his mark.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf, the space between a woman's thumb and her scar, a word said low and watched, an empty place in the fold, a slack frame, a silence where an argument used to stand, a coal gone up with a bird, a stone gone from a fold, a board gone under silt, a space under a rack entered by nobody, a finger's width of open door, or a new hairline of grey beside a left-in-place line; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dusts, the cords, the crusts, the stone, the intervals, the honeyed ice, the frost-bloom, the higher brook, the oiled paper, the knot-string, the prepared and performed sweepings, the sealed room, water-over-water, water going around a line, a foot that stays cold, a head turned half a beat late and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the gruel and the gone crust have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides, and Jorra's silence a third of the same shape; that Ulda's half-beat is a tell held for the day the sale lands, and that the warm loaf has gathered interest in an account the child cannot read; that the near-full note, now three answers, is a reply to the interval he has carried since Stone 13 and not yet the third thing; that the six like rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell and the heard bell and the early note and the district's note and the kiln's gap must be kept apart for the same reason, and so must the word on its tray and the sound in his ear, and the Greybank saying and the gap under his own roof, and the rising sheet and the falling drag, and his own turning and the child's turning a half-beat later; that a left thing and a burned thing are two ways of keeping, and a thing called gone may still be in the room as a hollow in wool or an empty place in a fold; that the three hands on the tray are three hands and not a cause; that a mercy-lie becomes a fact because nobody alive can correct it; and that the piece of the count nobody enters is the piece that would tell him who is paying.
