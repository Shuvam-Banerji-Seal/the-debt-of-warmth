## The moment

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and twelve days old at the close of Day 212 and six months and thirty-two days (two hundred and ten and six months and thirty days on the first morning, two hundred and eleven and six months and thirty-one days on the second), carried everywhere: under the Vail roof at the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle and the kindling; in the Clay Yard, at the kiln mouth, the drying racks and the yard well; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room and the Debt Loft never entered by him or the narration). ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and ten through two hundred and twelve days --- six months and thirty days through six months and thirty-two days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said twice over a body that cannot answer, now said a third time over the district and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard three times now from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon, a basket, a yard, a market, a carrying, a morning, a dusk, a reading and a slate burning in another quarter are not openings. That a cost asked as a direction goes to the pot, the house and the weather and arrives nowhere; that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it; that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost; that a keeping can be maintenance and not love; that a row of record-first hands standing long enough will call itself an account and want a payer; that a month can go out as a fire goes out, by degrees, with nobody calling the hour, and a man come into the yard at first grey would not know that it had been a month at all; that an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged; and that an inch held to a breath counted in another room is a coordination and not a power, an answer and not a sign, worth nothing even to the person counting it.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with six answers from Day 210 through the small hours of Day 212, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told twice on Days 205 and 208, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling, advanced and not paid, not retold in this chapter. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord tied once low with teeth and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit and the breath held under the board on Day 166, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** the pan's gone ring and its empty round place, held with no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and twelve days old, or six months and thirty-two days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and shut her hand half over nothing on each of these three days, and her little-door burn closing on air. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, and what went on in the space between the two silences under one roof. What the stone is that Tarn took out of its pot and set on the sill in the light, or why he put it back. What ails a glaze-room hand, and what the man carried out on Day 188 cost, and whether he lived or died after he went up the road breathing --- and the narration has not invented an ending for him; none of the three readings gave a figure, name or cause for him. Why his own hand can hold an inch to a rhythm nobody counts, can keep an inch to the river's one rhythm with no count at all, can hold an inch through a whole breath counted at a fire in a room that does not touch his, and can also come down before a counted breath or at once under those same conditions; he has made no rule out of any of the answers and no payer stands to any of them. Why Sella sets the pot handle two inches beyond his hand with a half-beat at the setting and never looks toward the bed, and whether the half-beat is a measuring of something. What the widow's mark mended, or why it was touched once before a kiln and never again; whether the keeping of Garl in objects is love or only maintenance, and he will not make a lesson of either. When Grain 10 will come and what the district's arithmetic will be then, and no figure under the roof is counting toward it on anything a slate could take.

## What Chapter 54 must not forget

Chapter 54 opens on Day 213, Grain 4, Fourthday, with the count exact and unspent at **two hundred twelve**, six months and thirty-two days at the close of Day 212 and six months and thirty-three days on the morning of Day 213, spoken by nobody, the fire's fifth missing with no payer and unpaid for two hundred and thirteen days, and the firing thinned into the ordinary tending of a year begun with no boundary drawn under Ember and neither of the district's two month-names corrected; Grain 1 through 3 were lived in full and are not revisited. **It is a first working week of Grain with no market and no Account-day --- the first of Grain waits on Day 219 (Grain 10).** Let a small non-fatal edge gather rather than pay: a wrong-wind saying that a pot which sang early last month has not been heard from since, a saying that stays a saying, with no lesson made and no pot lost. Keep the burned-slate word as a direction arrived nowhere, told twice on Days 205 and 208, no owner, room, flame or cause named, no third telling of the shut counting room and no joining to Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three; Jorra's silence deepened, his promise to name the boy ungiven and \emph{he's not Vail} sealed. Keep the household's keeping of Garl --- the salt coat, the hollow seat, the clean dark line and finer grey called the thaw unswept, the proverb gone to the pot and the uncalled tile debt --- with no lesson made, no collector called and the dead man's name unspoken. Keep the **one opening** behind the household, the mark touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, market, morning, afternoon or dusk since --- now no opening in thirty-three days on the morning of Grain 4. Keep the **nine** stoppings as objects with no tenth before the seventh year is earned, the seventh now at its very end and still not given, the word behind Tarn's teeth, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, the ear-sentence short of the word that would call it, six hearings beside and never among with the sixth from a person and no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and unpaid, any note out of season filed as a danger and not promoted into a hearing. Keep the **three** held changes on three places (the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 beat and the Tallow 22 half-check), the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth with six answers, the last a whole counted breath at Sella's fire in the room that does not touch his --- an object and not a power, asked again as the days ask and held or refused as the body answers, the asking not always answered and no failure in a miss and no triumph in an answer. Keep the charcoal unread and Sella's finger on top of the shelf's front edge, the warm stone cold in its unmarked loft pot, the pan's gone ring, the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its empty place and the field's empty place on trays that do not touch --- no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawn across them. Keep Berr's locked box, Hessa's unread slate and the soft-cuffed clerk's inward measure offstage in the file, not opened and not reaching back. Keep the sixth green a colour, the foundling a shape, the six hearings six, the two tellings two and the two shut-room tellings two, with no loaf, no slate, no sale, no seventh green and no seventh sighting before Grain 10. No payer named, no collector called, no ending.
