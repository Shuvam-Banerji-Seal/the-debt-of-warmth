## The moment

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and thirty-three days old at the close of Day 233 and six months and fifty-three days (two hundred and thirty-one and six months and fifty-one days on the first morning, two hundred and thirty-two and six months and fifty-two days on the second), carried everywhere: under the Vail roof at the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle and the kindling; in the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the returning line; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the worked strips' edge; up and down the clay road at the going of the light. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and thirty-one through two hundred and thirty-three days --- six months and fifty-one days through six months and fifty-three days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.
