## The moment

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and eighty-two days old at the close of Day 182 and six months and two days (one hundred and eighty and six months exactly on the first morning), carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the bare trough; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth, the kindling and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room never entered by him or the narration); up and down the clay road at the going of the light. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and was left unwiped on all three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down. Nothing within two paces can be reached --- not the crust two inches from his ear, not the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, not the frozen thread at his mouth. He turned his head toward the pot that gave its note too early on Day 182 and got no further, having no knuckle to set against it and no word to tell anybody what he had heard. The wanting is small, immediate and animal and louder than the man who knows the order in which a body takes heat back: the small joint first and the core last, the almost-smile that cold puts on a face and that is a grimace and not peace. Both are true. The body is louder.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. This chapter added: that a month turns without a boundary being drawn and the naming of the turn belongs to the yard and not to any slate; that an opening is a thing done before the kiln and not at a shelf, a well, an afternoon or a dusk, and a hand that will not name what it mends has still mended it; that a thing with two uses is not therefore two things and not therefore a doctrine; that slate dust ground small as meal is parted from the slip of the wheel as a clot is parted from the vessel that holds it, one the material of a room and one the material of the work; that an order without a name can be laid out like a field and cannot be carried into a chart, because a chart wants a name and a payer under it; that a note out of season is a fault in the handling and not a fourth likeness waiting to be promoted into a hearing; that a finding held back is still a finding and a chart closed over it with the neat margin showing will be believed when a clean hand carries it into a room; that two silences standing side by side under one roof will call themselves an equality if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer; that a holding remembered is not a holding made again; that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry; and that the figure read is not the figure paid. None may be brought forward or made into a quest in Chapter 44.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and eighty through one hundred and eighty-two days --- six months exactly through six months and two days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it, and nobody to give it to. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon and a dusk are not openings; that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back, that two records made by two hands are not one account, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, that a hand kept away from the board and a hand held beneath it for a counted breath are two states and not one, that a breath held past its time costs the shoulders and the sternum and a tremor that everybody calls cold, that an order with no name can be laid out like a field and cannot be carried into a chart, and that three counts in three places are not one account.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause (T097 advanced, unpaid). **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, tied once low with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit, and on Day 166 the finger held under the board for one counted breath, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look; on all three days of this chapter the finger stayed away and the second state was kept apart from the first. **T058/T079 advanced, not paid:** the widow's mark touched once before the kiln on Day 180, held one breath, and not touched at any other hour; the one opening done, unpraised and unseen in its mending. **T062/T061/T082 carried and advanced, unpaid:** three held changes --- the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 held beat beneath the loaf shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- on three places touching neither, three held changes and not a pair, still less a doctrine, with no payer named and no word spoken over any of them; six hearings beside and never among with the sixth from a person and no seventh; three near-full answers on their own tray; and an order with no name filed as a danger and not promoted into a hearing. **T077 carried:** a cost asked as a direction and answered with a direction, leaving no entry.

- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and eighty-two days old, or six months and two days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it on each of these three days, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing. Who took the pan that stood out to dry through Reed, when, why, or whether the not-saying would have been the same had a woman set it back --- the round ring is all that is left and the house went around it again at the going of the light. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath** and on all three days of this chapter kept it away, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144, 147, 151, 157, 160 and 166 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 152, 159, 163, 164, 165, 166, 167, 168, 169, 170, 171, 172, 177, 179 and 180 through 182. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, and what went on in the space between the finger standing in the iron and Tarn's half-beat on the flagstones; what the boy's ear heard in the pot that sang too early and why the woman who set it down did not hear it. What the widow's mark mends, or why a hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, and whether touching it has ever changed anything under the roof; why a fire keeps four pulls and misses a fifth and who the payer would have been for a hundred and eighty-two days. What the figure read at the Cross would have cost if it had been paid, who would have paid it, and whether the hand that went in on Seed 15 ever came out of the field; what Berr believes he did as a kindness is a kindness to the man who received the slate or only to the man who wrote it. Why a runner would not read what she carries, why a clerk enters a figure before he looks at the man who gave it, why a cord is tied with a double turn and a knot tied when a woman lies, why a loaf went into a pocket that held no slate and was never sold; he keeps each on its own tray and has made no cause of any. Why the surgeon's register, which reads an order and an opening and a gap more exactly than anyone under this roof, belongs to a body that cannot lift a hand to its own chin. Who the soft-cuffed clerk is, who the man from Slate Row is, who the foundling's green is joined to, and whether the two shut-room tellings are the same telling carried by two winds; he has not joined them, named a payer to any of them or written an account at the bottom of them.

## What chapter 44 must not forget

Chapter 44 opens on Day 183, Ember 4, Fourthday, with the boy one hundred and eighty-three days old, the count crossing into the second working week of the kiln month unchanged, the carried fire still answering four and missing its fifth, and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf. Do not recap this close. The district keeps calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and neither is corrected in narration. Keep the count exact and unspent at **one hundred and eighty-two** and the fire's payer unnamed; the box gone up the clay road locked, the printed one in twelve unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid; Hessa's slate delivered unread to Greybank; the clerk still set at the landing for his uncalled measure. Keep the **one opening** behind the household --- the mark touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, and at no other hour --- and do not touch it again unless there is another opening; nobody praised it and nobody saw what it mended, and the fire still missed its fifth. Keep the nine stoppings as objects with no tenth before the seventh year is earned, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, no seventh hearing invented, six hearings beside and never among with the sixth from a person, three near-full answers on their own tray, and any note out of season filed as a danger and not promoted into a hearing. Keep the **three** held changes on three separate places --- the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 held beat beneath the shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- and never let them stand up as a pair, a doctrine or a rule with a payer; do not repeat any as a new change, and remember that Jorra's holding on Day 182 was a remembered repetition and not a fourth. Keep the two shut-room tellings with no third, the told bell beside the heard, Sella's glance at the clay jar without a touch, the charcoal unread, the crust's empty place unnamed, the two shelf gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place on separate trays with no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawing the line. Keep the sixth green a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale; keep Marra out of Sill, Dessa dead and unnamed, Alda's mercy-lie uncorrected, *he's not Vail* sealed and the foundling's name promise ungiven. Do not call the loan, give or cut the seventh year, spend the Seed 16 breath, name a payer to any keeping, open a tin, box or slate, read a private record, bring a third gap, spend the sixth glimpse, lose a pot or make the absences touch. The first Account-day of Ember is Ember 10 (Day 189), which belongs to Chapter 46; no bell, Cross or market before then. Keep the old life offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break, no departure from the boy's eyes. Forward, not closed.
