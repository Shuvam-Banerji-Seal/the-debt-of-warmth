## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and ninety-one days old at the close of Day 191 and six months and eleven days (one hundred and eighty-nine and six months and nine days on the first morning, one hundred and ninety and six months and ten days on the second), carried everywhere: at the Cross in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with cold iron at his cheek through the reading and the bell; at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the bare trough; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth, the kindling and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room and the Debt Loft never entered by him or the narration); up and down the clay road at the going of the light. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and was left unwiped on all three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He knows the afterdrop the way he once knew the back of his own hand: the core still giving its heat to the shell while shallow vessels open and take the warmth from the places that needed it; a body handed from dark into a warm room goes on getting colder in the hands of people being kind to it.

## Open dangers

- **The month's danger, paid small and non-fatally.** The kilns stand at their heat into Ember's second working week and the danger keeps an order and no name and cannot be carried into a chart: hot clay under tallow, fine grey slate dust ground small as meal under the nails and in the white of a knuckle when a fist opens, the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} on a wrong wind off \place{High Kiln}, and a pot set down wrong giving its high thin note before its time. The boy has turned toward the note and filed each sound where a danger goes and not among the near-full answers, because a note out of season is a fault in the handling and not a fourth likeness waiting to be promoted. The danger was paid in one particular physical edge on Day 188: a rack-worker's pot gave its note before its time and then sang itself out early, the woman making no lesson of it, and two men carried the glaze-room hand out of the kiln and across the yard toward the clay road, alive and breathing, the sweet-sickness on his breath and the pale line at his gum, one man's thumb hooked under his arm. On Day 191 the sweet edge came up again on a wrong wind and went out on the same wind, spoken of as a direction and answered with the pot and the weather; no second edge was paid and no High Kiln saying was made true as a lesson. **The cost stays entered nowhere (T077 advanced, not paid as a thread; T061/T082 advanced). ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and eighty-nine through one hundred and ninety-one days --- six months and nine days through six months and eleven days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it, and nobody to give it to. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- read in the surgeon's register, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces because there is no hand under fifteen inches to carry it. The shape of a reading said twice over a body that cannot answer, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than *I*. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon, a basket, a yard, a market, a carrying and a dusk are not openings; that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the man carried out of the yard, that two records made by two hands are not one account, that a heard bell need not answer for a told bell, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, that a hand under a board for one counted breath is a thing done and a shelf passed at dusk with the hands full is a thing not done and the two do not become one because they stand in the same room, that a keeping looked at and put back is not a payment and not forgiveness, that dust ground small as meal is parted from the slip as a clot from its vessel, that one piece of iron with two bites is not therefore two things and not therefore a doctrine, that an order without a name can be laid out like a field and cannot be carried into a chart, that a saying said aloud is not made true by the event it seems to predict, and that an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; Why the debt against a firing of tiles is never spoken, and what the number would be if somebody who knew what a number counted would speak it over the basket; he has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it.
