## The moment

Day 131, Reed 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813, the going of the light into the small hours. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and thirty-one days old at the close of Day 131 and four months and eleven days, carried everywhere: at the Cross in the moot-post's shade, up and down the clay road, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle and the hearth, at the wheel-shed's edge (back room not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard and flagstones, down to the Fishstairs and the fish-trade shade, along the river road to the Weir's plank walk and blessing stone, three doors down in House Silt, and lifted onto the bed under the roof each night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached, not the crust, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out, the door's finger's width of light or any of the three counts. Cold arrives in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from the joint so the cold finds the bare place and goes under the fold at his ear. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 131 without knowing she had done it, and covered the sign with the date she gave Dessa. He is cold at the ear and wants the one brown-edged crust two inches off in the fold, stiff and past smelling of anything, and the wanting is louder than the reading and cannot be handed to anybody.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing holding his slate to his forearm, the assessor at the reading block with hand to slate before eyes to rail, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the cord while deciding, Orra pulling a knot down as she gives a date, the reed-cutter turning his cord where no slate lay, Jorra speaking to the wheel before the pots and the people, Alda laying her birth-slates on the bench before she turned to the fold, and Sella giving the instruction to the wool, pot, broom, water, fire and wind before she gives it to anybody. He has stopped expecting to be told what the number counts, which is not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's voice is the one he hears most now, and that is its own small grief.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and twenty-nine, one hundred and thirty and one hundred and thirty-one days, four months and nine, ten and eleven days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which warmth is given out and the order in which a body loses it, especially the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure spoken over a crowd and a figure written in a private place are two objects even when they are the same figure, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip or a bundle, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a cost with no figure is still a cost and every cost in Low Kiln has a payer nobody will name; that a cost asked in the form of a direction can be answered with a direction and leave no entry, and the withholding be called the weather. That a cost asked as weather is still asked, and a fist shut on two fingers is a holding as surely as a fist put back in a sleeve. That care leaves no entry either: a full dish carried level, a cup turned a quarter and set back where it stood, a market walking over a step that is not a step, the yard going around a place with a look, a hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting. That three likenesses in a row will grow a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and that the labour of holding them apart is invisible and worth nothing to anybody who could see it; that a fourth likeness makes no cause merely by standing there, and that three stoppings are three objects and not a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. That a hand washing its own cuff has a hunger or a habit and a hand held to a forearm where nobody else can see the figure has a use, and a use wants a payer; that a figure entered before the looking finds the man who spoke it is a figure the looking cannot change. That a man standing at the stern and keeping his hands off the oars counts other men's pulling. That a note turned down behind a chart tells of the hand that turned it, and that a record nobody expects to be read is the hardest to lay beside another without making the two agree. That a held word, a held breath and a finger standing in cold iron are three keepings and not one law.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the neighbours' one-breath joinings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's check on one day and his stroke going on without it on the next, set beside the bent finger, the held breath and **three** stoppings without any of them joined. The near-saying itself: a sentence that came three times to the threshold of the word and stopped three times, with no figure and no name, held as an interval. The greens: four shapes in four places --- three at a mouth (Silt 30, Seed 28, Reed 7) and Berr's at his own mouth --- with the pale cord-line on the green-mouthed woman's hand and the cup set back a quarter on places of their own, no name, no ear and no trade. The seller's single wipe after touching fish, kept off Berr's tray. The foundling's delay: after the knock, not before it, not a hearing and not a seventh, his own turning kept beside it without touching, no loaf passing and no slate opened. The told bell beside the heard bell; the two tellings of the shut counting room beside the damper's double bite with no third; the clerk's washed cuff on one place and his slate on another; Orra's date and the man at the stern on a place of their own apart from the midwife's reaching hand and the thane's twice-wiped thumb.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and thirty-one days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it for a hundred and thirty-one days. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking**, though he has now seen both the marks' place and the hand's habit. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, and that the sale is years ahead. What the reed-cutter's knots count, what the cord says beyond *No more can the Court*, who the reed-cutter is, what the word forty counts, who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over and who the man at the stern of the collector's boat was. Whether the two tellings of the shut room belong together, and what the room under the Vail roof was in the war. Whether the woman at the loaf shelf will one day lay her finger on the two gaps above, and which of the district's private records will be laid beside the charcoal first.

## Open dangers

- **The planting crossed out of its last tend and one hand did not come back with the rest; the reed work has taken its place and still has no figure.** The reeds stand in rows in the ruts where the barley stood, green at the broken ends and pale at the frost-bitten tips, cut in a going-downriver and a coming-back at the going of the light and judged by the arm that carries them; at the Weir a cutter told a girl with a creel to lift a bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it. A cost asked as a direction is answered with the pot, the weather or the river and leaves no entry. **T098:** on the first Account-day of Reed a man from the strips stood at the edge of the reading and went around the empty place in the returning line with a look and a wide step and no number; if a hand is lifted out of the ground in a month or a year a slate will be found and a figure made to stand at its foot, and if no hand comes up the space will go on being gone around and the month will go on standing short without a figure. No name, cause, body, accident, payment or payer. Carry it into Reed 13 and the second Account-day, gather interest, do not pay. The bell's four, four and a gap was **heard** at the Cross on Day 129 and nobody crossed himself; the heard bell is kept beside the told bell and must not be joined to it. The steps at the Fishstairs came back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the whole market walked over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off; on Day 130 he entered a buyer's figure before looking at the buyer. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of a death. The district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **A fourth private record is going past him in a material no Court thumb will read, and a fifth is kept under the house's own shelf.** **T099 planted on Day 122, carried on Day 125, Day 128 and again on Day 131:** a reed-cutter's tally in grey wool or reed-fibre cord, knots set at arm's length, one tied low with teeth and thumb, carried on the plank walk at the Weir and not counted. *You can ask the cord* --- *I can't read a cord* --- *No more can the Court.* It lies on a place of its own beside Mira's grey cord and the second twist beneath it, Orra's knot-string beside Mira's board, Alda's six pages in the little tin turned down toward a floorboard, both tins shut, and Dessa's pewter tin above the abandoned bed, and touches none of them. **T100 planted on Day 129, carried on Day 131:** the charcoal tally on the underside of the Vail loaf shelf, grouped strokes of different darkness kept where nobody thought to look, with Sella's finger going beneath the board without a word. Do not count a knot or a stroke, read a figure, give the reed-cutter a name, name the charcoal's count, join either to T098, open a tin or box or speak the word forty in Chapters 31–33. The private records gather toward the far-off day they will be laid together and the joining called an account; no hand in the district has yet existed to do it.
- **Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold.** A woman wound it in three turns of wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing; on Day 130 he noted that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and the reading worked and bought nothing. The cold entered nowhere and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure, carried through the lean week, the turn of Silt, four weeks of Seed and twelve days of Reed; **the threads T093/T094/T096 are carried, not paid, and no silt-leach, cause or cure may be named through Chapters 31–33.**
- **The loaf shelf keeps two unnamed gaps and the fold keeps one crust and one unnamed gap, and a new count lies beneath the board.** Two neighbours, on Seed 24 and Seed 29, named the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the boy's ear a child's appetite in one breath, laying two rooms under one cause without seeing it, and went off with their pots. The boy set their sayings on places of their own beside the shelf's two gaps and the fold's crust and its empty place, wrote no cause and did not make the joining his own. A saying of the loaf-shelves of Low Kiln now stands in the file: two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name. Beneath the board the charcoal clusters lie on a separate place, and Sella's finger has been under it twice without a word. **T097 stands advanced toward the far-off day a finger lays the shelf and the fold together and an account wants a name; T100 stands planted toward the far-off day the district's private records are laid together.** Do not pay T097 early, do not let the boy make either joining his own, do not name, join or solve the three places in Chapters 31–33, let no third gap come and no fourth likeness make a cause. A line drawn through the places would be a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand is the thing he has spent his life declining to be.
- **Below the sill's other edge, Tarn's seventh year stands one day nearer its end and the loan that can hear is still uncalled.** The knife's worn mark has six years in it and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check; to the kindling he said *Six counted. The next I don't count* and pressed the held breath to its edge without spending it, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where the breath should have gone in. He was sent for a collector once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year. His stroke goes over the collector's road without once turning and no collector comes; Jorra's silence and his keep the equality, and the stroke now goes on over the bright patch without even the half-check. **The near-saying has now come three times to the threshold of the word that would call the loan --- on Reed 5, Reed 8 and Reed 11 --- and stopped three times with no figure and no name; all three stoppings are filed as intervals and not payments.** Do not call the loan, spend the breath, count the seventh year or let a collector come in Chapters 31–33; something must still give at the end of the seventh year and has not given here. Jorra's dust, the room standing a finger's width open, the unnamed men, the space under the rack and the broken birth-slates beneath it are keepings no slate can enter; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- **On a day in the last war a room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath**, their heat gone out with no way to feed it, as told by a man over a bucket at the well in chapter 13 and by a second man come downriver from Greybank on Day 84, and only *mentioned* by a third voice at the bucket-round on Day 102, each as a dated curiosity eighteen leagues east with the same words *not the war's hand*, differing only in breath through the nose against breath made in the throat. The two tellings have sat on their own tray through the whole of Reed so far, joined to no kiln and to no third telling: Sella, who lost a husband to that danger, heard them and told the fire it was the thaw, and Jorra's bent finger went to a damper that the house calls only a holding, never a taking, and the boy read the same iron as a holding and a taking with no Grathic word for both. No third telling came on the morning of Day 130 when men went into the back room. Planted toward ~650 (T065/T095). The carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, came into the house in a clay dish before the boy was a month old, warms the boards by degrees and misses one breath in every four; the payer of the coal has gone unnamed for a hundred and thirty-one days, the coal that went up with the brine-gull on Day 45 is entered in no account and missed in none, and one warmth less has gone out of the world with no account the poorer.
- **No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission**; she is more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and still unentered, the number gone into the water in her thumb. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb. Alda's date still stands over her face and no living person can take it off.
- **The number still goes around in fists, mouths, shoulders, loaves, glances, armfuls, cords, charcoal and weather:** a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, Berr's folded cord, twice-wiped thumb and self-removal, the true subtraction unspoken, the printed figure unchallenged, the house's arithmetic with two spaces on the loaf row that nobody names, a bundle judged by the arm that carries it, a man's fist shut and put back in a sleeve, the figure entered by the clerk before he looks at the buyer, and a cord and a shelf-underside that no Court thumb can read. Sella's private roll is unshown and her finger beneath the board is unexplained; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; the reed-cutter's cord is a third such record; Alda's **six** written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the pewter tin above the empty bed holds its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened, and the little tin in Alda's bodice was touched and not opened. The soft hands of a man from Slate Row handed a figure to a slate held at the edge on Day 115 and stand as a sixth shape off the five trays; no two touched, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand in three days, and no sale. The word forty was spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and has not been spoken in three days; the double turn of the forty-first knot is legible and unnamed.
- **Orra tied a forty-first knot over the story of a small warm bundle with a flower and said the number into the basket and watched;** the cord hangs in the Eel House where he cannot enter or read it, though he can read its shape without its name: a double turn, tail tucked, tied left over right, with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in. A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she stood at the lower boards on Day 131 giving the ninth of Ilmen and the boat with six oars and the man at the stern where a figure was asked and getting nothing from the basket. The shape of the knot is on its tray beside the word and the weight of a thumb apart from both; the man at the stern who never touched the oars is on a place of his own beside the midwife's reaching hand and the thane's twice-wiped thumb.
- **One of the two crusts in the fold is gone and nobody says who took it, that it is gone, or what became of it;** the spent one lies stiff beside an empty place that has crossed twelve days into Reed without a name. The pale river stone is gone from the fold in House Silt and nobody says it is gone either; the weak board is gone under the silt and nobody makes an accident of it; the broken birth-slates are under the rack and nobody enters them; the hollow where the stone lay is left in the wool and nobody says the stone was taken. A coal went up with a brine-gull on Day 45 and one warmth less went out of the world with no slate the poorer. A kindness that could not be answered has become an absence that cannot be answered.
- **A new person is in the yard and his attention can be bought.** Ulda's head went toward a pot a half-beat after the boy's on Silt 16, Seed 19 and again on Day 121 at the market's edge; each time he stopped, rubbed the green sliver and could not say why, and twice he opened his mouth on a kiln-hand's unfinished sentence and finished nothing. No loaf passes and no slate is seen. The soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs is not him. A man from Slate Row whose name is on no roll gave him a warm loaf on Silt 16 that went into a pocket holding no slate, and that loaf goes on gathering interest in an account the child cannot read. The boy kept the delay, the sliver, the loaf, his own turning and the unfinished sentence as objects on separate places and wrote no cause across any of them. A second man with soft hands handed a figure to a slate on Day 99 and again on Day 115 and was filed apart from all of them. The shape of a sale is in the file; the sale itself is not paid. The half-beat is held for the day it lands as the physical tell of a betrayal, and the watcher's turn lies beside the shelf tellings with no finger laid across any of them. He was offstage in chapter 30.
- **A green is on its way to being a face.** On Silt 30 a silent woman stood across the square with lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth and was filed; on Seed 28 a second silent woman stood looking at pots with the same green and was filed beside her and beside Berr's green at his own mouth; on Day 126 a third silent woman at the Cross turned a cup a quarter, set it back and went off with the light, the third green at a mouth and the fourth shape with Berr's. - **Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness;** it was not touched once in three days, because a shelf, an afternoon, a strip, a rack, a well, a bucket, a road, a market, a post, a door, a basket, a bed and a dusk are not openings and no opening came. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles remains never paid, never settled, never done with; his clean dark line is not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and the new finer grey beside it is not swept either, because the house says it is thaw and will go when it goes. Sella stood over both with a broom and left them. Her grey streak at the temple, her saying that it came in the winter Garl died, her half-beat over the basket, her counting under her breath and her finger beneath the loaf shelf are carried, not explained.
- **The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful;** what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the shut counting rooms eighteen leagues east, the damper called a holding and never a taking, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and House Silt is a fifth such room set beside them;** the boy has kept all five apart on purpose, and no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work. Calling a thing over after the fact is a third grammar: a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot.
