## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and forty-nine days old at the close of Day 149 and four months and twenty-nine days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard and the edge of the worked strips, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle and the hearth, at the wheel-shed's edge (back room not entered), \lore{the damper} and the flagstones, down the river road to the Weir's plank walk, blessing stone and fish-trade shade, into the Cross and the last market in the moot-post's thin shade, and lifted onto the bed under the roof each night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze again in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached, not the crust two inches from his ear, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out (the handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand), the loaf shelf, the charcoal under it, the cord in the cutter's coat, the knot-string at Orra's waist, the second twist beneath Mira's apron, the clerk's inward slate, the pewter tin above the abandoned bed, the hollow where the pale stone lay or the little tin in a woman's bodice. He wants the crust and the breast and the count spoken by somebody who would have known what a count was, and gets none of them and cannot ask for any.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to the fold, a woman putting a finger beneath a shelf before her eyes go to the row, a cutter feeling the weight settle before he looks at the girl who asked, a man shutting his mouth on a figure before he looks at the face that asked, a clerk's hand going to a slate before his eyes go to the stalls, Orra's thumb to the cord as a sentence ends, Mira's thumb under her apron when a figure is named, Jorra's finger into the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, Berr's twice-wiped thumb passing over a slate before his eyes went to the crowd, and Sella giving the instruction to the wool, wind, pot, broom and fire before she gives it to anybody. This chapter added: that the last bundles are an ending the whole yard can see and nobody will date, while his count is an ending nobody can see and a number nobody will speak; that a cost asked in the form of a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry; that a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would; that a knowing withheld from a kiln-master is not a hearing given; that a stopping is not a hearing and a ninth stopping no more a ninth hearing; that **nine** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid and the figure that goes into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back; that a market can perform one grammar five times over --- direction for cost, date for figure, measure ahead of the call, colour where a name should stand, a half-turn stopped in its arc --- with nobody seeing it is one grammar; that six greens must not stand up and call themselves a face, and six half-turns must not stand up and call themselves a sale, because a sale is a thing with a payer at its foot. The work is invisible and buys nobody anything, and he does it because there is nothing else in the room his hands can do.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and forty-seven, one hundred and forty-eight and one hundred and forty-nine days, four months and twenty-seven, twenty-eight and twenty-nine days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure spoken over a crowd and a figure written in a private place are two objects even when they are the same figure, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip or a bundle and does not know the hand that did not come back, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost and that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a cost asked as a direction can be answered with a direction and leave no entry, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, and a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; that a knowing withheld is not a hearing given; that one iron can hold a vessel whole and take a hand off a man with no Grathic word for both; that a covering laid over a sign is not the thing covered; that a stopping is not a hearing and a ninth stopping no more a ninth hearing; that **nine** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, that a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and that a payer named to none of the nine means no doctrine has stood up; that six likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a face or a sale if no hand keeps them apart, and a sale is a thing with a payer at its foot; that a mercy-lie told for a dying woman becomes a fact no living person can correct; and that three counts in three places need not become one account.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. A fourth private record in the house itself: the charcoal under the shelf, set on its own place below the two gaps above it, and the woman's finger that goes beneath it without looking, both unread and unjoined, the reason left between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, knotted with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf and the finger-under-board habit. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** the sayings on places of their own beside the earlier tellings and the watcher's late turn, no fourth likeness and no third gap. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's stroke going on without the half-check and his chest's small interval, the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat, set beside the bent finger, the held breath and **nine** stoppings without any of them joined. The near-saying itself: a sentence brought to the threshold of the word nine times --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- and stopped nine times, with no figure and no name, held as nine intervals and not nine payments, nine objects and not a rule, not hearings and not a ninth hearing, with the seventh year one tend from its end and the loan uncalled. Also on their own places: the sixth green as a colour joined to no ear, the foundling's sixth half-turn as a shape with no sale, the damper's two bites joined to no kiln, the two shut-room tellings with no third, the told bell beside the heard, the sign and the date and the hand that made them in House Silt, the cold foot, and the figure read at the last market that was not the figure paid.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and forty-nine days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it for a hundred and forty-nine days. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking**, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144 and 147 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148 and 149. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Which house holds the floorboard under Alda's sixth page, and what the second hand says. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, whether he will ever speak, and that the sale is years ahead. What the reed-cutter's knots count, what the cord says beyond *No more can the Court*, who the reed-cutter is, what the word forty counts, who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over and who the man at the stern of the collector's boat was.
