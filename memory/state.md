# State of things — at the close of chapter 1 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 2. Day 1 has been lived, from the small hours before dawn on Stone 7 to morning breaking over the Clay Yard. Nothing has been resolved: a stone is cold, a name is written in a hand Dan cannot read, and a hand has hovered over him and gone away.*

---

## The moment

Day 1 is over but the third day has not come. The boy has been awake the whole of his first night and nobody asked him once whether he wanted to be. Three things have been done to him, none with his consent and none explained: a stone was warmed at a neighbour's fire and put at his belly and has finished giving up its heat; a name was entered on a slate before anyone looked at his face; a woman's hand came into the room, felt for the warmth and found none, hovered over the bundle, and withdrew. He was entered twice --- once in the dark hand on the slate and once in the count he keeps for himself --- and the count is the only entry in the room that belongs to nobody.

Day count: **Day 1 complete.** Day 2 (Stone 8, Eighthday) has not begun.

---

## Place

- **Sarn:** \place{Grath}, the town of \place{Sill}, the poor quarter of \place{Low Kiln} on the south bank below the sill. The birth room is one of two clay-daubed rooms of \place{House Silt}, opening off the \place{Clay Yard}: a floor of trodden ash, a board-bed under a fold of grey wool that will not stay on his shoulder, a door that will not shut, an oiled-paper window, a ridge of \lore{the Ash} the width of a thumb along the skirting board that nobody will sweep before \lore{the Last Day}, and a slate lying flat on the bench by the door with all writing turned under. A kiln breathes somewhere off the yard --- the Vail fire, heard before the house itself is seen. The \place{Ilm} lies under ice; they say at \place{the Weir} it will hold three days; \lore{the Five} are three days off.
- **Earth, in memory only:** the northbound \place{expressway} near the split in \place{Chicago}, February, 2:17 in the morning. Sodium lamps make orange tunnels in the snow; the wipers have stopped; a minivan burns with its fuel hissing and the antifreeze under it; a flare stands in the snow. This breaks through in the present tense and then is gone; it is not a place the boy can return to.

---

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, a few hours old by morning. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty years old, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into the minivan for a child pulled from under the dashboard, and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He drools. He cannot hold up his head, cannot turn over, cannot turn his head past a point without making a small animal sound, cannot reach a cup two paces off, cannot wipe his own chin. The head goes where it wants and the wool comes loose at the shoulder. Across the third rib on the left and down the forearm to just short of the wrist runs a branching red mark, deep ordinary red, sitting in the skin: \person{Alda Reed} has named it \lore{birth-flower} in front of him; he has called it a burn map. Nobody has treated it as a thing to be explained. He is hungry, cold, and furious about it, and he has been awake all night.

**Mind.** The double consciousness is staged in full. The surgeon's register reads pulses, nail beds, the grey-blue under the nails, the sieve-wire scar, the long shallow breathing, the exact shape of a hand stopping, and the rate of a stone's cooling; the infant's body wants the breast and cannot be argued with. *He knew the afterdrop and he wanted the breast; both were true and the second was louder.* The competence is intact and there is nothing behind it --- an instrument in a dead man's hand. He has come back to a room he has never been in that is arranged as though it had been waiting for him. His first small grief was not for the dead but for a pair of hands that can do nothing. He found a dry joke in that and it cost him something.

**Powers.** None usable, and no rank change. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} (spoken or written), cannot read a ledger or bargain for food. He has a clinical ear --- he will hear the full note and the flat before anyone taps --- and he counts pulses, nails, steps, exits and breaths without deciding to. These are habits of the old life, not gifts; he has never been told they were a gift. The count he keeps is four on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should be; on the kiln it is four breaths, four breaths, and a gap where the fifth should be. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it.

**Possessions and money.** The small pale river stone, colour of skimmed milk, now cold in its own right in the fold of damp grey wool at his belly; it was warmed by \person{Dessa Silt} at the Vail hearth across the yard and carried here in her apron, a favour not entered anywhere. The grey wool, which will not stay put. The flower on his ribs. A crust of brown-edged barley bread was given to Dessa by Mira from a warm house (the woodsmoke on it is the Vail kiln's) and eaten or set aside; no bread is in the child's keeping. He owns no coins and none change hands in this chapter. Costs are reckoned in other units: three of four loaves to the levy; the slip to be paid for in the thaw; the price of keeping a foundling with no house-name, which is the room's unasked question and is never answered aloud.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The call he never made to \person{Nina Marchetti} --- a boat on Lake Michigan promised for nine years, never bought; the weight of the unsent sentence made him portable. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a systems failure. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle. Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one, Ostrowski's full name, Vesci's warm *butcher*. He does not know Sarn's word for any of it. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### In the birth room and Clay Yard

- **Corin/Dan** --- on the board-bed under the fold of wool, awake, hungry, counting. Wants the breast; wants to know what the room is hiding; cannot have either.
- **Alda Reed**, 42, the hearth-maid --- in the room, steady hands, clay under her scrubbed nails, the little tin at her bodice touched twice. She crossed two fingers over the flower, caught herself, and covered it with the birth-flower word and an exact unasked-for history from Nine Pots (Y.H. 806, fourth day of Stone, a potter's third child, now a carter in Stonemouth). Her chalk column sits under the dark line on the slate; at the end she laid the slate flat so no writing showed. Wants the child warm and breathing and the account in its proper order; will lie to keep both. She is steady and afraid, and there is a second record on her person that is not the slate.
- **Dessa Silt**, 24 --- on the pallet, spent, face to the wall, her pulse up now that the counting has stopped; the sieve-wire scar on her left forearm rubbed whenever she decides how much to say. She has refused the name and calls him the little hot one and the flower: *a name's a thing a person says when they mean to keep something.* She got specific about nineteen steps, four loaves, three to the levy, the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve. Wants him kept and cannot say so; believes the levy will take him. She does not know Sella is already waiting. She will die on Day 38, uncollected, in the \place{Slip Brook}.
- **Mira Silt**, 27, her sister --- stood all night in the one patch of floor the slate did not reach, back to the writing, face to the door, the small smile on the latch. Eel slime dried on her sleeves, silt and salted perch on her, the top of her left ear missing (hook, she says; the scar says knife), an eel-bone ticking in her pocket. She said the secret is in what happens to a child after it is born, said she told nobody and there is nothing to tell, said “Cold enough to keep,” and gave the crust of bread briskly before a speech could be made. Wants him kept and will not say it. She knows more than she has said and has arranged her face to keep it. The knot for a thin child in Nine Pots is still on its separate cord and has never been shown.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- came to the doorway and went back out into the Clay Yard without entering, speaking, or looking at anyone. Woodsmoke and wet wool on her; the burn shaped like a little door, lintel and threshold, across the back of her hand. She felt for heat at the child's belly, found none, opened and shut her fingers above the bundle, and did not take him. Wants to take him on the third day --- still three days away --- and has not yet. Garl has been uncollected for a year; Enna died of the second chill; the Ash ridge in her house is the thickest in Low Kiln. She keeps the house's own roll and will not show it to a man from Slate Row. She has decided, without knowing why, not to call a collector for anyone in her house.

### Offstage but live

- **Jorra Vail**, 16, at the wheel in the kiln-house, which he took at thirteen when Garl died. He has broken three birth-slates and buried the pieces under the drying rack and told nobody. He has not decided whether he is with the Broken Slates. He will say *he's not Vail* when the boy arrives.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand, six years into a seven-year term, in the Debt Loft above the wheel-shed. He can hear the full note of a pot and has told nobody. He turned back at the frozen ford when sent for a collector for Garl and has carried the unsaid sentence two years.
- **\person{Berr Clay}**, 48, Thane of Clay, at home in High Kiln: the slate taking forty children of Low Kiln for a private account is signed, the purchaser's name deliberately forgotten, the true deduction (one in three) locked in a box he has never destroyed.
- **\person[Marra]{Collector Marra}**, still not in Sill. In Chicago at the edge of the flare light, in a coat the colour of wet ash, writing on a slate; the hand does not stop. Her fingertips will go grey between Y.H. 803 and now. She forged the assessment for a whole return with the deduction entered as zero and has been paying for it ever since with her fingers, booked nowhere. The boy has noticed that the pause in her writing matches the pause in the dark hand on the birth-slate and has not put the two together. She is not named in Sarn narration and is not called a collector in it.
- **\person{Orra Weir}**, 34, at the Weir one league upriver in the Eel House, with a cord of forty knots and, not yet tied, a forty-first. Her son Kess is one of the Forty. She cannot spell the name on the collector's slate but remembers its shape.
- **Rusk of the Weir**, 70, walking the traps at dusk in a coat lined with ice-mouse fur. Unbanked since Y.H. 675. In his locked drawer: Haldre's *The Sealed Vessel*, nine names with eight struck out, and a rubbing of a branching red mark from Y.H. 588. He has not seen the boy and has already decided what to make of him.
- A Waiting Room clerk in Slate Row whose name is on no roll takes small secrets from children and pays in loaves and ale. Ulda will sell him the boy's ear in a few years and, later, the mark.

### The wider world, asleep or waking

- **Greybank** (18 leagues east): \person{Veno Brine} in the counting house holds a damp copy of the slate for a whole return and cannot spell the purchaser's name; he has kept it ten years. \person{Solene Ember}, kept in a room with good light, removes a ring one at a time and does not know the boy exists.
- **The Hearthseat** (150 leagues south-east): Steward Derren has authorised a private whole return of forty unspent measures without recording the purchaser; in the sealed file he has written *The instrument is in place. The account is carried.* Provost Wessen has a slate corrected in a second hand in his second file and has entered nothing about it in any report. Rennick, 26, posted to Sill in Y.H. 810, is not yet in the town but is already asking why forty unspent measures are needed for one intact one.
- **Keld** (120 leagues west-north-west): Hald Iron reads Haldre's paper on the Last Day from a tin lined with warm-stone. Siv of the First Seam keeps the crossed-out Y.H. 588 survey and the shape of the mark on the back of his tally. The Vorr sit against the Listening Stones and say only that the numbers are correct.
- **Vhalen** (80 leagues north-east): Pia of the Quay, 14, does not yet know the boy. In ten years she will sell him a herring twice and keep the question on a slate-rubbing in a dry place.
- **The Waste** lies beyond the Rim, taking what nobody keeps. The Ash in the Vail corner is one unkept promise already made in that house.

### On Earth

- **Nina Marchetti**, grown, lives a life organised around not waiting. She called her father on the night of the crash; the missed call is on her phone still and she has never deleted it. She appears in this chapter only as a lit screen and a name he will not look at. She will never learn that the warmth that crossed was not hers, and that the child in the minivan --- not she --- carries the last of it.
- **Deshawn Carter** took the 3 a.m. call and laughed for exactly one minute before he put the radio down. He has not yet told Nina her father was working late.
- **Lena Ostrowski**, retired, has a photograph of Dan as a junior and a little book of every patient he lost, with Tasha Broussard's name in his own handwriting.
- **Paul Vesci**, in private practice in Evanston in a room with good light, keeps a file on Dan's career with one page about Tasha's chart, and has forgotten the unkind thing he said about Nina.

## Open dangers

- The room is cold, the door will not shut, the wool will not stay on, and no one comes when the child cries. In his first hour he read the long shallow breathing two pallets over as the shape that comes before an afterdrop, and put it away because there was nothing to do with it. Dessa is spent and her pulse has come up.
- The Five are three days off and the Ilm is under ice. Low Kiln says three days when it has counted what it has and does not want to say it.
- A claim has already been made on him in a hand he cannot read, before anyone saw his face. Whoever wrote it has not looked up from writing since.
- The household's silences have a shape and he is inside it. Keeping here is a form of entry: three women keeping a thing warm between them without asking the thing whether it wants to be kept.
- Somebody will come on the third day. Nobody has said who, or why, or what will be done when they do. A foundling with no house-name is a coat on a peg in a poor house: anybody can wear it, anybody can take it, nobody is obliged to say who left it.
- He has been entered in a record he cannot see and cannot refuse. The charge of warmth from the Vail hearth is unpaid and unacknowledged, and the same house has paid twice for the same cold family.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, the small evidence a body carries, and triage --- the ninety-second sorting of the wounded. He knows the smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: the stone was warmed by Dessa at the Vail fire and carried in her apron; the name on the slate was written before anyone looked at his face; the writing hand on the slate has the same pause as the writing hand at the flare, though he set the two side by side without joining them; Alda crossed herself over the flower and covered it with a word and a date; Alda keeps something in a tin on her person; Mira says the secret is in what happens to a child after it is born; a household that keeps its own roll will not show it to a man come down from Slate Row with a slate in his hand (his inference from the shape of the room, not anyone's speech); somebody will come on the third day. He has been entered twice, once in the dark hand and once in his own count.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is and what she wrote; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know who wrote the name, what is in Alda's tin, why Sella's hand paused, or whether either hand --- the one that hovered or the one that wrote --- will come back for him. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago. He does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the doctrine he will be taught is a kind of accounting; that the midwives' second chill has a second cause; that the household's silences are arranged around him and that he is the subject of them; that he is being watched. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 1

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. Reached only as a lit phone and a name he will not look at.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices: the laugh he cannot reach, the full name he hears at every decision, the warm voice that gave his institution the sentence it needed. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* is the grammar the Warm Court will speak in.
- **Alda** --- the woman who lied over his flower in the same breath she told the truth about the weather, and who keeps a second record. She will lie to Dessa for his safety.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, who will not name him because a thing with no name cannot be claimed by a woman with no house. She believes the levy will take him and does not know Sella is waiting. She will die believing him dead.
- **Mira** --- the aunt who knows the secret is in what happens after a child is born and has said nothing for three years. She gave the bread and kept her back to the slate. Her face is the most arranged thing in the room.
- **Sella** --- the woman who will keep him and never ask where his memory came from. Her hand has now hovered and withdrawn; the taking is still three days off. Kindness as a wall.
- **Jorra, Tarn** --- the house's wound and its debt; both will be given a reason to resent the foundling before he can speak.
- **Marra and Rusk** --- the two halves of a purchase he knows nothing about. She pays with her fingers; he pays with his silence. Both are paying for him before he has been taken up.
