## The moment

Day 84 is done. On Silt 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813, at dusk, \person{Sella Vail} carried the boy up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She said nothing about the man with the mud on his boots, or the room shut up eighteen leagues east, or the damper closed; if she had connected any of it she would have put the connection where she put the rest of it, in her private roll nobody was shown, but she had not connected it and there was nothing to put away. He is \person{Corin}, eighty-four days old, two months and twenty-four days, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, with **one** brown-edged crust in the fold where two had lain and the empty place at his ear beside it, still carried without a name and with no third coming. The crust is stiff and gone past smelling of anything and has never been a sermon. The door stands a finger's width open; through the gap comes the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor comes the carried fire, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds kept in two separate places as they have been for eighty-four days. The boards above keep four nails and four and a fifth that is not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts do not meet. In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned lies where it has always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lies beside it unswept because the house says it is the thaw and it will go when it goes; Sella stood over both with a broom and swept neither. The space under the lowest rack was gone around on the last day and entered by nobody, and the broken birth-slates lying there were entered by nobody. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood at six, three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word forty lay on a place of its own with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both, the two tellings of the shut room lay on one tray without touching, four low sounds stood in four places with the river's note a fifth thing, and one crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place beside it, and none of these had joined and none had been spent. - **Day 84, Silt 25, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday).** The bucket-round at the yard well began before the light and a second man come downriver from \place{Greybank} waited his turn with road-mud dried grey to the calf and the salt-and-hot-clay smell of the east road on him. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and made nothing of it, and did not touch the widow's mark because a well, a basket and a story told over a bucket are not openings. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, eighty-four days old at the close of Day 84, two months and twenty-four days, carried everywhere: down to the mud of the Clay Yard, the racks and the well, to the Cross and the moot-post's shade, down the road to the Fishstairs, three doors down just inside a finger's-width-open door in House Silt, to the wheel-shed and the back room's open door (not entered), downriver to the Weir and the plank walk by word, back to the well for the bucket-round, and lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth or the water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from his wrist so the cold finds the place. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; on Day 80 Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge without knowing it and covered the sign with a date. He wants the crust, wants the breast and wants the bucket two inches beyond his hand, and can reach none of them; the body humiliated him exactly all week, and he was the only adult at the well and the least use in it. Dessa Silt is forty mornings dead at the close and his number for his own days is still given by nobody.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired seven more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs, Alda going to her slate before the fold and then to the tin in her bodice, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the grey cord while deciding, and the man from Slate Row who handed over a loaf without a slate. This chapter added: that a thing left where it is can be kept more surely than a thing put on a shelf and written down, because a whole household steps around it; that a figure let fall from a fist toward a well is not entered either, and that the printed figure stands unchanged over fewer bodies; that a head can turn half a beat after the knock and the turner not know why, and that a delay after the knock is a different object from one before it; that the afterdrop read backwards is a district going home warm on a season nobody owned; that a recovery can be a keeping-by-avoidance and a sweep around a thing a way of not entering it; that a danger which cannot be placed is a danger nobody has to act on; that a landing a hand's breadth out of line can be walked over carefully by a whole market without anyone making a fall of it; that a beautiful entry can stand where a true one should and a room will arrange itself around the beautiful one rather than the true one; that dust can come back after a clean day and a room can hold two kinds of obedience without either cancelling the other; that a term running out is legible in a worn thumb-place and a chest held tight against a breath; that agreeing with both sides of an argument is a way of spending nothing and paying later; that three likenesses in a row --- or two tellings of one thing --- will grow a cause unless a hand keeps them four; and that he knew the anatomy of the sealed counting room before the man had finished describing it, and could not put a hand to the door.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the bell's four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told bell in the dark; the boards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the kindling's four, four and a space; the firelight's four, four and an empty shelf; the Fishstairs' four, four and a landing out of line; and now the seven exact days, seventy-eight through eighty-four, set edge to edge against the house's loaves, glances and space at the row's end. The ear still holds six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them, not a seventh. No seventh came all week; a vessel gave its note past the door on Days 81 and 82 and neither he nor Tarn turned toward it, and a note that is not turned toward has entered nobody's keeping. The near-full answer of Day 35 (Ilmen 6), the second of Day 52 (Ilmen 23) and the third of Day 65 stand on their own tray, advanced and not paid, neither hearings nor the third thing. The three intervals (Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25) stay objects on a third tray. The word forty remains a saying, not a vessel's sound, on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn --- Mira's second cord twisted twice beneath the grey --- beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; it was not spoken again this chapter. Water-over-water, the falling water's drag, the low note out of the stone, the told bell, the heard bell and the district's note under strain stay fifth things on places of their own and not hearings at all. The file gained one object this week: a second telling of the shut counting room, held beside the first on a tray that does not touch it.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the one brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 remains, stiff and gone past smelling of anything, uneaten, unlifted and unnamed, carried through a seventh day; the empty place beside it has still not been named and no third came in seven days. Money: none.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was paid on Day 38, in past tense and untagged; nothing like it returned in chapter 17, and the old life stayed offstage. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature stood behind the yard's sweeping around what it left, the figure that arrived nowhere, the order of reaching, the mercy-lie laid over a sign, the afterdrop read backwards on a season named after the fact, and the room sealed against its own heat. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all, and is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.

## Open dangers

- The lean week has ended and the district's habits have not changed. The yard has turned eight days of water into loaves, glances, *the thaw that was* and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The boy heard the note of the whole district under strain --- many small failures inside one slow certain one, with a thin place nobody would name --- and filed its going beside its coming with no word, no pointing hand and no cause written across either. The weak board in the plank walk is gone or trodden flat under the silt, seen by nobody, made into no accident, set beside the missing Fishstairs step and the stopping hairlines in three gaps entered in no account; the steps have come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off. The honeyed ice, the stopping hairlines and the early Sill note are spoken of only in the past tense, and the district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold; a woman wrapped it in wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing. The cold entered nowhere and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure; **a new thread must be opened for it in part 2**.
- A man described, over a bucket at the well in chapter 13, a room shut in the last war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath, their heat gone out with no way to feed it. On Day 84 a **second** man, come downriver from Greybank, told the same story over the same well with the same small sound of iron shut on iron and the same words *not the war's hand*, differing only in that he drew breath through his nose where the first made the sound in his throat. The yard received it both times as history with a date; Sella, who lost a husband to that danger, heard both and told the fire it was the thaw. The boy filed the second telling on the same tray as the first, without letting the two touch and without joining either to any kiln in Low Kiln. Planted toward ~650 (T065). The carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the payer of the coal has gone unnamed for eighty-four days, the coal that went up with the brine-gull on Day 45 is entered nowhere and missed in none.
- No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission; she is forty mornings gone and still unentered. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument, strengthened by the water going around a thing the house set down and leaving it standing, by a yard sweeping around what the water left without entering it, and by a week in which nobody went down to see which board was gone. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.
- The number still goes around in fists, mouths, shoulders, loaves, glances and weather: one in twelve printed and unchallenged over a thinner crowd, the true subtraction unspoken, Berr's figure that goes into a slate and not into a well, the number for the flood that passed from fist to mouth to shoulder and reached nobody, the printed figure and the unspoken one on two trays, and the house's arithmetic with a space at the end of the loaf row that nobody calls a number. Sella's private roll is unshown; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; Alda's **six** written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the pewter tin above the empty bed holds its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened, and the little tin in Alda's bodice is shut. The neat upper line has been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand and the Fishstairs clerk's ordinary order stay filed apart. A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she stood at the flood's edge with her board and string and got no answer. The shape of the knot is on its tray beside the word and the weight of a thumb apart from both.

A kindness that could not be answered has become an absence that cannot be answered.

The boy kept the delay, the sliver, the loaf and the unfinished sentence as four objects on a place of its own and wrote no cause across any of them, because a better-lit room taught him what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. The shape of a sale is in the file; the sale itself is not paid. The loaf will gather interest in an account the child cannot read, and the half-beat is held for the day it lands as the physical tell of a betrayal.

Sella stood over both with a broom all week and left them.
- Tarn has heard flat notes and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, has swept the road to a collector without turning and noticed that he has noticed. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. The seventh year is nearly gone and the one entry the house cannot check presses toward its end; something must give, and the end was not written here. Jorra's dust and the room standing a finger's width open, the unnamed men in the back room, the space under the rack where slate should have stood and the broken birth-slates beneath it are keepings no slate can enter; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful; what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the two sealed counting rooms eighteen leagues east, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day.
- The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept six likenesses apart on purpose; no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work. Calling a thing over after the fact is a third grammar: a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot. Alda's date laid over the sign is a fourth: a beautiful entry standing where a true one should be, in a dead woman's face that no return can alter. Agreeing with both sides of a question is a fifth, and costs nothing in words and everything somewhere else.
- The flat note has six hearings --- five from four sources and one from a person --- and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. Two whole vessels have given their notes past the yard door this week and neither he nor Tarn turned toward them, and a note that is not turned toward has entered nobody's keeping. The nearly full note now has three answers on its own tray, advanced and not paid, and is not a hearing and not the third thing. The river's own note of water going over water, its note of a district under strain and the falling water's drag are each fifth things on places of their own and not hearings at all. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, four, four and a gap in the square and in a story told in the dark, kept in two places; the told bell, the heard bell, the early note, the district's note and the kiln's gap stay separate. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling and the firelight, and the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter; the seven exact days now lie edge to edge against the house's bad arithmetic.
- A woman is dead and stays dead, uncollected, her number in her thumb and her name under her scar; the mercy told her became a fact nobody alive can correct, and she believed him spent among children she never saw to the last. The word *the Breaking* has not been said in his hearing; the word forty was said once, low, is not explained, and was not repeated in seven days.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles; the exact look and sound of a vessel handing its work to the next thing until the skin holds more than it was made to hold and the face arranges itself in the ordinary way; the anatomy of a room sealed against its own heat, and how a vessel with no way to feed what it holds cooks its own heat out of the air; the look of a term running out in a worn thumb-place and a tight chest. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that recovery can be a keeping-by-avoidance and a sweep around a thing a way of not entering it; that the afterdrop can be read backwards, a recovery named and the preceding fall left out, so a district can go home warm on a season nobody owned; that a figure carried from fist to mouth to shoulder arrives nowhere and a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, while the printed figure can stand unchanged over fewer bodies; that a head turned after the knock is a different object from a head turned before it, and a child may not know why he turned; that a danger which cannot be placed is a danger nobody has to act on; that a landing a hand's breadth out of line can be used carefully by a whole market without anyone making a fall of it; that a beautiful entry can stand where a true one should and a room will arrange itself around the beautiful one rather than the true one; that dust can return after a clean day and one man can hold two obediences without either cancelling the other; that agreeing with both sides of a question spends nothing in words and everything somewhere else; that a likeness laid on a likeness, or two tellings of one thing, will grow a cause unless a hand keeps them apart; and that he knew the anatomy of the sealed room before the stranger had finished describing it, and had no door to open.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote six times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the notes go, what goes on in the back room whose door stands a finger's width open, who the two men were who came in since first light on Day 81, why grey slate dust gets worked past Jorra's little finger on some mornings and not others, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know why no collector came for the woman in the brook or that the omission will one day have a name, nor that the room shut eighteen leagues east is the same danger as the breath under his own roof, nor that the second telling matters more than the first. He has heard the word forty spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and does not know that it is the number of children who paid for him or that he is one of them, nor what the forty-first knot counts, nor that Orra tied it the night a woman carried him up the Clay Yard with a flower on the bundle. He can read the shape of the knot but not its name and has no word to spend it on. He does not know that the foundling's half-beat will land as the physical tell of a betrayal, or that the loaf has gathered interest in an account he cannot read. He has no Grathic word for afterdrop, nearly, weather as the district uses it, or the cold foot below the sill.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf, the space between a woman's thumb and her scar, a word said low and watched, an empty place in the fold, a slack frame, a silence where an argument used to stand, a coal gone up with a bird, a stone gone from a fold, a board gone under silt, a space under a rack entered by nobody, a finger's width of open door, a new hairline of grey beside a left-in-place line, or a second telling of a room shut eighteen leagues east; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dusts, the cords, the crusts, the stone, the intervals, the honeyed ice, the frost-bloom, the higher brook, the oiled paper, the knot-string, the prepared and performed sweepings, the two sealed rooms, water-over-water, water going around a line, a foot that stays cold, a head turned half a beat late and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the gruel and the gone crust have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides, and Jorra's silence a third of the same shape; that Ulda's half-beat is a tell held for the day the sale lands, and that the warm loaf has gathered interest in an account the child cannot read; that the near-full note, now three answers, is a reply to the interval he has carried since Stone 13 and not yet the third thing; that the six like rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell, the heard bell, the early note, the district's note and the kiln's gap must be kept apart for the same reason, and so must the two tellings of the shut room, the word on its tray and the second cord beneath the grey; that a line left where it is is kept more surely than a line entered and filed, and that stepping around a thing is a form of knowing; that a thing said after the fact is treated as a thing said before it, and that this is how a district goes home warm from a danger nobody owned; that Sella's not-going toward the cold foot belongs under her own roof and not across the yard; that Berr's reason is good and his hands are clean, which is what fifteen years of wearing a reason looks like; and that the infant in him will win every argument against the surgeon, as he has won every one for eighty-four days.

Under her own roof she sat up three nights with a child of hers who cooled while everybody was kind, and the boy kept that under her roof and did not lay it across the yard. On the last day she heard the story of the shut room and told the fire it was the thaw. One of three hands on the tray of possible takers of the crust, which is not the same thing as a cause.
- **Alda Reed** --- the hand that presses the fold too long, crosses the sign without knowing it, covers it with a date and has now written six notes she will not say; the sixth turned down on Day 64 and read by nobody, not written again this chapter. Another of the three hands on the tray, and the hand that took or did not take the stone from the fold without a word. She came under the roof again on Day 80 and the boy carried her silence past rather than joining it to the tin or the hollow.
- **Mira Silt** --- the hand that put the crust there and keeps two cords no officer can read; the third of the three hands and the one he has wanted the crust from longest. On Days 79 and 83 she moved eel-bones while deciding and put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was spoken, keeping both records off every slate. The hand that went to the cord now stands on the tray with the other two, kept and not entered.
- **Tarn** --- the other ear under the roof, who heard the vessel's note past the door on Days 81 and 82 and did not turn his head. The loan that can hear stays uncalled, and the sentence neither man can say lies under the house. His seventh year is nearly gone and he still will not count it. His silence and Jorra's sit side by side as the same shape and are not joined; the boy keeps his own beside them and lets none touch.
- **Jorra Vail** --- the man who came out of the back room with grey dust on Day 81, who moved the damper ahead of Sella's sentence and would not say what he knows of a Vail pot before a kiln-master. He has not once looked toward the bed or into the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of a name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn; the hollow seat waits. His silence and Tarn's keep their equality under one roof.
- **Berr Clay** --- came to the block on Account-day and read one in twelve over fewer bodies, taking himself out of his own sentence and going off believing he had done a kindness. The boy keeps his good reason on its own place and writes no cause across it, because a better-lit room taught him what a good reason looks like when it has been worn fifteen years. His pricing and Mira's keeping are two not-lookings of one shape that do not touch.
- **Ulda** --- seen once this chapter, at the well on Day 78, and unnamed on the page: the child who says what everybody is thinking in a voice that makes it harmless, who turned his head toward a pot a half-beat after the boy's and could not say why, and who took a warm loaf from a man whose name is on no roll into a pocket that held no slate. The boy kept his own turning and the child's side by side on a tray and did not let the two touch. He is the hidden enemy disguised as a friend: a poor child who learned that a secret is property and that property is the only thing a foundling can own. Nothing has been sold yet that a slate could read, and the reader has not yet been told to suspect him.
