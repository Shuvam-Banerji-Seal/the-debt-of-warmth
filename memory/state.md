## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and forty days old at the close of Day 140 and four months and twenty days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard and the edge of the worked strips, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle and the hearth, at the wheel-shed's edge (back room not entered), \lore{the damper} and the flagstones, the Cross and the moot-post's shade, the pot board and the market stalls, the out-of-line landing, down the river road to the Weir's plank walk and blessing stone and the fish-trade shade below the sill, and lifted onto the bed under the roof each night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached, not the crust two inches from his ear, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out (the handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand), the loaf shelf, the charcoal under it, the cord in the cutter's coat, the knot-string at Orra's waist, the second twist beneath Mira's apron, the widow's mark at Sella's throat or the frozen thread at his own mouth. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing holding his slate to his forearm, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the cord while deciding whether to lie, Orra pulling a knot down as she gives a date, the reed-cutter turning his cord where no slate lay, Jorra speaking to the wheel before the pots, Sella giving the instruction to the wool, wind, pot, broom and fire before she gives it to anybody, and a woman reaching a finger beneath a board without looking. This chapter added: that a record kept in a carrying arm tells what was carried and not what it cost, and is exact, complete and worth nothing; that a cost asked in the form of a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry; that a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would; that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason is left between the two; that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid and a performance of arithmetic can be read and not entered; that a date given where a figure was asked is an answer with the question taken out of it; that **six** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, and that a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; that a colour is not a face, a likeness is not a cause, and an absence is not an absence of the same thing; that a thing stopped six times at the teeth is still owed and not paid; and that the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody will perform can be read exactly and still buy nothing. He is the only adult under this roof and the least use in it: a man who once laid out instruments on a towel and knew every one by the wear on its handle, now a child who cannot close two inches of his hand on the rim of the basket two paces off. Both are true and the second is louder, and he has stopped expecting the first to make the second any smaller.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb; the river's note as a fifth low thing never counted among the full note; the clerk's three counts in three places that do not become one; and 138, 139 and 140 exact days set edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, carried unspent. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and thirty-eight, one hundred and thirty-nine and one hundred and forty days, four months and eighteen, nineteen and twenty days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which warmth is given out and the order in which a body loses it, especially the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure spoken over a crowd and a figure written in a private place are two objects even when they are the same figure, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip or a bundle, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost and that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a cost asked as a direction can be answered with a direction and leave no entry, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, and a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; that **six** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, that a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and that a thing held six times at the teeth is a held word still --- six times owed is not six times paid; that a colour is not a face, a likeness is not a cause, and an absence is not an absence of the same thing.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the neighbours' one-breath joinings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. A **fourth** private record in the house itself: the charcoal under the shelf, set on its own place below the two gaps above it, and the woman's finger that goes beneath it without looking, both unread and unjoined, the reason left between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, knotted with teeth on the blessing stone and put back inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf and the finger-under-board habit. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** the two neighbours' tellings on two places beside the earlier tellings and the watcher's late turn, no fourth likeness and no third gap. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's stroke going on without the half-check and his chest's small interval, and now the **unchecking stroke** of Day 140 laid beside the earlier half-beat, set beside the bent finger, the held breath and **six** stoppings without any of them joined. The near-saying itself: a sentence that came to the threshold of the word six times --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17 and 20 --- and stopped six times, with no figure and no name, held as six intervals and not six payments. The greens: **five at mouths and six shapes of the colour** --- Silt 30, Seed 28, Reed 7, Reed 15 and the silent woman of Reed 20, with Berr's glaze making the sixth shape --- all colours on places of their own, no name, no ear and never Fenna in the narration. The heard bell beside the told bell; the two tellings of the shut counting room with no third; the damper's double bite filed joined to no kiln; the cold foot read exactly and worth nothing; the clerk still set for an uncalled measure; Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's tin shut.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and forty days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it for a hundred and forty days. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking**, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138 and in the night inventory of 140. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, and that the sale is years ahead. What the reed-cutter's knots count, what the cord says beyond *No more can the Court*, who the reed-cutter is, what the word forty counts, who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over and who the man at the stern of the collector's boat was. What the silt-leach is, or that the cold in the child's foot has a name and a habit Arc 3 away. Which hand went into the strips on Seed 15 and did not come out on Seed 27, whether it is a hand or a body that will be found or a figure that will be given, and whether anybody will ever enter the place in the line. What the green on the silent woman's mouth will one day be read as, or whose mouth it will be joined to, and what the pale line on the back of her hand once held. Whether the loan that can hear can be called, what word would call it, and what must give at the end of Tarn's seventh year; he knows only that the sixth stopping has been held and the seventh has not come. What the clerk's inward slate holds, and what measure was set down before anybody called it.

## Open dangers

- **The planting crossed out of its last tend and one hand did not come back with the rest; the reed month now stands in its last tend with no figure and the second Account-day has stood without giving one.** The reeds stand in long grey ranks in the ruts where the barley stood, green at the broken ends and pale at the frost-bitten tips, cut in a going-downriver and a coming-back and judged by the arm that carries them; a cutter told a girl with a creel to lift her bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it, that being the whole of what it knew, and a woman at the racks who asked what the hands would be paid was answered with the weather and the river. A cost asked as a direction is answered with the pot, the weather or the river and leaves no entry. **T098:** the yard went around the empty place in the returning line with a look and a wide step and no number; if a hand is lifted out of the ground in a month or a year a slate will be found and a figure made to stand at its foot, and if no hand comes up the space will go on being gone around and the month will go on standing short without a figure. No name, cause, body, accident, payment or payer. The figure Berr read aloud on Day 139 did not know the hand that had not come back. The bell's four, four and a gap was **heard** again at the Cross on Day 139 and nobody crossed himself; the heard bell is kept beside the told bell and must not be joined to it. The steps at the Fishstairs came back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the whole market walked over carefully, a girl with a creel taking it sideways without a fall. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off; his hand goes to the slate before his eyes find the buyers. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of a death. The district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **Three private records in wool and teeth and thumb went past the basket in the same hour, and a fourth is kept under the house's own shelf.** **T099 planted on Day 122, carried on Days 125, 128, 131, 132, 137, 138 and 140:** a reed-cutter's tally in grey wool or reed-fibre cord, knots set on the flat blessing stone, one tied low with teeth and thumb, carried on the plank walk at the Weir and not counted. *You can ask the cord* --- *I can't read a cord* --- *No more can the Court.* **T024/T059/T025:** Orra's knot-string, with its last double turn legible (tail tucked, left over right, the small weight of a thumb pressed in) and its name withheld, and Mira's grey cord with a second twist twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted, the knot for the thin child untouched; the two boards side by side and not touching. **T100 planted on Day 129, carried on Days 132, 134, 135, 138 and 140:** the charcoal tally on the underside of the Vail loaf shelf, grouped strokes of different darkness kept where nobody thought to look, with Sella's finger going beneath the board without a word. Do not count a knot or a stroke, read a figure, give the reed-cutter a name, name the charcoal's count, join either to T098, open a tin or box or speak the word forty in Chapters 34–36. The private records gather toward the far-off day they will be laid together and the joining called an account; no hand in the district has yet existed to do it.
- **Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold.** A woman wound it in three turns of wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word and no hand two paces long; he knows that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and the reading works and buys nothing. He did not lay the foot beside the story of a child under this roof who had cooled while everybody was kind; a story from a yard and a foot below a sill are two things in two places, and a colour is not a foot. The cold entered nowhere on Days 139 or 140 and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure, carried through the lean week, the turn of Silt, four weeks of Seed and twenty-one days of Reed; **the threads T093/T094/T096 are carried, not paid, and no silt-leach, cause or cure may be named through Chapters 34–36.**
- **The loaf shelf keeps two unnamed gaps and the fold keeps one crust and one unnamed gap, and a new count lies beneath the board.** Two neighbours, on Seed 24 and Seed 29, named the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the boy's ear a child's appetite in one breath, laying two rooms under one cause without seeing it, and went off with their pots. The boy set their sayings on places of their own beside the shelf's two gaps and the fold's crust and its empty place, wrote no cause and did not make the joining his own. A saying of the loaf-shelves of Low Kiln now stands in the file: two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name. Beneath the board the charcoal clusters lie on a separate place, pressed hard and faded nearly into the grain, and Sella's finger has been under it on Days 132, 134, 135, 138 and 140 without a word. **T097 stands advanced toward the far-off day a finger lays the shelf and the fold together and an account wants a name; T100 stands carried toward the far-off day the district's private records are laid together.** Do not pay T097 early, do not let the boy make either joining his own, do not name, join or solve the three places in Chapters 34–36, let no third gap come and no fourth likeness make a cause. A line drawn through the places would be a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand is the thing he has spent his life declining to be.
- **Below the sill's other edge, Tarn's seventh year stands one day nearer its end and the loan that can hear is still uncalled.** The knife's worn mark has six years in it and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check; to the kindling he said *Six counted. The next I don't count* and pressed the held breath to its edge without spending it, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where the breath should have gone in. He was sent for a collector once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year. His stroke goes over the collector's road without once turning and no collector comes; Jorra's silence and his keep the equality, and the stroke now goes on past the bright patch without even the half-check. **The near-saying has now come SIX times to the threshold of the word that would call the loan --- on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17 and 20 --- and stopped six times with no figure and no name; all six stoppings are filed as intervals and not payments.** Do not call the loan, spend the breath, count the seventh year or let a collector come in Chapters 34–36; a seventh stopping is still owed and something must still give at the end of the seventh year and has not given here. Jorra's dust, the room standing a finger's width open, the unnamed men, the space under the rack and the broken birth-slates beneath it are keepings no slate can enter; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- **On a day in the last war a room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath**, their heat gone out with no way to feed it, as told by a man over a bucket at the well in chapter 13 and by a second man come downriver from Greybank on Day 84, and only *mentioned* by a third voice at the bucket-round on Day 102, each as a dated curiosity eighteen leagues east with the same words *not the war's hand*, differing only in breath through the nose against breath made in the throat. The two tellings have sat on their own tray through the whole of Reed so far, joined to no kiln and to no third telling: Sella, who lost a husband to that danger, heard them and told the fire it was the thaw, and Jorra's bent finger went to a damper that the house calls only a holding, never a taking, and the boy read the same iron as a holding and a taking with no Grathic word for both. **No third telling came on the morning of Day 140** when three or four men went into the back room. Planted toward ~650 (T065/T095). The carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, came into the house in a clay dish before the boy was a month old, warms the boards by degrees and misses one breath in every four; the payer of the coal has gone unnamed for a hundred and forty days, the coal that went up with the brine-gull on Day 45 is entered in no account and missed in none, and one warmth less has gone out of the world with no account the poorer.
- **No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission**; she is more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and still unentered, the number gone into the water in her thumb. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb. Alda's date still stands over her face and no living person can take it off; her six pages and both tins stayed shut through chapter 33.
- **The number still goes around in fists, mouths, shoulders, loaves, glances, armfuls, cords, charcoal and weather:** a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, Berr's folded cord, twice-wiped thumb, two marks short and self-removal, the true subtraction unspoken, the printed figure unchallenged, the house's arithmetic with two spaces on the loaf row that nobody names, a bundle judged by the arm that carries it, the figure entered by the clerk before he looks at the buyer, and a cord and a shelf-underside that no Court thumb can read. Sella's private roll is unshown and her finger beneath the board is unexplained; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; the reed-cutter's cord is a third such record; Alda's **six** written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the pewter tin above the empty bed holds its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened, and the little tin in Alda's bodice was touched on earlier days and not opened. The soft hands of a man from Slate Row handed a figure to a slate held at the edge on Day 115 and stand as a shape off the record-first trays; no two touched, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand in three days, and no sale. The word forty was spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and has not been spoken in three days; the double turn of the forty-first knot is legible and unnamed.
- **Orra tied a forty-first knot over the story of a small warm bundle with a flower and said the number into the basket and watched;** the cord hangs in the \place{Eel House} where he cannot enter or read it, though he can read its shape without its name: a double turn, tail tucked, tied left over right, with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in. A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she stood at the lower boards on Day 140 giving the ninth of Ilmen and the boat with six oars and the man at the stern where a figure was asked and getting nothing from the basket. The shape of the knot is on its tray beside the word and the weight of a thumb apart from both; the man at the stern who never touched the oars is on a place of his own beside the midwife's reaching hand and the thane's twice-wiped thumb.
- **One of the two crusts in the fold is gone and nobody says who took it, that it is gone, or what became of it;** the spent one lies stiff beside an empty place that has crossed twenty-one days into Reed without a name. The pale river stone is gone from the fold in House Silt and nobody says it is gone either; the weak board is gone under the silt and nobody makes an accident of it; the broken birth-slates are under the rack and nobody enters them; the hollow where the stone lay is left in the wool and nobody says the stone was taken. A coal went up with a brine-gull on Day 45 and one warmth less went out of the world with no slate the poorer. A hand went into the strips on Seed 15 and did not come out on Seed 27 and nobody says which hand, why, or what it cost. These are subtractions entered in no account, and the boy keeps all of them without a word in \lore{Grathic} for any of them. A kindness that could not be answered has become an absence that cannot be answered.
- **A new person is in the yard and his attention can be bought.** Ulda's head went toward a pot a half-beat after the boy's on Silt 16, Seed 19 and again on Day 121 at the market's edge; each time he stopped, rubbed the green sliver and could not say why, and twice he opened his mouth on a kiln-hand's unfinished sentence and finished nothing. No loaf passes and no slate is seen. The soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs is not him. A man from Slate Row whose name is on no roll gave him a warm loaf on Silt 16 that went into a pocket holding no slate, and that loaf goes on gathering interest in an account the child cannot read. The boy kept the delay, the sliver, the loaf, his own turning and the unfinished sentence as objects on separate places and wrote no cause across any of them. A second man with soft hands handed a figure to a slate on Day 99 and again on Day 115 and was filed apart from all of them. The shape of a sale is in the file; the sale itself is not paid. The half-beat is held for the day it lands as the physical tell of a betrayal, and the watcher's turn lies beside the shelf tellings with no finger laid across any of them. He was offstage in chapter 33.
- **A green is on its way to being a face, and it advanced this chapter.** On Silt 30 a silent woman stood across the square with lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth and was filed; on Seed 28 a second silent woman stood looking at pots with the same green and was filed beside her and beside Berr's green at his own mouth; on Day 126 a third silent woman at the Cross turned a cup a quarter, set it back and went off with the light, the third green at a mouth and the fourth shape with Berr's; on Day 134 a fourth silent woman did the same at the pot board, the fourth green at a mouth and the fifth shape with Berr's; **on Day 139 a fifth silent woman across the square at the reading turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the fifth green at a mouth and the sixth shape of the colour (T050/T051 advanced, not paid).** Berr's green was seen again on Days 139 at the Cross and the stalls. On a far day an ear will be spoken with that colour and a name will join it to a person, and until then it is only green: no dialogue, no name, no trade, no price, no joining, and never Fenna in the narration. The pale line on the fifth woman's hand is a shape a cord leaves and was not joined to any cord, eel, door or sale. **No sixth green came.**
- **Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness;** it was not touched once in three days, because a shelf, an afternoon, a strip, a rack, a well, a bucket, a road, a market, a post, a board, a basket, a bed and a dusk are not openings and no opening came. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles remains never paid, never settled, never done with; his clean dark line is not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and the new finer grey beside it is not swept either, because the house says it is thaw and will go when it goes. Sella stood over both with a broom and left them. Her grey streak at the temple, her saying that it came in the winter Garl died, her half-beat over the basket, her counting under her breath and her finger beneath the loaf shelf are carried, not explained.
- **The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful;** what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the shut counting rooms eighteen leagues east, the damper called a holding and never a taking, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and House Silt is a fifth such room set beside them;** the boy has kept all five apart on purpose, and no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work. Calling a thing over after the fact is a third grammar: a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot. Alda's date laid over the sign is a fourth: a beautiful entry standing where a true one should be, in a dead woman's face that no return can alter. Agreeing with both sides of a question is a fifth, and costs nothing in words and everything somewhere else, as Tarn agreed with an early thaw and a late while wanting the late. Mira's keeping and Berr's pricing are two faces turned the same way that mean opposite things, a sixth shape with no Grathic word for either, and the record-first reach is a seventh. A man taking himself out of his own sentence as a hand goes out of a field before the dressing goes on is the shape Berr carried up the road with him; the yard going around its empty place with a look is the same shape performed in the open air. A word held at the teeth and kept is an eighth, and a kept thing is owed; the new saying of the loaf-shelves is a ninth, naming the account that waits for the finger that will not come yet, the charcoal under the shelf is a tenth, kept where nobody thought to look, the **sixth** stopping at the word is an eleventh, held on the tray as an object and not a rule, and the **fifth** green as a colour on its own place is a twelfth. No two touch and no payer has stood up among them.
- **The hundred-and-fortieth day is kept, not paid.** The count goes on edge to edge and unspent and nobody under the roof knows what the column would count. Keep it exact into Day 141 and do not let anyone say it. The reeds stand in their last tend and the bundles go past judged by the arms that carry them, but the empty place in the returning line is still gone around with a look and no figure; the reed-cutter's cord is carried and not counted; the charcoal under the Vail shelf is unread and the finger beneath the board is unexplained; the two shelf gaps and the fold's gap stay apart in two rooms that do not touch, the new grey stays unswept and is called the thaw, the cold foot stays the cold, the clerk stays set, the loan stays uncalled with **six** stoppings on the interval tray and the unchecking stroke held beside the half-beat, *he's not Vail* sealed, the promise ungiven, the word forty unspoken, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting, no sixth green, no sale, no watcher on stage, Marra not in Sill and Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy. T097 waits advanced and unpaid with the two neighbours' joinings held on places of their own and no third gap; T098 waits carried and unpaid with no name, cause or body; T099 waits carried and unpaid with no knot counted; T100 waits carried and unpaid with no stroke counted and no word spoken over it; T050/T051 waits advanced with the fifth green a colour and not a name. Nothing has been closed, so Chapter 34 opens on Day 141, Reed 22, Secondday, with the boy one hundred and forty-one days old and nobody saying so, the reed month in its last week, the third Account-day at Reed 30 nine days off, Berr's figure gone into the air and the true subtraction in the box, the charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, six stoppings on the tray and a seventh stopping still owed at the threshold of the word that would call the loan. Forward, not closed.
