# State of things — at the close of chapter 8 (private)

## The moment

Day 30 is done. On Ilmen 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813, the Opening of the Slates, the grey that had been left where it lay through the Last Day was burned before the light cleared the sill by a woman at the yard mouth with a pail by her knee; where the ridge had turned its corner unbroken for a year and two days there was a clean dark line, and a neighbour at the tubs said it was gone, burned this morning, dealt with, in the flat tone of a woman reporting weather. \person{Sella Vail} stood over the place with a broom in her hand and did not sweep and did not say what had been there; \person{Corin} lay in the basket at her hip and held the left thing and the burned-and-called-gone thing under the same hand without putting one over the other, knowing that a thing called gone is not the same as a thing gone, and that smoke clings longer than the thing that made it. The coals were still banked under their ash from the night before. There had been no opening of the kiln, so the hand at her throat did not go to \lore{the widow's mark}, and would not go there at a reading, a bank or a dusk; the fire that had come up the yard on \lore{Kindling} was still in the house, five days on, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four.

Outside, the yard spoke of \place{Slate Row} as a direction and not a place anybody meant to go that morning; the new year's accounts were being opened in a room with a bench and a pot and a draught along the floor. One in twelve stood printed where it had stood last year and nobody challenged it, and the true subtraction that would have put a figure on what a house had actually lost was spoken by nobody and went past the basket shut and unopenable. Work turned toward the thaw and the slip pits: \person{Jorra Vail} came in with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms, the bent little finger going in ahead of him at the damper, which he moved before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, and said to the wheel what he never said to a person; \person{Tarn} swept before the light was up, set the small knife back on its sill and told the empty yard it could be checked, six years counted and the seventh held back again. The yard called the cold a warm spell and the warm spell the weather, and under the ice \place{the Ilm} did not stir. Sella put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, fed him barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, said \emph{the house} where another woman would say \emph{I}, and said nothing about where the warmth in the dish had come from or who had paid for it. In the fold the two crusts lay where they had been put, neither lifted, eaten or named, and no third had come in all five days.

Nothing had been joined. Five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; the intervals and the silence where a name should stand kept apart; five pauses lying side by side in a row and not joined; the \lore{Drawing} collector's ordinary hand with no pause filed in its own place; the charred slate lying in the kiln ash where the broom had gone around it for a year. He was thirty days old, cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below \lore{Thin}, with twelve to fifteen embers at best and a flaw nobody had named.

Day count: **Day 30 complete.** Day 31 is Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813, Secondday, and opens chapter 9 at the yard well. No day was skipped and no day was poured into another.

The five earlier days of this chapter, held in full:

- **Day 25, \lore{Kindling}, Y.H. 812.** Before first light a clay dish came up \place{the Clay Yard} with a coal under ash, passed from hearth to hearth, each house touching its own fire to it; nobody said where it had been lit or what it cost. Sella carried the twenty-five-day-old boy to the threshold and touched \lore{the widow's mark} before she opened the kiln, held it one breath, and tipped the dish in; the little-door burn opened on the iron and shut on nothing, and the mark was not touched at the bank. He counted the light from the fire: four, four and a place where a fifth would be if there were a fifth --- an absence with the shape of a presence, filed beside the bench's interval and kept from the hearings. Somewhere offstage a collector took the year's first measure; a woman came back with a shut fist and set it on the bench like a cup, and no figure was spoken under the roof. Tarn put the knife on its sill and said six counted and the next he did not count; Jorra's bent little finger led at the damper and the back room was not named; the coat, the hollow seat, the unbroken ridge and the charred slate kept their places. Sella fed him from the house's own dish, said the warmth was the house's, gave the instruction to the fire and the wool, and did not wipe his mouth. At dusk she banked the coals with the back of a broken pot; nobody had spoken to the basket all day.
- **Day 26, \lore{Weighing}, Y.H. 812.** An officer read the year's measures across the boards in a clean voice that wore a temple's clothes --- the passive grammar Berr had used at the Cross, now with a uniform, a cadence and no yard in it. Sella set the basket at the outer fringe of \place{the Cross}, a half-pace from every open slate, and spoke to the wool and not to his face. A whole \lore{the Return} was announced for a person not in the yard, with a figure and a name that the household would not repeat afterward; the boy could not spend, correct or hand back the figure, and counted the silence after the name among the gaps, not the hearings. \person{Berr Clay} was glimpsed with the red cord of his office folded under his coat and green at one corner of his mouth, wiped his thumb twice before touching his slate, passed a finger over the figure, looked nowhere near the basket and went off toward his house with the box still locked. The voice then went through a doorway into \place{the Court's room}, which the boy learned by hearing: a bench by the door, a pot on it, a draught along the floor to the corner, a place where a line should stand and does not. He set it beside the birth room, the Vail kiln-house and the death-house and wrote no cause. Back under the roof there was no opening, so the mark was untouched; the crusts stayed put and Tarn's knife was back on its sill.
- **Day 27, \lore{the Long Night}, Y.H. 812.** No fire was relit after dusk. The short day went in ordinary work --- drying, mending, sweeping, the wheel, the damper moved early --- and the house sat down in the dark to tell stories of the Return. \person{Alda Reed} told of a child come back thin who knew the warming stone before a mother's hands and grew worse on the third day, and went on giving dates after the question had stopped; Tarn said in flat words that a man cannot be returned to himself and that his mother came back with somebody else's hands; a woman told the year's bell as four, four and a gap over the Cross with nobody crossing himself. The boy counted the gaps between voices instead of flames --- the space between question and answer, the place where a name should stand, the point where a story stopped short and went on with a date --- and filed them with the intervals, while the told bell stayed beside but never on the heard bell. Cold came at cheek, temple and wrist; the breast and the crust were not given. Tarn held his seventh year back in the dark, Jorra did not say the back room was cold, and Garl's coat held a broader man's shape. The night ended without fire and without an explanation.
- **Day 28, \lore{the Remembering}, Y.H. 812.** Names of the dead went along the yard from door to door, and the boy counted the spaces between them before he understood what the district was doing. Under the Vail roof Garl's name was not spoken: the coat kept its peg, the wheel-seat its hollow on the left, the ridge its unbroken corner, and Sella went around the name as she went around ash left in a corner, three times in one morning coming up to a word and going around it. The boy set the silence where the name should have stood with the other gaps and not with the hearings. Alda Reed came with the birth-slates laid writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold, gave a date where a name should stand, touched the tin inside her bodice once without opening it, pressed the fold too long, crossed two fingers at the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with the same date. In the old register a hand went into a coat pocket and found wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain; the promise of a boat on cold water came and went, untagged, in past tense, and the name was not looked at. The charred slate lay in the kiln ash; the two crusts lay in the fold; Jorra's grey dust was worked into the creases past his bent finger and he said nothing about where he had been.
- **Day 29, \lore{the Last Day}, Y.H. 812.** Bread was baked before light and the brown-edged loaves went onto the rack set a little apart; Sella counted them with her lips and no figure was said. The two crusts in the fold stayed where they were put and no third came in. \person{Mira Silt} came up from the eels with slime dried on her sleeves, silt in her neck and her head held to the right against the cold where the top of her left ear had been; she answered a question with a question and her own answer, flicked an eel-bone against her thumb, put that thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a figure was spoken, and shifted her weight so the second cord twisted twice beneath it showed for a moment; the knots on neither were counted, and she did not look into the basket. Nobody swept the ash early: Sella laid her thumb flat into the Vail ridge, walked two doors down and laid it into the neighbour's ridge a finger's width thicker, and came back without saying which was which, leaving both to turn their corners unbroken. The boy read a thing left in place as a thing still accounted for. Garl's coat, seat and proverb kept the house in his shape; Tarn scraped ash from his knuckles with the knife and set it back; Jorra moved the damper before Sella had finished and did not say the back room was cold. At the bank the coals went under blind ash and the mark was not touched.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, thirty days old at the close of Day 30. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: in the basket at Sella's hip, set at a threshold or the outer fringe of the Cross, lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into the Debt Loft. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth freezes in the small hours and nobody wipes it, because a basket at the edge of a board is a pot set down and left. The head goes where it wants and makes a small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. The blanket will not stay on. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and on the Long Night it set in early, with the oiled paper lifting and a grey skin holding on the brook. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 28 without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with a date. He wants the breast and wants the crust even cold, and gets neither; the old crust is stiff past smelling of anything, the newer still gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns, and both lie in the fold, uneaten, unremoved, unnamed, with no third crust in five days.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired a week's worth of new objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk at the Court's table, the man who priced him, the woman who kept him, a collector for a neighbour, a clerk at the market's edge and an officer reading a clean sentence: the speaker disappears from the sentence and the room accepts what is left. He knows that a thing that does not happen leaves a space with the shape of a sound; that an interval is not a hearing, a gap in a story is the same kind of object as a gap in a vessel's breath, and a pause is not a hearing either; and that the trays must not touch. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he has kept the ridge, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the dust, the told bell and the heard bell, the sign and the date over it, and the four room-likenesses apart on purpose. He knows that a thing left in place and a thing burned and called dealt with are two ways of keeping a room; that smoke clings longer than the thing that made it; that a year is a change of handwriting on the same account; and that a count nobody has thought to enter cannot be taxed. He knows the household keeps its arithmetic in loaves, glances and absences, that the district gives out numbers it has already decided to spend, and that a figure passed in a fist or read in a clean voice cannot be corrected or handed back. He knows Tarn has turned his head at a flat note and said nothing, and that two silences under one roof are nearer an equality than anything the house has a name for. He knows a gift that cannot be answered cannot be refused, which is a lesson in bread before it is a lesson in anything else. He is the only adult in every room and the least use in it; correctness is still the whole humiliation, and he has learned to find it funny without laughing.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the boards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the bell's four, four and a gap; the Fishstairs' four, four and a landing; the kindling's four, four and a space; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the prints of the man from the river, four and four and a slide; the hairline in the ice that stops short of the edge; the firelight's four, four and an empty shelf; the told bell's four, four and a gap; the officer's four measures, four measures and a missing closing line. The ear holds \lore{the full note} in five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and this chapter added no sixth, though he listened to a whole house telling in the dark. The intervals and gaps on their own trays are: the pot turned toward on the bench that gave nothing, the half-heard note that went past the door on Day 16, the pot that cracked in the yard on Day 19, the empty fifth in the kindling firelight, the silence after the Weighing's name, the silence where the house's dead man's name should stand, the three told gaps from the Long Night (question-to-answer, a name left out, a story finished with a date), the officer's missing closing line, and the Court's room's missing line, all filed beside but never among the hearings. There are still five sightings of the pause in the neat dark hand (the flare, the birth-slate, the square's slate on Stone 10, the Court's table on Stone 20, the market's edge on Stone 30) lying side by side and unjoined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause stays filed apart. There are five pauses lying side by side and not joined. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it. The knowing buys nothing: no hand has reached a vessel, nobody has tapped a slate for him, nobody has said what the sound was.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 is still there, stiff and gone past smelling of anything; the fresh brown-edged crust from Mira, given on Day 22 and still giving a faint breath of barley smoke when the wind turns, still lies beside it. Neither has been eaten, lifted or named in five days, and no third crust has been added though bread was baked on the Last Day. **Held/unchanged:** the small pale river stone remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in \place{House Silt}, unmentioned all chapter; the small bench knife is back on its sill, announced unasked on every day of the Five and again on Ilmen 1; the charred slate still lies in the kiln ash, black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark; the basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word; Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched once on Day 28 and never opened, and the narrow slate she wrote on Day 18 is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name. The \lore{Ash} ridge is gone from the Vail corner, burned on the morning of Ilmen 1 and called dealt with. **Filed but not possessed:** the Day 16 collector's grey fingertips, Jorra's grey dust, the cracked pot, the carried firelight's empty shelf, the told bell, the silence where a name should be and Mira's two cords are on separate trays in his private file. **Money:** none to the boy and none changes hands to him. Figures go past him and are not his to spend: one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the year's first measure taken offstage and unspoken, a whole return announced for somebody absent, a levy called for the feast, a ridge near two thumbs and a finger thicker a year ago and now gone, nineteen steps, four loaves on the seventh of Stone. The gruel, the fire, the basket, the wool and both crusts remain entered nowhere; every kindness has a payer who can be named and a recipient who cannot refuse it.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was reached for once this chapter, on Day 28, in past tense: a hand in a grey sleeve went into a coat pocket and found wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain, and the name was not looked at. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature stood behind that reaching; the flare light and the hand that stopped between two clusters had made a fifth sighting on Stone 30 and have not made a sixth. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a failure of the system, were recognised again in the officer's passive sentences and the Court's borrowed forms. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one; Des's laugh did not return. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear and the two crusts in the fold at his side, awake, silent, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. Wants the breast, wants the crust, wants a word to correct the room; gets none of the three and cannot ask for any. His count goes on into a year already entered on the district's slates.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the fire and the bed, having banked the coals without touching the mark. On Day 25 she took the mark before the lid and held it one breath; she has not touched it since, not at a bank, a reading, a remembering, a baking or the turn of the year. She stood over the burned ridge with a broom and said nothing while a neighbour called the grey dealt with, and she measured both ridges with one thumb the day before and came back without saying which was which. She put the fold right without looking at his face, fed him from the house's own dish, said the warmth was the house's, gave the instruction to the fire and the wool, and did not wipe his mouth or name either crust. Has not written him as Vail, keeps a private roll in a careful hand she will not show, and will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take his coat down. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained; another turn of the year has made that harder without giving her anyone to answer.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- in the yard and under the roof, broom set against the wall, small knife back on its sill and announced to the empty yard on every day of the Five and again on Firstday. Six years of the seven are counted; he will not count the seventh, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifts; his broom still checks half a stroke where the pot cracked. He told the dark and the yard that six were counted and the next he did not count, and in the Long Night said his mother came back with somebody else's hands. He has kept silent about the flat note past the door and about having seen the boy hear one; the two silences under one roof have not touched. He wanted a late thaw, a longer week before the paying, and now the paying is a year nearer. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist and the mark worn almost flat on the knife handle are his own records. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and grudges the house the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence has never been said in the house and lies in it all the same.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- in and out of the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger of his left hand going in ahead of him at the damper, which he moves before Sella has finished. On the Remembering fine grey slate dust came back into the creases past the bend and he said nothing about where he had been; by Ilmen 1 his forearms were clean again and the boy keeps the two states on separate places. He tells pots they will do and the wheel that it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady, and he has not once looked toward the bed. The room off the shed keeps its finger's width of open and he does not say it is cold. The full note was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel and he would not say so before a kiln-master. Still wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself. The sentence *he's not Vail* is as sealed as on the hour it was spoken; the broken slates under the drying rack and the men whose names are not spoken remain unshown.
- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- absent and the shape of most of the house, though the ridge that was his ash-marker is now a clean dark line on the wall, burned and called gone. His warmth lies where it fell, the coat keeps his broad shape on the peg, the seat keeps his twenty-year leftward lean, and the debt against a firing of tiles remains neither paid nor settled nor done with. Under the Vail roof his name was not spoken on the Remembering, and the house went around the word as Sella went around ash in a corner. His proverbs live in the house's mouth: *feed the fire before you feed yourself*, and *leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled* --- the proverb went on being said to the pot after the crack was gone.

## Open dangers

- The year's first measure was taken out of the yard on Kindling and entered without a figure in the house; a whole return was read out for somebody absent on Weighing; on Ilmen 1 one in twelve stood printed and unchallenged and the true subtraction went past the basket shut. The boy could not address, refuse, reach or correct any of them, and a district that has already entered a new year on its slates has still not entered him.
- He is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and does not know he is inside the third. Sella's private roll is still unshown and the wall around it held; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's grey wool and second cord are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; Alda's three written notes are turned down where no mother or officer will read them. The neat upper line has been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause stays filed apart.
- Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness; it is held while the fire never goes out and touched only before an opening, never at a bank, a reading, a remembering, a baking or a dusk, and the rule held across the turn of the year. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles has been spoken around through another Account-day and another named day and remains never paid, never settled, never done with; his name was not said under the Vail roof on the Remembering and his objects keep his place.
- Tarn has heard a flat note and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, and has turned his own head a degree at one going past the door and kept that silent too. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. Jorra's changing dust and the unnameable back room, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and both not-lookings, and Dessa's count in her thumb are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful. The fire was damped and not relit on the Long Night, and the house sat in the dark telling of people returned wrong; a woman from High Kiln still says the thaw will come early and the yard still wants it late because the levy falls in the thaw. What the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cracked pot, the numb hand, the cough, the debt and the grey are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day of it. The grey has now been burned and called dealt with, which lets the whole yard go home without owning the year's ash.
- The child on the quilt in the death-house was worse on the third day of its return and the room called it a graft settling; in the dark Alda said the returned child grew worse on the third day and gave dates where wonder should stand. The boy's own flower has been crossed at by a woman who did not know she did it and covered with a date. The death-house ridge was measured a finger's width thicker than Vail's and both were burned on Ilmen 1.
- The Court's room has the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has now kept four likenesses apart on purpose; no cause has been written in any of them. The officer's clean sentences wore a temple's clothes and left a missing line in the figures, which he filed among the gaps.
- The flat note has five hearings from four sources and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. A third object --- a pot that cracked two paces from his head --- lies on the interval tray beside the bench's silent set-down and the half-heard note past the door, and the count is still five. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained; it went four, four and a gap over the square on Stone 30 and came back four, four and a gap in a story told in the dark, and he kept the two in two places. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling, the firelight and the space between a woman's thumb and her scar; the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter.
- Low Kiln reads one in twelve and will not say what it counts; one in twelve stayed printed and unchallenged through the turning of the year. The Ilm has a hairline crack and the Singing Sill goes on giving its low note without explanation. The Five are behind them, the year has opened, and the boy keeps one day after tonight is tomorrow while the district has entered twelve months on its slates and left him unentered. The word *the Breaking* has not been said in his hearing.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that a year's fire is carried from hearth to hearth in a clay dish and nobody says where it was lit or what it cost; that the mark is touched before an opening and at no other hour, a rule held at banks, readings, rememberings and the turning of a year; that a collector can take a measure while a door shuts and a fist closes, and a number can reach the house without being spoken in it; that an empty shelf where a fifth would be is an absence with the shape of a presence and is not a gap; that a clean voice can take the speaker out of his own sentences and leave a room accepting the grammar; that a whole return can be announced for somebody absent and a name read that the household will not repeat; that a room learned through a doorway can be set beside three other rooms without writing a cause; that a story told in the dark leaves gaps with the shape of a vessel's intervals, and that a told bell is not a heard bell; that a house remembers by keeping objects and withholding one word; that a midwife can cover an unaware sign with a date, as a hand once went past a note turned face-down on a chart; that a thing left in place and a thing burned and called dealt with are two ways of keeping the same room; that smoke clings longer than the thing that made it; that a year is a change of handwriting on the same account and a count nobody enters is a count nobody can tax; that the hand goes to the pocket-place and comes back with wool, in past tense, and the name is not looked at.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, whose return was announced on Weighing or whose name the household would not repeat, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote three times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the further note will go, whether the officer wrote the neat upper line or only reads it, what the collector's grey fingertips mean, what goes on in the back room whose door is kept shut, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know that Dessa has put him among the bits she counts, that Mira is kin to the house three doors down, or that either woman knows more about him than she will say on a slate. The true figure is still one he has never heard spoken; the word forty has not been said in his hearing, and the word *the Breaking* has not been said at all. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf where a fifth would stand, or the space between a woman's thumb and her scar; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dust, the cords, the crusts, the intervals and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the crusts and the gruel have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides; that the five pauses are five objects of one shape and that making a sixth is not his work either; that the four rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell and the heard bell must be kept apart for the same reason; that a left thing and a burned thing are two ways of keeping, and a thing called gone may still be in the room as a smell; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerks' reasons are more dangerous than cruelties would have been because they are easier to live beside; that the things a house calls weather or mercy are the things it has entered nowhere; that Mira's wool is the only record worth keeping because the institution does not know it exists; that Dessa's count keeps something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 8

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. The unread name in a lit pocket was reached for once, on the Remembering, in past tense, and the hand came back with wool. Nine promises, no boat.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices. None returned in voice this chapter; the officer's clean report, the Court's borrowed forms and the dark told stories stood in for them. Still no tag on a remembered line.
- **Sella** --- the woman who took him and will never ask where his memory came from. She carried a fire into the house from hands along the yard and did not enter its payer; kept the mark to its hour through a reading, a night without fire, a remembering, a baking and the turn of the year; measured both ridges with one thumb and left them; burned nothing herself and let a neighbour call the grey dealt with; moved his basket without looking at his face and fed him with the warmth entered nowhere. Kindness as a wall, confirmed at Greybank and still unanswered.
- **Tarn** --- the house's debt, visibly a second ear who has kept a flat note and his own head-turn silent through five days. The knife is back where it can be checked; he said in the dark that his mother came back with somebody else's hands and held his seventh year back again. The equality between the two silences is closer than it was and still has no name on either side.
- **Jorra** --- the house's wound; slip on his forearms, dust that came back on the Remembering and was gone again by Firstday, *he's not Vail* still sealed by the boy's arrival. He speaks to pots and the wheel and shifts the damper, not to Sella about the boy, and has not once looked toward the bed this chapter.
- **Alda** --- came into the dark and told of a child returned thin, then came under the roof with birth-slates; dates stood where wonders should have stood, and the two-finger sign at his wrist was covered by a date nobody had asked for. The tin is still inside her bodice unopened, the third note still hidden, and the lie *warm and breathing* is exact.
- **Mira** --- the aunt who keeps accounts in counts, cords and grey wool and says nothing. She answered a question with a question and her own answer, touched one cord at a figure and showed a second that has never gone upriver, brought no third crust and did not look into the basket. She has not said she knows and must not.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, offstage all five days, alive, her cough still weather. She believes him dead and counts him among the bits without knowing it, will not say the number or the name, and will drown on Day 38 believing the lie. No visit to the bank may become a quest.
- **Berr Clay** --- the local face of the Court, thumb wiped clean before the slate, read of one in twelve in a voice that takes the speaker out of the sentence; now also the passive grammar behind an officer's clean reading. His careful not-calling of Garl's debt keeps it alive another year. He believes himself kind and his box stays locked.
- **The collectors, the clerk and the officer** --- the man who came for the neighbour has washed cuffs and milk-grey fingertips and took a measure from a dying person two doors down; the clerk at the market's edge laid his hand on the slate before his eyes went to the woman; the collector who took the year's first measure is a shut fist and a door; the officer at the Weighing read a whole return in a temple's voice. None is Marra and none is the man with the cold iron tube. The boy could no more address any of them than the slate under a hand.
- **The storytellers, the woman at the tubs and the neighbours** --- the told bell, the dates, the changing figure, the name left out, the grey left where it lay and burned in the morning, the thaw called weather: all passed through the yard and left no name behind. Figures carried in mouths, keepings turned down against aprons.
- **Garl** --- an absence arranged around, his ridge now gone from the corner and called dealt with, his uncalled debt still spoken around, his coat still on its peg, his seat still hollow, his name not said under the roof on the Remembering, and both proverbs still said to the pot. *Leave the crack showing.*
