## The moment

## The protagonist's condition

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and nineteen through two hundred and twenty-one days --- six months and thirty-nine days through six months and forty-one days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said four times over four districts and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard again on Grain 10 from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That a seventh green at a mouth is a colour and a seventh shape at a market's edge is a shape, and that six behind a seventh make a row and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; that a pale line is a pale line and a row of them is not a cause; that a market's arithmetic can change for two hundred people while the printed figure stands and the figure read is not the figure paid. That a saying can turn by a hair, from a general rule about pots into the report of one pot's silence, and bend back again into a general proverb, three likenesses standing on one tray and not three witnesses to one death; that a silence reported is not a second hearing of the same pot; that a wrong-wind sweetness has a place and a going and is not a cause; that the burned slate can stand at four tellings and still not be a third telling of the shut room, and four tellings are not four witnesses to one fire. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon, a noon, a basket, a yard, a market, a carrying, a morning, a midday, a dusk, a reading and a slate burning in another quarter are not openings; that a roof ending at a hand is only a board. That asking is not always answered and an answer refused is an object on its own tray; that a holding is a holding and a keeping may be maintenance and not a story; that exactness and humiliation can occupy one motion and neither be a sign; that a direction with a date is not yet a slate opened, and the market did not get to read the inch.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; the pale line on the silent woman's hand is kept on its own place beside it and not laid across it. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with eighteen answers from Day 213 through the small hours of Day 221, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named; the last kept two rhythms at once. **Beside the hearings, as a colour and a shape on places that do not touch:** the seventh green from the mouth of a silent woman at the pot board and the seventh sighting of the foundling at the market's edge, two days gathered, no eighth, no name, no joining, neither Fenna nor Ulda in the narration. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told four times on Days 205, 208, 215 and 218, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling of the shut room, advanced and not paid. **Beside the hearings, on the near-full tray:** three shapings of the pot saying --- sung early, not heard from since, gone quiet on every row --- sayings about a pot with no owner, room, cause or figure, advanced and not paid. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord tied once low with teeth and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit and the breath held under the board on Day 166, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look; Sella's finger stayed on top of the front edge through all three days of this chapter. **T065/T095 carried and advanced:** the two shut-room tellings held at two and the burned slate told four times, with no joining and no third telling.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and twenty-one days old, or six months and forty-one days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and shut her hand half over nothing on each of these three days, and her little-door burn closing on air. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room on the eleventh and twelfth, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them, though the word has stood at four. What became of the pot that sang early last month, why it is not heard from, whether any pot is gone quiet on every row come the thaw, or whether any saying has anything to do with the man carried out on Day 188; the sayings have dates in them and no owner, room, cause or figure, and no name has been given to what ailed that man. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, why he once timed a slate burning in four minutes and did not say it now, and what went on in the space between the two silences under one roof. What the stone is that Tarn took out of its pot and set on the sill in the light, or why he put it back. What ails a glaze-room hand, and what the man carried out on Day 188 cost, and whether he lived or died after he went up the clay road. Whether any count kept under any roof in this yard is about him at all. Who the soft-cuffed clerk is set for, what the waiting man from Slate Row wants with children, who the silent woman at the pot board is or whose mouth the green beside her lip once belonged to, what the foundling's green sliver is, and whether the pale line on her hand has anything to do with the pale line at the gum. **None of these is to be answered, named or joined in Chapter 57 unless the days themselves earn it.**
