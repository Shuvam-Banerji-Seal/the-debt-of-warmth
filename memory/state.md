## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: 390 through 392 days — six months and 210 through 212 days — kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; **three hundred and sixty answers** stand on the sixth place, and 366 was a whole year stood edge to edge in the only count that is exact, with no threshold anybody crosses, and he has stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers count, which is not the same as not wanting it. That a holy day arrives the way weather arrives, with a name and no tend-day and no market, and that an hour earns what comes in it and not what is coming to it — he did not bring the collector's hour into Kindling's small hours or the clean voice's reading into Weighing's. The order in which cold arrives — cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back — and the order in which a body takes heat back and gives it up, the reverse and sooner than anybody likes; that pink returning is the surface still warm and the core still cold; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, that a tourniquet let go too soon weeps into the dressing, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. That a person's face can be composed because the cold has composed it, and that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways is the rewarming he would choose and cannot ask for. That a hand at the fold's gap which closes nothing is exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; that the findings at the fold stand at thirteen and add no placing, that a keeping found where put is still a keeping however many hands find it, and that the small alarm at a double count is itself only a finding. That a district's arithmetic can be changed where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box with one figure printed and one paid and a space between that no signature fills; that twelve readings are twelve and not one account and no doctrine comes out of twelve. That a hand to the record before an eye to the person is an order a man uses until the doing has gone into the muscle and left the mind free for other work, and that a hand with no pause in it is not therefore a hand with no reason in it. That a voice can take the speaker out of his own sentences and leave a name standing empty, and that the silence after a name is a silence and not a witness, a reader, a payment or a doctrine. That the bell told from mouth to mouth is a told bell and not a heard one, that a gap in a telling is not a source standing under a sound, and that three counts in three places stay three because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stands. That a gap in a story is the shape of a vessel's interval and not the interval itself, and that a pause is not a person, learned from pots before he learned the word for it: a cracked vessel answers flat, and the flatness is a fact about the vessel and not a confession from whoever held it. That a man who files likenesses without a hand to join them and a payer to stand under them has filed nothing but likenesses. That the small satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found costs nothing, which is how he knows it is not peace.

- **Suspects, without proof and without making a figure of it.** That the house keeps by not-doings and that the keeping may be maintenance rather than mercy; that Sella's hand going toward the crust and coming back inside the wool, her half-beat at the pot's setting, her thumb at the wire, her finger beneath and then on top of the shelf, her glance at the clay jar and her hand half over nothing, her fingers a breath longer on the pot's hairline in the dark and her refusal to touch the mark are a grammar with an order and no speaker; that the year's fire coming up the yard from a source nobody names or prices is of the same order as the carried fire's unsupplied fifth and gathers toward a giver nobody here can name; that the Kindling collector's ordinary flat hand, the clean voice's clean grammar and Berr's twice-wiped thumb are hands removed from a field so that somebody else must close it, and that the belief each carries — kindness, keeping the world alive, a return recorded — is the part of each that will one day be remembered against him; that Tarn's breath at the sill, his stopping at the calling word and his stroke over the bright flagstones without the half-check belong to the loan that can hear and to a want to be called something that is not a house's possession; that Jorra's dust, his forearms parted as clot from vessel, his knowing of the full note three turns ahead and his finger's double bite in the iron belong to the room nobody names and the promise neither given nor withdrawn; that Ilva's strip in her sleeve and Hessa's turned writing up the road are keepings of the same order as the charcoal under the shelf; that the fourth direction of Stone 23 and the three older Ash movements are further shapings of one finding about a room and a slate and a man nobody names, and that the finding wants a witness who can be asked; that the form of the voice that crossed on the Long Night is a seam between the two lives and will gather interest toward a later discovery of what crossed and what did not. He suspects none of these is a sign, a cause, a payment or a rule, and has filed every suspicion on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other kept thing. The far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account is suspected, not seen, not his and not come; the far-off hand that will lay the district's private keepings together is likewise not his and not come.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with answers 121 through **three hundred and sixty** from Frost 4 through the small hours of the Long Night, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, not a freedom, with no payer named. The twelve arithmetics stand as twelve on twelve places and not one account, the finding on a place of its own with no payer and no doctrine stood up. The heard bell, the told figure, the told bell, the seventh colour, the seventh shape, the nine hands, the nine stoppings, the five askings, the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions, the fourth shaping, the Ash 6 turning and the Stone 14 turning of the writing going up stand each where put. - **Does not know.** That he is three hundred and ninety-two days old, or six months and two hundred and twelve days, or that the year has finished and the Five have begun, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no \place{Grathic} word in which to say what a life would count; 392 is exact only in the old-life register and he would not say so to anyone. Where the year's fire was first lit, what it cost to keep, or who supplies the fifth pull under the floor; why each house says it kept the flame and nobody says where the dish was filled. Who the collector was, what the measure he banked was a measure of, or why the neighbour's pale round place was not a wound; the collector's belief he did not know and would not have entered if he had, for a reason is a thing a man carries in him and not a thing standing in a room to be asked. Who spoke with the clean voice, what name was spoken once under another roof, what whole return was read or for whom, and why the household would not repeat the name; a voice at a distance is not a room entered and a name spoken is not a person produced. Whether the neighbour's story left out the same name Weighing's voice spoke, or a different one; he kept both silences on separate places. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though Sella glanced at it and shut her hand half over nothing on all three days, and her little-door burn closing on air. What the widow's mark mended, why the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, or why a collection, a reading and a holy day put no hand on it; he has seen the acts and set them down as maintenance on a vessel, not as signs or payments. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6 or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reached a finger beneath it for one counted breath on Day 166 and for two on Frost 5 and kept her finger on top of the shelf through the Five**, and what she counts under her breath at the fire. Why Sella's thumb stayed one breath longer at the wire crossing on Ash 9 and again in the dark of the Long Night, why Tarn's hand stayed a breath longer at the knife-sill on Ash 14, why Jorra left his bent finger a breath longer in the cold iron on Ash 26, why Sella tucked the wool into the crust's empty place on Stone 15 and then only found it there, and whose hand went around the wrongly set pot on Stone 5 — whether any meant anything, whether any was a habit of the hand or a thing a man would do again, and who might one day read them; he has the intervals and their edges and nothing else, made no payer of them and no lesson. Whether Hessa will ever read what she carries up the road, whether Ilva's notches hold a count anybody will ask for, whether the burned slate will be told a sixth time, whether the promise under the wheel-shed will be called in, whether the man who went in on Seed 15 will be found, whether the fifth burned-slate telling and the four directions will get an owner, a room or a witness, whether the second chill's cause will be named, and whether the waiting man from Slate Row will one day stand somewhere and be asked. He does not know that the Remembering, the Last Day and Ilmen 1 stand beyond the dark, or that a new year's first arithmetic waits on Ilmen 10; nothing under the roof has said so. He does not try to bring the rest of the remembered white room across for the sake of two sentences, because a man who does that will end by putting a payer under both lives and none stood under either.

## What Chapter 114 must not forget
