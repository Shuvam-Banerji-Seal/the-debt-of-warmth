## The moment

Day 275, Frost 6, Sixthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room whose floor does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from it; the empty place beside the crust keeps its name to itself and no third came in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth has frozen in the dry heat and is unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waits beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house will perform. He knows the composed face that is not composure, knows a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wants the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both are true and the second is louder; the surgeon takes the inventory and enters it nowhere, not even on the place where he keeps the things he does not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now cannot close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-five days, and nobody lifts the floor and nobody supplies the pull; overhead \place{the boards} keep four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rises into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stands to any. No fire-sitter stands over the coals and no house count comes to be borrowed. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on the last market-day of Smoke, stays in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it is not brought under this roof, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. A woman's low counting comes from the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy takes only what the hand can take and leaves the letting-go where it was.

At the close he has laid the hand down against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. On the hundred and twenty-sixth answer it went up an inch toward the river's note, took the small intake and hold of a count from the room whose floor does not touch his, stayed past its letting-go and through the little silence after it, set the held interval and the empty interval side by side with a finger between, borrowed no house count, supplied no fifth to the kiln, crossed no silence that did not belong to the river, took nothing from the two-gapped row, the charcoal under the shelf or the covered grain at the rack end, and came down on the river's next low note. The held interval and the empty interval are two objects on two places with the finger still between them, and neither was made to agree with the fifth missing under the floor, for agreeing any two would want a payer and none stands. He made nothing of the week's small movements and would not let any stand up as a gift, a freedom or a claim; the restraint was the work and he did not promote it. **One hundred and twenty-six** honest answers stand on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The file lies open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved.

Two hundred and seventy-five stands exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-five days are told by nobody; the fire's fifth is unpaid the same number of days. \lore{The widow's mark} was touched once before the kiln on Frost 2, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, hearth, afternoon, dusk or going of the light on Frost 4, 5 or 6, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Sixthday; only the hand was placed down, forward and unresolved.

## The protagonist's condition

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and seventy-three through two hundred and seventy-five days --- six months and ninety-three through six months and ninety-five days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said at six readings from Grain 10 through Smoke 30 and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard at every reading over the same basket from the same man, and called a kindness. That a district's arithmetic can be changed in a square where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box to two hundred tables with two figures in it, one printed and one paid, and a space between them nobody signed; that the reading knows the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing, and that a thing covered is not therefore paid for; that a figure read a third time is no nearer the thing under the cover than a chart laid over a covered body is nearer the wound beneath it, and that three readings are three and not one account. That an arm can know what a handful weighs and not what it cost, and a slate can know the figure and not the grain. That a thing can be examined as closely as a hand can examine it and still enter nothing --- the grain turned beneath the wool and set down where it lay --- and that a month and its first firing can go out having taught nobody anything from an act and keep the nothing an exact finding. That an opening is a kiln mouth uncovered and a fire taken, that a surgeon may see a thing mended and call it nothing, and that four openings in four months are four and not a rule. That a hand may take the intake and hold of a count from the next room without taking its letting-go, may hold two intakes apart with a finger between, may end before a letting-go and leave it where it was, and may lay a held interval and an empty interval side by side without either becoming a claim. That a house may change by two held breaths and say nothing, and that a change seen and kept is not therefore a cause, a gift or a doctrine. That a wrong wind's sweet edge is a direction arrived nowhere, and that a wrapped foot going cold before the core has no word in Grathic and buys no cure.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; the pale line on the silent woman's hand is kept on its own place beside it and not laid across it. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with one hundred and twenty-one through one hundred and twenty-six answers from Frost 4 through the small hours of Frost 6, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, not a freedom, with no payer named; 121 the small intake taken and down on the next note, no house count borrowed; 122 the intake and hold of a counted breath with the hand down before the letting-go, the little silence left whole and empty; 123 held past a count's letting-go and through the silence, one house rhythm carried across the interval; 124 two intakes held apart with a finger between, the letting-go between them left where it was; 125 the hand down on the river's note while the count was still letting go, a separation made by ending early; 126 the hand held past the letting-go and through the silence, the held interval and the empty interval laid side by side with a finger between and neither made to agree with the fifth under the floor, nothing taken from the row, the charcoal or the grain. The house's count stays on its own place, the river's notes on another, the empty interval on a third and the bell's gap in the square on a fourth, none promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom or a rule, and the house's not-doings kept on a place of their own with his own gaps on another and a finger between. The finding that an opening is an opening and that the not-making of it is a finding with no payer stays on its own place with a finger between it and the house's not-doings and his own gaps, and the returned held beat stays on the shelf's own place beside the older held changes, not a fourth likeness and not a doctrine. **A seventh place:** the **five** askings of the ownerless question with the one further shaping beside them, a finger between each pair, no witness standing to be asked and no row named.

- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and seventy-five days old, or six months and ninety-five days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though Sella has glanced at it and shut her hand half over nothing on these mornings, and her little-door burn closing on air. What the widow's mark mended, why the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, or why the first firing of Frost came on Frost 2 and not on the turning of the month; he has seen the act and set it down as maintenance on a vessel, not as a sign or a payment, and did not look at it again afterward. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them, though the word has stood at four. He suspects nothing that stands up as a cause; his not-knowing is exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.
