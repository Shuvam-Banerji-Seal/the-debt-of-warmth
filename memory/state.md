## The moment

Day 172, Tallow 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and seventy-two days old at the close of Day 172 and five months and twenty-two days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the bare trough; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth, the kindling and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room never entered); and up and down the clay road at the going of the light. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze days ago and was left unwiped on all three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its instruction; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down. Nothing within two paces can be reached --- not the crust two inches from his ear, the pot handle set out beyond his hand, the charcoal under the shelf or any person who could tell him a figure. The dry heat of the last tend has gone leathery into the crust and lifts the oiled paper at the windows; want comes up small and importunate for the crust and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to the fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, a reed-cutter straightening his cord before lifting his trap, Sella reaching a finger beneath the shelf before her eyes went to the row, Sella's glance at the clay jar before anything else, a Sill assessor putting the pigs' figures on a slate with hand to slate before eyes to the rail, and Hessa Rale settling her strap and turning her wrist before her eyes took in the yard; Hessa's reaching was filed as the eighth such hand in the district on a place of its own and joined to none, and the square's doing of it on the first Account-day was counted as nine, with Berr's twice-wiped thumb and finger laid on a figure set among them. Hessa, Berr and the other Weir and Cross figures are offstage in this chapter and their trays stand as they stood. This chapter added: that a hand held back from the iron is still a hand that knows where the iron stands, and that when it happens a second and third time it is not the same change twice over but a holding; that a hand kept away from a board on another morning is a second state of the same woman and must not stand in for the first; that a stroke checked for half a beat behind a standing finger, with both men looking elsewhere, widens the room an account cannot enter without settling any of it; that a thing watched closely enough gives up its order before it gives up its name, and an order without a name is not a thing a man can carry into a chart; that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, and that buying nothing at two paces is still knowing exactly; that three held changes on three places must not be permitted to stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer; that a clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine; that the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody will perform, and something must give at the end of the seventh year though the end is not here; and that an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand, and the man who cannot lift the instrument is still the man who knows what it is for. The double consciousness stays a burden and not an advantage: the surgeon's register keeps out of the way of the field as a man keeps a hand off a field he cannot enter.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and seventy through one hundred and seventy-two days --- five months and twenty days through five months and twenty-two days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That an opening is a thing done and gone past, that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back, that two records made by two hands are not one account, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, that a hand kept away from the board on another morning is a second state and not the first repeated, that a hand that stays a beat longer than the setting needs tells what the withdrawing hand cannot, that a clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine, that a margin is a margin and not a key, that a finding is a finding and not a hand, that a thing watched closely gives up its order before its name, and that three likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing and wants an answer badly enough. That a stroke checked for half a beat is the small distance between a hand's intention and a hand's arrest, measurable without a clock and worth nothing once written into the body of a note; that two things can be held at once under one roof without either man asking to be told what the other holds, and that the holding is the whole of it. That he is the only adult under this roof and the least use in it.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause (T097 advanced, unpaid). **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, tied once low with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit, and on Day 166 the finger held under the board for one counted breath, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look; on Days 170–172 the finger stayed away and the second state was kept apart from the first. **T062/T061/T082 carried and advanced, unpaid:** three held changes --- the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 held beat beneath the loaf shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- on three places touching neither, three held changes and not a pair, still less a doctrine, with no payer named and no word spoken over any of them. **T080 carried and unpaid:** Hessa's record-first order of reaching filed as the eighth such hand in the district, joined to none; the bell four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the heard bell beside the told; the man in Mud End quiet as a lamb and given no figure; the number from a fist arriving nowhere; a soft-cuffed clerk set for an uncalled measure and the girl with a creel taking the landing sideways without a fall. Eight such hands, then nine in the square, and no likeness between them made a cause; shapes only, every one.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and seventy-two days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it for a hundred and seventy-two days, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing. Who took the pan that stood out to dry through Reed, when, why, or whether the not-saying would have been the same had a woman set it back --- the round ring is all that is left and the house went around it again on the going of the light on Day 172. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath** and on Days 170–172 kept it away, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144, 147, 151, 157, 160 and 166 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 152, 159, 163, 164, 165, 166, 167, 168, 169, 170, 171 and 172. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, and what went on in the space between the finger standing in the iron and Tarn's half-beat on the flagstones; what the warm stone in the loft cost, and why it is kept cold, and why he has not looked at it in a year. Which house holds the floorboard under Alda's sixth page, and what the second hand says. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. What a Grathic word for both mercies of the damper would be, if one existed. Why a number spoken in the square is not the number later paid, and whether the figure in Berr's box was ever meant to be read by anyone who would know what it counted. Whether the sweetish edge on the wrong wind is the same thing that took the man in Mud End, and what a pot singing before its time is a sign of; he has the order of it and no name, and an order without a name is not a thing a man can carry into a chart. What will give at the end of the seventh year, and when the end will come; he knows only that it is not here.
