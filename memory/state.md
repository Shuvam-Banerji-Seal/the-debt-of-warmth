## The moment

Day 224, Grain 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, \person[Corin]{the boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear, one leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from that ear and its empty place still unnamed, no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform. Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-four days; overhead the boards keep four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rises up and becomes the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would put a payer under all three and no payer stands to any. The fire goes on in the room that warms him and the river in the room he cannot enter, and neither stops for the ear under the roof.

At the close he has put the question to his hand and been answered. Sella went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there is no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that comes through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof; the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed; he took it through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of those counted breaths, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended and brought it down on the river's next low note. Two rhythms, one held against the other across a wall that does not touch, remembered, not a change made again. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and twenty-two through two hundred and twenty-four days --- six months and forty-two days through six months and forty-four days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said four times over four districts and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard on Grain 10 from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That a seventh green at a mouth is a colour and a seventh shape at a market's edge is a shape, and that six behind a seventh make a row and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; that a pale line is a pale line and a row of them is not a cause; that a market's arithmetic can change for two hundred people while the printed figure stands and the figure read is not the figure paid. That a saying can turn by a hair, from a general rule about pots into the report of one pot's silence, and bend back again into a general proverb, three likenesses standing on one tray and none of them a witness to one death. That a cost asked as a direction is answered with the pot and the weather and arrives nowhere, and that a number brought up out of a fist and put back into a sleeve is a number not paid and not a number kept back. That a field gives back what the fist will not count and the carrying arm knows weight and not cost, and that a bundle covered and gone around is kept and not entered. That a place gone around with a look and a wide step is kept and not counted, and a man can go around it a whole season without turning it into a cause. That a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stands under the absences; that five walked-around things standing in a row are still five and not an account. That a voice from a direction is not a witness and a word about a pot is not a word about a person; that a half-beat at the setting of a pot is an interval kept and not a promise, a key or an opening; that the two rooms do not touch and the breath counted in the other room is kept from memory and not from hearing. The surgery of it all is exact and the reach is two inches short.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; the pale line on the silent woman's hand is kept on its own place beside it and not laid across it. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with twenty-four answers from Day 213 through the small hours of Day 224, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named; the last kept two rhythms at once across a wall that does not touch. **Beside the hearings, as a colour and a shape on places that do not touch:** the seventh green from the mouth of a silent woman at the pot board and the seventh sighting of the foundling at the market's edge, gathered on Day 219 and carried unjoined, no eighth, no name, no joining, neither Fenna nor Ulda in the narration. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told four times on Days 205, 208, 215 and 218, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling of the shut room, advanced and not paid. **Beside the hearings, on the near-full tray:** three shapings of the pot saying --- sung early, not heard from since, gone quiet on every row --- sayings about a pot with no owner, room, cause or figure, advanced and not paid. **Under the roof, covered:** the year's first cut grain, set near the hearth under a fold of wool, counted in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, entered on no slate and given no figure. **T098 carried, advanced and NOT paid:** the returning-line place gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, no name, cause, body, figure or payer, held for the far-off figure, and never a third gap or a fourth likeness made a cause. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord tied once low with teeth and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed forty and the little tin and pewter tin three doors down; no figure, no reader. **T100 carried and unpaid:** the charcoal tally beneath the loaf shelf, unread, uncounted, uncopied, with the finger on top of the front edge and the one held beat of Day 166 on its own place.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and twenty-four days old, or six months and forty-four days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and shut her hand half over nothing on each of these three days, and her little-door burn closing on air. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room on all three mornings, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them, though the word has stood at four. What became of the pot that sang early last month, why it is not heard from, whether any pot is gone quiet on every row come the thaw, or whether any saying has anything to do with the man carried out on Day 188; the sayings have dates in them and no owner, room, cause or figure, and no name has been given to what ailed that man. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, why he once timed a slate burning in four minutes and did not say it now, and what went on in the space between the two silences under one roof. What the stone is that Tarn took out of its pot and set on the sill in the winter Garl died, or why it went back unreconciled. What Garl's tile debt was for, who was sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, and what the half-beat under the board, the unchecking stroke over the bright patch, the scrape in the loft and the held inch would mean if a payer stood under them; he has named none and joined none. **Which hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, whether it is alive or dead, whether an accident took it, whether any body will be found, and what the month cost:** T098 advanced and not paid, kept open for the far-off figure and never filled in to make a row come out even. Whether the grain that came home knows anything of the place gone around in the line, and he has not laid the bundle across it. Why the half-beat at the setting of a pot is longer than the setting requires, and whether Sella has ever looked toward the bed at the setting; he has filed the thing done and the thing withheld in one motion and named no payer to it. That the figure read twice over the basket on Grain 10 is not the figure paid, though nobody under the roof will say so; what the true subtraction in Berr's box actually is, and what Hessa's slate carries that she will not read. That there is a Warm, that he is a vessel with a flaw, that he was a man in another world with a daughter and a name he cannot speak here, and that any of these could be entered on a slate: the old life has stayed offstage and he has not said a word of it under this roof.
