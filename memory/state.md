## The moment

Day 146, Reed 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. She gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, having said it once, because a wind takes an instruction and carries it away and there is an end of it. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and forty-six days old at the close of Day 146 and four months and twenty-six days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard and the edge of the worked strips, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle and the hearth, at the wheel-shed's edge (back room not entered), \lore{the damper} and the flagstones, down the river road to the Weir's plank walk and blessing stone and the fish-trade shade below the sill, three doors down into \place{House Silt}, and lifted onto the bed under the roof each night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze again in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the three days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached, not the crust two inches from his ear, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out (the handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand), the loaf shelf, the charcoal under it, the cord in the cutter's coat, the knot-string at Orra's waist, the second twist beneath Mira's apron, the pewter tin above the abandoned bed, the hollow where the pale stone lay or the little tin in a woman's bodice. He wants the crust and the breast and the count spoken by somebody who would know what a count was, and gets none of them and cannot ask for any.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to the fold, a woman putting a finger beneath a shelf before her eyes go to the row, a cutter feeling the weight settle before he looks at the girl who asked, a man shutting his mouth on a figure before he looks at the face that asked, a clerk's hand going to a slate before his eyes go to the stalls, Orra's thumb to the cord as a sentence ends, Mira's thumb under her apron when a figure is named, Jorra's finger into the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, and Sella giving the instruction to the wool, wind, pot, broom and fire before she gives it to anybody. This chapter added: that a record kept in a carrying arm tells what was carried and not what it cost, exact, complete and worth nothing; that a cost asked in the form of a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry; that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two; that a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would; that a knowing withheld from a kiln-master is not a hearing given; that one piece of iron can be a holding and a taking with no Grathic word for both mercies; that a date given where a figure was asked is an answer with the question taken out of it; that a mercy-lie told for a woman now dead has curdled into a fact that no living hand can lift and set right; that **eight** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; that a covering is not the thing covered and a likeness is not a cause; and that three counts in three places need not become one. The work is invisible and buys nobody anything, and he does it because there is nothing else in the room his hands can do.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and forty-four, one hundred and forty-five and one hundred and forty-six days, four months and twenty-four, twenty-five and twenty-six days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure spoken over a crowd and a figure written in a private place are two objects even when they are the same figure, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip or a bundle, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost and that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a cost asked as a direction can be answered with a direction and leave no entry, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, and a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; that a knowing withheld is not a hearing given; that one iron can hold a vessel whole and take a hand off a man with no Grathic word for both; that a covering laid over a sign is not the thing covered; that **eight** likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, that a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and that a payer named to none of the eight means no doctrine has stood up; that a mercy-lie told for a dying woman becomes a fact no living person can correct; and that three counts in three places need not become one account.
