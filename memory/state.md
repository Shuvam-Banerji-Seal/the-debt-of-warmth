## The protagonist's condition

- **Corin/Dan** — on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear, one crust with its corner hair-lifted two inches off and gone leathery past the point where a child would still eat it, the empty place beside it holding the fold put there once on the fifteenth and found where put through Ilmen 17, 18 and 19, still without a name and with no third and no fourth crust, the thread frozen and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and bare wrist-joint and the afterdrop waiting. Awake, silent, hand outside the wool having just let a small intake go whole on the river's note under its winter skin and laid itself back against the fold on the **four hundred and second** answer — exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air and filed on the sixth place with no payer named, not spent or explained, the sixth answer in a row and not one of them a doctrine. Four hundred and thirteen days old and six months and two hundred and thirty-three days, told by nobody that a number has been reached. Wants the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted; gets none and cannot ask for any. Holds six hearings with no seventh and the Drawing's knock outside, the heard bell beside the told with both gaps in their own squares, nine record-first hands and nine stoppings with no tenth, the fifth shaping on its own tray between the eighth hand and the road saying, the clerk's entered figure on its own place with a finger between it and the degree and another between it and the face at the yard's edge, Berr's low saying and his securely wiped thumb each on a place of its own, the man from Slate Row's face filed and not counted among the hands, the green at the pot board and the shape at the market's edge with no eighth of either, twelve arithmetics behind one unsigned figure beside the first of a fresh count with no key to Berr's box, the figure read over covered grain that does not know its cost, the silence after Weighing's name, the Long Night story's empty name, the clean voice's missing name and the place the Remembering did not fill on the gap tray, the called-gone grey beside the left-in-place ridge, the seven walked-around places with a wide step that is a keeping and not a sign, and the keeping at the fold found where put and still a finding.

- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire — under the roof at the close after the going of the light, her hands never still when they had nothing to do, counting spoons, nails and the breaths of a child nobody had numbered and coming back to the hairline at the wire; what she said low went to the pot and the fire, and when she spoke of the boy it was *the boy* and when she spoke of what she meant to do it was *the house*, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. On all three days she went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, turned them with the back of a broken pot, gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening with neither gap figured, kept her finger along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread, moved her lips on a two-gapped row unfigured, glanced once at the clay jar with the hand half over nothing and the little-door burn closing on air, and did not touch the widow's mark. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, left the hairline showing, said both proverbs to pot and fire and never to a person, turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and no figure or handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. She carried him down and up the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place, set him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold, and went back to her fire saying nothing at all — not of the word that came up at the well, not of the figure entered before a face, not of the arithmetic up the road, not of the pot's crack. She wants the house kept as a house is kept and the fire kept as a fire is kept, with nothing entered and no door drawn across a tend.

- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand — at the kindling and flagstones on all three days, offstage at the close and found where put. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}; the stack kept its own arithmetic of four, four and a space, and the space was not a fifth and was not filled. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach — the coming away the whole of the checking. He cut nothing, counted nothing, spent nothing, and said to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, *Six counted. The next I don't count* on the seventeenth and again on the nineteenth; on the eighteenth he said nothing at all to the wood, and the boy filed the silence on a place of its own, a change found where put and not made a habit. He took the broom only after the flagstones had been given their instruction and said *Yes, house* to the question and to the thing behind it, agreeing so thoroughly the question had nowhere left to put a foot, and went over the stones without a word and without a turn of the head toward the landing. His chest had been tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was, his stroke over the flagstones going without the half-check; he was helpful when he was frightened and on these days he was not frightened, and the household read the quiet of him the way it read the quiet of Sella. He owns nothing and knows where every small thing is. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there: a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, no figure and no name. The \emph{Seed 16} breath was drawn and left unspent at the top of the reach; the seventh year had run to its very end and the term was already ended, which is exposure and not freedom, with the fear of being entered again for vagrancy his without a figure and a want he did not voice for a slate with his own name and no mark against it. His head went a degree toward the landing on Ilmen 3 and back to the broom without a word, and the degree stayed a shape found where put and **was not repeated on Ilmen 17, 18 or 19**; the warm stone in its pot in the loft stays unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving. He wants the loan never called and a slate with his name and no mark against it, and does not turn his head toward the landing to ask for either.

- **\person{Jorra Vail}**, 16 — at the Vail wheel-shed edge and the cold damper on all three days, offstage at the close and found where put. He came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel; three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — and told a pot *You'll do* in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence; he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no \place{Grathic} word covered both mercies; he took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of \emph{Ash 26} a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. *He's not Vail* stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise under the shed was neither given nor withdrawn while the burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two and the four ownerless directions apart and unspent. He wants the wheel kept steady and the note kept back, and gives no name to the dust.

- **\person{Garl Vail}** — absent four hundred and thirteen days and the shape of most of the house, kept in objects and not in a story on all three days: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door; the hollow wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow on that side that nobody is allowed to fill; the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom has gone in two years; the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the original break between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person; the unforged collector sentence about the man who turned back at the frozen ford lying beside the seventh notch at the kindling with the knife at the sill, and \lore{the tile debt} against a firing of tiles circling without a name and worse for being uncalled. His name has not been spoken under this roof in four hundred and thirteen days and the two counts — his name and the fire's fifth — stand side by side without becoming one; the keeping of him is maintenance and not a story, no lesson was made of any of it and his fifth pull under the floor is still unsupplied. ### In the yards, on the road, and farther off

- **\person{Berr Clay} (gone up the road; not on stage in these three days).** He went up the clay road toward High Kiln on Ilmen 10 with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat, the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner, the smell of nothing at all under the hot clay of him, and the narrow slate under his arm with the first true subtraction of Y.H. 814 inside the locked box. He wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, held the slate unopened to his forearm, passed a finger over the figure through his sleeve, read low *One in twelve, as the notice says* and sent it twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never *I*, taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on. To a man who brought a number up out of a shut fist he did not give the number back; the man was sent to a slate at the watch and the number went from fist to mouth to shoulder and arrived nowhere. Something low he said to the block about kindness being entered, with no anger and no triumph and no payer standing; it went into the stone and stayed there as a stain stays in a board. He wants the district's arithmetic to stand as entered and his own hands to have been withdrawn from it; his belief in the kindness is the part of him that will one day be remembered against him.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, the half-beat lying in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. ### The Account-days and the world asleep

The district's arithmetic changed for the twelfth time on Stone 30 (Day 389), cannot be changed back, and began again on Ilmen 10 (Day 404) with the first arithmetic of Y.H. 814, which also cannot be undone. The printed one in twelve stands unchallenged over the basket with the figure read still not the figure paid, and Stone's last subtraction and the year's first went up the clay road locked in Berr's box beside the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone and Ash and Stone's first and second. **Twelve arithmetics stand behind one unsigned figure, twelve and not one account, and the first of the new year stands beside them as one reading and not an account, not a thirteenth**, the finding on a place of its own with no payer standing and no doctrine out of twelve or out of one. The Five are over: they were holy days with names and no tend-days and no market, no thirteenth arithmetic was brought into them and the district's figures were not altered by one mark on any of them. The year turned on Ilmen 1 with nothing signed, no boundary drawn and no door across the morning; the reset supplied no payer and did not stand up as a signature. Ilmen 5 through 19 have passed with no boundary drawn and no week made a threshold; Ilmen 10 was the first Account-day and market of the new year, Ilmen 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18 and 19 were ordinary tend-days of the second working week, and Ilmen 20 stands one morning off. Far-off in the dark: Marra is not in Sill; the foundling's seventh half-turn gathers toward a sale years away; Alda's mercy-lie has curdled into furniture; the Waiting Room clerk takes small secrets for loaves and ale; Rennick is already asking why forty unspent measures are needed for one intact one; Solene Ember removes a ring one at a time in a room with good light and does not know the boy exists; Hald Iron reads Haldre's paper from a tin, and the Fortnight Book's tally at the year's turn stays unreconciled.
