# State of things — at the close of chapter 9 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 10. Days 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36 and 37 have been lived, one to a dated entry: seven days of a thaw that comes on in small things and is called weather by everyone who can taste it; the boy thirty-one days old at the well and a month old on the second morning and thirty-seven at the close; a knife announced to an empty yard, grey slate dust from a room nobody enters, a fourth hidden note laid under a date, a pot that answers nearly full under nobody's knuckle, a hairline in the ice of the Ilm, a number nobody gives, and a woman at the end of nineteen steps whose cough is worse and still called weather. Five hearings of the flat note still stand at five from four sources and no sixth; the nearly full answer is not among them; the five pauses do not join; the told bell is still beside and never on the heard bell. ## The moment

Day 37 is done. On Ilmen 8, Eighthday, Y.H. 813, at dusk, \person{Sella Vail} carried the boy up \place{the Clay Yard} under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof and set him on his bed, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. He is \person{Corin}, thirty-seven days old, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, with the fold at his ear and the two crusts in it, neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third. The house went about its evening as it had gone about all seven days: \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept its broader shape on its peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years and nobody filled it, the proverb went to the pot and not to the basket, and the clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} had turned its corner for a year and two days held nothing and was not swept. Sella turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a bank is not an opening; the fire that came up the yard on \lore{Kindling} was still under blind ash and the back of the broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap.

Earlier that day Sella had set him in the shade of the doorway at the end of nineteen steps below \place{House Silt}, a pot's place chosen with her knee and not her eye. \person{Dessa Silt} stood there with her shoulder against the jamb as though the jamb were the only thing holding her up, slip-grey nails, wet clay dried at her hem, the cough out of the pits lower and shorter than it had been on Stone 28 and still called weather. She put the nineteen out and the nineteen back into her thumb, laid out four loaves on the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve, three to the levy, one eaten with her feet up and bits to the woman who came with a basket, drew her thumb once over the pale sieve-wire scar and brought the door-shut smile down over her face. *A body can be warm and still be gone. You learn that in the pits.* She did not look into the basket going past her shade and did not say the number or the name. \person{Mira Silt} came up from the eels and passed her sister without looking at her either; when Dessa said the figure of the loaves her thumb went under her apron to the grey cord and the second cord showed twisted twice beneath it, never taken upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door, neither counted. She brought no crust. Behind the drying racks \place{the Slip Brook} ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should, under a thin film that no longer held. As Sella lifted the basket past the doorway, Dessa's hand came off the jamb and went to the wicker, the thumb along the edge, the small quick grip of a woman who had carried clay up nineteen steps and children down them, and stayed the space of one breath and let go. Neither woman looked at the other. The boy kept the shape where he kept the things that had no hour yet. He did not know it was the detail he would be given to keep and never to spend.

The count went on exactly through all seven days while the household kept its number badly in fists and mouths and weather. Nothing had been joined. Five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; the nearly full answer of Day 35 filed on its own tray as an answer to an interval; the told bell beside but not on the heard bell; the clean dark line where the ridge had been; the silence where a name should stand; five pauses lying side by side and not joined; the well-roof's stone that does not ring, the missing step at \place{the Fishstairs}, the hairline in the ice and the higher brook kept each in its own place. The district had entered a year on its slates and had not entered him, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth behind him and came down.

Day count: **Day 37 complete.** Day 38 is Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813, Ninthday, and opens chapter 10. No day was skipped and no day was poured into another.

Dessa stood at the end of them, failing visibly, her cough worse and still weather; she got specific about nineteen steps, four loaves, three to the levy and bits to the woman with a basket, drew her thumb over the sieve-wire scar, shut her smile and kept the nineteen in her thumb to hold a larger thing out of the room. She did not look into the basket or say the number or the name. Mira passed her sister without looking and answered Sella's silence with her own answer; the two not-lookings had the same shape and were opposite things and the boy would not let them touch. The Slip Brook ran clay-heavy and a little high. Dessa's thumb went to the wicker for one breath, and the grip reached toward a hand in a grey sleeve going into a coat pocket for a small cold rectangle of light with a name on it, a boat on cold water nine promises old, the name unlooked-at, the hand coming back with wool; nothing crossed into the basket that a baby's hand could carry. At dusk he was carried up the yard under the roof, thirty-seven days old, with the two crusts in the fold and no third.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, thirty-seven days old at the close of Day 37. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: in the basket at Sella's hip, set at the edge of the racks or in the shade of a doorway where a pot would not be slopped, lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into \place{the Debt Loft}. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth freezes in the small hours and nobody wipes it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The head goes where it wants and makes a small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. The blanket will not stay on. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the oiled paper at the Vail window has lifted less hard every morning of the thaw. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 34 without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with a date. He wants the breast and wants the crust even cold, and gets neither; the old crust is stiff past smelling of anything, the newer still gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns, and both lie in the fold, uneaten, unremoved, unnamed, with no third in all seven days.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired a week's worth of new objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, a woman who kept him and Berr passing a finger over a figure before he touched nothing. He knows that a thing that does not happen leaves a space with the shape of a sound; that an interval is not a hearing, a gap in a story is the same kind of object as a gap in a vessel's breath, and a pause is not a hearing either; and that the trays must not touch. This chapter added that almost is its own kind of note, not a failure of the full one: nearly full has air under it and a want in it, and he learned the difference between nearly and full before he knew that he knew it. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he has kept the honeyed ice, the hairline, the higher brook, the missing step, the told bell, the sign and its covering date, the two not-lookings and the four rooms apart on purpose. He knows that a district can taste a change in its water and give it a word that lets everybody go home without owning the day; that a number kept in a fist or passed mouth to shoulder is a number nobody has given; that a woman going thin arranges her face in the ordinary way and calls the evidence weather; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. He is the only adult in every room and the least use in it; correctness is still the only thing he can offer nobody.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the boards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the bell's four, four and a gap; the Fishstairs' four, four and a landing; the kindling's four, four and a space; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the prints of the man from the river, four and four and a slide; the hairline in the ice that stops short of the edge; the firelight's four, four and an empty shelf; the told bell's four, four and a gap; the officer's four measures, four measures and a missing closing line; the well-platform's four rafters, four, and a stone that does not ring. The ear holds \lore{the full note} in five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and this chapter added no sixth. The nearly full answer of Day 35 is a third sound, neither the flat answer nor a silence, and is filed as the first earthly answer to the Stone 13 interval, advanced and not paid; there is no Grathic word for nearly and no hand to tap the pot and ask it to say the thing again. The intervals and gaps on their own trays are: the pot turned toward on the bench that gave nothing, the half-heard note that went past the door on Day 16, the pot that cracked in the yard on Day 19, the empty fifth in the kindling firelight, the silence after the Weighing's name, the silence where the house's dead man's name should stand, the three told gaps from the Long Night (question-to-answer, a name left out, a story finished with a date), the officer's missing closing line, and the Court's room's missing line, all filed beside but never among the hearings. There are still five sightings of the pause in the neat dark hand (the flare, the birth-slate, the square's slate on Stone 10, the Court's table on Stone 20, the market's edge on Stone 30) lying side by side and unjoined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause stays filed apart; Berr's twice-wiped thumb is filed with the record-first hands but not called the same hand as any of them. Five pauses lie unjoined. No rank, no word, no hand, no answer has been bought by seven days of keeping.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 is still there, stiff and gone past smelling of anything; the fresh crust from Mira, given on Day 22, still gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns; both lie in the fold, uneaten, unlifted, unnamed, and no third came in seven days though bread is in the house. **Held/unchanged:** the small pale river stone remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in \place{House Silt}, unmentioned; the small bench knife is back on its sill, announced unasked on every morning of the seven days; the charred slate still lies in the kiln ash, black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark; the basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word; Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched once on Day 34 and never opened, and a fourth narrow slate is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name; Sella's private roll is unshown; Berr's box is locked in his house; Mira's grey wool cord and the second cord twisted twice beneath it have knots uncounted; the \lore{Ash} ridge is gone from the Vail corner, burned on Ilmen 1 and called dealt with, and both of Garl's proverbs are still said to the pot. **Filed but not possessed:** the honeyed ice, the hairline that stops short of the edge, the nearly full note, the higher clay-heavy brook, Dessa's nineteen in her thumb, the two not-lookings, the thumb on the wicker and the unsent sentence it reached toward, Jorra's grey dust, the cracked pot, the carried firelight's empty shelf, the told bell, and the silence where a name should be are on separate trays in his private file. **Money:** none to the boy and none changes hands to him. Figures go past him and are not his to spend: one in twelve printed and unchallenged, a figure shut in a fist at the well and passed around as a shape, Berr's figure on a narrow slate that goes into a slate and not into a well, the Singing Sill's low note called weather, a levy that falls in the thaw, Dessa's nineteen steps and four loaves, three to the levy and bits to the woman with a basket. The gruel, the fire, the basket, the wool and both crusts remain entered nowhere; every kindness has a payer who can be named and a recipient who cannot refuse it.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was reached for once this chapter, on Day 37, in past tense and untagged: the shape of a woman's thumb on a basket handle went into a coat pocket for a small cold rectangle of light with a name on it, and the name was not looked at, and the hand came back with wool. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature stood behind that reaching; the flare light and the hand that stopped between two clusters had made a fifth sighting on Stone 30 and have not made a sixth. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a failure of the system, were recognised again in Berr's passive sentences and the district's grammar of weather. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one; Des's laugh did not return. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear and the two crusts in it, awake, silent, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. Wants the breast, wants the crust, wants a word to correct the room; gets none of the three and cannot ask for any. His count goes on into a year already entered on the district's slates. - **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the fire and the bed, having banked the coals without touching the mark. She touched the widow's mark before the kiln opening on Day 35, held it one breath, and has not touched it since, not at a bank, a reading, a visit from Alda, a dusk or the carrying of a basket. She carried him three doors down on Day 37, chose the shade with her knee, set him down without looking into it and gave the instruction to the wool. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained; the thaw coming on in plain sight has made that harder without giving her anyone to answer.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- in the yard and under the roof, broom set against the wall, small knife back on its sill and announced to the empty yard on every morning of the seven days. Six years of the seven are counted; he will not count the seventh, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifts; his broom checks half a stroke where no note has come. He turned his head a degree toward a pot that answered nearly full in open light on Day 35 and swept on without naming it, and did not look toward the basket. He agrees with everything so thoroughly that an argument has nowhere to stand, and told the boards that late was his, a longer week before the paying; the ice is going against him and he goes on sweeping. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist and the mark worn almost flat on the knife handle are his own records. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and grudges the house the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence has never been said in the house and lies in it all the same.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- in and out of the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms on most mornings, and on Day 33 fine grey slate dust worked into the creases past the bend of his little finger, which he said nothing about. He knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it. He has not once looked toward the bed. The room off the shed keeps its finger's width of open and nobody pushes it wider. Still wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself. The sentence *he's not Vail* is as sealed as on the hour it was spoken; the broken slates under the drying rack and the men whose names are not spoken remain unshown.

His warmth lies where it fell, the coat keeps his broad shape on the peg, the seat keeps his twenty-year leftward lean, and the debt against a firing of tiles remains neither paid nor settled nor done with. ## Open dangers

- The district can taste a change in its water and will not say the word for it; the oiled paper sags, the Slip Brook has lost its stiff skin, the Singing Sill gives its low note early, the Ilm shows a hairline that stops short of the edge, and the brook behind the racks runs clay-heavy a little higher than it should. Half the yard wants the thaw early and half wants it late because the levy falls in it, and both sides end in the word that lets a person go home without owning the day. He knows the anatomy of a vessel going thin and has no word in Grathic for the failure and no hand to point at it.
- The number of the crack goes from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder-shrug and is never given out; Berr sends a neighbour to a slate at the watch; one in twelve stays printed and unchallenged and the true subtraction goes past the basket shut. The boy can spend, correct or hand back none of it. He is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and does not know he is inside the third. Sella's private roll is still unshown and the wall around it held; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; Alda's four written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them. The neat upper line has been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause stays filed apart.

- Tarn has heard a flat note and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, has turned his head a degree at a pot that answered nearly full in open light and kept that silent too. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. Jorra's changing dust and the unnameable back room, the space under the rack where slate should have stood, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and her not-looking at her sister, Dessa's count in her thumb and her hand on the wicker are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful; the carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. What the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day of it.
- The child on the quilt in the death-house was worse on the third day of its return and the room called it a graft settling; Alda told the story in the dark with dates where wonder should stand. The boy's own flower has been crossed at by a woman who did not know she did it and covered with a date. ## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles; the exact look of a vessel handing its work to the next thing until the skin holds more than it was meant to hold and the face keeps arranging itself in the ordinary way. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that a district can taste a change in its water and call it weather; that a number can go around as a shape nobody gives out; that the hand goes to the record before the hand to the person, and that Berr wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing; that a room off the shed can be present in a house only as dust on a sleeve, and a broken thing under a rack only as the work that goes around it; that almost is a note of its own, with air under it and a want in it, and is not a failure of the full note; that the ear that would be a gift in another house is a thing that merely happens to him here; that a woman can cover an unaware sign with a date and tell an exact lie for mercy; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that a count can keep a larger thing out of the room; that a grip on a basket handle can reach toward a pocket that holds only wool. He knows Tarn has turned his head at a note and said nothing, and that two silences under one roof are nearer an equality than anything the house has a name for.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote four times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the notes go, what goes on in the back room whose door is kept shut, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know that Dessa has put him among the bits she counts, that Mira is kin to the house three doors down, or that either woman knows more about him than she will say on a slate. He does not know that the flat note will be heard a sixth time tomorrow, or that the sixth will come from a person, or that the weight of Dessa's thumb on the wicker is the detail he will be given to keep and never to spend. The true figure is still one he has never heard spoken; the word forty has not been said in his hearing, and the word *the Breaking* has not been said at all. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the way home is real.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf where a fifth would stand, or the space between a woman's thumb and her scar; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dust, the cords, the crusts, the intervals, the honeyed ice, the higher brook and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the crusts and the gruel have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides; that the five pauses are five objects of one shape and that making a sixth is not his work either; that the nearly full note is an answer to the interval he has carried since Stone 13 and not yet the third thing; that the four rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell and the heard bell must be kept apart for the same reason; that a left thing and a burned thing are two ways of keeping, and a thing called gone may still be in the room as a smell; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerks' reasons are more dangerous than cruelties would have been because they are easier to live beside; that the things a house calls weather or mercy are the things it has entered nowhere; that Dessa's count keeps something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.
