## The moment

Day 263, Smoke 24, Fourthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}. \person[Corin]{the boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from it; the empty place beside the crust keeps its name to itself and no third came in the afternoon to make the row come out even. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and sixty-one through two hundred and sixty-three days --- six months and eighty-one days through six months and eighty-three days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said at five readings from Grain 10 through Smoke 20 and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard at every reading over the same basket from the same man, and called a kindness. That a district's arithmetic can be changed in a square where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box to two hundred tables with two figures in it, one printed and one paid, and a space between them nobody signed; that the reading knows the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing, and that a thing covered is not therefore paid for. That an arm can know what a handful weighs and not what it cost, and a slate can know the figure and not the grain. That a pot set down wrong gives its note before its time and that the note is neither a hearing nor a seventh; that a bell's gap kept as a gap is a finding and a gap filled with a supplied stroke is a finding forged; that two intervals held apart are two objects and a bridge between them would want a payer and none stands. That a house keeps itself by what it refuses to do as surely as by what it does, and that a not-doing kept is a finding and a not-doing turned into a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. That a week can go out having learned nothing from a reading and the nothing learned is itself a kind of keeping. That a fist arriving nowhere has not therefore been withheld and a supplied figure is a forged one. That a well, a rack, a yard and a bed are not openings and the mark is not for them. That a silence left empty is an object in its own right and a keeping, not a miss.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; ## What Chapter 71 must not forget
