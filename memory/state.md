# State of things — at the close of chapter 10 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 11. Days 38, 39, 40, 41, 42, 43 and 44 have been lived, one to a dated entry: seven days of a thaw that comes on in small things and is called weather by everyone who can taste it, the boy thirty-eight days old on the bank and forty-four at the close. A woman went into the clay-heavy rise of the Slip Brook and stayed there, uncollected, and the sixth hearing of the full note came out of her --- the second from a person --- and lay beside the five from four sources without joining them. The hand-on-the-basket detail was paid in past tense, untagged, and kept where things with no hour are kept; no collector came and nobody found a word for the omission; a clerk was set at the Fishstairs for an uncalled measure; a tin above an empty bed and two cords under an apron and a knot-string in a hut stood as three records no Court can read; a green-mouthed woman looked at pots and was gone unnamed; and at the Fish Blessing a woman who had kept a son in knots for three years tied a forty-first knot and said the word forty low into a basket and watched. Nothing has been joined, paid or explained except a sound, a shape and a word. Day 45 is tomorrow.*

## The moment

Day 44 is done. On Ilmen 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813, at dusk, \person{Sella Vail} carried the boy down the river road from \place{the Weir}, up \place{the Clay Yard}, and set him on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. He is \person{Corin}, forty-four days old, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, with the two crusts in the fold, neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third. The house went about its evening as it had gone about all seven days: \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept its broader shape on its peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years and nobody filled it, the proverb went to the pot and not to the basket, and the clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} had turned its corner for a year and two days held nothing and was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept. Sella turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a bank is not an opening; the fire that came up the yard on \lore{Kindling} was still under blind ash and the back of the broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap.

The day had been the Fish Blessing. On the flat stone at the Weir the hearth-maids had sprinkled salt and said the old words in a low half-song while the eels ran as they always ran. \person{Orra Weir}, rope-handed and eel-scarred, smelling of silt and cold fish and tallow, had crouched by the basket and told the pot that an eel goes in and cannot find the way out, which is all a trap is. Then, with the knot-string at her waist, she had tied a forty-first knot she could not account for --- the one tied the night she stood at the end of the weir and watched a woman come up the Clay Yard with a small covered bundle, warm enough to show steam in the cold, with a flower on it --- and had said the word forty low, into the basket, and watched what the boy would do with it. He did nothing. The surgeon in him noted the double turn with the tail tucked under, tied left over right, the small extra pressure of her thumb, and that the word was said low and watched, which told him it was a count before he knew what it counted. He filed the word on its own tray with the shape of the knot beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker laid apart from both. Up the river the cord hangs in the \place{Eel House} with the new knot on it, which no clerk can spell, and the boy does not know what it counts.

The count went on exactly through all seven days while household and district kept their numbers badly in fists and mouths and weather. Six hearings now: five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside them and never among them, and no seventh. The nearly full answer of Day 35 lies on its own tray, advanced and not paid; the told bell is still beside and never on the heard bell; the five pauses do not join; the two cords of \person{Mira Silt} and the knot-string of Orra stand on separate boards and do not touch; the word forty is a saying and not a vessel's sound and does not go near the six. A woman is dead and stays dead, no collector was called for her, and nobody called the omission anything. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth behind him and came down.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, forty-four days old at the close of Day 44. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: in the basket at Sella's hip, set at the edge of the racks, in the moot-post's shade, on the boards of the Weir or in the shade of the Eel House where a pot would not be slopped, lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into \place{the Debt Loft}. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth freezes in the small hours and nobody wipes it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The head goes where it wants and makes a small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool does less than the wind wants. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 34 without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with a date. He wants the breast and wants the crust even cold, and gets neither; the old crust is stiff past smelling of anything, the newer still gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns, and both lie in the fold, uneaten, unremoved, unnamed, with no third in all seven days. A woman went into the water two paces from where he lay and he knew exactly how the vessel gave and could not lift a hand toward her.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired seven more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs and Alda going to her slate before the body. He knows that a thing that does not happen leaves a space with the outline of a sound that did not come, and filed the uncollected woman that way: an absence with the outline of a collection, not a hearing, not an answer, touching none of the other trays. This chapter added that a word said low and watched is a count before one knows what it counts; that a knot has a shape and a thumb has a weight and neither can be spent; that an account can be kept in wool or in a tin on a shelf in a material no officer would think to read; that a district can bless eels over a woman it did not collect and go home warm; that the hand that reaches for the record first is a fact of the same shape as other such hands and not the same fact; that a likeness between a board that will not bear a man and a breath that is missing in a house's count is still only a likeness. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he keeps the honeyed ice, the hairline, the higher brook and the new rise in four places, the told bell beside but never on the heard bell, and six like rooms in six places. He knows that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with, and that he is the only adult in every room and the least use in it.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the bell's four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the Fishstairs' four, four and a missing step; the boards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the kindling's four, four and a space; the firelight's four, four and an empty shelf; the told bell's four, four and a gap; the officer's four measures, four measures and a missing closing line; four ways out of the Cross; four oars on the collector's boat in Orra's story. The ear now holds six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa as the men lifted, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them, not a seventh. The nearly full answer of Day 35 remains a third sound on its own tray, advanced and not paid, not added to the six. The word forty is a saying, not a vessel's sound, and lies on its own tray with the shape of the knot beside it and the thumb on wicker apart from both. The trays do not touch: intervals, gaps, told bell, absence-shaped-like-a-collection, word and knot. No Grathic word for the flat note or for nearly, and no word of his own for the thing Orra put in the cord.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 is still there, stiff and gone past smelling of anything; the fresh crust from Mira, given on Day 22, still gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns; both lie in the fold, uneaten, unlifted, unnamed, and no third came in seven days though bread is in the house. The shape of Dessa's thumb on the wicker is now a held thing, paid and kept where things with no hour are kept. Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched on Day 38 and again at the Weir on Day 43, never opened, and her fourth narrow slate is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name; Sella's private roll is unshown; Berr's box is locked in his house; Mira's grey wool cord and the second cord twisted twice beneath it have knots uncounted; Orra's knot-string with the forty-first knot hangs in the Eel House, unread; the \lore{Ash} ridge is gone from the Vail corner, burned on Ilmen 1 and called dealt with, and both of Garl's proverbs are still said to the pot. **Filed but not possessed:** the honeyed ice, the hairline that stops short of the edge, the higher clay-heavy brook, the Ilm's new rise under a thinning skin, the weak board, the green at a mouth and the looking, Orra's knot and the word forty, Dessa's nineteen in her thumb, the two not-lookings, the paid thumb on wicker, the clerk's record-first order, Jorra's clean forearms, the cracked pot, the told bell, the absence shaped like a collection and the silence where a name should be are on separate trays in his private file. **Money:** none to the boy and none changes hands to him. Figures go past him and are not his to spend: one in twelve printed and unchallenged at the Cross, a figure shut in a fist and passed from mouth to shoulder, Berr's number that goes into a slate and not into a well, the levy and bits, marks for eels given six for five or five for six, the number that went around the Weir and was changed into weather, and the word forty spoken low and watched, whose count he does not know.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was paid on Day 38, in past tense and untagged, in the shape of Dessa's thumb on the wicker reaching toward a pocket that held a small cold rectangle of light with a name on it; the name was not looked at and the hand came back with wool. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a failure of the system, were recognised again in Berr's passive sentences, the Court clerk's soft *it's entered as it stands*, the district's grammar of weather and the way a woman went to her slate before the body. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one; Des's laugh did not return. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all, and is now gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear and the two crusts in it, awake, silent, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. ## Open dangers

- The thaw has stopped being a taste in the water and has taken a person. The Slip Brook came up clay-heavy behind the racks in the night and carried Dessa Silt away uncollected; the Ilm runs higher under a skin so thin the current shows through it in dark veins; the Singing Sill gives its low note early through the boards of both kiln-house and Weir; a board in the plank walk will not bear a man's weight when the river comes up in Silt and a man steps over it without comment. The district has one word for all of it --- weather --- that lets a person go home warm without owning a day. He knows the anatomy of a vessel going thin and has no Grathic word for the failure and no hand to point at it.
- No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of a heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument. The clerk at the Fishstairs was set there for a measure nobody called for and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls; the absence of a draw for the dead woman is entered nowhere. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is now gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.
- The number still goes around in fists and mouths and weather: one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the true subtraction over the basket, Berr's figure that goes into a slate and not into a well, a number for the blessing that nobody gives out, marks for eels given six for five or five for six. Sella's private roll is still unshown and the wall around it held; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; Alda's four written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the pewter tin above the empty bed holds a pale fold nobody has opened. The neat upper line has been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand and the Fishstairs clerk's ordinary order stay filed apart. The word forty has been spoken once, low and watched, and the boy does not know it is a count of children or that he is one of them.
- Orra tied a forty-first knot over the story of a small warm bundle with a flower and said the number into the basket and watched; the cord hangs in the Eel House where he cannot enter or read it. A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she is watching to see what he does with a word he cannot answer. The green-mouthed woman who looked at pots across the market is another ear planted years before it is spoken of, and the lead-glaze answer lies inside her colour.
- Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness; it is held while the fire never goes out and touched only before an opening, never at a bank, a market, a reading, a Weir or the carrying of a basket. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles has been spoken around through another turning of the year and remains never paid, never settled, never done with; his name was not said under the Vail roof on the Remembering and his objects keep his place. His clean dark line is not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept.
- Tarn has heard a flat note and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, has swept the road to a collector without turning and noticed that he had noticed. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. Jorra's clean forearms and the unnameable back room, the space under the rack where slate should have stood, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and two cords, Orra's knot-on-a-lie, the green at a mouth and the weakness of a board are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful; the carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. What the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day of it.
- The child on the quilt in the death-house was worse on the third day of its return and the room called it a graft settling; Alda told the story in the dark with dates where wonder should stand. The boy's own flower has been crossed at by a woman who did not know she did it and covered with a date. The death-house ridge was measured a finger's width thicker than Vail's and both were burned on Ilmen 1.
- The Court's room and now the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept six likenesses apart on purpose; no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work.
- The flat note has six hearings now --- five from four sources and one from a person --- and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. The nearly full note lies on its own tray as an answer to the Stone 13 interval, advanced and not paid, and is not a hearing and not the third thing. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, four, four and a gap in the square and in a story told in the dark, kept in two places; the count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling, the firelight and the space between a woman's thumb and her scar, and the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter.

The word *the Breaking* has not been said in his hearing; the word forty has now been said once, low, and is not explained.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles; the exact look and sound of a vessel handing its work to the next thing until the skin holds more than it was made to hold and the face arranges itself in the ordinary way. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that a body cracked all the way through gives a small flat knock and that it came out of a person he loved without knowing her; that a district can resume its arithmetic over an uncollected woman; that a number can go from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and never arrive; that the hand goes to the record before the hand to the person, in a midwife, a market clerk, a Court clerk, a man who priced him and Berr; that a woman can keep an account in wool or in a tin on a shelf and no officer will think to read it; that a word said low and watched is a count before one knows what it counts; that a knot has a shape and a thumb a weight; that a district will bless its eels over a woman it did not collect; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. He knows Tarn has turned his head at a note and said nothing, and that two silences under one roof are nearer an equality than anything the house has a name for.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote four times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the notes go, what goes on in the back room whose door is kept shut, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know why no collector came for the woman in the brook or that the omission will one day have a name. He has heard the word forty spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and does not know that it is the number of children who paid for him or that he is one of them, nor what the forty-first knot counts, nor that Orra tied it the night a woman carried him up the Clay Yard with a flower on the bundle. He does not know that the green-mouthed woman who looked at pots has a name or an ear, that Dessa put him among the bits she counted, that Mira is kin to the house three doors down, or that either woman knows more about him than she will say on a slate. The true figure is still one he has never understood; the word forty has been said in his hearing and the word *the Breaking* has not been said at all. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the surgeon he was is gone beyond recall.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf where a fifth would stand, the space between a woman's thumb and her scar, or a word said low and watched; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dust, the cords, the crusts, the intervals, the honeyed ice, the higher brook, the knot-string and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the crusts and the gruel have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides; that the five pauses are five objects of one shape and that making a sixth is not his work either; that the nearly full note is an answer to the interval he has carried since Stone 13 and not yet the third thing; that the six like rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell and the heard bell must be kept apart for the same reason, and so must the word on its tray and the sound in his ear; that a left thing and a burned thing are two ways of keeping, and a thing called gone may still be in the room as a smell; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerks' reasons are more dangerous than cruelties would have been because they are easier to live beside; that the things a house calls weather or mercy are the things it has entered nowhere; that Dessa's count kept something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that a word watched is a thing someone expects a reaction to, and he has given none. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.
