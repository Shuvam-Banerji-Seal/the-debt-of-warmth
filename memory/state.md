## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: three hundred and nine through three hundred and eleven days --- six months and one hundred and twenty-nine through six months and one hundred and thirty-one days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the round three hundred was a threshold kept on a place of its own and not spent, and he has stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers count, which is not the same as not wanting it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said **eight** times from Grain 10 through Bone 10 (twice on Frost 30 and twice on Bone 10) and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard at every reading over the same basket from the same man, and called a kindness; and now the smaller movement of taking himself out of the sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on. That a district's arithmetic can be changed in a square where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box to two hundred tables with two figures in it, one printed and one paid, and a space between them nobody signed; that a figure can be sent over a basket twice, once for those who stood and once for those who came late, and be one figure spoken two times; that the reading knows the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing, and that a thing covered is not therefore paid for; that seven readings are seven, three Frost readings are three and **four readings are four and not one account**. That a voice may carry a burning with no owner standing under it and no hand at the flame; that a person can carry a record for years and not be its reader; that a shaping is not a witness and three shapings are three and not a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked; that five tellings of a burned slate are five and not five witnesses to one fire; that four held changes are four and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood; that an arm can know what a handful weighs and not what it cost, and a slate can know the weight of a thing and not its price; that a cost asked before the work is done is not therefore true; that the ease on a cold face is not ease; that repair is not restoration; and that a finding with no payer can be carried a long way without its becoming a rule, which is the only honest work available at two inches of air.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; the pale line on the silent woman's hand at the Frost 30 pot board is kept on its own place beside it and not laid across it. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with answers one hundred and twenty-one through one hundred and ninety-eight from Frost 4 through the small hours of Bone 12, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, not a freedom, with no payer named; 131 through 156 repeat the forms without adding a new one; 157 through 162 are Chapter 80's six, 163 through 168 are Chapter 81's six, 169 through 174 are Chapter 82's six, 175 through 180 are Chapter 83's six, 181 through 186 are Chapter 84's six, 187 through 192 are Chapter 85's six, and **193 through 198 are Chapter 86's six**: the small intake let go whole on the river's note, the small intake let go whole at the going of the reading-day, the empty interval beside the held interval with the finger between, the small intake beside a letting-go with the half-beat of the pot's set-down laid beside them, the small intake held against the kiln's fourth low pull as it gave, and the final small intake let go whole on the same note. The four arithmetics stand behind one printed figure on a place of their own with no payer and are not a row, the finding of four being kept apart from the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the four held changes and his own gaps with the finger going between; the bell's gap in the square is a thing kept somewhere else again. **Nothing on the twelfth of Bone was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended.**
- **Does not know.** That he is three hundred and eleven days old, or six months and one hundred and thirty-one days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no \place{Grathic} word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though Sella glanced at it and shut her hand half over nothing on these mornings, and her little-door burn closing on air. What the widow's mark mended, why the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, or why the household's first firing of Bone came on Bone 2 and not on the turning of the month; he has seen the act and set it down as maintenance on a vessel, not as a sign or a payment, and did not look at it again afterward. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reached a finger beneath it for one counted breath on Day 166 and for two on Frost 5 and came away both times without a word;** he read the change in the hand and not the charcoal, and filed the change where it lay. What the men want who come before light to the back room on these three days and who keep the room open and low-voiced, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the five tellings have anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room, the two shut-room tellings or the soft-handed man from Slate Row; he has not joined any of them, and no owner stood under the fifth voice, which went through the reading untouched. What became of the pot that sang early last month, why it is not heard from, whether any pot is gone quiet on every row come the thaw, whether the note a pot gave before its time on Smoke 14 means anything, and whether the pot Jorra knows will give its note three turns ahead with air under it and a want in it; Jorra withholds the knowing and the boy has not made it a hearing or a seventh. What figure Berr carried up the road in his locked box, what the true subtraction of Bone says inside it beside the three of Frost, what Hessa carries in her edge-on slate or whether she will one day read it, and what the soft-cuffed clerk entered inward below the north bridge; no figure came off any of those slates under this roof. Why the figure printed on the notice is one in twelve, who stands under it when the levy is called, and why the man who calls it says *your house* and never *I*; he has read the grammar and not found the payer. What became of the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, whose it was, and whether the field's empty place and the pan's gone ring have anything to do with one another; no body was found and no figure given. Who carried out the man on Day 188, what his name was, what the pale line at his gum meant, and why no reading has ever answered for him. What the seventh green at the silent woman's mouth and the seventh shape at the market's edge might become; he called them a colour and a shape, gave neither a name and made no eighth of either. What the reed-cutter's cord holds, what Mira's second twist counts, what Orra's last knot means, what Ilva's notches keep, what the charcoal beneath the shelf says, and what the four true subtractions would say if a hand not his laid them together; all stay in their own materials where no slate has taken a figure off them. Whether the composed face he knows from a better-lit room has ever been warmed here by anybody, and why the knowledge has never once made a warm place for anybody; he does not ask it to now.

## What Chapter 87 must not forget

Chapter 87 opens on Day 312, Bone 13, the first morning of the middle working week, with the hand coming up for the **one hundred and ninety-ninth** answer, the count standing exact at **311** --- six months and one hundred and thirty-one days told by nobody --- the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken that number of days. The file lies open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved; the returned half-check of Bone 8 stands on the flagstone's own place beside the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat and the Frost 5 returned beat as **four held changes on four places and not a rule**, was not made on the eleventh or twelfth, and Bone 13 carries it unchanged. **Four arithmetics of the year --- the three of Frost and Bone's first --- stand behind one printed figure with one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and four readings four and not one account;** the finding stays on a place of its own with no payer, not a lesson, grievance, mending or doctrine, and is not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the four held changes, the returned half-check or the boy's gaps --- the edges nearly touch and the finger goes between. Bone came on without a boundary drawn; neither habit of naming the months was set right or left wrong. The burned-slate word stands at **five** tellings with a finger between the fifth and the four, five being five and not a rule, and the sixth telling is not due on a day that has not earned it; the ownerless voice of Bone 5 is not brought in as a sign, lesson, mending, cause, witness, confession, payment or payer, and no hand is named at the flame. The twice-mended pot stands with its crack showing and its proverbs said to the pot and the fire; a repair that holds is not restoration and mends nothing anybody will name. Ilva's strip stays in her sleeve and her ninth hand stands nine with no tenth and no figure off the wood; she was offstage on Bone 10, 11 and 12. The dressed grain lies covered and unfigured at the rack end, read over by the arithmetic of Bone 10 that did not know what it cost; the four arithmetics in Berr's box still will not know what it cost. All four arithmetics and their true subtractions remain unread up the clay road in Berr's locked box with one in twelve printed and unchallenged; Hessa's slate went up unread toward Greybank on Bone 10; the clerk's inward slate stays at the out-of-line landing; the soft-cuffed clerk, the green-mouthed woman and the foundling are offstage after Bone 10. The stayed frost is gone off the well's north lip, the trough skinned and the silt-clouded plate on its stone, all called weather and entered nowhere; the seven walked-around places are gone around with a look and no number; Berr's figure went twice over the basket on Bone 10 and the bell's four, four and a gap stays in its square beside the told bell, not made a seventh hearing or a fifth pull. The widow's mark is touched at no hour here; the next opening stands far off in a later month's first firing. Do not supply a tenth hand, a tenth stopping, a seventh hearing, a sixth asking, a sixth telling, a fourth shaping, a fourth likeness, a third shut-room telling, a fifth held change or an eighth green or shape; do not name a payer, open the box, slate, strip, charcoal or tin, pay a debt, make a lesson or cause of the reading, join the trays, bring Bone 20 (Day 319) or a new opening early, or end anything. Nothing settled in this chapter may reach the middle week as a sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment; the story moves because the hand keeps answering and the count keeps going.
