## The moment

Day 74 is done. On Silt 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813, at dusk, \person{Sella Vail} carried the boy up \place{the Clay Yard} from the yard well at a half-pace from every open slate, set him on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. He is \person{Corin}, seventy-four days old, two months and fourteen days, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, with **one** brown-edged crust in the fold where two had lain and the empty place at his ear beside it, still carried without a name. The crust is stiff and gone past smelling of anything and has never been a sermon. The flood has gone out of the yard and left mud that takes a boot and gives it back slowly, and the loaves stand with a space at the row's end that nobody calls a number. Behind him the carried fire lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The door stands a finger's width open; through the gap comes the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor comes the fire, the two sounds kept apart as they have been for seventy-four days. In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned lies where it has always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lies beside it unswept because the house says it is the thaw and it will go when it goes. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down. No opening came in any of the seven days, and the widow's mark was not touched all chapter. Six hearings stay six and no seventh came; both cords, the knot-string, the pewter tin, the turned sixth page and the broken slates stay unread. The count goes on into Silt 16 unspent.

The seven days of this chapter, each lived in full:

- **Day 68, Silt 9, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday).** Before light the Ilm was over the lower steps at \place{the Fishstairs} and coming up the road in a sheet so thin the ground seemed to have sweated it out; brown water went over brown water with a small steady sound like a cloth drawn over a table, and the district read it as weather and went on using the steps. Below the stone sill women set pots on higher stones and children on window ledges; the rope at the well had mud to the last strand. The sixty-eight-day-old boy, carried at the edge at a half-pace from every open slate, heard under it all a low turning felt in the roots of the teeth --- many small failures inside one slow certain failure, a whole district as a vessel of such capacity that no hand in it could get around it, with a thin place nobody would name --- and filed it beside the intervals and the four low sounds, writing no cause across it; there was no Grathic word and no hand to point. Word came up from \place{the Weir} that the weak board in \place{the plank walk} was trodden or gone under and nobody had gone to see, and the boy kept it beside the missing Fishstairs step and the stopping hairlines and made no fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk held his slate to his forearm, hand before eyes, for a measure nobody had called, and a woman laid a mark on the stall and took it away again. Sella gave every instruction to the wool, the fire and the bucket and never to the basket, said *the house*, touched no mark because a road is not an opening, and told the fire it was the thaw. Tarn agreed so entirely with early and late that no argument could find a foot and went on wanting the late; his broom went over the collector's road without turning and his head did not turn at a vessel's note. At dusk one brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed, the door a finger's width open, the river's note through the gap and the fire's four-four-and-a-gap through the floor kept apart.
- **Day 69, Silt 10, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday, Account-day).** First light found the water sitting on the second step at \place{the Cross} and going nowhere, with \lore{Account-day} held on the dry step above it and the market spreading around the standing water as a pot takes a cleared shelf. The boy was set in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with cold iron at his cheek. \person{Berr Clay}, red wool cord folded under his coat, green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth and hot clay under the smell of nothing, wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over an unopened narrow slate and read *one in twelve, as the notice says* twice over the water and the basket, taking himself out of his own sentence. The true subtraction went over the top of the basket into the noise of the stalls and was spoken by nobody; the number for the flood went from fist to mouth to shoulder and arrived nowhere, and a man shut his hand on a door-latch and went away without it. When a man with water to his boot-top asked what the rise would take, Berr said *a number goes into a slate, not into a well; there'll be a slate at the watch*, to the step and the notice and the top of the crowd. The bell went four, four and a gap, longer than any temple bell, and nobody crossed himself; a woman at the turnip heap started at the third stroke and wiped her apron instead. Word came up that the clerk was still set at the Fishstairs. The boy kept the printed figure and the unspoken subtraction on two trays and spent neither, knowing that a figure read aloud was not the figure paid; the six hearings stayed six, three near-full answers advanced, the word on its own tray with the shape of the double turn and the weight of a thumb apart. Berr went off toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded away, the box locked in his house and the true deduction inside, believing he had done a kindness. At dusk Sella carried him home at the half-pace, said *the house*, told the fire it was the thaw, and put the fold right without looking at his face.
- **Day 70, Silt 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday).** The water had stopped coming and went around instead: around the spent slip, the upturned barrow, the stone set for a step and the foot of \place{the drying racks}, taking a little from each and leaving the rest. A rack-woman said the water had gone around the ridge and left it standing and another said the house was saved, and that was the whole of what was said; Sella did not say it. The boy was carried down the yard at the half-pace past the death-house ridge two doors down, where the old dark line stood with the brown water a hand's breadth short of it and nobody had swept it, and past the Vail corner, where the clean dark line was dry above the mud in a strip no wider than two thumbs. Beside it the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay unswept. He set the standing line, the water that went around it and the new grey in three places that did not touch; and into the file went a thing the water had done and nobody had said: that a thing left where it is may still be kept. He felt the pull of it where his old life had taught him that what is left unentered goes bad, and filed the feeling as an object without making a doctrine or a cause. A man with salt-white wrists shut a figure in his fist and passed it from fist to mouth to shoulder and arrived nowhere. Garl was kept by his salt-stiff coat, his hollow wheel-seat with its twenty-year lean and the proverb about the crack said to a pot with no crack in it; the tile debt lay where it lay. Tarn held his broom in both hands without sweeping, went over the collector's road and did not turn. At dusk Sella set him on his bed, said the house was saved to nobody, and told the fire it was the thaw.
- **Day 71, Silt 12, Y.H. 813 (Secondday).** The water went down all night and left the cold behind it. Below the sill three children waded in the inches left in the hollows of the bank, the colour of grey slip-water in a shallow dish, and one came up with a foot dark to the knee that did not come back when she slapped it. A woman wrapped it twice in wool, crossed the fold over the instep, pressed it between her palms and called it the cold; a woman passing with a bucket said the water was weather and the two facts went past each other on the same morning and never met. The seventy-one-day-old boy read the extremity going before the core, the blood going back too fast and too cold, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and knew what to do and had no Grathic word and no hand and no standing to put on the shin; the reading worked perfectly and was worth nothing. Sella did not go toward the child; she turned the basket with her knee, gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and said the house and the thaw. The boy put her not-going on the tray with the fold put right without a look, Mira's not-looking and Berr's not-looking, four not-lookings of one shape and not a cause; under her roof she had sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and he did not lay the two together. At the well the rope still had mud to the last strand and the thaw argument gave no figure and ended in weather. The crust was two inches from his ear and the adult's reading and the child's hunger were both true and the child was louder.
- **Day 72, Silt 13, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday).** The water gave back the lower steps one at a time; falling, it was a dragging over stones, and the boy set that shape beside the rising spreading and wrote no cause across either. Below the bridge \person{Mira Silt} worked the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and silt in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been and her small smile kept like a door on the latch; asked whether the run would be thin she moved eel-bones while deciding and put one away when the deciding was done. When a buyer named a figure her thumb went under her apron to the grey cord and a shifting creel showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried to \place{Nine Pots}, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s \lore{knot-string} and did not touch it. Orra stood at the flood's edge with her board and cord and nothing else, turned the board on its side, pulled one knot down with her thumb, said no word and got no answer; the shape of the forty-first knot was legible to the boy without its name. Sella carried him three doors down to \place{House Silt} and set the basket just inside a finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light, opened no slate and turned no errand into a walk to \place{Slate Row}. Above the empty bed the round pewter tin kept its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, a record of touch worn into the lid; below it the fold of grey wool held only the hollow where the small pale river stone had lain, and neither was spoken of. \person{Alda Reed}'s sixth page stayed turned toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name and her little tin stayed shut inside her bodice. Three private accounts in two materials stood unread while the water went down, and no number in the district would come to his hand.
- **Day 73, Silt 14, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday).** The sweeping that had been done twice before went around the ridge a third time and the ridge stood through all three; Sella gave the instruction to the broom before anyone (*Round it. You'll pay for it in the thaw if you take it up*). The space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where whole slate should have stood was gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and nobody entered it; the boy had learned the grammar of a ward walking around a bed for a week and never writing why, and set the space on a tray of its own beside the ridge and the finer grey. \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel, and said nothing of the two men who had been in \place{the back room} since before light. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone --- it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady --- told a pot it would do, knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and the end of her sentence arrived at the pot, and the household straightened around his silence. He did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the bed or the basket; *he's not Vail* stayed sealed and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. Tarn set the knife on its sill and told the empty yard where it could be checked, six years standing on the handle and the seventh nearly gone and still held; his broom went over the collector's road and his head did not turn at a vessel's note, and the two silences under one roof kept their equality unjoined. The boy was the one thing in the room neither swept around nor entered nor counted: set down like a pot set down and left.
- **Day 74, Silt 15, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday).** The flood went out of the yard with one thing left behind that nobody would name, and the mud stood where the water had stood. The yard counted what it took in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end and not in figures: three days' baking, a row of pots and the board off the weir-steps became *a good deal*. The seventy-four-day-old boy kept the count exactly against the house's bad arithmetic and had the printed figure, the figure nobody spoke and the figure the water would have written if a hand had held the slate, and let the three lie where they lay. Word came that the weak board was gone or trodden flat under the silt and nobody made an accident of it; he set it beside the missing Fishstairs step and the stopping hairlines and let none touch. The things refused for a week were now named past tense as the thaw that was --- the honeyed ice, the stopping hairlines, the early Sill note --- and he kept each on its own place and did not let the past tense join them. At the well the rope had mud to the last strand, the skin-on-the-water argument gave no figure, and the clerk was reported still set and answered with weather. Under the roof the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, four, four and a gap, while the boards kept four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb and the two counts did not meet. Jorra came through the door in the afternoon with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and no grey past the bend of his little finger, and the boy held the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray as two states of one week. Sella stood over the two lines in the corner with a broom and did not sweep either, and the saying about the crack went to a pot with no crack in it. Six hearings stayed six and no seventh came, though a vessel gave its note past the yard door; three near-full answers advanced, three intervals, the word on its tray, four low sounds in four places and the river's note a fifth thing not a hearing. At dusk one crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place unnamed, the hand went up an inch toward the water and came down, and the count crossed into Silt 16 unspent.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, seventy-four days old at the close of Day 74, two months and fourteen days, carried everywhere: to the edge at the Fishstairs, to the moot-post's shade at the Cross, to the racks and the death-house corner, to the water's edge below the sill, to the Fishstairs again, three doors down just inside a finger's-width-open door in House Silt, out into the Clay Yard on the third sweeping and the last mud, to the yard well, and lifted onto the bed under the roof, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth or the water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from his wrist so the cold finds the place. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; it was not crossed in this chapter. He wants the crust, wants the breast and wants the bucket, and can reach neither: the spent crust lies stiff in the fold and the warmth he most wanted is an empty place in the fold. Dessa Silt is thirty-six mornings dead at the close and his number for his own days is still given by nobody.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired seven more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs, Alda going to her slate before the child, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the grey cord while deciding whether to lie. This chapter added: that a whole district under water has a note of its own, many small failures inside one slow certain one, and no word or pointing hand for it; that rising water spreads and falling water drags and the two are separate shapes; that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid; that a thing left where it lies may still be kept, and that this can be felt as a pull without becoming a rule; that a cold foot can stay cold after the water goes, with the afterdrop waiting where no warming will come; that sweeping around a space is a way of not entering it; that a house can straighten around a man's silence as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted; that the men who came into a back room need not be named for their dust to be read; that a thing said after the fact is not the same as a thing said before it, and calling a thing over is another way of never having owned it; and that a man can be set down like a pot set down and left, and be the one thing in a room neither swept around nor entered nor counted. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he keeps the sealed-room saying beside but never on the kiln's gap; the told bell beside the heard bell, and both beside the early note, the kiln's gap and the district's new note; six like rooms in six places; the six hearings beside and never among; **three** near-full answers advanced and not paid.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the bell's four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told bell in the dark; the Fishstairs' four, four and a landing where a step should have been; the boards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the kindling's four, four and a space; the firelight's four, four and an empty shelf. The ear still holds six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them, not a seventh. The near-full answer of Day 35 (Ilmen 6), the second of Day 52 (Ilmen 23) and the third of Day 65 stand on their own tray, advanced and not paid, neither hearings nor the third thing. The three intervals (Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25) stay objects on a third tray. The word forty remains a saying, not a vessel's sound, on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; it was not spoken again this chapter. Water-over-water, the falling water's drag, the district's new note, the early Sill note, the honeyed ice and the early thaw argument each kept their own place and no cause was written across any of them; four low sounds in four places stayed four, with the river's note a fifth thing and not a hearing. No Grathic word for the flat note, for *nearly*, for the river's own note, for the note of a whole district, for the cold that stays after the water goes, for the failure in a vessel, for a thing taken and not spoken of, or for the difference between a left line and a burned one.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the one brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 remains, stiff and gone past smelling of anything, uneaten, unlifted, unnamed, carried through Silt; the empty place beside it has still not been named. Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched and unopened; her **sixth** narrow slate is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name, and the hidden record stands at six pages, all unread (the sixth paid on Day 64, not to be paid again). Sella's private roll is unshown; Berr's box is locked in his house with the true deduction inside; Mira's grey wool cord and the second cord twisted twice beneath it have knots uncounted and have never gone upriver to Nine Pots or been lifted at a door; Orra's knot-string with its forty-first knot hangs in the Eel House, unread, its shape legible to the boy without its name; \lore{the Ash} in the Vail corner is gone, burned on Ilmen 1 and called dealt with, and a new hairline of grey lies beside the clean dark line, unswept. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature stood behind Berr's figure read over the basket, the district's grammar of weather, the order of reaching and the shape of a hand going to a slate before a person; the yard calling the thaw *the thaw that was* was another chart closed with a neat line drawn under it. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all, and is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.

## Open dangers

- The year's flood has come and gone and left the district unchanged in its habits. The Ilm came up over the lower steps in a thin brown sheet and went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner, and the yard has already turned the flood into loaves, glances and *a good deal*, and then into the thaw that was. The boy heard the note of the whole district under strain --- many small failures inside one slow certain one, with a thin place nobody would name --- and filed it with no word, no hand to point and no cause written across it; he knows the anatomy of a vessel going thin and has no Grathic word for the failure in a quarter of a town. The weak board in the plank walk is gone or trodden flat under the silt, seen by nobody and made into no accident, set beside the missing Fishstairs step and the stopping hairlines in three gaps entered in no account. The clerk at Fishstairs has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to the stalls, and nobody has taken him off. The honeyed ice, the hairlines that stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge and the early Sill note are spoken of now as the thaw that was, as though a thing said after the fact were the same as a thing said before it. The district has one word, *weather*, and now one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold; a woman wrapped it in wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts never met. The boy read the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing. The cold entered nowhere and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure; **a new thread must be opened for it**.
- A man described, over a bucket at the well in chapter 13, a room shut in the last war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath, their heat gone out with no way to feed it. The yard received it as history with a date; Sella, who lost a husband to the same danger, heard it and told the fire it was the thaw, and in this chapter sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind. The boy filed the saying on its own tray beside the told bell, the heard bell, the district's note and the kiln's gap and wrote no cause across them. It is a description of the danger under his own roof carried into a yard that had no use for it, and no one to receive it; planted toward ~650 (T065). The house keeps its own low word for the kiln's breath, which names the danger and the mercy in one breath. The carried fire is still under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the coal that went up with the brine-gull on Day 45 is entered nowhere and missed in none.
- No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission; thirty-six mornings gone and still unentered. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument, and strengthened this chapter by the water going around a thing the house set down and leaving it standing. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.
- The number still goes around in fists, mouths, shoulders, loaves, glances and weather: one in twelve printed and unchallenged and read twice over the water, the true subtraction unspoken, Berr's figure that goes into a slate and not into a well, the number for the flood that passed from fist to mouth to shoulder and reached nobody, the printed figure and the unspoken one on two trays, and the house's arithmetic with a space at the end of the loaf row that nobody calls a number. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid. Sella's private roll is still unshown; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter, and the knots are still uncounted; Alda's **six** written notes lie turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the pewter tin above the empty bed holds its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened, and the little tin in Alda's bodice is shut. The neat upper line has been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand and the Fishstairs clerk's ordinary order stay filed apart. The word forty was spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and was not spoken again this chapter.
- Orra tied a forty-first knot over the story of a small warm bundle with a flower and said the number into the basket and watched; the cord hangs in the Eel House where he cannot enter or read it, though he can read its shape without its name. A woman has kept a count in wool for three years and knows something about a child carried up the Clay Yard in the year of the flower, and she stood at the flood's edge this chapter with her board and string and got no answer. The shape of the knot is on its tray beside the word and the weight of a thumb apart from both.
- One of the two crusts in the fold is gone and nobody says who took it, that it is gone, or what became of it; the spent one lies stiff beside an empty place that has crossed into Silt without a name. The pale river stone is gone from the fold in House Silt and nobody says it is gone either; the weak board is gone under the silt and nobody makes an accident of it. A coal went up with a brine-gull on Day 45 and one warmth less went out of the world with no slate the poorer. These are subtractions entered in no account, and the boy keeps all four without a word in \lore{Grathic} for any of them. A kindness that could not be answered has become an absence that cannot be answered.
- Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness; it was not touched once in seven days, because no opening came and a road, a basket, a rack, a shore, a door, a bed and a yard are not an opening. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles remains never paid, never settled, never done with; his name was not said under the Vail roof on the Remembering and his objects keep his place. His clean dark line is not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and the new finer grey beside it is not swept either, because the house says it is thaw and will go when it goes. Sella stood over both with a broom and left them.
- Tarn has heard flat notes and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, has swept the road to a collector without turning and noticed that he has noticed. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. The seventh year is nearly gone and the one entry the house cannot check presses toward its end. Jorra's grey dust and clean slip, the unnamed back room, the space under the rack where slate should have stood, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and two cords, Orra's knot-on-a-lie, the green at a mouth and the gone board are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful; what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the sealed counting room eighteen leagues east, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day of it.
- The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept six likenesses apart on purpose; no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work. Calling a thing over after the fact is a third grammar: a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot.
- The flat note has six hearings --- five from four sources and one from a person --- and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. The nearly full note now has three answers on its own tray, advanced and not paid, and is not a hearing and not the third thing. The river's own note of water going over water, its new note of a district under strain, and the falling water's drag are each fifth things on places of their own and not hearings at all. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, four, four and a gap in the square and in a story told in the dark, kept in two places; the told bell, the heard bell, the early note and the kiln's gap stay four. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling and the firelight, and the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter.
- A woman is dead and stays dead, uncollected, her number in her thumb and her name under her scar; the mercy told her became a fact nobody alive can correct, and she believed him spent among children she never saw to the last. The word *the Breaking* has not been said in his hearing; the word forty was said once, low, is not explained, and was not repeated.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage; the way a room cools from the edges inward and a small body goes at the extremities before the core admits to it; how a man pulled from cold water can worsen before he settles; the exact look and sound of a vessel handing its work to the next thing until the skin holds more than it was made to hold and the face arranges itself in the ordinary way; and the anatomy of a room sealed against its own heat. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that water spreading and water dragging are two sounds of one river and must be kept on two places; that a whole district under water has a note of its own that no vessel gives and that the note is many small failures inside one slow certain one, with a thin place no hand in it could get around; that a figure read aloud in a room that has already decided it is settled is not the figure paid; that a thing left where it lies may be kept, and that the pull of that is a feeling to be filed and not a rule to be spoken; that a foot can go dark in falling water and stay cold after the water goes, and that you do not rub such a foot and do not set it near a fire; that sweeping around a space and going wide with a tray are ways of not entering it; that a household can straighten around a man's silence as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off one corner; that a thing said after the fact, called the thaw that was, is another way of never having owned it; that a pot with no crack can still hear the proverb about cracks; and that he can be the one thing in a room neither swept around nor entered nor counted, and set down like a pot set down and left. He knows Tarn's head did not turn at the vessel's note on Day 74, and that two silences under one roof are nearer an equality than anything the house has a name for.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, where the carried fire was first lit and who pays to keep it giving, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote six times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the notes go, what goes on in the back room whose door is kept shut, why grey slate dust gets worked past Jorra's little finger on some mornings and not others, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after it is entered, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, or what the Court's soft sentences actually do. He does not know why no collector came for the woman in the brook or that the omission will one day have a name. He has heard the word forty spoken once, low, by a woman who watched him, and does not know that it is the number of children who paid for him or that he is one of them, nor what the forty-first knot counts, nor that Orra tied it the night a woman carried him up the Clay Yard with a flower on the bundle. He can read the shape of the knot but not its name and has no word to spend it on. He does not know who rubbed the pewter lid bright, who took the crust from the fold, who took the stone from the board-bed, who took or broke the weak board, or that any of these takings will be read years hence; he does not know that the cold foot will one day have a name, or whose child it was that Sella sat up with for three nights. The district's new note has no word in Grathic and he does not know that it is the sound of a world failing.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge, an empty shelf, the space between a woman's thumb and her scar, a word said low and watched, an empty place in the fold, a slack frame, a silence where an argument used to stand, a coal gone up with a bird, a stone gone from a fold, a board gone under silt, a space under a rack that nobody enters, or a new hairline of grey beside a left-in-place line; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dusts, the cords, the crusts, the stone, the intervals, the honeyed ice, the frost-bloom, the higher brook, the oiled paper, the knot-string, the prepared and performed sweepings, the sealed room, water-over-water, water going around a line, a foot that stays cold and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that the passed fire has a payer somewhere beyond the yard, as the stone and the gruel and the gone crust have payers inside it; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides, and Jorra's silence a third of the same shape; that the near-full note, now three answers, is a reply to the interval he has carried since Stone 13 and not yet the third thing; that the six like rooms with a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that the told bell and the heard bell and the early note and the district's note must be kept apart for the same reason, and so must the word on its tray and the sound in his ear, and the Greybank saying and the gap under his own roof, and the rising sheet and the falling drag; that a left thing and a burned thing are two ways of keeping, and a thing called gone may still be in the room as a smell or a hollow in a fold; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerks' reasons are more dangerous than cruelties would have been because they are easier to live beside; that a yard which walks around a space has made a record without writing one, and a house which leaves a line alone has kept it; and that the surgeon in him and the child in him want different things --- the surgeon the rule, the child the crust two inches from his ear --- and the child, as usual, is louder. He suspects nothing about the watcher in the yard; the watcher has not yet been seen.
