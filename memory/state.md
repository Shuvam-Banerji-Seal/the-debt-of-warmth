## The moment

Day 218, Grain 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, \person[Corin]{the boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear, one leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from that ear and its empty place still unnamed, no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform. Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighteen days; overhead the boards keep four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rises up and becomes the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would put a payer under all three and no payer stands to any. The fire goes on in the room that warms him and the river in the room he cannot enter, and neither stops for the ear under the roof.

At the close he has put the question to his hand and been answered. Sella went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there is no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that comes through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed; he took it through one of those counted breaths and kept it through the little silence after her count had ended and brought it down on the river's next low note. The two rooms do not touch and neither do the two rhythms; he had kept one before this and he keeps both now, remembered, not a change made again. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in that small hand and both are true; once he reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he cannot lift a crust two inches, and the two facts stand on separate trays and neither is a lesson about the other. He filed the coordination on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a miss, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and eighteen days old at the close of Day 218 and six months and thirty-eight days (two hundred and sixteen and six months and thirty-six days on the first morning, two hundred and seventeen and six months and thirty-seven on the second), carried everywhere: under the Vail roof at the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle and the kindling; in the Clay Yard, at the kiln mouth, the drying racks and the yard well; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room and the Debt Loft never entered by him or the narration). As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. - **The back room and the counting room.** Three or more unnamed men went low-voiced before first grey on the mornings of Days 216, 217 and 218 into the room off the wheel-shed that keeps a finger's width of open; Sella gave them no name and did not say the room was cold, and Jorra gave them no name either, growing quieter through the burned-slate word now told four times. Two tellings of a counting room shut in a war stand side by side with no third, planted toward ~650. The boy has not joined the tellings to the men, to the kiln, to the reading, to the glaze-room hand carried out or to the burned slate.
- **The Account-days and the subtracted figure.** Ember's three Account-days came on Days 189, 199 and 209; in each the printed one in twelve was read aloud, unchallenged, the true subtraction went up the clay road locked in Berr's box, Hessa's slate went up unread, the bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and the figure read was not the figure paid. Keep the printed figure, the unread slate and the locked subtraction on places of their own; the district's arithmetic has changed **three** times for the month and cannot be changed back, but nothing under the Vail roof was paid by any reading. - **The cold foot and the inward slate.** Below the sill a child's foot stays dark in three turns of wool, the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform; the surgeon's exact knowledge buys nothing at two paces and no \emph{silt-leach}, cause or cure may be named in this arc. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and sixteen through two hundred and eighteen days --- six months and thirty-six days through six months and thirty-eight days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said twice over a body that cannot answer, now said a third time over the district and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard three times now from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That a saying can turn by a hair, from a general rule about pots into the report of one pot's silence, and bend back again into a general proverb, three likenesses standing on one tray and not three witnesses to one death; that a silence reported is not a second hearing of the same pot; that a wrong-wind sweetness has a place and a going and is not a cause; that the burned slate can come a fourth time and still not be a third telling of the shut room, and four tellings are not four witnesses to one fire. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon, a noon, a basket, a yard, a market, a carrying, a morning, a midday, a dusk, a reading and a slate burning in another quarter are not openings; that a roof ending at a hand is only a board. That asking is not always answered and an answer refused is an object on its own tray; that a keeping may be maintenance and not a story; that exactness and humiliation can occupy one motion and neither be a sign; that a direction with a date is not yet a slate opened.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with twelve answers from Day 213 through the small hours of Day 218, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told four times on Days 205, 208, 215 and 218, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling of the shut room, advanced and not paid. **Beside the hearings, on the near-full tray:** three shapings of the pot saying --- sung early, not heard from since, gone quiet on every row --- sayings about a pot with no owner, room, cause or figure, advanced and not paid. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord tied once low with teeth and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit and the breath held under the board on Day 166, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look; Sella's finger stayed on top of the front edge through all three days of this chapter. **T065/T095 carried and advanced:** the two shut-room tellings held at two and the burned slate told four times, with no joining and no third telling.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and eighteen days old, or six months and thirty-eight days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and shut her hand half over nothing on each of these three days, and her little-door burn closing on air. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them, though the word has now come four times. What became of the pot that sang early last month, why it is not heard from, whether any pot is gone quiet on every row come the thaw, or whether any saying has anything to do with the man carried out on Day 188; the sayings have dates in them and no owner, room, cause or figure, and no name has been given to what ailed that man. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, why he once timed a slate burning in four minutes and did not say it now, and what went on in the space between the two silences under one roof. What the stone is that Tarn took out of its pot and set on the sill in the light, or why he put it back. What ails a glaze-room hand, and what the man carried out on Day 188 cost, and whether he lived or died after he went up the clay road. Whether any count kept under any roof in this yard is about him at all. Who the soft-cuffed clerk is set for, what the waiting man from Slate Row wants with children, what the foundling's green sliver is, and whose mouth the green beside her lip once belonged to. **None of these is to be answered, named or joined in Chapter 56 unless the days themselves earn it.**
