## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and sixty-nine days old at the close of Day 169 and five months and nineteen days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the bare trough; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth, the kindling and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room never entered); down the clay road to the Weir's plank walk, flat blessing stone, lower boards, fish-trade shade and the out-of-line landing below the north bridge; and on Day 169 in the moot-post's finger's width of shade at the Cross with cold iron at his cheek while the crowd leaned over the reading block. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat and was left unwiped on all seven days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its instruction; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down. Nothing within two paces can be reached --- not the crust two inches from his ear, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out, the loaf shelf, the reading block or a child's dark foot.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired seven more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to the fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, a reed-cutter straightening his cord before lifting his trap, Sella reaching a finger beneath the shelf before her eyes went to the row, Sella's glance at the clay jar before anything else, a Sill assessor putting the pigs' figures on a slate with hand to slate before eyes to the rail, and Hessa Rale settling her strap and turning her wrist before her eyes took in the yard. On Day 169 Hessa's reaching was filed as the **eighth** such hand in the district, on a place of its own and joined to none; on the first Account-day the square's doing of it had been counted as nine, with Berr's twice-wiped thumb and finger laid on a figure set among them. This chapter added: that a hand held back from the iron is still a hand that knows where the iron stands, and that when it happens a second time it is not a second change; that a hand that stays under a board for one counted breath tells something the hand that withdraws cannot, and tells it to nobody else in the room; that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, and that buying nothing at two paces is still knowing exactly; that a figure read aloud in a square and a figure locked in a box up the road are two records made by two hands for two purposes, and laying them together and calling them one has already chosen which hand to believe; that a runner who carries and does not read, and a clerk set for a measure nobody called, are two records going inward and not one cause; that two waiting hands are two waiting hands and a likeness between them would call itself a rule if left standing, and a rule is an account that has found a payer. He has stopped expecting to be told what a number is, which is not the same as not wanting it.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb; the river's note as a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the bell's four, four and a gap beside the told bell; three counts in three places that do not become one; and 163 through 169 exact days set edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, carried unspent. He did not call it treachery and he did not call it proof.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and sixty-three through one hundred and sixty-nine days --- five months and thirteen days through five months and nineteen days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That an opening is a thing done and gone past, that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back, that two records made by two hands are not one account, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, that a hand that stays a beat longer than the setting needs tells something the hand that withdraws cannot, that a knowing withheld is not a hearing given, that a hand held back from the iron is still a hand that knows where the iron stands, and that a waiting hand repeated is not a second change. That an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause (T097 advanced, unpaid). **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, tied once low with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit, and on Day 166 the finger held under the board for one counted breath, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look. **T080 carried and advanced, unpaid:** Hessa's record-first order of reaching filed as the eighth such hand in the district, joined to none; the bell four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the heard bell beside the told; the man in Mud End quiet as a lamb and given no figure; the number from a fist arriving nowhere; a soft-cuffed clerk set for an uncalled measure and the girl with a creel taking the landing sideways without a fall. - **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and sixty-nine days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it for a hundred and sixty-nine days. **Who took the pan that stood out to dry through Reed, when, why, or whether the not-saying would have been the same had a woman set it back** --- the round ring is all that is left and the house went around it again on the going of the light on Day 169. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath**, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144, 147, 151, 157, 160 and 166 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 152, 159, 163, 164, 165, 166, 167, 168 and 169. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, and whether the holding back of his finger on Days 161 and 164 means anything at all beyond a sentence finished; what the warm stone in the loft cost, and why it is kept cold. Which house holds the floorboard under Alda's sixth page, and what the second hand says. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, whether he will ever speak, and that the sale is years ahead. Why the green-mouthed woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back, and who she is. What Berr's true subtraction was, and whether the figure read aloud in the square was true or false; what Hessa's slate says and whether she knows; why the soft-cuffed clerk was set down for a measure nobody called and who will take him off. What the boy's number is for, and whether anybody under any roof will ever say it.
