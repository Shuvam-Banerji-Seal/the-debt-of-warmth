## The moment

Day 299, Frost 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, carried there at the going of the light by \person{Sella Vail} from the finger's width of shade under \place{the reading block under the moot-post} at \place{the Cross} and set under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, with the fold at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from it; the empty place beside the crust keeps its name to itself and no third came in these three days to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth has frozen in the dry heat and is unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waits beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house will perform. He knows the composed face that is not composure, knows a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. He wants the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and cannot close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both are true and the second is louder; the surgeon takes the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and enters it nowhere, not even on the place where he keeps the things he does not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now cannot lift a crust to his mouth; the thought came and went without bitterness, which is its own small betrayal. There are two men under the roof and only one of them has a body that can be warmed: the older keeps the inventory and the younger keeps the wanting, and neither has authority over the other, and the naming buys nothing at two inches of air.

Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety-nine days, and nobody lifts the floor and nobody supplies the pull; overhead \place{the boards} keep four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and ninety-nine days old at the close of Day 299 and six months and one hundred and nineteen days (297 and six months and one hundred and seventeen on Frost 28, 298 and six months and one hundred and eighteen on Frost 29, 299 and six months and one hundred and nineteen on Frost 30), carried everywhere: on all three days about the yard at the hearth, loaf shelf, drying racks and rack end, and down and up at the going of the light; on Frost 30 carried up to the Cross and set in the moot-post's finger's width of shade through the reading and carried home; set on his bed under the roof each night, never in \place{the Debt Loft}; the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him, lift a hand to his own chin or lift a crust two inches to his mouth. Chapter 83 opens on Day 300, Bone 1, Firstday, in the small hours under the same Vail roof with the hand still laid down on the **one hundred and seventy-fourth** answer, the file still open on the towel with every gap left gap, the count at **299 exact** --- six months and one hundred and nineteen days told by nobody --- the carried fire's fifth unpaid 299 days with its payer unnamed, and Garl's name unspoken 299 days. Frost went out in its last tend without a boundary drawn; neither habit of naming the months was set right or left wrong. The first firing of Frost stands behind the household with no lesson, sign, mending anybody would name or payer made of it; the widow's mark was touched once before the kiln on Frost 2 and at no other hour; the dressed grain lies covered and unfigured at the rack end with a turning given on each morning of Frost 28, 29 and 30 and read over by three numbers that did not know its cost; Frost's **first, second and third** arithmetics remain unread up the clay road in Berr's locked box with one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the printed figure was read twice on Frost 30, once for the moot and once for the late households. The heard bell is beside the told and the told figure beside the paid; the finding that three readings are three and not one account stands on a place of its own with no payer, not a lesson, grievance, mending or doctrine, and was not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain or the boy's kept gaps. The row question stands at **five askings with one further shaping**, no witness and no row named; nine stoppings stand with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the burned-slate word stands at four, the shut-room tellings at two, the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note apart; the seventh green is a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth; the stayed frost, the skinned trough and the silt-clouded plate of ice stand advanced and not paid (T083/T085), called weather and entered nowhere; the **Frost 14 well-saying stands on a place of its own beside Hessa's eighth hand with a finger between every pair, one saying and three shapings, advanced and not paid (T080)**; and Ilva's **ninth** record-first hand stays nine with the notched strip unread in her sleeve, no tenth filed, the count in wood where no slate can take a figure off it (T080 held, not paid). Tarn's loan stays uncalled and no collector came on the road even on the Account-day; Jorra's promise stays ungiven and \emph{he's not Vail} stays sealed; Garl stays in objects; the charcoal stays beneath the shelf; the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing with his inward slate.

Bone comes on its own morning, the hunting-and-repair month with a hunter's carrying arm, and is not brought into a day that has not earned it. The hand may answer more times and the count may go past 299, but no round number of answers, no three arithmetics, no four openings in four months, no nine hands, no nine stoppings and no three shapings may stand up and call themselves a doctrine or a rule, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stands. Do not supply a tenth hand, a tenth stopping, a seventh hearing, a sixth asking, a fourth shaping, a fourth likeness, a third telling or an eighth green or shape; do not name a payer, do not open the box, the slate, the stick, the charcoal or the tin, do not make a lesson, sign, mending, cause or payment of any of them, and do not join the three readings to the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the stayed frost, the well-saying, the clerk's slate or the boy's own kept gaps. The next real opening stands in Bone's first firing if the household fires, earned by Bone's own morning, five if it fires and not a rule, not hoped for and not counted toward. Nothing may be opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on an unearned day.
