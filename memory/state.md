- **Corin/Dan** — on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear, **one** crust in it with its corner hair-lifted two inches off, and the empty place beside it holding a fold of wool put there once on the fifteenth and found where put on the mornings and at the goings of the light of Stone 28, 29 and 30, still without a name and with no third; awake, silent, drool frozen and unwiped, hand outside the wool having just let a small intake go whole on the river's note under its winter skin at the very end of the year and laid itself back against the fold on the **three hundred and fifty-fourth** answer — exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air and filed on the sixth place with no payer named, not spent or explained. - **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire — at the close she was under the roof, having come to the hearth for the last time in the year that was going out. On Stone 30 she went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I, counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening with neither gap figured; her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze with the sweetish edge coming and going on a wrong wind and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times and was not touched: a market, a going of the light and a bed are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on the year's last day. She carried him down before first light at the half-pace from every open slate, set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade and stood half a pace off through the reading and the bell, giving no instruction and no reason to the basket and saying nothing of what had been read over the wicker; at the going of the light she carried him home, set him on his bed under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. The twice-mended pot was lifted thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle, the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, repair kept apart from restoration. The dressed grain was turned once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, lifting no figure and taking no handful, and the pot rim was set two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. - **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand — last seen on Stone 29 in the afternoon at \place{the kindling}, six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it cannot, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt; \lore{the tile debt} circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. To the hare-carrier, the cracked-jar man, the kindling woman and the fence-menders he said *Yes, house* to each and kept the late thaw, because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back; his chest had been tight since the dark, the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the Vail corner, and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch of \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, the Bone 8 returned half-check staying on the stones as a held change and not a rule. The equality under the roof held by a hair and went unspoken; the household straightened around it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody had ordered the straightening.

- **\person{Jorra Vail}**, 16 — last seen on Stone 29 at the shed's edge and the damper, coming through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. - **\person{Garl Vail}** — absent and the shape of most of the house, kept in objects and not in a story on all three days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder keeps his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed keeps its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody is allowed to fill it; the ridge that was his ash-marker is now the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the month's dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom has gone in two years; the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} carries the hairline of the original break between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person — *Leave the crack showing. He was a big man with clay in the creases of his hands and a laugh that ended in a cough, who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him. His name has not been spoken under this roof in three hundred and eighty-nine days; the keeping of him is maintenance and not a story, and no lesson was made of any of it. ### In the yards, on the road, and farther off

- **\person{Ilva}** — on stage only on Stone 29 at \place{the yard well}, drawing with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Stone cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up, the water dark and moving without a sound; clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. An open slate lay on the lip a pace from where she stood, the writing turned toward its owner and nobody reading it aloud, a nearness and nothing more. To the woman waiting her turn who asked what Stone's firings had taken, her hand went toward her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away, to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, to the cord, to the knot-string or to any saying that had arrived nowhere, and no figure came off the wood. She answered the question put and not the one behind it: *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.* Then she put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice; her thumb did not pass over the notches while the asker waited or after she had gone, and she had not looked into the basket and had no notion she had been read. The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart and filed it on a place of its own as the **ninth** record-first hand, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth; he filed no tenth, made no reader of her keeping, joined her to none and made no figure of the slate's nearness. - **\person{Hessa Rale}** — on stage on Stone 30, going up \place{the clay road} toward \place{Greybank} before noon with Berr Clay's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned toward her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was, the second strap let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud unbrushed on the shortened skirt. The year's last figure did not make her a reader; her **eighth** record-first hand stays eight with no tenth, and she is joined to none — not to a strip in a sleeve at a well a mile away, not to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, not to the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf, not to the clerk's inward slate. - **\person{Berr Clay}**, 48, Thane of Clay — came up short off \place{the clay road} on Stone 30 to the reading block under the moot-post with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one corner of his mouth and, under the hot clay of him, the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: 387 through 389 days — six months and 207 through 209 days — kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; **three hundred and fifty-four answers** stand on the sixth place, and 366 was a whole year stood edge to edge in the only count that is exact, the month he was born in come round again, a date named to nobody, no threshold anybody crosses, and he has stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers count, which is not the same as not wanting it. The order in which cold arrives — cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back — and the order in which a body takes heat back and gives it up, the reverse and sooner than anybody likes; that pink returning is the surface still warm and the core still cold; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, that a tourniquet let go too soon weeps into the dressing, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. That a person's face can be composed because the cold has composed it, and that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways is the careful rewarming he would choose and cannot ask for. That a hand at the fold's gap which closes nothing is exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a warmth laid against the cold side of a vessel that will not hold it; that twelve findings by a hand that only read add no twelfth placing, that a keeping found where put is still a keeping however many hands find it, and that the small alarm at a double count is itself only a finding. That the small satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found costs nothing, which is how he knows it is not peace. That a district's arithmetic can be changed where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box with one figure printed and one paid and a space between that no signature fills; that twelve readings are twelve and not one account, and that the hand that would lay them together and the payer under them do not stand, which is the only victory he has and costs nothing. That an account wants a hand and a payer and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and none stood to any of the kept things.

- **Suspects, without proof and without making a figure of it.** That the house keeps by not-doings and that the keeping may be maintenance rather than mercy; that Sella's hand going toward the crust and coming back inside the wool, her half-beat at the pot's setting, her thumb at the wire, her finger beneath and then on top of the shelf, her glance at the clay jar and her hand half over nothing, and her refusal to touch the mark are a grammar with an order and no speaker; that Tarn's breath at the sill, his stopping at the calling word and his stroke over the bright flagstones without the half-check belong to the loan that can hear; that Jorra's dust, his forearms parted as clot from vessel, his knowing of the full note three turns ahead and his finger's double bite in the iron belong to the room nobody names and the promise neither given nor withdrawn; that Ilva's strip in her sleeve and Hessa's turned writing up the road are keepings of the same order as the charcoal under the shelf, held by hands that put the record before the person; that Berr's twice-wiped thumb, his finger through the sleeve and his taking himself out of his own sentence are the order of a man who removes his hands from a field so that somebody else must close it, and that the belief that he did a kindness is the part of him that will one day be remembered against him; that the fourth direction of Stone 23 and the three older Ash movements are further shapings of one finding about a room and a slate and a man nobody names, and that the finding wants a witness who can be asked. He suspects none of these is a sign, a cause, a payment or a rule, and has filed every suspicion on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other kept thing. The far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account is suspected, not seen, not his and not come.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with answers one hundred and twenty-one through **three hundred and fifty-four** from Frost 4 through the small hours of Stone 30, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, not a freedom, with no payer named; the repeats through Chapters 94 through 112 add no new form. The staying-at-the-inch object, the early letting-go, the handful, the thumb's resting, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the breath at the sill, the holding in the iron, the river's winter skin, the seventh opening, the setting-down-wrong, Sella's wide step around the returning-line place, the fourth room with the gap, the completed year, the keeping at the fold and its twelve findings, and Stone 23's fourth direction sit each on its own place with a finger between every pair; none is a habit, a way, a hearing, a near-full answer, a sign, a mending, a cause, a payer or a rule. - **Does not know.** That he is three hundred and eighty-nine days old, or six months and two hundred and nine days, or that a year has finished, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no \place{Grathic} word in which to say what a life would count; 389 is exact only in the old-life register and he would not say so to anyone. ## What Chapter 113 must not forget
