## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and seventy-nine days old at the close of Day 179 and five months and twenty-nine days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks, the yard well and the bare trough; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth, the kindling and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room never entered by him or the narration); up and down the clay road at the going of the light; and at the Cross on Day 179, set down in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay and lifted again at the going of the light from the side the iron had not cooled. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and seventy-three through one hundred and seventy-nine days --- five months and twenty-three days through five months and twenty-nine days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it, and nobody to give it to. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That an opening is a thing done and gone past, that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back, that two records made by two hands are not one account, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, that a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, that a hand kept away from the board on another morning is a second state and must not stand in for the first, that a displacement made without a reason is still a displacement and must not be joined to a half-pace from open slates and called a cause, that a thing repeated is a thing kept and not the same change twice over, that a thing held nine times is still owed, that a note out of season is a fault in the handling and not a fourth near-full answer, that an order without a name cannot be carried into a chart, that the twice-wiped thumb cleans the field before the instrument goes in, and that a bell keeping a hole in its strokes is not the kiln keeping a gap in its breathing.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause (T097 advanced, unpaid). **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, tied once low with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit, and on Day 166 the finger held under the board for one counted breath, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look; on all seven days of this chapter the finger stayed away and the second state was kept apart from the first. **T062/T061/T082 carried and advanced, unpaid:** three held changes --- the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 held beat beneath the loaf shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- on three places touching neither, three held changes and not a pair, still less a doctrine, with no payer named and no word spoken over any of them. **T080 carried and advanced:** Hessa's record-first reaching as the eighth such hand, joined to none of the other record-first hands. **T078 carried:** the heard bell beside the told, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself and nobody asking why the bell went longer than a church bell. **T050/T051 and T033/T034 held:** the sixth green a colour joined to no ear, the foundling a shape with no sale. Nothing in this chapter made any two of these into a cause.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and seventy-nine days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it through all these days, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing. Who took the pan that stood out to dry through Reed, when, why, or whether the not-saying would have been the same had a woman set it back --- the round ring is all that is left and the house went around it again on the going of the light. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath** and on all seven days of this chapter kept it away, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144, 147, 151, 157, 160 and 166 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 152, 159, 163, 164, 165, 166, 167, 168, 169, 170, 171, 172, 177 and 179. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, and what went on in the space between the finger standing in the iron and Tarn's half-beat on the flagstones; what the warm stone in the loft pot means to the man who keeps it and does not look at it. Why Sella set the basket in the post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay, or stood half a pace off, or gave no word about it at the going of the light; whether the half-pace from open slates and the strip where no slate lay are the same keeping or two, and he has not made them one. What the true subtraction in Berr's locked box actually is, who paid the printed one in twelve, who the figure that went into the slate does not know, whether the box's man believed his kindness, what the clerk's uncalled measure is for, what the cords and knot-strings count, what is in Alda's six pages and both tins, what is in Dessa's pewter tin, what the green sliver at the foundling's throat was taken from, or who the pale line on the green-mouthed woman's hand belonged to; and he did not ask, could not ask, and has nobody to ask of. He does not know that the figure read over him on Day 179 will be paid by somebody, or what a number counted to the crowd that took it without a challenge. He knows the count and knows nobody under any roof shares it.

## What chapter 43 must not forget

Chapter 43 opens on Day 180, Ember 1, Firstday, with the boy one hundred and eighty days old and six months, and nobody saying so. Do not recap this close. The district keeps calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and neither is corrected in narration. Keep the count exact and unspent at **one hundred and seventy-nine** going into Ember and the fire's payer unnamed; the box gone up the clay road locked, the printed one in twelve unchallenged for the third time and the figure read still not the figure paid; Hessa's slate delivered unread to Greybank; the clerk still set at the landing for his uncalled measure. Keep the nine stoppings as objects with no tenth before the seventh year is earned, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, no seventh hearing invented, six hearings beside and never among with the sixth from a person, three near-full answers on their own tray with Jorra's knowing among them. Keep the **three** held changes on three separate places --- the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 held beat beneath the shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- and never let them stand up as a pair, a doctrine or a rule with a payer; do not repeat any as a new change. A first firing of Ember under the Vail roof may bring a real opening, and if it does the widow's mark is touched before the kiln and at no other hour, exactly as on Tallow 1 (T058/T079), with nobody to praise it and nobody to see it broken. Keep the two shut-room tellings with no third, the told bell beside the heard, the widow's mark untouched, Sella's glance at the clay jar without a touch, the charcoal unread, the crust's empty place unnamed, the two shelf gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place on separate trays with no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawing the line. Keep the sixth green a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale; keep Marra out of Sill, Dessa dead and unnamed, Alda's mercy-lie uncorrected, *he's not Vail* sealed and the foundling's name promise ungiven. Do not call the loan, give or cut the seventh year, spend the Seed 16 breath, name a payer to any keeping, open a tin, box or slate, read a private record, bring a third gap, spend the sixth glimpse or make the absences touch. Keep the old life offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break, no departure from the boy's eyes. Forward, not closed.
