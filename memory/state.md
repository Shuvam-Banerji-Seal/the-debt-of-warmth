- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his ear, **one** crust in it and the empty place beside it still without a name, awake, silent, drool frozen and unwiped, hand outside the wool having just let a small intake go whole on the river's note under its winter skin at the very end of the day and laid itself back against the fold on the **three hundred and sixth** answer --- exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air and filed on the sixth place with no payer named, not spent or explained. Three hundred and sixty-five days old and six months and one hundred and eighty-five days, told by nobody that a number has been reached; three hundred and sixty-five is round to the old register and is no threshold anybody speaks, and he has long ago stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. Wants the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted; gets none and cannot ask for any. Holds the six hearings with no seventh, the heard bell beside the told, the told figure beside the paid with **nine** arithmetics locked away and the finding on a place of its own with no payer, **seven openings in seven months** with the seventh done before the kiln on Stone 5 and no eighth made due, **three hundred and six honest answers** on the sixth place and not one a doctrine, the river-under-skin keeping on its own place beside water-over-water, the dressed grain covered and unfigured under a number that did not know its cost, the **handful** let fall back at the rack end on Bone 22 as one object and not made again, the stayed-at-the-inch object and the early letting-go as two objects on two places and not made again, **Tarn's thumb's resting on the seventh notch** as one object on its own place and not made again, the **pot-crack resting of Ash 9**, the **held breath at the knife-sill of Ash 14** checked again on all three days and found where put, **the extra holding Jorra left in the cold iron on Ash 26** found where put and not made again, and the **setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around of Stone 5** on a place of its own beside the premature note of Smoke 14, gathered once and not spent. None is a sixth, seventh or eighth of anything, no payer stands under any, and none was laid across any other.

- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the close she carried him up at the going of the light on Stone 6 from the Clay Yard, at the half-pace from every open slate with the whole of her attention on the basket's level, went back around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number and widest around the returning-line place, set him on his bed under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, her hand resting the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of the hare, the jar, the kindling or the mending; that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker. On all three days the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the wind and the river wind and never to the basket. She went to the hearth before the basket and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I. On all three mornings and at all three goings of the light she told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment counting small intake, hold and letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, the cord of her \lore{widow's mark} oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times; she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person and left the hairline showing; she turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure and no handful; and she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. On Stone 5 she touched the mark once before the kiln, held one breath and let go, then lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot; she set the basket level out of the draught, and did not touch the mark again at shelf, well, rack, yard, going of the light or bed. To neighbours who asked what the firing cost she answered *Ask the pot. It knows what a firing takes. So does the weather, and neither of them keeps a slate.* Seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule and she made no lesson of the one she had done.

- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- last seen on Stone 6 in the Clay Yard at \place{the kindling}, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it cannot, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off; he passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching what little light there was and giving it back, and the hand went away when the checking was done and no breath stayed at the top of the reach. **The held breath he left at the sill on Ash 14 was where it had been put:** checked again on all three days, found, and not made again; filed on a place of its own beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair, not a sixth held object, not a seventh of anything, not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not the calling of the loan, not a habit, a way, a sign, a hearing, a near-full answer, a mending, a cause, a payment or a rule, with no payer standing under it. The Bone 26 thumb's resting was likewise found where put and not made again. He said *Six counted. The next I don't count* to the wood and *Yes, house* to the early thaw and the late, keeping the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped, the ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach and the loan uncalled; he did not look along the road. His chest had been tight since the dark, the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, the returned half-check of Bone 8 staying where it lay. The seventh year stands at its very end and nothing in the tending of Stone, the firing, the river's skin, the seventh opening or the absence of a voice paid any of it.

- **\person{Jorra Vail}**, 16 --- last seen on Stone 6 at the shed's edge and the damper, coming through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey on all three afternoons and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* --- and told a pot *You'll do* in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time; the boy kept the knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them, and no seventh hearing stood up in the empty place. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted --- one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \place{Grathic} word for both mercies. **On Ash 26 he left the finger standing a breath longer than the work required after the sentence was finished and the household had straightened; on every day since, including Stone 4, 5 and 6, he took it out when the sentence had arrived and did not leave the extra holding a second time, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, both of which want a payer and none stood.** *He's not Vail* stays sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn. His knowing of the pot's note was not joined to the setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around of Stone 5.

- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- absent and the shape of most of the house, kept in objects and not in a story on all three days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder keeps his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed keeps its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody is allowed to fill it; the ridge that was his ash-marker is now the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both and thickened in the month's dry cold; the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} carries the hairline of the original break between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled* and *If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one* --- improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man said them. Repair stays apart from restoration because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The unforged collector sentence about the man who turned back at the frozen ford lies beside the knife at the kindling sill. He was a big man with clay in the creases of his hands and a laugh that ended in a cough, who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him. The debt against a firing of tiles remains neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled; his name has not been spoken under this roof in three hundred and sixty-five days; the keeping of him is maintenance and not a story, and no lesson was made of any of it, not even of the firing on Stone 5. The held breath at the sill belonged to a living man and was not laid across his objects. Full sheet at slug `garl-vail`.

### In the yards, on the road, and farther off

- **\person{Ilva}** --- on stage once in this chapter, on Stone 4 (Day 363), at \place{the yard well}, drawing with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Stone cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up, the water dark and moving without a sound; clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and the people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A slate lay open a pace away on the stone lip with the writing turned toward whoever had left it, and nobody was reading it aloud. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat between the looking and the word; she answered the question put and not the one behind it: *Ask the pot what it cost. - **\person{Hessa Rale}** --- offstage on Days 363, 364 and 365; last on stage at the Cross and on the road on Ash 30 (Day 359), before noon taking the slate up the road toward \place{Greybank} at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. I've got a road to walk.* She went on leaving the rest of the conversation standing where it had been set down, without rudeness and without apology. She is a runner and not a reader, of Slate Row but the soft-handed man's opposite, with working hands and not soft ones, and the boy did not make a reader of her because of a reading that had gone up under her. ### The Account-days and the world asleep

The district's arithmetic changed for the ninth time on Ash 30 (Day 359) and cannot be changed back; the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged over the basket with the figure read still not the figure paid, and the true subtraction went up the clay road locked in Berr's box beside the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone. **Nine arithmetics of the year now stand behind one unsigned figure, nine and not one account**, with the finding held on a place of its own and no payer standing and no doctrine out of nine. Stone 4, 5 and 6 were ordinary tending days with no Cross, market or bell, no collector on the clay road, no runner on any road and no ownerless voice over the boards; the river lay under its skin on all three and the boy spent nothing on it, the first firing came and went on Stone 5 with no figure and no lesson, and no fourth arithmetic was brought into a month that has not earned one. The deep-winter month holds without a boundary drawn; its first firing stands behind the household, the first birthday on Stone 7 (Day 366) stands one morning ahead as a date named to nobody, and the Hearth Drawing on Stone 12 (Day 371) further still. Far-off in the dark: Marra is not in Sill; the foundling's seventh half-turn gathers toward a sale years away; Alda's mercy-lie has curdled into furniture; the waiting man from Slate Row and the Waiting Room clerk take small secrets for loaves and ale; Rennick is already asking why forty unspent measures are needed for one intact one; Solene Ember removes a ring one at a time in a room with good light and does not know the boy exists; Hald Iron reads Haldre's paper from a tin lined with warm-stone and Siv keeps the crossed-out survey; Pia of the Quay does not yet know him; the Waste takes what nobody keeps. The reincarnation's old life stays asleep behind the boy's eyes and was named to nobody in these three days.

## Open dangers

- **The danger of a year begun, paid small and non-fatally, now with nine arithmetics gone up the road and a seventh opening held behind the household's firings.** The first firing of Stone was made on the morning of Day 364 and stands behind the household with no lesson anybody names and no payer; it was made out of the first opening since Ash 2, and the kiln stood cool through the close. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: 363 through 365 days --- six months and 183 through 185 days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; three hundred and six answers stand on the sixth place, and three hundred and sixty-five is round to the old register only, a number nobody under any roof speaks or marks and no threshold anybody crosses, and he has stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers count, which is not the same as not wanting it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back and gives it up, the reverse and sooner than anybody likes; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, that a tourniquet let go too soon weeps into the dressing, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. That a person's face can be composed because the cold has composed it, and that pink returning to the skin is the surface still cold and not the core; that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways is the careful rewarming he would choose and cannot ask for. The grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than *I*, and a reading called a kindness, entered to a block that cannot answer. That a district's arithmetic can be changed where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box with one figure printed and one paid and a space between nobody signed; that a figure can be sent over a basket twice and be one figure spoken two times; that nine such readings are nine and not one account, and that averaging them would want a payer put under all nine and none stands; that a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. That a person can carry a record for years and still not be its reader and can carry a figure up a road unread; that a voice may arrive nowhere and a sighting is not a payment; that weather and a price can answer a saying first and last and leave it standing nowhere; that a direction is not a witness and a reason spoken by nobody is not a reason; that a hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and that what stood under the roof on Stone 5 was a doing put behind the household with no lesson made of it.

- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with answers one hundred and twenty-one through **three hundred and six** from Frost 4 through the small hours of Stone 6, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, not a freedom, with no payer named; the repeats through Chapters 94 through 104 add no new form. The staying-at-the-inch object, the early letting-go, the handful, the thumb's resting, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the breath at the sill, the holding in the iron, the river's winter skin, the seventh opening and the setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around sit each on its own place with a finger between every pair; none is a habit, a way, a hearing, a near-full answer, a sign, a mending, a cause, a payer or a rule. The nine arithmetics stand as nine on nine places and not one account, the finding on a place of its own with no payer and no doctrine stood up. The heard bell, the told figure, the seventh colour, the seventh shape, the nine hands, the nine stoppings, the five askings, the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the fourth shaping, the Ash 6 turning, the sweet glaze wind that touched the fold and went out, the thickened ridge, the breath at the sill, the river's skin and the setting-down-wrong stand each on a place of its own with a finger between, and were not laid across one another.

- **Does not know.** That he is three hundred and sixty-five days old, or six months and one hundred and eighty-five days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no \place{Grathic} word in which to say what a life would count; three hundred and sixty-five is round to him only in the old-life register and he would not say so to anyone. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though Sella glanced at it and shut her hand half over nothing on these mornings and evenings, and her little-door burn closing on air. What the widow's mark mended, why the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, or why the household's first firing of Stone came on Stone 5 and not on the turning of the month; he has seen the act and set it down as maintenance on a vessel, not as a sign or a payment. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reached a finger beneath it for one counted breath on Day 166 and for two on Frost 5 and came away both times without a word;** he read the change in the hand and not the charcoal, and does not know what she counts under her breath at the fire, and put a finger between her sound and his three. Why Sella's thumb stayed one breath longer at the wire crossing on Ash 9, why Tarn's hand stayed a breath longer at the knife-sill on Ash 14, why Jorra left his bent finger a breath longer in the cold iron on Ash 26, and whose hand went around the wrongly set pot on Stone 5 --- whether any meant anything, whether any was a habit of the hand or a thing a man would do again, and who might one day read them; he has the intervals and their edges and nothing else, made no payer of them and no lesson, and none was made again on these three days. What the men want who come before light to the back room each afternoon and who keep the room open and low-voiced, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men who burn slates are the same men; what was carried out on Day 188 and what ailed him, and whether the pot's premature note has anything to do with it. Who laid the winter skin on the river, and whether it will be taken off, and when; what the hand that went in on Seed 15 was looking for, and whether a body will ever be found. What the burned slate said, which room was shut, who burned it and why a count would not go up; whether a witness will ever stand somewhere and can be asked; whether Fenna's ear or Ulda's sale to the Waiting Room clerk will ever come due, and what becomes of the foundling. What the nine subtractions in Berr's locked box truly are and why the figure printed knows nothing of the figure paid; whether anyone will sign the space between; whether heat, dust, glaze and sayings have any cause at all. **He does not know that a hand will one day lay the district's private keepings together, and if he did he would not know it was not his.** The reincarnation's old life stays asleep behind his eyes and was named to nobody in these three days.

## What Chapter 105 must not forget

Chapter 105 opens in the small hours of Day 366, Stone 7, Seventhday, with the hand still laid down on answer **three hundred and six** and the count still exact at **three hundred and sixty-five** --- six months and one hundred and eighty-five days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number, a round number nobody under any roof speaks and no threshold anybody crosses. Nothing from Stone 4, 5 or 6 crosses forward as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer: the first firing of Stone stands behind the household with no lesson anybody names, and the seventh opening is seven in seven months and not a rule with no eighth made due; the setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around stays gathered once on its own place beside the premature note of Smoke 14; the river went under its winter skin on the first afternoon and the boy spent nothing on it, and it was not spent on the firing or on any date; Ilva's ninth hand at the well on Stone 4 came and went without a tenth and her absence on Stone 5 and 6 promoted nothing. Carry forward unchanged and unspent: **three hundred and six honest answers** on the sixth place, each an object and not a power and not one standing up and calling itself a doctrine; **nine arithmetics of the year --- the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash's first, Ash's second and Ash's third --- behind one unsigned figure, nine and not one account**, with all three Ash subtractions locked and unread in Berr Clay's box up the clay road, the key in a coat pocket that does not come back down, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space; six hearings with the seventh place empty; the heard bell beside the told, four, four and a gap; nine record-first hands with no tenth (Ilva's ninth, Hessa's eighth); nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled; five held objects with the pot-crack resting beside them, the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that and the holding of Ash 26 beside all, each found where put and not made again; the row at five askings with no witness; the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, and the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction held apart with a finger between every pair, all advanced and not paid; **the river's winter skin on its own place beside water over water, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing, not a sign, not a cause, not a payment and not spent on any date.** The birthday on Stone 7 (Day 366) is lived as an ordinary day with no party, no cake, no figure and no recognition from anybody under any roof, and the Hearth Drawing on Stone 12 (Day 371) stands three mornings beyond that close, unbrought. No ownerless voice, no collector, no runner, no market, Cross or bell, no green-mouthed woman or foundling sighting and no eighth of anything. The boy's first birthday is not brought into the day as a lesson, a mending, a payment or a threshold; nothing stands up and calls itself a doctrine out of the round number. The hand goes on being laid down where the next hand will find it without looking.
