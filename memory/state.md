## The moment

Day 119, Seed 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, Account-day and the last market of Seed, at the going of the light. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, \person{Sella Vail} has carried \person[Corin]{the boy} up from the moot-post's shade at \place{the Cross} at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody in this house has been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the way a hand rests on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot bring itself to let go of. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath that no man could take up as a figure, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, set the pot where it could be found in the dark, and did not look toward the bed. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals; she says it came in the winter Garl died and will not discuss it further. It is told of her in the yard that she once sat three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and that *your house has had practice*; the story has come from other mouths and never from hers, and she has never laid it beside the dark foot below the sill. The carried fire took its coal under blind ash and the back of a broken pot and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees, as it has for a hundred and nineteen days; the payer of the coal has gone unnamed for all of them. The door stands a finger's width open. Through the gap comes the river going over itself with the low note of water over water; through the floor comes the kiln's four, four and a gap. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and nineteen days old at the close of Day 119, three months and twenty-nine days, carried everywhere: at the edge of the worked strips (a direction, not a place he can reach), the loaf shelf in the kiln-house, the Clay Yard, the yard well, the wheel-shed's edge (the back room not entered), the damper, the stalls beyond the Cross, the end of the fish-trade shade at the Fishstairs, just inside the finger's-width-open door of House Silt, the moot-post's shade at the Cross and lifted onto the bed under the roof every night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup, close two inches on the basket's rim or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the four days, because nobody has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from the wrist so the cold finds the bare place and goes under the fold at his ear. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 118 without knowing it and covered the sign with a dead woman's date. He wants the stiff brown-edged crust, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted; he gets none of them and can ask for none of them. He has stopped expecting to be told and has not stopped wanting it.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired four more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs, the Slate Row clerk at the Cross writing a figure in a second hand, Alda going to her slate before the fold and then to the tin in her bodice, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the cord while deciding, Jorra speaking to the wheel before the pots and the people, Sella giving the instruction to the broom, the bucket, the wool, the water, the pot and the fire before she gives it to anybody, two neighbours reaching to the shelf before they looked at the boy, the weigher, the buyer and the man with the slate in the market, and his own watching of that order. This chapter added: that a district can go around an absence for a month and call the going-around care, and a place gone around with a look is still a place entered by nobody; that a hand that went in and did not come out is entered in no slate until a body is found, and a figure given then would be a different thing from the one withheld now; that obedience arriving before the instruction that calls it is obedience and a correction at once, and neither is named; that a stroke which checked yesterday and goes on today are two objects on one tray and not a rule, and the flagstones keep the place where a stroke stopped though no foot will enter it; that a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and the hearing of a joining is not the making of it; that two faces turned the same way can mean opposite things, and there is no Grathic word for the difference between keeping and pricing; that a colour on a mouth is a colour on a mouth until a day it wants an ear, and filing it buys nothing; that a cost with no figure is still a cost and everywhere in Low Kiln the cost has a payer nobody enters; that a mercy-lie laid over a sign in a dead woman's face curdles into a fact no living person can take off; and that a figure read aloud over the very ground that took a hand does not know the hand, the strip or the cost.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and nineteen days, three months and twenty-nine days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space. The order in which warmth is given out and the order in which a body loses it, especially the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip, that a figure withheld in the very act of planting is withheld like any other figure, and that a figure read from a slate at a date is entered everywhere --- the same missing thing in different clothes. That a cost with no figure is still a cost and every cost in Low Kiln has a payer nobody will name. That every warmth has a payer, so ask who paid before asking what a thing is worth. That a breath stopped behind a clamp is a breath spent whether it goes in or not and the price is in the hand that holds the iron. That a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the unsaid sentence is kept with the year a man will not count. That a household straightens around a silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, obedience arriving before the instruction and being also a correction nobody will name. That agreeing with both sides of a question costs nothing in words and everything somewhere else, and a frightened man becomes helpful to keep a fourth thing from being seen. That the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes a hand off a man are one piece of iron with no word in Grathic for both, and that a stroke checked one day and going on the next are two intervals on one tray and not a cause. That a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, that the hearing of a joining is not the making of it, and that a line drawn through three absences will send a man looking for a clean hand to sign it. That two faces turned the same way can mean opposite things --- keeping and pricing --- with no Grathic word for the difference. That a place gone around with a look is a place entered by nobody, and a hand that went in and did not come out stays unentered until somebody finds a body and invents a figure. That a mercy-lie laid over a sign curdles into a fact no living person can take off.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the neighbours' one-breath joinings of an old gap to the absence at his ear. **T098 planted and unpaid:** the field's empty place in the returning line, gone around with a look and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** two tellings on two places beside the earlier tellings and the watcher's late turn, no fourth likeness and no third gap. The direction become worked ground, held beside the number the yard would not give and now beside the hand the yard would not count, still two things on two places. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's check on one day and his stroke going on without it on the next, set beside the bent finger and the held breath without any of them joined. The green at a silent woman's mouth beside Berr's green and the Silt 30 green, three colours on three places, no name and no ear. The delay in the foundling's turn: after the knock, not before it, not a hearing and not a seventh, and his own turning kept beside it without touching. The cold foot, uncured, uncaused and unnamed, the cold and the weather never in the same sentence. The soft hands and the loaf on separate trays, not joined and not called a sale, and the soft hands a sixth shape kept off every record-first tray. Berr's true subtraction, which he knows exists because the figure said is not the figure paid, but which he cannot read or carry. The sixth hearing out of a person, the three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the unspoken word with its readable shape, the two shut-room tellings and the damper's double bite. Two women keeping two records in wool and knots on boards that do not touch, and a clerk set at a landing for a measure nobody called --- set down ahead of a death, a shape he does not make a rule of. The heard bell kept beside the told bell in two places although they are the same four and the same missing stroke. The unforged collector sentence kept with the seventh year; the sealed sentence *he's not Vail*; the promise of the foundling's name neither given nor withdrawn.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and nineteen days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it for a hundred and nineteen days. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one in three. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, and that the sale is years ahead. What the cords and knots count, what the word forty counts, and who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over. What the silt-leach is, or that the cold in the child's foot has a name and a habit Arc 3 away. Which hand went into the strips on Seed 15 and did not come out on Seed 27, whether it is a hand or a body that will be found or a figure that will be given, and whether anybody will ever enter the place in the line. What the green on the silent woman's mouth will one day be read as, or whose mouth it will be joined to. Whether the loan that can hear can be called, and what must give at the end of Tarn's seventh year. Whether the two tellings of the shut room belong under his own roof. Who paid for the fire, the gruel, the seed, the hands and the loaves, and why every cost in Low Kiln has a payer nobody will name. He does not know that his exactness is the only arithmetic in the district, or that keeping it is at once his whole dignity and the proof that he has no standing in the house he has never left.

## Open dangers

- **The planting crossed out of its last tend and one hand did not come back with the rest.** The worked brown skin of the strips east of Low Kiln holds the heel-marks, the little dark pits and the pale dust of broken clods; the hands went in on Seed 15 in glances and handfuls and never a figure, a man brought a number up out of a fist on Seed 24 and put it back in his sleeve, and on Seed 27 the hands came out one short. **T098:** the yard goes around the place with a look and no number; if a hand is lifted out of the ground in a month or a year a slate will be found and a figure made to stand at its foot, and if no hand comes up the space will go on being gone around and the month will go on standing short without a figure. No name, cause, body, accident or payment. Carry it into Reed, gather interest, do not pay. On Day 119 Berr read one in twelve over the very absence and the figure that went into the slate did not know there had been a hand or a strip. The bell's four, four and a gap was **heard** on Day 119 and nobody crossed himself; the heard bell is kept beside the told bell and must not be joined to it. The steps at the Fishstairs came back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the whole market walked over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off; a measure set down before anybody called it is set down ahead of a death. The district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold.** A woman wound it in three turns of wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing. - **Below the sill's other edge, Tarn's seventh year stands four days nearer its end and the loan that can hear is still uncalled.** The knife's worn mark has six years in it and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check; to the kindling he said *Six counted. The next I don't count* and pressed the held breath to its edge without spending it, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where the breath should have gone in. He was sent for a collector once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year. His broom went over the collector's road without once turning and no collector came; Jorra's silence and his keep the equality. **The change was held and not repeated:** Jorra's bent finger led at the damper again and Tarn's stroke went on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch where it had stopped as a thing nobody would enter, and neither man named the other's silence. The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence that would call it has never been said under this roof; something must give at the end of the seventh year and has not given yet. Jorra's dust, the room standing a finger's width open, the unnamed men, the space under the rack and the broken birth-slates beneath it are keepings no slate can enter; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed. - **On a day in the last war a room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath**, their heat gone out with no way to feed it, as told by a man over a bucket at the well in chapter 13 and by a second man come downriver from Greybank on Day 84, and only *mentioned* by a third voice at the bucket-round on Day 102, each as a dated curiosity eighteen leagues east with the same words *not the war's hand*, differing only in breath through the nose against breath made in the throat. - **No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission**; she is more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and still unentered, the number gone into the water in her thumb. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument. - **Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness;** it was not touched once in four days, because a strip, a rack, a shelf, a well, a bucket, a road, a market, a post, a door, a basket and a bed are not openings and no opening came. - **The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful;** what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the shut counting rooms eighteen leagues east, the damper called a holding and never a taking, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and House Silt is a fifth such room set beside them;** the boy has kept all five apart on purpose, and no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work.
