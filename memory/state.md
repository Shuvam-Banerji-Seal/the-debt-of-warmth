# State of things — at the close of chapter 6 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 7. Days 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 have been lived, one to a scene: a first light in which a kindling stack was measured and a midwife wrote down what she would not say; a morning in which a man went still for two breaths at a kindness and a pot gave nothing; a day at the well where the old life broke through over a hand reaching for a slate; a dusk at which a hairline in river ice was held up to a tallow light; and Account-day at the Cross, where a figure was read, a clerk's soft sentence finished a woman's business, and the pause in the neat dark hand came a fourth time without joining the other three. Nothing has been explained. The boy is fourteen days old, his private file has grown by a missing fifth bundle, two intervals, a sign at his wrist, a shard of ice and a fourth pause, and the crust in the fold has gone from warm to cold without ever being eaten. The district has read one in twelve and will not say what it counts; the Five are eleven days off.*

## The moment

Day 14 is done. On Stone 20, Tenthday, Account-day, Sella Vail carried the boy down before the light had cleared the sill and set the basket at the north end of \place{the Cross} in the moot-post's finger's width of shade, keeping her half-pace from every open slate all the way. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. \person{Berr Clay} wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched the slate and read *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the top of a crowd of two hundred; the levy was called for the feast and was not called by him. He went around \person{Garl Vail}'s uncalled debt against the firing of tiles the way a woman goes around Ash left in a corner, set the printed figure where everyone could see it, and did not look into the basket. At the Court's table at the Slate Row fringe a collector's clerk with a cuff turned back twice from washing laid his hand on the top slate before he looked at the woman waiting, and said it was entered as it stood and there was nothing in it the household need correct. The upper line was in the strange neat dark hand, and the small stop between two clusters of strokes came a fourth time --- the orange flare light, the birth-slate, this square on Stone 10, now this slate --- and lay beside the other three without joining them. The Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in it stayed in its own place.

At dusk Sella carried him up the yard with woodsmoke and river wind caught under the fold, put the fold right without looking at his face, set the basket on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot and said *that'll hold*, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}: a reading is not an opening. \person{Tarn} set the small bench knife back on its sill and told the yard he had done so, though the yard was empty, and his broom stopped half a stroke at the place where a pot had been set down and given nothing. \person{Jorra Vail} came in from the shed with bone-coloured slip and no slate dust, spoke to the pot and moved the damper before she had finished telling him the fire was low, and did not say the back room was cold or look toward the bed. Garl's coat kept its salt shape on the peg, his seat its hollow on the left, the grey ridge ran to the corner and turned it without a break, and the proverb went to the fire and not to the basket. The Five are eleven days off.

Day count: **Day 14 complete.** Day 15 is Stone 21, Y.H. 812, a Firstday. The household is counting fuel and bread into the tend without saying what it counts; the district has read its figure and gone back to the pigs; the boy is the only person in Low Kiln keeping the days as a number and has no hand, word or standing to enter one.

The four earlier days of this chapter, held in full:

- **Day 10, Stone 16, Sixthday.** First light under the Vail roof. The coals held under ash; Sella touched the widow's mark before the lid and at no other hour; the pot stood where it had stood on Stone 13; the crust was cold in the fold. Tarn measured the kindling with his eyes --- four, four, a space where the fifth should be --- and told the stack six were counted and the seventh held back; Sella moved her lips along the loaf shelf so the days to Kindling stood as a shape no collector could take up. Jorra worked in bone-coloured slip with no slate dust and moved the damper before she finished. \person{Alda Reed} came mid-morning, reached for her slate before the child, touched the tin once inside her bodice, pressed the fold flat too long, crossed two fingers at the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} at his wrist without knowing it and covered the sign with an unasked-for date; on a narrow slate she wrote what she did not leave lying about, turned it writing-down into her shawl toward a floorboard in her house he does not know, and went on giving exact dates after the question had stopped. *Warm and breathing --- that's all I've set down.*
- **Day 11, Stone 17, Seventhday.** Tarn swept before light, set the knife on the sill and announced it unasked; six years counted, the seventh held back. When Sella said *you've done well* the broom stopped altogether for two breaths and he said *Yes, house* and went on. A pot was turned toward on the bench and gave nothing; the boy turned before the set-down, Tarn's broom checked half a stroke, and neither named the second interval --- filed beside the first, with the hearing count still five from four sources. Sella named the kiln's breath as the danger the house lived beside; Tarn said a man in Mud End had slept in front of a kiln with the damper shut and the district had called it a merciful death. Later he scraped Ash from his knuckles with the knife, held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist to the light and put the knife back where it could be checked. The boy's hand went up an inch toward the broom; Tarn did not look.
- **Day 12, Stone 18, Eighthday.** The number of days went around the yards in a closed fist and arrived under the roof as an absence. A neighbour at the threshold held her slate to her chest, spoke around Garl's debt and called the sharing a kindness that was entered where it was entered; Sella stood a half-pace off, said *the house* and told her to ask the pot. At \place{the yard well} another neighbour set her slate on the stone lip before she put a hand out to her child; for two sentences the hospital broke through in the present tense --- a ceiling light at four, a chart with its note turned face-down, a hand past it toward a signature, a pocket holding wool where a lit phone with a name had lain --- and the river wind closed over it. He filed the reaching hand with the midwife's, the clerk's, the man who priced him and the woman who kept him, and looked at no name.
- **Day 13, Stone 19, Ninthday.** A man come up from \place{the Ilm} held a palm's breadth of ice in which a dark hair ran and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge. A woman from \place{High Kiln} said the thaw would come early; neighbours who wanted it late contradicted her because the levy fell in the thaw, and Tarn said a late thaw was a longer week before the paying. Sella called it weather. A neighbour back from \place{the Court's room} described a bench by the door, a pot on it, a draught going to the corner and a place in the figures where a line should stand and did not; the boy set the room beside the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house in four separate places and made nothing of the likeness. Sella measured the ridge with her thumb --- near two thumbs here, a finger more two doors down --- and said it would be swept on \lore{the Last Day}. Sella slid the basket out of the draught without looking at his face.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, fourteen days old by the night of Day 14. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: in the basket at Sella's hip, set in the moot-post's thin shade with cold iron at his cheek, lifted back onto the bed under the roof. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth freezes and nobody wipes it, because a basket at the edge of a board is a pot set down and left. The head goes where it wants and makes a small wet sound when it goes too far, and the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward a broom, a pocket-place, a shard of ice held to the light, the warmth, and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; its red edge showed at the wrist on Days 10 and 11, and Alda crossed two fingers at it without knowing she did. Cold comes in at cheek, temple and wrist, in the order he knows and cannot argue with. He wants the breast and the crust at once and got neither; the crust is cold now, stiff at the edges, smelling of smoke and barley, and stays in the fold.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired a household scale and a fourth object it cannot use. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk at a Court's table, the man who priced him and the woman who kept him, and recognises it as his own habit from a white room. He knows a thing that does not happen leaves a space with the shape of a sound, that an interval is not a hearing, and that a pause is not a hearing either. He knows that a man can be stopped for two breaths by a kindness from the woman who holds his debt, that a loan that can hear is worth more than the debt that holds a man and is therefore dangerous, and that the house calls dangerous things weather or mercy because both let a man go home warm. He knows that the district puts its arithmetic into loaves, fists and absences so no figure can be taken up and entered, and that the same grammar that finished the woman at the Court's table is the grammar of the chart he signed without reading its note. He knows that four rooms --- birth room, kiln-house, death-house, Court's room --- hold the same bench, pot, draught and gap, and has kept them in four separate places rather than write a cause where he has noticed a likeness. He is the only adult in every room and the least use in it; correctness is the whole humiliation, and he has learned to find it funny without laughing.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the floorboards' four, four and a knot the width of a thumb; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the bell's four, four and a gap; the Fishstairs steps' four, four and a landing that is not a step; the kindling's four, four and a space where a fifth bundle should lie; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the prints of a man come up from the river, four and four and a slide; the hairline in the ice that stops short of the edge. The ear holds \lore{the full note} in five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and this chapter added no sixth. The two pots turned toward on the bench gave nothing and left intervals, not hearings; the hearing count is unchanged after fourteen days. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it. The knowing buys nothing: no hand has reached a vessel, nobody has tapped a slate for him, and nobody has said what the sound was.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Changed:** the brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 is still there, now cold and stiff; he can feel its cold through the wool but cannot lift or eat it, and Sella has neither taken it out nor called it a kindness. **Lost/unchanged:** the small pale river stone, colour of skimmed milk, remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in \place{House Silt}, unmentioned. The small bench knife is back on its sill, announced twice unasked; the charred slate still lies in the kiln ash; the basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word; Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched once and never opened. **Money:** none to the boy and none changes hands to him. Figures go past him and are not his to spend: one in twelve, a levy called for the feast, eleven days to the Five, the district's closed fist of days, a ridge near two thumbs and a finger's width, four loaves on the seventh of Stone, nineteen in a woman's thumb. The gruel, the fire, the basket and the crust remain entered nowhere; every kindness has a payer who can be named and a recipient who cannot refuse it.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was reached for twice this chapter, at the well and in the Cross, and each time the hand found wool, woodsmoke and river wind where a lit phone with a name had lain; the name was not looked at then and cannot be looked at now. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature came back at the well; the orange flare light and the hand in the grey sleeve that stopped between two clusters came back at the Cross and made a fourth sighting, still unread. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a failure of the system, were recognised in the grammar of Berr's passive sentence and the clerk's soft one. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one; Des's laugh did not return. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his chin and the cold crust in the fold at his side, awake, silent, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. Wants the breast, wants the crust, wants a word to correct the room; gets none of the three and cannot ask for any.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the fire after banking the coals with the back of a broken pot, having carried him down to the Cross and back with a half-pace kept from every open slate. Her hand went to the widow's mark before each opening and at no other hour, not at the bank and not on Account-day; the little-door burn shut on nothing all day. She said *the house* at the threshold, asked the neighbour to ask the pot, moved the basket out of the draught and would not have called it love, measured the Ash by thumb and said leave it until the Last Day. Has not written him as Vail, keeps a private roll in a careful hand she will not show, and will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take his coat down. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained; the Greybank confirmation and the day's reading have made that harder without giving her anyone to answer.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- in the yard and under the roof, broom set against the wall, small knife back on the sill and announced twice unasked. Six years of seven counted; he will not count the seventh, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. He stopped altogether for two breaths at *you've done well*, then said *Yes, house*. His broom has twice stopped half a stroke at the bench where no note came; he has said nothing about either and will say nothing. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist and the mark worn almost flat on the knife handle are his own records. He named the kiln's breath, told of the merciful death in Mud End, wanted the late thaw, and offered to check the ridge. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and grudges the house the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence has never been said in the house and lies in it all the same.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- in from the wheel-shed on all five days with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms, bent little finger, and no slate dust. Said *you'll do*, *the shoulder's thick*, and *the wheel doesn't care whose hands, only if they're steady*; shifted the damper before Sella had finished and left the end of her sentence where it was. Did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the bed. Still wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself. The sentence *he's not Vail* is as sealed as on the hour it was spoken; the broken slates under the drying rack and the men whose names are not spoken remain unshown.
- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- absent and the shape of most of the house. Not collected in the Long Cold; his warmth lies where it fell, the \lore{Ash} ridge is its shape, the coat keeps his broad shape on the peg, the seat keeps his twenty-year leftward lean, and the ridge is unswept and will not be swept before the Last Day. His debt against a firing of tiles was spoken around at the Cross again on Stone 20, never named, and remains neither paid nor settled nor done with. His proverbs live in the house's mouth: *feed the fire before you feed yourself*, and *leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled.*

## Open dangers

- The year's first measure was taken at the Drawing out of a dying neighbour and entered in the figure the district reads aloud; Account-day has now come and gone with one in twelve read as the notice says, and the boy could not address, refuse, reach or correct it. The household has counted fuel, bread and kindling against the days before Kindling, and the number goes around as a fist and enters the house only as an absence.
- He is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and does not know he is inside the third. Sella's private roll is still unshown and the wall around it held; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's grey wool is a record no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter; Alda's second note has been written and turned down toward a floorboard he cannot name. The neat upper line has now been seen four times and the writer still has no name in his file.
- Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness; it is held while the fire never goes out and touched only before an opening, never at a bank, a reading or a dusk. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles has been spoken around through another Account-day and remains never paid, never settled, never done with.
- Tarn has heard a flat note and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, and has now stood still for two breaths at a kindness he cannot enter. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. Jorra's no-dust forearms and the back room, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and both not-lookings, and Dessa's count in her thumb are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named now as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful. What the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy; the crack in the Ilm, the debt and the Ash are all called something that lets a man go home warm. A woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and the yard wants it late because the levy falls in the thaw; the argument is about a figure nobody will say.
- The child on the quilt in the death-house was worse on the third day of its return and the room called it a graft settling. Alda's two written notes are turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the second cause stays in the tin and in the struck word nobody speaks. The boy's own flower has been crossed at by a woman who did not know she did it and covered with a date.
- The Ash in the death-house is thicker by a finger's width than the ridge at Vail, near two thumbs here; both turn corners without a break and neither will be swept before the Last Day. The charred slate in the kiln ash and the barrows of grey going out through the north gate are unread. The Court's room has the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept the four likenesses apart on purpose.
- The flat note has five hearings from four sources and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, and went four, four and a gap over a square where nobody crossed himself. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling and the space between a woman's thumb and her scar; the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter.
- Low Kiln reads one in twelve and will not say what it counts. The Ilm has a hairline crack and the Singing Sill goes on giving its low note without explanation. The Drawing is past, the year's first measure is gone, Account-day is done, and the Five are eleven days off. Chapter 7 will give a fifth sighting of the neat hand and a number the district changes in the telling; the boy will go on keeping his own.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that the mission's order never changes --- Sella's mark before the lid and no other hour, not at a bank, a reading or a dusk; that the household keeps days as loaves, glances and absences rather than figures; that a kindling stack can be measured like a supply shelf, four, four and a place where the third count should have been; that Alda reaches for the slate before the child, crosses two fingers at a flower without knowing it, writes down what she will not leave lying about, and covers a sign with dates nobody asked for; that *warm and breathing* can be said exactly and be a lie; that Tarn goes still for two breaths at a kindness and keeps the seventh year out of the account; that a pot turned toward can give nothing and the interval can be filed beside the first without becoming a sixth hearing; that the kiln's breath takes a man in his sleep and can be called merciful; that a neighbour at a well reaches for the slate before the child; that the hand in a remembered chart goes past the turned note to the signature; that the mouth of a pocket holds wool where a lit phone and a name had lain; that a Court's room has a bench, a pot, a draught and a gap, and four such rooms are still four; that a hairline in ice runs and stops short of the edge; that some people want a late thaw because the levy falls in an early one; that the ridge at Vail is near two thumbs and the death-house ridge a finger thicker; that the bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; that Berr reads one in twelve and says a levy was not called by him; that a clerk's hand goes to the slate before his eyes go to the woman; that an account can be declared as it stands and a person's business finished before she begins it; that the pause in the neat dark hand has now come four times; that a figure heard at an Account-day cannot be spent; and that a cold crust stays in the fold whether or not anyone calls it a kindness.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote twice and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the second note will go, who wrote the neat upper line or what it says, whether the clerk at the Court's table wrote the pause or only handles the slate, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after the collector banks it, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, why Jorra's back room is cold, what the second cord is for, or what the collector's soft sentence actually does. He does not know that Dessa has put him among the bits she counts, that Mira is kin to the house three doors down, or that either woman knows more about him than she will say on a slate. The true figure is still one he has never heard spoken; the word forty has not been said in his hearing. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge or the space between her thumb and her scar; that the two ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the cord, the crust, the two intervals and the four pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides; that the four pauses are four objects of one shape and that making a fifth is his work, not anyone else's; that the same bench, pot, draught and gap in four rooms are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerk's reason is more dangerous than a cruelty would have been because it is easier to live beside; that the things a house calls weather or mercy are the things it has entered nowhere; that Mira's cord is the only record worth keeping because the institution does not know it exists; that Dessa's count keeps something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 6

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. The unread name in a lit pocket was reached for twice, at the well and at the Cross, and the hand came back with wool both times. Nine promises, no boat.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices. None returned in voice this chapter; the chart at the well, the flare at the Cross and Berr's passive grammar stood in for them. Still no tag on a remembered line.
- **Sella** --- the woman who took him and will never ask where his memory came from. She carried him down to the Cross and back, kept her half-pace from open slates, kept the mark to its hour, moved his basket out of the draught without looking at his face and would not call it love, measured the ridge and left it for the Last Day, and left the crust where Mira put it. Kindness as a wall, confirmed at Greybank and still unanswered.
- **Tarn** --- the house's debt, now visibly a second ear and a man who can be stopped by a kindness. The knife is back where it can be checked; he saw the boy anticipate a note twice and kept silent both times, and the boy kept silent too. The equality is closer than it was and still has no name on either side.
- **Jorra** --- the house's wound; slip on his forearms and no dust from the back room on all five days, *he's not Vail* still sealed by the boy's arrival. He speaks to pots and the wheel and shifts the damper, not to Sella about the boy, and has not once looked toward the bed this chapter.
- **Alda** --- came under the roof and reached for the slate before the child; the two-finger sign at his wrist was covered by a date nobody had asked for. A second note is written and hidden, and the lie *warm and breathing* is exact. The tin is still inside her bodice, unopened.
- **Mira** --- the aunt who keeps accounts in counts, bones and grey wool and says nothing. Offstage this chapter; she has not said she knows and must not.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, offstage and unwell, who counted him among the bits without knowing it and believes him dead. She has not said the number or the name in three years and will die on Day 38 believing the lie.
- **Berr Clay** --- the local face of the Court, thumb wiped clean before the slate, reading one in twelve in a voice that takes the speaker out of the sentence; his careful not-calling of Garl's debt keeps it alive another year. He believes himself kind and his box stays locked.
- **The collectors** --- the clerk at the Court's table is an ordinary soft man with a washed cuff, not Marra and not the man with the cold iron tube; his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the woman and his sentence finished her business. The boy could no more address him than the slate under his cuff.
- **The man from the river, the women of High Kiln and the neighbours** --- the shard, the early thaw and the Court's room passed through the yard and left no name behind. Figures carried in mouths, keepings turned down against aprons.
- **Garl** --- an absence arranged around, with his uncalled debt spoken around again at the Cross, the ridge nobody will sweep and the two proverbs. *Leave the crack showing.*
