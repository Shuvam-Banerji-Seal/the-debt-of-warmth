# State of things — at the close of chapter 7 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 8. Days 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 and 24 have been lived, one to a dated entry, in the last tend of Stone: a Firstday on which fuel and bread were counted into the tend and the kindling's missing fifth was measured; a Secondday on which a collector with milk-grey fingertips was fetched for a neighbour and a carried vessel gave its flat note past the door; a Thirdday on which slate dust came back into a thrower's forearms after five clean days; a Fourthday on which Alda Reed touched her tin unopened and turned a narrow slate face-down; a Fifthday on which a pot cracked two paces from the basket and was called a pot gone wrong; an ordinary Sixthday on which the number for the Five went around as three, then two, then whatever the moot gave out; a Seventhday of an early-thaw argument and ice-mice; an Eighthday on which Mira put a fresh crust into the fold and both her cords were glimpsed; a Ninthday of brooms and ridges measured by a thumb; and Stone 30, Account-day and the year's last market, where one in twelve was read again, the pause in the neat dark hand came a fifth time, and the flare light broke through for one beat. Nothing has been explained. The boy is twenty-four days old, his private file has grown by a collector's grey fingertips, a third interval, Jorra's dust, a further hidden note, a second crust, two cords and a fifth pause, and the five hearings of the flat note still stand at five from four sources. The district says the Five are three days off; the boy keeps that one day after tonight is tomorrow.*

## The moment

Day 24 is done. On Stone 30, Tenthday, Account-day and the last market of the year, \person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the light had cleared the sill, touched \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat the space of one breath and only then lifted the lid --- a reading, not an opening, so she would not touch it again at \place{the Cross}. She carried the boy down in the basket with her half-pace kept from every open slate and set him at the north end of the square in the moot-post's finger's width of shade, cold iron at his cheek and the two crusts in the fold at his side. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. \person{Berr Clay} came to the block with the red cord of his office folded under his coat and green glaze at one corner of his mouth, wiped his thumb twice, read *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the crowd, passed a finger over the figure, said the levy was called for the feast and was not called by him, went around \person{Garl Vail}'s uncalled debt against the tiles and did not look into the basket. The household's stores went into the last tend and the Five were spoken of over the market as three days off by a district that had counted what it had and did not want to say it.

At the Court's table at the Slate Row fringe a collector's clerk with a cuff turned back twice from washing laid his hand on the top slate before he looked at the woman waiting, and said it was entered as it stood and there was nothing in it the household need correct. The upper line was in the strange neat dark hand, and the small stop between two clusters came a **fifth** time --- the orange flare light, the birth-slate, the square's slate on Stone 10, this table on Stone 20, now this slate at the market's edge --- and lay beside the other four without joining them. The Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in it stayed in its own place. For one beat, untagged, the light broke through: orange on wet snow, a hand in a grey sleeve writing and stopping between two clusters, the hiss of snow taking the heat, and a hand going into a coat pocket to find wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain; the name was not looked at. Nothing crossed back that he could carry.

At dusk she lifted the basket off the post, carried him up the yard with woodsmoke and river wind under the fold, set the fold right without looking at his face, set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and banked the coals with the back of a broken pot. She did not touch the mark. \person{Tarn}'s knife was back on its sill and he had told the yard so once more, unasked; \person{Jorra Vail} had moved the damper and not said the back room was cold or looked toward the bed. The coat kept its salt shape, the seat its hollow, the ridge its unbroken corner, the debt its unspoken circle, and the fire took its breath in fours and a gap. Kindling was tomorrow.

Day count: **Day 24 complete.** Day 25 is Kindling, Y.H. 812, the first of the Five; the era turns on Day 30, which is Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813. The household is keeping a number it will not say; the boy keeps one day after tonight was tomorrow and has no hand, word or standing to enter it.

The nine earlier days of this chapter, held in full:

- **Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday.** First light under the Vail roof: coals holding under blind ash, the little-door burn closing on nothing before the lid was lifted, the brown-edged crust cold in the fold where it had been put six days ago. Sella and Tarn counted fuel and bread into the last tend in loaves, glances and absences, three here, two there, one brown-edged loaf set a little apart and come back to twice and never explained. The boy extended his private count to the kindling stack's missing fifth --- four, four and a space --- and filed the gap beside the bench's interval. Somebody come back from looking at the ice brought a number in a shut fist, said *it's come a hair further* and called it a cold; Sella answered with the fire. Jorra worked in bone-coloured slip with no slate dust and moved the damper before she had finished. The kiln breathed four, four and a gap, and at the bank she did not touch the mark.
- **Day 16, Stone 22, Secondday.** Tarn swept before light, set the small knife on its sill, announced it to the empty yard and said to the kindling *six counted, the next I don't count*. Sella sent him for a collector for a neighbour two doors down. The collector came with a cuff soft from washing and fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off, carried a flat satchel, and set his hand to the slate before the bed, saying *the measure is taken, and the account stands as entered* in the Court's soft grammar. The boy filed the grey fingertips on a separate tray, unjoined --- not Marra, not the clerk at the Cross. On the way back a person carried a vessel past the door and it gave its flat note; Tarn's head turned a degree and he did not look at the basket or name a sound. Under the roof the boy heard the shape of it, too far to confirm, and filed it with the intervals and not among the five hearings. At evening the knife was back on its sill and the loan that could hear had been exercised and bought nobody anything.
- **Day 17, Stone 23, Thirdday.** After five mornings of bone-coloured slip only, Jorra came in from the shed with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of the little finger, said nothing about where he had been and went to the damper before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, the bent finger going in ahead of his hand. He told the wheel *the wheel doesn't care whose hands, only if they're steady*. Low talk in \place{the back room} and whole slates missing under \place{the drying racks} stood only as absences with a shape; the men whose names are not spoken were not shown. Sella got quieter and the yard straightened around it; Tarn swept around the place by the bench where a pot had once given nothing. The dust was filed beside the ridge, the charred slate and the barrow of grey, and made into nothing.
- **Day 18, Stone 24, Fourthday.** \person{Alda Reed} came under the roof smelling of soap and warm milk with the birth-slates under her arm, laid them writing-down on the bench and only then turned toward the fold. She touched the tin once inside her bodice without opening it, pressed the fold flat too long, crossed two fingers at the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} at his wrist without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with a date nobody had asked for: *Stone seven, the account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out*. The dates went on arriving after the question had stopped --- a cooper's seventh in Mud End, a potter's third child in Stonemouth. She said *warm and breathing*, which was exact and not true. On a narrow slate she wrote what she did not leave lying about, in a second hand no Court physician had seen, and turned it writing-down into her shawl toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name.
- **Day 19, Stone 25, Fifthday.** Cold deepened another degree: oiled paper lifting at the windows, a grey skin on \place{the Slip Brook}, drool frozen at the boy's mouth. Sella set the basket facing the kiln wall to keep the river wind off his ears. Two paces from his head a pot in \place{the Clay Yard} cracked all the way through and gave the small flat knock he knew and nobody called a sound. He tried to turn toward it and could not; a woman said *that's a pot gone wrong* and went on. He filed it as a third object on the interval tray, not a sixth hearing, and the count stayed five. Tarn's broom checked half a stroke where the pot had stood and went on; he said nothing and the boy said nothing. Jorra's forearms were bone-coloured and clean again. At the bank Sella fed him from the house's own dish with the warmth entered nowhere.
- **Day 20, Stone 26, Sixthday.** An ordinary Sixthday, **not** Account-day: no bell, no slate read, only mending, drying, sweeping and eating. The number for \lore{the Five} went around the yards in mouths and fists --- a neighbour at the well said three, a second corrected it to two, a third said whatever the moot gave out --- and reached the house as an absence. The argument ended in weather. The boy kept the one number none of them would give: one day after tonight was tomorrow. Sella went around the days in loaves and glances; the crust lay cold in the fold, neither taken out nor named; Jorra's forearms carried no dust. Tarn swept twice and put the knife back on its sill. Garl's tile debt lay in the space where a sentence about the district's paying should have stood and was not called.
- **Day 21, Stone 27, Seventhday.** A woman from \place{High Kiln} with sweetish lead-glaze at one corner of her mouth said the thaw would come early; neighbours who wanted it late contradicted her because the levy fell in the thaw, and Tarn said to the thatch *a late one, late's mine, a longer week before the paying*. Sella called it weather. Ice-mice were found in the thatch and a spreading numb grey patch on a child's hand was called what the cold did. The boy kept the thaw argument and the hairline in the ice on the same tray and drew no line between them, as he had once drawn a wrong line in a better-lit room. The kiln breathed four, four and a gap and the district's figure and the fire's figure did not agree.
- **Day 22, Stone 28, Eighthday.** \person{Mira Silt} came up from the eels past the yard well with silt on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone, answered a question with a question and her own answer (*a basket's a basket; it came from a warm house, that's all, and it's going back to one*), and put her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a figure was spoken. She put a fresh brown-edged barley crust briskly into the fold --- *it's a crust, not a sermon* --- and went back to the eels without looking in; the old crust of Stone 15 lay cold beside it and neither woman named either. When she shifted the basket a second cord twisted twice under her belt showed below the grey one, never taken upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door; the knots were not counted. Three doors down \person{Dessa Silt} came to the end of nineteen steps with slip-grey nails and wet clay at her hem, coughed from the pits and called it weather, gave her count (nineteen out and nineteen back, four loaves on the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve, three to the levy, one eaten with her feet up and bits to the woman who came with a basket), smoothed the sieve-wire scar and shut the door-shut smile over her face without looking into the basket.
- **Day 23, Stone 29, Ninthday.** The yard made ready for \lore{the Last Day} with brooms; a date was said aloud two or three times and a woman said the grey would be left where it lay until the Last Day and burned the morning after and called dealt with. Sella laid her thumb into the ridge at the corner, walked two doors down and laid the same thumb into the death-house ridge, a finger's width thicker, and came back without saying which was which; both turned their corners unbroken. Garl's salt coat kept its shape on the peg and nobody took it down; the uncalled debt against the tiles went around in a careful circle. Tarn offered to check the width with his thumb: *I've a thumb as good as any*, and set the knife on its sill. Jorra went quieter as the sweeping talk went round, moved the damper and had no dust today; the boy noted that the full note was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel, a thing Jorra would not say before a kiln-master, and kept it where questions can last for years. At the bank the coals were turned with the back of a broken pot and the mark was not touched.
- **Day 24, Stone 30, Tenthday, Account-day.** Told in full above: the basket at the moot-post, Berr's reading of one in twelve, the figure for the Five given out as three days off, the fifth pause at the market's edge, the one untagged flare beat, the two crusts going home under the fold, and the fire keeping four, four and a gap.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, twenty-four days old at the close of Day 24. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: in the basket at Sella's hip, set in the moot-post's thin shade with cold iron at his cheek, lifted back onto the bed under the roof. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic}, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth freezes in the small hours and nobody wipes it, because a basket at the edge of a board is a pot set down and left. The head goes where it wants and makes a small wet sound when it goes too far; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Two paces was the cracked pot on Stone 25, which he could not turn toward. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; its red edge showed on Days 17 and 18 and Alda crossed two fingers at it without knowing she did. Cold comes in at cheek, temple and wrist, in the order he knows and cannot argue with. He wants the breast and wants the crust even cold, and gets neither; the old crust is stiff at the edges, the new one still smells of barley smoke when the wind turns, and both lie in the fold, uneaten, unremoved, unnamed.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired a tend's worth of new objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk at the Court's table, the man who priced him, the woman who kept him, a collector for a neighbour two doors down and a clerk at the market's edge. He knows that a thing that does not happen leaves a space with the shape of a sound, that an interval is not a hearing and a pause is not a hearing either, and that the two trays must not touch. He knows the difference between a likeness and a cause because a better-lit room taught him the cost of getting it wrong, and he has kept the ridge, the charred slate, the barrow of grey, the dust, the bell's gap and the kiln's gap in separate places on purpose. He knows that the household keeps its arithmetic in loaves, glances and absences, that a district gives out a number it has already decided to spend, and that a figure passed in a fist cannot be corrected or handed back. He knows Tarn turned his head at the flat note past the door and said nothing, and that two silences under one roof are nearer an equality than anything the house has a name for. He knows a gift that cannot be answered cannot be refused, which is a lesson in bread before it is a lesson in anything else. He is the only adult in every room and the least use in it; correctness is still the whole humiliation, and he has learned to find it funny without laughing.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read Grathic in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the floorboards' four, four and a knot; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the bell's four, four and a gap; the Fishstairs' four, four and a landing; the kindling's four, four and a space; the well-roof's four, four and a stone that does not ring; the prints of the man from the river, four and four and a slide; the hairline in the ice that stops short of the edge. The ear holds \lore{the full note} in five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and this chapter added no sixth. The three intervals on their own tray are: the pot turned toward on the bench that gave nothing, the half-heard note that went past the door on Day 16, and the pot that cracked in the yard on Day 19. There are now five sightings of the pause in the neat dark hand (the flare, the birth-slate, the square's slate on Stone 10, the Court's table on Stone 20, the market's edge on Stone 30) lying side by side and unjoined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause stays filed apart. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it. The knowing buys nothing: no hand has reached a vessel, nobody has tapped a slate for him, and nobody has said what the sound was.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing he can use. **Held/changed:** the brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone 15 is still there, stiff and cold; a fresh brown-edged crust from Mira now lies beside it, given on Day 22 and still smelling of barley smoke when the wind turns. He can smell both and lift neither, and neither woman has taken the old one out or named either. **Held/unchanged:** the small pale river stone remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in \place{House Silt}, unmentioned; the small bench knife is back on its sill, announced unasked on Days 16, 20, 23 and 24; the charred slate still lies in the kiln ash; the basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word; Alda's little tin is inside her bodice, touched once on Day 18 and never opened, and the narrow slate she wrote on that day is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name. **Filed but not possessed:** the Day 16 collector's grey fingertips, Jorra's grey dust, and the cracked pot are on separate trays in his private file. **Money:** none to the boy and none changes hands to him. Figures go past him and are not his to spend: one in twelve, a levy called for the feast, the Five given out as three days off, the number changing three to two to whatever the moot decided, a ridge near two thumbs and a finger thicker, nineteen steps, four loaves on the seventh of Stone. The gruel, the fire, the basket and both crusts remain entered nowhere; every kindness has a payer who can be named and a recipient who cannot refuse it.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The unsent call to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was reached for once this chapter, in the one untagged beat at the market's edge, when a hand in a grey sleeve went into a coat pocket and found wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain; the name was not looked at. The chart with its note turned down and the hand going past it to a signature stood behind that beat; the orange flare light and the hand that stopped between two clusters made a fifth sighting, still unread. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a failure of the system, were recognised again in Berr's passive sentence and the clerk's soft one. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one; Des's laugh did not return. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- of which the third is the quietest because nobody thinks it is an account at all.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his chin and the two crusts in the fold at his side, one cold and one given, awake, silent, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. Wants the breast, wants the crust, wants a word to correct the room; gets none of the three and cannot ask for any. One day after tonight is tomorrow.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the fire after banking the coals with the back of a broken pot, having carried him down to the Cross and back with a half-pace kept from every open slate. Her hand went to the widow's mark before the lid on Stone 30 and at no other hour; she did not touch it at the bank or at the reading, and the little-door burn shut on nothing all the way up the yard. She measured both ridges with one thumb and said nothing, moved the basket out of the draught without looking at his face, fed him from the house's own dish with the warmth entered nowhere, and neither took the old crust out nor named the new one. Has not written him as Vail, keeps a private roll in a careful hand she will not show, and will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take his coat down. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained; another Account-day and another confirmation have made that harder without giving her anyone to answer.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- in the yard and under the roof, broom set against the wall, small knife back on its sill and announced to the empty yard. Six years of seven counted; he will not count the seventh, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. He fetched a collector for a neighbour on Day 16 and his head turned a degree at the flat note going past the door; he has said nothing about either turning or note. His broom checks half a stroke at the place where the pot cracked; he said the grey would be left, and on Day 23 offered to check the ridge width with his thumb. He wanted a late thaw, a longer week before the paying, and said so to the thatch. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist and the mark worn almost flat on the knife handle are his own records. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and grudges the house the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence has never been said in the house and lies in it all the same.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- in and out of the wheel-shed through the tend with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger of his left hand going in ahead of him at the damper, which he moved before Sella had finished. Grey slate dust came back on Day 17 after five clean days, worked into the creases past the bend, and came and went again; he said nothing about the back room and did not look toward the bed. The sentence *he's not Vail* is as sealed as on the hour it was spoken; the broken slates under the drying rack and the men whose names are not spoken remain unshown.
- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- absent and the shape of most of the house. Not collected in the Long Cold; his warmth lies where it fell, the \lore{Ash} ridge is its shape, the coat keeps his broad shape on the peg, the seat keeps his twenty-year leftward lean, and the ridge is unswept and will not be swept before the Last Day. His debt against a firing of tiles was spoken around again on Days 23 and 24, never named, and remains neither paid nor settled nor done with. His proverbs live in the house's mouth: *feed the fire before you feed yourself*, and *leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled.*

## Open dangers

- The year's first measure was taken at the Drawing out of a dying neighbour and entered in the figure the district reads aloud; another Account-day has come and gone with one in twelve read as the notice says, and the boy could not address, refuse, reach or correct it. The household has counted fuel, bread and kindling into the last tend, and on Stone 30 the Five were given out as three days off by a district that had already decided what it would pay.
- He is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and does not know he is inside the third. Sella's private roll is still unshown and the wall around it held; Berr's box stays locked; Mira's grey wool and second cord are records no Court can confiscate and no slate can enter; Alda's further note has been written and turned down toward a floorboard he cannot name. The neat upper line has now been seen five times and the writer still has no name in his file.
- Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness; it is held while the fire never goes out and touched only before an opening, never at a bank, a reading or a dusk. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles has been spoken around through another Account-day and remains never paid, never settled, never done with.
- Tarn has heard a flat note and said nothing, has seen the boy hear one before it happened and said nothing about that either, and has now turned his own head a degree at one going past the door and kept that silent too. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; two people keeping the same silence under one roof is an equality neither can name, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count. Jorra's changing dust and the back room, Sella's half-pace and her unnamed moving of the basket, Mira's question-answers and both not-lookings, and Dessa's count in her thumb are all keepings no slate can enter. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful; a woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early and the yard wants it late because the levy falls in the thaw. What the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cracked pot, the numb hand, the cough, the debt and the Ash are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day of it. The ice-mice spread and nobody answers for them.
- The child on the quilt in the death-house was worse on the third day of its return and the room called it a graft settling. Alda's three written notes are turned down where no mother or officer will read them; the second cause stays in the tin and in the struck word nobody speaks. The boy's own flower has been crossed at by a woman who did not know she did it and covered with a date.
- The Ash in the death-house is thicker by a finger's width than the ridge at Vail, near two thumbs here; both turn corners without a break and neither will be swept before the Last Day, when the grey will be left where it lies, burned the next morning and called dealt with. The charred slate in the kiln ash and the barrows of grey going out through the north gate are unread. The Court's room has the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and the boy has kept the four likenesses apart on purpose.
- The flat note has five hearings from four sources and has bought him nothing; nobody has tapped a vessel for him and there is no Grathic word for it. A third object --- a pot that cracked two paces from his head --- now lies on the interval tray beside the bench's silent set-down and the half-heard note past the door, and the count is still five. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained, and went four, four and a gap over a square where nobody crossed himself. The count is four, four and a gap in the kiln, the boards, the bell, the bench, the well-roof, the river-prints, the kindling and the space between a woman's thumb and her scar; the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter.
- Low Kiln reads one in twelve and will not say what it counts. The Ilm has a hairline crack and the Singing Sill goes on giving its low note without explanation. The Drawing is past, Account-day is done, the year's last market is over, and the boy keeps one day after tonight was tomorrow while the district gives out three. Chapter 8 will open on Kindling with a fire coming into the house from somewhere he cannot see or reach; the figure one in twelve and the true deduction remain unspoken.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that the mark is touched before the opening and at no other hour, not at a bank, a reading or a dusk; that the household keeps days as loaves, glances and absences rather than figures; that the kindling stack can be measured like a supply shelf, four, four and a place where the fifth should be; that an ungiven number arrives as a shut fist and a mouth that thinks better of speaking; that a collector's hand goes to the slate before the bed and says the account stands as entered; that a flat note can go past a door on a carried vessel and be kept by two people who name it neither; that grey fingertips and grey dust and a grey ridge are four different objects until one is proved the same; that a thrower's forearms can report a room the face refuses to report; that a midwife can touch a tin unopened, cover an unaware sign with a date and put a further note under a floorboard in a house he cannot name; that a pot can crack two paces off with a note nobody calls a sound and he cannot turn to place it; that the number for the Five changes in the telling, three to two to whatever the moot gives out; that the thaw is argued about because the levy falls in it; that a spreading numb patch can be called ice-mice and a cough from the pits can be called weather; that a woman can put a crust into a fold before anyone can make a speech of it and say it is a crust, not a sermon; that two cords can lie under one apron and only one of them has never been taken upriver; that a woman can count her life in steps and loaves at the end of nineteen of them; that both ridges are measured with one thumb and neither is swept early; that the bell goes four, four and a gap over a square where nobody crosses himself; that Berr reads one in twelve and says a levy was not called by him; that a clerk's hand goes to the slate before his eyes go to the woman and finishes her with *entered as it stands*; that the pause in the neat dark hand has now come a fifth time without joining the other four; that a hand in a grey sleeve can stop between two clusters and find wool where a lit rectangle with a name had lain.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is, what she wrote, or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote three times and turned down, which house holds the floorboard under which the further note will go, whether the clerk at the market's edge wrote the pause or only handles the slate, what the collector's grey fingertips mean, what goes on in the back room whose door is kept shut, what the missing slates under the drying rack were for, why the second cord has never gone upriver, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after the collector banks it, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, why Jorra's back room is cold, or what the collector's soft sentence actually does. He does not know that Dessa has put him among the bits she counts, that Mira is kin to the house three doors down, or that either woman knows more about him than she will say on a slate. The true figure is still one he has never heard spoken; the word forty has not been said in his hearing. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, kindling, a bell, a landing, a woman's thumb, a hairline in ice that stops short of an edge or the space between her thumb and her scar; that the ridges, the charred slate, the barrows of grey, the benches, the dust, the cords, the crusts, the intervals and the pauses are one thing seen many ways, and that joining them would be a deciding he has no standing to make; that Tarn's silence and his own are the same object held from two sides; that the five pauses are five objects of one shape and that making a sixth is not his work either; that the same bench, pot, draught and gap in four rooms are a likeness he must not turn into a cause, because he has made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the clerks' reasons are more dangerous than cruelties would have been because they are easier to live beside; that the things a house calls weather or mercy are the things it has entered nowhere; that Mira's wool is the only record worth keeping because the institution does not know it exists; that Dessa's count keeps something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that two not-lookings with the same shape can be opposite things; that a gift that cannot be answered cannot be refused, twice over in bread; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 7

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. The unread name in a lit pocket was reached for once, in the flare beat at the market's edge, and the hand came back with wool. Nine promises, no boat.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices. None returned in voice this chapter; the chart, the flare and Berr's and the clerk's passive grammar stood in for them. Still no tag on a remembered line.
- **Sella** --- the woman who took him and will never ask where his memory came from. She carried him to the Cross and back, kept her half-pace from open slates, kept the mark to its hour, moved his basket out of the draught without looking at his face, measured both ridges with one thumb, fed him with the warmth entered nowhere, and left the old crust where Mira's new one lay beside it. Kindness as a wall, confirmed at Greybank and still unanswered.
- **Tarn** --- the house's debt, now visibly a second ear who has turned his own head at a flat note and kept silent. The knife is back where it can be checked; he saw the boy anticipate a note and kept silent, and the boy kept silent too. The equality is closer than it was and still has no name on either side.
- **Jorra** --- the house's wound; slip on his forearms, dust come and gone from the back room, *he's not Vail* still sealed by the boy's arrival. He speaks to pots and the wheel and shifts the damper, not to Sella about the boy, and has not once looked toward the bed this chapter.
- **Alda** --- came under the roof and reached for the slate before the child; the two-finger sign at his wrist was covered by a date nobody had asked for. A third note is written and hidden, the tin is still inside her bodice unopened, and the lie *warm and breathing* is exact.
- **Mira** --- the aunt who keeps accounts in counts, cords and grey wool and says nothing. She put a crust into the fold before a speech could be made of it, touched her cord when a figure was spoken, and showed a second cord that has never gone upriver. She has not said she knows and must not.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, who came to the end of nineteen steps, coughed from the pits and called it weather, counted him among the bits without knowing it and believes him dead. She has not said the number or the name in three years and will die on Day 38 believing the lie.
- **Berr Clay** --- the local face of the Court, thumb wiped clean before the slate, reading one in twelve in a voice that takes the speaker out of the sentence; his careful not-calling of Garl's debt keeps it alive another year. He believes himself kind and his box stays locked.
- **The collectors** --- the man who came for the neighbour has washed cuffs and milk-grey fingertips and took a measure from a dying person two doors down; the clerk at the market's edge is an ordinary soft man who laid his hand on the slate before his eyes went to the woman. Neither is Marra and neither is the man with the cold iron tube. The boy could no more address either than the slate under his hand.
- **The man from the ice, the woman of High Kiln and the neighbours** --- the shut fist, the early thaw, the changing number and the Court's room passed through the yard and left no name behind. Figures carried in mouths, keepings turned down against aprons.
- **Garl** --- an absence arranged around, with his uncalled debt spoken around again, the ridge nobody will sweep, the coat nobody takes down and the two proverbs. *Leave the crack showing.*
