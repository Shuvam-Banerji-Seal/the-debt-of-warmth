## The moment

Day 242, Smoke 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, the small hours, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}. \person[Corin]{the boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from that ear; the empty place beside it keeps its name to itself and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth has frozen in the dry heat and is unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as though it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it goes no further and does not go away, and the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform. He knows the afterdrop when he sees it and he wants the crust; both are true and neither is a sign.

Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-two days; overhead the boards keep four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and forty-two days old at the close of Day 242 and six months and sixty-two days (two hundred and forty and six months and sixty days on Smoke 1, two hundred and forty-one and six months and sixty-one days on Smoke 2), carried everywhere: under the Vail roof at the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle and the kiln mouth; in the Clay Yard, the drying racks, the yard well and the returning line; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the worked strips' edge; down the clay road at the going of the light and home again. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him, lift a hand to his own chin or lift a crust two inches to his mouth. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat and was left unwiped on all three days. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; the afterdrop waits past a warming nobody in this house will perform. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lies two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. The one change is the hand: it can go up an inch toward a sound and stay for a small intake, for an interval held between intake and letting-go, for a whole counted breath, past the end of a counted breath and down on a second rhythm, for the river's own low interval with no count in the house to hold it against, or come down before the letting-go or at once under conditions that have sometimes answered. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in that small hand and both are true; the child wants the crust and the adult keeps the tray, and neither is promoted into a sign.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and six more answers from the hand, and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, Sella reaching a finger beneath the shelf before her eyes went to the row, Sella's glance at the clay jar before anything else, Hessa settling her strap and turning her wrist before her eyes took in the crowd, Berr wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over the figure before his eyes took the crowd, and the soft-cuffed clerk entering a buyer's number before he looked up to find the man who had said it. Hessa's reaching stays filed as the **eighth** such hand in the district, joined to none, and the clerk's inward order of reaching is kept apart from hers and from Berr's twice-wiped thumb --- records going inward are records going inward and a row of such hands will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer. ## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and forty through two hundred and forty-two days --- six months and sixty days through six months and sixty-two days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said at three readings in Grain and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard on Grain 10, 20 and 30 from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That a seventh green at a mouth is a colour and a seventh shape at a market's edge is a shape, and that six behind a seventh make a row and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; that a pale line is a pale line and a row of them is not a cause; that a market's arithmetic can change for two hundred people while the printed figure stands and the figure read is not the figure paid, and that a figure read over a basket knows nothing of the hands that dressed the grain under wool at home. That a saying can turn by a hair, from a general rule about pots into the report of a particular silence and back to a general rule again, and that three sayings standing together are three sayings and not one rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer. That a cost asked as a direction can go to a pot and the weather and come back as neither; that a figure kept in a sleeve has arrived nowhere and is not a missing figure. That the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; that the mark is a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it; that the coal is tipped under the back of a broken pot and the fire takes without anybody seeing what it mended. That a thing held is a thing kept and a thing kept need not be entered, and that sixty inches filed as objects are still worth nothing.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers; the pale line on the silent woman's hand is kept on its own place beside it and not laid across it. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with sixty answers from Day 213 through the small hours of Day 242, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named; the last two answers holding two rhythms across a wall that does not touch, remembered and not changes made again. **A seventh place:** the **four** askings of T101, the question about the row, with no owner, room, hand at the kiln, cause, name, witness or payer, filed with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the hearings, the sayings, the tellings and the count, waiting on the far-off day a witness stands somewhere and can be asked. **Beside the hearings, as a colour and a shape on places that do not touch:** the seventh green from the mouth of a silent woman at the pot board and the seventh sighting of the foundling at the market's edge, gathered on Day 219, seen again on Day 239 and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, no eighth, no name, no joining, neither Fenna nor Ulda in the narration. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told four times on Days 205, 208, 215 and 218, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling of the shut room, advanced and not paid. **Beside the hearings, on the near-full tray:** three shapings of the pot saying, advanced and unpaid, no fourth likeness made a cause, no pot lost. **Beside the fire, held on their own trays:** the widow's mark and the one opening before the kiln on Smoke 1, with no lesson, figure, slate, bond or payer and no second touch at shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk; the told bell beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid, the nine stoppings with no tenth, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, the damper's two bites with no Grathic word for both, the three held fractions with the loft scrape, pale line and held inch beside them, and the dressed grain covered and unfigured under wool.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and forty-two days old, or six months and sixty-two days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and shut her hand half over nothing on these mornings, and her little-door burn closing on air. What the widow's mark mended, why the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, or why sixty days went around before the opening came; he has seen the act and set it down as maintenance on a vessel, not as a sign or a payment. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room on all three mornings, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them, though the word has stood at four. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, why he once timed a slate burning in four minutes and did not say it now, whose name he has promised and to whom, or what *he's not Vail* is meant to seal. Whether the sixth hand in the file, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, will ever be joined to her name or her number; he will not speak either. Whether the inch that crossed two rhythms is anything more than an inch, and who would stand under it if it were; he has filed it as an object and will not promote it. What the district's private records would say if they were laid together, and who would lay them; the figures, slates, cords, knots and charcoal go on gathering beyond his reach. He does not know that any of this is a story, or that it has a reader.

## What Chapter 64 must not forget
