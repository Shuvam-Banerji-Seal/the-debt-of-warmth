## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: three hundred and twenty-four through three hundred and twenty-six days --- six months and one hundred and forty-four through one hundred and forty-six days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the round three hundred was a threshold kept on a place of its own and not spent, and he has stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers count, which is not the same as not wanting it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, that a tourniquet let go too soon weeps into the dressing, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. That a person's face can be composed because the cold has composed it, and that the pink returning to the skin is the core still cold. The shape of a reading said over a body that cannot answer, now said at the account-days from Grain 10 through Bone 20 (twice on Frost 30, twice on Bone 10 and twice on Bone 20) and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than *I* --- heard at every reading over the same basket from the same man, and called a kindness; and now the smaller movement of taking himself out of the sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on. That a district's arithmetic can be changed in a square where two hundred people stand and carried home in a locked box to two hundred tables with two figures in it, one printed and one paid, and a space between them nobody signed; that a figure can be sent over a basket twice, once for those who stood and once for those who came late, and be one figure spoken two times; that a thing covered is not therefore paid for; that **five readings are five and not one account** and the figure read is not the figure paid; that a repair is not a restoration, that a twice-mended pot carries its crack and is truer for showing it; that a hand to a record before an eye to a person is an order a person keeps without knowing he keeps it; that two likenesses are not a cause and a rule wants a payer; that a thing held by being left alone is still held; that a touch laid on a place a man has decided not to open, and left unopened, took nothing and paid nothing and is kept because it was true.
