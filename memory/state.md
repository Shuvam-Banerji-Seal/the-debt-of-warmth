# State of things — at the close of chapter 2 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 3. Days 2 and 3 have been lived: a house has been known by its objects before its people, a hand that hovered has now taken, and the boy has passed from a house that would not name him into a house that will not ask where his memory came from. Nothing has been explained. A cup has given back a flat note twice and nobody has said what it means.*

---

## The moment

The third day is done. Sella Vail carried the covered bundle up the Clay Yard in the dry cold after the sleet, took it under the Vail roof, and laid Corin on a bed of fresh straw while Tarn was given the loft above the wheel-shed. Alda told Dessa the boy had gone in the levy among the children taken for the account, and gave dates faster than dates had any business arriving; the figure was never spoken and Dessa did not follow. The household has sorted its own members and called the sorting kindness. The boy is warmer than he was that morning and more watched, and cannot say a word about either. He has come to the end of the count he kept through a wall: the breath of the fire is now the air he lies in.

Day count: **Day 3 complete.** Day 4 (Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday) is Account-day and the last market before the Five.

---

## Place

- **Sarn:** \place{Grath}, the town of \place{Sill}, the poor quarter of \place{Low Kiln} on the south bank below the sill. The boy is now in the \place{Vail kiln-house}: trodden-ash floor, heat of coals banked under ash, a ridge of \lore{the Ash} near two thumbs wide along the skirting board and turning the corner without a break, the kiln to his left, a door that does not quite shut. Off it the \place{Vail wheel-shed} holds the wheel with its seat worn hollow on the left (Garl Vail's twenty-year lean, which Sella will not let anyone fill), and behind the kiln-house door a salt-stiff coat keeps a broader man's shape on its peg. Above the shed is the \place{Debt Loft}, where a man crossing overhead makes a board settle. In the kiln's ash lies the charred edge of a slate, black on one side, eaten into the shape of bark, the writing gone off the end. \place{House Silt}, three doors down, holds the cold stone in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed and the slate flat on the bench with all writing turned under. The \place{Clay Yard} lies between, with \place{the Slip Brook} clay-coloured under ice at its edges and \place{the Ilm} under its skin of ice beyond the last roofs. The Five are spoken of as three days off, and Low Kiln says three days when it has counted what it has and does not want to say it.
- **Earth, in memory only:** a car park in \place{Chicago} after a bad shift, wet slush under the lamps, the dashboard clock past three, cold pizza in a paper bag on the console, the rig smelling of diesel and lemon disinfectant, \person{Deshawn Carter}'s gold tooth catching the lamp and his laugh arriving in the present tense, going on through the space where the eleventh minute would have been and then on a dispatcher's radio for exactly one minute. It breaks through and goes out like a lamp; it is not a place the boy can return to.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, three days old by the afternoon of Day 3. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He drools and cannot wipe his chin; cannot hold up his head, roll over, or turn it past a point without the small animal sound; cannot reach a cup two paces off or lift a hand to take part in what is done to him. The head goes where it wants. Heat has come back into his hands and feet under the Vail roof with the itch and sting of a rewarming, which he knows from men coming out of the cold into a lit room. The branching red mark runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; \person{Alda Reed} has called it \lore{birth-flower} in front of him, he has called it a burn map, and nobody has examined it as a mark to be explained. He is hungry, no longer out in the cold, awake most of three days, and furious with the exactness of a man who can measure everything and do none of it.

**Mind.** The double consciousness is staged in full and has acquired a new instrument. The surgeon's register reads hand-grips, the pressure of a lifting arm through cloth, pulses in throats, the bend of a little finger, the not-looking, the return of blood to cold extremities, the arithmetic of a bed and a loft, and the shape of a man in a hollow seat; the infant's body wants the breast and cannot be argued with. *The surgeon wanted the cup. The infant wanted the breast. Both were true and neither was reachable, and the second was louder, and the first did not therefore stop.* He has heard his own life rewritten as a kindness behind him --- a chart that said one thing and a room that had done another, with a signature at the bottom to make the two agree --- and could not turn around to correct it. A voice from the old life said *Decide, Marchetti. Then carry it* while he lay being sorted, and he carried it without deciding. He came from a house that would not name him into a house that would not ask where his memory came from, and has begun to suspect that a kindness which cannot be answered is a kindness which cannot be refused.

**Powers.** None usable, and no rank change. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} spoken or written, cannot read a ledger or bargain for food. What has changed is the ear: the first actual hearing of a flat note (T061), from a cup across the birth room, then the second from a pot set down in the Vail kiln-house. He knows \lore{the full note} from its flat shadow --- a bright knock that carries through the bottom and comes up clean under the knuckle against a small dead sound that stops at the surface --- and has no Grathic word for either and has not needed one. The knowing is a tool with nothing in the world for it to touch: he cannot reach the cup, cannot ask for the pot to be tapped, and nobody in either room says what the sound was. His counting is a habit of the old life, not a gift, and extends now to floorboards (four, four, a knot), the kiln's breaths (four, four, a gap where the fifth should be), Dessa's nineteen out and nineteen back, Sella's steps up the yard (a line, not a wheel), and the hollow seat, the salt coat and the Ash ridge. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it.

**Possessions and money.** The small pale river stone, colour of skimmed milk, is no longer his: it was left in the fold of grey wool on the board-bed in House Silt when the bundle was taken, and he feels the place of it at his belly as an empty place felt with an exactness that had nothing to do with temperature. What he has now was given under another roof: a bed of fresh straw with a fold of cloth over it, and the wool of the covering bundle. He owns no coins and none change hands in this chapter. Costs are reckoned in other units: three of four loaves to \lore{the levy}, the slip in the pit to be paid for in the thaw, a bed and a warmth for a foundling nobody has agreed to pay for. The stone and the brown-edged barley crust were both warmed at the Vail hearth and carried across the yard, and neither was entered against anybody; the house that paid twice has now taken the boy without a word, and nobody has said why.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The call he never made to \person{Nina Marchetti} --- a boat on Lake Michigan promised for nine years, never bought; the weight of the unsent sentence made him portable. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a systems failure. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle. Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one, Ostrowski's full name, Vesci's warm *butcher*. Des's laugh in the car park arrived whole and in the present tense and went out; there is no word in this country for a man who rode in the back of a rig and made jokes instead of asking. He does not know Sarn's word for any of it. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof

- **Corin/Dan** --- on the bed of fresh straw, awake, warmer, watched, hungry, counting. Wants the breast, wants to know what he is being kept for, wants to correct the story told behind him; can have none of the three.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- in the kiln-house, the boy installed, the kiln opened after she touched the widow's mark, the pot told not to be set near the door or paid for in the thaw. She took him with the hand whose burn is shaped like a little door, closing first on nothing and then on his weight; said *the boy*, not *my son*, and *the house*, not *I*; said the house would keep him and gave no reason. She has not written him as Vail and will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take his coat down. She keeps a private roll entered in a careful hand that she will not show to a man come down from \place{Slate Row} with a slate in his hand, and has decided, without knowing why, not to call a collector for anyone in her house. Garl has been uncollected a year; Enna died of the second chill; the Ash ridge in her house is the thickest in Low Kiln. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, and the keeping never explained. Her silence is now a wall, not a pause.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- at the wheel in the shed, settled into the hollow seat he has tried for three years to fill and cannot, slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms, the left little finger bent at its middle joint from a splinter he got at eleven. He said *he's not Vail* in Scene 1, was refused an answer, and the boy's arrival sealed it: a child is under the roof and nobody has written him as Vail, which is what Jorra wanted and what he cannot bear. He does not look at Sella while obeying her instantly, speaks to the wheel and the pots and not to the boy, and has not said whether he is with the Broken Slates. Wants the foundling kept outside the name Vail; wants the hollow to be his; keeps a temper nobody has seen the bottom of and three broken birth-slates buried under the drying rack.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- came down from the Debt Loft with a broom, swept, banked the coals, took the small bench knife out and put it back where it could be checked, and said of his own accord that he had taken it and put it back and they could check. Six years of a seven-year term lie behind him, counted every one, and he will not count the next: *Six years. One to go. I've counted every one, and I won't count the next.* He calls Sella *house*, says *I owe* where another man says *I am*, and agreed with everything so thoroughly that an argument has nowhere to go. His deference has a nail in it: *He's a foundling. I'm a debt. Don't put us in the same sentence, Sella.* He stopped his broom for two strokes when a pot was set down and gave its flat note, turned his head a degree and said nothing. Wants to keep the one thing he owns --- the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes --- and grudges the house even the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called.
- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- not in the room and the shape of most of it. Went in the Long Cold when there was no crossing the river for a collector and was not collected; his warmth lies where it fell, the Ash ridge is the shape of it, the coat keeps his shape on the peg and the seat keeps his leftward lean. His proverb lives in the house's mouth: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*

### In House Silt and the Clay Yard

- **Alda Reed**, 42, the hearth-maid --- was last seen behind Dessa in the Clay Yard, smoothing her apron in two passes and going on giving exact dates after the question had stopped. She did not come under the Vail roof. She told Dessa the boy was entered in the levy among the children taken for the account that month, that the account went up on the eighth before the paper went out, and that a woman with a thin return who goes asking draws a hand down on a slate better left where it lay. She said *warm and breathing was all I set down*, which was not true. Her slate is flat on the bench in House Silt with the dark writing turned under; the little tin on its cord inside her bodice was touched once in the birth room and once more. Wants the child warm and breathing and the account in its proper order; will lie to keep both. She is steady and afraid, and there is a second record on her person that is not the slate.
- **Dessa Silt**, 24 --- last seen at the end of the Clay Yard where the houses stop and the rise toward the wheel-shed begins; she followed that far and did not take the last nineteen steps or any part of them. Her pulse was in her throat, her thumb on the pale sieve-wire scar, the door-shut smile on her face. She did not say his name and counted nineteen out and nineteen back under her breath instead. She believes him gone in the levy and will believe it for three years. By the afternoon she is presumably back in House Silt, though the chapter does not show it. She coughs from the pits and calls it weather, and a body can be warm and still be gone. She wants him kept and cannot say so; she does not know Sella took him. She will die on Day 38, uncollected, in \place{the Slip Brook}.
- **Mira Silt**, 27 --- does not appear in this chapter (held for Chapter 5 per `plan/next.md`). Last seen in House Silt on Day 1: eel slime dried on her sleeves, silt and salted perch on her, the top of her left ear missing, an eel-bone ticking in her pocket, her back to the slate and her face to the door. The brown-edged barley crust she gave was the last warmth that crossed that room from her hands. She knows more than she has said and has arranged her face to keep it; the knot for a thin child in Nine Pots is still on its separate cord and has never been shown.

### Offstage but live

- **\person{Berr Clay}**, 48, Thane of Clay, at home in High Kiln: the slate taking forty children of Low Kiln for a private account is signed, the purchaser's name deliberately forgotten, the true deduction (one in three) locked in a box he has never destroyed. Tomorrow, on Account-day, he wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate and says *one in twelve, as the notice says*.
- **\person[Marra]{Collector Marra}**, still not in Sill. In \place{Chicago} at the edge of the flare light, in a coat the colour of wet ash, writing on a slate; the hand does not stop. Her fingertips will go grey between Y.H. 803 and now. She forged the assessment for a whole return with the deduction entered as zero and has been paying for it ever since with her fingers, booked nowhere. The boy has set the pause in her writing beside the pause in the dark hand on the birth-slate and has not joined them. She is not named in Sarn narration and is not called a collector in it.
- **\person{Orra Weir}**, 34, at the Weir one league upriver in the Eel House, with a cord of forty knots and, not yet tied, a forty-first. Her son Kess is one of the Forty. She cannot spell the name on the collector's slate but remembers its shape.
- **Rusk of the Weir**, 70, walking the traps at dusk in a coat lined with ice-mouse fur. Unbanked since Y.H. 675. In his locked drawer: Haldre's *The Sealed Vessel*, nine names with eight struck out, and a rubbing of a branching red mark from Y.H. 588. He has not seen the boy and has already decided what to make of him.
- A Waiting Room clerk in Slate Row whose name is on no roll takes small secrets from children and pays in loaves and ale. Ulda will sell him the boy's ear in a few years and, later, the mark.

### The wider world, asleep or waking

- **Greybank** (18 leagues east): \person{Veno Brine} in the counting house holds a damp copy of the slate for a whole return and cannot spell the purchaser's name; he has kept it ten years. \person{Solene Ember}, kept in a room with good light, removes a ring one at a time and does not know the boy exists.
- **The Hearthseat** (150 leagues south-east): Steward Derren has authorised a private whole return of forty unspent measures without recording the purchaser; in the sealed file he has written *The instrument is in place. The account is carried.* Provost Wessen has a slate corrected in a second hand in his second file and has entered nothing about it in any report. Rennick, 26, posted to Sill in Y.H. 810, is not yet in the town but is already asking why forty unspent measures are needed for one intact one.
- **Keld** (120 leagues west-north-west): Hald Iron reads Haldre's paper on the Last Day from a tin lined with warm-stone. Siv of the First Seam keeps the crossed-out Y.H. 588 survey and the shape of the mark on the back of his tally. The Vorr sit against the Listening Stones and say only that the numbers are correct.
- **Vhalen** (80 leagues north-east): Pia of the Quay, 14, does not yet know the boy. In ten years she will sell him a herring twice and keep the question on a slate-rubbing in a dry place.

### On Earth

- **Nina Marchetti**, grown, lives a life organised around not waiting. She called her father on the night of the crash; the missed call is on her phone still and she has never deleted it. She will never learn that the warmth that crossed was not hers, and that the child in the minivan --- not she --- carries the last of it.
- **Deshawn Carter** took the 3 a.m. call and laughed for exactly one minute before he put the radio down. He came back this chapter in a car park with cold pizza and a gold tooth, and did not know he had been heard. He has not yet told Nina her father was working late.
- **Lena Ostrowski**, retired, has a photograph of Dan as a junior and a little book of every patient he lost, with Tasha Broussard's name in his own handwriting. Her voice is held for Chapter 3.
- **Paul Vesci**, in private practice in Evanston in a room with good light, keeps a file on Dan's career with one page about Tasha's chart, and has forgotten the unkind thing he said about Nina.

## Open dangers

- The boy is under a roof that is not his, warm for the first time in three days and watched by people who do not address him. He cannot ask why he was taken, cannot refuse the bed, cannot turn his head far enough to see who is watching. A foundling with no house-name is a coat on a peg in a poor house: anybody may wear it, nobody must say who left it.
- Nobody in the Vail house has said what was paid for him or why, and the house keeps a private roll it will not show to a man from Slate Row with a slate in his hand. Garl has been uncollected a year and the Ash ridge is the shape of his warmth; the charred slate in the kiln has been swept around for a year and nobody has picked it up.
- Dessa believes him gone in the levy and will not ask after him; Alda has made the lie exact with dates and a warning about a hand coming down on a slate better left where it lay. The figure is unspoken. A mother has been disposed of by a story that believes itself to be mercy, and the boy heard it done without being able to correct it.
- Jorra's *he's not Vail* stands in the room like a flaw the house has decided not to mend, and his grievance has a temper under it; Tarn's deference has a nail in it and he can hear the full note of a pot. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. His kept knife is back where it can be checked, and he has one year of his term left that he will not count.
- The Five are three days off and the Ilm is under ice; the ice is spoken of as holding, which means somebody went to look and came back with a number they did not give. Low Kiln says three days when it has counted what it has and does not want to say it. Account-day and the last market are tomorrow, and the Cross is ahead.
- A claim was made on him in a hand he cannot read before anyone saw his face, and the hand's pause is still set beside the pause at the flare, unjoined. The widow's mark is held on the condition that the fire never go out, and there are people in the district who say it will go out if she ever forgets.
- The warmth of the stone and the bread, both from the Vail hearth, remains unentered and unpaid, and the same house has paid twice for the same cold family. He is the only one keeping that account and cannot speak it.
- The clinical ear has found its object and is still a prisoner. The flat note is in him and he cannot reach either vessel or make anyone tap one.

## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage --- the ninety-second sorting of the wounded. He knows the smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: the flat note of a vessel that does not answer full, heard first from a cup and again from a pot, and the full note it shadows; that Sella took him and said the house would keep him, saying *the boy* and *the house*; that Dessa was told he went in the levy and that a number went unspoken; that the cold stone was left behind on the abandoned bed; that Jorra said he was not Vail and nobody wrote him as Vail; that Tarn is six years into a seven-year term, is a debt and not a foundling, calls Sella house and took the small knife out and put it back; that Sella touches the widow's mark only before she opens the kiln; that Garl sat in the hollow seat for twenty years and was not collected and the house keeps a roll of its own; that the proverb is *leave the crack showing*; that a charred edge of a slate lies in the kiln ash; that a poor household performed triage on its own members and gave the boy the bed and Tarn the loft; that the warmth of stone and crust came from this hearth, was paid twice, and was never entered. He has been entered twice, once in the dark hand and once in his own count, and now also under a roof where nobody asks where his memory came from.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is and what she wrote; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, what is in Alda's tin, who wrote the name or what it says, what the charred slate was, or why the figure Alda did not speak matters. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago. He does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the room was arranging something around him before he left it, and that the arrangement was a kind of kindness he had no words to refuse; that the household has sorted him as a trauma bay sorts the wounded, and that he has done the same thing himself in a steady voice with clean hands; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused; that the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps or the shape of an absent man; that Tarn heard the pot's note and that nobody said what it was; that Dessa's counting going from a rhythm to a straight line meant something he could not answer; that Alda's dates, the pause in the writing hand and Sella's mark before the fire have the same shape and do not yet make a fourth. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 2

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. Reached only as a lit phone and a name he will not look at.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices: the laugh that arrived whole and went out, the full name heard at every decision, the warm voice that gave his institution the sentence it needed. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* is the grammar the Warm Court will speak in. A remembered voice said *Decide, Marchetti. Then carry it* as he lay being sorted.
- **Sella** --- the woman who took him and will never ask where his memory came from. The hovering hand is gone; the taking is done. Kindness as a wall: a bed, a fire, an instruction to a pot, and no explanation. He is inside the house whose breath he counted.
- **Jorra** --- the house's wound. He wanted the foundling not written as Vail; the arrival sealed it and gave him the thing he cannot bear. He will not look at Sella and will not speak to the boy.
- **Tarn** --- the house's debt, now resentful of the foundling who was given a bed while he was given the loft. He keeps the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and has heard a second flat note he did not name.
- **Alda** --- the woman who lied over his flower on Day 1 and lied over his life on Day 3, both as mercy, and who keeps a second record. Her exact dates are the sound a covering makes.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, who will not name him and now believes him spent among the levy children. She counted nineteen steps she did not take and did not follow. She will die believing him dead.
- **Mira** --- the aunt who knows the secret is in what happens after a child is born and has said nothing for three years. Her face is the most arranged thing in the room she has left.
- **Marra and Rusk** --- the two halves of a purchase he knows nothing about. She pays with her fingers; he pays with his silence. Both are paying for him before anyone has told him he was taken.
- **Garl** --- an absence the household has arranged itself around, and the first shape Dan has read with his new count. *Leave the crack showing.*
