## The moment

Day 122, Reed 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, the going of the light into the small hours. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, \person{Sella Vail} has carried \person[Corin]{the boy} up the clay road from \place{the Weir} at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. ## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and twenty-two days old at the close of Day 122 and four months and two days, carried everywhere: at the edge of the worked strips, the Clay Yard, the wheel-shed's edge (back room not entered), the damper, the stalls beyond the Cross, the road downriver and the plank walk at the Weir, the outer boards of the fish-trade shade, and lifted onto the bed under the roof every night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the small hours and nobody wiped it on any of the three days, because nobody has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down; nothing within two paces can be reached. Cold arrives in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool does less than the wind wants, riding back from the joint so the cold finds the bare place and goes under the fold at his ear. The branching red birth-flower runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; Alda crossed two fingers at its red edge on Day 118 without knowing she had done it and covered the sign with a date. Nobody in the yard, the sheds, the stalls or under the roof has said how old he is, or would have known what a number of days counted if they had. He has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it; the wanting goes on quietly below the not-expecting the way the fire goes on below the ash.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has seen it in a midwife, a neighbour at a well, a clerk behind a Court table, a man who priced him, Berr wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing, the Slate Row clerk at the Cross, Alda going to her slate before the fold and then to the tin in her bodice, Mira moving eel-bones and then her thumb to the cord while deciding, Jorra speaking to the wheel before the pots and the people, Sella giving the instruction to the bucket, broom, wool, water, pot and fire before she gives it to anybody, the weigher, the buyer and the man with the slate in the market, the reed-cutter turning his cord where no slate lay, and his own watching of that order. Three likenesses still grow a cause, so he keeps every object on a place of its own and lays no finger across the space between them.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and twenty-two days, four months and two days, kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which warmth is given out and the order in which a body loses it, especially the extremity going before the core, the face putting on an almost-smile that is not peace, and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate is not a figure put into a strip, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, and that a tally kept in cord is a record no Court thumb can enter. That a cost with no figure is still a cost and every cost in Low Kiln has a payer nobody will name. That every warmth has a payer, so ask who paid before asking what a thing is worth. That a breath stopped behind a clamp is a breath spent whether it goes in or not and the price is in the hand that holds the iron. That a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the unsaid sentence is kept with the year a man will not count. That a household straightens around a silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, obedience arriving before the instruction and being also a correction nobody will name. That the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes a hand off a man are one piece of iron with no Grathic word for both, and that a stroke checked one day and going on the next are two intervals on one tray and not a cause. That a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, that the hearing of a joining is not the making of it, and that a line drawn through three absences will send a man looking for a clean hand to sign it. That a thing which comes after the knock is different from a hearing that comes with it, that two faces turned the same way can mean opposite things --- keeping and pricing --- with no Grathic word for the difference. That a place gone around with a look is a place entered by nobody, and a hand that went in and did not come out stays unentered until somebody finds a body and invents a figure. That a mercy-lie laid over a sign curdles into a fact no living person can take off.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the neighbours' one-breath joinings set beside the first two. **T098 planted and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 planted and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, carried and not counted, knots unread, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string, Alda's six hidden pages and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** the two neighbours' tellings on two places beside the earlier tellings and the watcher's late turn, no fourth likeness and no third gap. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's check on one day and his stroke going on without it on the next, set beside the bent finger and the held breath without any of them joined. The green at a silent woman's mouth beside Berr's green and the Silt 30 green, three colours on three places, no name and no ear. The foundling's delay: after the knock, not before it, not a hearing and not a seventh, his own turning kept beside it without touching, and the green sliver rubbed twice with a thumb. The cold foot, uncured, uncaused and unnamed, the cold and the weather never in the same sentence. The soft hands and the loaf on separate trays, not joined and not called a sale, and the soft hands a sixth shape kept off every record-first tray. Berr's true subtraction, which he knows exists because the figure said is not the figure paid, but which he cannot read or carry. The sixth hearing out of a person, the three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the unspoken word with its readable shape, the two shut-room tellings and the damper's double bite. The clerk set at a landing for a measure nobody called --- set down ahead of a death, a shape he does not make a rule of. The heard bell kept beside the told bell in two places although they are the same four and the same missing stroke. The unforged collector sentence kept with the seventh year; the sealed sentence *he's not Vail*; the promise of the foundling's name neither given nor withdrawn.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and twenty-two days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it for a hundred and twenty-two days. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, and that the sale is years ahead. What the reed-cutter's knots count, what the cord says beyond *No more can the Court*, who the reed-cutter is, what the word forty counts, and who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over. What the silt-leach is, or that the cold in the child's foot has a name and a habit Arc 3 away. Which hand went into the strips on Seed 15 and did not come out on Seed 27, whether it is a hand or a body that will be found or a figure that will be given, and whether anybody will ever enter the place in the line. What the green on the silent woman's mouth will one day be read as, or whose mouth it will be joined to. Whether the loan that can hear can be called, and what must give at the end of Tarn's seventh year. Whether the two tellings of the shut room belong under his own roof. Who paid for the fire, the gruel, the seed, the hands, the reeds and the loaves, and why every cost in Low Kiln has a payer nobody will name. He does not know that his exactness is the only arithmetic in the district, or that keeping it is at once his whole dignity and the proof that he has no standing in the house he has never left.

## Open dangers

- **The planting crossed out of its last tend and one hand did not come back with the rest; the reed work has taken its place and still has no figure.** The worked brown skin of the strips east of Low Kiln holds the heel-marks, the little dark pits and the pale dust of broken clods; hands went in on Seed 15 in glances and handfuls and never a figure, a man brought a number up out of a fist on Seed 24 and put it back in his sleeve, and on Seed 27 the hands came out one short. **T098:** the yard goes around the place with a look and no number; if a hand is lifted out of the ground in a month or a year a slate will be found and a figure made to stand at its foot, and if no hand comes up the space will go on being gone around and the month will go on standing short without a figure. No name, cause, body, accident or payment. Carry it into Reed, gather interest, do not pay. On Day 120 the yard turned toward the reeds and on Day 122 the bundles came in, judged by the arm that carried them; a woman asked what the hands that cut them would cost and the reed-cutter went past with the question unanswered, and nobody gave a figure to the work that would want hands before the week was out. The bell's four, four and a gap was **heard** on Day 119 and nobody crossed himself; the heard bell is kept beside the told bell and must not be joined to it. The steps at the Fishstairs came back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the whole market walked over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk has sat with his slate for a measure nobody called for weeks and nobody has taken him off; a measure set down before anybody called it is set down ahead of a death. The district has one word, *weather*, and one past tense, *the thaw that was*, that let a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **A third private record is now going past him in a material no Court thumb will read.** **T099 planted on Day 122:** a reed-cutter's tally in grey wool or reed-fibre cord, knots set at arm's length, one tied low with teeth and thumb, carried on the plank walk at the Weir and not counted. *You can ask the cord* --- *I can't read a cord* --- *No more can the Court.* It lies on a place of its own beside Mira's grey cord and the second twist beneath it, Orra's knot-string beside Mira's board, Alda's six pages in the little tin turned down toward a floorboard and Dessa's pewter tin above the abandoned bed, and touches none of them. The private records gather toward the far-off day they will be laid together and the joining called an account; no hand in the district has yet existed to do it.
- **Below the sill a child came out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold.** A woman wound it in three turns of wool and called it the cold and the yard called the water weather, so the two facts still never meet. The boy read the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing. The cold entered nowhere and was neither treated nor spoken of; a name for it is years away and he does not know he is waiting for one. This is the silt-leach seed planted on Day 71 without name, cause or cure, carried through the lean week, the turn of Silt, four weeks of Seed and three days of Reed; **the threads T093/T094/T096 are carried, not paid, and no silt-leach, cause or cure may be named through Chapters 28–31.**
- **The loaf shelf keeps two unnamed gaps and the fold keeps one crust and one unnamed gap.** Two neighbours, on Seed 24 and Seed 29, named the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the boy's ear a child's appetite in one breath, laying two rooms under one cause without seeing it, and went off with their pots. The boy set their sayings on places of their own beside the shelf's two gaps and the fold's crust and its empty place, wrote no cause and did not make the joining his own. **T097 stands advanced toward the far-off day a finger lays the shelf and the fold together and an account wants a name.** Do not pay T097 early, do not let the boy make either joining his own, do not name, join or solve the three places in Chapters 28–31, and let no third gap come and no fourth likeness make a cause. A line drawn through the places would be a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand is the thing he has spent his life declining to be.
- **Below the sill's other edge, Tarn's seventh year stands three days nearer its end and the loan that can hear is still uncalled.** The knife's worn mark has six years in it and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check; to the kindling he said *Six counted. The next I don't count* and pressed the held breath to its edge without spending it, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where the breath should have gone in. He was sent for a collector once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year. His broom went over the collector's road without once turning and no collector came; Jorra's silence and his keep the equality. **The change was held and not repeated:** Jorra's bent finger led at the damper again and Tarn's stroke went on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch where it had stopped as a thing nobody would enter, and neither man named the other's silence. The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence that would call it has never been said under this roof; something must give at the end of the seventh year and has not given yet. The first movement toward the near-saying belongs in Chapters 28–29 and must stop at the threshold of the word. Jorra's dust, the room standing a finger's width open, the unnamed men, the space under the rack and the broken birth-slates beneath it are keepings no slate can enter; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed. The boy is the only person who has seen them and has no standing to ask.
- **On a day in the last war a room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath**, their heat gone out with no way to feed it, as told by a man over a bucket at the well in chapter 13 and by a second man come downriver from Greybank on Day 84, and only *mentioned* by a third voice at the bucket-round on Day 102, each as a dated curiosity eighteen leagues east with the same words *not the war's hand*, differing only in breath through the nose against breath made in the throat. The two tellings have sat on their own tray through three days, joined to no kiln: Sella, who lost a husband to that danger, heard them and told the fire it was the thaw, and Jorra's bent finger went to a damper that the house calls only a holding, never a taking, and the boy read the same iron as a holding and a taking with no Grathic word for both. No third telling came. Planted toward ~650 (T065/T095). - **No collector came for the woman in the brook and nobody named the omission**; she is more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and still unentered, the number gone into the water in her thumb. Warmth can be left where it fell and the morning still comes: the first edge of the heresy that will one day refuse to collect, planted as something simply not done, with no word, no doctrine and no argument. The boy is inside three accounts that never asked him --- the house's, the Court's and Dessa's count of bits --- and the third is gone into the water with the number still in her thumb.
- **The number still goes around in fists, mouths, shoulders, loaves, glances, armfuls and weather:** a number goes into a slate and not into a strip, Berr's twice-wiped thumb and self-removal, the true subtraction unspoken, the printed figure unchallenged, the house's arithmetic with two spaces on the loaf row that nobody names, a bundle judged by the arm that carries it, and a cord that no Court thumb can read. - **One of the two crusts in the fold is gone and nobody says who took it, that it is gone, or what became of it;** the spent one lies stiff beside an empty place that has crossed into the new month of Reed without a name. The pale river stone is gone from the fold in House Silt and nobody says it is gone either; the weak board is gone under the silt and nobody makes an accident of it; the broken birth-slates are under the rack and nobody enters them; the hollow where the stone lay is left in the wool and nobody says the stone was taken. - **Sella's widow's mark stands confirmed by a factor at Greybank and entered as a kindness;** it was not touched once in three days, because a strip, a rack, a shelf, a well, a bucket, a road, a market, a post, a door, a basket, a bed and a dusk are not openings and no opening came. - **The kiln's breath is named as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, and Mud End called the death merciful;** what the house cannot measure it calls weather or mercy: the cough that killed a woman, the debt, the numb hand, the ridge burned and called dealt with, the shut counting rooms eighteen leagues east, the damper called a holding and never a taking, and the thaw itself are all called something that lets a person go home warm without owning a day.
- **The Court's room and the Eel House have the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house, and House Silt is a fifth such room set beside them;** the boy has kept all five apart on purpose, and no cause has been written in any of them. Berr takes the speaker out of his sentences as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, and the district's grammar of weather does the same work.
