# State of things — at the close of chapter 4 (private)

*This is the snapshot the author reads before writing chapter 5. Days 5 and 6 have been lived: a house kept its fire and its boy through a quiet morning, a midwife gave kind wrong answers over a child going grey, and the year's first measure was taken out of a dying neighbour in a room with a pot on a bench while the boy lay in a basket against the wall. The figure read aloud at the Cross has become a figure written on a slate beside a bed. The flat note has now come from four sources in five hearings --- cup, Vail pot, district's slate twice, and a person giving --- and nobody has said what it is. Nothing has been explained. The Drawing is behind him; Low Kiln still says three days to the Five and does not say what it counts.*

---

## The moment

Day 6 is done. On Stone 11 the coals held under their ash through the night, and Sella Vail touched the widow's mark at her throat before she lifted the kiln lid and at no other time; the boy, five days old, lay under a wool fold that would not stay on his chin, and she put it right without looking at him and gave every instruction to the fire. Tarn swept the yard before light, took the small bench knife from the sill and put it back where it could be checked, and said so unasked; his broom stopped for two strokes at the flat note of a pot set down in the wheel-shed, and he did not turn his head. Jorra Vail worked the wheel with slip dried to old bone on his forearms and spoke to the pots. Alda Reed came to look at the child she had delivered, reached for a slate before the child, touched the tin inside her bodice, gave exact dates where a neighbour mother asked for comfort, called the third-day chill a graft settling, wrote a note she turned writing-down, and went away; the boy knew the afterdrop and was corrected by every woman in the room.

On Stone 12, the Hearth Drawing, Sella carried him in the basket two doors down to a neighbour's house and set it at the edge of the light, not where a slate lay open. A collector with a soft washed cuff set his hand on his slate before he looked at the man on the board-bed, took a tube of cold iron from its cloth, found the way with two fingers, and held it without pressing; the man gave with the small flat knock, the fifth hearing and the first from a person. The collector wrote the figure Low Kiln had heard at the Cross, passed his finger over it once, and said *the measure is banked; the account stands; the return is recorded.* His reason was the world kept alive, and nobody in the room was cruel. Later the rekindled child was worse, the women called it a graft settling, Alda wrote a second note and turned it down under a milk cup, and the grey ridge along the skirting board was thicker by a finger's width than the ridge at Vail and turned the corner without a break. The year's first measure had been taken from the dying and the newly born on the same day. At dusk Sella banked the coals and fed him from the house's own dish; Tarn swept the yard again; Jorra came in from the back room with slate dust on his forearms; Dessa Silt stood at her threshold three doors down, coughed from the pits and called it weather, counted nineteen steps into her thumb, and did not look into the basket. The boy filed the two ridges, the charred slate and the barrow of grey side by side and kept four, four and a gap.

Day count: **Day 6 complete.** Day 7 is Stone 13, Y.H. 812, a Thirdday. The Hearth Drawing is past; the Five are still spoken of as three days off by a district that has counted what it has and does not care to say it.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, six days old by the night of Day 6. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He is carried everywhere: out of the bed into the basket at first light on Stone 12, set at the edge of a room, lifted back at dusk. He drools; the thread at his mouth had frozen by the walk home; the head goes where it wants; the hand outside the fold goes up an inch toward a bench, a tube, his own chin, and comes down; there is no surface within two paces it can reach. He cannot roll, cannot hold up his head past a point without the small wet sound, cannot keep wool under his chin. The branching red \lore{birth-flower} runs from the third rib on the left down the forearm and stops short of the wrist; its edge was shown and covered twice this chapter. The cold comes in at cheek, temple and wrist; he knows its order and cannot argue with his own body's ledger.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired a new object and a new humiliation. He knows the order of an operation --- field, way in, instrument, vessel's answer, complication, cost --- and found it in a room that smelled of tallow, not in a white room. He knows the Afterdrop twice over and has been corrected twice by kinder, older, more useful women; he knows the shape of a chart signed without reading the note underneath it and recognised it again in the soft exactness of Alda's dates. He now knows that a figure read over two hundred heads in a square is the same figure that can be taken out of a man and entered under a hand in a room where nobody leans in. He knows the rule of Sella's mark: touched before the kiln opens, at no other time. He knows the two kinds of keeping he can see --- Jorra's not-looking, Sella's refusal to speak, Dessa's count in her thumb, Tarn's silence over the note --- and that he cannot enter any of them. He is the only adult in the death-house and the least use in it. Correctness is the whole humiliation, and the collector goes on being reasonable.

**Powers.** None usable, no rank change. He cannot move Warm, cannot clamp, cannot speak, cannot make a fire, cannot read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, cannot enter a figure anywhere. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the floorboards' four, four and a knot the width of a thumb; the death-house bench's four, four and a knot; the bell's four, four and a gap; the ridge a finger's width thicker; nineteen steps counted by another woman. The ear holds the flat note in five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and a man giving at the Drawing --- with this chapter adding the Vail pot and the first human source. He knows \lore{the full note} from its flat shadow and has no Grathic word for either. The knowing buys nothing: no hand has reached a vessel, nobody has tapped a slate for him, and no one has said what the sound was. The hot trace that carried him is in him still and he has no name for it.

**Possessions and money.** Nothing gained, nothing lost. The small pale river stone, colour of skimmed milk, is still in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in House Silt, unmentioned. The small bench knife is back on the sill where it can be checked; the charred slate still lies in the kiln ash; the basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word. He owns no coins and none change hands. Figures go around him and are not his to spend: one in twelve, the year's first measure, three days to the Five, a finger's width, nineteen steps. The gruel, the bread and the warmth of the Vail hearth remain unentered; the house that paid twice has now taken a measure out of the district and goes on keeping the boy without saying so.

**Wounds carried from the other life.** The call he never made to Nina --- nine summers, nine promises, a boat never bought --- was reached for twice in a place where a pocket should have been; the lit name was not looked at then and cannot be looked at now. Tasha Broussard's chart, signed without reading the nurse's note, and the institution calling it a systems failure, came back in the kind exactness of a midwife's dates. Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* was not spoken this chapter; Ostrowski's voice stayed offstage. His father's casket slightly crooked in the aisle; Elena's quiet leaving, Marisol's temperate one. Des's laugh did not return. The pause in the neat hand has been set beside the pause at the flare and the pause on the birth-slate three times and has not made a fourth. In Sarn's terms he is an unclosed account heavy enough to crack a slate, and he has been entered in two accounts that never asked him.

## Who is where, and what they want now

### Under the Vail roof at night

- **Corin/Dan** --- on his bed under the roof with the fold at his chin, fed from the house's own dish, cold at the door's finger's width, awake, silent. Wants the breast, wants the day entered somewhere, wants a sentence that would let him correct the room; gets none of the three and cannot ask for any.
- **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the Vail fire --- at the fire after banking the coals with the back of a broken pot. She touched the widow's mark once, before the opening on Stone 11, and not at the bank on Stone 12. She carried the boy to the Drawing and back, set the basket where he could see and not join, put the fold right without looking, and said nothing about where they went or what was taken. She has not written him as Vail, keeps a private roll in a careful hand she will not show, and will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take his coat down. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained; the Greybank confirmation has made that harder without giving her anyone to answer.
- **Tarn**, 22, the Vail's clay-bound hand --- in the yard after a second sweep, the small knife back on the bench where it can be checked. Six years of a seven counted; he will not count the seventh. His broom stopped two strokes at the Vail pot's flat note on Stone 11 and he did not turn his head or name the sound. Said *I took the small knife. I put it back. You can check* and *I'll sweep it again* to the yard, unasked. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and grudges the house the loan of a bed for somebody else. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, still unsaid.
- **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- come in from the back room with slate dust grey and fine worked into his forearms past the bend of the little finger, which is not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Said *the back room's cold*, which was neither answer nor question, and shifted the damper again before Sella had finished. Still wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself. The broken slates under the drying rack and the men whose names are not spoken remain unshown.
- **\person{Garl Vail}** --- absent and the shape of most of the house. Not collected in the Long Cold; his warmth lies where it fell, the Ash ridge is its shape, the coat keeps his broad shape on the peg, the seat keeps his twenty-year leftward lean. His debt against a firing of tiles was left uncalled at the Cross and remains neither paid nor done with. His proverb lives in the house's mouth: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*

### In and from the death-house two doors down

- **The unnamed collector** --- gone out with his satchel and his slate. Wet-wool coat, cuff soft from washing and folded back twice, grey fingertips he kept closed until needed; fingers exact and not cruel. He set his hand on the slate before he looked at the man, took the cold iron tube from its cloth, held it, wiped the place with a folded rag, kept pressure two breaths after the giving, and wrote the figure in a neat small upright hand. He spoke once, to the slate: *The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded.* He believes he keeps the world alive and will go home to a warm room. **He is not Marra and not the clerk from the Cross.** One line, no sheet; log him in `characters/_minor.md`.
- **\person{Alda Reed}**, 42, the hearth-maid --- back from the death-house with the little tin on its cord inside her bodice, touched once in the Vail house and once beside the child, never opened. Slate before face, instrument before person; exact dates where comfort was asked for; a second note written at Vail and turned writing-down on the bench, another written in the death-house and slid under a milk cup, still unread. Crossed two fingers at the flower's edge on the boy's wrist and at the red mark at a child's hairline without knowing she did either. Will lie to keep a child warm and an account in order; wants both, and keeps a second record on her person that is not the slate.
- **The neighbour mother and her rekindled child** --- walk-ons. The child was carried into the Vail house on Stone 11 and is worse on the third day of its return in the death-house on Stone 12: grey fingertips, breath shallow and then deep with a pause the longest of the four, a face set like a smile. The mother sat with undrunk milk, asked whether to wrap him, warm the stone and wake him for milk, and was answered with a date and an instruction not to rub the feet. The midwives and household women who corrected the boy with proverbs addressed to pots are walk-ons. No sheets required.

### Three doors down

- **\person[Dessa Silt]**, 24 --- at her own threshold with her shoulder against the jamb, thin and worked older than her years, slip-grey nails, wet clay at the hem. Cough from the pits and the water in the pits, which she calls *Weather*; thumb on the pale sieve-wire scar on her left forearm; the door-shut smile. Nineteen steps from pit to drying rack and back every day for six years, now counted in her thumb because the count keeps a larger thing out of the room. She did not look into the basket and does not know her son is in it. She believes him spent among the Forty and will not say the number or the name; Alda's lie and dates lie over the belief. She will die on Day 38, uncollected, in \place{the Slip Brook}. Wants him kept and cannot say so.

The boy watched both and could not address, refuse, reach or speak to either. ## What he knows and does not know

**He knows.** How a body fails: airway, bleeding, bones, burns, cold, infection, afterdrop and rewarming, the small evidence a body carries, and triage. The smell of machine coffee and hospital sanitizer, snow going up a sleeve, orange streetlight on wet snow, his mother's kitchen, his daughter's photograph, the shape of an unsent sentence. He knows he died and that he is in a baby's body. This chapter added: that Sella touches the widow's mark before opening the kiln and at no other time, and says instructions to the fire and never to him; that Garl's objects keep their places and the debt is spoken around; that Tarn's broom stops two strokes at a pot's flat note and he does not turn his head; that Jorra's forearms carry slate dust from a back room no one asks about; that the order of a hand is slate before face in three rooms; that a midwife's exact dates answer a question nobody has put into words and close it all the same; that the third-day child is worse in the way a rewarming patient is worse, and that the face that looks peaceful is not peaceful; that the room's kindness is older and kinder and wrong; that a cold iron tube against a way inside the forearm ends in a small flat knock and a pale round place with no wound and no blood; that the collector settles the slate before he looks at the man and says the verbs with the people taken out; that the figure on the slate beside the bed is the figure two hundred people leaned in to hear; that he cannot reach the tube, read the slate or lift a knuckle to either; that the death-house ridge is a finger's width thicker than the Vail ridge and turns the corner without a break; that Dessa counts nineteen steps in her thumb and does not look into the basket.

**He does not know.** The word *Sarn*, or that the Warm is the stuff the world is made of; any Grathic word beyond what he has absorbed by listening; that the Return is an industry with collectors, bankers and a deduction; that he was bought; that forty children paid for his whole return; who the woman in the grey coat is and what she wrote or what her name says; why the flower is on his ribs; why his fevers will come; that ways run in spirals; that Dessa is his mother and Sella knew; that the Court is a bank and the temple an invoice; that somebody has watched him since before his birth and intends to use his coldness to seal the world. He does not know why Sella took him, what happened to Garl's warmth beyond the household's version, what is in Alda's tin or what she wrote twice and turned down, who wrote the neat upper line or what it says, where the Ash goes beyond the north gate, where the measure goes after the collector banks it, what the uncalled debt was for, why the mark was confirmed, why Sella's roll is kept private, why Jorra's back room is cold, or what the collector's soft sentence actually does. The true figure is still one he has never heard spoken. He cannot explain why he thinks in English in a country with no word for Chicago, and does not know that the way home is real or what it would cost.

**He suspects, without evidence.** That the gap in the count is the part he keeps, whether in boards, breaths, steps, a bell or a woman's thumb; that the two ridges, the charred slate and the barrow of grey belong together and that joining them would be a kind of deciding he has no standing to make; that Tarn heard a flat note and kept it, which makes two of them keeping the same thing in one house; that the pause at the flare, the pause on the birth-slate and the pause in the neat hand are three things of one shape and that making a fourth is his work, not anyone else's; that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused, and that the collector's reason is more dangerous than a cruelty would have been because it is easier to live beside; that Alda's exact dates are a record written the wrong way up; that Sella's rule of the mark is a hold somebody has on the house; that Dessa's count keeps something larger out of the room in the same way his own count keeps the gap; that knowing a thing exactly has never once given him a hand to act with. Suspicion, not knowledge --- never let him know more than he has earned.

## Relationships at the close of chapter 4

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. Reached only as a pocket with nothing in it and a lit name not looked at, twice in two days.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices. None returned; the market voice was left to stand on its own. The shape of Vesci's chart sentence came back in Alda's exact, kind, wrong dates.
- **Sella** --- the woman who took him and will never ask where his memory came from. She carried him to the Drawing and set him where he could see without joining, put the fold right without looking, banked the fire without an entry, and said nothing at all about what was taken. Kindness as a wall, confirmed at Greybank and still unanswered.
- **Tarn** --- the house's debt, now visibly a second ear. He put the knife back where it could be checked, heard the pot's note and kept silent; the boy kept silent too, which was the first thing in the house he could have called an equality.
- **Jorra** --- the house's wound; slate dust on his forearms, the back room between them, *he's not Vail* still sealed by the boy's arrival. He speaks to pots and damps, not to Sella about the boy.
- **Alda** --- the woman who lied over his flower on Day 1, over his life on Day 3, over his birth date in a market on Day 4, and now twice over a dying child with dates that answered nothing. Each time as mercy, each time with a hand on the tin or a sign she did not know she made.
- **Berr Clay** --- offstage; the local face of the Court whose relief at a settled figure Dan understood perfectly. His careful not-calling of Garl's debt keeps it alive.
- **Mira** --- offstage; the aunt who keeps accounts in counts and bones and says nothing.
- **The collector** --- an officer with a reason and a soft cuff, exact and not cruel; the grammar of the Cross now bending over a board-bed. Neither Marra nor the Cross clerk, and the boy could no more address him than the slate under his hand.
- **Dessa** --- his mother, who will not name him and believes him gone in the levy. She stood three doors from him at dusk and did not look into the basket. She will die believing him dead.
- **Garl** --- an absence arranged around, now with his uncalled debt beside the measure that was called. *Leave the crack showing.*
- **Marra and Rusk** --- the two halves of a purchase he knows nothing about. She pays with her fingers; he pays with his silence. Both are paying for him before anyone has told him he was taken.
