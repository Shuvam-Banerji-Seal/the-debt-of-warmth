## The moment

Day 209, Ember 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, \person[Corin]{the boy} lies on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear, one leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from that ear and its empty place still unnamed, no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform. Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and nine days; overhead the boards keep four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rises up and becomes the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stands to any of them. The fire goes on in the room that warms him and the river in the room he cannot enter, and neither stops for the ear under the roof.

At the close he has put the question to his hand and been refused. Sella has gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and there is no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that comes through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. Under the very conditions that gave the one-rhythm coordination on Day 206, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down **at once** against the fold. It was a miss: the asking is not always answered and there is no failure in the miss and no triumph in the answer. He filed it on a sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them, and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The river did not stop, and the fold took the hand without a word. Two hundred and nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; the count stands ready to go forward into a day nobody under the roof has named.

## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, two hundred and nine days old at the close of Day 209 and six months and twenty-nine days (two hundred and seven and six months and twenty-seven days on the first morning, two hundred and eight and six months and twenty-eight days on the second), carried everywhere: under the Vail roof at the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle and the kindling; in the Clay Yard, at the kiln mouth, the drying racks and the yard well; at the wheel-shed's edge, the cold damper and the flagstones (the back room and the Debt Loft never entered by him or the narration); on Day 209 down the clay road to the moot-post's thin shade at the Cross and back again. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him, lift a hand to his own chin or lift a crust two inches to his mouth. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat and was left unwiped on all three days. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform. The one change is the hand: it can go up an inch toward a sound and stay for a small intake, for a whole counted breath, past the end of a counted breath and down on a second rhythm, or with no count anywhere in the house for the length of the river's own low interval and down on the next low note --- and on other mornings and on this last night refuse to stay at all.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired three more days of objects, one more telling of a direction that arrives nowhere, one more reading of the district's arithmetic, and one more small refusal, and still no hand to enter any of them. Absences standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a hand between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No finger of his has drawn the line.
- **Far-off dangers, still asleep.** \person[Marra]{Marra} is not in Sill; the foundling's sixth half-turn gathers toward a sale years away; Alda's mercy-lie has curdled into furniture; the waiting man from Slate Row and the Waiting Room clerk take small secrets for loaves and ale; Hessa's road between Cross and Greybank, the clerk's inward slate, the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's second twist, the charcoal under the shelf and the locked subtraction go on gathering toward the far-off hand that will lay the district's private records together. **None may be brought forward or made into a quest in Chapter 53.**

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: two hundred and seven through two hundred and nine days --- six months and twenty-seven days through six months and twenty-nine days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The reading of a body with lead in it --- the pale line at the gum, the held or curled hand, the colour of the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the tremor in the wrist, the care with which a dipper is set down when the fingers have stopped telling the man where it is --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The shape of a reading said twice over a body that cannot answer, now said a third time over the district and still not the figure paid, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence with *your house* rather than \emph{I} --- heard three times now from the same man over the same basket, and called a kindness. That an opening is a thing done before the kiln by a hand that will not name what it mends, and that a bed, a shelf, a well, an afternoon, a basket, a yard, a market, a carrying, a morning, a dusk, a reading and a slate burning in another quarter are not openings. That a cost asked as a direction goes to the pot, the house and the weather and arrives nowhere; that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it; that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost; that a keeping can be maintenance and not love; that a row of record-first hands will call itself an account if no hand holds it apart; and that a district's arithmetic can change three times in a month without one mark of it crossing the wall into this house. And now: that the asking is not always answered, and that a hand can be refused under the very conditions that once answered, with no failure in the refusal and no sign standing up out of it; that a second telling of a direction is not a third and a third reading of a figure is not a payment; and that a competent sort of a crowd in ninety seconds buys nothing when there is no hand to carry the sorting to a slate.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside them. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. **A fifth place:** the pale line at the gum, exact and worth nothing at two paces, where a danger of the month goes and not among the hearings or the near-full answers. **A sixth place:** the held inch, with answers from Day 198 through the small hours of Day 209, filed on its own place beside the others and never laid across them, not a reaching, not a power, not a key, not a sign, with no payer named. **Beside the dust and the two tellings, with a hand between the three:** the burned-slate word, told twice, a direction arrived nowhere, no cause, no third telling, advanced and not paid. **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord tied once low with teeth and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and advanced, unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit and the breath held under the board on Day 166, with the reason still standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look. **T097 advanced and unpaid:** the pan's gone ring and its empty round place, held with no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause.
- **Does not know.** That he is two hundred and nine days old, or six months and twenty-nine days, or that a number was reached at all: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it on each of these three days, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and on Day 166 held it there for a counted breath.** What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Whose slate burned in Slate Row, which room it stood in, who set the flame or why, and whether the burned slate has anything to do with Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings; he has not joined any of them. What Jorra wants of the men who come to the room, what the holding back and then the holding of his finger at the damper means beyond a sentence finished, and what went on in the space between the two silences under one roof. What the stone is that Tarn took out of its pot and set on the sill in the light, or why he put it back. What ails a glaze-room hand, and what the man carried out on Day 188 cost, and whether he lived or died after he went up the road breathing --- and the narration has not invented an ending for him; none of the three readings gave a figure, name or cause for him. Why his own hand can hold an inch to a rhythm nobody counts, can keep an inch to the river's one rhythm with no count at all, and can also be refused under those same conditions; whether the inch is growing or the body merely keeping its own time. And the printed one in twelve, the locked subtraction, Hessa's unread slate and the clerk's inward measure remain figures he cannot enter, and no hand in the basket can carry any of them to a slate.

## What Chapter 53 must not forget

Chapter 53 opens on Day 210, Grain 1, Firstday, with the count exact and unspent at **two hundred nine**, six months and twenty-nine days at the close of Day 209 and six months and thirty days on the morning of Day 210, spoken by nobody, the fire's fifth missing with no payer and unpaid for two hundred and ten days, and the kiln month gone out with no boundary drawn under it and neither month-name corrected; Grain comes on in the dark beyond the door as Ember came on before it. **Grain 1 is an ordinary morning with no market and no Account-day --- the first of Grain waits on Day 219.** Keep the burned-slate word as a direction arrived nowhere, told twice in the yard, no owner, room, flame or cause named, no third telling of the shut counting room and no joining to Jorra's dust, the men in the back room or the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three; Jorra's silence deepened, his promise to name the boy ungiven and \emph{he's not Vail} sealed. Keep the household's keeping of Garl --- the salt coat, the hollow seat, the clean dark line and finer grey called the thaw unswept, the proverb gone to the pot and the uncalled tile debt --- with no lesson made, no collector called and the dead man's name unspoken. Keep the **one opening** behind the household, the mark touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, market, morning, afternoon or dusk since --- now no opening in thirty days on the morning of Grain 1. Keep the **nine** stoppings as objects with no tenth before the seventh year is earned, the seventh now at its very end and still not given, the word behind Tarn's teeth, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, the ear-sentence short of the word that would call it, six hearings beside and never among with the sixth from a person and no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and unpaid, any note out of season filed as a danger and not promoted into a hearing. Keep the **three** held changes on three separate places --- the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 held beat beneath the shelf and the Day 171 flagstone half-check --- and never let them stand up as a pair, a doctrine or a rule woken by a payer; keep the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth with all its answers, the newest a refusal, and nothing laid across them. Keep the sixth green a colour and the foundling a shape, with no seventh of either before the market of Grain 10. Keep the charcoal unread, the clay jar untouched, the warm stone cold and unreconciled in its loft pot, the pan's gone ring with no third gap, the widow's mark untouched, and no finger of his drawn across the absences. Ask the inch again as the days ask, held or refused as the body answers; never make it a reach, a power, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a key or a rule, and never let the market read it or name a payer to it. Forward, not closed.
