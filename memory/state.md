## The protagonist's condition

**Body.** \person{Corin}, one hundred and fifty-nine days old at the close of Day 159 and five months and nine days, carried everywhere: at the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the drying racks and the worked strips; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf's low angle, the hearth and the bed; at the wheel-shed's edge and the flagstones (the back room never entered); down the river road to the Weir's plank walk, blessing stone and fish-trade shade and the out-of-line landing below the north bridge; and on the first Account-day in the moot-post's finger's width of shade at the Cross with his cheek against cold iron and one crust's worth of tallow and wet wool in his nose, lifted onto the bed under the roof each night, never into the Debt Loft. He was \person{Daniel Marchetti}, forty, twelve of them a trauma surgeon at Cook County; he died going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard and his warmth left him in the giving and did not cool. As Corin he is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, unranked --- below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named and a rate of loss nobody has measured. He cannot roll, stand, speak, read \lore{Grathic} in speech or writing, reach a cup, close two inches on the rim of the basket that holds him or lift a hand to his own chin. He drools; the thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat of the yard and was left unwiped on all seven days, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound when it goes too far, and nobody under the roof has been given an instruction about that sound either. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops there as if it had reached the end of its instruction; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward warmth or the note of water and comes down. Nothing within two paces can be reached --- not the crust two inches from his ear, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out, the loaf shelf, the charcoal under it, the clay jar, the cord in the cutter's coat, the knot-string at Orra's waist, the second twist beneath Mira's apron, Hessa's edge-on slate, the clerk's inward slate or the warm fact of the kiln's heat, which came across his cheek and was gone.

**Mind.** The double consciousness has acquired seven more days of objects and still no hand to enter any of them. He knows the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and has now seen it in a midwife laying slates before turning to the fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, a reed-cutter straightening his cord before lifting his trap, Sella reaching a finger beneath the shelf before her eyes went to the row, Sella's glance at the clay jar before anything else, a Sill assessor putting the pigs' figures on a slate with hand to slate before eyes to the rail, a stall-keeper taking a mark into her palm before the loaf, and Hessa Rale settling her strap and turning her wrist before her eyes took in the yard. Eight hands performing one grammar in one district and not one explaining it, each filed on its own place; on the Account-day the square's doing of it was counted as nine hands, with Berr's twice-wiped thumb and finger laid on a figure set among them on a place of its own. This chapter added: that an opening is a thing done and gone past the way a door is gone past once a man is through it; that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen and the hands that know where the screen stands are the hands that must not go behind it; that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry; that a muscle keeps the weight of what it carried and has no nerve that can enter the cost; that a thing ground small as meal got there by being broken while a thing mixed was put together on purpose; that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it; that a smile kept like a door on the latch can be shut without anybody seeing the movement; that the small flat knock of a knot set down and the small flat knock of a vessel cracked through are two sounds on places that do not touch; that a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, and a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; that a knowing withheld is not a hearing given; that a second voice does not answer the first but takes the question off the ground and carries it away level so nothing slops; that a cost asked of a pot is a cost put where no Court thumb will find it; that a hand carries a slate edge-on not to hide the writing from itself but so it is not rubbed and no goer-by takes a figure off it; that a figure read aloud is not a figure paid and a chart can say one thing while a room has gone and done another; that the hand goes to the mark and then to the loaf, record first, person after, cost wherever it can be set down without a name; that a set clerk is kept like a clamp left standing --- not what it did, but where it stood and how long it had been standing there; that **eight** likenesses in a row will call themselves a grammar and **nine** a doctrine if nobody keeps a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; that a tenth standing up before the seventh year has been earned is a debt invented to make the other nine into a bill, and the absence of a tenth is a keeping, not a payment; that two ears have kept their equality under this roof for a hundred and fifty-nine days --- Tarn's, which hears a pot before a knuckle touches it, and his, which turns toward the note before anybody makes it --- and a loan that can hear has never been called. The surgeon's perfect reading of the cold foot, the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform, worked as exactly as the day he learned it and bought nothing.

## Open dangers

- **The kiln's breath, the danger of the month.** It has changed address with the firing season and now has a name spoken sideways in the yard: four low draws and a gap where the fifth should have been, coming through the wall. The boy knows the order in which a man goes when the breath takes him wrong --- head first, the skin doing what the vessels under it do, the core holding on after the extremities have given out, the almost-smile that is a grimace and not peace --- and the knowing is worth nothing with the heat two paces off and no hand long enough to reach it. The theatre keeps its danger behind a screen; the hands that know where the screen stands are the hands that must not go behind it.

## What he knows, suspects and does not know

- **Knows, exactly and uselessly.** His count: one hundred and fifty-three, one hundred and fifty-four, one hundred and fifty-five, one hundred and fifty-six, one hundred and fifty-seven, one hundred and fifty-eight and one hundred and fifty-nine days --- five months and three days through five months and nine days --- kept edge to edge against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces; the more exact it grows the more it isolates him, and he has no hand to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it. The order in which cold arrives --- cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back --- and the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last; the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; that a cold patient cannot clot, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire, and that all of it buys nothing without a hand two paces long. The order of reaching for the record before the person, and the grammar of taking the speaker out of a sentence. That an opening is a thing done and gone past, that a theatre keeps its danger behind a screen with a gate of two bites, that a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry, that a date given where a figure was asked has had the question taken out of it, that a bundle judged by the arm that carries it tells what was carried and not what it cost, that the arm keeps its record in muscle with no nerve that can enter a figure, that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, that a figure put into a slate does not know the hand that did not come back, and that a tally kept in cord or charcoal is a record no Court thumb can read. That a keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would, and a hand under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; that a knowing withheld is not a hearing given; that a stopping is not a hearing and a ninth stopping no more a ninth hearing; that a thing ground is a thing broken and a thing mixed is a thing put together; that a runner can carry a record edge-on without reading it and the not-reading is not a hiding; that **eight** likenesses in a row call themselves a grammar and **nine** a doctrine if nobody holds them apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; that a tenth stood up too early is a debt invented to make the other nine into a bill, and the absence of a tenth is a keeping and not a payment; that an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand. The damper as the clamp of the old life --- one iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies.
- **Holds as shapes, not facts.** Three places that do not touch: the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust with its empty place, and the field's empty place in the returning line, with the sayings set beside the first two and the saying of the loaf-shelves --- two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name --- set beside them all. **A fourth place:** the pan's gone place on the wall, held on a tray of its own with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth colour or the sixth shape; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause (T097 advanced, unpaid). **T098 carried and unpaid:** the field's empty place, gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure, no name, cause, body or count. **T099 carried and unpaid:** the reed-cutter's cord, knotted with teeth on the blessing stone and closed inside his coat, held beside Mira's grey cord and second twist, Orra's knot-string with its legible unnamed double turn, Alda's six hidden pages and both tins, Berr's locked box with the true subtraction, Hessa's edge-on slate with its unread figure, the clerk's inward slate and Dessa's pewter tin, touching none of them. **T100 carried and unpaid:** the charcoal clusters under the Vail shelf, the finger-under-board habit, and the reason standing between knowing the row by heart and being told not to look. **T080 carried and unpaid:** Hessa's record-first hand, the eighth such shape in the district, filed on a place of its own and joined to none; the bell four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the heard bell beside the told; the man in Mud End quiet as a lamb and given no figure; the well water different to one woman and weather to another; a number from a fist arriving nowhere. Eight record-first hands in one district, and on the Account-day nine in the square with Berr's thumb and finger among them on a place of its own, not one explaining the grammar and no payer named to any. The sixth green as a colour joined to no ear and the foundling's sixth half-turn as a shape with no sale, no loaf, no slate and no payer. The near-number of hands that went from strip to teeth and back, on the interval tray; Tarn's stroke going on without the half-check and his chest's small interval, the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat, set beside the bent finger, the held breath and **nine** stoppings without any of them joined. The near-saying itself: a sentence brought to the threshold of the word nine times --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- and stopped, with no figure and no name, held as nine intervals and not nine payments; no tenth standing up was a keeping, not a payment. The two shut-room tellings with no third, three counts in three places that do not become one. He keeps a finger between all of them without moving anything.
- **Does not know.** That he is one hundred and fifty-nine days old: nobody has told him, nobody under the roof knows he has a number, and there is no Grathic word in which to say what a life would count. What the clay jar on the wall shelf is for, though he has watched Sella glance at it and not touch it for a hundred and fifty-nine days. **Who took the pan that stood out to dry through Reed, when, why, or whether the not-saying would have been the same had a woman set it back** --- the round ring is all that is left and the house went around it. Who took the smoky crust, who took the pale stone, what became of the weak board, or what the broken birth-slates were. Why another loaf went off the shelf on Seed 6, or who carried it out; **what the charcoal tally under the loaf shelf says, or why Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking**, though he has now seen the hand go under and come back on Days 132, 134, 135, 138, 140, 141, 143, 144, 147, 151 and 157 and in the night inventories of 140, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 152 and 159. The name Dessa held under the sieve-wire scar or the number in her thumb, and no one can take either off a dead face now. What the men want who come before light to the back room, or why the room is a finger's width open, or whether there were three or four of them; whether the men in that room and the men shut in the counting room eighteen leagues east are in any way the same fact, and he has not joined them. Which house holds the floorboard under Alda's sixth page, and what the second hand says. What the promise of the foundling's name means, or who the men who burn slates are. Why the foundling's head went half a beat late, whether he will ever speak, and that the sale is years ahead. What the reed-cutter's knots count, what the cord says beyond *No more can the Court*, who the reed-cutter is, what the word forty counts, who Orra's forty-first knot was tied over and who the man at the stern of the collector's boat was. What figure Hessa carries to Greybank and who set it down; why her road runs where it runs and who will read the slate at the far end. What the silt-leach is, or that the cold in the child's foot has a name and a habit Arc 3 away. Which hand went into the strips on Seed 15 and did not come out on Seed 27, whether it is a hand or a body that will be found or a figure that will be made of it, and what a slate would find if a hand came up in a month or a year --- nothing has come up. Why the collector's boat came round the bend on the flood with six oars and one man at the stern who never touched them, and whether Orra's date was a lie or the habit of counting. What the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate carries, why the measure was set down before anybody called it, and who set him there. What Berr's true subtraction is, and that the figure read is not the figure paid; why he believes the locked box and the printed figure are the same account. Why the tiny bend in the low, low note under the floor is a fifth pull that misses, and who has not paid for the warmth it gave. What the widow's mark cost when it was taken, or who was named under the foot of the account when the fire was first carried. Whether the opening he watched at the kiln mouth will be paid for, by whom, and in what; who the man in Mud End was, and why the yard called his going merciful.
