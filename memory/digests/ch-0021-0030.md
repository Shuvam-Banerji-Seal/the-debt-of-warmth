# Digest — Chapters 21–30 (Days 101–131)

*Seed 12 to Reed 12, Y.H. 813: the planting's last weeks and the first third of the reed month, still Arc 1, "The Cold Stone." Day 131: the boy one hundred and thirty-one days old, one crust, two shelf gaps, a charcoal tally under that shelf, the count exact against a house that keeps it badly. Nothing joined, paid or ended.*

**Ch 21 (Days 101–103, Seed 12–14).** The planting is ordinary waiting: seed in the worked strips, hands a direction and not a figure, the house keeping days in loaves, glances and two unnamed spaces. A neighbour at the loaf shelf calls the old space a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the boy's ear a child's appetite in one breath, never seeing they are one absence: **T097 planted**, toward a far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call them an account. Berr reads one in twelve from the north road, wipes his thumb twice and goes off with his box locked; a neighbour only *mentions* the shut counting room, so no third telling comes. Men come before first grey and Jorra's bent finger goes into the cold damper ahead of Sella's sentence, a pot's note withheld from a High Kiln master; the boy reads the iron as one bite that holds and takes. Sella instructs pot, fire and broom, never the basket. Mira shows the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's. Alda crosses the flower unknowing and covers the sign with a dead woman's date; *warm and breathing* is exact and not true. Below the sill a woman winds wool round a dark foot and calls it the cold; the yard calls it weather. A pot lifted too high comes up to the rim of a note and goes back down: an interval, no seventh. Count 103.

**Ch 22 (Days 104–106, Seed 15–17).** Hands go into the worked strips: seed out of uncounted fists, a woman who will not say what her fist holds, a barrow-man who shapes the number and thinks better of it --- the district's withholding done inside the planting. Berr says a number goes into a slate, not into a strip, and goes off with the box locked; a neighbour names the old gap again (T097 advanced). Men come before first grey and Jorra's dust goes past the bent finger to the damper ahead of Sella's sentence. At the well Tarn agrees with an early thaw and a late and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying; his knife holds six counted years and a seventh nearly gone, his broom goes over the collector's road without turning, and no collector comes. Mira shows both cords; Alda covers the sign. Count 106.

**Ch 23 (Days 107–109, Seed 18–20; Account-day).** Tarn's knife lies on its sill with six years worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone; his chest is tight with the breath unspent and he tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*. A second neighbour names the old gap, so T097 has two tellings on two places, not joined, with the watcher's late turn as a third shape. Men come before first grey and Jorra leads at the damper; at market a foundling turns his head toward a pot half a beat after the boy's, rubs the green sliver and lets nothing out a clerk could take down --- no loaf, no slate, no sale. On Account-day Berr reads one in twelve over the worked strips: *The levy is called. It is not called by me* and *A number goes into a slate, not into a strip*; the figure read is not the figure paid and the true subtraction stays in the box. The bell goes four, four and a gap. Count 109.

**Ch 24 (Days 110–112, Seed 21–23).** The planting enters its last tend with the seed up and no figure. A neighbour makes a cause of two bare boards in one breath without seeing the empty place at the boy's ear: **T097's third shaping**, not paid. Jorra's grey dust goes past the bent finger, the full note is known before the pot leaves the wheel and withheld from a kiln-master, and his finger stands in the damper ahead of Sella's sentence. **For the first time the equality under the roof moves:** Tarn's stroke checks half a beat behind that finger and goes on without a word or a turn of the head; the flagstones keep the place where it stopped, neither man names the other's silence and the loan stays uncalled. The boy files the half-check beside the finger without a cause across them. Count 112.

**Ch 25 (Days 113–115, Seed 24–26).** A neighbour lays the shelf's old gap and the ear-gap under one appetite in a single breath: T097's fourth shaping, held apart. **The change is held and not repeated:** Jorra's finger leads at the damper again but Tarn's stroke goes on without the half-check, the stones still keeping yesterday's stopped place. At the Cross a soft-handed man from Slate Row hands a figure to a slate held at the edge, rests his thumb on the corner, and is filed apart from every other record-first hand --- not a sale. Alda's sixth page and both tins stay shut. Count 115.

**Ch 26 (Days 116–119, Seed 27–30; Account-day and last market; four lived days).** The last hands come out of the strips with the seed up, counted in glances and never a figure, and **one hand that went in on Seed 15 does not come back**. The yard goes around the empty place with a look and no number: **T098 planted**, no name, cause, body or payment, held for the far-off day a body is found or a figure given. T097 takes a fifth shaping on Seed 27 and a sixth on Seed 29. On Seed 28 a silent woman with lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth is filed beside Berr's green and the Silt 30 green, three shapes in three places, years before an ear will be spoken with that colour (not Fenna in the narration). On Account-day Berr reads one in twelve over the ground the hands went into and the figure told does not know the missing hand. Count 119; Reed comes on in the dark, a direction and not a figure.

**Ch 27 (Days 120–122, Reed 1–3).** Reed comes on without a boundary: work is a going-downriver judged by the arm that carries it, and a cost asked as a direction is answered with the pot, the weather or the river. The yard goes around the two unswept lines, the rack space with its broken birth-slates and the missing hand's place, with a look and a wide step and no number. Jorra's finger stands in the damper ahead of Sella's sentence; Tarn's seventh year is pressed past the planting's end and his stroke goes on without the half-check. The foundling is glimpsed a fifth time with the half-beat and green sliver, no loaf and no slate; the clerk stays set. **At the Weir T099 is planted:** a reed-cutter's cord gone grey with handling, knots at arm's length, one tied low with teeth, turned once for the boy and put back inside the coat --- *You can ask the cord*; *No more can the Court* --- a third private record carried and not counted, joined neither to T098 nor to the word forty. Count 122; river, fire and floorboards keep three counts that do not become one.

**Ch 28 (Days 123–125, Reed 4–6).** The reeds go into the beds judged by the carrying arm; the hands that cut them are counted in nothing. Men come before first grey, Jorra's dust goes past the bent finger to the damper and Tarn's stroke goes on without the check. That night **for the first time the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt comes to the threshold of the word that would call the loan and stops**, no figure and no name; the boy files it on the interval tray as an interval and not a payment. The reed-cutter's cord goes past unread, Orra gives a date, Mira answers *Ask the river* and Berr takes himself out of his sentence with *your house*. Count 125.

**Ch 29 (Days 126–128, Reed 7–9).** Reeds stand in the ruts, green at the broken ends, pale at the frost-bitten tips; the arm is a record in muscle that tells what was carried, not what it cost. At the Cross a woman turns a cup a quarter and goes off with lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth --- the **third** green at a mouth and the **fourth** shape with Berr's, unnamed and never Fenna in the narration. Men come before first grey, the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, the full note withheld from a High Kiln master, and Berr reads one in twelve. The ear-and-debt sentence comes to the threshold a **second** time and stops again: a second interval, not a second payment. At the Weir the reed-cutter ties one knot low with his teeth; Orra gives a date, Mira keeps her thumb on the grey cord, Alda leaves six pages unread. A saying of the loaf-shelves stands in the file: two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account wants a name. Count 128.

**Ch 30 (Days 129–131, Reed 10–12; first Account-day of Reed).** Berr comes up short with his cord folded and green at one mouth-corner, wipes his thumb twice, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says*, calls the levy and denies it with *your house*. The figure goes into the air, the true subtraction stays in the box; the bell goes four, four and a gap and the heard bell is kept beside the told. A man from the strips goes around the missing hand's place. Sella sets the basket low under the Vail loaf shelf and from its angle the boy sees **T100**: short charcoal marks in clusters on the **underside** of the board, some pressed hard, some faded into the grain, a keeping put where nobody thinks to look above a row kept badly on top. He does not read, count or show them or lay them beside the two gaps above, the crust, Alda's tin, Mira's cords, Orra's knots, Dessa's tin or the reed-cutter's cord. The damper's double bite is filed as an object joined to no kiln. The ear-and-debt sentence comes a **third** time to the word and stops: a third interval, not a third payment, and three stoppings must not stand up and call themselves a doctrine. At the Weir Orra gives the ninth of Ilmen and the boat of six oars, Mira keeps her thumb on the grey cord, Alda covers the flower with a dead woman's date. On Day 131 Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking and brings it away, and the boy makes no cause of it. Count 131.

**Turns and changes.** No power gained or spent: still cracked, unranked, below Thin, no speech, Grathic or hand to lift. Four threads opened --- T097 (Ch 21), T098 (Ch 26), T099 (Ch 27), T100 (Ch 30) --- none paid or joined. The near-saying came three times to the threshold and was stopped three times; the equality under the roof moved once (Ch 24) and held (Ch 25). The count crossed 101 to 131, exact and unspent. Six hearings stayed six, no seventh; three near-full answers advanced; the word forty unspoken with the double turn and thumb-weight apart. The cold foot stayed the cold to one woman, weather to the yard; the watcher a shape with no sale; Marra out of Sill; Dessa dead, uncollected, unnamed to the boy. Sella's grey streak, her counting under her breath and her instruction to everything but the basket were carried, not explained. Forward, not closed.
