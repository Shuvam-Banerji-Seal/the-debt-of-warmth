# Digest — Chapters 1–10 (Days 1–44)

*Stone 7, Y.H. 812 to Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813: all of written Arc 1, "The Cold Stone." Day 44 closes with the boy forty-four days old, one death, one sound added to his ear, one paid gesture and one word in a basket. Nothing joined, explained or ended.*

**Ch 1 (Day 1).** In the birth room of House Silt, three doors down the Clay Yard in Low Kiln, Corin is born two hours old carrying all the memories of Daniel Marchetti, a Chicago trauma surgeon who died at 2:17 a.m. going back into a burning minivan for a child under the dashboard. His warmth left him in the giving and did not cool; at the edge of the flare a woman in a grey coat wrote on a slate without stopping. In the room a river stone warmed at the Vail hearth goes cold against his belly, unremarked. Alda Reed, the midwife, reaches for her slate before the child, sees the branching red birth-flower across his ribs and forearm, crosses two fingers over it unawares and covers the act with a word and an unasked-for date. On the slate a name stands in a strange neat dark hand, written before anyone looked at his face, with a pause in it he recognises from the flare but does not join to it. Dessa Silt, on the pallet, counts nineteen steps to the slip rack and back and will not say a name; Mira Silt keeps her back to the writing, answers a question with a question and gives a crust briskly. Sella Vail's hand comes in ahead of her, feels for heat at his belly, hovers and withdraws. He counts four nails, four, and a knot where the fifth should be. The Ilm is under ice; the Five are three days off.

**Ch 2 (Days 2–3).** Under the Vail roof the house is arranged around Garl Vail, absent since the Long Cold: a hollow wheel-seat, a salt-stiff coat, an unswept ridge of Ash, a proverb said to the pot. Jorra says *he's not Vail*; Sella answers the pot. Tarn, six years into a seven-year clay debt, keeps the bench knife where it can be checked. A cup gives the first flat note; a pot in the shed gives the second. Des's laugh breaks through from a Chicago car park and goes out. Alda washes the boy, reaches for the slate first, makes the sign again and tells Dessa he went in the levy among the Forty — a mercy-lie, never spoken as a figure. On the third morning Sella takes the covered bundle up the yard; the cold stone is left on the abandoned bed; Dessa stands at the end of nineteen steps and lets him go. He is laid on straw under the roof, Tarn is given the loft, and the charred edge of a slate lies in the kiln ash.

**Ch 3 (Day 4).** Account-day at the Cross. Sella carries him a hundred and nine steps, keeps a half-pace from open slates and says *the house*. Berr Clay wipes his thumb, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* twice and never says *I*; the district's slate answers flat twice, the third and fourth hearings. Ostrowski's two duties and Vesci's chart-grammar break through in the market; the neat hand's pause is seen a third time, unjoined. Berr confirms Sella's widow's mark at Greybank as a kindness that is entered, while leaving Garl's tile debt uncalled. Alda covers her sign with a date; Mira never looks at the basket; Ash goes out in a barrow toward the north gate. The bell strikes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself.

**Ch 4 (Days 5–6).** The mark is touched before an opening only. Tarn's broom stops at a pot's flat note and he does not turn; Alda reaches for her slate before a dying neighbour's child and calls the second chill a graft settling, while Dan recognises the afterdrop and a withheld second cause. At the Hearth Drawing a collector with grey fingertips takes a cold iron tube to a man's arm; the man gives with the small flat knock — the fifth hearing, the first from a person — and the collector says *the measure is banked; the account stands; the return is recorded*. The rekindled child worsens; Alda turns two notes down; the death-house Ash ridge is a finger's width thicker than Vail's. Sella feeds him from the house's own dish and says nothing.

**Ch 5 (Days 7–9).** A pot turned toward before it is set down gives nothing: the first interval, kept as an object. Tarn sees the boy turn before the set-down and stops his broom; two ears now live under one roof without a word. At the Fishstairs Mira short-weights Berr by two eels against two marks short for six years, answers a question with her own answer, touches a grey cord at a figure and shows a second cord twisted twice beneath it that has never gone to Nine Pots. A collector's cuff brings two sentences of a cafeteria at four in the morning. Dessa counts him among the bits in her thumb and never looks in. Mira puts a brown-edged crust into the fold; a green-mouthed silhouette looks at pots across the market. The crust lies warm against him and cannot be lifted.

**Ch 6 (Days 10–14).** Kindling is four, four and an empty fifth; Alda writes a note turned face-down toward a floorboard and signs the flower again unawares; a neighbour at the well reaches for a slate before her child; a man holds up ice with a hairline stopping short of the edge; a neighbour describes the Court's room with bench, pot, draught and a gap. The pause in the neat hand comes a fourth time at the Court's table and does not join the other three; the Drawing collector's hand with no pause stays apart. Berr reads one in twelve again and goes around Garl's uncalled debt. Sella names the kiln's breath as the thing that takes a man in his sleep; the crust goes cold in the fold. Five likenesses of one room are kept in five places and no cause is written.

**Ch 7 (Days 15–24).** The last tend is counted in loaves and glances; a man brings a number in a shut fist. A collector with grey fingertips takes a measure; a carried vessel gives a flat note past the door and Tarn's head turns a degree. Jorra's grey slate dust returns after five clean mornings; a pot cracks two paces off with the flat knock and is called a pot gone wrong — a third object on the interval tray, no sixth hearing. Alda writes another hidden note; Mira puts a fresh crust beside the spent one; the pause comes a fifth time at the market's edge in one beat of orange flare light and changes nothing. Berr reads one in twelve; the Five are given as three days off. Two crusts lie in the fold; Kindling is tomorrow.

**Ch 8 (Days 25–30).** The Five are lived one day each. A coal under ash comes up the yard in a clay dish from hearth to hearth, its payer unnamed; he counts the firelight four, four and a hypothetical fifth. At the Weighing an officer reads in the Cross's grammar wearing a temple's clothes, announces a whole return for somebody absent and a name the household will not repeat. The Long Night gives a told bell of four, four and a gap, kept beside the heard bell; the Remembering gives an unspoken Garl and another unaware sign; the Last Day gives bread and no third crust. Mira shows the second cord again, knots uncounted. Sella measures both Ash ridges with one thumb, leaves them, then burns the grey and lets a neighbour call it dealt with. On Ilmen 1 the coals are still banked, the mark untouched, one in twelve printed, and the count — five hearings from four sources, no sixth — crosses into Y.H. 813.

**Ch 9 (Days 31–37).** The thaw comes on in things the district calls weather: the well water tastes different, the ice is *honeyed*, the Singing Sill gives its note early, a hairline opens in the Ilm and stops short of the edge, the Slip Brook runs clay-heavy. On Ilmen 6 a pot set down out of a woman's shadow answers *nearly full* — neither a flat hearing nor a silence — filed as the first earthly answer to the Stone 13 interval, advanced and not paid; Tarn's head goes a degree and says nothing. Alda writes a fourth hidden note; Jorra brings grey dust from the back room; Berr keeps a number he will not give and his box locked; Mira brings no crust. Dessa, worse and still calling her cough weather, puts nineteen into her thumb; as her thumb goes along the wicker an old hand in a grey sleeve reaches for a pocket and comes back with wool. The count is kept exactly for seven days and buys a shape he cannot spend.

**Ch 10 (Days 38–44).** The clay-heavy rise takes Dessa Silt from the end of nineteen steps in the night; the yard lifts her out with hands and no words, the boy held on the bank. As she is lifted, a small flat knock comes out of her body — the sixth hearing of the full note, the second from a person — filed beside the five from four sources, never among them, no seventh. The hand-on-the-wicker detail is paid in past tense and untagged: a coat pocket, a small cold rectangle with a name unlooked-at, a boat nine promises old unsaid, wool coming back, nothing crossing. No collector is called and nobody names the omission — the first edge of the later heresy that warmth left where it fell does not end the world. A clerk with a slate is set at the Fishstairs for an uncalled measure; the market resumes one in twelve over her unentered body, the true subtraction going over the basket and the thaw's cost from fist to mouth to shoulder. At House Silt a pewter tin above the empty bed, with a pale fold at its seam and a thumb's rub on the lid, becomes a record no assessor can read; Sella fetches a clay jar and turns no errand into a walk to a bank. Orra Weir comes onstage at the Weir, rope-handed, eel-scarred, half-sung, keeping her accounts in knots; on Ilmen 15 she ties a forty-first knot over the story of a small warm bundle with a flower, says *forty* low into the basket and watches. He does nothing. The word goes on a tray of its own, beside but never among the six hearings.

**Turns and changes.** No power used or gained: still cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin, unable to move Warm, clamp, speak, read Grathic or lift a hand to his chin. What changed is the record: six hearings; a paid gesture kept where things with no hour are kept; a tin, a cord and an uncalled slate in the private file; a district's arithmetic resuming over a dead woman; a word filed as a count before he knows what it counts. Relationships hardened without being spoken: Sella's kindness remains a wall, Tarn's silence an unnameable equality, Jorra's sentence sealed, Mira's two cords unread, Alda's lie now a fact nobody can correct, Orra watching to see what he becomes. Dessa is the first death and stays dead. Threads T089 (the tin) and T090 (the clerk) were planted, T024 (Orra's cord) moved from open to planted, T087 paid its Day 38 hearing, and the hand-on-wicker detail of T004/T044 was paid; nothing else closed. The next day is Day 45 at the Fishstairs: the knot untitled, the omission unnamed, both crusts still in the fold, and one of them about to go missing.
