# The Warm and the vessel — power in Sarn

*An account set down for the reader of the Compendium. The first part is what every child in Sarn is taught. The second part is what a man with a dead man's arm in his hands may learn by looking. Where the two disagree, the second is written last, because that is the order in which the truth arrives.*

*There is no spellcasting in Sarn. There are procedures: a body, a tool, a place to put the heat, and a price. Nobody chants a fire into being. They open a way, raise the pressure, and somebody gets cold.*

---

## Part I — What Sarn believes

### The common account

The world was a vessel, fired whole and perfect, with the **Hearth** at its centre and the sky laid over it like a lid. It was broken in a single moment of anger. That break is **the First Breaking**. The Warm went out through it and ran into the dark beyond the Rim, and that dark is **the Waste**. The **Nine Who Kept** came after, collected what had escaped, drove the first repairs, and left behind the rite of **the Return**, so that mortal hands might go on with the work until the crack is closed.

Every vessel that holds without leaking is **whole**. Every vessel that leaks is **thin**. When the walls give way the Warm goes out, and the collector comes with a cold iron tube and a slate. The measure is banked. The account stands. Nothing is lost that we have kept.

This account is a piece of administrative poetry. It has one loss, one repair, and one institution to do the repairing, which is the shape of a well-kept ledger rather than the shape of a thing that happened.

### The six named ranks of the common account

The ranks are trades, not prizes, and a man works years at one before he is called by its name.

- **Whole** — a vessel that holds without leaking.
- **Held** — a vessel that carries more than its natural measure.
- **Drawn** — a vessel that can shape and direct a flow.
- **Banked** — a vessel that can store Warm outside the body, in clay or iron.
- **Hearthward** — a vessel that can draw from the Hearth itself, and is therefore never cold.
- **Kept** — a vessel the Court has made effectively unbreakable. There are fewer than twenty in the world. They are spoken of with pity. They no longer feel the cold.

No one reaches these ranks by wishing. A way is cut, the pressure is raised, and something is paid.

## Part II — What is true

### I. The Warm: the world's stored heat

Sarn is not a world that contains heat. It is a world made of **the Warm**: a living, stored heat that runs through clay, water, bone, beast, hearth and star. The poor call it *the warm*, as they call a fire *the fire*. The learned call it **the kept heat**. It is not flame. Flame is what the Warm looks like when it is escaping. Fire is the world bleeding upward.

It behaves like a fluid under pressure and like a body under strain at the same time.

**Pressure and head.** A vessel's Warm has a **head**, the height to which it would rise if it were water in a pipe. A labourer in good case has a head of forty to sixty embers; a Held vessel a hundred or more; a Kept vessel three hundred and feels nothing at all. Head is not the same as capacity. A cracked cup may hold a little water under a high head and empty itself in an hour. The Court books embers and pretends this is the whole of the matter. It is not.

**Currents.** The Warm runs through the world in veins. There is a slow **Rim current** draining toward the Waste, which is why Keld's deep mines sweat and why warm-stone is found there. There is a **Sallow draw** seaward, which the Vhaleni dippers bless and the Court taxes. There are small local currents under every kiln, every body of water, every crowded room. Warm moves from the full to the empty, from the hot to the cold, from the many to the few, and never the other way without a tool and a price.

**Sources.** The great source is the **Hearth**, a chamber of stone beneath the Keel said to hold what the world had when it was made. The **Nine Wells** are smaller sources where the ground gives back more than it takes; the Court has sealed five of them and keeps the keys. Every living vessel is a small source while it burns food into warmth, and every hearth-fire is a small source that has been taught to sit still. Keldish **warm-stone** holds heat longer than any other substance in Sarn, which is a kind of source with the tap turned almost off.

**Sinks.** The great sink is the **Waste**, the cold country beyond the cracks, where everything unheld goes. **Cold iron** is a sink with a gate in it: it draws Warm out of a body and will not give it back. The **Ash** is a sink of a stranger kind: it does not hold Warm, it holds what is left when the Warm has been taken. The uncollected dead are a sink the Court cannot meter and therefore fears.

**Tides.** The Warm rises in Ilmen, when the Ilm breaks and the **Singing Sill** gives its note under the water; it falls in the Five Cold Days, when the year's measures are read. A practitioner learns to work with the tide as a sailors learn to work with wind. Working against it costs more. There are also long tides nobody has measured: the slow inward creep of the Waste, the great seepage the Court calls the Keeping.

**Too much.** Pour more Warm into a vessel than its walls can hold and the head rises. The vessel cooks its own contents. Memory thins first, then appetite, then the sense of self, then the walls. This is **overfilling**, and the Court's own physicians call the result *a beautiful return*, because the person who comes back is calm and has ceased to want anything. A vessel pushed past its bursting gives way with a small flat knock, like a pot tapped where the crack is.

**Too little.** A vessel that loses more than it takes in goes thin. It cannot clot, cannot keep a thought warm, cannot sleep properly, and in the end cannot want. Whole districts of Grath live in this condition and are called **walking thin**: people who keep moving because stopping is the same as cooling. The Waste takes the rest. Travellers who go far enough into it come back older and thinner and do not remember what they went for.

**Conservation.** Warm is never created and never destroyed. It is only moved. This is the first law and the one the Court's language is built to conceal. When an assessor says *nothing is lost that we have kept*, he means that the measure has been entered somewhere. He does not mean it is still in the person. There is no "source" in the sense of something that gives without being drained, not the Hearth, not the Wells, not the sun. Every light somebody keeps burning is paid for by somebody.

### II. The anatomy of a vessel

A **vessel** is anything that holds a measure of Warm. A person is a vessel. So is a beast, a tree, a river, a town, a kiln, a bowl, and the world itself. Every vessel has:

- **Capacity** — how much Warm it can hold, booked in embers.
- **Walls** — the boundary between held and unheld. In a person these are not skin but something beneath it, which a surgeon's hand can learn to feel as a resistance and a give.
- **Ways** — the channels along which Warm moves inside the vessel. In people the ways run in slow spirals, not branching trees. In beasts they run close to the skin. In places they follow watercourses and seams. No native of Sarn has noticed the spiral in eight hundred years.
- **Flaws** — a place where the wall was born thin. Every vessel has them. A flaw is not a crack until it is used.
- **Cracks** — a flaw that has opened. Cracks carry the measure out. They can be heard.
- **Scars** — a crack that has been closed and has closed badly. A scar holds less than the wall around it and is always the first thing to go next.
- **Patches** — material grafted over a break: living clay, iron, salt, another vessel's wall. A patch is never the wall it covers.
- **Rate of loss** — the leak. It is measured by watching a full vessel cool and comparing it to a sound one.
- **Head** — the pressure of the Warm within, which decides how fast it escapes through any opening.

**Repair is not restoration.** A mended vessel carries its break. The true test of a repair is the **full note**: tap a sound vessel with the knuckle and it answers full; tap a cracked one and it answers flat. A good repair gives a note nearly full and never quite. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The bottom of a cup, the wall of a house, the ribs of a man: all of them keep the history of their breaking under the glaze.

#### Vessels differ

- **People.** Ways run in spirals, deep, hidden, and slow. The measure sits in a shape; that shape is the self. A person is difficult to collect because the ways coil and must be followed inward, not cut across.
- **Beasts.** Ways run near the skin and in a few long loops. This is why a hunter knows where to put a knife and why a beast bleeds Warm so quickly. A beast's measure is small and simple; it cannot hold a complicated self, which is why a beast returned thin returns as almost nothing at all.
- **Places.** A town's ways are its streets and water; a river's are its channel; a kiln's are its fire. The capacity of a place is enormous and its walls are slow to fail, but when they fail a district goes cold for a season. The poor quarter of a town is always its crack: Low Kiln is the flaw in Sill that the rest of the town agrees not to mention.
- **The Vorr.** They hold their measure in the bone rather than in the blood, which makes them difficult to collect and impossible to bank in the ordinary way. They do not undergo the Return. When a Vorr dies the others seat the body upright facing the direction it came from and leave it. They say: *We were never owed.*
- **The world.** Sarn is a cracked vessel, imperfectly repaired, perhaps not the first. Its ways are the great seam under the world. The spiral in every human body is a small map of that great crack. The surveyors of Keld have entered the oldest repairs in the rim-seam as neither stone nor clay; the Keldish translate the entry as Ash, and the Court's copy translates it as *language*. Both translations are guesses.

#### What can be done to a vessel

- **Strengthened** — by firing, salting, tempering, clamping, or grafting. Firing drives the flaws shallower; salting seals small leaks for a season; tempering is the practice of repeated small strains so that the walls learn to yield without opening. A vessel strengthened badly holds less than it did before.
- **Broken** — by a cut across a way, by overfilling, by draining, or by the simple failure of an old scar. The sound of a vessel breaking all the way through is a small flat knock, not a crash.
- **Mended** — with a clamp set across a crack, a staple of iron, a patch of living clay, or the great patch of the Return. The staple is mercy and mutilation in the same bite.
- **Grafted** — wall taken from another vessel and set over the break. The donor pays. If the donor's measure was shaped differently, the patch keeps wanting its old shape: this is the **Second Want**, and it arrives in the person who was mended, wearing a life that was not theirs.
- **Drained** — a cold iron tube is set against a way and the Warm is drawn out. This is **collection**. The draw leaves grey fingertips in the collector and a bare account in the collected.
- **Poured into another** — the measure is pressed into a newborn vessel. If one measure goes into one infant, the return is whole and the person comes back with memory. If many measures are pooled and divided among many infants, the return is thin: what comes back is a stranger with a faint interest in a life it cannot name.

**Death and rebirth.** Death is the walls giving way. Where the Warm goes is the question every religion in Sarn exists to answer and the bank answers first. Rebirth is a pouring, not a resurrection: the Warm is pressed into a new vessel and takes the shape of its new walls. The old shape tries to return in the **Afterdrop**, the worsening before recovery. Usually this is only the graft settling. In a few cases the Warm tries to resume its former owner, and the Court calls that the Second Want and strikes the phrase from its records. A vessel can be poured many times. Each pouring thins the shape. A person returned five times may have five names and remember the wrong one, and will be called unsafe to marry, because the Second Want may arrive in a child's body wearing the wrong face.

### III. Where personal power comes from

A person's power is the measure they hold plus what they can route. It is not a gift from the Hearth and not a mark of blood. It is built the way a callus is built: by small, repeated, deliberate injuries that heal into capability.

**Sensing.** A practitioner reads a vessel the way a cooper reads a barrel: by the unchanged note under the knuckle, by the feel of a temperature gradient through cloth, by the smell of Ash along a skirting board, by the colour of the fingertips after a draw. He hears a faulty vessel before he touches it and is right more often than he is wrong. The Vorr read the world through the Listening Stones and come away with numbers. Dan Marchetti reads a body with a surgeon's hands and a **clinical ear**: he knows where the wall is thin because he has spent twenty years finding where the wall is thin in the old life.

**Training.** The first act is to stop leaking. The second is to cut a way and learn to move Warm within the self without losing it. The third is to carry more than the natural measure. Each stage is a procedure with a body and a tool and a scar. Masters teach slowly and badly, because a practitioner who knows the method is a practitioner who no longer needs the master. The disciplines are guild secrets, and the guilds are the gates.

**Spending.** Every working moves Warm from somewhere, and the caster pays first out of their own measure. A practitioner who opens a way in the forearm and draws heat up through it afterwards is cold, and the fingertips grey. A great working leaves a district cold for a season. The bill is never abstract: a name, a year, a hand, a memory, a season of cold in a street of houses that did not agree to pay.

**Exhaustion and recovery.** Spending past the measure produces grey fingers, a tremor, a chill that food does not fix, thinning memory, and finally the **hollow**: a vessel with a head so low it can no longer pull Warm from food. Recovery is warmth taken in from fire, food, or another vessel. There is no free recovery. Standing close to a kiln is theft from the kiln's account; a warmed stone against a belly is theft from whoever paid to warm the stone. Sleep helps because a sleeping vessel leaks less. The poor recover by huddling. The Court books this as *dwelling*, which is one of its eleven words for a deduction.

**The laws.** Warm moves from full to empty. A way once cut stays cut. A crack once made is never gone. Pressure equalizes unless iron holds it back. The shape of the measure is the self, and the shape is fragile. There is no law that says a vessel must be refilled, which is the one law the Court cannot abide.

### IV. Disciplines and schools, with procedures at strength

These are trades, not schools of sorcery. Each has a body of lore, a tool, a price, and a guild that gate-keeps it.

| Discipline | Where taught | Gate-keeper | Core procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Holding** | Grath clay yards | the kiln-masters | the full-note tap, the banked breath |
| **Cutting** | Keld, the First Seam | the clamp-masters | the first way, the clamp-duel |
| **Drawing** | Greybank, the collectors' table | the collectors | the cold iron draw, the outward way |
| **Banking** | the Hearthseat | the College of Assessors | the cup-bank, the slate |
| **Grafting** | the Bowl of Sere | the bowl-surgeons | the living patch, blent-work |
| **Sealing** | kept in the sealed files | the Unbanked | the quiet seal (forbidden) |
| **Listening** | the Listening Stones | the Vorr | the Long Sitting, the seam-tide |
| **Tide-lore** | Vhal, the salt pans | the dippers | the Sallow draw |
| **Ash-craft** | the Fenmarch | the peat families | quiet ash, the mud-keeping |
| **Hearth-drawing** | the Weighing Hall | the keepers | the hearth-tongue |

Example procedures, from least to most:

- **The full note** (Whole). Tap the vessel with the knuckle and hear whether it holds. Costs nothing. Requires the ear.
- **The banked breath** (Whole to Held). Slow the loss by holding the chest and the ways quiet, the way a potter turns the drying rack away from the fire. Costs a little head; leaves the practitioner slightly cold.
- **The first way** (Opened). Open a channel in the forearm by cutting across a surface way and keeping it open while it scars. Pain, a permanent mark, and a fortnight in which the arm is useless. Every Keldish duellist has one.
- **The cold iron draw** (Opened to Held). Set the tube against a way and draw Warm out of another vessel. Leaves grey fingertips in the drawer. The Court's collectors do this for a living and are paid partly in other people's heat.
- **The clamp** (Held). Set iron across a way and stop the flow. A tourniquet in Chicago, mercy in Sarn. Leave it too long and the limb below the clamp goes the colour of old rope and does not come back.
- **The living patch** (Held to Drawn). Cut wall from a donor and set it over a break, holding pressure until the vessel takes. The donor pays in head; a mismatched donor pays in a life the mended person will start to want.
- **The outward way** (Drawn). Open a way and push the flow out of the body: a shove, a burn, a pull at another's measure. Range is short. The caster is cold afterwards and must be warmed by somebody.
- **The cup-bank** (Banked). Store Warm outside the body in clay or iron, to be spent later or by another. The store leaks unless tended. A district pays for a great bank.
- **The well-draw** (Welled). Draw on a store the world already holds: a sealed spring, a seam, another's bank. The well is not owned by the drawer, and the level drops for everyone.
- **The hearth-tongue** (Hearthward). Draw directly from the Hearth through the Keel. The practitioner is never cold and loses a memory each time. The Court calls this the highest mercy. The Kept around the Hearthseat have lost so many they are only politeness with a pulse.
- **The quiet seal** (forbidden). Close a vessel so that nothing leaks in or out. The result cannot want, grieve, love, or feel cold. The Unbanked call it paradise and have been calling it that for eight hundred years.

### V. The named ranks of a vessel

Nine ranks, each tied to what the vessel can hold and do. They are not achievements. A person works years at one and is called by it only when the work is done. The six in the common account are the ones the Court will admit to; the other three are known to craftsmen, Keldish duellists, and the people who live near the seams.

| Rank | What the vessel can do | Rarity | How the breakthrough happens | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thin** | Cannot hold; leaks faster than it takes in. Most of the poor, the spent, and the returned poor never leave this rank. Barely a trade: a condition. | Common — perhaps half of Sarn | No breakthrough without first stopping the leak. Most never do. | Warmth taken in is lost again; a life of being cold. |
| **Whole** | Holds without leaking under ordinary strain. | Common among free craftsmen; rare in Low Kiln | Stop the leak. Find the flaw, clamp or temper it, and learn the full note. | Months of care; a scar; the flaw remains under the repair. |
| **Opened** | Has cut and learned to use a way; can move Warm deliberately within the self. | Uncommon; taught in the First Seam and a few lineages | Cut the first way and keep it open while it scars. | Pain, a permanent mark, a fortnight of uselessness; a way once cut is never closed. |
| **Held** | Carries more than its natural measure; can clamp and graft. | Guild masters, factors, successful mercenaries | Raise the head slowly by tempering; carry more than feels safe until the walls learn it. | Grey fingertips, shortened temper, an extra appetite nobody can satisfy. |
| **Drawn** | Shapes and directs a flow outside the body. | Rare; the working core of the collectors and the better duellists | Learn to push the Warm out through an open way without losing the self's shape. | The caster is cold after every working; someone must pay to rewarm them. |
| **Banked** | Stores Warm outside the body in clay or iron and spends it later or through another. | Rare; essentially the Court's bankers and a few Vhaleni factors | Make a vessel that holds outside the skin and keep it from leaking. | The store is always someone's warmth; the banker lives off the account, and the account is never his. |
| **Welled** | Draws on stores held in the world itself — wells, seams, another's bank — without owning them. | Very rare; almost never admitted to in the Hearthseat | Follow a way out of the body into the world and find a source that answers. | The level drops for everyone; a district pays in cold. The Vorr say the drawer has borrowed from a debt that was not theirs. |
| **Hearthward** | Draws from the Hearth itself; never cold. | Handfuls; the great keepers and the favorites of the Court | Cut a way that reaches the Keel and hold it against the Hearth's head. | A memory each drawing. A Hearthward practitioner is a library burning itself to light a room. |
| **Kept** | Effectively unbreakable; nothing leaks in or out. | Fewer than twenty in the world | The Court makes it, not the practitioner: perfect retention imposed from outside. | Everything. The Kept no longer feel cold, hunger, pain, or affection. Perfect retention is perfect numbness. |

The order of breakthrough is always the same: stop the leak, open a way, raise the head, route the flow, store it, find a greater source, and last of all be sealed. No rank is skipped and none is bought with a wish. A man who tries to take a rank before his walls are ready cracks, and what leaks out of him is the shape of his former self.

### VI. What the world believes and what is true

| The world believes | What is true |
|---|---|
| The world was fired whole and cracked once, in anger. | It was made cracked and repaired in layers; the oldest repairs are not clay. |
| The Return is the mercy of the Hearth; the dead come back by grace. | The Return is an industry. Collectors draw, bankers press, assessors deduct. |
| The deduction is one part in twelve. | In Low Kiln it is one in three, and the difference is the mechanism by which the poor are spent. |
| The Kept are the most blessed vessels in Sarn; they no longer feel the cold. | They feel nothing at all. The Final Banking is their condition extended to every vessel. |
| A person's ways branch like a tree, as blood vessels do. | They run in slow spirals. Nobody has noticed in eight hundred years. |
| Ash is dirt that gathers where promises are unkept. | It is what is left of a self when the Warm has been taken. It remembers. |
| Fire is a gift of the Hearth. | Fire is the Warm escaping. The world bleeding upward. |
| The Hearth is a reservoir that holds what the world had when it was made. | The Hearth is a wound. The whole economy is salvage from a failing repair. |
| A whole return brings back the same person. | It brings back the shape the new walls can hold. Memory is the most expensive thing to keep. |

### VII. Secrets about power that the world gets wrong

1. **The shape of the ways.** Everyone who has dissected a body has seen the spirals and written them off as the wanderings of a vessel. They are a map of the great crack under Sarn. Every vessel is a miniature of the world's wound. The discovery comes late, by dissecting a saint whose body is said to hold no flaw at all.
2. **The Hearth is a wound, not a reservoir.** The Court's entire economy is a salvage operation: because Warm leaks into the Waste, it must be forced into new vessels faster than it escapes, and every life is conscripted as mortar in a repair nobody will admit is failing. The oldest repairs under the world are made of unkept promises made solid — Ash as structure.
3. **Perfect retention is death without the courtesy.** The Court calls the Kept blessed and the Final Banking paradise. Both are the destruction of want, which is the same as the destruction of self. A world of unbreakable vessels would be a world of the walking dead, and the Unbanked have wanted it for centuries.
4. **The Afterdrop has two causes.** The ordinary one is a graft settling. The other is the Warm trying to resume the shape of its former owner. The Court struck the phrase *Second Want* from the record in Y.H. 651 and has spoken only of the graft ever since.
5. **A working does not need to be done.** The most heretical fact in Sarn is that a dying person's Warm can be left where it is and the world does not end. Nothing happens. That nothing is the crack in the whole structure.
6. **There is no such thing as a clean return.** Every pouring costs a piece of the shape. The rich pay to lose less; the poor are divided and lose everything. No institution has ever returned a person whole, however neat the handwriting.

### VIII. Combat

There is no spellcasting. A fight is anatomy under pressure.

A practitioner wins because they know where the ways run close to the skin, not because they are stronger. In Keld a duel is fought with iron clamps: the first man to open a way in his own forearm usually wins, because the other is busy bleeding. A knife goes into the thin place; a clamp stops a flow; a graft taken from the wrong donor makes an enemy want a life that is not his and hesitate at the wrong moment. An overfilled vessel can be made to cook from within. Ash in the eyes blinds the clinical ear as surely as a fist. A drawn flow can push a man off a ledge or burn the surface of his arm, but the range is a few paces and the caster is cold and shaking afterwards.

Fights are short and clumsy: a blade, a grip, a fall, and a long undignified interval of dragging and breathing and trying to stop the bleeding. Dan wins by knowing where the vessel is thin. His amateur boxer's chin lets him take one punch and wait. He will never be a fighter, only a man who knows what a fight feels like in the mouth. Monsters are anatomical problems before they are adversaries: a thing with its ways in the wrong place, a beast overfilled by a banker's error. The horror is clinical and therefore worse.

### IX. What is forbidden, and why

- **The Second Want induced deliberately** — grafting a wall whose measure was shaped differently into a person, to make them want a life that was not theirs. Forbidden because it produces a person who is a stranger in their own body. The Court suppresses the very name.
- **The Final Banking and the quiet seal** — making every vessel perfectly retentive. Forbidden because perfect retention is perfect numbness, and the Unbanked have been called mad for wanting it. The Court's sealed file says something else.
- **Theft of warmth** — drawing Warm from another vessel without a slate. Punished by a cold iron brand on the wrist and a deduction from the thief's own measure.
- **Blent-work** — grafting beast-clay into human vessels. Condemned in the Hearthseat and practised in the back rooms of it and openly in Sere. The law says a blent person has the measure of the donor, which in practice means no measure at all.
- **Quiet ash sold to the poor** — the Court licenses it only to the rich. Uns licensed ash is one of the few crimes whose penalty is a greyed hand.
- **Refusing a lawful collection** — an unclosed account. The offender is clip-held by the wrist until consent.
- **Drawing from the Hearth without a keeper's key** — the Nine Wells are sealed and the keys are kept. A keeper who drew without authorisation was hanged in Y.H. 618.
- **Drawing from a saint** — a saint's body is held to have no flaw. It is never dissected. What is found when it is dissected is not written down.

### X. The protagonist's edge and his programme of discovery

#### The edge

Dan Marchetti was a trauma surgeon in Chicago for twelve years. His body of knowledge maps onto vessels with a closeness that is almost indecent:

- **Walls and pressure** become the vessel's walls and head.
- **Bleeding** becomes leakage; packing a cavity becomes clamping a way.
- **Sutures and grafts** become the staple and the living patch.
- **Infection** becomes Ash in the joint — except that Ash is not a living thing and does not respond to any drug.
- **Shock** becomes the hollow — except that a cold vessel is not a dry one and fluid will not fix it.
- **The Afterdrop** is the rewarming patient who gets worse before getting better; he recognised it on the third night of his own new life, in an infant's body, and was corrected by every midwife in the district.
- **Triage** — the ability to look at a body and say *this one is gone; leave it* — is the one thing no priest of the Hearth can do. In a world whose founding doctrine is that nothing is ever beyond keeping, this is heresy. It is also the instrument he was recalled to use.

His method is the real weapon. He observes, dissects, records with charcoal on slate, repeats, is wrong, corrects the note, and is wrong again. In a world of sacred craft, method is heresy. He does it anyway. His **clinical ear** hears a faulty vessel before he touches it; what the world calls a gift is twenty years of finding the thin place under the skin.

The map fails in the important places. There are no tissue planes in the Warm. Ash does not culture. A vessel has no heart. The places where the surgical analogy breaks are the places where the great discoveries live.

#### The discoveries, in rough order

Tagged as **(T)** true, **(D)** dead end, **(F)** forbidden, **(P)** partial.

1. **(T)** A person's ways coil; they do not branch. He assumes he has made an error and repeats the dissection. He has not.
2. **(T)** The full note is a physical fact, not a craft mysticism. A cracked vessel answers flat. He can hear it through a wall.
3. **(D)** *Warm is heat in the ordinary sense; I can measure it with a warmed stone and a clock.* It is not, and the stone measures only its own loss.
4. **(T)** A clamp stops a flow and a man can survive it — briefly. The limb below goes the colour of old rope. This is a tourniquet with a different name.
5. **(T)** A dying person's Warm may be left where it is. The world does not end. Nothing happens. This is the first heresy.
6. **(D)** *Ash is a miasma from bad clay.* He tries to drain a corner, seal it, and keep a room clean. The Ash returns in a single night, heavier.
7. **(P)** Mould grown in a dark cup kills the rot in a wound sometimes and kills the patient sometimes. He cannot grow penicillin and will spend years learning why.
8. **(T)** The Afterdrop has two causes; the second is the old shape trying to come back. The midwives call it the second chill; the Court struck the other name from the record.
9. **(T)** A graft takes best when the donor's measure is shaped like the recipient's. A mismatched graft takes anyway, and wants.
10. **(D)** *If I can cut a vessel open I can mend anything inside it.* Opening a way is permanent. Some things let out more than they let in.
11. **(T)** Overfilling cooks memory before it cooks the body. The calm of a beautiful return is a burn.
12. **(T)** The deduction in Low Kiln is one in three. The notices say one in twelve. The difference is written on no slate, which is how the Court likes its cruelty kept.
13. **(D)** *Anaesthesia can be found in a weed.* He will try many. He will kill two patients with a tincture that numbs without making safe.
14. **(T)** The Ash is what is left of a self when the Warm has been taken. It remembers, though nobody has asked it what.
15. **(T)** A town is a vessel; its poor quarter is its crack. Cooling Low Kiln cools the whole account, and the gateway house does not notice for a season.
16. **(F)** A man can be sealed so that nothing leaks in or out. He tries it once, on a dying enemy, and learns why the Kept are spoken of with pity.
17. **(T)** The spiral in the body and the crack under the world are the same shape, repeated. The discovery comes by dissecting a saint.
18. **(T)** The Hearth is a wound, not a reservoir; the Keeping is salvage.
19. **(F)** The hot trace that brought him across can be followed back. Going back means drawing the warmth out of the woman the child in the minivan became. The mechanism is real. The account cannot be settled by dying again.
20. **(P)** Fire is escaping Warm. He can reason about it correctly and still cannot make a fire with his hands. Knowing a thing and doing a thing are different vessels.
21. **(T)** The oldest repairs are Ash made structural — unkept promises, made to hold. Both the Keldish and the Court's translations were guesses; both were near.
22. **(D)** *If I can understand the Deep Hand I can read the first account.* He cannot. The Vorr say only that the numbers are correct, which is not a translation.
23. **(T)** A Vorr's measure is in the bone. This is why they are never collected and why they say *we were never owed*.
24. **(T)** The Unbanked and the Court want the same ending by different methods: a world in which nothing can be given away.
25. **(F)** Blent-work: a beast's clay grafted into a human wall increases capacity. It also gives the recipient the beast's want. He will be tempted when his own walls are failing.
26. **(T)** The Kept feel nothing. Perfect retention is the destruction of want, which is the destruction of self.
27. **(D)** *A great enough draw can empty the Waste and end the leak.* He tries to reason the current backward. He nearly cracks himself doing it, and learns that the Waste is not a place a person can stand against.
28. **(T)** He is not the first mender recalled. Across the chain of broken worlds there have been others: one made the repair beautiful and lied, one made it honest and let the world break, one became the Court.
29. **(T)** A repair can leave the crack visible and the Warm free to move. This is the third thing, and it is not a better clamp.
30. **(T)** Attention is the one thing no institution can tax. He keeps the notes. The notes survive him.

#### The hard limits

- He is a clinician, not a physicist. His sense of the Warm is a surgeon's intuition, not instrumentation. He can tell that a vessel is failing; he cannot tell him exactly why in numbers the Court will accept.
- He cannot create Warm. He can only move it, and every move has a payer.
- He cannot grow penicillin reliably, cannot anaesthetise safely with what grows in a field, cannot make a fire with his hands, cannot track a beast, cannot bargain for food, cannot read a Sarnish ledger when he arrives, and cannot make a native believe that a clamped vessel might survive.
- His own vessel began cracked and low-capacity. Every working costs him head, and he pays in grey fingers, a bad knee, thinning sleep, and memory.
- Method is heresy. A false measure entered on a slate costs the hand that wrote it. His experiments are done in secret, with a charcoal stick and a slate, and a wrong answer costs someone a hand, a memory, or a life.
- The world's own rules are partly wrong. The common account is a founding invoice. He must discover the laws while every institution teaches the opposite.
- Scale defeats him. He is one tired man with a clinical ear; the Court is a ledger with a thousand years of entries. He cannot out-work a bank.
- Who gets mended is decided by power, not truth. His discoveries do not change that. Only politics and money change that, and he has neither.
- He is almost always the oldest person in the room and never the most powerful. He will be wrong repeatedly, and each wrong answer will be carried by somebody else.
- Triage is his gift and his condemnation. To say *leave it* in Sarn is to deny the world's founding mercy. He will say it, and the world will remember.
- The last account cannot be paid. The debt of the unsent call can only be carried, and carrying it is the work of a whole life.
- He never becomes a god. He stays a large, tired man gone soft in the middle, with a click in one knee, surgeon's hands on a boy's small wrists, and a habit of counting the exits in every room.

#### The long progression curve, by arc

These are private working names for the first twelve arcs. The pace is the point: nine ranks across thousands of chapters, each one bought with a discovery and a loss. Corin begins below Thin — a cracked, low-capacity infant with no gift anyone can name.

He never reaches Kept. To do so would be to win the world's highest rank and lose the only thing he came with: the willingness to look at a body and decide. The progression stops where the person does.
