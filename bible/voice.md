# Voice manifesto — *The Debt of Warmth*

**Everything in `design/STYLE-REFERENCE.md` binds this saga.** Its person, tense, sentence music, handling of violence, grief, tenderness and humour, its dialogue discipline and its banned list are the house style. Read it before every scene. This file adds only what is new because the hero is a man from 2020s Chicago inside the body of an infant in Sarn, and because the magic here is anatomy and accounting rather than incantation. Where this file and the style reference touch the same subject, the style reference wins.

---

## Person, tense, distance (keep)

**Third-person limited, past tense.** The narrator sits close inside Dan's head but is not Dan: a chronicler who has learned his rhythms. The narrator reports what he notices, in the order he notices it, and is allowed to know a little more than he does when the knowledge is his but buried. Free indirect style is the workhorse — the chronicler's sentence slips into the surgeon's judgement without a signpost, then out again. The narrator does not explain the world to him. He is as lost in it as the reader, and cleverer than most of the people in it.

**Present tense belongs only to the first life.** When memory takes him the grammar tightens and the sentences go present for a beat or two: *The stair fills. The snow is at his waist. The phone is lit and he will not look at it.* Then it falls back into the past. That small tense-shift is the seam in the prose where the two lives meet. It must not be used for anticipation, only for the actual breaking-through of Chicago.

Point of view stays with Dan almost always. When the story must leave him — a chapter in the Warm Court, a chapter inside the Hearth — keep it to a single scene and make the change felt, like a room gone cold.

Use `\memory{...}` **only for remembered words** — his mother at the table, Ostrowski correcting him by his full name, Des laughing in the car park, the half-line of a call he never made. The macro italicises them and makes them feel like a voice in the room. Do not use it for general recollection. Memory as *scene* is written straight into the prose, unmarked, with the tense-shift above.

## The double consciousness in an infant's, then a boy's, body (new)

He is a man of forty in a body that cannot hold up its own head. Never explain this to the other characters. Never have him say *I was a surgeon once* to someone who would not understand the words. It shows instead:

- **In competence that has no business being there.** An infant who tracks a visitor's limp with his eyes and knows before anyone speaks that the man's left knee is the bad one. A boy who stacks wood the way he once stacked instruments: handles out, edges in, nothing that can roll. Children in the clay yard think he is odd. The Warm Court, when it notices, thinks he is trained.
- **In the argument between registers.** The adult's interior is dry, exact, unsentimental, given to counting and differential diagnosis. The infant's body is immediate, sensory, impatient, hungry, cold. Let the two quarrel on the page. *He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the breast. Both were true and the second was louder.* That argument is the whole texture of the early chapters; as he grows, the boy's body stops being quite so loud and the surgeon's voice becomes the one he hears most, which is its own small grief.
- **In wrong familiarity.** A smell of tallow and wet wool, the shape of a slate, the way a lamp throws a circle on a ceiling: these make him feel at home where he has never been. Write the unease of recognition — the sense of having come back to a room after it has been rearranged.
- **In grief at absences nobody else registers.** He mourns a daughter in a country that has no word for what he lost. His sorrow has no object in Sarn, so it attaches to objects: a small stone warmed against his belly, a mark in a ledger, a grey line of Ash along a skirting board, the shape of a hand that is not his daughter's hand.
- **In the thing held in the mouth.** He keeps the fact of his past life like instruments between the lips: always there, almost never spoken. When he does speak of it, it is in the plainest words, and someone always misunderstands.

A rule for the early chapters: **the body is allowed to humiliate him.** He drools. He cannot turn over. He is carried, washed, dressed, and fed by people who discuss him in the third person. He is a trauma surgeon who cannot reach a cup. Play this for its exactness, not for easy pathos, and let the dry joke live there: he has reduced a thousand shoulders and he cannot keep a blanket on.

## Sentence music (keep)

Long, winding sentences made of subordinate clauses and small conjunctions — *and, and, but, so* — to carry the weight of memory and sensory detail. Break them with very short declaratives. *The ice gave. The water came up.* Vary paragraph length hard: a single-line paragraph for the thing that lands; a slow half-page for the thing that must be dwelt in. Silence is a paragraph break. Do not fill it.

Use parataxis for violence: short clauses, no metaphor, no writer between the event and the reader. Use anaphora for grief: *He had kept the slate. He had kept the ash. He had kept the lie.* Use the plain word where a fancy one would show off: *cup, clamp, slate, coal, water, hand, account*. Rhythm is breath. A wounding sentence should be short enough to be taken in one breath. A sentence of tenderness may wind on longer than is comfortable.

Avoid modern simile in Sarn. A face is not like a screen unless Dan is remembering a screen; a sound is not like a train unless the train is in the old life. In remembered Chicago the opposite holds: the machines of that life are part of the texture and may be named. When a modern comparison *does* appear in the narration of Sarn, it must be a deliberate seam between the two lives, never decoration.

## Violence, grief, tenderness, humour (keep, with the saga's applications)

**Violence** is physical, exact and unsentimental. Write what the body does and what it sounds like: a nose breaking like wet cardboard, the wet flap of a scalp, the warm secondary flood when a clamp slips, the small flat knock of a vessel that has cracked all the way through. Do not choreograph it like a contest. Real violence is brief, clumsy, and followed by a long undignified interval. Consequences outlast the scene — a limp, a tremor, a stain that will not come out, a name the victim will not say. Never make an atrocity elegant. Never look away from it either. Slavery, torture, war crimes and the daily administrative cruelty of the bank are depicted plainly, with the victim's interiority intact and the perpetrator's banality exposed. There is no forbidden knowledge here that requires a euphemism; there is only the obligation to be exact.

**Grief** is understatement plus one betraying physical detail. People do not deliver speeches over the dead. They fail to put down a cup. They set a clamp wrongly for the first time in twenty years. They write the wrong name on a slate and do not cross it out. The body's small mistakes carry the feeling. Let the reader supply the rest. When the feeling finally breaks, do it in one sentence and move on. Dan's grief is complicated by being almost forty years older than everyone who could comfort him; he will often be the only adult in the room and him with the hands of a child.

**Tenderness** is almost always an act of repair: warming a stone for a cot, cutting bread, holding pressure on a graft until the vessel takes, sitting with someone through the Afterdrop. Touch is specific — a thumb on a scar, a flour-dusted finger on a wrist, a hand smoothed over the place where a burn map disappears under a sleeve. Write desire between adults with the same restraint: the unbuttoning, the shift of weight, the smell of tallow, the thing said quietly that could not be said loudly. **There is no sexual content involving anyone under eighteen, ever**, and Dan's earliest life in Sarn is a child's life; keep it so. Sexual violence may exist in Sarn as a fact of power, but it is never depicted graphically or erotically, and it is never used as seasoning.

**Humour** is dry, northern, working-class, the joke made because the alternative is weeping. A man in a clay yard says *the account stands* the way another man says amen, and Dan thinks he could learn to be that good at not feeling. A grown man's despair observed by a hungry infant's body is funny and awful at once. Never undercut a painful moment with a quip to make the reader comfortable. The joke must cost someone.

## Dialogue (keep, with Sarn's idiolects)

Every important character has an idiolect and must be readable by voice alone. Record it on the sheet; write it in the ear.

- **Dan/Corin** speaks little, and never in the old-life idiom to people who would find it strange. He is plain, low, exact. His question is usually the shortest one in the room. With other adults who can bear it, the Chicago rhythm comes back: faster, profane, warm under the withering.
- **The Warm Court** speaks in soft, ledger-like phrases: *The measure is banked. The account stands. Nothing is lost that we have kept. Your return is recorded.* They never raise their voices. Their violence is grammatical.
- **The common folk of Grath** speak in proverbs, half-sentences and practical instruction: *Don't set that near the fire. You'll pay for it in the thaw.* They address the pot, the clay, the stone, and only then each other.
- **Rusk** speaks in questions, and answers his own questions badly. He never says what he wants; he asks what Dan wants, then corrects the answer.
- **Marra** apologises in clauses she does not finish. She is precise about other people's warmth and vague about her own.
- **The first life** — Chicago voices are faster, warmer, more profane, with Italian depths in the Marchetti house and a paramedic's laugh in Des. Render them without phonetic spelling; give them rhythm and idiom instead.

No character explains the plot to another. Exposition is folded into a clamp, a bargain, a repairing of something. Subtext is the rule: what is not said is what the scene is about. Dialogue tags are usually *said*; the elaborate verb is a treat, not a habit. No *okay, guys, whatever* in Sarn. No *system, level, quest, skill tree, stats* anywhere in the fantasy world. A rank is a trade name that people have worked in for years.

## Banned (keep, then extend)

Never use: *a testament to, little did he know, shivers down his spine, a tapestry of, in that moment, it was as if time stood still, the weight of the world, a dance of, unbeknownst*. Avoid also: *suddenly* (at most once a volume), *somehow, seemed to, began to, felt a lump in her throat, a faraway look, like a parent to a child, that was when she knew*, and any sentence beginning *And then* twice on one page. No stacked adjectives. No repeated sentence openings across a paragraph. No character explaining what the reader already knows.

Never say a thing is *ironically* or *symbolically* anything. Let the clamp be a clamp. Let the Ash be Ash. The moment the prose points at its own meaning, the music dies.

Saga-specific bans: never call the Warm *magic* in narration; never call a rank a level; never write *the system*, *the spell*, *mana*, *energy* (the word is *the Warm*), or *soul* where Sarn would say *measure*. Do not use the abandoned draft's names — Wren, Nell, Kit, Elias, Cauldwick, Kilnwick, Cindermere, the House of Seams, the Mending, the quick, the Owed, seam-metal, the Pale Water, Riveters, Culls, Ildra.

## Combat and magic in this voice (new)

There is no spellcasting. Every use of the Warm is a **procedure** with a body, a tool and a price. Write it as surgery is written: the field, the exposure, the clamp or graft, the response of the vessel, the complication, the cost. A Drawn practitioner does not wave a hand; he opens a way in his own forearm, and the heat comes up through it, and afterwards he is cold and his fingers have begun to grey. The classic image families are pressure and leakage: a hand over a sluice, a seam that holds, a cup with a flaw, a fever that is the vessel cooking its own contents.

**Costs are never abstract.** Warm spent is warmth lost from somewhere: a chill in the caster, Ash in the joints, a memory burned thin, a year taken off someone else in another district. A great working leaves a district cold for a season. The reader must always be able to name who paid.

**Fights are short.** A knife goes in where the ways run close to the skin; a man goes down; there is a long, ugly interval of dragging and breathing and trying to stop the bleeding. Dan wins because he knows where the vessel is thin, not because he is stronger. His amateur boxer's chin lets him take one punch and wait; it will save him once and cost him once, and he will never be a fighter, only a man who knows what a fight feels like in the mouth. Monsters are anatomical problems before they are adversaries: a thing with its ways in the wrong place, a beast whose measure has been overfilled by a banker's error. The horror is clinical and therefore worse.

Never write a fight as a sequence of elegant moves. Never write a discovery as a triumphant announcement. Dan notes a thing, is wrong, corrects it, and loses something while doing so.

## Motifs and image-families of Sarn (new)

Draw every image from Sarn's own materials: **clay, slip, tallow, banked coals, grey Ash, river ice, silt, iron, lead glaze, wet wool, salt, bone, birch bark, hearthstone, the slate**. The world's cold is a material substance. Heat is never metaphor for long; it is something kept in a pot and counted.

- **Vessels and cracks.** Cups, bodies, towns, the world. The flaw, the patch, the visible repair. The true sound of a sound vessel under the knuckle is a full note; a cracked one answers flat. This is Dan's first tool and remains his last.
- **The Warm and the clamp.** Heat as a thing held and a thing stopped. The clamp is mercy and mutilation in the same iron bite. To stop the Warm is a heresy in Sarn and a tourniquet in Chicago. Both are the same object.
- **The Hearth and the Waste.** The source under the world, the sink beyond its cracks. A light somebody else paid to keep burning; a cold that has somewhere to put what is lost.
- **Ash and the slate.** Grey silt in the corner; neat handwriting on a slab of stone. The human residue and the institution that records it. Bureaucracy is the horror; the ink is always neat.
- **Warm-ways and the spiral.** The channels in a vessel, coiling where they should branch. The body as a map of the world's wound. The dissection as an act of intimacy.
- **The Afterdrop and the Second Want.** The worsening before recovery; the old shape trying to come back. Useful for any scene in which a person gets worse at the moment of rescue, or a memory arrives wearing the wrong face.
- **Hands.** Stained fingers, ash-grey fingertips, a pale line where a ring was, short steady hands on a boy's small wrists. Hands know what a face lies about. Dan reads a body the way other men read a room.
- **The unsaid call.** A small cold rectangle of light with a name on it. Every death in Sarn arrives with something unspoken; every important arrival in Dan's life has been a hand reaching for a pocket that holds nothing.
- **Bread and the last day of the year.** The ordinary, stubborn, domestic life that survives accounting. Let a motif lie dormant for fifty chapters and then return changed; that is how the reader learns to read this book.

## Three sample paragraphs — not part of the story

*These demonstrate the voice. They are not scenes and must never be filed as story text.*

**I — daily life in Sarn.**

> The stone had been warm when the midwife put it against him and was warm no longer. Corin lay on his back in a room that smelled of wet wool and clay dust and counted the nails in the floorboards because there was nothing else to count, four on the near board, four on the next, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. His hands were his own hands and small. They had once been the hands of a man who could reduce a shoulder in the dark; now they could not hold a cup. A woman sang somewhere below, a low song with the words flattened out by work. He understood every word. He could not yet make one. Outside, the river moved under its skin of ice, and the whole house held its heat the way a cup holds water, with care, and with one crack in it that nobody would mention until the thaw.

**II — the modern life breaking through (note the tense-shift and `\memory{...}`).**

> The taste comes first: machine coffee, bitter and too hot, and under it the metal edge of a hospital cleaned too many times with the same mop. \memory{Don't set that down, Marchetti. You're back on in ten.} Ostrowski's voice, with a file in it. The trauma bay is a white room with a body on the table and a nurse holding pressure in a cavity with both hands, and he is already past the airway and into the question of the spleen, and the page over the speakers says a name he will hear again in a country that has no word for a trauma bay. His hands are steady. His hands are four years old and shaking. There is a light on somewhere, a small cold rectangle under a sheet with a daughter's name on it, and he does not look. He never looks.

**III — magic, violence and cost.**

> Rusk set the clamp across the boy's forearm and told him to watch. There was no ceremony in it, no words over the vessel, only cold iron and the small hiss of the Warm backing up behind it the way meltwater stands against a jam of ice. The arm below the clamp went the colour of old rope. Corin watched the line where the heat stopped and thought of a tourniquet left too long on a man who would lose the hand, and he said nothing, because here they would call it mercy to leave the Warm running until the vessel burst. The draw took four beats. On the fifth the iron slipped, the heat came through in a warm rush up his own sleeve, and the old man's fingers greyed at the tips, and nobody in the room pretended the cost had not been paid.
