# Glossary of Sarn

*Entries for the reader. Every coined name set down in the Compendium so far is gathered here, in alphabetical order. Where a thing is known by different names in different countries, the common Grathic name is given first. A term whose deeper meaning is will be found in its own place in the Compendium; this glossary gives only what a traveller may hear in a market or a hearth.*

---

## A

**Account-day.** The tenth, twentieth and thirtieth day of every tend, when the slate is read aloud. In Grath these are also market-days.

**The Afterdrop.** The worsening before recovery in a rekindled or rewarmed vessel. The ordinary cause is a graft settling; midwives call it the second chill.

**The Ash.** Grey silt that gathers where promises are unkept, in corners and under beds, invisible in daylight and plain by tallow-light. The poor leave it on the Last Day and burn it next morning. On Stone 29, Y.H. 812 a woman at the tubs told a girl beside her that the grey would be left where it lay until the Last Day and burned the morning afterward and called dealt with.

**Ash-mite.** A small grey mite that lives in the Ash and drinks the thin edge of a sleeper's measure. Invisible by day; troublesome in poor rooms.

**Ash-runners.** Criminal smugglers of the Fenmarch who move Warm and sell quiet ash without a Court licence.

**Ash Rising.** The rising in Low Kiln in Y.H. 668 after ninety children's warmth was taken for a private account; put down by Assessor-Marshal Pell Varr.

**Ash-sickness.** The condition of dreaming in another person's voice after long exposure to an Ash-wraith.

**Ash-wraith.** A grey shape seen by tallow-light where many have been spent; it follows the Ash ridge from room to room.

**Assessor.** An officer of the Warm Court who decides what a life was worth and how much of its measure may be kept back.

## B

**The bell at the Cross.** A bell struck at market morning and afternoon, longer than a church bell; nobody crosses himself at it. The boy counts four strokes, four, and a place where the fifth should be. No one in Sill explains what it is for.

**Birth-flower.** The branching red mark some infants are born with, shaped like frost on a pane or a burn gone into the skin. Hearth-maids call it a flower, cross two fingers over it and say nothing about the sign. Alda Reed crossed herself over Corin's on Stone 7, Y.H. 812, and covered the act with the word.

**Birth-slate.** The flat slate a hearth-maid keeps within reach at a birth, on which the child and the day are entered. In Low Kiln it lies on the bench, and a midwife's hand goes to it before it goes to the child. A second hand may have written on it before anyone looked at the face.

**The Bowl.** The Sere coming-of-age feast at sixteen, where a young person empties a bowl of warm wine and names a debt they intend to carry.

**Bought breath.** A person sold for a term, usually a season or a year, to repay a debt. The common form of slavery in the Fenmarch.

**The Breaking.** The spring flood, when the Ilm comes up over the Fishstairs and takes the low houses; also the Silt thaw itself. The year's flood of Silt 9–15, Y.H. 813 came up in a thin brown sheet, stood on the Fishstairs and went around both the Vail corner and the burned ridge two doors down, then went off into mud; the district called it weather and then the thaw that was. It is not the great Breaking of later years, when the low houses are taken and the town's thin place is named.

**Brine-gull.** A Sallow-coast gull that steals anything warm: a loaf, a warmed stone, a coal not yet out. On Ilmen 16, Y.H. 813 one went off upriver below the north bridge with a coal from somebody's banked fire that was not yet cold, carrying the warmth out of the fire's account; no slate in Sill stood one mark the poorer for it.

**Broken Slates.** A rebel network of Low Kiln, descended from survivors of the Ash Rising, which seeks to destroy the slates and end the levy.

**C**

**The carried fire (the year's fire).** The new fire taken into every house on Kindling: a coal under ash comes up the yard in a clay dish, each hearth touches its own flame to it and passes it on, and the last house keeps what is left. In Low Kiln nobody says where the flame was first lit or what it cost, and the Vail house keeps it banked under ash and never answers a question about its payer. On Ilmen 16, Y.H. 813 a brine-gull carried off one of its coals, not yet cold, upriver; one warmth less went out of the house's account and was missed in no account, and the fire went on taking its breath in fours and a gap. Through Ilmen 19–25, Y.H. 813 it still lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the coal the gull took was entered in no slate and missed in no account.

Through Silt 2–8, Y.H. 813 it still lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four, its payer unnamed; the coal a brine-gull took on Ilmen 16 was twenty-two days gone and entered in no slate. Through Silt 9–15 it still lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot at four, four and a gap, its payer unnamed; the coal the gull took was twenty-nine days gone and entered in no slate.

**Clay-bound.** A debt-servant working off a term, commonly seven years, with interest gathering as grey Ash in the household accounts.

**Clay-name.** The name a slave is given by the household that owns them; the old name is forbidden.

**Clay Road.** The packed-clay road from the Cross at Sill to Greybank, eighteen leagues; a Court road since the treaty of Y.H. 418.

**Clay Yard.** The slip pits, wheel shed, drying racks and kiln in Low Kiln; the whole world of a clay child.

**The clinical ear.** The ability to hear a faulty vessel before touching it. Dan possesses it to an unnatural degree; the Kiln-Masters of Sill have quietly noted it.

**Closed counting room (the Greybank saying).** A counting room shut during the last war with the damper closed, in which the men inside died of the breath rather than of the war's hand. On Ilmen 26, Y.H. 813 a man come downriver from Greybank told it at the Low Kiln well as a curiosity with a date, eighteen leagues east; the yard took it for history and nobody joined it to any kiln in the district. In houses that keep their breath in fours and a gap the saying is heard differently, though no one says so aloud.

**The Cold.** The absence of Warm; a material substance in Sarn, with a place to put what is taken.

**Cold Acre.** A barley-and-turnip hamlet two leagues north of Sill on the ridge, with one well that never freezes.

**Cold iron.** Iron that draws Warm out of a vessel and gives none back; used in the collector's tube and in clamps.

**Cold Pass.** The mountain pass beyond Keld Keep, closed in Y.H. 741 and fixed as Grath's northern border by the agreement of Y.H. 747.

**The cold that stays.** A foot or hand that remains cold after the water around it has gone down and the person has been wrapped in wool and carried inside. On Silt 12, Y.H. 813 a child wading below the sill at Sill came up with a foot that stayed cold after the water fell; a woman wrapped it and called it the cold, while the yard called the water weather, so the two names never met. Nobody in the yard gave the staying cold a cause or a treatment, and it was entered in no account. A surgeon's eye would read the extremity going before the core and the worse hour waiting on the far side of a warming.

**Cold work.** Work done in cold rooms, especially the greyed-finger labour of a collector; the Court's phrase for a punishment that damages the hand.

**Cold-eater.** A Wasteling shape that stands close to a vessel and draws its Warm away; it takes, and does not give.

**Collector.** An officer of the Warm Court who draws the measure from the dying with a cold iron tube and records it on a slate.

**College of Assessors.** The academy in the Hearthseat that teaches Ledgerhand and the valuation of lives, and holds the only right to write a lawful deduction.

**Council of Tides.** The elected government of Vhalen, composed of ship-owners and salt-masters, whose decrees are written on salt-paper that rots in damp.

**Court's room.** The room in Slate Row where the slate is read aloud on market-day and the district's measure is spoken for; in the Keeping, bank and church are one room. A neighbour come back from it on Stone 19, Y.H. 812 described a bench by the door, a pot on the bench, a draught under it going along the floor to the corner, and a place in the figures where a line should stand and does not --- the same furniture as a birth room, a kiln-house and a death-house, which the boy keeps in four separate places rather than call them one room.

**Crown.** A gold coin worth twenty silver marks, common in Sere and rarely seen in Grath.

**Cup-bank.** A procedure for storing Warm outside the body in clay or iron, to be spent later or by another.

**Cupworm.** A blind worm that bores clay from the inside, so that a pot looks sound and breaks at first pouring.

**D**

**Debt left lying.** A debt nobody has picked up, which is not the same as a debt forgiven; a saying kept in the Vail kiln-house in Silt, Y.H. 813, where Garl's tile debt lay where it lay through seven days of higher water.

**Debt Loft.** The narrow sleeping loft above a potter's wheel-shed in Low Kiln where a household's clay-bound hands sleep. In a poor house a foundling may be given a bed under the roof while a debt is given the loft, and the difference is not argued about.

**Deep Hand.** The unread script of the oldest slates and seam records; the Vorr recognise its numbers but will not translate it in full.

**Deduction.** The portion of a banked measure kept back for the Hearth. Taught as one part in twelve and printed on the Weighing notices.

**Descent.** The Keldish coming-of-age at thirteen: one day down the mine, after which some have their number changed.

**Dippers.** The salt priests of Vhalen, who bless nets by trailing them in the sea at dawn and read the Sallow draw.

**E**

**Eel-bones.** The small bones a Fishstairs seller flicks against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, one put away when the deciding is done. Mira Silt's are a tell the boy watches without anybody knowing he watches; the movement is entered nowhere.

**Ember.** The Court's unit of booked warmth, said to be worth five marks; a newborn vessel holds twelve to fifteen embers.

**Empty Cantons.** A province sixty leagues south of Grath with roads, wells and doorways but no people after Y.H. 40.

**F**

**The Fenmarch.** The reed and peat confederacy south-west of Grath, governed by reeves chosen for standing longest in a bog.

**Fen-leech (bog-cow).** A large horned beast of the peat bogs that stands half-submerged and pushes a man down into the mud.

**The Ferr.** A cold tributary of the Ilm, about forty leagues long, carrying lead dust from the western workings.

**The Final Banking.** The Unbanked's design for making every vessel perfectly retentive, so that nothing leaks, nothing is owed, and nothing can be given away.

**The First Breaking.** The temple's name for the single moment of anger in which the world was cracked.

**The First Firing.** The Grathic coming-of-age at twelve: a child makes a pot, fires it, and knocks it with a knuckle. A clear note means a feast; a flat note means another year in the yard.

**The First Seam.** The deep survey shaft beneath Keld Keep, where the oldest repairs are recorded and a crossed-out entry of Y.H. 588 is kept.

**The first way.** The procedure of opening a channel in the forearm by cutting across a surface way and keeping it open while it scars.

**The Five (Five Cold Days).** The five days outside the months at the year's end: Kindling, Weighing, the Long Night, the Remembering and the Last Day.

**Fish Blessing.** The rite held at the Weir one league upriver of Sill on Ilmen 15 (in Y.H. 813 it fell on Day 44), when the hearth-maids stand on the flat stone with its crossed mark and bless the weir and the eel traps. The traps have their mouths upriver so an eel goes in and cannot find the way out; the eels run whether anybody blesses them or not. A saying of the Weir: *the brook takes what no slate will enter; the knot keeps what no clerk can spell; the eels run whether anybody blesses them or not.*

**Flat note.** The small dead sound a cracked vessel gives when it is set down or tapped --- the shadow of the full note, a sound that stops at the surface and will not go through. Dan hears it before anyone touches the vessel and has no Grathic word for it. By Stone 12, Y.H. 812 he has heard it five times from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail house, the district's own reading slate twice, and a dying man at the Hearth Drawing, the fifth and the first from a person --- and nobody has tapped any of them for him. On Stone 13 a pot was turned toward before it was set down and gave nothing --- an interval with the shape of a sound, not a sixth hearing, which the boy kept as an object. On Stone 17 a second pot was turned toward under the Vail roof and gave nothing; the two intervals lie on a separate tray from the hearings, On Stone 22 a vessel carried past the Vail door gave it too far and too muffled to confirm, and on Stone 25 a pot cracked all the way through two paces from the basket and was called a pot gone wrong; both went on the separate tray of intervals, and through Stone 30 the count stands at five from four sources with no sixth. On Ilmen 6, Y.H. 813 a pot set down out of a woman's shadow answered *nearly full* --- a third sound, neither the flat answer nor a silence --- and was filed as the first earthly answer to the interval first kept on Stone 13; it is not a sixth hearing and there is no Grathic word for nearly. On Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813 the small flat knock came out of a woman lifted from the clay-heavy Slip Brook --- the sixth hearing, and the second that had ever come from a person --- and lay beside the five from four sources without being among them; no seventh came. Through Ilmen 16–18 the count stood at six, five from four sources and one from a person; the clerk's open slate at the Fishstairs gave nothing and no seventh hearing came. On Ilmen 21, Y.H. 813 a vessel carried past the Vail yard door gave its note too far and too muffled to place and Tarn's head did not turn, and on Ilmen 23 a second pot set down in the Clay Yard answered *nearly full* with air under the note and a want in it; the boy filed the second answer beside the first as an answer to the interval kept since Stone 13, advanced and not paid. The count still stood at six with no seventh, no hand to tap either pot and no Grathic word for nearly.

Through Days 61–67 the count stood at six hearings --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person --- beside and never among, with no seventh; a third pot answered *nearly full* on Silt 6 and was filed advanced and not paid beside the answers of Ilmen 6 and Ilmen 23, while three intervals stayed objects and water-over-water was a fifth low thing that was not a hearing at all. Through Silt 9–15 the count stood at six beside and never among, with no seventh; a vessel gave its note past the yard door on the afternoon of Silt 15 and Tarn's head did not turn, and the boy did not call it a hearing. In the flood the note of the whole district went on underneath the four low sounds as a fifth thing with no word in Grathic.

**The full note.** The full sound a sound vessel gives under the knuckle; a cracked vessel answers flat. The first tool of the clinical ear.

**Forty.** The number the hearth-maids and the people of the Weir give to the children of Low Kiln taken in the levy in Y.H. 812, kept in knots on grey wool rather than on any slate. It is spoken of as a count and is not explained in the market. Orra Weir tied forty knots in the year of the flower and then a forty-first she could not account for; on Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813 she said the word low into a basket and watched what the child in it would do, and the child could not answer. On Ilmen 16 the boy read the shape of the extra knot without a name for it, and the word was not spoken a second time.

**Frost-bloom.** An ice flower that grows where Warm has recently left; a map of the leak that made it. On Ilmen 18, Y.H. 813 it stood up in white fronds on the stone lip of the Low Kiln yard well where the Warm had lately gone out of the stone; the bucket knocked the heads off it and the sun took the rest by noon, and the yard called it weather. On Ilmen 19 and again on Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813 it wrote itself back onto the same lip in white fronds after the argument over the ice had ended; the rope went over the lip without touching it, and the yard called it weather both times, so that a woman could go home warm without owning the morning.

**G**

**Glass eel.** An eel of the Ilm and Ferr that stores a little Warm in a sac behind the head and can stop a wader's ways in the water.

**The Glaze Cliffs.** Blue-green cliffs on the Vhaleni coast stained by old lead workings, sweet-smelling in the heat.

**Grath.** The clay country of river towns, kilns and peat in the north-west of the bowl; Corin's birth country.

**Grathic.** The common tongue of the bowl: short vowels, hard *k* and *thr*, little inflection, written on slate with chalk.

**Greybank.** The Court's granary and factor's house eighteen leagues downriver of Sill, where the Clay Road ends and the Hearth Road begins.

**Grey fingers.** The discoloration of a collector's fingertips after years of drawing Warm; a mark of the trade and, in extreme cases, of exhaustion. On Stone 22, Y.H. 812 a collector fetched for a neighbour two doors down had them the colour of skimmed milk gone off under a cuff soft from washing; the boy filed him on a tray of his own, not joined to the woman at the flare or to the clerk at the Cross.

**H**

**Hearth.** The great source of the Warm, a chamber of stone beneath the Keel, said to hold what the world had when it was made.

**The Hearth Drawing.** The first collection of the year, on Stone 12; a collector may draw from the dying and the newly born on the same day. In Y.H. 812 the year's first measure was taken in a Low Kiln death-house and entered on a slate in the same figure the Cross had read aloud on Account-day; before the day was out a newly born child in the same room grew worse on the third day of a return.

**Hearth-fire (Hearth-flame).** The flame kept behind a grating of cold iron in the Weighing Hall; it has never gone out and no priest may touch it.

**Hearth-maids.** The midwives, collectors' assistants and keepers of the birth-slates in Grathic villages.

**Hearth Road.** The merchants' road from Greybank south-east to the Hearthseat, a hundred and forty leagues.

**Hearth-tongue.** The Hearth-fire drawn up through the Keel; a procedure by which the Hearthward draw, and the name of the fire in the Weighing Hall.

**Hearth-wisp.** A small light that gathers where a banked fire has been left and draws warmth from a sleeper.

**Hearthseat.** The Court's city on the Keel, built over the Hearth, where the Weighing Hall, the sealed files and fewer than twenty Kept vessels are kept.

**Hearthward.** The rank of a vessel that can draw from the Hearth itself and is therefore never cold.

**Held.** The rank of a vessel that can carry more than its natural measure.

**High Kiln.** The master potters' quarter on the terrace above Low Kiln, where the glaze is mixed and the lead smell hangs.

**The Hollow.** A vessel whose head has fallen so low it can no longer draw warmth from food; also a thin-return child with almost no shape in it.

**Honeyed ice.** The low country's word for ice going soft from underneath while the top still looks sound; on Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813 a man come up from the Ilm told the yard the ice was honeyed under the mud, and a woman at the well said the water tasted different. The district passes the word around and will not say the word for the thaw itself, which lets a person go home without owning the day. On Ilmen 19 and Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813 the man who had said *honeyed* kept his counsel and the woman who had said the ice would hold said nothing at all, so that the old argument ended with nobody left to carry it.

On Silt 8, Y.H. 813 a man come back from looking said the ice was *honeyed all through, not just at the edge; you could cut it and find it yellow in the middle*, and the dark current showed through the skin of the Ilm in veins; the boy set the saying and the sight on places that did not touch. On Silt 15 the ice that had been honeyed all through was spoken of past tense as the thaw that was.

**Hot trace.** Warmth that leaves a vessel in an act of giving and stays hot, tangled with an unpaid promise, instead of cooling into the bank.

**House roll.** A household's own slate of names and entries, kept close and not shown to the Court's assessor. In Low Kiln a house that keeps its own account does not need to explain the keeping, only to keep it.

**House Vail.** The kiln-house of Low Kiln held by the Vail family for four generations; Sella Vail keeps the fire.

**I**

**Ice-mouse.** A field mouse of frozen fields whose bite leaves a spreading numb patch, frostbite by another road.

**The Ilm.** The great silty river of Grath, frozen from Stone into Ilmen, breaking in the Silt flood; it rises in the Rim and drains into the Sallow Sea.

**K**

**Keld.** The mountain kingdom of the Rim, living on iron, lead, coal and warm-stone and ruled by the Warden of the Rim.

**Keld Keep.** The Warden's fortress city where a man states his measure before his name and the deep survey shaft begins.

**Kept.** The highest rank in Sarn: a vessel the Court has made effectively unbreakable. Fewer than twenty exist; they are spoken of with pity and no longer feel the cold. Also a term for chattel slaves in Grath.

**Kiln's breath.** The invisible poison of a closed kiln house or cellar; it kills in sleep and is called a merciful death by those who have not identified the body. On Ilmen 26, Y.H. 813 a man come downriver from Greybank described at the well a counting room shut in the last war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath; he said it as something that happened eighteen leagues east, and nobody in the yard connected it to the kiln's own missing fifth breath under their roofs.

Through Silt 2–8, Y.H. 813 the carried fire under the Vail roof still took its breath in fours and a gap, keeping the missing fourth as carefully as the breaths that came.

**Kiln-hound.** A feral dog of old kilns whose hot breath carries the kiln's own poison.

**Kiln-Masters of Sill.** The craft guild that holds the kilns, the First Firing and the knowledge of the full note in Grath.

**Knot-string.** A length of grey wool in which a woman keeps her accounts by tying knots instead of writing names, so that an officer cannot read it and a fire cannot take a name from it. Orra Weir of the Weir keeps forty knots and a forty-first she cannot account for; Mira Silt of the Fishstairs keeps two cords, one twisted twice beneath the other, and has never counted the knots on either. A knot in wool has to be looked at by the woman who tied it, and stays where she left it. On Ilmen 16, Y.H. 813 the boy could read the **shape** of Orra's forty-first knot --- a double turn with the tail tucked, tied left over right, the small extra weight of a thumb setting it --- but not its name; the word was not spoken again and the cord went back upriver to the Eel House unread. On Ilmen 24, Y.H. 813 Mira Silt showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey at the Fishstairs, never carried to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, the knots on neither counted, and her board stood beside Orra's without touching it; the shape of the extra knot was legible to the boy without its name and the word was not spoken.

On Silt 2, Y.H. 813 Mira again showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey when a figure was spoken at the Fishstairs, never carried to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, the knots on neither counted; her board lay beside Orra's knot-string and did not touch it. On Silt 13 Mira again showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey when a buyer named a figure, never carried to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, the knots uncounted; Orra stood at the flood's edge with her board and her cord and got no answer, and the word was not spoken.

**Kindling.** The first of the Five, when every hearth in Sarn is lit from the Hearth-flame and collectors take the year's first measure.

**L**

**Last Day.** The fifth and last of the Five Cold Days. Bread is baked, and the grey Ash in the corners is left where it lies to be burned the next morning and called dealt with.

**The last market.** The Account-day at the end of Stone, when the year's stores are counted into the last tend and the Five are spoken of as three days off by a district that has already decided what it will pay. Sill's last market of Y.H. 812 fell on Stone 30; no market is held during the Five that follow.

**Ledgerhand.** The Court's script: Grathic grammar with the verbs removed, in a neat upright hand, all in the passive voice.

**Levy.** The share a Grathic household owes to the account, spoken of in loaves and bits rather than named outright: three of the four loaves baked in Grain go on the levy. People speak around the word, not at the thing it counts.

**The Listening Stones.** Standing stones in the high Rim against which the Vorr sit for days and come away with numbers.

**The living patch.** A graft of wall taken from a donor and set over a break, held until the vessel takes.

**Loan that can hear.** A debt whose borrower has an ear for vessels. A loan that can hear is worth more than the debt that holds the borrower and can therefore be called; in the Vail house on Stone 22, Y.H. 812 such a loan was exercised when a flat note passed the door and Tarn's head turned a degree, and it bought nobody anything.

**The Long Night.** The third of the Five; no fire after dusk, and stories of the Return are told in the dark.

**The Long Sitting.** The Vorr coming-of-age at forty: a season sitting still against a stone, listening.

**Low Kiln.** The poor quarter of Sill on the south bank below the sill, where the kilns stand and the Ash ridge lies thick.

**M**

**The Marsh Mother.** The peat cult of the Fenmarch, which keeps warmth in the mud and gives it back to whoever can reach it.

**Measure.** The amount of Warm a vessel holds, booked in embers; also the shape of that warmth, which is the self.

**Mender's failure.** The name given in the deep accounts to a vessel recalled in an earlier age to solve the same problem, who failed in a particular way.

**Mire-fever.** A blight of the Fenmarch water that burns and then leaves the victim colder after each cycle.

**Mud End.** A peat hamlet three leagues downriver of Sill, whose winter peat decides whether anyone stays warm.

**N**

**Nine Pots.** A pottery hamlet eight leagues west of Sill with four kilns, a shared clay pit and a permanent feud with Sill over the road.

**The Nine Wells.** Hot springs in the Keel where the ground gives back more than it takes; five are sealed by the Court and the keys are kept.

**The Nine Who Kept.** The temple's figures who came after the First Breaking, drove the first repairs and left the rite of the Return behind.

**O**

**One in twelve.** The figure printed on the Weighing notices and read aloud at Account-day: one part in twelve of a household's measure is kept back for the Hearth. It is spoken as *one in twelve, as the notice says*, in a voice that makes a market lean in. It was read again at the year's last market on Stone 30, Y.H. 812, as the levy was called for the feast and said not to be called by the man who read it. What Low Kiln actually gives is another matter; see **Deduction**.

**Opened.** The rank of a vessel that has cut a way and can move Warm deliberately within the self.

**The Opening of the Slates.** Ilmen 1, when the Court opens the new year's accounts.

**The outward way.** A procedure by which a Drawn practitioner pushes a flow of Warm out of the body at short range.

**Overfilling.** Pouring more Warm into a vessel than its walls can hold; the head rises and the vessel cooks its own contents.

**P**

**The Peat Line.** The wet frontier where Sere's northern advance stopped in Y.H. 634 and where the two powers trade, raid and buy measures.

**Pot gone wrong.** A Grathic household's name for a vessel cracked from lip to foot. The phrase names the vessel, not the sound it made, and a pot gone wrong is swept around and gone past rather than decided about. On Stone 25, Y.H. 812 one cracked two paces from the boy in the Clay Yard and nobody called the knock a sound.

**Quiet ash.** The Court's licensed medicine, sold to the rich who want their previous life to stop arriving in dreams.

**The quiet seal.** The forbidden procedure of closing a vessel so that nothing leaks in or out.

**R**

**Reed Cut.** The Fenfolk coming-of-age at twelve: cutting a bundle of reed alone in the dark and bringing it back without speaking.

**Reed-wolf.** A wolf of the lower Ilm reedbeds that hunts in packs and goes for the throat and the inside of the elbow.

**The Remembering.** The fourth of the Five, when names of the dead are spoken and whole returns announced in a clean voice.

**The Return.** The rite by which the dead are held to come back in new bodies through the mercy of the Hearth; the first thing a child learns and the last thing a dying person hears.

**The Rim.** The chain of cold mountains that rings Sarn, broken by a few passes, beyond which lies the Waste.

**Rim current.** The slow current of Warm draining toward the Waste, which makes the Keldish mines sweat.

**Rim Road.** The road from Sill west-north-west to Keld Keep, a hundred and twenty leagues of improving road and worsening weather.

**Rim-seam.** The seam at the horizon where sky and clay meet, riveted with stars; it carries many repairs laid one over another.

**S**

**The Salt Road.** The trade route from Sill through Greybank to the Vhaleni ports, eighty leagues.

**Salt Walk.** The Vhaleni coming-of-age at fourteen: walking the pans barefoot at low tide and naming a tide never seen.

**Salt-thing.** A shape of salt and cold water in the Sallow that taps the hull of a boat and draws its store away.

**The Sallow draw.** The current of Warm running seaward into the Sallow, blessed by the dippers and taxed by the Court.

**The Sallow Sea.** A cold inland sea the colour of skimmed milk whose tides run against the wind and are said to follow the Warm rather than the moon.

**Seam-blight.** A blight that travels along an old repair and widens it by a hair's breadth each season.

**Seam-serpent.** A long thing that moves along the under-seam and draws Warm out through the stone.

**Seam-tide.** The rising and falling of the Warm in the seams of the world, read by the Vorr from the Listening Stones.

**The Second.** A person in whom a graft from a differently shaped donor has produced a want for a life that was not theirs.

**Second Want.** The Court's struck-from-the-record name for the Warm trying to resume the shape of a former owner; the common name is the second chill.

**Sere.** The hot southern empire of grain, wine, bowls and open chattel slavery, ruled by the Sun-Emperor.

**Sere factors.** The empire's agents in the north, who buy poor measures with credit and ship them south.

**The shape of the measure.** The pattern of a vessel's Warm, which is the self; it is fragile and thins with every pouring.

**The Singing Sill.** The ledge of hard stone under the falls at Sill, which gives a low note when the Warm rises in Ilmen. On Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813 it gave that note earlier than it should and the yard called it weather; the boy kept the early note beside the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's own missing breath in four places that did not touch.

On Silt 7, Y.H. 813 it gave its low note a week ahead of its hour, felt in the roots of the teeth before the ear placed it out in the water, and the yard said the water ran fast under the ice and then said weather; the boy kept the early note beside the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's gap in four places, joined by nobody. On Silt 15 it was spoken of past tense as the thaw that was, along with the honeyed ice and the hairlines that had stopped short of the edge.

**Sill.** The town of just over four thousand souls where the Ilm drops four feet over a ledge; Corin's birthplace.

**Slate-name.** The only name an officer of the Keeping has, entered on the rolls in Ledgerhand on entering the service.

**Slate Row.** The Court quarter east of the Cross at Sill, holding the collector's table, the assessor's house and the Waiting Room.

**Slip.** The wet clay worked in the pits of Low Kiln and poured into moulds on the wheel; it dries the colour of old bone on a thrower's forearms. Work in the slip is commonly paid for in the thaw.

**Slip pits.** The clay pits of the Clay Yard in Low Kiln, counted as nineteen steps to the drying rack and nineteen back; Dessa Silt has walked the count every day for six years. The cough that comes off them in cold weather is called weather, as if the pits owned it.

**The Slip Brook.** A narrow clay-coloured stream through the Clay Yard whose colour the children of Low Kiln read as weather. On Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813 its clay-heavy rise came up behind the drying racks in the night and took Dessa Silt from the end of nineteen steps; the yard lifted her out with their hands and no collector was called.

Through Days 61–67, Y.H. 813 it ran clay-heavy and higher behind the drying racks, going over itself with a low note the district had no word for, and the yard called it the thaw.

**The Spending.** The Sere doctrine that a vessel was made to be emptied gloriously and that a life hoarded is a life wasted.

**The Standing Post.** The raised boardwalk in the Fenmarch where reeves settle fines and feuds and which is the only Fen place outsiders are shown.

**Star-rivet.** A rivet of the rim-seam, one of the stars; one is said to have fallen in the Ash Times.

**Steward.** The head of the Warm Court; Steward Derren holds the office in the present year.

**Stone.** The month of deep winter, river ice and counted fuel; the year begins in it.

**The Sweating.** A Keldish calamity in which Warm comes up through the stone of the deep mines in a lethal flood, heralded by the smell of hot lead.

**T**

**Tallow Works.** A rendering house for sheep fat two leagues north-east of Sill, whose smell carries into town on a north wind.

**Tempering.** The practice of repeated small strains so that a vessel's walls learn to yield without opening.

**Temple of the Return.** The public church of the Keeping, which teaches that the world was made whole and cracked once.

**Tend.** A week of ten days; every month holds three whole tends, and the tenth day of each is Account-day.

**Thane of Clay.** Grath's ruler, chosen by the kiln-masters of Sill and confirmed by the Court's factor at Greybank.

**The thaw.** The spring working season, when the brook comes up and the clay goes soft. Debts in Low Kiln are often put off until then, in the phrase *you'll pay for it in the thaw*. Through Ilmen 19–25, Y.H. 813 the thaw came on in frost-bloom written back on the well-lip, oiled paper hanging slack in its frame, a silence where an argument used to stand, more hairlines stopping short of the edge of the Ilm, an early low note from the Singing Sill and a high clay-heavy brook, and the district had one word for all of it: weather. On Ilmen 25 a weak board in the plank walk at the Weir was said plainly to be still there and trodden by nobody. On Silt 1 a new hairline of grey gathered in the corner of the Vail kiln-house beside the clean dark line where the old Ash ridge had turned; Sella stood over it with a broom, called it the thaw and did not sweep it.

On Silt 2–8, Y.H. 813 the higher clay-heavy brook going over itself behind the drying racks, the early Singing Sill note, the weak board trodden by nobody, the widening hairlines, the honeyed-all-through ice and a new fine grey in the Vail corner were all called the thaw or weather, and the year's flood stood four days off. On Silt 15, after the water had come and gone, the honeyed ice, the hairlines that had stopped short of the edge and the early Singing Sill note were renamed all at once as *the thaw that was*, as though a thing said after the fact were the same as a thing said before it. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and went on not sweeping the new grey beside the clean dark line; the yard went back to its steps and its mud on the one word.

**Thin.** A vessel that leaks faster than it takes in; the common condition of the poor, the spent and the returned poor. Also the bottom rank of a vessel.

**Thin return.** A return in which many measures are pooled and divided among many infants; what comes back is a stranger with a faint interest in a life it cannot name.

**Thin-child.** *See* The Hollow.

**Tide-lore.** The Vhaleni discipline of reading the Sallow draw, taught by the dippers.

**Triage.** The old-life word for the surgeon's act of sorting the wounded; Dan's gift and his heresy in Sarn.

**U**

**The Unbanked.** The Court's name for a secret faction of persons of the sealed opinion, descended from the Ash Rising and dedicated to the Final Banking.

**Unclosed account.** The crime of refusing a lawful collection; the offender is clip-held by the wrist until consent.

**The Uncollected.** A dead vessel whose Warm has not been drawn and which walks until it leaks into the Waste.

**Under-seam.** The seam beneath the ground, below which lies the dark where nothing is kept.

**V**

**The Vail kiln-house.** The kiln-house in the Clay Yard held by the Vail family for four generations, where Corin is taken in as a foundling.

**Vhal.** The principal Vhaleni port on the Sallow shore, seat of the Council of Tides, with salt pans and glass furnaces.

**Vhalen.** The salt republic of port towns on the Sallow, governed by the Council of Tides.

**Vorr.** A people apart, tall, long-lived, who hold their measure in the bone, keep the Listening Stones and do not undergo the Return.

**W**

**Walking thin.** People with the measures of the returned poor who keep moving because stopping is the same as cooling.

**Warm.** The living, stored heat of which Sarn is made; the poor call it *the warm* and the learned call it *the kept heat*. It has pressure, currents, sources and sinks.

**Warm Court.** The institution that owns the Hearth, the banks and the Returns and calls itself the Keeping.

**Warm-stone.** A Keldish stone that holds heat longer than any other substance in Sarn. In Low Kiln a common river stone, warmed at a neighbour's fire and wrapped in wool, does the same work for a newborn for a few hours; it is not a warm-stone and nobody calls it one.

**Warm-stone grub.** A beetle larva of the deep mines that eats warm-stone from within and is a sign that a sweating may follow.

**Warden of the Rim.** The hereditary ruler of Keld; Karric VII closed the Cold Pass in Y.H. 741.

**The Waste.** The cold country beyond the cracks at the edge of all things, where everything unheld goes.

**Wastelings.** The bands beyond the Rim who take what the world loses; they have no banks, no Returns and no word for a debt.

**Water over water (the river's own note).** The low continual turning a stream makes when it has more of itself than its channel will carry; water going over its own rise. Heard behind the drying racks on Silt 2, Y.H. 813, it is not a vessel's note and is not counted among the hearings; the district has no word for it and calls it the thaw. The boy files it on a place of its own beside the told bell, the heard bell, the early Sill note and the kiln's gap, and writes no cause across any of them. In the year's flood of Silt 9–15, Y.H. 813 it became the note of a whole district under strain --- a low turning of many small failures inside one slow, certain one, a vessel of such capacity that no hand in it could have got around it, with a thin place nobody would name. The boy filed it beside the told bell, the heard bell, the early Sill note and the kiln's gap and wrote no cause across any of them; no one in the district had a word for it or a hand to point at the thin place.

**Weather.** The district's one word for whatever a person can go home warm on. It does not name the thing it is said over; it grants permission to stop looking at it. On Silt 7, Y.H. 813 a washwoman said the water ran fast under the ice and then said weather and went home warm on the word, and a man come back from looking at the honeyed ice was answered with the same word and nodded as though it settled him.

**Weighing.** The second of the Five, when the year's measures are read aloud and the rich learn how whole they are. In Sill the reading is given in a clean voice that wears a temple's clothes and takes the speaker neatly out of his own sentences, in the same grammar heard at the Cross; a whole return may be announced for a person who is not in the room.

**Weighing Hall.** The hall in the Hearthseat where the measures are read at the Weighing and the Hearth-fire burns behind cold iron.

**The Weir.** The eel traps and plank walk one league upriver of Sill, where the Fish Blessing is held and Rusk walks at dusk.

**Welled.** The rank of a vessel that can draw on stores held in the world itself — wells, seams or another's bank — without owning them.

**Well-draw.** The procedure by which a Welled practitioner draws on a store the world already holds; the level drops for everyone.

**Whole.** A vessel that holds without leaking; also the rank of such a vessel.

**Whole return.** A return in which one person's warmth is kept intact and the person comes back as themselves, with memory.

**Widow's mark.** The Grathic institution by which a widow holds a kiln in her own right; the only Grathic institution nobody argues with. A thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the kiln scratched into it, worn at the throat and touched only before the kiln is opened. The Court's factor at Greybank confirms the mark; the confirmation is spoken of as a kindness, and kindness is entered.
