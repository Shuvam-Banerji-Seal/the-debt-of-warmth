# Premise — *The Debt of Warmth* (private)

*Nothing here is told to the reader at once. It is the architecture behind the book: the answer-key, the ledger of things planted and not yet paid. Every chapter must be writable without it and every chapter must survive being re-read with it.*

---

## Logline

Daniel Marchetti, a Chicago trauma surgeon who dies saving a child from a burning car in a blizzard, wakes as an infant in the cold country of Grath, in the world of Sarn — where the dead are not reborn by a miracle but **rebanked** by an institution that measures the heat in a body as carefully as it once measured his blood, and where a man who learned to decide who is beyond saving finds that the whole world is kept alive by nobody being allowed to make that decision at all.

---

## The world energy and the vessel metaphysics, in brief

### The Warm

The world is not made of matter that sometimes glows. It is made of **the Warm**: a living, stored heat that runs through clay, water, bone, beast, hearth and star. The poor call it *the warm* as they call a fire *the fire*. The learned call it **the kept heat**. It is not fire, though it is hottest where fire has been. It has pressure, currents, sources and sinks, the way a body has blood pressure, arteries, a heart and wounds. A thing's warmth can be measured, drawn off, poured, pooled, banked, spent or lost.

The great source beneath the world is **the Hearth**, a chamber of stone that holds what the world had when it was made. The great sink is **the Waste**, the cold country outside the world's cracks, where everything unheld goes. The space between the two is the whole of Sarn, and every vessel in it is a small, failing dam.

### Vessels

A person, a beast, a tree, a town, a river, the world: each is a **vessel** that holds a **measure** of the Warm. Vessels have walls, channels (called **ways**), capacity, flaws, old patches and a rate of loss. A person's ways run in slow spirals, not branching trees — a fact no native has noticed in eight hundred years and the first thing Dan discovers with a dead man's arm in his hands.

A healthy vessel is called **whole**. A vessel that leaks is **thin**. Death is the walls giving way: the Warm goes out, and where it goes is the central question of every religion and every bank in Sarn. A vessel can be clamped (flow stopped with iron), grafted (wall patched with living clay from another vessel), overfilled (which cooks the memory), or poured into another vessel (which is what the Return actually is). Dan's surgical knowledge maps onto all of this with a sickening closeness: walls, flow, pressure, bleeding, sutures, grafts, infection, shock, the afterdrop. The map is useful and wrong in the important places. The places where it fails become the great discoveries.

### The common account, and the truth

Everyone in Sarn is taught **the Return**: the dead come back in new bodies by the mercy of the Hearth. It is the first thing a child learns and the last thing a dying person hears. In truth the Return is an industry:

- **Collectors** draw the Warm out of the dying with a cold iron tube and record the measure. To be collected is called *being banked*.
- **Bankers** press that measure into newborn vessels. A **whole return** keeps one person's warmth intact and a person returns as themselves, with memory. The rich buy whole returns. A **thin return** pools many measures together and divides them among many infants; what comes back is a stranger with a faint interest in a life it cannot name. The poor get thin returns. Their children are, quite literally, made of several dead people.
- **Assessors** decide what a life was worth and therefore how much of the measure may be kept back for the bank. The Court calls this *the deduction*. It is a tax on dying.
- **The Ash** is the grey silt that gathers where promises are unkept — in the corners of poor rooms, under beds, in the joints of the world. The poor know it and do not sweep it away on the last day of the year. The Court knows it too and bottles it as a medicine for the rich who want to forget a previous life. The Ash is not symbolic. It is what is left of a self when the Warm has been taken.

### The Warm Court

The institution that owns the Hearth, the banks and the Returns. It does not call itself a thief; it calls itself **the Keeping**. Its officers speak in the soft grammar of accounts: *The measure is banked. The account stands. Your return is recorded. Nothing is lost that we have kept.* Its violence is paperwork. It has no dark lord, no army of monsters, no single villain — only reasonable men and women who have decided that a world kept alive is worth any number of people spent to keep it.

### The ranks (provisional; to be fixed when the power system is built)

In-world names, not game words. **Whole** (a vessel that holds without leaking) → **Held** (can carry more than its natural measure) → **Drawn** (can shape and direct a flow of Warm) → **Banked** (can store Warm outside the body, in clay or iron) → **Hearthward** (can draw from the Hearth itself, and is therefore never cold) → **Kept** (a vessel the Court has made effectively unbreakable; the highest rank in Sarn, and the most terrible). Dan begins life as a cracked, low-capacity infant with no gift anyone can name. What he has instead is a *clinical ear*: he can hear a faulty vessel before he touches it, and he is almost always right. He will climb these ranks one costly discovery at a time, across thousands of chapters, and will never be more than a tired man with a good ear and a bad knee.

### The edge his old life gives him, and where it fails

He can intubate in blood, pack a cavity, reduce a shoulder, recognise hypothermia by the grimace that resembles a smile, know the exact moment a rewarming patient gets worse before better, and sort twenty injured people in ninety seconds. He knows how a body fails.

He cannot grow penicillin. He cannot anaesthetise with a weed. He cannot make a fire, track a beast, bargain for food, read a Sarnish ledger, or make a single native believe that a clamped vessel might survive. He is a clinician, not a physicist: his sense of heat is a surgeon's intuition, not a science. His contempt for his own comfort makes him accept cold that others pay for. He will be wrong, repeatedly, and each wrong answer will cost someone a hand, a memory or a life.

## Core themes

1. **Repair is not restoration.** A mended vessel carries its break. A repair that pretends the break never happened is a lie told in the language of mercy. The whole saga asks whether a person can be returned without being erased, and refuses the comfortable answer.
2. **The account outlives the debtor.** Unfinished business has weight in Sarn: it is the substance of the recall, the thing that makes a warmth too heavy to bank, the crack the Hearth cannot seal. What we leave unsaid is what returns us.
3. **The old life keeps its dead.** Reincarnation is no clean slate. Grief, guilt, skill, a surgeon's tired hands and a daughter's name cross over with the memory. Dan cannot begin again; he can only continue differently.
4. **Triage is the real power.** The one thing Dan can do that no priest of the Hearth can is look at a body and say *this one is gone; leave it.* In a world whose founding doctrine is that nothing is ever beyond keeping, this is heresy, and it is the instrument he was recalled to use. His gift is a moral one, and it is the most dangerous thing in the book.
5. **Mending is political.** Who gets banked, who gets a whole return, who gets grafted with clay taken from whom, and how many times a person may be spent: these are the central injustices of Sarn. The rich are beautiful and repeated. The poor are common and thin. Every institution calls this keeping.
6. **The cost of each repair is a piece of the self.** A whole return costs more than a poor district's yearly warmth. An overfilled vessel cooks its own memories. Those who have been banked five times walk around calling themselves whole. Identity here is erosion that has learned to introduce itself.
7. **Love is what the clamp cannot hold.** Some breaks cannot be repaired. The story insists on the difference between mending and pretending, between forgiving and forgetting, between a debt paid and a debt carried.
8. **A world is not worth saving at the price of everyone in it.** The Court says yes. The Unbanked say yes by a different route. Dan spends thousands of chapters deciding what his *no* actually means, and discovers that a no with no alternative is just another way of letting the world break.
9. **Attention is a form of love.** Dan's only genuine edge at the start is that he looks. He notices the nail that is a knot, the note in the chart, the crooked coffin, the ash in the corner. In a world of rote and rite, looking is the first radical act, and it is the one thing no institution can tax.

## The true nature of the reincarnation

*To be revealed in layers. The reader must be able to re-read chapter one and find the answers lying in plain sight.*

### Layer 1 — roughly chapters 1–150

Dan believes he has been reborn. The doctrine of the Return explains him: a man died and a baby woke. He is Corin now, a foundling in a clay-working household in Grath, with a branching red birthmark across his ribs and forearm that nobody will explain. He is an infant with forty years behind the eyes, unable to hold up his own head. The strangeness is, he assumes, in him. The Warm Court is the natural order of things. He learns its words before he can speak his own.

### Layer 2 — roughly chapters 150–650

The Return is a craft, not a miracle. Collectors, bankers, assessors, a slate, a deduction. The poor are pooled; the rich are kept whole. Corin had a **whole return**, paid for by someone who wrote his name before she had seen his face. He begins his experiments in secret — charcoal on slate, a dead bird's warm-ways, a clamp made from a broken buckle — and learns that his medical map is partly right. His first heretical discovery is small and enormous: **a dying person's Warm can be left where it is, and the world does not end.** He refuses a collection once, and nothing happens. That nothing is the crack in the whole structure.

### Layer 3 — roughly chapters 650–1,500

Sarn is itself a cracked vessel, and the Hearth is not a reservoir but a wound. The Court's entire economy is a salvage operation: because the Warm leaks into the Waste, it must be forced into new vessels faster than it escapes, and every life is conscripted as mortar in a repair nobody will admit is failing. The Ash is the dissolved selfhood of everyone whose warmth has been spent. Sarn is not the first world to be banked. The oldest repairs under the world are made neither of clay nor iron but of compressed, unkept promises — the Ash made structural. Dan's spiral warm-ways are a miniature map of that great crack, repeated in every vessel. He discovers this by dissecting a saint.

### Layer 4 — roughly chapters 1,500–2,800

The faction called **the Unbanked** — Marra, the collector at the expressway, and Rusk, the old man who taught him to read a vessel — intends a **Final Banking**: make every vessel perfectly retentive, so that nothing leaks, nothing is owed, and no one can ever give anything away. A sealed world is an unbreakable world, and a world of unbreakable vessels is a world of the walking dead. They recruited Dan because his particular coldness is the one tool the Court lacks. The public enemy and the secret friend want the same ending by different methods. Dan's answer has to become a third thing: a repair that leaves the crack visible and the Warm free to move.

### Layer 5 — deep into the thousands

There have been others like him. Across the chain of broken worlds, the Unbanked have recalled menders before: one made the repair beautiful and lied about it; one made it honest and let the world break; one became the Court. Dan is the latest, and the answer he must arrive at is not a better clamp. It is a different relationship to breakage: a vessel that admits its crack, a debt that is carried rather than settled, a warmth that can be given without being taxed. He will not break the world to save it, and he will not save it by emptying it. He will make a third thing. The saga does not end there; a made thing has to be lived in.

### Why him, specifically

He died **giving**: at the moment his vessel broke he poured his warmth into a child under a dashboard, in the snow. In Sarn's physics this makes a **hot trace** — warmth that will not cool into the Hearth because it is tangled with an unpaid promise. The Unbanked have spent centuries trying to manufacture one. They found his by accident and paid, dearly, for a whole return with memory intact. They wanted a man from a world that has no Hearth, who could look at a dying body and decide without prayer. His grief is the equipment they sent for. His birthmark is the fire writing itself into the trace. His daughter's unsent call is the shape of the debt that made him portable.

## Deep mysteries, with their hidden answers

Each is planted early in a physical detail; each is paid late, in a scene where someone has to act on the answer.

**1. The branching red mark on the infant's ribs.**
*Plant:* Chapter 1; the midwife crosses herself and says it is a birth-flower. *Hidden answer:* it is the map of the fire on Dan's own body, carried in the hot trace into the new vessel. When he finally sees the same branching pattern on an old man's forearm, he knows the Unbanked have done this before and that he is not the first.

**2. The woman at the edge of the flare light.**
*Plant:* The death scene: a grey coat, a slate, writing that does not stop. *Hidden answer:* Marra, a collector of the Warm Court. She did not cause the crash and did not touch the child. She saw an unbankable trace and wrote its measure down. She paid for his return and has been paying for it ever since with her own fingers, which are going grey.

**3. Why his return was whole.**
*Plant:* He remembers a life no thin return could hold; the Court's records show a deduction of zero. *Hidden answer:* Marra forged the assessment. A whole return is the most expensive single purchase in Sarn, and the money came from the Warm meant for forty poor children in a district called Low Kiln. Those children are a debt he will carry for a thousand chapters.

**4. The phone.**
*Plant:* The glow under a sheet; a name he will not look at; every important death in Sarn arriving with something unsaid and his hand reaching for a pocket with no phone in it. *Hidden answer:* the call was to Nina, promising the boat on Lake Michigan, a promise nine years old. The exact words of that unsent call are not in Sarn. What crossed was their *shape*: a debt heavy enough at the moment of breaking to make a man unbankable. The phone never crosses. The debt does.

**5. Why the Warm runs in spirals.**
*Plant:* His first dissection: a dead bird, the ways coiling instead of branching; he assumes he has made an error and repeats it. *Hidden answer:* every vessel is a small map of the world's wound. The spiral is the shape of the crack under Sarn. The discovery comes around chapter nine hundred, by dissecting a saint whose body is said to hold no flaw at all.

**6. The grey ash in the corners.**
*Plant:* The poor do not sweep it away on the last day; children are told it is dirt. *Hidden answer:* dissolved selves of the spent, the raw material of the oldest repairs and the Unbanked's Final Banking. It remembers because selfhood does not evaporate. The Court's "quiet ash" medicine is an addict's cure for a previous life, sold at a price no poor district can pay.

**7. The Afterdrop.**
*Plant:* Midwives call it the second chill: a rekindled child gets worse before getting better. Dan recognises it from rewarming hypothermia patients and is corrected by every midwife in the district. *Hidden answer:* two causes. The vessel walls settle after a poor graft — the ordinary answer. In a few cases the Warm tries to resume the shape of its former owner, which the Court calls **the Second Want** and suppresses in its records. Dan's own early fevers are a Second Want he cannot name, because the shape trying to return is Chicago.

**8. Rusk's allegiance.**
*Plant:* The old man who teaches the strange boy to listen to vessels; never asks where he came from; keeps a locked drawer. *Hidden answer:* Rusk is Unbanked, and the Final Banking is his life's work. He taught Dan to make him the instrument. He betrays Dan in the middle war, and, broken, helps him in the last. He is not a villain and not a friend; he is a man who has decided that a sealed world is worth one more spent life.

**9. The child in the minivan.**
*Plant:* The last thing Dan saw; a shape he never names in Sarn. *Hidden answer:* she lived, grew, and is still warm on Earth. The trace that brought Dan across is attached to her. He can go back — the mechanism is real and will be found — but going back means drawing the warmth out of the woman she became. The last account cannot be settled by dying again. It can only be carried.

**10. The story the temple walls tell.**
*Plant:* Temple walls show a world whole once and broken in a single moment of anger; the oldest repairs are laid over one another, and the oldest of all are not clay. *Hidden answer:* Sarn was never whole. It was made cracked, and the first Warm Court sold the world's heat to buy itself a thousand years of whole returns. The creation story is a founding invoice.

**11. The deduction.**
*Plant:* Every banked person is told the measure taken for the Hearth is a customary few percent. *Hidden answer:* the deduction is the exact difference between what the poor can be given and what the rich require. It is not a tax. It is the mechanism by which the poor are spent.

**12. The rank of Kept.**
*Plant:* The Court's highest vessels are spoken of with pity, as people who no longer feel the cold. *Hidden answer:* they no longer feel anything. Perfect retention is perfect numbness. The Final Banking is the condition of the Kept extended to every vessel in the world, and the priests have been calling it paradise for eight hundred years.

## The spine

A man with an unpaid call wants to get home. To get home he must understand why he was brought here. To understand that he must learn how the Warm works. To learn how it works he must become a vessel of a kind Sarn has never seen: a man who treats the sacred heat as anatomy, who experiments in secret, who discovers that the world's mercy is an account and its God is a bank.

Each arc tightens the noose and widens the question: childhood in a clay town under a paying stranger's eye; apprenticeship under Rusk; the first clamp, the first graft, the first refusal to collect; a district of thin returns; a school of assessors; a war fought over who may be banked; love with a woman who has been returned three times and remembers each death; betrayal by the teacher; the discovery that the Court and the Unbanked want the same ending; the building of a third thing that can be lived in. Beyond that, further worlds, further failed menders, and the slow, ordinary business of paying a debt that cannot be paid. The engine is not a quest to defeat a dark lord. It is **an unresolved account**, which is the only thing in Sarn that is heavier than a man.

Every discovery costs. Every gain narrows him a little. The saga runs on the tension between his clinical gift — the ability to decide a body is beyond help — and the growing knowledge that the world itself is the body he has been asked to decide on.

## Tonal palette

Wet wool, tallow, clay dust, river ice, the animal heat of a crowded kitchen, the sour tang of ash in a corner, old iron gone cold in the hand. The dryness of a hospital corridor at four in the morning translated into a clerk's soft voice: *The account stands.* Grief as one small physical mistake — a cup not put down, a staple set wrongly, a name omitted from a list. Tenderness as maintenance: a stone warmed against a belly, a graft taken from the wrong man, a hand held at the exact moment a fever breaks. Humour that costs something, usually the teller. The world is cruel and administration is crueler, but there is always bread, weather, work, argument, appetite, lust and the stubborn comedy of a body that refuses to die quietly.

Violence is brief, clumsy, physical and followed by a long undignified interval. There are monsters, but the most frightening thing in Sarn is a reasonable man with a ledger and a good reason. There is slavery, torture, war crime and the daily small theft of a poor district's warmth; all of it is shown plainly, with the victim's interior intact and the perpetrator's banality exposed. No sexual content involving anyone under eighteen, ever. Sexual violence may exist in the world as a fact of power and is never depicted graphically or erotically and never used as seasoning. Intimacy between adults is written as restraint: the unbuttoning, the shift of weight, the smell of tallow, the thing said quietly that could not be said loudly.

---

## What makes this story unlike any other

Magic here is not spellcasting; it is **accounting and anatomy**. Nobody chants a spell to make a fire — they adjust a vessel's pressure, and the cost is measured in heat lost, ash in the joints and memory burned away. The ranks are trades, not achievements. The hero's defining ability is not power but **triage**: the willingness to decide that a body is beyond saving, which in this world is both a medical act and a revolution. Reincarnation is not a mystery gift but an industry with a paperwork trail, a tax rate and a class system. The most important object in the saga is a missed phone call whose words never cross between worlds; only the debt does. The villains do not want to rule the world; they want to keep it alive, and they are almost right.

And the protagonist never becomes a god. He stays a large, tired man gone soft in the middle, with a click in one knee, a surgeon's hands now attached to a boy's small wrists, a dead daughter on another planet who is not dead at all, and a habit of counting the exits in every room he enters. The reader stays with him because he notices things, and because noticing is the only honest magic anyone in Sarn has.
