# Premise — private, spoilers throughout

## Logline

Daniel “Dan” Marchetti, a Chicago trauma surgeon who died in a blizzard fire, wakes as an infant in Sarn — a world where magic is the theft of warmth and the poor are literally cold — and must climb the Nine Keeps while uncovering why his life was stolen from him.

## The world in brief

Sarn is a cold interior world beneath a permanent rime called **the Hoar**. Its seasons are **the Still** (deep freeze, when the Hoar grips the ground like a fist) and **the Melt** (the short thaw, when bodies, roads and old crimes come up out of the peat). People live in turf-roofed villages along the **Rimewash**, huddled behind flues and banked stones, counting the coals that keep a room alive through the Still.

Magic here is **the Kept Art**. Its one law, which the Keepers half-know and the protagonist recognises outright, is that warmth cannot be made, only moved: every coal lit is a coal taken from somewhere, and every transfer loses a portion to the Hoar. Warmth pulled from a living body leaves that body cold, then still, then dead. The powerful are **the Kept**, ruddy and soft in their warm halls in the capital **Verran**; the masses are **the Spent**, blue-fingered, hollow-cheeked, dying of “the cold cough.” The Nine Keeps are the graded ranks of the Art: **the Shivering, the Warded, the Kindled, the Banked, the Drawn, the Cautered, the Hearthbound, the Rime-Spoken, the Unwintered.** Each is a year's-to-decades-long discipline, a cost paid in flesh and in the warmth of others. There is no spell list, no mana, no game ladder — only a craft with a body count.

## Core themes

1. **Warmth is theft.** Every good in this world — a heated room, a healed child, a victory — has been paid for by someone made colder. The saga asks whether any love is possible that does not spend another life.
2. **The body keeps the record.** Sarn writes class on skin: the flush of the Kept, the blue nails of the Spent, the blisters of a bad drawing, the last warmth leaving a face. Dan's forensic eye reads it all; his own body carries a lie — a burn-mark he did not get in this life.
3. **A mind is not a home.** Dan remembers a daughter, a city, a career, and cannot speak a word of it to anyone. Memory is both his only treasure and the thing that keeps him from belonging.
4. **What the Melt brings up.** Truth in Sarn is seasonal. The thaw uncovers bodies and buried roads; every mystery in the saga surfaces only when the ice gives way, years after it was planted.
5. **Systems deserve no trust.** Dan learned in Chicago that institutions absorb guilt and call it procedure. The Warm Court does the same with warmth. He is correct to distrust both — and his distrust becomes its own blindness.
6. **To save is to choose whom to spend.** Medicine and magic are the same dilemma at different scales: resources are finite, triage is real, and the "right" choice leaves someone freezing.
7. **The old life is not over.** His past life keeps surfacing — in dreams of the L train, in a lullaby, in the way he counts ribs — until the two lives turn out to have been the same crime seen from opposite ends.
8. **No ending, only a longer debt.** The saga never closes. Each arc pays one mystery and opens two.

## The true nature of the reincarnation

Revealed across hundreds of chapters, in pieces: the Hoar is not merely frozen ground. It is a membrane between worlds and the grave of every warmth ever spent. Sarn's own heat-source failed centuries ago in an event called **the Going-Out**; its civilisation has survived by reaching through the Hoar and drawing warmth from other worlds. Earth is one such source, harvested for decades.

On the night of Dan's death, Keeper **Ildren Casset** made a desperate, unauthorised draw through the Hoar into Chicago to gather enough concentrated warmth to save his dying son **Orin**. The draw took the heat of a man burning in a wreck on a snowbound expressway — Dan, dying with his own daughter's unanswered voicemail in his pocket. The theft should have taken only warmth. Instead Dan's consciousness travelled with it, spilling into the infant body of a dead peasant's child, **Wen**, in the village of Grath.

Dan is therefore not chosen, blessed or lucky. He is **leakage** — the first theft in Sarn's history to come back with a witness inside it. That is why he remembers, why his new body bears the shape of his death-burn as a birthmark, and why the Warm Court will eventually hunt him: he is proof of a crime they have committed for centuries, walking among them in a Spent child's skin.

## Deep mysteries — hidden answers

1. **Why did he remember when no one else does?** Past leaks have occurred, but the Court's **Stillness** officers find and “cool” them in infancy. Dan survived because Casset's draw was rushed and unrecorded. Hidden answer: he is not unique by nature, only by accident — and there are older leaks still alive, hidden in plain sight.
2. **Who was the infant whose body he took?** Hidden answer: Wen, the dead child of **Sella**, a Spent woman of Grath. Casset's servant placed Dan in Wen's cradle to hide the theft. Sella's love for him is real; so is a buried, shameful resentment she will not admit for twenty years.
3. **What is the birthmark?** Hidden answer: it is the exact branching shape of the burn Dan died with, reproduced on infant skin — proof that body and memory travelled together. Later, the same branching mark is found on Orin Casset, who received Dan's stolen warmth. Two bodies, one fire.
4. **What was the price of his survival?** Hidden answer: Wen's warmth was used as the "packing" for the transfer. Dan lives because another infant was made cold. The Melt of the year he turns nine uncovers Wen's unmarked grave.
5. **Was Dan's death an accident?** Hidden answer: it was not. Casset's draw through the Hoar caused the fuel fire on the expressway — a tug on the world Dan could not see. The pileup is a theft with sixteen victims, of which Dan is the one that came back.
6. **What is the Hoar, truly?** Hidden answer: it is the accumulated cold of drained worlds — a landfill of dead warmth that is slowly waking. Every world Sarn feeds on becomes another layer of it.
7. **Why is Sarn dying now?** Hidden answer: the Going-Out was not a natural event. The first Keepers caused it, fleeing a warmer world and burning it out to escape; the Nine Keeps are a cage they built to keep anyone from doing the same to Sarn.
8. **What happened to Nina Marchetti?** Hidden answer: Dan's daughter grew up, had a life, and died decades later on Earth. Her final warmth was among the last the Hoar ever took from that world, and her voice — preserved in a stray recording that crossed with her — becomes the basis of a heretical saint's cult in Sarn centuries after Dan's disappearance. He will meet a version of her without ever knowing it is her.
9. **Who is the“Unwintered”?** Hidden answer: not a person but a condition — one who carries their own warmth and owes nothing to the Hoar. The prophecy is a trap: achieving it means cutting every tie, including the ones that make a person human.
10. **What does Dan owe?** Hidden answer: the warmth in him is Wen's, Orin's, and sixteen strangers'. The saga's spine is not revenge but the slow discovery that he cannot return any of it without ceasing to be himself — and that he must try anyway.

## The saga's spine

A stolen man grows up in the cold. From a Spent cradle in Grath he learns the Kept Art by hydrolysis and hard knocks — half from Halven Rusk, a disgraced lower Keeper, half from his own fatal habit of testing everything — while his modern knowledge saves a few lives and kills others through overconfidence. Each arc raises his keep, costs him someone, and opens a question. He will leave the Rimewash for Verran, be schooled and used by the Warm Court, love and betray and be betrayed, lose the people who made the cold bearable, learn the truth of his arrival in fragments, and at last stand before the choice at the heart of the world: keep the stolen warmth and become the thing he hates, or spend it and disappear. Thousands of chapters. No final answer, no ending — the story stops only when the telling does.

## Tonal palette

Cold, plain, granular. Domestic warmth set against public cruelty. The interior of an old soul in a small, hungry body; the adult's dry exactitude interrupted by an infant's bladder and a boy's fists. Violence is brief, clumsy, consequential, and its consequences outlast the scene. Grief is a cup not lifted; tenderness is a stone warmed against a belly; humour is the joke a freezing man makes because the alternative is to weep. The past life breaks in as present-tense flashes of sodium light, hospital corridors, phone glow, motorway rain — the only modern comparisons permitted, and permitted because they are the wound.

## What makes this unlike any other

- Magic is literal, rule-bound thermodynamics with a moral victim in every transaction — and the hero's advantage is that he *recognises* the rule, not that he can break it.
- The protagonist's power is medicine: he treats the casualties of magic rather than casting it. His scalpel, his eye for a corpse and his knowledge of shock are real edges with hard ceilings.
- Reincarnation is a theft, not a gift: he was stolen to save someone else, and the theft killed a child who is the true owner of his body.
- The old life is written on the new body as a birthmark shaped like a death-burn; his past will not stay past.
- A class system expressed in body temperature, a world whose seasons are Still and Melt, and a power ladder whose ranks are named for the stages of a kept fire.
- No ring-companion, no hidden true magic, no academy named for a beast, no game vocabulary, no cheat code, and never an ending.
