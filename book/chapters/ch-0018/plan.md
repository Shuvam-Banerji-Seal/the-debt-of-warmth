## Scene 1 — The Last of What the Water Left

**Day and time.** Day 85, Silt 26, Sixthday, before light through dusk.
**Place.** The Clay Yard — the yard well and the drying racks — then the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; yard women and a bucket-round of walk-ons. Jorra, Alda, Mira, Berr and Ulda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the last image of Chapter 17 without recap: the mud has set into a brown skin over the ruts, faithful to a thing gone, holding a boot's print and giving it back slowly. No "the lean week had ended" sentence.
2. Sella gives the broom its instruction before she gives it to anybody — go around what the water left, it cost enough to come in and will cost more to take out — and goes wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner, the new finer grey and the space under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie. She sweeps none of them.
3. The yard counts its losses in loaves pushed along a shelf, glances at the clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody calls a figure. A woman asks for a number and is told to ask the pot what a wet thing costs. The boy, carried at the edge of the work, keeps eighty-five exactly against it.
4. At the bucket-round a woman calls the shut counting room "that story over the bucket" — no third telling, just the second one already in the yard. The boy keeps the two tellings on their own tray beside the told bell, the heard bell, the early Sill note and the kiln's gap, holding the likeness (both say *not the war's hand*) and the difference (breath through the nose against breath in the throat) without letting the two touch and without joining either to any kiln.
5. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold at his ear with the empty place beside it unnamed; no third came through the week or comes now. He wants the crust, the breast and the bucket's rim two inches beyond his hand and can reach none of them. The thread of drool freezes at his mouth and nobody wipes it; his head goes where it wants; cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from his wrist; his hand goes up an inch toward the rim and comes down. He is the only adult at this well and the least use in it.
6. Tarn's knife is on its sill; his broom goes over the collector's road without turning. Below the sill a child's cold foot is still the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard; the boy's surgical reading works and buys nothing.
7. At the turn of the wind Sella lifts the basket level, carries him up at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and sets him on his bed under the roof, not in the Debt Loft. She puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen.

**Emotional turn.** From the yard's careful avoidance of what the water left to the boy's exact, useless keeping of it: a quiet settling that nothing is owed to anyone by the counting, and that keeping is the only work available to him. Forward, not rest.

**Sensory anchors.** Brown skin of mud holding a boot print; wet wool, woodsmoke, tallow; the cold iron rim two inches off; broom bristles going around; the stiff, smell-less crust; a frozen thread of drool; the river's muttered note nearer at the going of the water; the fire's missing fifth breath coming through the floor.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (85 exact against bad arithmetic), T086/T060 (crust and unnamed empty place), T095/T065 (two tellings held apart on their own tray), T009/T013/T092 (two lines unswept, thaw), T031 (space under the rack), T058/T079 (instructions to fire, wool, broom, bucket), T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer), T093 (cold foot carried uncured).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The month about to turn on mud; the kept tray that grows no cause; the watcher held as a shape for the last market of Silt; the count that will cross into Seed and keep going.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with one stiff crust in the fold and the empty place still unnamed; the door a finger's width open with the river's note through the gap and the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor in two separate places; his hand outside the wool up an inch toward the water and down; the count going into Silt 27 unspent.

## Scene 2 — Six Counted

**Day and time.** Day 86, Silt 27, Seventhday, before light through the day.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the Vail wheel-shed and its damper, the floor under the Vail roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Tarn, Jorra Vail, Sella Vail; yard voices arguing the thaw. The two back-room men are present only as the dust Jorra brings out and the low talk already ended.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before the light Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the empty yard where it is, six years standing in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, the seventh nearly gone and still held — the one entry in this house the house cannot check. To the kindling: *Six counted. The next I don't count.*
2. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back.
3. The yard argues an early thaw against a late. Tarn says *Yes, house* to both, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the flood.
4. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector comes.
5. Jorra comes through the finger's-width-open wheel-shed door with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not bone-coloured slip. Men came in since first light and spoke low; he gives the house no name for them. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around and entered by nobody.
6. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the damper ahead of her sentence, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence.
7. He does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed or the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn. The boy keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
8. A vessel gives its note past the door. Tarn's head does not turn and he does not name the sound; the boy does not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences keep their equality under one roof and the loan that can hear stays uncalled.

**Emotional turn.** The seventh year pressing against a breath the man has decided not to spend: something must give at the end of it, and it does not give here. The house straightens around Jorra's silence rather than around any word.

**Sensory anchors.** The small knife on its sill and the worn thumb-mark; Ash in knuckles the colour of the dark line; the pale circle of the clip-ring; grey dust past a bent little finger; cold iron of the damper; the wheel's low note; a pot's full note known and unsaid.

**Threads advanced.** T036 (six counted, seventh held — **do not pay**), T076 (two silences), T009 (broom over the collector's road), T090 (clerk still set, by report), T061/T082 (note past the door, no seventh), T035/T031 (dust, broken slates), T057/T084 (name ungiven), T058/T079, T071/T072 (86).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The end of the seventh year and what must give; the unnameable back room and the birth-slates under the rack; a loan that can hear and will one day be called; Jorra's promise to the men who burn slates, still ahead.

**Exact last image.** The small knife back on its sill where it can be checked and the seventh year still held where it cannot; the boy on his bed under the roof with one crust and the empty place; the carried fire at four, four and a gap under blind ash.

## Scene 3 — The Tin and the Turned Slate

**Day and time.** Day 87, Silt 28, Eighthday, midday three doors down and dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** House Silt, just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light, away from every place a slate might lie; then the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Alda Reed, the neighbour scrubbing in the back room (walk-on). Dessa is dead and stays dead, offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every open slate, opens no slate and turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row. She stands a half-pace off; her little-door burn shuts on nothing.
2. From the basket's low angle the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed keeps its pale fold at the seam and thumb-bright rub on the lid, unopened. The fold of grey wool below holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone once lay. Nobody says either is gone.
3. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay still under them. She lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold flat at his shoulder a breath too long, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower before she knows it and covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa — a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of.
4. To Sella's *he lies quiet* she says *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down*, exact and not true, and goes on with dates after there is no question left to answer, because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry. Name, number and third day stay unsaid.
5. Her hand goes once to the little tin inside her bodice and comes away without opening it. Her sixth narrow slate stays turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name — written on Day 64, six pages, all unread, no seventh written and T023 not paid again.
6. Two private records in two materials stand apart in one room, read by nobody and carried to no door; the boy sets them on separate places and writes no cause across them. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the chipped cup and comes down.
7. At dusk Sella carries him home at the half-pace, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; the river's note and the fire's gap come through in two places.

**Emotional turn.** The mercy-lie curdling further against a death nobody alive can correct, held against the small, exact tenderness of a fold pressed too long and a tin touched but not opened: kindness that has written down what it will not say.

**Sensory anchors.** Soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt; lye sour under the soap; clay under scrubbed nails; the pale fold on pewter and thumb-bright rub; the hollow in grey wool; grey wool shawl; a narrow slate turned face-down; exact dates arriving after the question stops.

**Threads advanced.** T023 (carried, **not** repaid again), T089/T074 (tin and hollow), T007/T008/T073 (unaware sign, date, mercy-lie), T081 (a fourth likeness of room), T058/T079 (instructions to fire and wool, mark untouched), T086, T071/T072 (87). Per `plan/next.md` the woman is "forty-two mornings dead" at this point in the count, but **do not speak her number or her name to the boy**.

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The tin opened years after Alda is gone; the convergence of tin, turned slate, cords and knot-string into one record; no recognition now and no cause written across the two materials.

**Exact last image.** Under the Vail roof with one crust stiff in the fold and the empty place unnamed; three doors away the pewter tin and the turned slate still shut and unread; the river's note through the door and the fire's gap through the floor in two places; the count unspent.

## Scene 4 — Neither Knot Counted

**Day and time.** Day 88, Silt 29, Ninthday, dawn market through afternoon; dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge and the edge of the fish-trade; the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Mira Silt; a weir woman with salt white at her wrists, an eel buyer, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (all walk-ons). Orra Weir is present only as her board lying beside Mira's and not touching it.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the fish-trade shade where no slate lies, keeps her half-pace from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the water, never to the basket.
2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers the question and her own: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away when the deciding is done.
3. When a buyer names a figure, her thumb goes under her apron to the grey wool cord and stays there while she decides. Shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots and never lifted at a door; neither knot is counted by any thumb but her own and the knot for the thin child upriver is untouched.
4. Her board lies beside Orra Weir's knot-string and does not touch it. Orra stays offstage, watching still, and will get no answer from this bed.
5. The steps below the bridge have come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk still sits at the landing with his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; he is filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector and the Court-table clerk, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand comes.
6. The boy sets Mira's not-looking — a keeping — beside Berr's not-looking — a pricing — in places that do not touch, and has no word in Grathic for either.
7. At dusk home, fold right without a look at his face, thread left frozen; the count goes into the last day of Silt unspent.

**Emotional turn.** A secret kept out of love going on gathering interest in a record no Court can confiscate, carried out in brisk, ordinary work; against it the market's small administrative cruelty — a clerk posted for a measure nobody called, a market walking over a bad step without making a fall of it.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried pale on sleeves; salted perch and river silt; the missing top of an ear; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; grey wool cord and the second cord twisted twice beneath it; an out-of-line landing; a cuff gone grey from washing; a slate held to a forearm.

**Threads advanced.** T059/T025/T024 (both cords, uncounted knots, board beside Orra's without touching), T090/T080 (clerk still set, record-first hands kept apart), T083 (out-of-line step), T058/T079, T071/T072 (88).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The knot for the thin child upriver left untouched; the convergence of private accounts read together years hence; the clerk posted ahead of a death; no door knocked in Nine Pots.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with one crust and the unnamed empty place; three doors down the two cords lying uncounted under an apron and a board beside a knot-string not touching it; at the Fishstairs the soft-cuffed clerk's hand still going to his slate before his eyes go to the stalls; the count crossing into Silt 30 unspent.

## Scene 5 — The Last Market of Silt

**Day and time.** Day 89, Silt 30, Tenthday — Account-day and the last market of Silt — morning to afternoon; dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Cross, in the moot-post's finger's width of shade and at the block; the Fishstairs landing; the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), Ulda (second glimpse, silent and unnamed in the narration); the crowd, the soft-cuffed clerk and market voices as walk-ons. Mira may be glimpsed at the eel boards; Orra offstage with her knot-string; Fenna still unnamed, a green at a corner of a mouth filed in silence.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with his cheek to the cold iron and does not look into it; she keeps the half-pace from every open slate.
2. Berr comes to the block with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, smelling of hot clay and nothing under it. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, reads one in twelve twice over the basket, passes a finger over the figure, says *the levy is called; it is not called by me*, and goes off toward High Kiln with the box locked in his house and the true deduction inside, believing he has done a kindness.
3. The true subtraction goes over the basket unspoken while the printed figure stands unchallenged; a figure read aloud is not the figure paid. The number for the thaw goes from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and reaches nobody.
4. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the boy keeps the heard bell and the told bell of the Long Night in two places that do not touch.
5. Down at the Fishstairs a whole market walks carefully over the out-of-line landing without making a fall of it, and the soft-cuffed clerk is still set with his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls.
6. At the market's edge Ulda is glimpsed a second time, unnamed in the narration: cast-offs worn like a uniform, a gap where a front tooth had been, a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string at his throat. When a pot is set down his head goes toward it a half-beat after the boy's — after the knuckle and after the note — and then stops; he rubs the sliver with his thumb and cannot say why he turned. No sentence is finished for a kiln-hand, no loaf passes, no slate is seen, no secret sold. The boy files the delay on a place of its own beside his own turning without letting the two touch, because a thing that comes after the knock is a different object from one that comes before it. The sale stays a shape and the betrayal stays years ahead.
7. A green at a corner of a mouth across the market is seen and filed in silence, unnamed, years before the ear it belongs to is spoken; do not name her or give her dialogue.
8. At dusk Sella carries him up at the half-pace, sets him on his bed under the roof, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen; the river's note and the fire's gap come through in two places. The last market of Silt closes and the count crosses into Seed unspent.

**Emotional turn.** The district renews its arithmetic over a thinner crowd on the month's closing market-day, untouched by the boy's exact count; underneath, the watcher's half-beat is held as a physical detail with no cause written across it — affectionate, ordinary, and not yet a betrayal.

**Sensory anchors.** Cold iron of the moot-post at his cheek; red wool cord folded under a coat; green glaze at a mouth; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; the printed figure and the finger passed over it; a bell at four, four and a gap with nobody crossing; an out-of-line landing; a cuff grey from washing; a green sliver rubbed with a small thumb; a gap in a grin.

**Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047 (Berr's figure, self-removal, box), T077/T072 (figure read aloud not figure paid; number arrives nowhere), T078 (heard and told bell kept apart), T090/T080 (clerk still set, record-first hands apart), T033/T034 (Ulda's second glimpse, half-beat, no sale), T050/T051 (green at the mouth filed in silence), T071/T072 (89).

## Scene 6 — Seed

**Day and time.** Day 90, Seed 1, Firstday, before light through dusk.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the drying racks and the yard well; the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; yard voices and a bucket-round of walk-ons. Jorra may be heard at the wheel-shed and not seen.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The month named for planting opens on mud. The yard goes around what the water left with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken; the first work of Seed is sweeping, not planting. The planting strips belong to the next chapter.
2. In the Vail corner the new grey is still finer than the old clean dark line, still unswept and still called the thaw that would go when it goes. The carried fire under blind ash and the back of a broken pot keeps four, four and a gap, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; its payer is still unnamed and the coal the brine-gull took weeks back is entered in no slate and missed in none.
3. The house moves its loaves along the shelf, glances at the clay jar and leaves the space at the row's end that nobody calls a number. The boy, ninety days old and three months, carried everywhere, keeps the count exactly against it across the turn of the month; he wants the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted and nobody speaks it.
4. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it carried into Seed without a name, and no third came. The body humiliates him exactly: the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, the head going where it wants and making the small wet sound, cold arriving cheek, temple, wrist with the wool riding back from the wrist, and the hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water going over water and coming down. Wants the crust, the breast, the bucket two inches off; can reach none. Reducing a shoulder was ordinary work in the life that is not this one; keeping his own hand on a lip of cold iron is not, and the joke has never once been funny enough to say aloud.
5. The private file is checked and unchanged: six hearings beside and never among, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, three intervals on another tray, the word forty unspoken with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both, four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth, the two tellings of the shut room on their own tray joined to no kiln, the loan that can hear uncalled, and the cold foot below the sill still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured and unnamed.
6. Sella tells the fire it is the thaw; touches the widow's mark at no hour, because no opening came in six days and a month's turning is not an opening. At dusk she sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen. The door stands a finger's width open; the river's low note from the Ilm comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor — the two sounds in two separate places as they have been for ninety days.

**Emotional turn.** A month turns and nothing in it is settled: the count crosses the boundary that nobody will mark, and the keeping goes on unchanged into the planting year — forward momentum rather than arrival.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud set into a brown skin; wet wool and tallow; broom bristles going around; loaves moved along a shelf; the stiff, smell-less crust; a frozen thread of drool; the cold order of cheek, temple, wrist; the river's muttered note nearer at the going of the water; the fire's missing fifth breath; a hand going up an inch and down.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (90 exact across the month's turn), T086/T060 (crust and unnamed empty place carried into Seed), T092/T013 (two lines unswept, called thaw), T061/T082 (six hearings, three near-full answers, no seventh), T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer), T093/T094 (cold foot still uncured, unnamed), T065/T095 (two tellings on their own tray, joined to no kiln), T009 (left things still kept), T058/T079 (fold put right without a look at the face).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The planting strips east of Low Kiln and the first planting week; the count that will go on into a hundred days; the two unswept lines and the two unnamed absences carried further; the cold foot carried toward the silt-leach of Arc 3; the watcher's half-beat held for its later landing.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof at dusk on Seed 1 with one brown-edged crust stiff in the fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed; the new finer grey unswept beside the old clean dark line; the river's note through the finger's-width-open door and the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor kept in two places; his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the water and coming down; the count at ninety, unspent, with nothing joined, paid, explained or ended.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 19

The last image of Chapter 18 is a hand going up an inch toward the note of water going over water and coming down, with the count at ninety unspent and Seed 1 closing over mud. Chapter 19 opens on Day 91, Seed 2, Secondday, on the mud the last flood left: the planting strips east of Low Kiln are being worked with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken, and the boy, ninety-one days old and carried at the edge, keeps the count exactly while the house keeps it badly. The new grey lies unswept in the Vail corner, the one crust stays stiff in the fold with the empty place unnamed, the six hearings stay six, the three near-full answers stay advanced and not paid, the two tellings stay on their tray, and the loan that can hear stays uncalled. Nothing in the turning into Seed has settled anything; the planting week begins under the same arithmetic, and the count goes on. Forward, not closed.
