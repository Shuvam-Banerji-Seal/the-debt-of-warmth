\daymark{85}{Silt 26, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he mud} had set into a brown skin over the ruts of \place{the Clay Yard}, faithful to a thing gone, holding the print of a boot and giving it back slowly. It had taken the boot to the ankle four mornings back and let go of it in its own time, and now it kept the shape with the obstinacy of a thing that has been stood in and will not say by whom. The skin went dull where the sun found it and kept its darkness in the hollows, and the yard, which had spent a week walking around what the water left, went around it still with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken. There was a print at the rack-end where a man had stood to look at the water and then thought better of going down to it, and another at the well where a bucket had been set and lifted and set again. The mud kept both. It gave nothing back but the shape. Wet wool, woodsmoke and tallow went over it on the wind, and under those the sour mineral tang of a ridge nobody swept.

\person{Sella Vail} had given the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Go around,” she said to the bristles, and then, because a broom is a slow listener, she said it again to the yard: “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and the house had said it through her for six years. Before she had come down she had told the fire it was the thaw and told it to be fed, and the fire had answered with four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash and the back of a broken pot. She went wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its own corner and stopped, and wider still around the new hairline of grey that had gathered beside it, finer than the old, which the house called the thaw and said would go when it goes. She swept neither. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} was the space where whole slate should have stood and where broken ones lay now, gone around by three sweepings and entered by nobody all week; she went around that too, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and brought the broom back to the edge of the print without touching it.

The boy came down at the edge of the work in a basket that was not his, carried level as a pot filled. \person{Corin} was eighty-five days old and had been carried everywhere since the day he was born, and the age sat in him with the exactness of a thing counted at night and not entered anywhere. She kept a half-pace from every open slate and set the basket at the edge of the light where no slate lay, and went wide around a slate propped at the rack end as though it were a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. A basket set down is a pot set down and left; that was the rule of the house and he had learned to lie under it without expecting to be asked. She gave the instruction to the wool and the wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. The wool smelled of woodsmoke and tallow, and under those the ridge's old sourness, the same smell that had come up from the floor of every room he had ever lain in.

The yard counted what the water had taken as it had counted it all week, in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at the clay jar on the wall, and in the space at the row's end that nobody called a number. “Nine went in where eleven stood,” a rack-woman said to the shelf, not to Sella. “That's two for the levies and one for the pot that was broken.”

“Ask the pot,” Sella said to the shelf. “It'll tell you what a wet thing costs set down there.”

The rack-woman put her thumb to the row's end where the gap was and took it away again without putting a figure to it, and went back to her sleeves. The boy, carried at the edge of it all, kept eighty-five exactly against the loaves and the glances and the unnamed space, and set the number down nowhere, because there was no slate in this yard that would take it and no hand among them that would have known what it counted.

\scenebreak

The bucket-round at \place{the yard well} had begun before the light had decided what it wanted of the day, and the rope came up stiff, keeping the shape of the hand that had hauled it. A skin lay on the water where the bucket broke it, thin as the skin on milk gone cold, and the women spoke of the skin to the bucket and not to the child in the basket. Near the tubs a woman said it was a pity about that room shut up in the war, and another said that story over the bucket had a date in it and so was nobody's business, and the yard let it lie where it lay. There was no third telling. The second man from \place{Greybank} had brought his story to this same well yesterday and gone back down the road with his mud on him and no name given, and the yard had taken it as a curiosity with a date, as it had taken the first months before; one telling sat in the boy's keeping from Ilmen and the other had come to sit beside it, and neither had grown a third.

The boy kept the two tellings on a tray of their own, beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap in the dark; the bell heard at \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the early note out of the stone at \place{the Singing Sill}; and \lore{the kiln's breath} under blind ash, four low pulls and a missing fifth warming the boards by degrees. Four low things in four places. The river's own note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. On the tray of the two tellings he kept the likeness and the difference without letting either become the other: both men had said the words \emph{not the war's hand}, and the first had made the small sound of iron shut on iron in his throat while the second drew his breath in through his nose against his teeth. A likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not a hand puts one there, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can make of two tellings of the same thing; so he kept them two, and wrote no cause across either, and joined neither to the chimney across the yard standing with its breath held under blind ash.

He knew the room before either man had finished describing it, having lain awake under this roof and counted its breath in the dark, four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been. A vessel with no way to feed what it holds cooks its own heat out of the air; the face arranges itself in the ordinary way at the end, and the bill is paid by the bodies inside. He had once been the man who wrote down how such a room would finish, in a calm voice, and gone on to the next body. Here he had the anatomy and no word for it in the language of the yard, and no hand to open a door if there had been one. The telling had come twice into this well and had stayed a story with a date both times, and the chimney across the yard stood with its breath held and was joined to neither telling by anyone who had spoken. He did not join them either.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six — five from four sources and the sixth out of a person who had been warm that morning — beside and never among, and no seventh came to the well. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on a tray of their own, neither hearings nor the third thing, and the word forty was on a place of its own with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and it was not spoken in this yard on this morning. He had no hand to tap the pots and ask them to say it again, and no \lore{Grathic} word with which to ask.

\scenebreak

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold against his ear, and the empty place beside it had crossed another night without acquiring a name. No third came. The crust was gone past the smell of barley and had never once been a sermon, and it lay where a hand had put it years ago and nobody had moved it since. He wanted it. He wanted the breast and the cold iron of the bucket's rim two inches beyond his hand, and the three wants sat in him together with the strict equality of three tools laid out on a tray. He could reach none. A thread of drool had frozen at the corner of his mouth in the small hours and no hand came to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and the surgeon's register in him noted the sound and its causes and did not need to hear it. Cold arrived in its known order: cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the wrist so the wind found the bare place. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the rim and came down. There had been a life in which reducing a shoulder was work done before breakfast and a man put his hand where he wanted it; here he was the only adult at this well and the least use in it, and the joke had never once been funny enough to say aloud.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill before the light and told the empty yard where it was, so that it could be checked. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb and the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and no collector came. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the woman who wanted the thaw early and to the man who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts all week. Below the sill a child had come out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold, and a woman had wrapped it twice in wool and pressed it between her palms and called it the cold, while the yard called the water weather; the two facts went past each other again this morning and never met. The boy read the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no hand and no standing to put on the shin. He knew the shape of it as he knew the inside of a room that had cooled from the edges inward while everybody was kind. The reading worked perfectly and bought nothing.

\scenebreak

At the turn of the wind Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up the Clay Yard at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered by nobody. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. She left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. She touched \lore{the widow's mark} at no hour of this day, because a well is not an opening and a basket is not a door. The door stood a finger's width open, and through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire's four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds kept in two separate places as they had been for eighty-five days. The clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the new finer grey lay beside it unswept, and the boards above kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. The payer of the coal had gone unnamed all this time. Nobody had said who kept the fire giving, and the district had one word for the water and one past tense for the thaw and went home warm on both.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Nothing had been joined, paid or explained. The two tellings lay on their tray and the six hearings stayed six beside them and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was not spoken and no collector came. The boy was eighty-five days old and nobody had told him so. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count crossed into Silt 27 unspent.
