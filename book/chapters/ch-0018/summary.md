## Chronicle

Silt went out on the mud it had come in on. The yard swept around what the water left and entered none of it --- the brown skin holding a boot's print, the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the space under the rack. Sella Vail carried the boy at the half-pace from every open slate to his bed. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and a place nobody would call a number; the boy kept his count exactly and could spend none.

Tarn set his knife on its sill and told the empty yard where it was, six years worn flat by his thumb, the seventh nearly gone and still held. *Six counted. The next I don't count*, he told the kindling, wanting the late thaw because it puts off the paying; his broom went over the collector's road without turning. Jorra Vail came in with grey slate dust past his bent finger, told a pot it would do and moved the damper ahead of Sella's sentence; he did not look toward the bed.

Three doors down the pewter tin above the empty bed kept its pale fold. Alda Reed laid her birth-slates on the bench before she turned to the fold, pressed the wool a breath too long, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower before she knew it and covered the sign with the date of a woman more mornings dead than any living person kept count of. *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down*, she said, exact and not true. Her hand went once to the tin in her bodice without opening it; her sixth slate lay face-down.

At the Fishstairs Mira Silt answered a question with her own --- *It comes when it comes. Ask the river*. When a buyer named a figure her thumb went to the grey cord, showing the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted. The steps came back four, four and a landing out of line that a market walked carefully over. On the last market Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve and said, *The levy is called. It is not called by me*; the true subtraction went unspoken and the number for the thaw arrived nowhere. A foundling turned his head toward a pot a half-beat after the boy's and could not say why.

Seed opened on mud and the first work of it was sweeping. The carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash; one crust lay stiff in the fold and the empty place beside it crossed into Seed without a name. At dusk Sella put the fold right and left the thread at his mouth frozen, the river's note and the fire's gap in two places as for ninety days. He was ninety days old and nobody had told him so. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Dusk of Day 90, Seed 1, under the Vail kiln-house roof. Sella has lifted the boy level, carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, gone around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed (not the Debt Loft). She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note from the Ilm comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot --- two sounds in two separate places as for ninety days and as they will come tomorrow. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; no third came. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water going over water and comes down. The count is ninety, unspent. Present: Corin only (Sella has set him down and withdrawn). Nothing joined, paid, explained or ended.
- **Day and time at the end.** Day 90, Seed 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813, at the going of the light (dusk). Chapter covered Days 85--90 (Silt 26--30, then Seed 1), six lived days with one `\daymark` each, at the seven-day cap for a passage chapter. No day skipped, poured into another or summarised.
- **Where everyone is and their condition.** Corin/Dan: on his bed under the roof, ninety days old (three months), carried everywhere during the day to the Clay Yard, the well, the racks, House Silt, the Fishstairs, the Cross and the moot-post; awake, silent, drool frozen, hand outside the wool going up an inch and down; cannot roll, stand, speak, read Grathic, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. Sella Vail: at the fire, the broom and the bed, having given every instruction to the wool, fire, bucket and broom and never to the basket, said *the house*, gone around the two lines and the space under the rack, told the fire it was the thaw, and touched the widow's mark at no hour in six days because no opening came. Tarn: in the yard and under the roof, his small knife back on its sill with six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held; Ash in the creases of his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his left wrist, broom over the collector's road without turning, head not turning at the vessel's note on Days 86 and 90. Jorra Vail: in the wheel-shed and offstage; grey slate dust past his bent little finger on Day 86, spoke to the wheel, moved the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, did not look toward the bed; heard at the wheel on Day 90 (a word to a pot, *You'll do*) but not seen. Alda Reed: came short three doors down on Day 87, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay under them, mercy-lie curdled further against a death nobody can correct. Mira Silt: at the eels below the north bridge on Days 88 and 89, cords uncounted, board beside Orra's not touching. Berr Clay: at the block on Account-day (Day 89), box locked, true deduction inside, gone off toward High Kiln believing he had done a kindness. Ulda: glimpsed a second time at the market's edge on Day 89, unnamed in the narration, no sale, no slate. Orra, Fenna, Marra, Dessa (dead), Rusk and the wider-world figures all offstage.
- **Items, money, powers.** Nothing gained that he can use; no money. Held: the one spent brown-edged crust, stiff and smell-less, with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third; the smoky crust gone since Day 46, the pale river stone gone from House Silt, the weak board gone under silt, the broken birth-slates under the rack and the coal gone with the gull on Day 45 (all subtractions entered in no account). Unchanged: the small bench knife on its sill, the charred slate in the kiln ash, the basket and wool lent by Sella, the clay jar, Garl's coat and hollow seat, the uncalled debt against the firing of tiles, the clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept. Alda's little tin in her bodice (touched Day 87, unopened); her sixth narrow slate turned face-down, six pages unread, **no seventh written and T023 not paid again**. The pewter tin above the empty bed unopened; Berr's box locked; Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string with knots uncounted. Powers: none usable --- no rank, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no recognition; counting is an old-life habit, not a gift, and the surgeon's register keeps working perfectly and buying nothing. Cost: each day the body humiliates him exactly (frozen drool, wandering head, cold in the order cheek--temple--wrist with the wool riding back, the hand up an inch and down), and the reading of the cold foot below the sill is worth nothing.
- **Facts, lies, promises, debts.** Facts carried: a month turned from Silt to Seed on mud and nothing in it was settled; the boy is ninety days old and nobody has told him so. Lies: Alda's *warm and breathing* remains exact and not true over a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of, and her date stands over the sign where no living person can take it off. Self-removals: Berr's *the levy is called; it is not called by me*, Sella's *the house*. Promises held, not paid: Jorra's promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn and *he's not Vail* stays sealed; Tarn's seventh year is nearly gone and still held; Sella's private roll unshown; Garl's debt neither paid nor settled. Debts: the carried fire's payer unnamed for ninety days; the loaf from the man from Slate Row gathers interest in an account the boy cannot read. No new debt incurred and none paid this chapter.
