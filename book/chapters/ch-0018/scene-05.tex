\daymark{89}{Silt 30, Y.H. 813}

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket where \place{the moot-post} gave its finger's width of shade, with the cold iron against his cheek and the rest of him in the thin light of \place{the Cross}. She set it level and let go only when the whole weight had committed itself to the ground, the way she sets a pot down, and then she stood a half-pace off from it. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it was the river wind, and the river wind had got under the square in the night. The little-door burn across the back of her hand shut on nothing. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and the house had carried him down at the half-pace from every open slate and had not once turned the walk toward \place{Slate Row} or a door.

It was \lore{Account-day}, the thirtieth of the tend and the last market of Silt. \person{Corin} was eighty-nine days old, and the number sat in him with the exactness of a thing set down at night and entered nowhere. The square had four ways out, as it had on every Account-day since he could hold a thought still enough to count them, and the crowd stood thinner than at the flood's reading: gaps in the turnip rows, gaps in the shoulders along the trough, the place where the low-house family used to stand given over to a man with a slate under his arm and nobody in particular. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold against his ear and the empty place beside it had crossed another morning without acquiring a name, and no third came. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the cold iron two inches beyond his hand, and the three wants sat in him with the strict equality of three tools laid out on a towel. He could reach none. A thread of drool had frozen at the corner of his mouth in the small hours and no hand came to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the wrist so the air found the bare place. The hand outside the wool went up an inch and came down. In the life that was not this one he had reduced a shoulder before breakfast and put his hand wherever he wanted it; here he was the only adult in the square and the least use in it, and the joke had never once been funny enough to say aloud.

\person{Berr Clay} came up to the block with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He smelled of hot clay and nothing under it, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean; he wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, and the crowd leaned in because he never raised his voice.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said low, over the top of the basket, and read it a second time for the households that had come late. He passed a finger across the figure to make sure it was still where he had put it. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.”

A man come down from the turnip rows with a number shut in his fist asked what the thaw would cost now the water was gone. Berr did not look at the fist.

“A number goes into a slate, not into a well,” he said. “If the house wants it, there'll be a slate at the watch.”

The number went from the man's fist to Berr's mouth to the shoulder of a man going up the road toward \place{High Kiln}, and arrived nowhere. The boy watched the order of it the way he had once watched a hand go past a note turned down on a chart and on to the signature at the foot of the page: hand to thumb, thumb to slate, slate to the leaning crowd, and the person last if at all. The true subtraction went over the basket unspoken, and the printed figure stood unchallenged; a man could stand under it all morning and go home with the number still in his fist and never have entered it. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid. He had no way into the box locked in a house he had never been in and no way to ask the question that would have made Berr turn and look at him, and Berr was already taking himself out of his own sentence with the neatness of a surgeon taking a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, never saying I, going off with the box still locked and the true deduction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The boy put Berr's twice-wiped thumb on a tray of its own and did not join it to any other hand.

The crowd went back to its pigs and turnips as water goes back to its course after a stone, and the slate stood where he had set it with the figure on the top line for anybody to read and nobody to challenge. A woman with a basket on her hip looked at the figure and went on to the turnips without giving it a number of her own, which was the same arithmetic the house performed with loaves and glances and a space at the end of the row. The boy lay under the wool with the moot-post's iron at his cheek and felt the cold come into him through the place where the wool had ridden back, and thought that a chart closed over a note was a small thing beside a whole square closed over a figure. Neither of them had a hand willing to lift the lid.

The \lore[the bell at the Cross]{bell at the Cross} went at the turn of the morning, four, four and a gap where the fifth should have been. The stall-hands stopped for the strokes and went back to what they had been doing without a word between them; a man took his cap off, looked at the uncovered heads around him and put it back on. Nobody crossed himself. The boy kept the heard bell in one place and the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} in another, the bell in the dark that went four, four and a gap in the telling, and he did not lay the two together, because a bell told in a story is not a bell heard under a roof and a gap in a story is not the gap that came up through the floor. Across the market a woman stood with green at one corner of her mouth, the same lead-glaze green Berr carried, and the boy filed it in silence without a name for the mouth or the woman or the ear that would one day be spoken with it. There was nothing in this morning's market that wanted an answer from that green, and he gave it none.

\scenebreak

Down at \place{the Fishstairs} the market went over the landing the way it had gone over it all week: four steps, four, and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walked over carefully without making a fall of it. Feet felt for the place, set down, lifted, set down again. A girl with a creel went over it sideways, caught herself on the rail and went on selling; nobody made a fall of it. In the life that was not this one a bad step in a public place would have been roped off by noon and a man sent with a form to say who had charge of it; here the carefulness was the whole of the arrangement and nobody went home owning a day. The boy kept four and four and a landing out of line on the tray where he kept the bell's four and four and a gap and the fire's four and four and a gap and the boards' four and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and no one of them touched another.

An eel buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure twice and then a third time to a man who was not listening, and the figure went into the noise of the stalls and was gone; nobody made a fall of that either. The boy had no word in the common tongue for a number that arrived three times and belonged to nobody, and kept it with the others that had gone from fist to mouth to shoulder.

The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing with his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called. His cuff had gone grey from washing and was turned back twice from the wrist, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, over and over, the way a man puts a hand to a rail before he looks over the edge. Nobody had taken him off. He was not the woman in the coat the colour of wet ash at the flare, not the grey-fingered man at the Drawing, not the clerk behind the Court's table up at the Cross; four hands in four places on a tray that had been filling since his first morning, and no joining of them. A measure set down before anybody had called it was a measure set down ahead of a death in this district; the order here put the slate down before the body arrived, and the boy kept the uncalled measure with the other things that stood ahead of a death and made nothing follow from it.

At the outer boards \person{Mira Silt} worked the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and river silt in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right in the place where the top of her left ear had been. She did not look into the basket. Her board lay beside the little length of cord \person{Orra Weir} kept on her own board and did not touch it; Orra watched from where she watched and would get no answer from this bed. Mira's not-looking was a keeping and Berr's not-looking at the block was a pricing, and the boy set the two in places that did not touch, having no \lore{Grathic} word for either.

At the edge of the trade, where the fish smell thinned into river wind, a small foundling stood in cast-off clothes worn like a uniform, a coat with a man's shoulders and breeches cut off at the knee. There was a gap where a front tooth had been and a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string at his throat. He was always in somebody's kitchen and never in his own, and he smelled of river silt, small ale and the warm stone of other people's hearths. He watched the grown faces the way a child watches a door that may open, and the boy knew the shape of the waiting without having a word for it.

When a pot was set down on the stone beside him the boy's head went toward it before the knuckle came down, as it always did, and the foundling's head went toward it a \lore[Half-beat]{half-beat} after: after the knuckle, after the note, after everything that could have told him where to look. Then it stopped. The child rubbed the green sliver with his thumb and could not say why he had turned, and was as surprised as anybody at the small movement of his own neck. No sentence was finished for a kiln-hand this morning, no warm loaf passed from a soft hand into a pocket holding no slate, no slate was seen, and no secret was sold.

The boy filed the delay on a place of its own beside his own turning and did not let the two touch. A thing that came after the knock was a different object from one that came before it, and this one had come after; it was neither a hearing nor a seventh, and he did not make it one. The sale stayed a shape and the betrayal stayed years ahead, gathering nothing he could count and costing nothing he could yet name.

\scenebreak

The afternoon went over the landing the way the morning had, and the count in the basket did not move. The boy checked the file as a man checks instruments laid out on a towel before he begins, though there was no beginning and no instrument to lay hands on. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six from four sources, five of them from vessels and the sixth from a person, filed beside and never among and never a seventh. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid. The told bell, the heard bell, the early note of \place{the Singing Sill} and the kiln's gap stayed four low things in four places, with the river's note of water going over water a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. The word forty remained a saying on its own tray, unspoken this morning, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their own tray joined to no kiln in \place{Low Kiln}, the likeness kept and the difference kept --- breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both saying \emph{not the war's hand} --- and neither turned into the other. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Below the sill a child's cold foot stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the market, and the two facts went past each other again without meeting. Three doors down the pewter tin and the turned slate stood shut and unread, with no seventh page written and no record carried to any door. A woman was more mornings dead than any living person kept count of and uncollected, and the number stayed in a thumb gone into the water. No collector came for her, and the morning came all the same.

At the going of the afternoon Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She told the fire it was the thaw and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash and the back of a broken pot; its payer had gone unnamed all this time and the coal the brine-gull took was entered in no slate and missed in none. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a bed is not an opening and a basket is not a door and no opening had come into this house all day. The clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept, and the boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for eighty-nine days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was not spoken, and no collector came. The boy was eighty-nine days old and nobody had told him so. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The \lore[the last market]{last market of Silt} closed and the count crossed into Seed unspent.
