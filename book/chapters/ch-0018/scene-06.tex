\daymark{90}{Seed 1, Y.H. 813}

The month named for planting opened on mud. Before the light the \place{the Clay Yard} lay with the ruts gone stiff in the dark, the brown skin the water had left holding the print of a boot and giving it back slowly as the air cooled over it, faithful to a thing that had gone and would not come again in that shape. Seed was a name for putting things into the ground. There was nothing in the ground here yet, only the left trace of what had come down in Silt and the space it had taken up and would not give back to the broom. Wet wool and woodsmoke went before the women; the tallow in the shed had gone soft in the warm and set again in the cold, and the whole yard smelled of a fire that had been fed and then left. The bucket hooks rang faintly where the wind found them, and the oiled paper in the shed window hung slack in its frame. The first work of the planting month was sweeping.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. She stood in the Vail corner with the bristles across her two hands and the grey of the ground showing between them, and she said what she had to say to the bristles: “Round it. The month's for planting and there's nothing planted here. It cost enough to come in; it'll cost more to take out.” The little-door burn across the back of her hand shut on nothing. Then, and only then, she let \person{Tarn} take the broom out of her hands. She went around the clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} ridge had turned its corner, and around the new hairline of grey finer than the old that lay beside it unswept, and around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, going as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She swept none of the three. The new grey was the thaw, and the thaw would go when it went; the house had said so all through Silt and said it again at the turning of the month, and a line the house had not swept in a year was left where it lay and called a season.

At \place{the yard well} a woman with her sleeves wet to the elbow asked what the month would cost now the mud had set. Sella moved her lips along the shelf of loaves inside the kiln-house doorway, glanced at the clay jar on the wall, and left the place at the row's end where a loaf had stood and stood no longer. She did not put a figure to the place.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. Then, to the woman: “The house has said it.”

She gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody: keep the silt out, the water had come up thin twice and would not bear a hard hand. The bucket went down and came up and set itself on the stone with the care of a pot filled that must not be slopped. The boy, carried at the edge of the work with the cold iron two inches beyond his hand, watched the order of it and kept it --- instruction to the bucket, then to the women, then nothing to the basket, and the person last if at all.

The woman went away with her sleeve still wet and no number in her hand, and the loaf row stood with its space at the end that nobody called a figure. A man with a barrow said the strips east of town would want hands by the morrow, and a woman said the weather would have the last word, and nobody put a figure to the hands or the strips. The boy set the strips on a place of their own, a direction and not a number. A month had turned on a boundary the yard would not mark, and the arithmetic went over it unchanged, the way water goes over a stone without stopping to be counted.

\person{Corin} was ninety days old, three months, carried everywhere at the edge of the work; the number sat in him with the exactness of an instrument laid out on a towel and entered nowhere. He could have told the hour of a burn and the term of a debt and the difference between a wound three days old and one four. Here he could not get anybody to say how old he was. He wanted the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted, and nobody in the yard had any use for the number, and the house kept its account in loaves and glances and a space at the end of a row. He had reduced a shoulder before breakfast in the life that was not this one and put his hand wherever he wanted it; here he lay under a fold of wool and could not lift that hand to his own chin. He had watched a hand go to a record before it went to a person in a better-lit room, and the house performed the same order with loaves and a glance at a jar, and the person came last if at all. A chart closed over a note turned down was a small thing beside a whole yard closed over a space nobody would name. Neither of them had a hand willing to lift the lid. The joke had never once been funny enough to say aloud.

The body humiliated him exactly and in its known order. A thread of drool had frozen at the corner of his mouth in the small hours and no hand came to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and came back. Cold arrived at cheek, then temple, then wrist, the wool riding back from the wrist so the air found the bare place and stayed there. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold against his ear, gone past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it had crossed the boundary into Seed without acquiring a name; no third came. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the lip of the bucket two inches beyond his hand, and the three wants sat in him with the strict equality of three tools laid out for a procedure he would never perform. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water behind the racks and came down. He was the only adult in the yard and the least use in it.

Tarn had set the small bench knife on its sill before the light and told the empty yard where it was, six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check.

“Six counted,” he said to the kindling. “The next I don't count.”

He said it as a man says the weather and went on holding the breath he had decided not to spend, though his chest was already tight with it. He had said the same thing to the same yard every morning of six years and would say it once more on the last morning, if the last morning came under this roof. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. A woman with a barrow said the thaw would come early and be done with it, and a man at the tubs said late was better for the ruts.

“Yes, house,” Tarn said to the one. “Yes, house,” he said to the other. He agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came. From \place{the Vail wheel-shed} came the low note of the wheel and a word spoken to a pot --- \emph{You'll do} --- and no figure came out of the shed onto the yard, and \person{Jorra Vail} was not seen. In the afternoon a vessel gave its note past the door and Tarn's head did not turn and he did not name the sound, and the boy did not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences kept their equality under one roof and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled.

Under the Vail roof the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Sella told it it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of the broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for ninety days. The coal the brine-gull took upriver weeks back was entered in no slate and missed in none; one warmth less had gone out of the world with no account the poorer, and the month had turned over it without noticing. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at the turn of the month, because a month's turning is not an opening and no opening had come into this house in six days, and she had lost a husband to the danger and had never once described it that way herself. The clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the finer grey lay beside it, and the boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Every warmth in the house had a payer and none of them stood in the room to be thanked; the boy had learned to ask who paid before he asked what a thing was worth, which was a surgeon's question in a country that had no use for it.

The boy checked the file as a man checks instruments laid out on a towel before he begins, though there was no beginning and no instrument to lay hands on. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six from four sources, five from vessels and the sixth from a person, filed beside and never among and never a seventh; no seventh came on the first day of Seed. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, and the three intervals --- Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25 --- stayed on another tray, objects with the shape of sounds that had not been sounds. The word forty remained a saying on its own tray, unspoken at the turning of the month, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both. Four low things stood in four places --- the told bell, the heard bell, the early note of \place{the Singing Sill}, the kiln's gap --- with the river's note of water going over water a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their own tray joined to no kiln in \place{Low Kiln}, the likeness kept and the difference kept --- breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both saying \emph{not the war's hand} --- and neither turned into the other though two men had carried the same danger into the same yard. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Three doors down the pewter tin and the turned slate stood shut and unread, with no seventh page written and no record carried to any door; a woman was more mornings dead than any living person kept count of and uncollected, the number gone into the water in a thumb. No collector came for her and the morning came all the same. Below the sill a child's cold foot was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured and unnamed. He knew the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the look severe cold puts on a face that other people take for a smile, and had no word for any of it and no hand two paces out. The reading worked and bought nothing. He had no \lore{Grathic} word for any of it and no hand to lay on any of it.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket level, carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, went around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She told the fire it was the thaw and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, with its payer still unnamed and the gull's coal entered in no slate.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety days and as they would come tomorrow. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was not spoken, the two tellings stayed on their tray, the loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came. The boy was ninety days old and nobody had told him so. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The first work of the planting month had been sweeping; the work after it lay east of Low Kiln in strips he had not yet seen, and the keeping went on unchanged into the planting year and the count went on.
