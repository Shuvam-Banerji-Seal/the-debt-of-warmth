\daymark{88}{Silt 29, Y.H. 813}

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket at the outer edge of the shade at \place{the Fishstairs}, where the fish-trade thinned out below \place{the north bridge} and no slate lay open on any board. She set it level and let go only when its whole weight had committed itself to the ground, the way she sets a pot down, and then she stood a half-pace off from it and gave the instruction to the wool and the water and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it was the river wind, and the river wind had come up in the night with salt in it. The little-door burn across the back of her hand shut on nothing and stayed shut. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and the house had carried him down the road and put him where he could see the trade and not be seen into, and had turned no errand on the way into a walk toward \place{Nine Pots} or a door.

\person{Corin} was eighty-eight days old, and the number sat in him with the exactness of a measure set down at night and entered nowhere. From the low angle of the basket the steps went down four, four, and a landing where a step should have been, and that landing stood a hand's breadth out of line with the rest. The dawn market went over it carefully all morning: feet feeling for the place, setting down, lifting, setting down again, and nobody made a fall of it. In the life that was not this one a bad step in a public place would have been roped off by noon and a man sent with a form to say who had charge of it; here the market merely walked differently and went home having made nothing of the matter. He had no \lore{Grathic} word for the carefulness and kept it exactly, four and four and a landing out of line, on the tray where he kept the bell's four and four and a gap and the fire's four and four and a gap and the boards' four and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and no one of them touched another.

Below the landing \place{the Ilm} went by with its skin of silt, low and muttering, nearer at its going than at its coming. The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing with his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called. His cuff had gone grey from washing and was turned back twice from the wrist, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, over and over, the way a man puts a hand to a rail before he looks over the edge. Nobody had taken him off. He was not the woman in the coat the colour of wet ash at the flare, not the grey-fingered man at the Drawing, not the clerk behind the Court's table up at \place{the Cross}; four hands in four places on a tray that had been filling since his first morning, and no joining of them. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came down the road with him. A measure set down before anybody had called it was a measure set down ahead of a death in this district; the order of things here put the slate down before the body arrived, and he kept the uncalled measure with the other things that stood ahead of a death and made nothing follow from it.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold against his ear and the empty place beside it had crossed another morning without acquiring a name, and no third came. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pale eel-bone lying on the boards two paces beyond his hand, and the three wants sat in him with the strict equality of three tools laid out on a tray. He could reach none. A thread of drool had frozen at the corner of his mouth in the small hours and no hand came to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the wrist so the air found the bare place. The hand outside the wool went up an inch and came down. There had been a life in which reducing a shoulder was work done before breakfast and a man put his hand where he wanted it; here he could not lift a hand to his own chin, and the joke had never once been funny enough to say aloud.

\scenebreak

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels at the outer boards with pale slime dried on her sleeves and river silt in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right in the place where the top of her left ear had been. She kept the small smile on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch; it could be shut without anybody noticing the movement, and it had not moved all morning. She worked standing, as she had stood since she was twelve, and there was no table within reach of her with a slate open on it. A woman come down from \place{the Weir} with salt dried white at her wrists asked whether the run would be thin now the water had gone down.

“Thin?” Mira said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.”

She had answered the question and then her own answer, and there was nothing left in the sentence that wanted a reply. She flicked an eel-bone against her thumb while she decided whether to lie, moved two of them hand to hand, and put one away when the deciding was done. The boy read the small movement from the basket as he had once read a hand hovering over a chart: a decision taken, a document not written, the lie and the truth both kept under the same door-latch smile. He had no way to ask which of the two she had put away, and no \lore{Grathic} word with which to ask it, and she did not look into the basket all morning. The eels went on moving in the shallow tray, long cold things with the slime drying pale where her sleeve had touched them, and the smell of salted perch and river silt came across the boards and went under his wool and stayed there.

A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord at her waist and stayed there while she decided, and the thumb did not come out with the answer. When she shifted a creel to reach another eel the second cord showed twisted twice beneath the grey, and neither knot was counted by any thumb but her own. The second cord had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and had never been lifted at a door, and the knot she kept for the thin child upriver was not touched this morning. The boy read the double twist by its shape as he read the readable shape of a number he had never heard spoken: an entry in a material no Court's thumb would think to look at, gathering interest in a record nobody could confiscate, kept out of love and paid for in silence. A woman had kept that count in wool for three years and kept in it something about a child carried up the Clay Yard, and he could read the shape of the double turn without its name and could not enter it, and he wrote no cause across it. The word stayed where it stayed, a saying on a place of its own and not a vessel's sound, with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both.

Her board lay beside the little length of cord \person{Orra Weir} kept on her own board, and the two boards did not touch. Orra was not on the steps this morning; she watched from where she watched, and she would get no answer from this bed, as she had got none in three years. Mira did not look into the basket. Her not-looking was a keeping, and \person{Berr Clay}'s not-looking at the block was a pricing, and the boy set the two in places that did not touch, having no \lore{Grathic} word for either and no way to say what the difference was. There was nothing in the sentence any of them had spoken that wanted an answer, and the market went on selling eels over the bad landing.

\scenebreak

The afternoon went over the landing the way the morning had, and the clerk's hand went to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls, and the count in the basket did not move. The boy checked the file as a man checks instruments laid out on a towel before he begins, though there was no beginning and no instrument to lay hands on. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six from four sources, five of them from vessels and the sixth from a person, filed beside and never among and never a seventh. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid. The told bell, the heard bell, the early note of \place{the Singing Sill} and the kiln's gap stayed four low things in four places, with the river's note of water going over water a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their own tray joined to no kiln in Low Kiln, the likeness kept and the difference kept and neither turned into the other. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Below the sill a child's foot stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the market, and the two facts went past each other again without meeting. The half-beat of a small head turning toward a pot, the green sliver rubbed with a thumb, the warm loaf into a pocket holding no slate and the sentence nobody finished stayed on a place of their own with no cause written across any of them; the loaf went on gathering interest in an account the boy could not read, and the sale stayed a shape and the betrayal stayed years ahead. A woman was more mornings dead than any living person kept count of and uncollected, and the number stayed in a thumb gone into the water. No collector came for her, and the morning came all the same.

At the going of the afternoon Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She told the fire it was the thaw and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash and the back of a broken pot; its payer had gone unnamed all this time and the coal the brine-gull took was entered in no slate and missed in none. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a bed is not an opening and a basket is not a door. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept, and the boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Three doors down the pewter tin and the turned slate stood shut and unread, and down at the Fishstairs the soft-cuffed clerk's hand still went to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls, and Mira's two cords lay under her apron with neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for eighty-eight days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was not spoken, and no collector came. The boy was eighty-eight days old and nobody had told him so. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count crossed into Silt 30 unspent.
