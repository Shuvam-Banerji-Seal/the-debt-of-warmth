\daymark{241}{Smoke 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

Two hundred and forty-one came on without a stroke to mark it, the way a vessel comes to heat in a room where nobody sits watching --- not by a bell, not by a voice, but by a small thickening of the dark before the grey, the same dark and a different one, a room that keeps no almanac and needs none. Nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said six months and sixty-one days over \person[Corin]{the boy} or within his hearing. The numbers stood where they stood, exact, complete, unspent. Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} drew four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-one days. Fire under the floor, river through the finger's-width door with its note of \lore{water over water}, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing them would want a payer put under all three and no payer stood to any. He kept them on three trays and let the trays lie.

Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. It was the kiln drawing in fours with its gap, a sound that went on after the ear had placed it, and there was no market on a Secondday and no Cross and no bell; Smoke 10 stood eight mornings ahead and had not been brought into a morning that had not earned it. He took the smells in the order they arrived and the sound in the order it failed, the near before the far, what was under the hand before what was behind it, the way he had once taken a room in the dark and counted the instruments laid out on the table before anyone thought to raise the lights.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to sit there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go on nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder before the light, because a child's hunger keeps no hours and the register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

\person{Sella Vail} had gone to the fire before the basket, as she went every morning, and gave the instruction to the wool to keep the river wind off his ears, and to the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. The dressed grain lay under a fold of wool near \place{the hearth} where she had stored it four days gone. She lifted the fold and turned it for damp, put her fingers into it to the knuckle and felt what the wool had held, looked at it a moment with the look of a woman who reads a field by the weight of it in her hand, spread it thin on the flat stone to finish, and gathered it back by the handful and set the fold flat again. It changed state and gained no figure. From the low angle he watched the handfuls come and go, the pale grain running through her fingers and back under the wool, and he counted what could be counted: a glance, a handful, another glance, the heft of the arm that lifted him as she moved him clear of the stone. There was no figure in it. There was a weight, and the carrying arm knew the weight and not the cost, the way a muscle remembers a load and not what the load was worth, and he had spent his life learning to tell the two apart and had no word here in which to enter either. The grain went on being dressed. No slate came near it and no figure was laid across any of the absences.

The damp was the thing that would have gone wrong if the fold had been left flat another day, and she had known it without being told, the way a woman knows a cellar wall by the feel of it in the morning. He read the fold as he had once read a dressing lifted from a wound: not for what it covered but for what it had kept from going bad. The wool came away warm at the edges and cool where it had lain heaviest, and the grain under it had a smell of stone and chaff, dry at the top and closer to damp below, and nothing in it asked to be entered. She turned it with the back of her fingers and not the palm, so that the heat of her hand did not go into it; a pot set down wet near a fire is a thing paid for later, and this house paid later for everything, but it did not pay twice for the same thing. The handfuls went back light. The fold came down flat. The dressed grain had been looked at, which was a keeping, and it had not been counted, which was another.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool.

“What'll the storing take now it's under wool?” she said.

Sella did not look at the grain or at the woman. “Ask the pot,” she said. “The weather knows the rest.”

The woman's pot had come up from the strip with her and was hitched against her hip by the handle, and she held it there with a hand that had carried it a long way and would carry it a long way further, without setting it down to make a speech of it. Sella's answer went out to the pot and the weather and not across to the woman, and there was no rudeness in it and no refusal either; a thing said to a pot is a thing said aloud in this yard, and the yard has ears of its own. The woman shifted the pot to her other hip and went off with the question still on her, carrying it the way she had carried the pot, level and unentered.

She carried the question away level, the way a woman carries a full dish across a room, so that nothing slopped and nothing was entered. The boy could not enter it either. The figure read aloud over the covered grain on Grain's last Account-day was not the figure paid, and the figure paid was not the cost of bringing it in, and the space between the two had no signature on it and was left apart, not averaged; the true subtraction sat locked beyond this roof in a box gone up \place{the clay road}, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, no figure, no slate, no cause and no name for what ailed him. A cost asked as a direction went off toward the pot and the weather. It came back as neither, and there was nothing in the house to take it down.

Later a man came down the clay road past \place{the drying racks} with a number in his fist. The road had taken a boot and given it back slowly, and the mud had dried in a pale scurf on the skirt of his coat; he did not brush it off, because a coat worn on that road is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. He brought the number up out of the fist toward \place{the yard well}, as though the well might take it, looked at it, looked at the pot beside \place{the trough} whose water was called weather, and put the number back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. He went off without saying it and the yard went on without it.

He had brought the number up where a man brings up a stone to see what is under it, and had put it back because there was nothing under it that would stay put. The boy watched the fist close again on nothing and filed no finding, because a figure that arrives nowhere is not a missing figure and does not want a payer; it is a figure kept in a sleeve, and a sleeve is not a slate. The pale scurf stayed on the coat and went off up the yard with it. The trough kept its dark water called weather and the well kept its cold, and neither took the number.

\scenebreak

She had come across \place{the flagstones} from the hearth with the pot in the burn-hand, and at \place{the loaf shelf} she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row that stood with two gaps in it. She gave the gaps no figure. The charcoal had gone in beneath the board on the first Account-day of Reed and had stayed there through Grain and into Smoke, a count kept in a hand nobody had been told to read; a man who lifted the board would find a row of marks wanting a payer and none standing under it, and would have made an account of them before the morning was out, so her finger stayed on top of the front edge where the wood was warm from her hand and nothing could be taken off it.

She glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and shut her hand half over nothing and brought it away without touching, the way a woman closes a hand over a draught and finds nothing there to hold. The burn across the back of her left hand was shaped like a little door --- two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside it puckered and pale --- and it shut on nothing now and opened on nothing when she took her hand away. People noticed the burn before the mark at her throat, which was the order she preferred, and in three years she had given nobody a reason to look at the second. The boy, who could not lift his own hand to his chin, watched the little door open and shut, and set it down beside the other objects on the tray and made no cause of it. When she set the pot rim out she put the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat through the shelf and the well and the rack and the basket. A shelf is not an opening, a well is not an opening, a rack is not an opening and a basket set down is not a door. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the rule held on Smoke 1 and held now on Smoke 2, and she did not touch it. The one opening was behind the household and was not looked at again, not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a payment, and nobody under the roof said what it had mended.

He put the question to his hand the third way of the chapter, in the narrow time between the setting-down of the pot and the going of the light off the floor. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. It was taken through the small intake and into the hold. The count at her fire was small intake, hold, letting go, a thing she kept toward no opening and had never offered to any ear under this roof; he had heard it through the wall so many mornings that it had stopped being a sound and become a measure belonging to the room that does not touch his, and he took it for his own the way a man keeps a rhythm he has heard a thousand times and cannot say where he learned it. Then it came down while that count was still going --- down before the counted breath had finished, an interval cut short on a rhythm that had not given its end; she let go and the little silence after her count arrived too late for the hand, which was already against the fold. It was filed on the sixth place with no payer named, not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Fifty-seven answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had taken a shoulder apart in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift a hand to his own chin or carry a crust two inches to his mouth; exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true, and neither stood up to say what the other was. The child wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a sign.

\scenebreak

No eighth had come. The seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape stayed a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, no second look joined to the first, no name spoken over either, no loaf, no slate and no sale, and neither the woman at the pot board nor the child in cast-offs was called by a name in any telling under this roof. Over the boards no fifth asking came. Four askings stood on their seventh place with a finger between each pair, nobody to own them and nobody standing anywhere who could be asked; four holes in a silence are still not one witness, and he did not make a fifth by wanting one. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and a day must come with a place in it before the question can be put to anybody, and none of these working mornings had brought such a day. The round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood kept its own tray with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word. No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up and called itself a cause, and no finger of his drew a line between the empty place beside the crust, the two gaps in the shelf's row, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips} and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. He did not count them as a row, because a row wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Two hundred and forty-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and sixty-one days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and not since, not at a shelf, a well, a rack or a basket on this morning; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given; Smoke had come on without a boundary and stood where it stood, and Smoke 10 was eight mornings off and unbrought. On the sixth place fifty-seven answers lay like instruments laid out on a towel, complete and worth nothing, and the file was open and no hand entered it.

The cover went back flat over the dressed grain while the cost-question went away level toward the pot, and nothing in the house took either one down.
