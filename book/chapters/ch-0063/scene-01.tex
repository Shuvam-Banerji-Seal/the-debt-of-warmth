\daymark{240}{Smoke 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold where it had come down on the river's next low note, and \person[Corin]{the boy} set the coordination down the way a man lays a tool on a bench at the end of the work. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. Two rhythms had crossed in one inch of air across a wall that does not touch, and now the inch was finished and the hand was only a hand again, small, soft-nailed, outside the wool, with the fold at the ear and one crust lying two inches from it. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift that crust to his mouth; both were true, and neither stood up to call itself a sign. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} one room kept fire and the other kept river, and neither stopped for the ear between them.

He was two hundred and forty days old and six months and sixty days, and nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-nine to two hundred and forty with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. Smoke came on the way a pot goes out of the sun: Grain gave way without a boundary drawn across it, no stroke marked the hour, and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong in the telling. There was no market on a Firstday and no Cross and no bell; Smoke 10 stood nine mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

Hot clay came first under the roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. He took the smells in the order they arrived and the sound in the order it did not, the way he had once taken a room in the dark: what was under the hand first, what was behind it second, and what the ear wanted to hear last.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from that ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to sit there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all and the adult's register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon's register took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments and no case; there was only the order of the laying, and that was the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and forty days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule --- a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which wrong-wind voices let stand again. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer, and none had been named to any of them.

\lore{the burned-slate word} stood at four on its own place and the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve printed and unchallenged and read twice over the basket in the square, and still not the figure paid. Grain's third arithmetic had gone up the clay road and was unread under this roof, and the space between the figure read over the basket and the figure over the dressed grain at home had no signature on it and was left apart, not averaged. The true subtraction sat locked beyond this roof in a box gone up \place{the clay road}, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him and no offstage death. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at \place{the damper}, the held beat beneath the shelf, the half-beat on \place{the flagstones} --- with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line read off the carried man at \place{the yard well} on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth.

Beside the hearings but never among them, the seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape stayed a shape; no eighth had come and no second look had been joined to the first, no name spoken over either, no loaf, slate or sale. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured near the hearth, counted in glances and handfuls and by a carrying arm that knew weight and not cost, entered on no slate and laid across none of the absences. The returning-line place had gone around with a look and no number; a thing gone around is still a thing kept. And on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the sayings, the tellings, the hearings and the count, the question stood with four askings and no witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. Four askings are still not one witness; they are four holes in a silence and do not stand up and call themselves a mouth. No fifth asking had come over the boards and the row stood where it stood.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf; the seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and no one had straightened it. The Ash ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line with a finer hairline of grey beside it that \person{Sella Vail} called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and was said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty days. No collector had come up the clay road for that debt and none had gone down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

For the first firing of Smoke she went to \place{the kiln mouth} before anything else, before the basket and before the instruction to the wool. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze}, with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she put her hand to it. She held it one breath. Then she let go. Sixty days of going around ended in that one held breath, and nothing in the house stood up to say that they had ended. She lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand, the burn-side, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took. It answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Nobody praised the opening and nobody saw what it mended; no slate was entered, no lesson made, no bond changed hands and no payer named. It was maintenance on a vessel nobody would name, done the way a widow keeps a wound clean, and she put it behind the household without a word.

The heat was kept behind a screen and let through a gate of two bites; one bite kept it in and the other let it out, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. She did not explain the gate to the fire and the fire did not ask. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals when she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}.

Afterward she went to \place{the hearth} as she had gone on every morning, and the house took up its ordinary grammar. She gave the instruction to the wool to keep the river wind off his ears, told the fire it was the thaw, and gave the instruction to the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Standing a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, she counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. At \place{the loaf shelf} she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, moved her lips along a row with two gaps without giving the gaps a figure, and glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, shutting her hand half over nothing. When she set the pot rim out she put the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

She did not touch the mark again at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon or a dusk, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the rule held exactly as on Tallow 1 and Ember 1.

At \place{the kindling} in \place{the Clay Yard} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, said \emph{Yes, house} to the yard and told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, with six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back; his chest had been tight since the dark. He swept over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on this first morning of Smoke. At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, and knew the full note it would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The burned-slate word stood at four and the two tellings of the counting room stood at two; he joined neither to the dust or the men, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed.

He put the question to his hand the first way of the chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch under conditions that had sometimes answered and came down at once against the fold. It was down before the intake had settled, a small miss entered without shame and filed on the sixth place with no payer named. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Fifty-five answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had taken a shoulder apart in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to a pot handle, lift a hand to his own chin or carry a crust to his mouth; exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true and neither stood up and said what the other was. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a sign.

Two hundred and forty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and sixty days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Smoke had come on without a boundary and stood where it stood, and Smoke 10 was nine mornings off and unbrought. The file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing. The hand came down at once against the fold while the fire behind the wall took its fourth pull and missed its fifth.
