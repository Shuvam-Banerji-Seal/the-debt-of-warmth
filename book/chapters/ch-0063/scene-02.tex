The afternoon thinned without ending. It went the way light goes off a clay floor, not by a stroke but by degrees, the near boards losing their warmth before the far ones, and the heat that had been set down in the morning at \place{the hearth} crossed the room and settled behind the pot and then into the fold at his ear, where it came to rest against the crust the way a palm pressed flat rests on a wound, holding and not bearing down. \person[Corin]{The boy} did not look again at what his hand had done before the kiln. It had been done and put behind the household, and a man does not spend the afternoon turning a tool over to admire the work; the work was the afternoon. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the house took up its ordinary grammar, which was instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one.

Woodsmoke came in low bars through the finger's-width door, and under the smoke the river wind with the taste of water over water, and under all of it the sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. He took them in the order they arrived, the near before the far, what was under the hand before what was behind it, the way he had once taken a room in the dark before he could see what was on the table. The crust kept its place two inches from the ear, stiff and leathery, brown at the edge. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to sit there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go on nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was the louder all afternoon, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all and the register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Through the door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. The afternoon went on doing what afternoons do, which is to use itself up without announcing that it is used.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf; nobody straightened the crease and nobody took the coat down. The seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and no one had filled it. The \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line, and beside it a finer hairline of grey had gathered that \person{Sella Vail} called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and was said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty days. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for that debt and none had gone down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} did not take up the broom until the broom had been told. Sella's instruction went out ahead of her into the yard --- a sentence addressed to the wood and the stones and only after that to the man who would move them --- and he stood with his hands loose at his sides until it had arrived, then took the broom up as a man takes up a tool already named, saying \emph{Yes, house} to nothing that stood near enough to answer. He was bent at the shoulders from six years in the slip pit, and the grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark.

The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He set it there with the flat of his hand and then checked it with his eyes, which was the order of the thing and not a second order; six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the one about the man who was sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, unentered because the man who turned back had been Garl himself and had given no name to the man. Tarn told the kindling what he told it every evening of this year: \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He did not count the next. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

He swept. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch that the stones had kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the clay road on this first morning of Smoke, and none went down it. The warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold long since in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft} above \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, two years gone and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. It reached him now only as a sound with a direction, the small settling of the loft when the day's warmth went out of the boards and they gave a little, the way an old cup gives when the hand lets go. He had stopped trying to reconcile the stone with the account he kept of it. A man who owns nothing should know where the small things are, and he knew where that stone was without ever looking toward the loft.

At the hearth, when the coals had been banked and the instruction given over, the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth. It came the whole way. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name. He had brought it to that word on Reed 5 and 8 and 11 and 14 and 17 and 20 and 23 and 26 and 29, and the stopping itself had become an object he kept on a tray of intervals, a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood. A tenth had not been stood up before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year had still not given. At the top of the reach he drew the breath that should have gone in and did not spend it; the small interval showed where it should have gone, and he said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with the broom. The loan was uncalled. The sentence went nowhere and stood where it stood, an interval and not a payment, an object and not a rule, with no payer named to any of the nine. Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. He did not ask about the room and nobody asked about the ear, and the two silences under the roof kept their old shape without a word between them.

At the edge of the wheel-shed \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks}, where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold, and the room kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices that arrived at the basket as a direction and nothing more. \lore{the burned-slate word} stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. No name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot had been lost.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, in the flat voice he used to the clay and the iron and only then to men. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, with no welcome in the sentence and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns ahead of its leaving his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. A kiln-master come up on a wrong wind from \place{High Kiln} would have called the note early and made something of it, and Jorra would not say it before him. The sayings about the pot went on being sayings --- that a pot which sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, that the one which sang early last month had not been heard from since, that a pot had gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and none had been named to any of them.

Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish. \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} she said, and the end of the instruction did not arrive at him. Before she had finished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, held while the little silence ran, and then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. The end of the sentence arrived at the pot. The household straightened around his silence without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it, and the quiet went before him into the shed like a cold air before a door. He had held the iron once before on Day 161 and again on the days after, and this was the holding remembered and not a change made again, paired with no other fraction and not joined to the damper fraction of Garl's year or the held beat under the shelf or the half-beat on the flagstones. The iron was one piece with two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. He read it as an object worth nothing and joined it to no kiln. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He put the question to his hand the second way of the chapter, in the long slope of the afternoon when the light lay along the floor in bars and the smoke went low above it. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. It was taken through the small intake and held there, and came down before the letting-go. It was down while the breath was still going in, a small interval ending early and filed on the sixth place with no payer named. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Fifty-six answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches to a pot handle, lift a hand to his own chin or carry a crust to his mouth; exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true, and neither stood up to say what the other was. The child wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a sign.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of water over water, and between the two she went wide. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey called the thaw beside it, around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, around the bare dark \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each of the seven got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row are still seven; they are not an account until a payer stands under them, and none stood under any of them, so she gave the yard no figure and the yard gave her none. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat through the going of the light and the setting-down, because a road and a basket and a dusk are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and the one opening that morning was behind the household and was not looked at again.

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. She gave the fire its instruction before the basket, told it the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward the bed. Then she went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves.

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Fire, river and boards kept their three counts and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and missed its fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a thumb-width knot. Agreeing them would want a payer put under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so they lay on three trays and the trays did not touch. The file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing, with the fifty-sixth answer on the sixth place and the empty place beside the crust still without a name.

Two hundred and forty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and sixty days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke had come on without a boundary drawn and stood where it stood, and Smoke 10 was nine mornings off and unbrought. The hand came down before the letting-go while the fire behind the wall missed its fifth.
