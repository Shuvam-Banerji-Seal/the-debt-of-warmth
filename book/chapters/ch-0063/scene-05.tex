\daymark{242}{Smoke 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

Two hundred and forty-two came on the way a board comes to warmth in a room where nobody sits to watch it --- not with a stroke or a bell, but with a small thickening of the dark before the grey, the same dark and a different one. Nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said six months and sixty-two days over \person[Corin]{the boy} or within his hearing. The numbers stood where they stood, exact, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody. Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} drew four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-two days. There was no market on a Thirdday and no Cross and no bell; Smoke 10 stood seven mornings ahead and had not been brought into a morning that had not earned it.

Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. It was the kiln drawing in fours with its gap, a sound that went on after the ear had placed it, the way a note goes on behind a note that has already been named. He took the smells in the order they arrived and the sound in the order it failed, the near before the far, what was under the hand before what was behind it, the way he had once taken a room in the dark and counted the instruments laid out on the bench before anybody thought to raise the lights.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to sit there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder before the light, because a child's hunger keeps no hours and the register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay.

\person{Sella Vail} had gone to the fire before the basket, as she went every morning, and gave the instruction to the wool to keep the river wind off his ears, and to the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it}, the sentence going out to the fire and not to any ear under this roof. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat; it had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no other hour since, because a shelf is not an opening, a well is not an opening, a rack is not an opening and a pot set down is not a door. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and the rule held on Smoke 1 and on Smoke 2 and held now on Smoke 3. The one opening was behind the household and was not looked at again, not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a payment, and nobody under the roof said what it had mended.

The dressed grain lay under a fold of wool near \place{the hearth}. She lifted the fold back and turned it for damp. The wool came away warm at the edges and cool where it had lain heaviest, and the grain below had a smell of stone and chaff, dry at the top and closer underneath. She put her fingers in to the knuckle and felt what the fold had held, the way a woman tests a cellar wall of a morning, and spread the grain thin on the flat stone so the last of the damp could go out of it. Then she gathered it back by the handful, the pale grain running through her fingers and back under the wool, and set the fold flat. It changed state and gained no figure. The damp was the thing that would have gone wrong if the fold had been left flat another morning, and she had known it without being told.

From the low angle he watched the handfuls come and go and counted what could be counted: a glance, a handful, another glance, the heft of the arm that lifted him as she moved him clear of the stone. There was no figure in it. There was a weight, and the carrying arm knew the weight and not the cost, the way a muscle remembers a load and not what the load was worth; he had spent his life learning to tell the two apart and had no word here in which to enter either. He had watched a dressing lifted from a wound change colour at the edge and knew the difference between a thing examined and a thing entered; the grain was examined. It was not entered. No slate came near it and no figure was laid across any of the absences.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot under her arm and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. There was a strip of wet sleeve where the handle had rubbed, and she shifted the pot from one hip to the other while she asked, and did not set it down, because a pot set down in a doorway is a pot that has to be lifted again.

“What’ll the keeping want now it’s under wool?” she said.

Sella did not look at the grain or at the woman. “Ask the pot,” she said. “The weather knows the rest.”

The answer went out to the pot and the weather and not across to the woman, and there was no rudeness in it and no refusal either; a thing said to a pot is said aloud in this yard, and the yard has ears of its own. The woman went off with the question still on her, carrying it level the way she had carried the pot, so that nothing slopped and nothing was entered.

He could not enter it either. Figures had been read aloud over the covered grain on the Account-days and a figure had been paid for bringing it in, and the space between the two had no signature on it; it was left apart, not averaged, the way a wound is left open rather than closed over something the surgeon has not seen. The true subtraction sat locked beyond this roof in a box gone up \place{the clay road}, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 --- no figure, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him. A cost asked as a direction went off toward the pot and the weather and came back as neither. A thing not priced is not therefore free; it is a thing whose payer has not been named, and no payer had been named to any of these.

Later a man came down the clay road past \place{the drying racks} with a number in his fist, coming toward \place{the yard well} the way a man comes toward a place that might take a thing off his hands. He brought the number up out of the fist and held it where the light fell, looked at it, looked at \place{the trough} beside the well with its dark water called weather, and put the number back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. He went off up the yard without saying it, and \place{the Clay Yard} went on without it.

He had brought the number up where a man brings up a stone to see what is under it, and had put it back because nothing under it would stay put. The boy filed no finding, because a figure that arrives nowhere is not a missing figure and does not want a payer; a number kept in a sleeve is a number kept, and there is no slate under a sleeve and no payer standing to a fist. A man who does not throw a figure away has only moved it to where no thumb can read it, which is the other way of keeping a number in this district. The trough kept its dark water and the well kept its cold, and neither took the number.

A wrong wind got up off \place{High Kiln}, came down \place{the terrace} and over \place{the boards} with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} on it --- a greenish sweetness that had no business in this yard. It rode the boards, went over them, touched the fold where the dressed grain lay, and went out on the wind that brought it. It was a direction arrived nowhere and not a second edge paid. No pot was lost. The boy let it pass the way a wind passes and made no cause of it; a man who wanted to make a cause of a sweet edge would have to put a payer under it, and no payer stood to it.

One of the three shapings of the pot saying stood again as a saying --- that a pot had gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and he let it stand where it stood. The sayings were three and they stayed three, and a fourth would have been a likeness standing up and asking to be believed, which is an account with no payer, the kind this house had learned to leave on the shelf. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to these. He took them apart the way he had once taken two pieces of tissue apart under a lens, telling them by what they did when pressed: one was an answer to a wound and one was the wound's own appetite. A saying was what was left when no payer had stood up; a rule was what a likeness became when somebody put a figure under it. No fourth likeness was made a cause, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. The sweet edge went out with the wind and took no figure with it.

Under the roof the house kept \person{Garl Vail} in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the wheel-seat with its twenty-year hollow, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned the corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, both left unswept. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-two days, and no collector had come up the clay road for the tile debt on Smoke 1, 2 or 3, and none had gone down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

She came across \place{the flagstones} from the hearth with the pot in the burn-hand, and at the loaf shelf stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row that stood with two gaps in it. She gave the gaps no figure. The charcoal had gone in beneath the board on the first Account-day of Reed and had stayed there through Grain and into Smoke, a count kept in a hand nobody had been told to read; her finger lay on top of the front edge where the wood was warm from her hand, and nothing could be taken off it.

Her eye went once, and only once, to the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without a touch. The burn on the back of her left hand had the shape of a little door --- lintel, threshold, two straight sides, the skin inside it puckered and pale --- and it closed on nothing now as it closed on nothing when she carried a cup. People noticed the burn before they noticed the mark at her throat, which was the order she preferred, and in three years she had given nobody a reason to look at the second. When she set the pot rim out she put the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The boy could not close the two inches to the handle, and the half-beat at the setting went by without a word from either of them.

\scenebreak

He put the question to his hand the fifth way of the chapter, in the narrow time between the setting-down of the pot and the going of the light off the floor. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. It was not taken through the small intake and the hold of any count kept in this house. There was a breathing number in the room that does not touch his, but it was kept toward no opening and had never been offered to any ear, and on this asking he borrowed none of it. He held the hand to the river's own low interval --- the space between one note of water over water and the next --- and brought it down on the next low note as the river gave it. It was filed on the sixth place with no payer named. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Fifty-nine answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Water over water stayed a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, and the six hearings kept their empty place without a seventh being invented to fill it.

Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not lift a crust two inches to his mouth; exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true, and neither stood up to call itself a sign. The child wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a finding, and the hand came down against the fold the way a man lays down a tool at the end of work.

No eighth had come. The seventh green stayed a colour at a silent woman's mouth and the seventh shape stayed a shape in cast-offs, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, no second look joined to the first, no name spoken over either, no loaf passed, no slate opened, no sale made and no romance paid. Neither was called by a name in any telling under this roof, and he made no name for either on his tray, because a name spoken over a colour would make a row of it and a row wants a payer.

Over the boards no fifth asking came. Four askings stood on their seventh place, each with a finger between it and the next, nobody to own them and nobody standing anywhere who could be asked; four holes in a silence are still not one witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and a day must bring a place before the question can be put to anybody, and this working morning brought none. He did not make a fifth by wanting one, and he drew no finger between the empty place beside the crust, the two gaps in the shelf's row, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips} and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, because a row wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The pan's gone round ring kept its own tray, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word.

Two hundred and forty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill and Grath; six months and sixty-two days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and not since, not at a shelf, a well, a rack, a basket or a pot on this morning; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given; Smoke had come on without a boundary and stood where it stood, and Smoke 10 was seven mornings off and unbrought. On the sixth place fifty-nine answers lay like instruments laid out on a towel, complete and worth nothing, and the file was open and no hand entered it.

The cover went back over the dressed grain while the sweet edge of glaze went out on the wind that brought it.
