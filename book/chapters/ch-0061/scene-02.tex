The afternoon thinned without ending. It did not go out the way a lamp goes out; it wore away the way the edge of a thumb-mark wears, a little at a time, so that a man who looked up from his work could not have said when the light had turned, only that it had turned and the day was thinner for it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water} and did not alter it for the light. Heat came under the low angle where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay, taking its route behind the pot first and into the fold afterwards, as though it knew the room by heart and had walked it a thousand times. It settled there. It went no further.

He had been set at the angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, and from there the room above him was all board and shadow and the bright edge of a pot rim now and then. He could not lift his head to see the shelf's row, and did not try, because a man who tries to lift a head that will not lift becomes a man arguing with his own body, and there is nothing to be gained in an argument that the body always wins. The crust lay two inches from his ear, stiff and leathery and brown at the edges, the last warmth that had crossed \place{House Silt} before he was taken, gone cold now and keeping the shape of what it had been. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, and he did not want one, because a third crust laid beside two absences would be a kindness with a figure on it, and a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account.

The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. Nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and the house had left it with the same care it left the pot: set down, covered, not looked into. Cold had stopped at his cheek, at his temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in the afternoon the second was louder than it had been in the small hours, because a child's hunger does not keep the hours an adult keeps, and the adult's register, which had been exact all morning, went on being exact while the child in him went on being hungry. The two argued without a word between them, and had argued so for two hundred and thirty-four days.

He did not turn out the file in the afternoon. That was work for the small hours, when the house was quiet and a man could lay his instruments on a towel without somebody coming to sweep the towel away. He left the trays where they lay: the hearings beside and never among, the near-full answers, the sayings, the tellings, the stoppings, the held inch with its answers. Each had its place and a finger between places, and the finger was the important thing, because a man who lets two things touch will find by morning that they have stood up together and called themselves a cause. No cause had stood up under this roof. Nothing had been paid.

\scenebreak

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder that no brushing would take out, and it held a broader man's shape the way a pot holds the shape of the hand that threw it long after the hand has gone to other work. In the shed the wheel-seat kept its twenty-year lean to the left and the hollow that went with it, and \person{Sella Vail} would not let anyone fill the hollow, though she let her son sit in it; the distinction had stood in the house for a year and had never been explained, and the boy had long since stopped expecting an explanation. In the corner of the fire room the clean dark line lay where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey that the house called \lore{the thaw} lay finer still, and neither had been swept. A line left where it is is still a line kept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, a saying that had outlived the thing it was true about and cost more for the outliving.

The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, because a man cannot pay a debt nobody will name and cannot settle it and cannot be done with it. It went around the house the way a woman goes around Ash in a corner: a careful circle and no word for the thing circled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and thirty-four days, and no lesson had been made of any of it, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Grain 25 and none had gone down it. In the district the arithmetic had changed twice and could not be changed back, and a man might have thought the change had something to do with the uncalled debt if he had wanted a cause badly enough to stand under it; the boy did not want one. He left the two on separate trays, with the true subtraction locked in a box going up toward \place{High Kiln} and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Two hundred and thirty-four stood exact, complete, unspent, and six months and fifty-four days had been told by nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. The fire's fifth had gone unpaid the same number of days, and the fifth had not been invented to make the row come out even.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named and handed to him, and took it up as an instrument. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back without appearing to know that it held any. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain for six years: the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, a sentence with no name in it and never one, though the man who turned back had been Garl himself.

The other sentence came to his teeth as he reached, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the remembered stopping added nothing to the nine and took nothing from them, an object on the tray and not a payment and not a rule. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the Seed 16 breath stood where it had stood since the planting, a small interval at the top of each reach, unspent. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road on Grain 25 and none went down it. He had said \emph{I owe six} so often that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark, and he had never once said the word that would make the seventh a number the house could enter. Somewhere above the shed the loft settled with a small dry sound, and it came to him as a direction and nothing more: up, and to the left, and no further. The warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold long since, and he had stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt, which is not the same thing as forgiving it. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and went on over the collector's road without once turning along it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and got no name from him, and he did not say the room was cold. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{the full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, knew it with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing. It went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and no fourth near-full answer stood up to join the three. The three sayings stayed three sayings, and \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at four, a direction over the boards from \place{Slate Row} arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two with no third, a hand between them and the three sayings. No fifth telling came and no fourth shaping of the pot saying stood up to call itself a cause, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188.

He grew quieter whenever the burned-slate word went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward \place{the bed} or the basket, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, ahead of his hand and ahead of the word. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back. He held until the sentence reached the pot and the end of it was given there, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, and it lay where the old fraction lay, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. He read the iron as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; a clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

\scenebreak

Sella gave the fire its instruction before she gave anything to the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. She told it it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed them, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count went nowhere that any slate could read. The fire answered the telling of the thaw with four low pulls behind the wall and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing at all, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn closed on nothing when the hand had nothing to hold, and opened again as she reached for the pot. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-four days, because an afternoon, a shelf and a basket are not openings. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

At the going of the light she carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. The half-pace kept its distance from every open slate, and there were no open slates on the clay road that evening, but she kept the distance all the same, the way a woman keeps a hand to a rail on a stair she has walked a thousand times. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the clean dark line, and wide again around the finer grey beside it; she went around the rack space under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood and stood no longer, around \place{the trough} bare and dark at \place{the yard well}, and out past the field's empty place on \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each of the seven got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under them, and none did. He did not lay them under one cause, did not let a third gap come among them, did not draw a finger across from one to the next, and found no payer. A thing gone around is still a thing kept; a thing kept is not a thing settled.

Small hours came on. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Sella had gone to her own bed before them and left the coals to keep themselves, and the two rooms did not touch. In the room that warmed him \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; in the room he could not enter the river kept its note; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places and none of them became one, because agreeing them would have wanted a payer and no payer stood to any. He borrowed no rhythm from the room that does not touch his. There was nothing in his room to borrow from but the fold and the crust and the cold, and the cold had stopped where it had stopped.

He put the second question of the chapter to the hand while the house was at its quietest. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's low note through the finger's-width door, under conditions that had sometimes answered, and he held it to the small intake only --- no further, not through the hold, not to the letting-go --- and brought it down before the letting-go came. It was an answer and it was a miss and it was both without shame. It went onto the sixth place with the others, filed with no payer named, not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Forty-four answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither stood up and said what the other was.

Grain 30, the third and last Account-day and market, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The ownerless question kept its two askings over the boards and was not given a third in an afternoon that had not asked it; that was held for a day still to come. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand came down before the letting-go and lay against the fold while the fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, neither stopping for the ear.
