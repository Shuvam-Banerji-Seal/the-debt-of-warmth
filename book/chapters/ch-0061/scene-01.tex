\daymark{234}{Grain 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold where it had come down on the river's next low note, and \person[Corin]{the boy} set the coordination down where a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of the work. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close the two inches to the pot handle, and both were true and neither was a sign; the two rhythms held one against the other across a wall that does not touch went onto the sixth place with the other answers, an object laid with objects, exact, complete, worth nothing. The room that warmed him and the room he could not enter kept the old wall between them. Fire went on in the one and river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

He was two hundred and thirty-four days old and six months and fifty-four days, and nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-three to two hundred and thirty-four with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. The surgeon's register took the three in at a glance and filed them apart, the way he had once filed three wounds from one blow that had no single instrument behind them.

\person{Sella Vail} had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The two rooms did not touch. In the room that was not his the fire kept its own time under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, and in the room that was not hers the river kept its low note through the gap, and between them a wall stood that had never been given a door. Nothing in his room held a count for him to borrow. He had learned to keep a rhythm without the sound, as a man keeps the step of a road he has walked a thousand times, but he borrowed nothing from the room that does not touch his and did not try. The dark went on being the dark, and the cold went on stopping where it had stopped, and he lay with the fold at his ear and listened to three things that would not become one.

Nothing from the old life broke through into the small hours. No voice came with its grammar of a different country, no lit rectangle with a name on it, no file in a white room; the surgeon was present only as a way of noticing, and the noticing was enough. He parted a colour from a hearing the way he had once parted clot from vessel, and a shape from a seventh of a note, and the figure read over a covered basket from the figure paid, and let each lie on the place it had earned. It was a dry and unsentimental business, and it cost him something that he could not name and did not try to name. He had a joke about it that he did not laugh at, because a laugh costs more than a man in his position can spare. The child in him wanted the crust and the adult kept the trays, and the two went on arguing without a word between them.

The body had its own exactness and did not care what he made of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and there was no third to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was the louder, and it had been the louder for two hundred and thirty-four days.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline and he had it still. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in a shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in all these days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for a finding kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under a shed wall that had answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and a knowing a wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind.

Beside the near-full answers stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule. A pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early. The pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since. A pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. None had been promoted into the hearings, no cause had stood up, no lesson had been made, no name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot had been lost in the yard. The burned-slate word stood at four on a place of its own, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two, with a hand between the three sayings and the tellings and no fifth and no third. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read low twice over a covered basket and still not the figure paid, while the true subtraction stayed locked in a box going up the road toward \place{High Kiln} and answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188.

Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth and the held inch with its answers on a sixth. Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, gathered at a market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, with no eighth of either, no name spoken over either, no loaf, no slate and no sale. The dressed grain lay covered near \place{the hearth} with no figure over it, counted in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight that knew weight and not cost, entered on no slate. Grain's second arithmetic stood changed and unread under this roof, and a row of seven walked-around things in \place{the Clay Yard} was still seven and not an account until somebody found a payer to stand under it, and none did.

On a seventh place of its own, with a finger between it and the hearings, the sayings, the tellings and the count, the question stood with two askings and no witness. A voice with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln and no cause had asked over the boards which row the pot had gone quiet on; the weather had answered and the price of barley had answered after it, and the voice had gone off up the terrace while the row stood where it stood. He had opened the question as a surgeon opens a wound and found no witness at the bottom. Two askings were not one witness; a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. The question was not a fourth shaping of the pot saying, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a fifth telling of the slate, not a cause, not a name and not a fifth witness. It was a seed kept on its own tray for a far-off day when somebody might stand somewhere and be asked.

\scenebreak

Sella came to the fire before she came to the basket and before she gave anything to the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and to no basket. She told the fire it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed them, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count went nowhere that any slate could read. The fire answered the telling of the thaw with four low pulls behind the wall and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing at all. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn closed on nothing when the hand had nothing to hold, and opened again as she reached for the pot.

At \place{the loaf shelf} she kept her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing without a touch. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since; there had been no opening in fifty-four days, because a morning, a shelf and a basket are not openings. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

A woman with a pot hitched against her hip came to the threshold and stood there a moment before she asked, the way people stand before they put a number into the air. “What'll the storing take,” she said, “now it's under wool? A body can't tell by looking at a fold.”

“Ask the pot,” Sella said.

“The pot doesn't keep a figure.”

“The weather knows the rest.” She said it flat, and went on turning the coals, and there was an end of it. The woman shifted the pot from hip to hip and went off down the step with her question still in her mouth, and nothing slopped, nothing was priced, refused or entered. The figure was a direction and the direction arrived nowhere. The hand was asked nothing by any of this, and the file waited where it had been laid.

The dressed grain lay under its fold near the hearth, turned and looked at for damp and gathered back by the handful, changed in state and given no figure. It had come off \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} and had been beaten out on \place{the flagstones} and winnowed so the chaff went up on the river wind; the carrying arm remembered the weight of it and not the cost, and the arm's record went into no slate. Out in the yard, when the half-pace took them through it, the seven walked-around places would get a look and a wide step and no number, and seven things standing in a row would still be seven, because a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. No payer came up \place{the clay road} on Grain 25 and none went down it.

He put the first question to the hand while the morning was still young, the first way of this chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's low note through the finger's-width door under conditions that had sometimes answered, held to the small intake only, and came down at once before the letting-go. It was an answer and it was a miss and it was both without shame, filed on the sixth place with no payer named. He did not ask the hand to extend itself by a hair's breadth beyond the inch, because a man who asks a thing to reach will make an account of the reaching and want somebody to stand under it, and nobody stood. The hand came down and lay against the fold while the fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, neither stopping for the ear. Forty-three answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

Grain 30, the third and last Account-day and market, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The ownerless question kept its two askings over the boards and was not given a third in a morning that had not asked it; that was held for the afternoon of a day still to come. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and between the two the count went forward exact and unspent into a day that had earned its heading and would not say it.
