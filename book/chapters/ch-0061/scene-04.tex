The afternoon came to its middle without a bell, because a Sixthday has no reading to mark the hour, and it came the way heat comes through a banked fire: quietly, and from a direction nobody could have pointed to beforehand. The light went flat on the front of \place{the loaf shelf} and then a little flatter. The warmth that had come up behind the pot in the morning ran down into the fold and settled there, so that the wool at \person[Corin]{the boy}'s ear was warm while the bare joint at his wrist kept its dry cold, two temperatures in one small body with a wall of wool between them he could not shift. He let the sounds arrive in the order they arrived --- the itch of coal settling, the long pull of \lore{the carried fire} behind the wall, the river's note of \lore{water over water} through the finger's-width door --- not borrowing one to hold another, the way a man lays three instruments on a towel and does not reach for a fourth to make the set look whole. Two hundred and thirty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-five days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name. The question with two askings stood over the boards and the day had not yet asked it, and he made no voice for it himself, because a voice made under this roof would have an owner and the question stood on the fact of having none.

Then, in the middle of the afternoon, it was asked.

The voice came over the boards. Not into the basket. Over the boards --- the front board of the loaf shelf where \person{Sella Vail} moved her lips along a row with two gaps in it --- and low, the way a man speaks to the weather when he has decided already that no person under the roof will answer him.

“Which row did the pot go quiet on?”

No owner stood behind it. No room, no hand at the kiln, no cause. It did not come from the fire, or the pot, or the wool, or the running water, or the river wind, all of which had their instructions and went on keeping them; it did not come into the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. It came over the boards and stopped there, low into the boards, a plain question in a plain voice, and the weather answered it first the way weather answers everything under this roof. A wrong wind got up off \place{High Kiln} and carried the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} over the boards, touched the fold at the boy's ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it, a direction arriving nowhere with nobody behind it to answer for the coming or the going. And after the weather the price of barley answered: a figure come up out of the square and going over the boards on the same small wind, arriving where the glaze had arrived, which was nowhere that any house could enter. Then the voice went off up the terrace with the barley price behind it, and the row stood where it stood. Nobody leaned in to take the answer down; there was nobody under the roof to lean, and nothing to have taken down, for the weather had answered in a smell and the barley in a figure that had no house attached to it.

\lore{The burned-slate word} came over the same boards from \place{Slate Row}, a quarter league off, and a man with a map could have put a finger on the place it came from even if no clerk could have entered it; this question had no quarter league behind it, no door, no threshold, no room where a voice had stood to form it. That was the whole of the difference, and it was enough to keep them on two trays.

He had opened this wound twice before and knew the shape of it, which did not make the third opening smaller or the finding at the bottom any different. He took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: not to close it, but to see what lay under it.

First the skin of it, which was what it asked. A row. It did not ask how the pot had gone quiet, or why, or who had carried out the man on Day 188, or what ailed him; it asked on which row, and a row is a place to stand. Under that lay where it stood. Over the boards, in the air, with nothing behind it and nothing under it. A thing that stands nowhere cannot be walked up to and cannot be asked a second question, which is a surgeon's way of saying that it is not a witness. Under that lay what it would want before any man could answer it. Somebody who had been there, who stood somewhere, who had seen which row and could be asked to say so and could be held to the saying. A witness. Under that lay the last layer, the one a man comes to only after he has opened the others and found them empty: who would have to stand under the answer to pay for it. Nobody stood. No owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, nobody to stand under the saying and nobody to be asked. He had opened it all the way down and found no payer at the bottom, which was a finding and not a disappointment, and he did not fill the empty place to make the layers come out even.

He set the third telling on the seventh place beside the first two. The first had come on Grain 21, a voice with no owner over the boards, answered with the weather and the price of barley, gone off up the terrace with the row standing where it stood. The second had come on Grain 23, in the same voice with the same absence of owner, answered the same way and gone the same way. This afternoon's had come the same way again. Three askings lay on the seventh place with a finger between each pair. He put a finger between them and the three shapings of the pot saying on the place beside, and a finger between them and the burned-slate word at its fourth telling, and a finger between them and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, and a finger between them and the six hearings of \lore{the full note} standing beside and never among, and a finger between them and the count. He had filed the proverb and the question on different trays on Grain 21 and again on Grain 23, and the third asking went onto the tray of the askings and not onto the proverb's, because three likenesses do not change what kind of instrument a thing is.

Three askings are not one witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could, so the third telling lay beside the first two as an object and not a fifth witness, as a seed for the far-off day when a witness might stand somewhere and be asked, not as anything that had grown this afternoon. It was not a fourth shaping of the pot saying, because a proverb about pots and a question about a row are different instruments and belong on different trays; a proverb says a thing over the boards and asks nothing, and the voice had asked. It was not a hearing, and not a seventh of the full note, because the six stood where they stood and no seventh had come in two hundred and thirty-five days and none had been invented to fill the empty place. It was not a fifth telling of the burned-slate word, which stood at four; it was not a third telling of the counting room shut in the war, which stood at two; it was not a cause, for nothing had been joined to it; it was not a name, for no name had been spoken over it; and it was not a fifth witness, for a witness stands somewhere and this stood nowhere. Three sayings stayed three, the burned-slate word stayed at four, the shut-room tellings stayed at two, and the question kept its three askings on a seventh place of its own with nobody to own them. He wrote nothing across any of the places.

The terrace went quiet behind the voice the way a board goes quiet after a hand has lifted from it. The row stood where it stood, which was the only answer a row could give, and it gave it to nobody.

\scenebreak

The afternoon went on at the same small pace, the way a coal goes on burning under ash with nobody turning it. Heat settled under the low angle, behind the pot and into the fold, and stopped there. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the boy's ear and would lie there into the dark; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third came to make the row come out even, because a third crust laid beside two absences would be a kindness with a figure on it and a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped where it had stopped in the small hours, at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder here in the afternoon, because a child's hunger does not keep the hours an adult keeps, and the adult's register went on being exact while the child in him went on being hungry.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow seat with its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, the clean dark line and the finer grey called \lore{the thaw} unswept, the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack, the tile debt circling without a name. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and thirty-five days, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Grain 26 and none gone down it. A keeping is a keeping by being left where it was left, and the house left him there.

At \place{the hearth} Sella told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. The fire answered the telling with four low pulls behind the wall and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing at all, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The wall stood between that count and the boy. He took its length only as a man takes the length of a shift he has worked a thousand times, and nothing crossed from that room that could be entered. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals.

The dressed grain lay near the hearth under its fold of wool. In the morning she had turned it for damp, put her fingers into it, spread it thin on the flat stone to finish, gathered it back by the handful and set the fold flat; it had changed state three times and gained no figure in any of them. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by the weight of the carrying arm, which is a record in muscle and knows weight and not cost. That arm had carried him up and down the clay road at the half-pace for two hundred and thirty-five days and had carried the grain across the yard past the racks on the same mornings, and knew the weight of both and the cost of neither, because the cost of the one had never been entered and the cost of the other was locked in a box going up toward High Kiln. Nothing had gone onto a slate, and nothing had been laid across the empty place at his ear or the empty place on the row or the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood. A keeping is a keeping by being covered or by being spread, and the grain lay back under its fold with no figure over it.

At the shelf she moved her lips along the two-gapped row without a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch. Then she kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-five days, because an afternoon, a shelf and a covered grain are not openings.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and said “Yes, house” to the wood and to nobody. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill: six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, the seventh at the lip where it could not, and the sentence about a man sent for a collector who had turned back at the frozen ford lying beside them with no name in it and never one. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach, the Seed 16 breath, and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have gone in. The loan stayed uncalled. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and went on over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. Above the shed the loft came to him as a sound with a direction and nothing more, and the warm stone lying cold in its unmarked clay pot was not reconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. His quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door, and the household straightened around it without an order. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, and gave the house no name for the men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey. He spoke to the wheel before anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back, held until the sentence reached the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. It was a remembered repetition and not a change made again, joined to no kiln and paired with no other fraction. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he did not look toward the bed or the basket.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. At every open slate she kept the half-pace and went wide. The yard went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey called the thaw beside it, unswept and kept apart by a look and a wide step, and the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates got its look and its wide step and no number. The round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood lay where the ring lay, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; at \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. Out on \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest kept its own look and its own wide step. Each of the seven got what it got and none got a figure: no name, no cause, no body, no identification of which hand, no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, and went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves.

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The two rooms did not touch. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any. No voice came over the boards in the dark. The question stood where the afternoon had left it, with three askings and nobody to own them, and he did not bring it down among the hearings or the sayings or the count to make a row look fuller.

He put the question to his hand the fourth way of this chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's low note through the finger's-width door and stayed, and he kept it there for the length of the river's own low interval, with no count in the house to borrow and nothing under the roof to hold it against --- not the small intake only, not down before the counted breath, not carried through the letting-go of a breath counted at a fire in a room that does not touch his, but its own length and its own ending --- and brought it down on the river's next low note. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. Forty-six answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at the length of two inches of air. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither stood up and said what the other was.

Two hundred and thirty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and fifty-five days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days; the widow's mark untouched with no opening in fifty-five days; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The question stood over the boards with three askings and no owner. Grain 30, the third and last Account-day and market, stood four mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand came down on the river's next low note with no count in the house borrowed for it, and the question stood over the boards with three askings and nobody to own it.
