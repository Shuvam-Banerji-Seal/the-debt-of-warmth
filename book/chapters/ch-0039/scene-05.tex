\daymark{162}{Tallow 13, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday)}

The last of the reeds had gone downriver before the boy did, and the work of them had stopped being a bundle and become a direction. \person[Corin]{The boy} felt it in the arm that carried him: not the reeds themselves, which were out of sight now, but the going down and the coming back, the two turns of the wrist that had made a morning's labour into a road the body remembered the way a dam remembered the weight of the water it had held and let go. \person{Sella Vail} carried him down the clay road to \place{the Weir} with the instruction already given to the wool and to the running water and never to the basket: \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} The arm that held him kept the weight and not the cost. When a woman coming up from the beds asked what the reed work would want set down there, Sella gave her the river and the weather, and the question went off with her, carried level in a full pail so that nothing slopped, and no entry stood anywhere. A cost asked as a direction was answered with a direction. He had once closed a chart over a finding and left the neat margin showing; he knew the shape of an answer that had had its question taken out of it when he met one, and he met one every morning under this roof.

\place{The Weir} lay one league upriver of \place{Sill}, and the boy had been carried the length of the road often enough that the place had the wrong familiarity of a room entered in the dark: \place{the plank walk} on its posts, \place[the blessing stone]{the flat blessing stone} where the hearth-maids blessed the traps, the lower boards where the eels were worked, and a landing set a hand's breadth out of line below \place{the north bridge}. \place{The Ilm} went under \place[the Singing Sill]{the sill} with the note of water going over water that had come through the finger's-width door every night of his life, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Sella set the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lay, gave the wool its instruction and the running water its own, and kept her half-pace from every open thing in the district. A basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot set down was left where it stood.

The reed-cutter came along the boards with the season finished and no bundle left to set down. On the flat stone he straightened the short length of wool-grey cord gone dark with the oil of a working thumb, turned it once so the knots stood, tied one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bit the end off, and put it back inside his coat with the flat of the hand, neither folded nor shown. A weir woman who could not read it asked what it held.

“You can ask the cord,” he said.

“And who'll answer me?” she said.

“No more can the Court,” he said, and went up the boards to lift a trap, with no anger in it and no smile, his hand to the cord before it went to the trap and before it went to the woman. No knot was counted. No figure came out of the cord into the morning. The boy kept the order of the reaching on a place of its own beside the other hands that did it, not laid across any of them, because a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart.

\person{Orra Weir} stood at the lower boards with her board on its side and a grey cord at her waist, turning it with the wrist that had turned it ten thousand times. Her hands were like old rope, the pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, salt dried white at her wrists, and the grey streak went first through the black hair when she moved her head. The nasal half-song of the Fen came into her voice where she was tired, a flat low thing that carried the words sideways. She pulled one knot down with her thumb and set it among the others with a small flat knock --- the knock of a thing laid where it belonged, which was not the small flat knock of a vessel cracked all the way through, and the boy kept the two sounds on places that did not touch.

A woman with the river in her hem asked what the levy would take now the hands were out of the reeds.

“The ninth of Ilmen,” Orra said. “The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them.”

“That isn't the levy,” the woman said.

“It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried,” Orra said, and went back to the knots.

Her thumb went to the cord again as the sentence ended. Whether that was a lie or only the habit of counting the boy did not decide, because a cause laid across the two would want a payer and no payer stood on the boards. The last knot kept its double turn legible, tail tucked, tied left over right with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in; the word that would have named it stayed where it had stayed since the year of the flower. A date laid where a figure had been asked was an answer with the question taken out of it, a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin left showing; the man at the stern who never touched the oars stood on a place of his own, apart from the midwife's reaching hand and the thane's twice-wiped thumb, and no cause was written across the three. The cord went back upriver to \place{the Eel House}, where no clerk could spell it.

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels below the boards with slime dried grey in long strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch. A weir woman asked whether the run would be thin now the reeds were out of the water.

“Thin? Who's to say thin?” Mira said. “It comes when it comes. Ask the river.”

She flicked an eel-bone against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, moved two from hand to hand and put one away when the deciding was done. A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure for two pair.

“Two marks the pair and two short,” Mira said. “You've said it twice and I've counted it twice. There's nothing in the sentence that wants an answer.”

Her thumb went under her apron to the grey cord and stayed there longer than the figure required. Shifting a creel showed the second twist twice beneath it, never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door, neither knot counted by any thumb but her own and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lay beside Orra's and did not touch it. She did not look into the basket. A keeping and a pricing were two things done with the same face turned the same way, and the boy had no word in Grathic that would hold them apart, and had given up looking for one.

\scenebreak

Below the sill, in \place{the fish-trade shade}, a woman wound a child's foot in three turns of wool and went on winding after the foot had gone dark inside them, the way a woman will go on winding when stopping would be an admission. A neighbour with a pail called it weather. The woman said nothing. The boy, carried past at two paces, read the extremity going before the core and knew the order of what would come: the vessels in that foot had gone cold and a cold vessel does not clot, and a foot in that state is not rubbed and is not set to the fire, because either brings the afterdrop on, and the afterdrop takes what the warming could not give back. He knew it as he knew the note of a pot under the knuckle, and the knowing bought nothing at two paces, in a body that could not reach the foot or speak the warning or lift the wool. No silt-leach was named, no cause and no cure, and nobody under the shade asked a man who could not answer. The afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform.

At \place{the out-of-line landing} below the bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody had called, his cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his slate turned inward to his forearm with the writing where no goer-by could read it. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, and he entered the figure before he looked at the man who had given it. Nobody had taken him off since the month turned, and the market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. A girl with a creel took the landing sideways, caught the rail with her free hand and went on selling without once looking at her feet; nobody made a fall of it and nobody helped her, and the stone lay where it lay.

The boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The note came through the day as it came through the door at night, a fifth low thing belonging to no count he could enter, and the count he kept went on where nobody could hear it.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and was not standing now, wide around the bare dark \place{trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, and wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. A turn made around a thing nobody would name was a turn and nothing more; he had learned to go around such places with her and to keep the shape of each on a tray of its own. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence, named from the hollow it left the way a missing instrument is named from the space it leaves on a towel, and it was not laid across the shelf's two gaps or the crust's empty place or the field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. No fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale off the coals, set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk was not an opening and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of the woman the brook took as the men lifted, from a person --- with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with \person{Jorra Vail}'s knowing of the pot that would sing before its time among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and no tenth standing up before the seventh year was earned. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies. The fraction from the morning before, the hand that had waited for a sentence to finish and had moved only when it was finished, stood where it had been left, held and not spoken, not a reconciliation, not a hearing, not a payment, with no payer named to it. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third; the told bell stood beside the heard bell.

On \place{the loaf shelf} the row kept its two unnamed gaps and the charcoal under its front edge waited unread for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the shelf's two gaps were not one gap twice and were not the crust's gap and were not the pan's gone place, and he set no finger between any of them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of a hand two rooms away and a new hairline of grey had gathered in the corner beside the old dark line, finer than the old and swept by nobody; a thing ground small as meal had got there by being broken, while a thing mixed was put together on purpose, and he kept the two on separate trays as he kept everything. The river's note of water over water, the fire's four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and the boards' four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb were three counts in three places and did not become one. The fire's payer had no name under any roof for a hundred and sixty-two days.

One hundred and sixty-two stood exact, complete and unspent as his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number was, which was not the same as not wanting it. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want came up small and importunate, for the crust two inches from his ear and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither; both were true and the second was the one he kept. Through the wall the fire missed its fifth under the floor, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-two days. The sentence that had been allowed to finish stood where it had been left. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand went up an inch toward the river's note and came down against the fold, and the count went on.
