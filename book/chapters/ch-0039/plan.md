# Chapter 39 — *The Sentence Finished*

**Days 160–162, Tallow 11–13, Y.H. 813, Firstday to Thirdday.** A normal chapter of three lived days, each with its own dated `\daymark` heading and each lived in full for at least 800 words, none skipped, poured into another or summarised. Day 159, the first Account-day of Tallow, is finished and is not revisited or recapped. Five scenes across the three days, 13,000 words planned. Drop capital belongs to Scene 1. The Cross is silent through these three days; the next reading is Tallow 20 (Day 169), in Chapter 40.

**Arc:** Arc 1 — *The Cold Stone*, “a warm stone, already going cold,” as in the metas of Chapters 34–38; `plan/arcs.md` needs no revision, because nothing in this chapter opens a new long-range direction. The arc's work here is to keep the count exact, the absences separate and the private records unread while the firing month gathers interest toward an edge that can be paid later.

**Meaningful change of the chapter:** the equality under the Vail roof moves by one fraction and is left unspoken. On Day 161, for the first time under this roof, **Jorra's bent finger does not lead Sella's unfinished sentence into the damper**. He carries the grey dust of the back room, speaks to the wheel, tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note and withholds it from the High Kiln kiln-master as always; but when Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his hand goes to the cold iron and *stops*. He waits. She finishes the sentence. Only then does he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave it standing. The household straightens around a sentence that was allowed to finish as it has straightened around his silences for three years, and nobody says a word of it. The boy files it as a change held and not spoken --- not a reconciliation, not a hearing, not a payment, with no payer named to it --- and keeps it on its own place. Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check; the two silences stay unjoined and the loan stays uncalled. This is a fraction, not a settlement: *he's not Vail* stays sealed, the promise to the slate-burners stays ungiven, and no bond changes hands.

**Secondary texture, not a resolution:** the working season deepens. The kiln's breath goes on in fours with its gap; the dust ground small as meal, the pot that sings before its time, the man in Mud End taken quiet in his sleep and the carried fire's silence all go on gathering interest. No figure is put to the Mud End death, no payer is named to the fire's fifth, and no lesson is made of any of it.

**Hard withholdings (continue from Ch 38):** no calling of the loan, no tenth stopping, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no giving or cutting of the seventh year, no seventh hearing of the full note, no name or number from Dessa, no silt-leach/cause/cure for the cold foot, no sale or betrayal paid, no opening of tin or box, no reading of charcoal or any private tally, no joining of the pan's gone place to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust or the field's empty place; no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause. Marra stays out of Sill; Alda offstage and her mercy-lie uncorrected; Dessa stays dead and unnamed to the boy; the foundling stays offstage (the sixth glimpse has landed and must not be spent); the green is never Fenna in narration; Berr's box stays locked and is offstage between Account-days; *he's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the foundling's name stays ungiven. Hessa Rale is held for Chapter 40. Old life offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break, no departure from the boy's eyes.

## Scene 1 — The Working Day After the Reading

- **Day and time:** Day 160, Tallow 11, Firstday, before first grey through mid-morning.
- **Place:** under the Vail kiln-house roof, the hearth, the kiln mouth, the Clay Yard and the yard well.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; yard women at the well and racks as walk-ons.

### Beats, in order
1. Open with the boy on his bed under the roof, fold at his ear and under his chin, one stiff brown-edged crust two inches away with its empty place unnamed, the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and coming down against the fold. One hundred and sixty days old, five months and ten days; nobody says so. Do not recap the Account-day.
2. The first smell is hot clay under tallow; the first sound is the kiln's breath with its gap, not a bell. Sella feeds the fire under the back of a broken pot; it answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty days.
3. The widow's mark hangs where it hung. She gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket, says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, and keeps the half-pace from every open slate. A shelf, a morning and a well are not openings; do not let her touch the mark.
4. In the yard the women go around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the rack space with its broken birth-slates and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out --- a look and a wide step and no number.
5. At the trough by the well the water stands bare and dark; the skin of ice is not revived and the water is called weather to nobody in particular. The clay jar stands cold under its glaze, glanced at once and not touched.
6. Tarn takes the broom only after it is instructed: six counted where the house can check, the seventh at the lip of the sill where it cannot, no collector on the road, nine stoppings still nine and the loan uncalled. His stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check.
7. The boy reads the firing as he reads an operating theatre he cannot enter: heat kept and counted behind a screen, a gate with two bites, a cost paid by a man whose name is not spoken. He reaches an inch toward the water and comes down with one hundred and sixty exact and unspent.

### Emotional turn
The first ordinary morning after a public reading: nothing in the house has changed by the figure that went up the road, and the day's danger has moved into the room the boy is carried through but cannot enter. The turn is from the yard's busy heat back to the small private cold of the count.

### Sensory anchors
Hot clay under tallow; wet wool; four low pulls and the missing fifth; the bare dark trough; dried slip on forearms; Ash worked grey into Tarn's knuckles; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist; the river note of water over water through the finger's-width door; four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb overhead.

## Scene 2 — What the Fire Keeps

- **Day and time:** Day 160, Tallow 11, Firstday, afternoon into the going of the light and the small hours.
- **Place:** under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf from the basket's low angle, and the hearth.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, a neighbour with a wet pot (walk-on).

### Beats, in order
1. The household's arithmetic stays in loaves pushed along a board, one glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The boy keeps one hundred and sixty exact against it and nobody tells him what a number is.
2. A neighbour with a wet pot asks what a firing costs set down there; Sella sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and carries the unanswered question away level so nothing slops.
3. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, Sella reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies, without looking and without a word, brings it away and does not go back to the shelf. The boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say: such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two. Do not read, copy or join the marks.
4. The shelf keeps two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6; no third comes. In the other room one stiff brown-edged crust lies two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, unnamed and with no third. The pan's gone place keeps the round shape of its absence on a place of its own and is not laid across the shelf's gaps, the fold's crust, the field's empty place, the sixth green as a colour joined to no ear or the foundling as a shape with no sale. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer; no finger of his draws the line.
5. At the going of the light she sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needs before it goes away.
6. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak pale off the coals), sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and does not look toward the bed. She does not touch the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening. Four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty days. The two rooms do not touch.
7. In the small hours he turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh and none invented to fill the place; three near-full answers advanced and not paid, Jorra's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings; the older intervals at Stone 13, 17 and 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the unchecking stroke; nine stoppings with no payer named to any and no tenth before the seventh year is earned; the damper's double bite joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the told bell beside the heard bell. Three counts in three places --- river, fire and boards --- do not become one.

### Emotional turn
A hand goes beneath a board and comes back, and the meaning stays exactly where it was. The boy's inventory of absences grows by care rather than by discovery: he keeps things apart so no account can stand up out of them. The half-beat at the setting is the only thing in the room that admits anything.

### Sensory anchors
Loaves pushed along a shelf; a glance at a cold clay jar; charcoal clusters under a front edge, some pressed hard, some faded into the grain; the little-door burn closing on the pot; tallow, woodsmoke, wet wool; the silence where the fifth pull should have been; the pot handle standing two inches beyond the hand.

### Foreshadowing planted or paid off
Gathered, nothing paid: the finger-under-board habit and the unread clusters toward the far-off hand that is not his; the absences toward the day a house is left where it is and still kept; the ninth stopping and the pressed seventh year toward the day something must give; the carried fire's unnamed payer toward Sella's second roll. No third gap and no fourth likeness.

## Scene 3 — The Sentence Finished

- **Day and time:** Day 161, Tallow 12, Secondday, before first grey into the morning, with a short return under the roof at midday.
- **Place:** the Vail wheel-shed, the flagstones, the cold damper, the drying racks, under the Vail roof.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Jorra Vail, Tarn, a High Kiln kiln-master (walk-on), three or four unnamed men (walk-ons, not followed or entered).

### Beats, in order
1. Before first grey three or four men go low-voiced up the Clay Yard and into the finger's-width-open back room; Sella carries the basket past at the half-pace, turns no head, gives the house no name for them and does not say the room is cold. The two shut-room tellings stay side by side with no third.
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds that knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who says the pot is singing before its time and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. The knowing goes to the near-full tray, beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.
4. **The change:** Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes to the cold iron and STOPS. He stands with his hand on it while she finishes the sentence, and only then moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing. The household straightens around a sentence that was allowed to finish; no one says a word of the waiting, no one looks at the boy, and no explanation is offered to the basket. The boy files it on a place of its own as a change held and not spoken --- not a reconciliation, not a hearing, not a payment, no payer named to it --- and the equality under the roof has moved by that much. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise to the slate-burners stays ungiven.
5. Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it stopped; neither man asks about the ear or the room and the two silences stay unjoined. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed, six counted where the house can check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it cannot, the small knife at the very edge, Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled.
6. The boy reads one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln beside the two shut-room tellings and the damper's silence. The pan's gone place, the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust and its empty place and the field's empty place stay on separate trays; no third gap comes, no fourth likeness is made a cause, and the charcoal waits unread.
7. The boy is one hundred and sixty-one days old and nobody says so. His hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down with the count unspent.

### Emotional turn
The house has learned to read a lowered voice and a waiting hand as weather signals for three years. This time the hand waits for the sentence instead of correcting it, and the change is so small that nobody can be made to name it. The boy, who has learned that a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart, keeps this fraction by itself.

### Sensory anchors
Fine grey slate dust ground small as meal; bone-coloured slip; the sweetish lead edge on a High Kiln wind; cold iron taking the heat out of a fingertip; the bent finger's stop against the damper; the small flat knock of a pot's true note, known and unsaid; the round empty ring on the wall.

### Threads advanced
T035/T031, T062, T061/T082, T065/T095, T036/T076, T060/T086, T097, T100, T088, T071/T072 (161 exact).

### Foreshadowing planted or paid off
Gathered, nothing paid: the dust and the unnameable back room toward the Broken Slates; the damper's double bite and the fraction at the iron toward the day something must give at the end of the seventh year; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the withheld full note toward the far-off hand that will lay the private records together. The firing's danger gathers through the kiln-master's saying without a pot being lost in this yard.

### Exact last image
The bent finger leaves the damper standing after the sentence has been finished, and through the wall the fire answers four low pulls with the fifth missing, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days; the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the river's note and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 4 — Nine, Still Nine

- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; no yard neighbours on stage this evening.

### Beats, in order
1. Sella moves her lips along the loaf row standing with two unnamed gaps, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, sends a woman asking what the firing costs to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and gives nobody a figure. She does not go back to the shelf that night; the charcoal stays where it is, unread and unjoined.
2. Four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days.
3. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction and says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument can find a foot, while going on wanting the late because it puts off the paying. His stroke goes over the collector's road without turning along it; no collector comes.
4. At the kindling, with the coals banked and the small knife set back at the very edge of its sill --- six counted where the house can check, the seventh where it cannot, Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it --- he tells the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count.* The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it is **not** brought to the word that would call the loan on this day; it stays with the seventh year, unspoken and unentered. The Seed 16 breath stays unspent, the chest stays tight, Ash stays worked into his knuckle creases, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back.
5. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room. Upstairs neither man's silence is joined to the other's. No tenth stopping stands up to join the nine; a thing held nine times is still owed.
6. In the small hours he turns out his file: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh and none invented; three near-full answers advanced and not paid, with Jorra's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings; the older intervals; nine stoppings as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and no tenth before the seventh year is earned; the damper's double bite joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the told bell beside the heard bell; one crust and its gap, two shelf gaps, the pan's gone place, a sixth green as a colour and a sixth half-turn as a shape, all on places of their own. An inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand.
7. One hundred and sixty-one stands exact, complete and unspent as his hand goes up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold. Cold arrives in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back; want comes up small and importunate for the crust and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither.

### Emotional turn
The day's one fraction of change is not spoken and not repeated; the household settles back into its grammar, and the boy's file comes back exact and worthless. The silence is not peace: it is the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

### Sensory anchors

## Scene 5 — A Date Where a Figure Was Asked

- **Day and time:** Day 162, Tallow 13, Thirdday, morning into the going of the light downriver at the Weir, and the small hours under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards, the fish-trade shade below the sill, the out-of-line landing below the north bridge, the clay road home, under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Mira Silt, Orra Weir; the reed-cutter, the soft-cuffed clerk, a weir woman who asks about the run, an eel buyer, a woman winding a child's foot and a neighbour with a pail (walk-ons). The foundling is offstage; the green is never Fenna in narration; Hessa Rale is held for Chapter 40.

### Beats, in order
1. The reed work is finished and has become a direction rather than a bundle: the going downriver and the coming back are counted in the arm that carries them, and a cost asked as a direction is answered with the river and the weather and leaves no entry. The arm keeps the weight and not the cost.
2. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies, gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket, and keeps her half-pace.
3. The reed-cutter straightens his grey cord on the flat blessing stone, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and puts it back inside his coat with the flat of the hand. To the woman who asks what it holds: *You can ask the cord* and *No more can the Court*. No knot counted, no figure read.
4. Orra, hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered, salt white at her wrists, the nasal half-song of the Fen where she is tired, pulls one knot down with a small flat knock --- the knock of a thing laid where it belongs, kept apart from the small flat knock of a vessel cracked all the way through. To the woman who asks what the levy will take now the hands are out of the reeds she gives the date: the ninth of Ilmen, the collector's boat round the bend on the flood with six oars and one man at the stern who never touched them; to *That isn't the levy*, *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* The forty-first double turn stays legible, tail tucked, left over right with the weight of a thumb; the word forty is unspoken, and whether her thumb's return is a lie or the habit of counting the boy does not decide.
5. Mira works the eels with slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. She answers the thin-run question with her own question and answer (*Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river*), flicks eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, and to the buyer's figure says *Two marks the pair and two short. You've said it twice and I've counted it twice. There's nothing in the sentence that wants an answer.* Her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; the second twist stays twice beneath the shifting creel, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's and not touching.
6. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool; the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform. The surgeon reads the extremity going before the core, knows a cold vessel cannot clot and that a frozen foot is not rubbed or set to the fire, and the reading buys nothing at two paces. No silt-leach, cause or cure is named.
7. At the out-of-line landing the soft-cuffed clerk stays set for a measure nobody called, his cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his slate turned inward and his hand to it before his eyes to the stalls. Nobody has taken him off; the market sells over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies; a girl with a creel takes the landing sideways, catches the rail and goes on selling without looking at her feet, and nobody makes a fall of it.
8. At the going of the light Sella carries him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, going wide around the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and is not standing now, around the bare dark trough at the well and around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, giving no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. ## The pull into Chapter 40

Forward, not closed.
