The light went off the \place{Clay Yard} in its usual order, leaving the racks first and the pots' bellies next and the clean dark line at the corner last, because that line had been gone around so many evenings that the dark came to it as to a place already known. \person[Corin]{The boy} came under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in the crook of \person{Sella Vail}'s arm, basket on hip, with the instruction already given to the wool and the pot and never to him: \emph{Keep it off his ears. It’s the river wind.} She set him low at the angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, base first and weight settled, and gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody, and the basket got none of it. A basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot set down was left where it stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat of the yard and nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed as it was, a small white line drawn across the day's end. Through the finger's-width door the low note of water going over water came and went to the wall and stayed there, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings.

She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row. The row stood with two gaps and she named neither: the old space at the end where a loaf had once lain, and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 and stayed. Her lips went over them the way a hand goes over a seam it knows by heart, neither stopping nor hurrying, and when she came to the end she began again. She glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze against the wall, the way a hand goes to an instrument before a case is opened, and away. The charcoal lay under the front edge of the board in clusters, some pressed hard into the grain and some faded until they were almost the grain itself, and she did not look at it and did not go back to the shelf that night. The two gaps were not one gap twice. The old space at the row's end was a thing the planting had brought in, spoken of by women who had stood at this board for twenty years; the palm's breadth had come in on a night and stayed, and neither had a name a woman would put on a slate. He kept them on two places and set no finger between them, because a cause drawn across two unlike absences wants a payer, and there was no payer under this roof who would answer to one.

A woman came up from \place[the worked strips]{the strips} with a wet pot in her hands and asked what a firing cost set down there.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.”

She carried the unanswered question away level, the way a woman carries a full dish across a room, so that nothing slopped, and the woman went off with her pot and no figure, and the row went on standing with two gaps. She had given nobody a number and had not touched \lore{the widow's mark}, because an evening at a shelf was not an opening and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The household had straightened around the fraction at \lore{the damper} that morning and had settled back into its grammar by the going of the light, and nothing in the evening repeated it. There was no word in the room about a hand that had waited for a sentence to finish. He had put the fraction on a place of its own and left it there --- a change held and not spoken, not a reconciliation, not a hearing, not a payment --- and a thing held once was not a rule any more than nine held a tenth, and no man under the roof could have been made to say what had moved.

\scenebreak

At the hearth she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. She had said it was the thaw for three years and the fire had never answered with the word, only with heat kept and counted behind the screen of the pot's belly, four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the way she had stood over the winter her own child cooled, when everybody had been kind and the child had gone cold all the same. With the wrist that carries the little-door burn she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. The pot rim she set out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand that would not close on it, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on the wood and opened again, shutting and opening, and it was the same hand that had felt for the heat at his belly on the night he came under the roof and found none and opened and shut once over the bundle. She had never told him what she had found, and he had never asked.

Four low pulls came through the floor and the silence where the fifth should have been, and the boy kept the count on its own place beside the two others in the house --- the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door, the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb --- and none of the three became one. The fire kept its accounts the way this house kept everything: in heat held under a broken pot and in one place left exactly as it was. He had learned to read such rooms as a theatre he could not enter, heat kept and counted behind a screen, a gate with two bites, a cost paid by a man whose name was never said in his hearing, and he stood at the edge of it with a towel in hands too small to hold one.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody, and he took the tool up as a man takes up a tool already named, and said, “Yes, house.” He said it to the ground and to the going of the light and to the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late, and agreed with both so entirely that no argument could find a foot anywhere in the room. He went on wanting the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. In the clay pot in the loft above the shed he kept a warm stone she had given him in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, two years gone and cold long since, and he could no more reconcile the stone with the debt than he could reconcile the house's kindness with the account he kept of it, and had stopped trying, which was not the same as forgiving.

His stroke went out over \place{the flagstones} and across the shallow bright patch where it had once stopped and gone on from, and it went over the patch now without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped as a thing nobody entered. The road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector ran off the yard end and down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, and his broom went over it and over it without once turning along it, and no collector came. He did not ask about the room. The wheel-keeper had not asked about the ear, and the two silences lay under the one roof like two tools laid on one towel that nobody had put together. The unforged collector sentence of the dead man stood where he kept it, beside the knife and the seventh year, and neither was entered.

At the kindling, with the coals banked under the back of the broken pot, he set the small knife back at the very edge of its sill where six were counted where the house could check and the seventh stood where the house could not. Six years stood in a thumb-mark worn almost flat, and the seventh stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He told the wood what he told it every evening. “Six counted. The next I don't count.”

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came as far as his teeth and no further. It had come that far before and had never gone into the word that would call the loan, and it did not go into it now. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the breath from Seed 16 stood unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in. The sentence stayed with the seventh year, unsaid and unentered. Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. She had heard that sentence come to the teeth before and had never once asked what stood behind them, and the boy had learned from her that a question not asked is a thing a house can keep for years without its becoming an entry. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Nothing stood up out of the nine stoppings to join them as a tenth, and a thing held nine times was still owed. The silence under the roof was not peace; it was the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the boy turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of the woman the brook took as the men lifted, from a person --- with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing withheld from the man on the wrong wind, all of them advanced and not paid, not hearings and not entered. \person{Jorra Vail}'s knowing of the pot that would sing before its time was among these and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check. The order of the reaching had not changed in all his days here: the hand to the record before the hand to the person, the slate before the child, the figure before the face, in the midwife bending over the bench, in the thane passing a finger over a figure, in the clerk with his slate turned inward below the north bridge, in her own finger going beneath the board. Eight hands in one district performing one grammar and not one of them explaining it, each on a place of its own, and the telling of the bell stood beside the hearing of it with nobody making the two agree. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings, each an object and not a rule, an interval and not a payment, no payer named to any and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man, set where it was needed and not asked to mean more. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third.

The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own. The loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third coming; the empty place in the returning line of the worked strips, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, stayed where it was, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number; the sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale; the sixth half-turn stayed a shape and not a hearing. An inventory was not a key and a list was not a hand. He had learned in this house that a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart, and he held these apart, exact, complete and worth nothing.

One hundred and sixty-one stood exact, complete and unspent as his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number was, which was not the same as not wanting it. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want came up small and importunate, for the crust two inches from his ear and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither, and both were true and the second was the one he kept.

Through the wall the fire missed its fifth under the floor, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days. The sentence that had been allowed to finish that morning stood where it had been left, held and not spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand went up an inch toward the river's note and came down against the fold, and the count went on.
