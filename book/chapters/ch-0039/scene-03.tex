\daymark{161}{Tallow 12, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

Before first grey the \place{Clay Yard} lay under a sky the colour of wet ash, and the pots on \place[the drying racks]{the racks} stood with their bellies full of the dark, each one holding its small private cold until somebody put a hand to it. \person[Corin]{The boy} came out from under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in the crook of \person{Sella Vail}'s arm, basket on hip, with the wool tucked at his ears and the instruction already given to the wool and not to him: \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} The door stood a finger's width open, as it had stood a finger's width open for three years, and the low note of water going over water came through that finger's width, went to the wall, and stayed there, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings.

Three men came up the yard before the light, and a fourth behind them. They came with their collars high against the sweetish edge that was on the wind --- \place{High Kiln} breathing over the roofs, \lore{lead glaze} gone metallic on a wrong wind --- and went low-voiced toward the finger's-width-open door of \place{the back room} off \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed}, and did not look at the basket. Sella carried past them at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, turned no head toward the door, gave \place{Low Kiln} no name for the men, and did not say that the room was cold. Her little-door burn closed on the wicker and opened again. A basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot set down was left where it stood, and nothing that had been said to the wool needed saying to a basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the sentence about the men went nowhere at all.

The boy kept the two tellings on a tray of their own. A man come downriver from \place{Greybank} had said it twice, on Ilmen 26 and again on Silt 25, of a counting room shut in the last war with the \lore{damper} closed and the men inside dead of the breath, eighteen leagues east; each time he said it as a curiosity with a date, and each time the yard took it for history and went back to its pots. Two tellings stood side by side with no third. The back room off the wheel-shed was a door that kept its finger's width of open and men who went low into it, and no telling at all had been made of that; he laid no finger of his between the room and the story. A likeness laid down by hand was a likeness held. A likeness left to lie was a cause waiting for a payer.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with the day's dust on him. It was fine grey slate dust ground small as meal, worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel: a thing ground small as meal had got that way by being broken, while a thing mixed was put together on purpose, and the boy kept the two states of one pair of forearms on one tray without asking which slate had been broken or whether it had been a birth-slate. The dust went into the creases and stayed there, the colour of a swept floor seen in a bad light, and the bent finger kept its bend whatever was on it, pointing the way a hinge points whether the gate is open or shut. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, where the yard went around them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered them into nothing. He wanted the two questions the way a man wants an instrument that is not on the towel, and kept them where questions can last for years.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He said it to the wheel the way another man says a thing to an animal he means to work, with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and the wheel gave back nothing. He set a pot on the bench and turned it a quarter, and told it, “You'll do.” The boy knew the full note that pot would give before it left the wheel, and the knowing went where such knowing always went in this house: onto the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, a thing known and not given, as a man knows a finding before he sets his name to the chart and leaves the margin clean. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the knowing that would not turn it into an account, and he did not make one.

\scenebreak

A kiln-master came up from High Kiln on the wrong wind before the pot had left the bench, with \lore{Ash} pale on his cuffs and that sweetish edge at the corner of his mouth. He stood a moment with his hands behind him, looking at the pot the way a man looks at a thing he has come to put a price on. “That one's singing before its time,” he said. “A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early.”

Jorra did not look at him and did not look at the boy. He put his hand back on the wheel. “It's a Vail pot,” he said.

“I didn't say it wasn't.”

“You said it early.”

The kiln-master let the sentence carry away down the wind and went off toward the racks with his cuffs pale and his bag at his side, and no pot was lost in the yard that morning and nothing was made of the saying. The boy kept the withheld knowing where it belonged. A knowing held back is not a hearing given; six hearings stood at six from four sources with a sixth from a person, and no seventh came to stand among them and none was invented to fill the empty place. He did not call the kiln-master a fool or the wheel-keeper a liar. Both men had said what their places required, and the finding stood on its own tray with nobody's name on it.

\scenebreak

The damper was cold. It had been cold since the coals were banked and would stay cold until the feeding, and Jorra went to it the way a man goes to a thing he knows by the feel of it in the dark, with the bent finger leading as it had led every morning for three years. Sella stood with her back to the basket and her hand on the back of the broken pot that kept the carried fire under its belly, and she was telling him what the house told him every morning.

“The fire's low,” she said. “It wants---”

And there it stopped. The rest of the sentence did not come. It stood in the room with nobody to carry it, the way a spoon stands in a dish that has been set down.

On every morning of the boy's life the bent finger had gone into the iron before that sentence was finished. He knew the order of it as he knew the order of the kiln's four: the finger going in ahead of the hand, the tongue of the iron moving a hair's breadth, the end of her instruction arriving at the pot instead of at the man, and the household straightening around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. An obedience that was also a correction neither of them would have named. A hand that knows where the iron stands even when it is held back from the iron. He had watched the lowering of the man's voice for three years the way the whole household had, as a weather signal, and had learned to read a lowered voice and a waiting hand as signs of what the day would do.

This morning the finger went to the cold iron and stopped.

It went as far as the iron and no further. The cold took the heat out of the fingertip --- cold iron does that, takes and does not give back until a hand has stayed a while and the iron has consented to give --- and the finger lay on it without moving, and the man stood over it with his weight on the balls of his feet the way he stood at the wheel when a pot was going crooked and he wanted it to come right by his not touching it. He did not look at Sella. He did not look at the basket. He did not move the tongue. The room went on around the waiting: the kiln breathing behind the wall, the river going over water beyond the door, Sella's hand on the broken pot with the little-door burn shutting and opening, shutting and opening, on the wood.

Sella's hand stayed where it was. She had begun a sentence and the sentence was hers to finish, and for the first time under this roof nobody took it off her before she had.

“It wants feeding,” she said.

Only then did the tongue of the iron move a hair's breadth, and stand where it had been moved.

The household straightened around a sentence that had been allowed to finish as it had straightened around his silences for three years. No one said a word about the waiting. No one looked into the basket to see whether the waiting had been seen. No explanation was given to the wool, the pot, the fire, or anybody. The pot that had been singing before its time went on standing on the bench with its note still in it, known and withheld and standing.

The boy put the fraction on a place of its own. It was a change held and not spoken: not a reconciliation, not a hearing, not a payment, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. He had learned in this house that a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart, and he held this one apart from the finger that had led at the damper on every other morning and from the half-check of Tarn's stroke on the flagstones. The equality under the roof had moved by that much, and the movement was so small that no man under the roof could have been made to name it. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken in, and the promise of a name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. Nothing had been settled. Nothing had been given. A sentence had been allowed to finish, and a hand had waited for it, and that was the whole of what had happened. He filed it and went on filing, the way he filed the rest: exact, complete, worth nothing, and kept.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody, and he took the tool up as a man takes up a tool already named, and said, “Yes, house.” He said it to the ground and not to the early thaw or the late, because nobody had spoken of the thaw that morning; the wanting stood all the same, the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not; the small knife lay at the very edge with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it and the breath from Seed 16 unspent, with its small interval at the top of each reach. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came as far as the teeth and no further, and was not brought to the word that would call the loan on this morning.

His stroke went out over \place{the flagstones} and across the shallow bright patch where it had stopped on the first fraction of change and gone on from, and it went over the patch without the half-check now, as it had gone every morning since, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped as a thing nobody entered. The road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector ran off the yard end and down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, and his broom went over it and over it without once turning along it, and no collector came. He did not ask about the room, and Jorra did not ask about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under one roof with nobody putting a word between them. Jorra's bent finger stood where it had been left in the iron, and the end of Sella's sentence had gone to the man it was said to. Nothing had been called. The loan that could hear was uncalled, and the ninth stopping stood where it stood with no tenth stood up to join it.

\scenebreak

At midday Sella carried him in from the racks and set him low near the bench leg at the angle where the underside of \place[the loaf shelf]{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, and gave the instruction to the wool and the pot and the broom, and the basket got none of it. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze against the wall and her eye went to it once and away, the way a hand goes to an instrument before a case is opened. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap, four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days and not named now. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water going over water, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one.

The iron at the damper was one piece with two bites, and the boy kept it on a place of its own. One bite held a vessel whole; the other could take the hand off a man. There was no word in Grathic that would hold both mercies in one breath --- the language had a word for the holding and a word for the taking and nothing that stood across the two --- and he had learned early not to make one, because a word made across two unlike things is a doctrine with a payer already found. The damper stood where it had been left, the tongue a hair's breadth from where it had been moved, and he filed it as an object joined to no kiln, beside the two shut-room tellings with no third and beside the silence where the fire's fifth should have been. A clamp was a clamp and not a doctrine. Set where it was needed, it was neither mercy nor mutilation until a hand put a name to it, and no hand under this roof had put one.

The round bare ring on the wall where a shallow clay pan had stood out to dry through Reed kept the shape of its absence on a tray of its own. The loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal under its front edge waited unread for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. In the fold two inches from his ear one stiff brown-edged crust kept its empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came. The empty place in the returning line of \place{the worked strips}, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, stayed where it was, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number. None of these was laid across another. The pan's gone place was not the shelf's two gaps; the fold's empty place was not the field's empty place; the sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer, and no finger of his drew the line.

\scenebreak

The boy was one hundred and sixty-one days old and five months and eleven days, and nobody under the roof said so, and nobody under the roof would have known what to do with the saying if a man had put the words into their mouths. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board and in a glance at a clay jar cold under its glaze and in a space at the row's end that no woman called a number. His own count had neither rim nor handle and would not have sat among them. It stood where it stood. It went on.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want came up small and importunate for the crust two inches from his ear and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither, and both were true and the second was the one he kept. One hundred and sixty-one stood exact, complete and unspent.

Through the wall the fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-one days. The bent finger stood where it had been left at the damper, the tongue a hair's breadth from where it had been moved, and the sentence that had been finished stood with it, held and not spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
