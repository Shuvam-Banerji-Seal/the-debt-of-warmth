\daymark{160}{Tallow 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

\dropcap{B}{efore first grey} the room held the shape of the night pressed into it: the hollow of \place{the bed}, the crease of the fold, the small warm place where a head had lain and was lying still. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold, gone leathery through the dry weeks, and the empty place beside it had no name and kept no third company. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on his back under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof}, and the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and kept its line, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. He was one hundred and sixty days old and five months and ten days, and nobody under that roof said so, and nobody under that roof would have known what to do with the saying if a man had put the words into their mouths. The adult who had once known the weight of a body on a table and the exact order of its failing watched the room through a child's eyes and could not lift his own head; the child who lived in those eyes was cold and wanted the breast and the crust, and did not know that either had ever been refused him. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in a glance at a clay jar, in a space at the end of a row that no woman called a number. His count had neither rim nor handle and would not have sat among them. It stood where it stood. It went on.

Beyond the wall \place{the Clay Yard} was coming up into the hot dry smell of clay giving up its water. That was the first smell of the day, hot clay under tallow, and the first sound was not a bell but the kiln breathing in fours with a gap, four low pulls and a silence where a fifth should have been, a breath the yard had long ago stopped listening for. In his bed he could not see the mouth of it, but he knew the shape of the heat behind the brick the way a man knows the shape of a thing from the sound it makes when it is shut away: kept, and counted, and not to be gone into. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. He had counted them in the dark and would count them again. Three counts in three places --- the river, the fire, the boards --- and none of them became one. A fourth likeness would make a cause, and a cause would want a payer. No finger of his drew the line.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came in from the yard with the basket on her hip, the little-door burn closing on the wicker handle and opening again, and she did not look into the basket. She gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom before she gave it to anybody, and never gave it to the basket, which was a vessel filled and carried and had no instruction of its own to answer. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To \person{Tarn} in the doorway she said the house wanted the yard swept before the sun came on \place{the flagstones} --- not \emph{I} want it, not \emph{you} sweep it --- and Tarn said “Yes, house” as though the sentence had been finished for him before he was born. She kept the half-pace from every open slate on the way across the room, and there was no slate in the room, and she kept it all the same.

At her throat \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it had hung since the first firing of the month, a thumb-length of clay under its glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it. A shelf was not an opening. A morning was not an opening. A well was not an opening. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and there had been no opening under this roof since the month came up, and there was none this morning. Her eye went once to the clay jar that stood cold under its glaze against the wall, the way a hand goes to an instrument before a case is opened, and went away, and she did not touch the jar and did not go back to it.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving on a row that stood in some other room of her head; then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple pale off the coals, tipped the carried coal under the back of the pot and set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. The fire took the coal the way a mouth takes food given to it, without thanks and without question, and answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. It had missed the fifth for a hundred and sixty days and no payer had been named to the silence. She did not look toward the bed. She did not touch the mark, because a hearth was not a door and a morning was not an opening.

\scenebreak

\place{the Clay Yard} had come up in the night into the hot dry smell of clay giving up its water, and the women went around what they would not enter as they had gone around it every morning since the kilns were brought up for the year's last working season. There was the clean dark line in the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, and neither had been swept. There was the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and the yard went around it as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. And there was the empty place in the returning line of \place{the worked strips} where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out: a woman looked once at the space between two stooping backs, took a wide step and said nothing, and another took the step behind her without looking at all. No number came out of the looking.

“That's where it was,” the first woman said low, to the ground rather than to the girl beside her, and set her foot down beyond the space. The girl went on with her hands in the slip. The name, the body, the cause, the figure and the payment had not come out of that place in all the days since, and the boy held the absence on a place of its own and did not lay it across \place{the loaf shelf}'s two gaps, the empty place at his ear, or the round bare ring on the wall where a pan had stood out to dry through Reed. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer; no finger of his drew the line.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the \place{the trough} stood bare and dark under its little roof, with the skin of ice that had covered it through the lean weeks gone these days and not revived, and the water in it was called weather to nobody in particular. One woman dipped a palm and tasted and said it was different, and another said Different from what, and a third said weather, and the argument ended not in a winner but in nobody left to carry it, the change standing as a shape with no name and no cause entered in no slate. Sella passed with the basket and gave the instruction to the running water and the wool and never to the basket, and kept the half-pace from every open slate, and went around the trough as wide as she went around the rack space, and gave no explanation of the turn to the basket. A basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot set down was left where it stood.

\scenebreak

Tarn took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before; he held it a moment with the small tool he had put into his pocket when he thought nobody looked, and put the tool back before anybody could check whether it had ever been away. To a woman who wanted the thaw early and a man who wanted it late he said, “Yes, house,” to both, agreeing so thoroughly that the argument had nowhere to put a foot, while wanting the late one in the private part of his head because a late thaw puts off the paying. His seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and was still held, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not, the one entry in this house which the house had never checked. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it, and the breath from Seed 16 was still unspent, standing as a small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in and had not. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood and no tenth stood up before the seventh year had been earned; a tenth stood up now would be a debt invented to make the nine into a bill. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it stayed where he kept it, unsaid. The loan that could hear was uncalled. He said nothing of any of it, and Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing, and the two silences under the one roof stayed unjoined.

\scenebreak

Mid-morning came up under the roof and the boy was carried through the door again in the basket, and he read the firing as he read an operating theatre he could not enter. Heat was kept and counted behind a screen of brick, and there was a gate with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath. He had once stood with a towel in his hands in a room where heat was kept behind a screen, and had watched a man stand at the gate and not go in, and a cost was paid there by a man whose name was not spoken. The grammar of it was older than this house: the name taken out of the sentence, the hand out of the field, the cost out of the account. Here the cost went up in four low pulls and a silence and was named by nobody for a hundred and sixty days. He filed the iron as an object joined to no kiln, beside the two shut-room tellings and the told bell standing beside the heard bell, and did not let it stand up as a rule. A rule wants a payer, and there was no payer named to any of it.

Near the basket's rim the air was warm from the hearth and cold from the door at the same time, and the note of water going over water came through the finger's-width door and went under the warmth and stayed. The adult who had once laid a chart closed over its finding and gone away leaving the neat margin showing watched the house keep its accounts in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, and knew that nothing under this roof had changed by the figure that had gone up the clay road. The child's body was carried under a board and was cold, and wanted the crust two inches from his ear and the pot handle standing beyond the reaching hand and the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got none of them and could not ask for any. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want came up small and importunate and was not answered. Both were true and the second was louder.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold with one hundred and sixty exact, complete and unspent in the dark. The fire missed its fifth under the floor and its payer was unnamed for a hundred and sixty days, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come.
