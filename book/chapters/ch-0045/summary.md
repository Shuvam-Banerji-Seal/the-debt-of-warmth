## Chronicle

The middle days of the kiln month came on under the Vail roof, the boy one hundred and eighty-six days old and told by nobody. Heat arrived before the grey: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, the sour breath of the unswept corner. The kiln drew breath in fours with a gap; the carried fire under the floor answered four low pulls and missed its fifth, its payer unnamed. Sella Vail told the wool, fire, pot, broom and running water, never the basket, said *the house*, set the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and left the widow's mark where it hung, for a morning and a shelf are not openings. River, fire and boards kept three counts in three places; none became another.

On the eighth morning three or four men went low into the back room and got no name. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms, told a pot it would do, knew its note three turns ahead and withheld it from a man on a wrong wind; his bent finger stood in the cold iron until Sella's unfinished sentence reached the pot. At the well a glaze-room hand from High Kiln drank with the sweet on his breath, and the pale line showed wet white at his gum. The boy read the line, the held hand, the pale nails and the short breath from two paces --- exact, complete, worth nothing --- and filed it where a danger goes. A neighbour asking a cost was sent to the pot and the weather.

On the ninth morning a rack-worker's pot gave its note before its time and then sang itself out early, and the woman went on with her hands and made no lesson of it. Two men carried the glaze-room hand out of the kiln and across the yard, one with a thumb under his arm to keep the head from lopping, the other at the feet, the man still breathing. Sella turned the boy's face as she would turn a pot to the light, went wide around the place the carriers crossed, and gave no name for what ailed him. The boy read a man with lead in him, and could not speak the reading or lift his chin. No collector was called, no figure given, no slate opened.

In the small hours he laid out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel: six hearings with no seventh, three near-full answers unpaid, nine stoppings as objects and not a rule, three held changes on three places, the loft-sill sound on a fourth. The pale line lay on a place of its own, across none of them. He wanted the crust, the breast, the pot handle and a number nobody would speak, and got none. One hundred and eighty-eight stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, and the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere.
