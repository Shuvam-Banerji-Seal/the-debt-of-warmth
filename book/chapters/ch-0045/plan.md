# Chapter 45 — "The Sweet on the Wind" (beat sheet)

**Days covered.** Day 186, Ember 7, Seventhday; Day 187, Ember 8, Eighthday; Day 188, Ember 9, Ninthday; Y.H. 813. A normal three-day chapter in the middle of the kiln month. Every day opens with its own `\daymark` and is lived in full in two scenes; nothing is skipped, poured into another or summarised. Weekday labels are checked against `bible/calendar.md` (*t* = 336 + *N* − 1): Day 186 → *t* = 521 → Ember 7, Seventhday; Day 187 → *t* = 522 → Ember 8, Eighthday; Day 188 → *t* = 523 → Ember 9, Ninthday. There is **no Account-day and no market** in these three days: Ember 10 (Day 189) is the month's first Account-day and belongs to Chapter 46, so there is no Cross, no bell rung, no Berr, no Hessa, no green-mouthed woman, no foundling and no soft-cuffed clerk on stage; the Cross, the bell, the moot-post's shade, the reading block and the clerk at the out-of-line landing are held as facts and no character from them comes on stage. The district keeps calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month; neither usage is corrected in narration. The boy is one hundred and eighty-six days old and six months and six days on the first morning, one hundred and eighty-seven and six months and seven days on the second, one hundred and eighty-eight and six months and eight days on the third, and nobody under any roof says so.

**The chapter's one meaningful change — paid small, non-fatally.** The gathered danger of Tallow and the first days of Ember is at last paid in one particular physical edge, and in one only: on Day 188 a glaze-room hand come up from High Kiln is carried out of the kiln and across the yard with the sweet-sickness on his breath, the pale line at his gum, and his own hand held limp with the fingers gone the colour of old rope and the nails pewter-pale. A pot set down wrong gives its high thin note before its time and sings itself out early; the yard goes around the place the carriers crossed. No major character dies. No collector is called, no figure is given, no slate is opened, no payer is named to the keeping, no lesson is made, no thread is closed and no doctrine stands up. The cost stays entered nowhere. The boy reads the body exactly --- the line at the gum, the sweet breath, the way the hand is held, the thumb hooked under the man's arm, the colour of the nail beds --- and cannot reach any of it or speak any of it; from two paces the clinical reading is exact, complete and worth nothing. He files the pale line on a place of its own, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a near-full answer, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, for a rule is an account that has found a payer. This is **T077 advanced (not paid as a thread) and T061/T082 advanced**; it is a single edge spent and entered nowhere, not a payoff of any of the long mysteries.

**Continuation from Chapter 44 (not recapped).** Day 185, Ember 6, is behind and is not revisited. The count stood at one hundred and eighty-five, exact and unspent; the boy lay on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, one leathery brown-edged crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the fire still answered four low pulls with its fifth missing and its payer unnamed; the nine stoppings stood nine with no tenth before the seventh year was earned, the Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled; six hearings stood beside and never among with the sixth from a person and no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; three held changes stood on three separate places with the stone's loft-sill scrape on a fourth; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no other hour; the charcoal waited unread under the loaf shelf; the stone lay back in its pot in the loft, cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving. Scenes 1–6 carry that state forward in silence and add the one edge.

## Scene 1 — "The Middle Days"
**Day and time.** Day 186, Ember 7, Seventhday: before first grey through the opening of the kiln, first light to mid-morning.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof: the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle; the kiln mouth; the edge of the Clay Yard the basket crosses; the drying racks; the wheel-shed edge.

**Who is present.** `dan-marchetti` (Corin, carried, set down with his back to the kiln), `sella-vail`, `tarn` (broom seen and heard), `jorra-vail` (at the wheel, glimpsed or heard through the finger's-width door, no line). Walk-ons only, none named and none given a sheet: a High Kiln kiln-master on the wrong wind; a rack-worker who sets a pot down wrong. No walk-on speaks to the boy.

1. Open on the boy, one hundred and eighty-six days old and six months and six days, nobody saying so. No recap of the close of Chapter 44. 2. 3. 4. Sella gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket --- *Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind* --- says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, keeps her half-pace, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shuts her hand half over nothing without touching it, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak shows pale off the coals, and sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needed (T058/T079).
5. The widow's mark hangs on its cord at her throat where it has hung since the first firing. 6. She carries him out and sets the basket with its back to the kiln mouth. The yard goes around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the pan's gone round place on the wall, the bare dark trough whose water is called weather, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out --- a look that asks for nothing and finds nothing, a wide step, no number, no explanation to the basket (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried).
7. The danger keeps an order and no name that cannot be carried into a chart: hot clay under tallow, fine grey slate dust ground small as meal under the nails and white in a knuckle when a fist opens, the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a wrong wind off High Kiln, and a rack-worker's pot set down wrong giving its high thin note before its time, which the woman who set it down does not hear. The boy turns toward the note and gets no further; he files it where a danger goes and does not promote it into a near-full answer or let it stand up as a fourth likeness. No pot is lost, no lesson made, no payer named (T077, T061/T082). **This is the first gathering of the edge paid in Scene 5; do not pay it here.**
8. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction and tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, six where the house can check and the seventh held where it cannot, the small knife on its sill and Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it; he says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late, agrees so entirely no argument can find a foot, keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, sweeps the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector comes (T036/T076).
9. In the wheel-shed Jorra works with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading, keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note three turns before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master come up on a wrong wind with the sweetish lead edge at one mouth-corner who says a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. ## Scene 2 — "What Is Remembered Is Not Done Again"
