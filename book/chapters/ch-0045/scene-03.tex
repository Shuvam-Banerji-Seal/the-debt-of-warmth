\daymark{187}{Ember 8, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

Morning came into \place{the Clay Yard} from below, the way heat comes off a banked fire before the light finds it. It lay in the dust under \place{the drying racks}, went up the wall of \place{the kiln mouth}, and arrived at the basket last. \person[Corin]{The boy} was one hundred and eighty-seven days old and six months and seven days, and nobody under any roof said so. The thread at his mouth had frozen again in the small hours and was unwiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread. The crust lay two inches from his ear, one stiff brown-edged barley cake gone leathery in the dry kiln heat, with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody had been given an instruction about that sound either; his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the door and came down against the fold. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-seven days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and the surgeon's register laid them on three towels and left the towels apart, for a row of likenesses left standing will call itself a truth if a man wants an answer badly enough. He wanted the crust. He wanted the breast. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. He got none of them and could not ask for any.

Three or four men went low through the finger's-width-open door of \place{the back room} before first grey, with their shoulders in and their heads down, and the door gave them no more than a finger's width and gave them no name. \person{Sella Vail} carried past at the half-pace with the basket against her hip and turned no head toward the door. She gave the house no name for the men and did not say the room was cold; the instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket, \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,} and the household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side in the house's ear --- one from a man at a well who had not lowered his bucket while he said it, one from a man going up \place{the clay road} with a narrow slate held edge-on to his sleeve and the writing turned inward --- and no third came to stand beside them. Nobody made the room behind the finger's-width door into the room in either telling. Nobody said what the men wanted or what they carried in. The boy lay at the edge of the light and heard the door give its small dry sound on its way shut, and put the sound where a sound out of a room one cannot enter must go, on a place of its own, entered nowhere.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} and were entered by nobody. The dust was parted from the slip as a surgeon parts a clot from the vessel that holds it: one the material of a room, one the material of the work, each lying where it belonged and neither answering for the other. The boy took the two apart from two paces without a hand that could have lifted either, and wrote no cause across the space between them.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,} he said, flat and low, the way a man speaks to an animal he means to work with, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel --- knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it --- and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind, with the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner and \lore{Ash} on his cuffs, said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. Jorra did not answer him and did not look up; the man took the silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying. The knowing stayed where Jorra had put it and did not become a hearing because a man in a clean apron had given the danger a name aloud. No pot was lost in the yard and nothing was paid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished in the middle of a sentence to the fire, Jorra's bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and waited. He did not look toward her and did not answer her. He held the iron while the sentence found its end and the end of it arrived at the pot, and only then did he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the cold. The household straightened around the waiting and nobody spoke of it. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again; it lay on neither the place of the Day 166 beat beneath the shelf nor the place of the Day 171 half-check on the flagstones, and the two tellings of the shut room stayed two with no third. The boy read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and not a doctrine, and the iron standing where it stood asked nothing of anybody.

\scenebreak

At \place[the yard well]{the well} a glaze-room hand come up from High Kiln waited his turn with the sweetish edge of lead glaze on his breath. He was taller than the men who worked the racks and thinner in the face, and the sweet went ahead of him and stayed a moment after he moved, the way smoke stays in a room after the pot is carried out. He took the dipper with his right hand and drank, and when he lifted his head the pale line showed at his gum, a wet white edge where the colour should have been. He wiped his mouth with the back of his wrist and kept his other hand curled at his side rather than using it, the fingers shut in against his coat, and went on standing there with his breath coming short through his nose.

The boy read the body from two paces as he had once read bodies on a table under lights, without choosing to and without any hand to follow the reading with. The line at the gum. The hand held curled at the side. The colour under the nails, pewter-pale where a man's nails should have been pink. The breath that went in short and came out shorter, and the small pause at the top of it where something hurt. The slight tremor in the wrist that lifted the dipper, and the way the man set the dipper down with care because his fingers had stopped telling him where it was. Each thing lay in its own hollow of the register, exact, complete, and worth nothing at two paces. He had no word in Grathic for what the line and the hand and the breath made together, and if he had had one he could not have said it, and if he could have said it nobody in the yard would have asked him. He could not reach the man's hand to turn it over. He could not reach his own chin to stop the thread at his mouth. He filed the whole reading where a danger of the month went, on a place of its own: not among the six hearings, not a seventh, not a fourth near-full answer, no name, no figure, no cause and no payer. The sweet breath went on up the yard over the basket and off toward the racks. Nobody called a collector. Nobody asked what ailed him. The question of a cost went to the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its absences the way it went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. It went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it, and neither was swept and neither had an instruction. It went wide beneath the lowest rack, where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody entered. It went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim. At the well \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and the water in it was called weather. The yard went around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The surgeon's register set the pan's gone round ring on a tray of its own and the field's empty place on another and did not lay one across the other, for three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-seven days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

A neighbour come up from the tubs with a wet pot against her hip asked what the firing cost now the kiln month's middle days were on. Sella's eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching it, the way a hand closes over an instrument it has decided not to lift. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,} she said, and gave the woman no figure and no name for anybody. The neighbour carried the question away level, with nothing slopped out of the pot, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered; the instruction had gone to the pot and the weather, which were the two things in the yard that would answer if either of them could, and neither did.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named. He told the wood what he told it at every going and coming of the light. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.} Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them and the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, and the breath from Seed sixteen stood unspent at the top of the next reach with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He said \emph{Yes, house} to a neighbour who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the stopped place where the movement had once waited and was not given it again. The Day 171 change stayed where it was laid, on its own place and not repeated. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector and did not once turn along it. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it.

The sweet breath passed over the basket and went on up the yard. The man at the well went off with his hand still curled and his dipper held carefully by the handle, and the men in the back room kept the name the house had not given them. The boy read the shape of a body he could not reach --- the line, the hand, the nails, the short breath --- and knew that an exact reading at two paces was the same as no reading at all when there was no hand under fifteen inches to carry it anywhere. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and eighty-seven stood exact and complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof, and the pale line at the gum lay on a place of its own, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended: the fire's fifth still missing under the floor with its payer unnamed, the six hearings still six with no seventh invented, the nine stoppings still nine, the Seed sixteen breath still held, the loan still uncalled, the two shut-room tellings still two. The morning went on into its middle hours with the work going on around the place where the sweet breath had passed, and the pot under Jorra's hands went on turning toward its note.
