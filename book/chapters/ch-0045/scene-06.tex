The afternoon thinned without ending, losing its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun. The work wound down into the smaller sounds a house makes once it has stopped being listened to: the rasp of a pot eased down on boards, the click of a tool put back, the pull of a breath a man had not known he was holding and let go without noticing. Hot clay lay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and beneath both the sour mineral breath of \lore{Ash} in the corner no broom had been given an instruction about in two years. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and its payer had no name and had had none for a hundred and eighty-eight days. Nothing in the yard had been entered for the man carried out. No figure had been given, no slate opened, no collector called, no lesson made of the glaze, no payer named to the keeping. A body had gone up the road under another man's thumb and the household had straightened around the space he left the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted and set down somewhere else.

The pot that had sung itself out early stood where the woman's hands had set it and was swept around, not made an omen. A broom went past its foot and did not touch it, and the woman who had set it down went on with her hands and had not turned once to look at it since. The fine grey dust ground small as meal went on lying white in the creases of a knuckle when a fist opened. The wrong wind that had brought the sweet edge off \place{High Kiln} had gone off toward the racks and left the smell of hot clay in its place, and the dust and the wind and the dust ground small as meal went on being spoken of the way a room speaks of its own weather --- as a direction and not a second edge paid. \person[Jorra Vail]'s knowing, kept behind a finger's width of door, stayed on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, and no pot was lost because of it. A cost asked of the yard had gone to the pot and the weather and come back with the weather and the pot. It had arrived nowhere and stood nowhere and was entered in nothing.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at \place{the loaf shelf} with her back to the basket, moving her lips along a row that stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. Her glance went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching it, the way a woman's hand closes on a child's fever in the night and opens again before the clock can see it. She kept her finger from beneath the front edge, so the charcoal tally lay under the board where it had lain and was not read, not counted, not copied, and the beat that had stayed there on the Day 166 held its place where it was left. An afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same as that night, and the difference was the whole of the rule: a finger under a board for one counted breath is a thing done, and a shelf passed at dusk with the hands full is a thing not done, and the two do not become one because they stand in the same room. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and sent the small sounds of the winding-down work to the pot and the fire and the running water, and never once sent an instruction to the basket.

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where a man could check it and find it where it should be. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them, and the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person[Garl Vail]'s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept with the seventh year and entered in neither. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} had swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} and no collector had gone down it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He told the wood what he told it at every going and coming of the light. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.}

The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach, with the small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and stood there, as it had stood on Reed 5 and 8 and 11 and 14 and 17 and 20 and 23 and 26 and 29, and it came no further. There was no figure in it and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and to an early thaw and a late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying. The nine stoppings stayed nine objects with no payer named to any, intervals and not payments, no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned, the loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He went on with the broom. His stroke passed over \place{the flagstones} and the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped and joined it to nothing.

\scenebreak

The warm stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft} above the shed, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. Nothing had come from that room all afternoon but the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, and that had been days ago and was a sound with a direction and no more. From below, \person[Corin]{the boy} kept the remembering on its own place and did not make it a holding done again. He did not stand it up with the three held changes on their three places, or with the loft-sill sound on a fourth, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and a fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause, and a cause wants a payer under it and none stood here.

Through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} kept on with the wheel. Fine grey slate dust was ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist opened, not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel; he kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and nobody entered. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} To a pot under his hands he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and he knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left the wheel and withheld the knowing from the men come up on the wrong wind, who had said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. What the men had said was not made true as a lesson by the pot's going-out that morning; a saying said aloud was still a saying, and no payer stood under it.

When \person{Sella Vail}'s voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and waited while her sentence reached the pot, and then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing there. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, not paired with the Day 166 beat beneath the shelf or the Day 171 half-check on the stones. The boy read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, an object joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Jorra gave the men who had gone low into \place{the back room} no name and did not say the room was cold. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket, did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room; the two silences stayed unjoined under the same roof.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-eight mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and gave no account of cost. It would have said, if a man could have asked it, that the weight was the same on the first morning and on this one, which was true, and that the cost was not the same, which was also true and was entered nowhere.

At the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim. She went around \place{the trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, bare and dark, whose water was called weather, and wide beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it lay unswept in the corner, neither with an instruction, and the household went around both.

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and had never told anybody and would not call kindness now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure. She told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since, because a basket and a shelf and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she prefers. She had gone to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them. Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in a hundred and eighty-eight days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost.

The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke that went on over the bright patch, and his own held breath from a market morning. The nine stoppings lay on the widened tray as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. No tenth had stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in Grathic that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Three held changes stood on three separate places --- the fraction at the cold iron, the beat under the shelf's front edge, the half-check on the flagstones --- and the loft-sill scrape stood on a fourth, and the two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell lay beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid figure; the sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, both offstage and unspent. And the carried body's pale line at the gum lay on a place of its own, a reading exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, not laid across any of them, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a near-full answer, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause.

The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its empty place, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-eight days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see. From two paces he had read a man with lead in him as exactly as any man in the yard had read a pot, and the reading had bought nothing, not a dipper lifted, not a bucket carried, not a word spoken to anybody who would have understood it. He had not named the sickness in Grathic, not called a collector, not given a figure, not joined the pale line to the sweet on the wrong wind or the dust ground small as meal or any absence in the yard. The gift that could say \emph{leave it} in another country bought nothing here. He was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-eight days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold had come to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. He got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and laid it down and went on lying there above him in the dark.

One hundred and eighty-eight stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The fire's fifth was still missing under the floor with its payer unnamed; the nine stoppings were still nine objects with no tenth; the Seed 16 breath was still held at the top of every reach; the three changes still stood on their three places and the stone lay back in its pot in the loft, cold and unreconciled; the mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the charcoal still waited under the shelf for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come. And the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold, and the count went forward.
