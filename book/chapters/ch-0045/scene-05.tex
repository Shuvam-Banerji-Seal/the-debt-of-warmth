\daymark{188}{Ember 9, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

The ninth morning of the kiln month came up out of the clay without a boundary drawn around it in \place{Low Kiln}, the heat arriving in the dust before the grey arrived in the sky, so that \person[Corin]{the boy} felt the day against his cheek a half-hour before there was anything in it for his eyes to find. He lay where the basket had been set with its back to \place{the kiln mouth}, one hundred and eighty-eight days old and six months and eight days, and nobody under any roof said so. The smell came as it always came: hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and beneath both the sour mineral breath of \lore{Ash} in a corner no broom had been given an instruction about in two years. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and its payer had no name and had had none for a hundred and eighty-eight days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and the surgeon's register laid them on three towels and left the towels apart, and none rose up and became the others.

The crust lay two inches from his ear, one stiff brown-edged barley cake gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody had been given an instruction about that sound either. He wanted the crust. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. He got none of them and could not ask for any, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap, four low pulls and the silence where a fifth should have been, and the morning went on being an ordinary morning that nobody marked.

The sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} rode up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind and went over the basket and on toward \place{the drying racks}, and fine grey slate dust ground small as meal sat white in the creases of a knuckle when a fist opened and would not scrub out. The danger kept an order and no name that could not be carried into a chart. A rack-worker set a pot down wrong on the edge of a rack, and the pot gave its high thin note before its time. Then, having given it, the pot sang itself out early: the note thinned and shortened and stopped where it should not have stopped, and the woman went on with her hands and made no lesson of it. This was the edge the month had been gathering, paid small, a sound spent and entered nowhere. What the men come up on the wrong wind had said about a pot singing before its time was not made true as a lesson by the pot's going-out; a saying said aloud was still a saying, and no payer stood under it. The boy turned toward the note and got no further. He had no knuckle to set against it, no word in \lore{Grathic} to give it, no hand that could reach a towel or a slate. He filed it where a danger of the month went, on a place of its own: not among the six hearings, not a fourth near-full answer standing up to call itself a cause, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to this. No pot was lost. No lesson was made. The high thin note hung a moment over the racks and went where the woman's hands had not followed it.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named, and swept at the edge of the work where the yard's edge of light began. He told the wood what he told it at every going and coming of the light. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.} Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them, and the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, and beside the knife lay \person[Garl Vail]'s unforged collector sentence, kept with the seventh year and entered in nothing. He said \emph{Yes, house} to a thaw wanted early and a thaw wanted late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because it puts off the paying. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and out onto \place{the clay road} a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the road and no collector went down it. Through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the wheel went on turning; \person[Jorra Vail] was at it with his bent little finger leading and kept \lore{the full note} of the pot under his hands to himself, and gave no word and no look toward the basket. The boy, carried past the edge of the door, took the two states of the morning apart without a question asked.

\scenebreak

Then two men came out of the kiln with a third man between them.

There was no choreography to it and no elegance. A grip under the arm. A hand at the feet. A shift of weight as the middle man's knees went and the two carriers took him up again. One of the men had hooked a thumb under the glaze-room hand's arm to keep the head from lopping forward, and he kept the thumb there. The other held the feet. They went out of the kiln mouth and across \place{the Clay Yard} toward the clay road in the long undignified interval of carrying a man who is still breathing, with the yard going on around them the way a yard goes around a barrow that has been set down in the wrong place.

The man between them was alive. His breath came in short and went out shorter, with the small pause at the top of it where something hurt, and the sweet-sickness went before him and stayed a moment after he had passed, the way smoke stays in a room after the pot is carried out. The pale line showed at his gum when his head went back, wet white where the colour should have been. His own hand was held limp and not used; the fingers had gone the colour of old rope and the nails were pewter-pale. He was taller than the men who worked the racks and thinner in the face, and he had the look of a man whose fingers had stopped telling him where things were, so that a dipper had to be set down with care. The thumb under his arm kept his head where it belonged. Nothing about the carrying was a lesson. Nobody walking past slowed to give it one.

A woman come up from the tubs with a wet pot against her hip stood a moment with the weight on her forearm and looked at the three men going across the yard, and then went on with the pot, because a body carried is a body carried and there was no instruction about standing still for it. A man with a barrow eased his load round the space they crossed and did not turn his head. The racks went on throwing their thin shadows on the ground, and the kiln kept its breath in fours behind the wall, four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and the fine grey dust lay white in the creases of a fist that opened and shut about its own work. Nobody gave the carriers a road or a figure or a word. The yard had a hundred ways of going around a thing and every one of them was the morning's work.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket down at the yard's edge, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She turned the boy's face as she would turn a pot so the light fell where she wanted it, without once looking into the fold, and the instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} Two men were crossing the yard with a body between them and she gave the basket no account of it and gave the yard no name for what ailed him. She went wide around the space the carriers had crossed with a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. Her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching the clay jar standing cold under its glaze, and the widow's mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, because a yard and a carrying are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the household straightened around her silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

The arm that held the basket at the yard's edge kept the height of the handle and the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that had carried him a hundred and eighty-eight mornings and gave no account of any of them. It was set now to a basket and not to the man going out under the thumb of another man's hand, and there was no instruction that would have made it otherwise. The boy felt the small shift of her weight through the wool as she went wide of the place the carriers had crossed, and the shift was a turn of the wrist and nothing spoken and nothing entered. She gave the house no name for what had been carried and the boy gave it no name either; two silences under the same edge of light, and neither of them a lesson made for the other.

From two paces the boy read the carried body as he had once read bodies under lights, without choosing to and without a hand to follow the reading with. The line at the gum. The sweet on the breath. The hand held limp and the fingers the colour of old rope. The nails pewter-pale. The thumb hooked under the arm and the way the head had to be kept from lopping because the neck had stopped holding it. The short breath and the pause at the top of it. Each thing lay in its own hollow of the register, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. He had no word in Grathic for what the line and the hand and the breath made together, and if he had had one he could not have said it, and if he could have said it nobody in the yard would have asked him. He read the man as a man with lead in him, and did not speak the reading or give it a name that any slate would take. He could not reach the hand to turn it over. He could not reach his own chin to stop the thread. He was the only adult in the yard and the least use in it; the gift that could say \emph{leave it} in another country bought nothing here, and bought no hand to carry a bucket or lift a dipper.

Nobody called a collector. Nobody gave a figure. Nobody said what ailed the man or made a lesson of the glaze. The question of a cost went to the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere, and no payer was named to the keeping and no slate was opened to receive it. The man was carried out breathing and went on breathing up the road; a breath kept is not an ending and nobody in the yard made it one. No collector came, no thread closed, no doctrine stood up and no second edge was owed. The yard took up its work behind the carriers as water closes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. A thing had been spent and nothing had been entered; the account went on standing exactly as it had stood before the pot gave its note and before the men came through the kiln mouth.

The sweet went on up the yard behind the carriers and thinned as it went, and the wrong wind that had brought it off the terrace went off toward the racks and left the smell of hot clay in its place. Nothing in the yard had acquired a name. The pot that had sung itself out early stood where the woman's hands had set it and went on standing there without an instruction, not made an omen and not given a second edge to pay. The dust ground small as meal went on lying white in a knuckle when a fist opened, and the knowing Jorra kept behind the finger's-width door went on lying on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, and no pot was lost because of it. The cost stood where it had always stood, a direction asked of the pot and the weather and answered with the weather and the pot.

The yard went around the place where the man had been carried and around its absences the way it goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. It went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it, and neither was swept and neither had an instruction. It went wide beneath the lowest rack, where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody entered. It went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim. At \place[the yard well]{the well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and the water in it was called weather. The yard went around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The surgeon's register set the pan's gone round ring on a tray of its own and the field's empty place on another and would not let them touch, for three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-eight days taking likenesses down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see. The carried man's pale line was not laid across any of them.

\scenebreak

He kept the reading where it belonged without waiting for the dark to lay it out, as a man leaves an instrument on the towel until the case begins. The pale line at the gum went on a place of its own: a danger read exactly and worth nothing at two paces, not among the six hearings, not a seventh, not one of the three near-full answers, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to this. It was not joined to the sweet on the wrong wind, not joined to the dust ground small as meal, not joined to the pot that had sung itself out early, and not joined to any absence in the yard. Three likenesses in a row will call themselves a truth if a man wants an answer badly enough, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-eight days refusing to want it badly enough to stand them up.

The two men went out of the yard with the glaze-room hand between them and took the clay road up toward the terrace, the one with the thumb still hooked under the arm, the other at the feet. The carried man's own hand came up once, a small movement that was not a greeting and not a reaching and not a sign that anybody read, and went down again as the carriers went on. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-eight days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. One hundred and eighty-eight stood exact and complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof, and the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
