# Chapter 7 beat sheet — The Number They Give Out

**Days 15–24** · Stone 21 to Stone 30, Y.H. 812 · Firstday through Tenthday · **passage mode, five scenes, ten dated entries, one lived day each, no day poured into another.** Day 20, Stone 26, is an ordinary Sixthday and is **not** an Account-day; Day 24, Stone 30, is Account-day and the last market of the year. The Five (Days 25–29) are held for Chapter 8; on Day 24 a district that has counted what it has says they are three days off. The boy is fifteen days old on the first page and twenty-four at the close.

**Continuity gate before the first word.** The previous chapter ended on Day 14 (Stone 20), night, under the Vail roof: coals banked with the back of a broken pot (*that'll hold*), fold put right without a look at his face, the brown-edged barley crust cold and stiff in the fold at his side, uneaten, unremoved, unnamed; Tarn's knife back on its sill and announced to an empty yard; Jorra's damper moved before Sella finished; Garl as coat, hollow seat, unswept ridge and proverb; the bell four, four and a gap; the fourth pause laid beside three others and not joined. He is cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin (twelve to fifteen embers at best), mute, carried everywhere, cannot read Grathic, cannot lift a hand to his own chin. **Five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; two intervals on a separate tray; four pauses on another, unjoined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause filed apart.** No Warm moved, no rank change, no clamp, no experiment. Do not explain the flat sound, the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate, the debt, the cords, the tin or why Sella took him. Do not say one in three, do not say forty, do not name Marra, do not show Sella's roll, Berr's box, the knots, the broken slates or the tin's contents. No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.

## Scene 1 — Counting Into the Tend

- **Day and time:** Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday, first light into dusk; then Day 16, Stone 22, Secondday, before light into evening. Two dated entries; each day gets its own section and at least a paragraph.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln; the yard path toward the neighbour's house two doors down.
- **Present:** Corin (fifteen, then sixteen days old, in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, Ash ridge and proverb; a collector fetched for a neighbour (walk-on, one line, no sheet, not Marra).
- **Beats, in order:**

  **Day 15, Stone 21, Firstday.**
  1. Open on the kept thing, not on a thought and not on a recap: coals holding under blind ash, the little-door burn closing on nothing, Sella's hand to the widow's mark *before* she lifts the lid and at no other hour. The pot stands where it stood. The cold brown-edged crust lies in the fold at his side. Nobody has spoken to the basket.
  2. Sella and Tarn count fuel and bread into the last tend before the Five in loaves, glances and absences: three here, two there, one set a little apart with its brown edge turned up, come back to and never said. The boy carries his own exact number alongside it and extends it to the kindling's missing fifth, four, four and a space, filing the gap beside the bench's interval.
  3. Somebody come back from looking at the ice arrives with a number they will not give; it reaches the house as a fist closed on the door-latch and a glance toward the fold, and Sella answers it with the fire. The household keeps the number badly and the boy keeps it exactly. Jorra works in bone-coloured slip with no slate dust and moves the damper before she has finished.
  4. The kiln takes its breath in fours and misses one. Day closes with the rule of the mark holding at the bank as exactly as at the opening.

  **Day 16, Stone 22, Secondday.**
  5. Tarn sweeps before light, sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the yard it is back where it can be checked. One year of the seven remains and he will not count it; he says *six counted, the next I don't count* to the kindling rather than to a person.
  6. Sella sends him for a collector for a neighbour. He goes; the boy is left under the roof with the ordinary work going on around him --- Jorra at the wheel, Sella feeding the fire, the crust where it was put.
  7. The collector comes back with Tarn: cuff soft from washing and turned back twice, fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off, a flat satchel. He is not Marra and not the clerk at the Cross; his hand goes to the slate before it goes to the bed. The boy files the grey fingertips on a separate tray (T080), unjoined. The collector says one formula in the Court's soft grammar, to the slate, not to the room.
  8. On the way back past the door a person carries a vessel and it gives its flat note as it goes. Tarn's head turns a degree; he does not look at the basket and does not name a sound. Under the roof the boy hears something he cannot place, too far and too muffled to confirm, and files it with the two intervals rather than among the five hearings; the count stays five from four sources. Nobody else in the house registers either head or sound.
  9. At evening the knife is back where it can be checked, the coals banked, the fold put right without a look. The loan that can hear has been exercised and no one has said what a loan that can hear is worth.

- **Emotional turn:** From the comfort of a counted room to the unease that the house's number is a shape nobody will state --- and that a sound the boy knows has gone past the door and been silently kept by the one other person who could hear it. The second ear is no longer only the reader's secret, and it has still bought nobody anything.
- **Sensory anchors:** cold ash under the pot-shovel; the little-door burn's pale inside; tallow, wet wool, woodsmoke; loaves rearranged on the shelf and the brown edge of the old crust; the sweet mineral tang of the Ash ridge; grey fingertips under a turned-back cuff; the flat note passing the door on a carried vessel; the kiln's four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T071/T072 (count extended to the tend and the kindling's fifth), T077 (number arrives as an absence and a fist), T079 (mark only before the opening), T036 (six counted, seventh held), T076 (the other ear exercised, both silences kept), T080 (grey fingertips filed separately), T061 (a flat note in the world, not added to the confirmed count), T044 (warmth entered nowhere), T065 (kiln's breath), T058 (Sella's unanswered keeping).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the collector's grey fingertips toward Marra's wrist years away; plants the unplaced sound and the kept fifth toward the nearly-full note; plants the count into the tend toward the number the district will give out. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** Tarn's knife back on its sill under the Vail roof while the carried vessel's note has gone past the door and died in the yard, and the kindling stack standing four, four and a space where the fifth should be.

## Scene 2 — Dust and Dates

- **Day and time:** Day 17, Stone 23, Thirdday, morning into dusk; then Day 18, Stone 24, Fourthday, mid-morning into afternoon. Two dated entries.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the drying racks; the yard threshold.
- **Present:** Corin (seventeen, then eighteen days old), Jorra Vail, Sella Vail, Tarn, Alda Reed; Garl as absence and proverb; the men whose names are not spoken (offstage, unshown).
- **Beats, in order:**

  **Day 17, Stone 23, Thirdday.**
  1. After five days of bone-coloured slip only, Jorra comes in from the shed with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of the little finger, and says nothing about where he has been. The boy notices the return of the dust the way he once read a resident who would not meet the senior's eye: by what the face refuses to report.
  2. Jorra speaks to the wheel and the pots (*the wheel doesn't care whose hands, only if they're steady*) and shifts the damper before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low, going in with the bent finger ahead of his hand. He does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed.
  3. Plant the low talk and the men whose names are not spoken, and the broken slates under the drying rack, only as an absence with a shape: a door kept closed, dust where slip should be, a question the boy cannot ask. Do not name the Broken Slates, do not show a broken slate.
  4. Sella gets quieter and the yard straightens around it; Tarn sweeps the trodden ash and keeps his head down. The boy files the dust beside the ridge, the charred slate and the barrow of grey and makes nothing of the likeness.

  **Day 18, Stone 24, Fourthday.**
  5. Alda Reed comes under the roof smelling of soap and warm milk with the birth-slates under her arm; she lays them writing-down on the bench and reaches for her slate before she reaches for the child.
  6. She touches the little tin once inside her bodice and does not open it; presses the fold flat too long at his shoulder; makes the two-finger sign at the red edge of the birth-flower at his wrist without knowing she does it, and covers the sign with the same unasked-for date she has used before. Her exact dates arrive faster than the question needs them and keep arriving after the question has stopped.
  7. On a narrow slate she writes what she does not leave lying about and turns it writing-down into her shawl, toward a floorboard in her house the boy cannot name --- a further note for the tin, still unread. She says *warm and breathing* exactly, which is not true, and goes on with the dates.
  8. The boy cannot hand the note back, cannot read it, cannot ask which house holds the floorboard. He keeps the gesture as he keeps the dust: two records hidden in one morning, one by breaking and one by writing, neither entered where a Court's thumb would look.

- **Emotional turn:** From a house that straightens at a silence to a house that hides two different truths in one day --- and the knowledge that the only person who sees both is the one person with no standing to ask about either.
- **Sensory anchors:** fine grey slate dust in the creases of a thrower's forearms against bone-coloured slip; the bent little finger at the damper; a door kept closed off the wheel-shed; soap and warm milk; the little tin's cord under a bodice; a fold pressed flat too long; the two fingers at a red edge; a narrow slate turned writing-down into a shawl.
- **Threads advanced:** T035 (dust returns after five clean days), T023 (a further note seeded toward the floorboard), T007/T008 (unaware sign covered by an exact date), T073 (the name and the number still unsaid), T060 (warmth given as maintenance and entered nowhere), T077 (the household's arithmetic kept in objects), T071/T072 (file extended by dust and note), T081 (dust filed beside ridge, charred slate and barrow without recognition).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants Jorra's slate dust and the unnameable back room toward the Broken Slates and his later use of the boy's name; plants Alda's note toward the tin under the floorboard and Dessa's death; pays nothing, joins nothing.
- **Exact last image:** Alda's narrow slate turned face-down into her shawl with the dates still coming, while Jorra's grey dust stays worked into the creases of his forearms and the boy lies in the fold with a sign on his wrist that nobody has read.

## Scene 3 — The Crack in the Yard

- **Day and time:** Day 19, Stone 25, Fifthday, morning into a colder dusk; then Day 20, Stone 26, Sixthday, a full ordinary day. Two dated entries; Day 20 is **not** an Account-day.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard and the drying racks; the yard path and the neighbour's door.
- **Present:** Corin (nineteen, then twenty days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; neighbours with figures in their mouths (walk-ons); Garl as coat, seat, ridge and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  **Day 19, Stone 25, Fifthday.**
  1. The cold deepens; the oiled paper lifts harder at the windows, the Slip Brook carries a skin, and the drool freezes at the boy's mouth before anybody thinks to wipe it. Cold arrives in its known order: cheek, then temple, then wrist.
  2. A pot in the yard cracks all the way through. It gives the small flat knock Dan knows and nobody calls a sound; a woman says a pot's gone wrong and goes on with her work. The boy hears it but is carried facing the other way and cannot turn his head far enough toward it to place it among the vessels he has already heard.
  3. He files it on the interval tray beside the two notes that did not come, not among the five hearings from four sources. The count stays five. Nothing in Grathic names what it was; nobody taps a pot for him. The cracked thing is swept around like the charred slate in the kiln ash.
  4. Tarn's broom checks at the place where the pot stood and goes on; he says nothing, and the boy says nothing, and the two silences keep their usual shape (T076).

  **Day 20, Stone 26, Sixthday.**
  5. An ordinary day with no bell and no slate read: the household mends, dries, sweeps and eats. The number for the Five goes around the yards in mouths and fists --- three, then two, then whatever the district decides to give out that morning --- and arrives under the roof as an absence. The boy keeps the one number none of them will give: Day 25 is Kindling, and one day after tonight is tomorrow.
  6. A neighbour says three days, a second corrects it to two, a third says it will be whatever the moot gives out; the argument is about a figure nobody wants to own, and it ends in weather. The boy hears figures he cannot spend, cannot correct, cannot hand back.
  7. Sella goes around the days in loaves and glances rather than a figure; Jorra's forearms carry bone-coloured slip and no dust today; the crust lies cold in the fold and is neither taken out nor named.
  8. At the bank she does not touch the mark. The count goes on because there is nothing else that is his.

- **Emotional turn:** A sound he knows finally happens within two paces of him and he cannot turn toward it or make anyone call it what it is; on the following day the number he keeps exactly is given out wrongly by a district that has already decided what it will pay. Correctness is still the whole humiliation, and it is still the only thing in the yard that cannot be taxed.
- **Sensory anchors:** oiled paper lifting at the window; a skin on the Slip Brook; a pot's small flat knock in the yard and the long ordinary silence after it; grey dust and broken clay under a broom; loaves counted with the lips; the cold crust stiff against his ribs; the warm smell of a kiln that is not Account-day.
- **Threads advanced:** T061 (the sound occurs in the yard, unplaced and unconfirmed), T082 (a third object on the interval tray, not a sixth hearing), T077 (number changed in the telling, three to two to whatever), T071/T072 (the private count set against the given-out number), T044 (warmth entered nowhere), T083 (ice and cold read as weather), T058, T076.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the cracked pot toward the nearly-full note of the third thing and toward Dessa's body on the bank (Day 38, where the sound will come from a person and be heard clearly); plants the changing number toward the printed figure and the true deduction. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The cracked pot swept around on the trodden ash and the household's loaves rearranged for another day, while the boy lies under the roof with a number in his head that nobody in the yard will say.

## Scene 4 — Two Records and a Crust

- **Day and time:** Day 21, Stone 27, Seventhday, morning through dusk; then Day 22, Stone 28, Eighthday, early morning in the Clay Yard. Two dated entries.
- **Place:** The Clay Yard, the yard well, the drying racks and the path toward House Silt; the Ilm's frozen edge is spoken of, not entered.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-one, then twenty-two days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Mira Silt, Dessa Silt; a woman from High Kiln and a neighbour who wants a late thaw (walk-ons, one line each). Garl as coat, seat and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  **Day 21, Stone 27, Seventhday.**
  1. A woman from High Kiln, sweetish lead-glaze at one corner of her mouth, says the thaw will come early; everybody who wants it late contradicts her, because the levy falls in the thaw and a late thaw puts off what is payable. Sella calls it weather; the boy files the argument beside the hairline in the ice as a shape, not a cause (T077, T083).
  2. Ice-mice are found in the thatch --- a spreading numb patch on a child's hand, talked about as a thing the cold does --- and the kiln takes its breath in fours and a gap. Tarn wants the late thaw still, a longer week before the paying, and says so to the thatch.
  3. The boy counts the four, the four and the missing one as he has counted them every day, and notes that the district's number for the Five and the kiln's number do not agree, and keeps them apart.

  **Day 22, Stone 28, Eighthday.**
  4. Mira Silt passes the yard with eels, pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in her neck and the top of her left ear gone; she answers a question with a question and then her own answer, and when a number is spoken her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord at her waist. The knots are not counted; the boy sees the hand, not the knots (T025).
  5. She gives the boy a fresh brown-edged crust briskly into the fold, before anyone can make a speech of it, and goes back to the eels without looking in. The old cold crust of Stone 15 is still where it was put; two warmths now lie side by side in the fold, one spent and one given, and neither is entered against anybody (T044, T060). Neither she nor Sella names either crust.
  6. The separate cord twisted twice under her belt --- the one never taken upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door --- is glimpsed again as she shifts the basket's weight. The boy keeps it in its own place beside the first cord and does not join them (T059).
  7. Three doors down Dessa Silt comes to the end of nineteen steps with slip-grey nails and clay dried at her hem; the cough from the pits comes up and she calls it weather, smooths the pale sieve-wire scar whenever a truth comes near, and does not look into the basket (T008/T073). She has not said the number or the name in three years.
  8. The boy cannot lift either crust to his mouth, cannot ask about either cord, cannot tell the cough from the weather. He keeps all three in the private file with the others.

- **Emotional turn:** Two women keep accounts in materials no Court can confiscate --- a cord of wool and a thumb's worth of steps --- and both put warmth into the fold without a word; the boy is inside neither account and cannot thank either woman. A gift that cannot be answered is a gift that cannot be refused, and the old crust going cold beside the new one is the same lesson in bread.
- **Sensory anchors:** ice-mice numbness in a hand; thatch ticks and dry frost; the sweetish lead edge at a glaze-mixer's mouth; eel slime, salted perch and river silt; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; grey wool cord under an apron and a second cord twisted twice beneath it; barley smoke in a fresh brown-edged crust beside a cold stiff one; wet clay at a hem and the sour cough off the pits.
- **Threads advanced:** T077 and T085 (number and thaw argued as weather), T065 (ice-mice and the kiln's breath), T059 and T025 (the second cord and the grey cord glimpsed, knots not counted), T060 and T044 (a second warmth entered nowhere), T008/T073 (Dessa's cough, scar, unspoken number and name), T071/T072 (the file grows by a cord, a crust and a cough), T013 (weather called over the Ash).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants Mira's second cord toward the thin child in Nine Pots and the reading of both cords years hence; plants the fresh crust toward the day a crust will be gone and nobody will say who ate it; plants Dessa's cough toward the Slip Brook on Day 38. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The fresh crust tucked into the fold beside the old cold one, Mira's thumb going back under her apron to the grey cord as she walks off with the eels, and Dessa's door-shut smile at the end of nineteen steps.

## Scene 5 — The Fifth Pause

- **Day and time:** Day 23, Stone 29, Ninthday, a day of preparation; then Day 24, Stone 30, Tenthday, Account-day and the last market of the year, morning at the Cross into dusk back under the Vail roof. Two dated entries.
- **Place:** The Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house; the Cross, the moot-post, and the Court's table at its Slate Row fringe; the market's edge.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-three, then twenty-four days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Berr Clay (short), the soft-cuffed collector's clerk at the market's edge (walk-on, the one formula, not Marra, no sheet), market voices; Garl as coat, hollow seat, unswept ridge and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  **Day 23, Stone 29, Ninthday.**
  1. The yard prepares for the Last Day sweeping: brooms, a spoken date, a woman saying the grey will be left where it lies until then and burned the next morning and called dealt with. Sella measures the Vail ridge against the ridge two doors down by the width of a thumb; both turn corners without a break and neither will be swept early (T013, T009).
  2. Garl's salt-stiff coat keeps its shape on its peg and nobody takes it down; the hollow seat stays unfilled and his proverb goes to the pot and the fire, not to the basket. The uncalled debt against the firing of tiles is spoken around at the door and never named.
  3. Tarn offers to check the ridge, says the knife is back where it can be checked, keeps the seventh year held back; Jorra goes quieter as the sweeping talk goes around and shifts the damper before Sella has finished.

  **Day 24, Stone 30, Tenthday, Account-day.**
  4. Before light, Sella goes to the fire and touches the widow's mark at the hour before an opening and at no other; this is a reading, not an opening, so at the Cross she will not touch it again. She carries the boy down in the basket, keeps her half-pace from every open slate, and sets him in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with cold iron at his cheek and the two crusts in the fold.
  5. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself. Berr Clay comes to the block with the red cord folded under his coat and green at one corner of his mouth, wipes his thumb twice before touching the slate, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says*, passes a finger over the figure and says the levy was called for the feast and not by him. He goes around Garl's uncalled debt and does not look into the basket (T015, T041, T047, T009, T078).
  6. The household's stores are counted and the Five are spoken of as three days off by a district that has counted what it has and does not want to say it. The boy keeps the number he has kept from the first day; the figure goes over the top of the basket and cannot be spent, corrected or handed back (T077, T071/T072).
  7. At the market's edge a collector's clerk with a cuff turned back twice from washing lays his hand on the top slate before he looks at the woman waiting, and says it is entered as it stands and there is nothing in it the household need correct. The upper line is the strange neat dark hand. The small stop between two clusters comes a **fifth** time --- at the flare, on the birth-slate, at the Cross on Stone 10, at the Court's table on Stone 20, now here --- and lies beside the other four without joining them. The Drawing collector's ordinary hand, with no pause in it at all, stays in its own place.
  8. For one beat, untagged, the flare light breaks through in the present tense: orange on wet snow, a hand in a grey sleeve writing and stopping between two clusters, the hiss of snow taking the heat. It goes out and the boy is back in the market shade with the cold crust, the fresh crust and the river wind. He looks at no name (T004).
  9. A fifth sighting changes the count and changes nothing else: no power to decide, no word to speak, no hand to point with, no standing to enter one stroke. The market goes back to the pigs and a barrow of grey goes out toward the Bone Orchard with nobody looking twice (T009).
  10. At dusk she lifts the basket, sets the fold right without looking at his face, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and banks the coals with the back of a broken pot. She does not touch the mark. Tarn's knife is back on its sill; Jorra has moved the damper and not said the back room was cold. The fire takes its breath in fours and a gap. Kindling is tomorrow.

- **Emotional turn:** A fifth sighting of the pause proves that accumulating evidence changes nothing when the only person who can read it has no hand, no word and no place in any account. The year's last market ends with the district giving out a number it has already decided to spend and the household keeping its own; what is left is the fire, which keeps a count nobody disputes because nobody has thought to enter it.
- **Sensory anchors:** brooms and the dry tick of thatch; a thumb laid against two ridges of grey; a salt-stiff coat on its peg; the bell longer than a church bell; a thumb wiped clean twice on an apron; green at the corner of a man's mouth; the soft cuff and the neat upper line with its small stop; orange flare light on wet snow for one beat; woodsmoke and river wind in the wool going home; coals hissing once under ash and holding.
- **Threads advanced:** T003/T005 (fifth sighting of the neat hand, still unread and unjoined), T015 and T041 (one in twelve read again, the box still locked), T047 (the passive grammar as ordinary market speech), T077 and T071/T072 (the given-out number against the kept one), T013 and T009 (ridges measured, Ash left, coat on peg, barrow going out), T078 (bell doing a temple's work), T004 (pocket-place reached under the flare, name unlooked-at), T060 (warmth entered nowhere), T058 (Sella's unanswered silence), T036 and T035 (knife, seventh year, damper).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the promised fifth sighting of the pause without joining it to the four; plants the fifth toward Slate Row and Marra's unfinished account, the bell toward the Court's borrowed temple forms, the uncalled debt toward Sella's wall, and the baked bread and unburned Ash toward the Last Day. Nothing is solved, joined or closed.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with the two crusts in the fold, one cold and one given, the fifth pause lying with four others and not joined to them, his hand going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down; the coals banked under ash, the door a finger's width open, and the fire keeping four, four and a gap.

## Pull into Chapter 8

The year's last market closes with the household still keeping the number it will not say and the boy still unable to spend it. Nothing has been explained: five pauses lie side by side and do not join; the flat note has five confirmed hearings from four sources, with the yard's cracked pot filed among the intervals; the two crusts stay in the fold; the collector's grey fingertips, the ribbon of dust, the cord for Nine Pots and Alda's note remain separate objects in a private file nobody can read; Low Kiln still says one in twelve and gives out a number the days do not obey. Kindling is tomorrow. Chapter 8, *The Five Cold Days*, opens on Day 25 with every hearth in Sill lit from the Hearth-flame, the boy watching a fire come into the house from somewhere he cannot see and cannot reach, and with the figure one in twelve still unspoken. Forward, not closed.
