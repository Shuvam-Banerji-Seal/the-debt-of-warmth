## Chronicle

The last tend of Stone came down on the Vail house and the boy counted it because there was nothing else that was his. Sella Vail touched the widow's mark before each opening and at no other hour, then lifted the lid. Fuel and bread went into the ten days in loaves and glances, one brown-edged loaf set apart, and nobody said what it counted. Fifteen days old in the fold, he carried his own exact number down to the kindling stack, four, four and a space where the fifth should be. Somebody back from the ice brought a number in a shut fist and called it a cold; Sella answered with the fire.

Tarn, six of seven years counted and the seventh held back, set his knife on its sill and went for a collector for a neighbour. The collector came with fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off, hand to the slate before the bed: the measure was taken, the account stood. A carried vessel gave its flat note past the door and Tarn's head turned a degree. Jorra came in with grey dust in the creases of his forearms after five clean days and moved the damper before Sella had finished.

Alda Reed came smelling of soap and milk, laid the birth-slates on the bench before the basket, touched the tin inside her bodice unopened, crossed two fingers at the flower unknowingly, and covered the sign with a date; on a narrow slate she wrote what she would not leave lying and turned it face-down toward an unnamed floorboard. The cold deepened and the Slip Brook took a skin; a pot cracked two paces off with the flat knock he knew and nobody called a sound, and he could not turn to place it. The number for the Five went around in fists and mouths, three, then two, then whatever the moot gave out, while he kept one day after tonight.

Mira Silt put a fresh crust into the fold beside the cold one of Stone 15 and went off with two cords; Dessa coughed from the pits and called it weather. On the Last Day's eve the brooms came out and Sella measured the Vail ridge against the death-house ridge. On Stone 30 Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve; the Five were three days off. At the market's edge the pause in the neat dark hand came a fifth time and did not join the other four. For one beat the flare light was orange on wet snow and a hand in a grey sleeve found wool where a lit phone with a name had lain; he did not look. At dusk Sella set him on his bed under the roof, banked the coals with a broken pot and did not touch the mark. The fire kept four, four and a gap. Kindling was tomorrow.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment / day and time.** Day 24 is done: Stone 30, Tenthday, Account-day and the year's last market, Y.H. 812, dusk into night under the Vail kiln-house roof. The boy, now twenty-four days old, lies on his bed under the roof (not in the Debt Loft) with the two crusts in the fold at his side --- the spent, stiff brown-edged one put there on Stone 15 and the fresh one Mira gave on Day 22 --- his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. The fifth pause lies with four others and does not join them; the Drawing collector's hand with no pause stays in its own place; the figure for the Five is going around the district in fists and mouths; one day after tonight is tomorrow. The coals hissed once under ash and held, the door stands a finger's width open, and the fire keeps four, four and a gap. Kindling is tomorrow.

- **Where everyone is and their condition.** **Corin/Dan** --- on the bed, awake, silent, cracked and low-capacity (below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best), unranked, carried everywhere, cannot roll, speak, read Grathic, lift a hand to his chin or feed himself; cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist; drool freezes at his mouth unwiped. Wants the crust, the breast and a word to correct the room, gets none. **Sella Vail**, 33 --- at the fire after banking with the back of a broken pot; carried him to the Cross and back with a half-pace kept from every open slate; touched the widow's mark only before the lid on Stone 30 (a reading, not an opening), and not at the bank; put the fold right without looking at his face; measured the ridge by thumb against the death-house; would not take down Garl's coat or fill his seat. Keeps a private roll she has never shown and will not let anyone read; wants the fire and the house kept and the keeping never explained. **Tarn**, 22 --- knife back on its sill and announced repeatedly to the empty yard; six years counted, seventh held back; broom checked half a stroke at the cracked-pot place; fetched the collector on Day 16 and turned his head a degree at the flat note past the door without naming it; wanted a late thaw and a longer week before paying; pale clip-ring at his left wrist. Wants the exact account of what he is owed and owes; still keeps the unspoken second silence. **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- in and out of the shed all tend, bone-coloured slip on his forearms, bent little finger leading at the damper, which he moved before Sella finished; grey dust returned on Day 17 after five clean days and came and went again; said *the wheel doesn't care whose hands, only if they're steady*; never said the back room was cold or looked toward the bed; on Day 23 the boy noted the full note was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel, a thing Jorra would not say before a kiln-master (unshown, unspoken). Wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself. **Garl Vail** --- absent and the shape of most of the house: salt-stiff coat on the peg, hollow seat on the left, near-two-thumbs Ash ridge turning its corner unswept, proverbs going to the fire and pot, uncalled tile debt spoken around and never named. **Alda Reed**, 42 --- came on Day 18 with soap and warm milk and birth-slates, reached for the slate before the child, touched the tin once unopened, pressed the fold too long, crossed two fingers at the red edge of the flower unknowingly and covered the sign with a date (Stone seven, the account went up on the eighth); gave exact dates that went on after the question stopped; wrote a narrow slate and turned it writing-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name; said *warm and breathing*, which is not true. The tin stays inside her bodice, unopened. **Mira Silt**, 27 --- came up from the eels on Day 22 with silt on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone, answered a question with her own answer, thumb going under her apron to the grey cord when a figure was spoken, gave the fresh crust briskly (*it's a crust, not a sermon*) and went off; a second cord twisted twice under her belt, never taken upriver to Nine Pots, was glimpsed again; knots not counted. Never said she knows the child; wants what she knows off every slate. **Dessa Silt**, 24 --- came to the end of nineteen steps three doors down on Day 22, slip-grey nails, wet clay at her hem, cough from the pits called weather; gave her count (nineteen out and nineteen back, four loaves on the seventh of Stone in 812, three to the levy, one eaten with her feet up, bits to the woman with a basket), smoothed the sieve-wire scar, door-shut smile, did not look into the basket; has not said the number or the name in three years and believes him spent among the Forty. Will die uncollected in the Slip Brook on Day 38. **Berr Clay**, 48 --- at the Cross on Stone 30, red office cord folded under his coat, green glaze at one corner of his mouth, thumb wiped twice before the slate; read *one in twelve, as the notice says*, passed a finger over the figure, said the levy was called for the feast and not by him, went around Garl's tile debt and did not look into the basket; went back to the pigs believing he had done a kindness. His box with the true deduction stays locked. **Collector for the neighbour** (Day 16) --- walk-on, not Marra, not the Cross clerk; soft washed cuff turned back twice, fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off, flat satchel; hand to the slate before the bed; *the measure is taken, and the account stands as entered*. **Clerk at the market's edge** (Day 24) --- walk-on, not Marra; cuff turned back twice from washing, laid his hand on the top slate before looking at the woman, said it stood as entered and there was nothing the household need correct; upper line in the strange neat dark hand; the woman went away with her fist shut. **Walk-ons** --- the man back from the ice with a number in a fist (*it's come a hair further*), the woman whose pot cracked (*that's a pot gone wrong*), the neighbours at the well arguing three/two/whatever the moot gives out, the woman from High Kiln with sweetish glaze who said the thaw would come early, the women who called ice-mice what the cold did, the woman who said the grey would be left until the Last Day and burned the morning after, the crowd at the Cross. Log in `characters/_minor.md` with no sheets. **Offstage and unchanged:** Marra (still not in Sill, not named, not mistaken for either collector), Orra at the Weir with forty knots and a forty-first not yet tied, Rusk walking the traps, the Waiting Room clerk in Slate Row, Greybank and the Hearthseat, Keld, Vhalen, and the Earth family (Nina, Des, Ostrowski, Vesci) --- none approached this chapter.
