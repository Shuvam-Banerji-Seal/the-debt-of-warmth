The afternoon came on over \place{the Clay Yard} the way cold comes into a house --- not through a door but through the walls, by degrees nobody marked, until the light had the colour of weak slip and every rut held its own small plate of standing water with a skin drawn over it. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the pace of a woman carrying a pot filled that must not be slopped, and the instruction went to the wool, to the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” The kiln stood cold behind its wall and would stand cold through two more mornings, and she had turned the banked coals with the back of a broken pot before she lifted him and said nothing about the hour or the number. Under the fold his hand lay back against the crust as a tool lies where the next hand will find it, and the note under \place{the finger's-width door} came low through its covering, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, muffled in the going. Nothing under the roof had changed hands since the light.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and a thing entered wants a payer standing and none stood. He took the going-around as a field is taken before work, by the small signs first, because the small signs do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the half-pace shortened a hair before the open slate and lengthened again beyond it, the look that did not settle, the step that widened without haste, the instruction given to the wool and the wind and the fire and not to the thing in the basket. The hand reads such a field before the mind has named it, and the attention was spent on the same nothing and entered nowhere. The woman carrying him was not being careful with him; she was being careful with the house, and he was part of the load. That was the whole of it and it was enough, and he wanted more and could not ask for it, which was the work as much as the counting was.

Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin on the trough that had broken under a hand and closed again, the plate of ice that lifted its clouded face a little out of the path of feet. Each sat on a place of its own. A man who laid them together and called them a rule would have wanted a payer under all three and none stood. The keeping of \place{the Ilm} under its winter skin sat on a place of its own beside \lore{water over water}, with a finger between and no joining; it was a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, not a fourth of the three, not a sixth low thing standing up as a rule, not a hearing, not a sign, a lesson, a cause, a payment or a payer.
