## Chronicle

Three days of the deep-winter month were lived under the Vail roof, the boy carried in the basket at Sella Vail's half-pace from every open slate. Stone came on in the dark without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by Account-days --- was set right or left wrong. Each morning she went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot's back and said *The house will keep it*; the charcoal stayed unread, the grain covered with no figure or handful, and the twice-mended pot left with the hairline of its first break showing between staple and wire. She went wide around seven yard places, widest where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, saying *Six counted. The next I don't count*. Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished --- one iron, two bites.

On the first afternoon the river's note under the finger's-width door came no longer water over water in the open but low under a skin: the Ilm had taken its winter ice from bank to bank. A man come down from Cold Acre, shifting a hare across his shoulders, told the woman who asked what the ice would cost to ask the weather, which had laid it on and would take it off; no slate opened, no figure came. The boy set the new note on a place of its own beside water over water --- a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, entered nowhere, no hearing and no fourth of the three coverings. On the second day Ilva stood at the well, her notched strip unread in her sleeve, and answered the woman who asked what Stone's firings had taken: *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*. Her reaching was filed as the ninth record-first hand; no tenth was made, and no ownerless voice came over the boards.

Each night the hand went up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake, held the interval whole on the note's finishing under the ice and came straight down --- answers 295 through 300, exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. Three hundred honest answers now stand on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stands up and calls itself a doctrine. Three hundred and sixty-two days stood exact and spoken by nobody, six months and one hundred and eighty-two days; the carried fire kept four pulls and a gap, the boards four, four and a knot. Nothing joined, nothing paid. What the days cost was attention, which no institution here has thought to tax.
