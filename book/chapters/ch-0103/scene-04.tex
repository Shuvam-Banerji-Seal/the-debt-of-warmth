The morning went off the clay without a boundary drawn, the light thinning to the colour of weak slip, and the afternoon came on the way cold comes into a room --- not at a door, through the walls, by degrees nobody marked. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the pace of a woman carrying a pot filled that must not be slopped, and the instruction went to the wool, to the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” The kiln stood cold behind its wall and would stand cold through two more mornings; she turned the banked coals with the back of a broken pot before she lifted him and said nothing about the hour or the number. Under the fold his hand lay back against the crust as a tool lies where the next hand will find it, and the note under \place{the finger's-width door} came low through its covering, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, muffled in the going. Nothing under the roof had changed hands since the light.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places in \place{the Clay Yard}, with a look and a wide step and no number, and a thing entered wants a payer standing and none stood. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} lay where the ridge had turned, with the finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both. The space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} held its broken birth-slates and was entered by nobody. The shallow clay pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} had skinned over from edge to edge, and the silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} a little out of the path of feet. The field's empty place lay out on \place{the worked strips}, and \place[the worked strips]{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, was gone around widest of all, wider than the rest by the width of the step that would have put a foot where the foot had been. Seven places entered none. She did not count them and she did not look twice, and the boy kept the seven where they were and made no eighth of them.

Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin on the trough that had broken under a hand and closed again, the plate of ice that lifted its clouded face a little out of the path of feet. Each sat on a place of its own. A man who laid them together and called them a rule would have wanted a payer under all three and none stood. The keeping of \place{the Ilm} under its winter skin sat on a place of its own beside \lore{water over water}, with a finger between and no joining; it was a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, not a fourth of the three, not a sixth low thing standing up as a rule, not a hearing, not a sign, a lesson, a cause, a payment or a payer. It had been found where it was put that morning and was not made again in the afternoon. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. The empty fingers went on a place of its own beside the one object of \place{Bone} 22, a keeping and not a second handful; the two did not join, and a second handful made to match the first would have been a finding forged.

The afternoon's hunts and repairs went by as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and stood a moment with the weight settling into the small of his back. To the woman who asked what the carrying had cost he said, “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price,” and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want, and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late. \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, and he agreed so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot to stand on. The \place{Ash} 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson, and no ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} to make a sixth telling of the burned slate, a third telling of the shut room or a sixth asking of the row. The hare, the jar, the kindling and the mending went off into their several directions and no slate was opened for any of them and no number crossed under any roof.

At the yard well \person{Ilva} stood with the pail against her knee while the bucket came up, the rope stiff in the \place{Stone} cold and giving its small dry rasp as it went over the lip; the water in it was dark and moved without a sound. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and the people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A slate lay open a pace away on the stone lip with the writing turned toward whoever had left it, and nobody was reading it aloud.

A woman waiting her turn with a pot on her hip said, “What has Stone's firings taken?”

The question was put and the question behind it stood where it was put. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between; it was not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted, not joined to the open slate a pace away, not to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, not to the cord, not to the knot-string, not to any saying that had arrived nowhere, and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word.

“Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.”

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it, and the well gave no fifth shaping to the saying that had gone around the boards in the cold month, and stood in no doorway to be asked.

The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart, by what was there and by what was not, and found it exact: the hand went to the record before the eyes went to the person, the eyes went to the person before the mouth opened, and the half-beat stood in the gap between the looking and the word where a lesser observer would have put an intention. He had seen the same order in a midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before he let his eyes rest on the crowd, in a keeper's finger beneath a board, in a runner settling her strap before she looked up, in \person{Berr Clay} wiping his thumb twice and taking himself out of his own sentence, in \person{Hessa Rale} turning her wrist before her eyes went to the goers-by. He filed this one on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand, with a finger between it and Hessa's eighth, and filed no tenth. He made no reader of her own keeping, took no figure from the nearness of the open slate, and joined her to none of them, for nine hands set edge to edge must not stand up and call themselves a rule, and a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one.

She was expressly not the speaker of any voice. No ownerless word came over the boards that afternoon; no fifth shaping came of the \place{Frost} 14 well-saying, no third telling of the room shut against the cold, no sixth asking of the row and no witness to any of them. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, still gathering and unspent, nowhere to be asked and buying no lesson under this roof. The strip in her sleeve waited for the far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together --- the charcoal beneath the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, the knot-string, the six pages, the tins, the inward slates and this wood among them --- and that hand was not his and was not yet come. He did not wish it come. Wishing it would have been a payer standing where none stood, and the finding was left as found. Underneath the exactness the infant went on wanting the crust lifted two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

\scenebreak

At the kindling Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off, and he passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and cut nothing and counted nothing and spent nothing.

He checked the Bone 26 thumb's resting and the Ash 14 held breath at the sill and found each where it had been put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair. The held breath was not a sixth held object and the thumb's resting was not a seventh of anything and neither was a tenth stopping, and no payer stood under either. He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as a surgeon draws a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on --- a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach and the loan uncalled. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check. He did not look along the road.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \place{Grathic} word for both mercies. He did not leave the extra interval of the twenty-sixth a second time. The holding stayed where nobody would enter it, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer and none stood. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the yard with the whole of her attention on its level, as a potter lifts a thing that is still soft, and went back around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, widest around the returning-line place. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of the woman at the well or the count kept in wood, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, she came to \place{the hearth} before the basket, fire before basket, as she had come at every going of the light for three years. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure, and her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air; the cord of \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because an afternoon and a going of the light are not openings and six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash without a name and no seventh made due.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and neither was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure lifted and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed.

Then she set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as at the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; he knew it exactly and it was worth nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

In the small hours the surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Three hundred and sixty stood exact behind the day; three hundred and sixty-one stood now, one more in a row nobody counted aloud, not round, not a threshold, only a day. Six months and one hundred and eighty-one days were told by nobody, and nobody under the roof called the number or marked a threshold where none was. \lore{The carried fire} kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and sixty-one days; the name of the man whose salt-stiff coat kept his shape on the peg behind the door had not been spoken under this roof the same number.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door. It was low under its skin now. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing under the covering that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and ninety-eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the nine arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the holding in the iron, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the river's skin, the ninth hand at the well, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and ninety-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Stone kept its second day without a boundary drawn. Neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for having them. No ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand, tenth stopping or fourth likeness invented to make a row come out even. The first firing of Stone stood ahead as a date and was named to nobody, and the boy's first birthday on Stone 7 and \lore{the Hearth Drawing} on Stone 12 stood further off still and were not brought into a day that had not earned them. The far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and ninety-eight; the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; and through the finger's-width door the river went under its skin, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Forward, not closed.
