The morning went its length as a banked coal goes, giving its heat by degrees that nobody marked, and gave way to the afternoon without a boundary drawn. In the afternoon \person{Sella Vail} lifted \person[Corin]{the boy} down from under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and took the basket level in both hands at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the pace of a woman carrying a pot filled that must not be slopped. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” Under the fold his hand lay back against the crust as a tool lies where the next hand will find it, and the crust's corner stood a hair off the wool with its empty place unnamed and no third. The kiln stood cold behind its wall and would stand cold through this day and open on the morrow; she had banked the coals under blind ash before she lifted him, with the back of a broken pot, and said nothing of the hour or of what the hour was for. The yard smelled of clay dust gone dry in the cold, woodsmoke, wet wool, and under them the sour mineral tang of the ridge along the skirting board that had not been swept in two years and would not be swept on an afternoon that was no opening.

He took the going-around as a field is taken before work, by the small signs first because the small signs do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. The half-pace shortened a hair at an open slate. The look did not settle where it fell. The step widened without haste. There was the small dry rasp of a rope at the well, the flat knock of a pail set down, a woman's low word to a pot, and over all of them the river wind that gave the large sounds their shape and took them away again; the yard's low talk went under his skin the way a song goes under a door, and he read it as he had once read a room before the lights came up, by what it kept back. The woman carrying him was careful with the house, and he was part of the load, and that was the whole of his place in it: a thing carried level, with the whole of her attention on the level and none of it on the person under the wool. He wanted more than to be part of the load and could not ask for it, and the wanting was quieter than the crust at his ear and did not win. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread at his mouth wiped, and the want kept the satisfaction from settling.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places in \place{the Clay Yard}, with a look and a wide step and no number, and a thing entered wants a payer standing and none stood. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} lay where the ridge had turned, with the finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both and thickened in the month's dry cold. The space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} held its broken birth-slates and was entered by nobody. The shallow clay pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} had skinned over from edge to edge, and the silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} a little out of the path of feet. The field's empty place lay out on \place{the worked strips}, and \place[the worked strips]{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, was gone around widest of all, wider than the rest by the width of the step that would have put a foot where the foot had been. Seven places entered none. She did not count them and did not look twice, and the boy kept them seven and made no eighth of them.

Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin on the trough, the plate of ice that lifted its clouded face a little out of the path of feet. Each sat on a place of its own, and a man who laid them together and called them a rule would have wanted a payer under all three and none stood. The river's note went low through \place{the finger's-width door} under its winter skin, found where it had been put and not made again on this day, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold and entered nowhere; it sat on its own place beside \lore{water over water} and was not a fourth of the three coverings, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing, not a sign, a lesson, a cause or a payment. It was not spent on the kiln's opening on the morrow, nor on the boy's first birthday on \place{Stone} 7, nor on \lore{the Hearth Drawing} on Stone 12, for a thing joined to a date wants a hand to join it and none had done it. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. The empty fingers went on a place of its own beside the one object of \place{Bone} 22, a keeping and not a second handful; the two did not join, and a second handful made to match the first would have been a finding forged.

The afternoon's hunts and repairs went by as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and stood a moment with the weight settling into the small of his back, the way a load finds its own place on a man who has carried it often enough to stop thinking about it. To a woman who asked what the carrying had cost he said, “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price.” He moved the hare up a hand's breadth and went toward \place{the clay road} with it unweighed, and nobody opened a slate and no figure came. The boy took the settling of the hare into the carrier's back as a thing the body knows before the mouth does and entered it nowhere. A man with a cracked jar in a rope sling asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late. \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, and he agreed so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot to stand on. The \place{Ash} 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson, and no ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} to make a sixth telling of the burned slate, a third telling of the shut room or a sixth asking of the row.

At the well \person{Ilva} stood with the pail against her knee, drawing, the rope stiff in the Stone cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up, the water dark and moving without a sound. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board, and had never been soft, and the people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred.

A woman waiting her turn, with her shawl tight against the river wind, asked what Stone's firings had taken.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, to the cord, to the knot-string or to any saying that had arrived nowhere, and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat between the looking and the word. “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.” She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart: hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention. He filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand found where put and not made a tenth on this day, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth, and he made no reader of her keeping and no figure of the slate's nearness. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice; nothing came over the boards to make a fifth shaping of the well-saying, a sixth telling of the burned slate or a witness to any of them, and the count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. She went on with her water and did not know she had been read, and the boy did not join her to the house or the road or to any of the directions that had gone past her well.

At the kindling Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt, and the man's name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and sixty-three days. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off, and he passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and cut nothing and counted nothing and spent nothing.

He checked the Bone 26 thumb's resting and the Ash 14 held breath at the sill and found each where it had been put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair. \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the yard with the whole of her attention on its level, as a potter lifts a thing that is still soft, and went back around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, widest around the returning-line place. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She said nothing of where they had been, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door. It was low under its skin, found where put and not made again. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing under the covering that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.
