The afternoon came on the way the morning had gone, by degrees and with no hand to mark the degree, the low light of \place{Stone} turning over the yard while the kiln stood cool behind its wall with the first firing put behind the household and no lesson anybody would name made of it. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down into \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the whole of her attention on the level of the basket and the instruction going to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back and never to the wicker. He took the going-down as a field is taken before work, by the small signs first because the small signs do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the half-pace shortening a hair at an open slate, the look that did not settle, the step that widened without haste. The woman carrying him was careful with the house, and he was part of the load. He wanted more than to be part of the load and could not ask, and the wanting sat under the exactness as it had sat for three hundred and sixty-five days without lifting its head and without going away.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, as she had gone on every afternoon since the frost: \place{the Vail corner}, where the clean dark line where the ridge had turned lay with the finer grey hairline beside it that was called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack}, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place{the yard well}; \place{the trough} skinned over from edge to edge; \place{the flat stone by the well} with its silt-clouded plate of ice set a little out of the path of feet; and \place[the worked strips]{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out. That last she went around widest of all, by the width of the step that would have put a foot where the foot had been, and she gave no number to any of them. Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin on the trough, the plate of ice on its flat stone. The river's winter skin lay on a place of its own beside \lore{water over water}, found where it had been put and not made again, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing, not spent on the firing behind the wall or on the birthday one morning off or on \lore{the Hearth Drawing} further still, for a thing joined to a date wants a hand to join it and none had done it.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it, no handful. The empty fingers went on a place of its own beside the one object of \place{Bone} 22, a keeping and not a second handful. The month's hunts and repairs went around him as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures: a man down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to the woman who asked what the carrying had cost, said “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price,” and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate; a man with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing; a woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, to each of whom \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house,” and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. Nothing was weighed, figured or entered, and no lesson under any roof came of the asking.

The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and cut nothing and counted nothing.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, which was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted --- one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies. He took the finger out when the sentence had arrived and did not leave the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time; a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer and none stood. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings, and no ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} on this afternoon to make a sixth telling or a third telling of the shut room. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

The going of the light came on as all the others had, by degrees and with no hand to mark the degree, and Sella carried him home at the half-pace, going wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number and widest around the returning-line place, giving the instruction to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back and never to the basket. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, righted the fold at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. She said nothing of where they had been, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker.

She came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, fire before basket, as she had come at both ends of every day for three years. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the way a woman turns a thing that has been left to keep overnight and must not be woken all at once. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and the sentence went to the fire and not to the wicker. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; the two gaps stayed where they were, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air; the cord of \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because a going of the light and a bed are not openings --- the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and it had been touched once before the firing on Stone 5 and at no hour since. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, the sixth held behind at Ash 2 without a name, and no eighth was made due.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. Both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them, and his name went unspoken while they went on being said; repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with that lighter touch and no figure and no handful, then set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver standing and none stood; he had once known a surgeon who could not resist closing that distance in a patient and had learned, by closing it, what it cost. The rim stayed where it was set. The hand could not close two inches on it and did not try.

The body took itself in the surgeon's old order, and the old order did not flatter it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place beside it unnamed and no third come to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes, and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. He had once taken a core temperature with a glass under a tongue and read the number off it in a white room; now he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

In the small hours the surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which is how he knew it was not the same as peace. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the room held the small sounds by which a field is read before the light and after it: the dry rasp of a coal settling under its ash at the hearth, the small tick of cooling clay somewhere along the loaf shelf, the river's note low under its winter skin through the finger's-width door, and over all the roof's own silence, which is not the absence of sound but the sound of a house holding itself level. Three hundred and sixty-four had stood exact behind the day and three hundred and sixty-five stood now, one more in a row nobody counted aloud, round to the old register and a threshold nobody crossed; six months and one hundred and eighty-four days had become six months and one hundred and eighty-five, told by nobody under any roof. He kept the number because keeping was what he had, and he had long since stopped expecting anybody to say what a number counted, which was not the same thing as not wanting it. The house kept its days in loaves, in glances, in the space where a loaf had stood and in the shape a room took when a weight was lifted out of it, and no line was drawn for a foot to cross and no foot crossed one.

\lore{The carried fire} kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and sixty-five days. The name of the man whose salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Two counts stood exact and unspent and nobody joined them, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

Three counts in three places did not become one: the fire's four low pulls with its fifth unpaid and its payer unnamed under the floor, the boards' four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not overhead, and the river's water under its skin through the finger's-width door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings. Attention was the one thing no institution in Grath had thought to tax, and it was the whole of what he had spent, and it cost nobody anything and bought nothing that could be entered on a slate. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was invented to make a row come out even, and the far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

Underneath the exactness the infant went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle; both were true, the second was louder, neither won, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the last day, and the argument was the work.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing under the covering that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his. Nothing crossed under the roof in that inch as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and no sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was supplied to make a row come out even.
