\daymark{364}{Stone 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer three hundred and two as a tool lies where the next hand will find it without looking, and the room went on around it in the order a room goes on around a thing put away for the night. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, in the dark before the fifth day of \place{Stone} had earned its light, and the dark had its weights and its smells: wet wool gone sour at the edges, tallow smoke settled into the boards, clay dust the cold kept from lifting, and under them the faint mineral tang of the line along the skirting board which had not been swept in two years. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, and the last line ended where three hundred and two honest answers ended with no third hundred standing up to be counted. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and sixty-four days; through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, muffled under its winter skin, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood; the bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into them.

Three hundred and sixty-three had stood exact through the day behind this one; three hundred and sixty-four stood now, one more in a row nobody counted aloud, not round, not a threshold, only a day. Six months and one hundred and eighty-three days had become six months and one hundred and eighty-four, and nobody under the roof said so. The fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days, and the salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door without anybody's giving it a word. The deep-winter month kept its fourth day behind it and would keep its fifth without a boundary drawn; neither of the district's two habits of naming it was set right or left in so many words, and the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. He had long ago stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it.

The surgeon took the body in its old order, as a field is taken before work, by the small signs first because the small signs do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third coming in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as at the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; he knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes, and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and he could not keep a fold of wool on his ear. The joke lived in the exactness, cost him nothing and was not funny to anybody else. Underneath the knowing the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

\scenebreak

The first sounds of the day came in their old order: the scrape of a pot's back under ash, the small cluck of a coal settling, a woman's breath taken and let go. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, fire before basket, as she had come at every opening of a day since she took the house. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the pot's back going in under the blind ash and lifting the red edge where the heat had been kept. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with her hands still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air; the cord of \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of \place{Ash} without a name and no seventh made due.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and neither was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful; then she lifted the basket, and the first firing of Stone went up the yard with her.

The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” She carried him down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the pace of a woman carrying a pot filled that must not be slopped, and went to \place{the kiln mouth} in \place{the Clay Yard} with the whole of her attention on the level of the load. The kiln stood cold behind its wall with the night's banked ash over its coals, and the yard smelled of clay gone dry in the cold, woodsmoke, and the sour tang that came off the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} where the ridge had turned and turned again unswept. She did not set the basket before the mouth. She set it down to one side, level, where the wall had taken the edge off the coming heat; the warmth arrived at him from the flank and not full in the face, the way warmth comes to a pot set at the back of a hearth to take it slowly. Out of the draught. Level. She gave no word to the wicker about any of it.

He knew the order in which a body takes heat back and the order in which it gives it up, and he knew a patient is warmed from the outside, by degrees, with the surface brought along before the core, never hurried and never rubbed and never set too near the fire. A basket set level out of the draught where the heat came to him sideways was the rewarming he would have chosen if anybody had asked him and could not ask for; it was maintenance performed on a vessel by a hand that knew the vessel's rate of loss. He read it as maintenance and not as a sign, not as a gift, not as a payment: a gift wants a giver standing and none stood here, and a sign wants somebody to make it mean something and no one under this roof was doing it. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both readings were true and the second was louder and neither won.

She stood over him with her hands still. The stillness was the first thing to notice, because her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child. Now they stopped. Her left hand went to the cord at her throat, the thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times; the little-door burn on the back of that hand shut half over the cord, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold closing on the thing it held. She held it one breath --- a small intake, a hold, a letting-go, none of the three figured --- and let go. Then she lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot.

The fire took. It answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and sixty-four days. Nobody praised the opening. Nobody named what it mended. No slate was entered and no lesson made of it, no sign stood up out of it, no cause and no payment, and the heat went into the yard as heat goes into a room when a door is opened, without an argument and without a witness. She did not touch the mark again. The woman whose hands were never still went back to the work around the mouth with hands that counted nothing aloud, and the household straightened around what had been done as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

There had been six openings in the six months behind, and this was the seventh: \place{Tallow} 1, \place{Ember} 1, \place{Smoke} 1, \place{Frost} 2, \place{Bone} 2, Ash 2 and this morning's Stone 5. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The sixth stood behind at Ash 2 without a name, held where it had been put and not looked at again as a sign or a payment, and no eighth was made due; the empty place where an eighth might have stood was not dug for and not filled. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and it was not touched again at shelf, well, rack, basket, morning, afternoon, going of the light or bed, because a shelf is not an opening and a bed is not an opening and a going of the light is not an opening.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river went low under its winter skin, found where put and not made again on this firing morning. He kept it off the opening. He kept it off the birthday on Stone 7 and off \lore{the Hearth Drawing} on Stone 12, which stood further off and were not brought into a day that had not earned them. A thing joined to a date wants a hand to join it and none had done it; the covering stayed on its own place beside water over water, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing, not a sign, a lesson, a cause or a payment, not spent on the kiln's opening or on any date named to nobody under any roof. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

On \place{the packing bench} a pot was set down wrong --- out of the shadow where it belonged and near the warm side of the wall --- and gave its high thin note before its time. A hand went around it and set it right without a word, sweeping the space with the flat of a palm the way a woman sweeps Ash around a stone she will not lift. No pot was lost, nobody made a lesson of the note and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. The boy filed the setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around on a place of its own beside the premature note of Smoke 14, with a finger between every pair; it was joined neither to \person{Jorra Vail}'s knowing of \lore{the full note} a pot would give three turns before it left his hands, nor to the sound itself, nor to the carried man. It was not a hearing, not a fourth likeness, not a cause and not a payment. One object, gathered once, advanced and not paid, with no payer standing under it. The far-off hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

He joined the firing to none of the things he kept. It did not stand with the nine arithmetics behind the unsigned figure, or with the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured under wool, or with the five held objects and the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that and the holding of Ash 26 beside all. It did not join the handful let fall at \place{the rack end}, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction or the Ash 23 direction, the six hearings of the full note with the seventh place empty, the heard bell of \lore{the Long Night} beside the told with four, four and a gap, the told figure beside the paid with no signature between, or his own kept gaps. Every joining wants a hand to join it and a payer standing under it, and no hand had joined and no payer stood; so the opening stayed where it had been done, with nothing mended by it that anybody could name and nothing bought by it that could be entered on a slate. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and the want kept the satisfaction from settling.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It was low under its skin, found where put and not made again. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing under the covering that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer three hundred and three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across none of the nine arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the seventh opening, the pot-crack resting, the river's skin, the ninth hand, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the setting-down-wrong-and-swept-around, the fourth shaping, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and sixty-four days; the name of the man whose salt-stiff coat kept his shape on the peg behind the door had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Six months and one hundred and eighty-four days were told by nobody.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer three hundred and three; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; and through the finger's-width door the river, \place{the Ilm}, went low under its winter skin, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. The basket stood level out of the draught under the loaf-shelf roof where the kiln's heat came to it sideways and not full; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Stone kept its fifth day without a boundary drawn and the deep-winter month held. Forward, not closed.
