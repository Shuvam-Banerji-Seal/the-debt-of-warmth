## Chronicle

The river came up through Low Kiln and went down again. Sixty-eight days old on the first morning and seventy-four on the last, he kept the count himself. The Ilm came up as a spreading thin brown sheet, not a crash; the district called it weather and went on using the steps. Under it he heard a note no vessel gave, many small failures inside one slow certain one, and filed it with no word and no hand to point.

On Account-day the water sat on the second step at the Cross. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve over the water and the basket, taking himself out of his sentence. The true subtraction went over the basket unspoken; the number for the flood went from fist to mouth to shoulder and arrived nowhere. The soft-cuffed clerk was still set at the Fishstairs. A figure read aloud was not a figure paid, and he set the two on trays that did not touch.

The water went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and stood a hand's breadth short of it. A neighbour said the house was saved; Sella Vail did not, but told the fire it was the thaw and gave every instruction to the wool. Beside the old line a new grey hairline lay unswept. He set the line, the water and the new grey in three places that did not touch; and into the file went a thing nobody had said, that a thing left where it lay might still be kept.

Below the sill a child came out of the water with a foot that stayed cold; a woman wrapped it in wool and called it the cold, the yard called the water weather. He read the extremity going before the core, and could not put a hand two paces out. Mira Silt put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was named and showed the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted; Orra Weir stood at the flood's edge with her board and got no answer. Three doors down the pewter tin kept its pale fold above the empty bed; the grey fold below held only a hollow. Nothing was opened, and no one said the woman's name.

The broom went around the space under the lowest rack, and nobody entered it. Jorra Vail came through the wheel-shed door with grey slate dust past his little finger, named neither the men nor the room, and put his bent finger to the damper ahead of Sella's sentence. The flood left mud; the yard counted its losses in loaves and a space at the row's end. At dusk Sella set him on his bed, one crust stiff in the fold and the empty place unnamed; the river's note through the gap, the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor. His hand went up an inch and came down. The count crossed into Silt 16 unspent.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment / day and time.** Dusk of Day 74, Silt 15, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday). Sella Vail has carried the boy up the Clay Yard at a half-pace from every open slate, set him on his bed under the Vail kiln-house roof (not in the Debt Loft), put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor, the two kept in two separate places as for seventy-four days. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down. The mud will be in the yard in the morning and the loaves will stand with a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The count crosses into Silt 16 unspent. Nothing joined, paid, explained or ended.

- **Where everyone is / condition at the close.** Corin/Dan, seventy-four days old, carried everywhere, mute, cracked, unranked, below Thin, cold arriving cheek–temple–wrist with the wool riding back from his wrist; wanting the crust, the breast and the bucket two inches off and able to reach none. Sella Vail is at the fire and the bed, having given the last instruction to the fire and the wool and never to the basket; she stood over the two lines in the corner with a broom and did not sweep either, and no opening came in seven days, so the widow's mark was not touched once. Tarn is in the yard with the small knife on its sill, six years counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held, Ash in his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his wrist; a vessel gave its note past the door in the afternoon and his head did not turn, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. Jorra Vail came through the wheel-shed door in the afternoon with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger (the grey dust was the day before, Silt 14); he spoke to the wheel, told a pot it would do, moved the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the bed or the basket. Garl Vail is absent and kept by his salt-stiff coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and proverb said to a pot with no crack. Mira Silt, Orra Weir, Berr Clay, Alda Reed, Dessa Silt (dead since Day 38), Marra, Rusk, the Greybank man and all Earth figures are offstage at the close. The Fishstairs clerk is reported still set, not seen.

- **Items gained / lost / used; money.** **Gained:** nothing usable. **Lost or changed, entered nowhere:** the weak board in the plank walk is reported gone or trodden flat and left under the silt, by nobody seen and by nobody made into an accident (Day 68 it had been "trodden, or gone under"); the child's cold foot below the sill is entered nowhere; the things the flood took are counted in loaves, glances and a space at the row's end, never in a figure. **Unchanged:** one brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold, gone past smelling of anything weeks ago, uneaten and unlifted, with the empty place beside it still unnamed; the smoky crust gone on Day 46, the coal taken by the brine-gull on Day 45 and the pale river stone gone from House Silt's fold on Day 64 stay gone and unmentioned. The pewter tin above the empty bed keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened; Alda's little tin stays shut inside her bodice; her sixth narrow slate stays turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name (T023 carried, **not** repaid again); Berr's box stays locked with the true deduction inside; Mira's grey cord and the second twisted twice beneath it have uncounted knots, never to Nine Pots or a door; Orra's knot-string hangs upriver unread; Sella's private roll is unshown; the bench knife is on its sill, the charred slate in the kiln ash, the basket and wool are Sella's, the clay jar is the house's. No money at any point.

- **Powers.** None usable: no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no speech, no Grathic reading, no moving of Warm, no hand for the crust or the bucket. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift. Six hearings of the full note stay six — five from four sources and the sixth, the flat knock out of Dessa, from a person, filed beside and never among — and no seventh comes, though a vessel gives its note past the yard door on Day 74. Three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid; three intervals stay objects on another tray; the word forty stays a saying with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both, and was not spoken. The district's note of a whole quarter under strain, the falling water's drag, the cold foot, the heresy of the left thing kept and the space under the rack each went onto a place of its own; no cause was written across any of them, and the four low sounds stayed four with the river's note a fifth thing, not a hearing.

- **Facts revealed / lies told / promises and debts.** No secret is revealed to any character and no present-tense Chicago break or `\memory{}` tag occurs. In the world: the flood came up over the Fishstairs steps and stood off the Ash line, then fell and left mud; the ice was honeyed all through and the Singing Sill sang early, both renamed past tense as the thaw that was; a child's foot stayed cold after the water fell; the weak board is gone or trodden, with no fall made of it; the clerk is still set. Berr again says one in twelve, takes himself out of his own sentence and goes off toward High Kiln with the box locked, believing he has done a kindness. Sella says *the house*, tells the fire it is the thaw, and gives the broom its instruction before anyone (*Round it / Go around*); it is told of her that she once sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and she did not go toward the child in the yard, and the boy did not lay the two together. Jorra's promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn; *he's not Vail* stays sealed; Garl's tile debt stays neither paid nor settled nor done with. Nobody says the woman's name, the number in her thumb, who took what is gone, or that the house was saved in Sella's voice.
