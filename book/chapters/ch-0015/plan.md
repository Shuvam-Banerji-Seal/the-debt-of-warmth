# Chapter 15 — The Water Goes Around: beat sheet

**Mode.** Passage, seven lived days at the cap: Day 68 to Day 74, Silt 9 to Silt 15, Y.H. 813. Each day opens with its own `\daymark{N}{date}` and is lived for at least 800 words; no day is skipped, poured into another or summarised. Seven scenes, one day each, 2,300–2,600 words each, ~16,900 words total. Day 67 is finished and is not revisited; no recap of it at the opening. POV stays inside Corin/Dan in close third-person past tense. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no Court or collector interior. The old life colours the noticing (a surgeon reading an extremity, a chart's withheld figure, a district as one failing vessel) without crossing into speech.

**Chapter movement.** The year's flood arrives and goes; nothing is explained, joining is refused, and the count crosses into Silt 16 unspent. Meaningful change: a sound the district has no word for enters the private file (a whole town as a vessel under strain), and the later heresy — that a thing left where it lies is still kept — is planted as plain fact in the water going around the ridge. Threads advanced: T083/T085/T092, T009/T013, T061/T082, T071/T072, T090/T080, T015/T041/T047/T077, T059/T025/T024, T089/T074/T023/T086, T035/T031/T036/T076, T065/T088. New thread to open at close: the cold foot that does not rewarm (silt-leach seed of Arc 3). Do not pay T023 again (the sixth page was written Day 64). Do not solve the crust, the stone or the coal; do not name Dessa, speak forty, count a knot, open a tin, give one in three, bring Marra onstage, add a seventh hearing or a sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, make an accident of the weak board, or end anything.

## Scene 1 — The Brown Sheet
**Day 68, Silt 9, Ninthday. Before light through dusk.**

**Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge; the low houses below the sill; the plank walk at the Weir reached by word; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk, yard and weir voices. Jorra and Alda offstage; Orra only as word and the presence of her board upriver.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open before light at the Fishstairs: the Ilm is over the lower steps and coming up the road in a thin brown sheet — not a crash, a spreading. The boy is carried to the edge at a half-pace from every open slate, basket turned with a knee like a pot filled that must not be slopped.
2. He hears the district's note: not the flat knock of one vessel but a low, enormous turning, many small failures inside one slow certain one — a vessel of huge capacity with a thin place nobody will name. He files it beside the intervals and the four low sounds and writes no cause across any of them; there is no Grathic word for it and no hand to point (T061/T082, T071/T072).
3. Word comes up that the weak board in the plank walk is either trodden or gone with the water. Deliberately do not stage a fall or a death. The boy sets the board beside the missing step and the stopping hairlines; the reader is made to remember the gun, not shown it fired (T083).
4. Sella gives every instruction to the wool, the fire and the bucket and never to the basket; says *the house*; touches the widow's mark before an opening only and tells the fire it is the thaw (T058, T079, T085).
5. The soft-cuffed clerk keeps his place, hand to slate before eyes to the stalls, silent, for a measure nobody called (T090, T080). Wardrobe, weather and bodies: wet wool, river mud ground into the well rope, cold at cheek–temple–wrist, drool frozen and unwiped, the hand outside the wool up an inch and down.

**Emotional turn.** From the familiar small sounds of one failing vessel to the unfamiliar sound of a whole place failing; the boy does the only work available — filing — and the adult in him recognises the shape of a system under strain while the infant wants the crust and the breast and gets neither.

**Sensory anchors.** Thin brown sheet on the steps; brown water over brown water; low turning felt in the roots of the teeth before the ear places it; silt under fingernails; tallow and wet wool; the half-pace; one brown-edged crust in the fold with the empty place beside it.

**Threads advanced.** T083, T085, T061/T082, T071/T072, T090, T080, T058, T079, T092, T065, T086. **Foreshadowing planted:** the district as a vessel with enormous capacity and a certain slow failure, held for the later years of Arc 3; the board kept on its own tray, held for the day it must carry weight. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** The thin brown sheet going up the road past the last low house, the boy's hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the sound of it and coming down, the note filed with no word and no cause written across it.

## Scene 2 — One in Twelve Over the Water
**Day 69, Silt 10, Tenthday, Account-day. First light through dusk.**

**Place.** The flooded moot-step at the Cross; the shade at its edge; under the Vail roof. **Present:** Corin, Berr Clay (short), Sella, the crowd at the step, the soft-cuffed clerk reported still set at the Fishstairs.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The moot is held on the higher step with water below it. Berr comes up short, red wool cord folded under his coat, green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, hot clay under the smell of nothing at all; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing (T041).
2. He reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* over the water and the basket, twice, taking himself out of his own sentence, never saying *I*, never looking into the basket (T015, T047). The true subtraction goes over the basket into the noise of the stalls; the number for the flood's cost goes from fist to mouth to shoulder and reaches nobody (T077).
3. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the boy keeps the heard bell beside the told bell and both beside the district's new note without joining them (T078, T061/T082).
4. Word comes up that the clerk is still set at the Fishstairs with a slate for a measure nobody called (T090). The boy keeps the printed figure and the unspoken one on separate trays, as he keeps the low sounds, and cannot spend either (T072).
5. Berr goes off toward High Kiln with the box still locked and the true deduction inside, believing he has done the district a kindness; the boy is carried home at the half-pace, fold put right without a look at his face (T058, T086).

**Emotional turn.** The account proves indifferent to the water: the same neat figure over a changed world. The boy sees a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and the surgeon's old anger at a chart closing a matter stands behind the clerk's soft sentence without being spoken.

**Sensory anchors.** Green glaze at the mouth; thumb wiped twice; cold iron and wet wool in the shade; the bell longer than a church bell; a fist closing on a door-latch; the locked box's weight implied by Berr's care not to open it; bread and money changing hands in marks nobody counts aloud.

**Threads advanced.** T015, T041, T047, T077, T090, T078, T080, T061/T082, T071/T072, T086, T058. **Foreshadowing planted:** the withheld figure kept toward the true deduction and Rennick's later arithmetic; the still-set clerk toward a standing charge on a district that has already spent its dead. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Berr's back going up the road toward High Kiln with the red cord folded away and the box locked, and the boy in the moot-post's shade with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it, the printed figure and the unspoken subtraction on two trays that do not touch.

## Scene 3 — The Water Goes Around
**Day 70, Silt 11, Firstday. Morning through dusk.**

**Place.** The drying racks; the death-house ridge two doors down; the Vail kiln-house corner; under the roof. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, yard neighbours (walk-ons). Garl kept by his objects; Jorra and Alda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The water comes up around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned its corner for a year and two days and does not take it; a neighbour says the house was saved. Sella does not say it. She gives the instruction to the fire and the wool, says *the house*, and turns the basket out of the draught without a look at his face (T009, T058).
2. The boy sets the standing line, the water that went around it and the new fine grey hairline beside it in three places that do not touch; the new grey is still unswept and called thaw (T013, T092).
3. Plant, as plain fact with no word and no argument: a thing can be left where it is and still be kept — the later heresy carried toward the day a house is left and called kept. It arrives as something the water did, not as a doctrine; nobody in the yard makes anything of it (T009, T013).
4. Garl Vail is kept by the salt-stiff coat on its peg, the hollow seat and the proverb said to the pot (*leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled*). His tile debt is neither paid, settled nor done with; Tarn's broom goes over the collector's road without turning (T036).
5. Dusk: Sella sets the boy on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash (T065, T088).

**Emotional turn.** The dull surprise that leaving a thing alone can be a form of keeping; the boy feels the pull of it where his old life taught him that everything left unentered goes bad — and files the feeling as an object rather than an answer.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud at the ridge's edge and the dark line dry above it; thin grey finer than the old line; woodsmoke, wet wool, the sour mineral tang of Ash; a broom held and not used; the pot that has no crack hearing the proverb about cracks.

**Threads advanced.** T009, T013, T092, T058, T086, T060, T071/T072, T065, T078, T036. **Foreshadowing planted:** the heresy that a house may be left where it is and still be kept, held for the later day a house is left and not swept; both lines kept in two places. **Paid:** nothing. Do not join the standing line to Dessa's uncollected warmth, and do not name the omission.

**Exact last image.** The water standing off the clean dark line while the new finer grey lies beside it unswept under the wall, the boy's hand going up an inch toward the corner and down, with nobody having said the house was saved.

## Scene 4 — The Foot That Stayed Cold
**Day 71, Silt 12, Secondday. Before light through dusk.**

**Place.** Below the sill at the water's edge; the yard well; under the Vail roof. **Present:** Corin, Sella, a child wading and the woman who wraps the foot (walk-ons), yard voices. Tarn in the yard, silent.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Below the sill a child has been wading where the water went down and comes up with a foot that stays cold after the water falls. A woman wraps it in wool and calls it the cold; the yard calls the water weather, so the two facts never meet (T083, T085).
2. The boy, seventy-one days old and carried at the edge of the standing water, notices the cold that does not come back when the water goes — the extremity going before the core, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming he cannot perform. He has no Grathic word for it and no hand to place on the foot (T012, T071/T072). Plant the silt-leach of Arc 3 here as a cold with no cause spoken — no name, no treatment, no beast.
3. Sella turns the basket with her knee so the light falls where she wants it, gives the instruction to the wool and the fire, says *the house*, and does not go toward the child (T058, T060). Her not-going is a keeping, not a cruelty; the boy files it on the tray with her other not-lookings and does not make a cause (T084).
4. At the well the rope still carries mud; the argument about an early or late thaw still gives no figure and ends in weather (T077, T085). Cold comes at cheek, temple, wrist; he wants the crust and cannot lift a hand two inches to it (T086, T060).
5. Dusk under the roof: fire four, four and a gap; one crust; the empty place unnamed (T065, T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** The clinical reading works perfectly and is useless: he can see the foot that will not rewarm as clearly as he once saw a patient going cold on a table, and here he is a basket two inches from a crust. The infant's hunger is louder and the adult's knowledge goes into the count.

**Sensory anchors.** Cold water on a child's shin; wool that does not hold warmth; mud ground into the well rope; grey slip-water colour under thin ice; tallow, wet wool, the smell of river silt; the small wet sound his head makes when it goes too far.

**Threads advanced.** T083, T085, T012, T058, T060, T071/T072, T061/T082, T084, T086, T065. **Foreshadowing planted:** the cold foot held past the rescue — the silt-leach seed, to be named years hence; open a new thread for it at close. **Paid:** nothing. Do not write a cure, a cause, or a recognition.

**Exact last image.** The child's foot dark and cold on the boards after the water has gone, the woman's wool around it, and the boy in his basket with his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward it and down, the cold entered nowhere.

## Scene 5 — Two Records in Wool and Tin
**Day 72, Silt 13, Thirdday. Morning through dusk as the water falls.**

**Place.** The Fishstairs; three doors down in House Silt; under the roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Mira Silt, Orra Weir (at the flood's edge, board and knot-string only, no word spoken), a buyer at the eels (walk-on). Alda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, small smile on the latch. Asked a question about the run she answers with her own answer, moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie and puts one away when the deciding is done.
2. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted (T025, T059). The knot for the thin child upriver is not touched; her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it (T024).
3. Orra is at the flood's edge with her board and cord only: the shape of the forty-first knot legible to the boy without its name, the weight of a thumb kept apart from it, the watching continuing and getting no answer. The word forty is not spoken (T024, T082).
4. Sella carries the boy three doors down and sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every open slate; half-pace kept; no slate opened, no errand turned into a walk to Slate Row (T058, T084).
5. The round pewter tin above the empty bed keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened; the fold of grey wool where the small pale river stone once lay holds only the hollow, and neither house nor boy says it is gone (T089, T074, T086). Alda's sixth page stays turned toward a floorboard the boy cannot name (T023 — do not rewrite the writing of it, do not pay it again). Nobody says Dessa's name or the number in her thumb; no recognition, no quest to a bank.

**Emotional turn.** Three private records in two materials stand unread while the water goes down; the boy reads the shape of a keeping he cannot enter — wool, tin, a turned slate — and the hunger for a number he cannot spend is sharpened by the nearness of all three.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried pale; silt in the hollow of the neck; the double twist of the second cord under the grey; pale fold and thumb-bright lid in a slant of falling light; the empty hollow in the wool fold; a door a finger's width open; woodsmoke and lye and cold fish.

**Threads advanced.** T059, T025, T024, T089, T074, T023 (carried, not repaid), T086, T081, T058, T084, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** both cords toward the day they are read as the district's private accounts; the thin child upriver toward Nine Pots; the pewter tin and the turned sixth page toward the convergence of second records after Alda is gone. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** The thumb-bright rub on the pewter lid in the falling light above the empty bed, the hollow in the grey fold below it saying nothing, and the boy carried back up the yard with one crust in his own fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed.

## Scene 6 — The Space Under the Rack
**Day 73, Silt 14, Fourthday. Morning through dusk.**

**Place.** The Clay Yard; the Vail wheel-shed and the room off it (not entered); under the roof. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Jorra Vail, Tarn, yard voices (walk-ons). The men in the back room are not named.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The sweeping that has already happened twice goes around the ridge again and does not remove it; the space under the lowest rack where whole slate should have stood is gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered by nobody (T013, T031).
2. Jorra comes through the finger's-width-open door off the wheel-shed with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger — not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel — and says nothing about where he has been. Two men came in since first light and spoke low; he gives them no name (T035). The boy keeps the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray as two states of one morning, asking neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
3. Jorra speaks to the wheel before anyone: it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady. He tells a pot it will do, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master (T035, T061). His bent finger goes in ahead of his hand at the damper before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low, so the end of her sentence arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence.
4. He does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed or the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the foundling's name to the people who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn (T057, T058, T084).
5. Tarn's small bench knife is on its sill with six years counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held; his broom goes over the collector's road without turning and his head does not turn at a vessel's note past the door. The loan that can hear stays uncalled (T036, T076, T061).

**Emotional turn.** The boy watches a house conduct its own triage of objects — what is swept around, what is entered, whose forearms are counted — and recognises an accounting that excludes him by design, without standing to ask. Jorra's silence and Tarn's silence sit under one roof as the same shape and are not joined.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey dust past the bent little finger; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone; wet clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a bad wind; the bent finger leading at the damper; the broom's half-stroke that checks and goes on; the space under the rack that nobody fills.

**Threads advanced.** T013, T031, T035, T057, T058, T084, T036, T076, T061/T082, T071/T072, T092. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the broken birth-slates under the rack and the men in the back room toward the Broken Slates and Jorra's ungiven promise; the ear worth more than the debt toward the day the loan is called. **Paid:** nothing. No back room entered, no men named, no accident made of the board, no calling of the loan.

**Exact last image.** The broom going around the empty space under the lowest rack while the broken slates lie under it entered by nobody, the boy in his basket with the dusty forearms and the clean ones held on one tray, and the seventh year still held back without a word.

## Scene 7 — What the Water Left
**Day 74, Silt 15, Fifthday. First light through dusk.**

**Place.** The mud of the Clay Yard; the yard well; under the Vail kiln-house roof. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, Jorra (wheel-shed), yard voices (walk-ons). Dessa and Orra offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The flood recedes into mud and the district counts what it took in loaves and glances and not in figures; the boy, seventy-four days old, keeps the count exactly against the house's bad arithmetic, and the number for the flood goes from fist to mouth to shoulder and reaches nobody (T071/T072, T077).
2. The weak board at the plank walk is gone or trodden by the flood and no accident is made of it; the honeyed ice, the hairlines that stopped short of the edge and the early Singing Sill note are spoken of now as the thaw that was, each still on its own tray (T083, T085, T092).
3. Under the roof the carried fire lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; above it the boards keep four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts do not meet (T065, T088, T071/T072).
4. The new grey in the corner lies finer than the old clean dark line and neither is swept, because the house says it is the thaw and it will go when it goes (T013, T092); Garl's coat, hollow seat and tile debt keep his place, and the proverb goes to a pot with no crack in it (T009).
5. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; he wants the crust and what was taken out of the fold and can reach neither (T086, T060). Six hearings stay six, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word and the knot's shape and the weight of a thumb apart, four low sounds in four places — nothing joined and no seventh (T061/T082). Sella sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, and the count crosses into Silt 16 unspent.

**Emotional turn.** The water's going is not a resolution: the yard has already changed the flood into loaves and weather, while the boy's file has grown by a sound and a cold foot and a heresy nobody spoke. He is seventy-four days old, still carrying an empty place he cannot name, and the only thing he can do with a week of exact attention is carry it into the next week.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud that takes a boot and gives it back slowly; loaves moved along a shelf with a space at the row's end; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; fire four, four and a gap through the floor; the river's low note through a finger's-width-open door, kept apart from the fire; one stiff crust at his ear.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072, T077, T083, T085, T092, T013, T065, T088, T009, T086, T060, T061/T082, T059/T025, T090. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the thaw renamed after the fact, toward the later day the signs are read backwards; the count and the empty place into the lean week; both tins, both cords and the knot-string still unread. **Paid:** nothing. No sixth hearing, no joining of trays, no name for the empty place, no ending.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it, the door a finger's width open with the river's muttered note coming through the gap and the fire's four-four-and-a-gap coming through the floor in separate places, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the water and down as the count crosses into Silt 16.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 16

The boy carries Silt 15 out under the roof with nothing joined, paid, explained or ended, and the count going on into Silt 16. The flood has gone but the mud has not: outside, a yard will go around what the water left with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken, and the boy will be carried to the edge of that work at a half-pace from every open slate, keeping the count exactly while the house keeps it badly. One crust will lie stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; the new grey will lie finer than the old clean line and will not be swept; the sixth page and the pewter tin will stay shut; both cords and the knot-string will stay unread. Into that mud steps the figure Chapter 16 will make plain for the first time — a neighbour who trades a small secret to a Waiting Room clerk for a loaf, and whose head turns half a beat late toward a pot. The watcher has been in the yard all along and has not yet been seen; the count he carries will one day price the boy's ear. Forward, not closed.
