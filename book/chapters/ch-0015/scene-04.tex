\daymark{71}{Silt 12, Y.H. 813}

The water went down all night and left the cold behind it. That was the whole of the morning below the sill: not a flood and not a rescue, but a brown falling-back along the shore, a child's shin showing where the water had stood, and the ordinary yard getting on with its buckets as though the river had merely changed its mind. \person{Corin} was carried to the edge in the basket under the fold, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep his face out of the draught. She gave every instruction to the wool and the fire and the bucket and never once to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and kept her half-pace from every open slate, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a shore is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

Below them \place{the Ilm} had drawn off the lower steps into the hollows of the bank, the colour of grey slip-water in a shallow dish, with the thin skin of it broken into pans that the wind pushed along and did not break. The mud it left lay in long wrinkles the way spent slip lies in a pit, and here and there a strand of rope-coloured weed had been cast up to dry on a stone. Three children were wading in the inches of it where the water had gone down, stamping to make the mud suck at their feet, and one of them kicked a tin along the shore and followed it with her toes. The water came up over their ankles and went down again and did not take them; it had spent itself on the houses and had nothing left for children. A woman at a tub higher up called something down that was either a name or a warning, and the children did not turn, and she went back to her tub.

One of the children came up out of the water with one leg dark to the knee and drew the other foot after her across the boards. She set it down and stood over it. The foot was dark and cold and did not come back. The child slapped it with her hand, the way a child will slap a thing to make it mind, and then stood looking at it with her mouth gone round and no sound in her, and only then cried.

The woman who had been calling her in came down the boards with her skirts gathered and took the child by the hand and asked the foot what ailed it, as though the foot could answer, and got no answer, and wrapped it in a fold of wool she had carried for some other purpose. She wrapped it twice, crossing the fold over the instep and bringing it up around the ankle, and pressed it between her palms, and said it was the cold. That was the name she gave it. “It's the cold,” she said to the wool and the child and the boards. “It'll go when it goes.” A woman passing with a bucket said the water was weather, and the woman with the child said yes, and went on pressing the foot between her palms, and the two facts went past each other on the same morning and did not meet. The water was weather. The foot was the cold. There was no hand in the district that would lay the one across the other and call it a cause.

The boy took it in from the height of the basket, where the world arrives first as a shape and only afterwards as a thing with a name. He knew the order of it as a man knows the back of his own hand: the extremity goes before the core, the fingers and the toes give up their warmth while the heart and the lungs go on doing their work, and the danger is not in the cold itself but in the warming that must come after, when the blood goes back too fast and too cold and the core falls while somebody is rubbed at the hands. You did not rub a foot like that. You did not set it near a fire. You warmed the whole vessel slowly or you lost it, and even then there was the afterdrop waiting on the far side, the worse hour that comes just when the watcher is willing to believe the patient is saved. A cold patient did not clot; a bad cold made a face that looked almost like a smile; the last thing to go was the last thing anybody would think to watch. He had once stood at the head of a table and read exactly this in a body, and had known what to do and done it, and here he was a basket two paces off with no \lore{Grathic} word for any part of it, no hand to put on the shin, and no standing in a yard to put a hand there even if he had one. The child's foot lay dark on the boards under the wool, and the reading worked perfectly and was worth nothing to anybody, least of all to him.

Seventy-one days. The number sat in him with the weight of a small stone he could neither set down nor lift, and there was nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} to give it to.

\scenebreak

Sella did not go toward the child. She turned the basket with her knee so the light fell where she wanted it and not where the draught came, and gave the instruction to the wool and the fire as though nothing down the boards had changed. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals, though there was no fire on the shore to hold. She told the water nothing. She told the fire it was the thaw. The woman on the boards wrapped the foot a third time and lifted the child under the arms and carried her up toward the houses, and Sella went on standing where she stood, her burn shut on nothing, her eyes on a pot that was not there.

The boy watched her not-going and put it on the tray where he kept her other not-lookings. It was the same shape as the fold put right without a look at his face, and the same shape as the woman at the eels answering a question about a basket without once turning toward it, and the same shape as a man reading a figure over the top of a child and never into the child. Four not-lookings of one shape, and he had learned by now that a shape is not a cause. Her not-going was not cruelty and it was not the same thing as a man's pricing or a woman's keeping, though it stood in the row with them; it was a woman deciding what a house could carry and going on carrying it, and the decision had no word in Grathic and had never been entered anywhere. She had sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind to it, and had learned what the midwives knew and what the Court's struck word did not say; but that was a thing under her roof and this was a child in a yard, and he did not lay the two together. He set the not-going beside the others and did not let the tray become an answer. What was left where it lay was left, and the morning went on.

The child was carried up the yard and the yard went back to its buckets. A woman at \place{the yard well} let down the rope and brought it up with mud ground into it to the last strand, and said to the bucket that the water had thickened in the night and would hold a poured spoon, and a woman beside her said it would not, and both went on drawing. A man come back from looking at \place{the Singing Sill} said to the well-roof that the note had come early again and the water ran fast under the skin, and a woman said “Weather,” and he nodded as if she had answered him. The thaw argument went as it always went: early against as-always, neither giving a figure, both wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had come with the water and was standing in the hollows of the bank. The number for it went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrived at nobody, and the boy kept it on the tray with the other numbers that went around the district and were never paid. He kept the woman's wool and the dark foot on a place of their own beside it, and did not carry one number into the other.

Cold came in its known order — cheek, temple, wrist — and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from his wrist so the cold found the place. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear and the empty place beside it went through another morning without a name. He wanted the crust and wanted what had been taken out of the fold and could lift a hand to neither. The crust was two inches from his ear. Two inches is a long way when the only thing that can cross it is a hand that has never held anything. The adult in him read a vessel going at its extremities and an afterdrop waiting where no warming would come; the child in him was hungry and wanted the breast, and the second was louder, and both were true.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the afternoon came down early, as though the flood had taken the light down the river with it. Sella went to the fire before she went to anything else, and the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. \lore[the kiln's breath]{The kiln's breath} went through the floor four, four and a gap, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came; above it the boards kept their other count, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two places under one roof. In the corner the clean dark line stood where it had always stood and the new grey lay beside it finer than the old and unswept, because the house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes.

\person{Tarn} was at the yard end with the small knife back on its sill and \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line. He held his broom in both hands and did not sweep with it, and said “Yes, house” to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying. His broom went over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and his head did not turn at the note a vessel gave past the door. Six years were counted; the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He did not look toward the basket, and the boy kept his silence beside his own without letting the two touch.

At dusk Sella lifted the basket level and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals. She had not touched the mark, because there had been no opening, and she had not gone down to the child.

Through the finger's width of open door came the low note of the water standing in the hollows of the bank, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap, and the two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, given in a dark room where no fire was lit and taken on trust; the heard bell at \place{the Cross}, under which nobody crossed himself; the early note out of the stone under the falls at \place{the Singing Sill}; the kiln's missing breath — four low things in four places, and the district's own note going on underneath all four, enormous and unnamed, and the thing the water had done to the line lying on a place of its own. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh had come. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid. The word that belonged to the shape of a double turn stayed a saying on its own tray and was not spoken in this room. He did not lay one of these on another. A likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anyone put one there, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can do with an answer he wants too much.

And the new thing lay on a place of its own as well: a foot gone dark below the knee, a fold of wool that did not hold the warmth, a woman saying the cold and a yard saying weather and never the one on the other. He had no name for it and no hand to put on it and no way to make the water and the foot into one fact without walking into a room he could not enter. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the cold sat where it sat and was neither treated nor spoken of. He set the whole of it down inside himself the way he set the intervals down, and it bought him nothing he could lift a hand to. Somewhere years away a word would be found for what had come into the district on a morning of falling water; he could not reach the years and he could not reach the word, and he did not know he was waiting for either.

The child's foot lay dark and cold on the boards below the sill with the woman's wool around it, and the boy lay in his basket under the roof with one crust at his ear and the empty place beside it, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the foot and came down. The cold was entered nowhere. The count went on.
