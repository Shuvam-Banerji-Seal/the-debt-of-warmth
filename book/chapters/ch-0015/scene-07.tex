\daymark{74}{Silt 15, Y.H. 813}

The water went out of \place{the Clay Yard} the way a man leaves a room he was never invited into: slowly, with his back to the door, and with one thing left behind that nobody would name. On Silt 15 the mud stood where the water had stood, and a boot went in to the ankle and came out unwillingly, the mud keeping a moment of it before it let go. \person{Corin} was carried out before the light had decided which way the day would go, level in the basket as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep the draught off his ears. She gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody, and to the fire and the wool after that, and never once to the basket. “Go around,” she said to the broom. “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, kept her half-pace from every open slate, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a yard is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

The yard was counting what the water had taken, and it did not count in figures. A woman at the tubs moved a loaf along a shelf and left a space at the row's end where another loaf had stood, and she did not call the space a number, because a number was the one thing the yard would not give. A man come up from the lower houses said the flood had taken three days' baking and a row of pots and the board off the weir-steps, and the woman with the loaf said it had taken a good deal, and that was the whole of the account. The number went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere all week, and the boy, seventy-four days old and carried at the half-pace, kept it exactly on the tray where the house kept it badly. He had the printed figure and the figure nobody spoke and the figure the water itself would have written if a hand had been there to hold the slate, and he let the three lie where they lay. He had learned in a better-lit room what a man can do with a figure he wants too much.

To the yard the going of the water was the end of a nuisance. To the boy it was the reading of a vessel that had been held near its capacity for seven days and had not, in the end, gone over. He had heard the note of the whole district under strain --- a low turning of many small failures inside one slow, certain one --- and now the note was going out of the mud as quietly as it had come up through it, and nobody would say what it had been. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for it and no hand to point at the place where it had been, so he filed the going beside the coming and wrote no cause across either. The work of deciding which bodies are worked on and which are waited on had once been done around him; here it was done for a whole quarter, and the waiting had a note, and the note had no name. He set that where he set everything he could not spend.

Word came up that the weak board in \place{the plank walk} was gone, or had been trodden flat by the flood and left where it lay under the silt. Nobody said which. No accident had been made of it, and nobody made one now; a gap in a walk is a gap in a walk, and the yard had long ago stopped putting a name to the thing that might one day stand on it. The boy set the gone board beside the missing step at \place{the Fishstairs} and the hairlines in the ice that had run and stopped short of the edge, three gaps in three places entered in no account, and let the three lie without touching. He had been made to remember the board for seven days and had not been shown a fall, and the remembering was the whole of what he could do with it.

The things the yard had refused to name for a week it now named all at once, and named them past tense. The ice had been honeyed all through, the hairlines had stopped a thumb's breadth short of the open water, the \place{Singing Sill} had sung early out of the stone --- all of it the thaw that was, as though a thing said after the fact were the same thing as a thing said before it. The boy kept the honeyed saying on one place, the stopping hairlines on another, the early note on a third, and the low turning of the river under it all on a fourth, and did not let the past tense of the yard join them. Calling a thing over was another way of never having had to own it; he had known a chart that closed a matter the same way, with a neat line drawn under it and a signature at the foot.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the rope had mud ground into it to the last strand and the stone lip was wet where it had been dry two days ago. A woman letting the bucket down spoke to the bucket about the skin on the water and did not speak to the boy, and another said the skin had thickened in the night and would hold a poured spoon, and the first said it would not, and both went on drawing. Sella set the basket down out of the draught and turned it with her knee so the light fell where she wanted it. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. She gave the instruction to the bucket and never to the basket, said the house, and went wide around a slate laid out to dry at the well's edge, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it.

Two men come up from the lower houses stood with their fists and gave the number of loaves the water had taken, and a third man with a mouth for it passed it on, and a woman with a shoulder for it carried it away toward \place{the drying racks}, and it arrived nowhere, as figures had arrived nowhere all week. A woman come back from the Fishstairs said the soft-cuffed clerk was still set there with his slate for a measure nobody called, his hand to his slate before his eyes to the stalls, and nobody had taken him off; and that was answered with the weather and gone into it. The yard had one word that let a person go home warm, and the word had done more work in seven days than the brooms had.

Cold came at cheek, temple, wrist, in that order, and the wool rode back from his wrist so the cold found the place it wanted. He wanted the crust at his ear and the breast that was not given and the bucket two inches from his hand, and could reach none of them. The adult in him read the small vessel going at its extremities before the core admitted to it, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of any warming he could not perform; the child in him was hungry, and the child, as usual, was louder. He put the argument where he put everything he could not spend, and kept the two registers apart as he kept the river's note apart from the fire's gap.

\scenebreak

Under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. Above it the boards kept four nails on the near board and four on the next and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, and never had, and nobody had thought to enter the gap in either. He had lain under those boards for seventy-four days and knew the shape of the missing breath the way he had once known a pulse that stopped between the second beat and the third: not the absence of a sound but the presence of a place where a sound should have stood.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} in the afternoon with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot it would do, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew \lore{the full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel and would not have said so before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln}, who would have called it early and made something of it. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it, and the end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off one corner. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed or into the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed where it had been said, sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

Beside the door \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on the left that nobody filled, and on the wall the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner lay as it had always lain. Beside it the new hairline lay finer than the old line, and neither was swept. Sella stood over the two lines with a broom in her hand and did not sweep either, and said to the fire that it was the thaw and would go when it went. She said the proverb to a pot with no crack in it --- leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the saying had outlived the thing it was true about and cost more now that there was nobody to say it in his own voice. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with. His objects kept his place better than a man could have, and the boy, who had once known the other side of a room arranged around an absence, entered none of it as a cause.

\scenebreak

At dusk one brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear with the empty place beside it still unnamed. He wanted the crust and he wanted what had been taken out of the fold, and could reach neither, and the two wants sat side by side on the same place and did not become one want, because he had learned to keep a likeness from becoming a cause. The crust had gone past smelling of anything weeks ago and was no longer food and had never been a sermon, and the empty place had crossed into Silt without a name and would cross out of it the same way. A kindness that could not be answered had become an absence that could not be answered, and he carried it as he carried the rest: without a hand to put it on a slate, and without a word in any tongue he could speak.

Six hearings stayed six, five from four sources and one from a person, and no seventh came, though a vessel gave its note past the yard door in the afternoon and \person{Tarn}'s head did not turn. He had set the small bench knife on its sill before light and left it there, six years standing on the handle in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb and the seventh nearly gone and still held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He told the empty yard where the knife was and did not tell it what he was counting, and his broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning to look along it, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. Two silences under one roof kept their equality without a word between them, and the boy kept Tarn's beside his own and did not let the two touch.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, neither hearings nor the third thing; three intervals from three stones stood on another; the word stood on a place of its own with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and the word was not spoken, and the cord hung upriver where he could not enter it. Both cords and \lore{the knot-string} stayed unread, and neither house nor yard had carried either to a door. Four low sounds in four places stayed four --- the told bell, the heard bell, the early note out of the stone and the kiln's own missing breath --- and the river's own note under the ice stayed a fifth thing on a place of its own, not a hearing and not one of the four. He set all of it down where he always set it, on trays that did not touch, and wrote no cause across any of them, and made no doctrine out of a thing that had simply not been done.

Sella carried him up the yard at the half-pace and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. She did not wipe the thread at his mouth where it had frozen in the small hours, and she said the house where another woman would have said I, and gave the last instruction to the fire and the wool and never to the basket. The widow's mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because a bed is not an opening and a basket is not a door. No opening had come in seven days.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than it had been at the coming, and through the floor came the fire, four and four and a gap, warming the boards by degrees. He kept the two sounds in two places as he had kept them for seventy-four days, the water and the warmth, and wrote no cause across the space between them. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear and the empty place beside it was still without a name. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The mud would be in the yard in the morning and the loaves would stand with a space at the row's end that nobody called a number, and the count crossed into Silt 16 unspent, and he carried it.
