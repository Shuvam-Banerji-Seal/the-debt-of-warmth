\daymark{72}{Silt 13, Y.H. 813}

The water had been falling since before light and by midmorning it had given back the lower steps of \place{the Fishstairs} one at a time, the way a pot tipped on its side gives back the mark of its own lip. \person{Corin} was carried down under the north bridge in the basket, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep his face out of the draught. She gave every instruction to the wool and the fire and the bucket and never once to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, kept her half-pace from every open slate, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a landing under a bridge is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

The steps came back in their old order: four, four, and a landing where a step should have been. The river had put them back a hand's breadth out of line and then thought better of it; the district would say the water had been weather and the steps were as they had always been. Below the bridge \place{the Ilm} ran low and the colour of grey slip-water in a shallow dish, with the wrinkle of the fallen flood still on it in long folds, and a strand of rope-coloured weed dried on the corner of the landing. The boy took it in from the height of the basket, where the world arrives first as a shape and only afterwards as a thing with a name. The falling water had a sound different from the rising water. Rising, it had been a spreading, a brown sheet going up the road; falling, it was a dragging, a slow withdrawal over stones that made no complaint and would not be entered anywhere. He set the two shapes on two places and wrote no cause across either.

At the outer boards \person{Mira Silt} worked the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and river silt in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right against the cold in the place where the top of her left ear had been. The small smile stayed on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch: it could be shut without anybody noticing the movement. A woman come down the steps with salt white at her wrists asked whether the run would be thin now the water had gone down. Mira flicked an eel-bone against her thumb.

“Thin? Who's to say thin?” she said. “It comes when it comes. Ask the river.” She moved two bones from hand to hand while the woman waited, and when the deciding was done she put one away in her pocket, and the woman went back up the steps with an answer that had cost her a question and gained her nothing. Mira went on with her knife. The eel she held was cold and alive and did not care what had been decided over it. She did not look into the basket once all morning. The boy put that not-looking on the tray where he kept her other not-lookings and a man's not-looking that stood beside it and was not the same thing: hers was a keeping, his a pricing, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for the distance between them. The smile stayed where it had been, the way a door stays on the latch without anybody's hand on it.

A buyer in a wet coat said a figure for a pair. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord at her waist and stayed there while she decided; shifting a creel to reach another eel showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey, and the boy saw the twist the way he saw the small wet sound his head made when it went too far --- before he could place it and without any word in Grathic for what it was. That second cord had never gone upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and had never been lifted at a door. The knots on neither had been counted by any thumb but her own, and the knot for the thin child upriver was not touched now. Her board stood beside \person{Orra Weir}'s \lore{knot-string} and did not touch it.

The order of it was the thing he kept. Hand to the record before hand to the person; thumb to the cord before thumb to the eel; a woman at a well laying dates where a name should stand before she turned to the child on the bench; a clerk with his slate before his eyes; \person[Berr Clay]{Berr} passing a finger over a figure he would not say aloud. He had watched the order in five rooms now and had no word for it and no standing to ask what any of them kept. A cord was a chart no Court's thumb could confiscate, a private account sewn in wool where no officer would think to look for one, and the keeping of it was a thing he could read the shape of and could not enter. He wanted a number. There was not one in the district that would come to his hand.

\scenebreak

Orra Weir stood at the edge where the water had gone down to the last wrinkle, with her board and her cord and nothing else. She had come down from \place{the Weir} before the light with silt on her boots and the smell of cold fish and tallow going ahead of her, and the grey streak showed first through the black hair, and the pale eel scars lay uncovered across both forearms, and her hands were like old rope. She set the board on its side and turned it with her wrist, the turn of a person who had done it ten thousand times, and did not speak. The watch was all she had brought with her and she kept it as she kept the traps, without making more of it than it was.

The knot-string at her waist worried under her thumb. From the basket the boy could read the shape of the forty-first knot as a man reads a line on a chart held at the wrong angle: double turn, tail tucked under, tied left over right, the small extra weight of a thumb pressed down to set it. He had no name for the shape. The woman who had tied it over a story told sideways had said one word low into the basket nearly a month ago and had not said it again, and the word stayed on a tray of its own, a saying and not a vessel's note, with the readable shape beside it and the weight of the thumb apart from both. He did not lay the three together. Three likenesses laid in a row will grow a cause whether or not anybody put one there, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can do with an answer he wants too much.

Once she pulled a knot down with her thumb and set it among the others. She did not look into the basket when she did it. Her cord hung where no clerk could spell it and no boy could read it, back upriver in the \place{Eel House} when she went, and the watching had had no answer for all the mornings since. She gave a date where another woman would have given an opinion and here there was not even a date, only the standing and the cord and the board turned on its side. The boy watched her watching him, and the wanting in him was the old wanting: to be addressed by somebody who would know what a number counted. She stood at the flood's edge and got no answer and went on standing. He could not have answered her if the whole cord had been read aloud to him. He had no hand that could reach a league upriver and no word in Grathic to carry one there.

The water went on going down behind her, dragging over the stones in the same slow withdrawal, and neither of the two records on the boards --- her count in wool and Mira's two cords twisted beneath the grey --- was entered in any slate that a Court's man would think to open.

\scenebreak

In the late morning Sella carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} and three doors down to \place{House Silt}, at a half-pace from every open slate. The door stood a finger's width open. She set the basket just inside it at the edge of the light and away from every place a slate might lie, opened no slate, and turned no errand into a walk to \place{Slate Row}. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, and to the pot she said it would do, and to the fire she gave the instruction about the draught. She did not touch the mark, because a door standing a finger's width open is not an opening, and she did not look into the basket.

The room had the same bench, the same pot on the bench, the same draught going along the floor to the corner, as the birth room and the death-house and the Court's room heard through a doorway; he kept the likenesses in four places and wrote no cause across any of them. The floor was trodden ash packed until it had the spring of old bread. Above the abandoned board-bed the round pewter tin kept its place on the shelf, with a pale fold showing at the seam of the lid and a thumb's bright rub worn on it by years of the same pressure in the same spot. It held its scrap of birth-cloth three years and was not opened. The bright place on the lid was not a stain but a wearing: the same thumb had come back to the same spot so often that the tin had given way under it, the way skin gives way under a ring that is never taken off. A record of touch, kept by a person who would not have called it a record at all. The boy read the small oval of it from the height at which shelves are highest and entered it nowhere. Nobody in the room said the woman's name, and nobody said the number that had stayed in her thumb when she went into the water; the two had gone where neither could be corrected, and the morning came all the same.

Below the tin the fold of grey wool on the board-bed held only the hollow where the small pale river stone had once lain against his belly. The stone had been gone since Silt 5 and neither the house nor the boy said it was gone. Nothing had been put in its place and nothing was going to be put in its place, and the hollow went on being a hollow in a fold that somebody had smoothed flat with a hand and left. He knew the exactness of such a place. He had known the place of it against his belly since the third morning of his life, before he had words to know what had been taken out of the world with it, and he had carried another absence in his own fold for near as long without once being told what had been taken there. Two hollows, one in wool and one at his ear, and he kept them on separate places because he had learned what a likeness laid on a likeness will do.

Somewhere else, in a house he could not name, the sixth narrow page lay turned face-down toward a floorboard, written in a second hand by \person{Alda Reed} and read by nobody; her little tin stayed shut inside her bodice. She was not in this room. The two records stood on the same tray as the tin above the bed and the hollow below it, and no one of them touched another. Three private accounts in two materials, standing unread while the water went down outside: wool, tin, a turned slate. He could read the shape of a keeping in each of them and could enter none of them, and the hunger for a number he could not spend was sharpened by the nearness of all three. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear. The empty place beside it had crossed into the second week of Silt without a name, and there was nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} who would have known what to call it if he could have asked.

\scenebreak

The afternoon came down early, as though the flood had taken the light down the river with it. Sella lifted the basket level and carried him back up the yard at the half-pace, and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals. She had not touched the mark. No opening had come in any of the places they had been, and the day went into the fire's keeping without an entry anywhere.

The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. \lore[the kiln's breath]{The kiln's breath} came through the floor four, four and a gap, and above it the boards kept their other count, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two places under one roof. Through the finger's width of open door came the low drag of the water going down over the stones, the river's own note with no vessel in it and no word in Grathic, and the boy kept it in its own place and did not lay it on the fire. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person; three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid; the word on its own tray with the shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both. The two records on the boards down the yard and the two in this room stood where they stood, and the count went on without spending any of them.

The thumb-bright rub on the pewter lid stayed in the falling light above the empty bed; below it the hollow in the grey fold said nothing. He was carried back under the roof with one crust in his own fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water going down and came down. The count went on.
