\daymark{68}{Silt 9, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{B}{efore light the river} was already over the lower steps at \place{the Fishstairs}, and coming up the road beyond them in a sheet so thin it seemed the ground had sweated it out rather than the river given it. It was not a crash. Nothing broke. The water went over \place{the Ilm}'s stone lip and across the landing where a step should have been, and then across the four steps below that, and then across the road, brown water going over brown water with a small steady sound like a cloth drawn over a table, and the sheet kept coming. \person{Corin} was carried down to the edge of it in the basket under the fold, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep his face out of the draught. She gave every instruction to the wool and the fire and the bucket and never once to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and stood a half-pace from every open slate, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a road is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

The steps had counted four, four and a landing where a step should have been since the boy first came under the bridge. Now the water put them back a hand's breadth out of line and went on over them, and the district read the water as weather and went on using them. The low houses of \place{Low Kiln} below the stone sill stood with their doorsteps in the thin brown sheet, and women set their pots on higher stones and their children on the window ledges, and nobody said what the water was doing, only what it had done to the step. A woman at a door with her skirts gathered said to the water, “You've come the wrong week for it,” and a woman at the next door said, “Weather,” and the first went in and shut the door on her own sentence, which was the way the district made a thing nobody owned. Another, lifting a child onto a ledge, told it to keep its feet off the step; the child asked when the step would be back, and the woman said it would come back when the water went, and when the child asked when the water would go she said weather and lifted it in through the window. At the well behind them the rope had mud in it to the last strand and came up under the fingernails of every woman who drew. The air below the bridge smelled of tallow and wet wool and river mud. The sheet went up the road past the last of the houses and kept going, and the boy, carried at the half-pace, saw brown water over brown water and the road going away under it.

Cold came in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from his wrist so the cold found the place. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound of the water and came down. Sixty-eight days. The number sat in him with the weight of a small stone he could neither set down nor lift, and he had no one to give it to and no word in \lore{Grathic} in which to give it even if he had.

The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing with his slate held to his forearm and his cuff gone grey from washing, and brought his hand to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. He did not speak. He had sat there for a measure nobody had called, and the eels went on being sold over the top of him, and word had come up more than once that nobody had taken him off. Once a woman laid a mark down on the stall in front of him and waited, and he did not lift his eyes from the slate, and she took the mark away again and went on with her eels; the measure he kept his place for was not called that morning either. The boy filed the order of reaching on its own place, as he filed all record-first hands, apart from the woman in the grey coat at the flare, apart from the man who had priced him, apart from the clerk at the Court's table at \place{the Cross} and apart from the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in it. Four hands reaching for four records and none of them joined. No fifth was made. He had learned what a row of likenesses laid too close together would do in a better-lit room, and he did not lay them close.

It was under all of it that he heard the note. It was not the small flat knock of one vessel going all the way through; he knew that sound and had kept six hearings of \lore{the full note}, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh had come. This was a low turning, felt in the roots of his teeth and against the roof of his mouth before the ear placed it out in the water and the road and the houses. Many small failures moved inside one slow certain one. He had once stood at the head of a table and read a body that was going at the edges while the middle still held, and known which part would go first if nothing were done, and here the failing thing was a whole district, a vessel of such capacity that no hand in it could have got around it, with a thin place nobody would name. He wrote no cause across it. He set it on the place where he kept the intervals and the told bell and the heard bell and the early note out of the stone under the falls at \place{the Singing Sill} and the river's own note going over itself behind \place{the drying racks}, and he let none of them touch. There was no word in the district's tongue for the sound and no hand in it to point at the thin place, and the only work available to him was to file it, which he did, and which bought him nothing.

\scenebreak

Word came up the road from \place{the Weir} before the eels were done, carried by a man with salt dried white at his wrists and river mud the colour of old rope on his boots. He said it to the boards and to whoever stood within hearing, the way a man says a thing he does not want owned. “It's trodden, or it's gone under,” he said. “Nobody's been down to see which.” The weak board in the \place{plank walk} was either trodden or gone with the water; nobody had seen it go, and nobody had gone to look. A woman at the tubs said the walk would be mended when the water fell and went back to her wringing, and that was the whole of what was said about it. Nobody made anything of the board. Nobody said anybody had been on it. The boy set it where he kept the missing Fishstairs step and the hairlines that ran into the ice and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the open water --- a gap in a thing that would one day have to carry weight, a gap in a flight of stairs, a gap in a sheet of ice --- and he still let nothing touch. He had been made to remember a thing that had not happened, and he kept it the way a man keeps a blade shown to him in its sheath and never seen drawn, with no hand of his own to lift it and no standing to ask who had sharpened it.

Far upriver a woman's board lay beside a grey wool \lore{knot-string} where no clerk could spell it, and the watching went on without an answer, and the word that belonged to the shape was not spoken. The day's thin brown sheet went around both boards and reached neither.

Sella carried him up the road at the half-pace and set the basket on the higher stone where the water had not come, and turned it with her knee so the light fell where she wanted it, and gave every instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals. “The house keeps it.” She told the fire it was the thaw, and the fire took the sentence the way a man takes a thing he has no answer to. She said the house where another woman would have said I. She had not touched the mark that morning and would not touch it at dusk, because there had been no opening, and she went around every open slate as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it.

\person{Tarn} was at the yard end with the small knife back on its sill and \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line. He said “Yes, house” to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying was already in the road below him. His broom went over the collector's road without once turning along it, and his head did not turn at the note a carried vessel gave past the door. Six years were counted; the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He did not look toward the basket, and the boy kept his silence beside his own without letting the two touch.

\scenebreak

Under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof the afternoon went its ordinary way, as though the road below had not moved all day. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came. Above it the boards kept a count of their own, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two different places under one roof. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, given in a dark room where no fire was lit and taken on trust; the heard bell at the Cross, four, four and a gap, under which nobody crossed himself; the early note out of the stone under the falls; the kiln's gap through the floor. Four low sounds in four places, kept apart for two months. The district's new note went on underneath them, enormous and unnamed, and the boy set it beside them without laying it on them, for to call it anything would have been to write a cause across five separate places. The neighbour's saying, brought from eighteen leagues east about a room shut in the war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath and received here as history with a date in it, lay on a tray of its own and had not crossed the floor into this room.

In the corner the clean dark line where the ridge had turned its corner for a year and two days lay as it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey beside it lay there still, finer than the old line, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes. A line left where it was was still a line kept, and a new line left beside it was kept the same way, and the boy set the two lines in two places and did not let one become the other. \person{Garl Vail} was kept by his salt-stiff coat on its peg, his hollow seat with its twenty-year lean, and the proverb said to the pot and not to a person; the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with.

When she set the basket down at last it was on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and she put the fold right without looking at his face. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still without a name. He wanted the crust and wanted what had been taken out of the fold and got neither; the hunger was louder and the older man's sorrow had nowhere in this house to put its hands, so it went where all of it went, into the counting. Sixty-eight days, and nobody under any roof gave a figure for it, so he kept it himself. The house kept its days in loaves moved along a shelf and a space at the end of the row that nobody called a number; the district kept its flood in fists and mouths and the one word that let a person go home warm. The boy kept sixty-eight, and the three accounts did not meet, and none of them was entered where the water had been.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the low note of the river moving in its thin brown sheet up the road past the last low house, nearer than it had been at the well or under the boards in the afternoon, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap. The two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months. The sheet went up the road past the last house and kept going, and the note that went with it had no word in the district's tongue and no hand to point and no cause written across it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound of it and came down. Nothing had been joined, nothing paid, nothing explained, nothing ended. The count went on.
