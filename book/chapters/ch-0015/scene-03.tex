\daymark{70}{Silt 11, Y.H. 813}

Morning came up over the yard without clearing it. The water stood where the night had set it down — in the ruts of \place{the Clay Yard}, in the shallow pans the slip pits had worn, along the foot of \place{the drying racks} where the poles went into mud the colour of a pot washed out into a bucket and left to settle. It had stopped coming. What it did now was go around. It went around the heap of spent slip, around the upturned barrow, around the stone a woman had set for a step, taking a little from each and leaving the rest, the way a hand goes over a table and does not lift what lies on it. There was no crash in it and no breaking. \person{Corin} was carried to the edge in the basket under the fold, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep his face out of the draught. She gave every instruction to the wool and the fire and the bucket and never once to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and kept her half-pace from every open slate, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a rack is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

The rack-women worked around it the way they worked around everything in this yard, with their feet and their skirts and a word spoken to the mud. One laid a board across two stones and set her wet slips on it, and a girl held the end down with her toe. Another pushed a creel an inch higher and let it stand. It came up around the legs of the rack and did not take the rack, and went on around the next pole, and the next. A woman come up from the lower yard with her skirts gathered said it to the poles and the mud and the sky, as people say a thing in this district that they do not want owned: the water had gone around the ridge and left it standing. A woman at the next rack said the house was saved. That was the whole of what was said about it. Nobody fetched a broom. Nobody went to look closer. A man with river mud to his calves came up, looked at the standing water, spat into it, and said it would go around again when it came again, and went back down the yard. The girl kept her toe on the end of the board. The slips dried where they lay.

A man come up from the water's edge with salt dried white at his wrists shut his hand on a figure and said the flood would cost the district a number; the number went from the fist to a mouth and from the mouth to a shoulder and arrived at nobody, and he went off with it still shut in his hand. The boy kept it on the tray where he kept the other numbers that went around and were never paid. Cold came in its known order — cheek, temple, wrist — and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from his wrist so the cold found the place. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the standing water and came down.

Sella did not say it. She had carried him to the edge at the half-pace, and she stood with the burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing and said the house where another woman would have said I, and gave the instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket, and turned the basket with her knee so the light fell where she wanted it and not where the draught came. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals, though there was no fire under the racks to hold. She told the water nothing. She told the fire it was the thaw.

Seventy days. The number sat in him with the weight of a small stone he could neither set down nor lift, and there was no one under any roof in \place{Sill} to give it to and no word in \lore{Grathic} in which to hand it over even if there had been.

\scenebreak

They went down the yard. Sella carried him at the half-pace past the house three doors down where the door stood a finger's width open and the mud lay against the step, and past \place[the Drawing house]{the death-house two doors down}, and the water had gone around that corner too. The ridge there had once been a finger's width thicker than Vail's and had turned the corner of the room without a break; now there was a line of old dark where it had been burned and called dealt with, and the water stood off that line as it had stood off everything else, a brown sheet gone flat in the thin light with a skin on it that the wind moved and did not break. Nobody had swept the line. Nobody had set a bucket under the door. The boy took it in from the height of the basket: the dark line, the brown water standing a hand's breadth short of it, the wet shine where the mud had come up and stopped. He had learned in a better-lit room what a man can do with a likeness laid too near to another likeness, and he set this line on a place of its own and did not lay the water on it or let either touch the other rooms of the same shape he kept, and wrote no cause across any of them. \person{Garl Vail}'s ridge was a different ridge. The line two doors down was a different line. He set them in two places, and neither place was a third.

At the corner of the \place{Vail kiln-house} the water had come up to within a hand's breadth of the wall and turned. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned that corner for a year and two days was dry above it, the colour of a board that has been rained on and dried, and the mud came to its lower edge and stopped as if a hand had drawn the line and told the water to keep to the other side of it. The boy lay with his cheek against the cold of the basket bow and looked at the narrow strip of dry wall between the mud and the line. It was no wider than two thumbs. It was the whole width of the house's standing.

Beside the clean dark line, higher up the wall where the damp had not reached, the new hairline of grey lay where it had lain since Silt 2, finer than the old line had ever been, a scratch a man might have taken for a hair caught in the lime. It had not been swept. The house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes, and it had not gone, and no hand had gone to it with a broom. The boy set three things in three places that did not touch: the line that stood, the water that had gone around it, the new grey that lay beside it unswept. Three objects in three places. He had no word in the district's tongue for the difference between a thing left where it lay and a thing taken away, and the only work available to him was to set them down inside himself where nobody could read them, which he did, and which bought him nothing he could lift a hand to.

And here, without a word and without an argument, something went into the file that was not a finding and not a rule. The water had gone around a thing the house had set down and the thing was still there. To leave a thing where it is and have it still kept: that was what the water had done. Nobody in the yard made anything of it. The woman at the rack had said the house was saved and gone back to her slips, and the man with mud at his calves had spat and gone down the yard, and no one had stood over the line with a proverb or a slate. He had once stood at the head of a table and watched a wound left open take a man, and here a house left a line standing and kept it, and he would not lay the two on one tray; he had learned what an answer wants too much will do to honest witnesses. He felt the pull of it where his old life had taught him the opposite — that what is left unentered goes bad, that a wound left open is a wound lost, that a chart turned down is a chart already closed — and felt the infant in him want the crust two inches from his ear and the breast under the wool, and both wanted, and neither was loud enough to become a cause. He filed the feeling as an object on its own tray, as he filed the intervals and the told bell and the low note of a district under water, and left it lying there unread. It was not an answer. It was a thing the water had done.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the afternoon went its ordinary way, as though the yard below had not stood full of water all day. Sella came in with wet wool and woodsmoke on her and the sour mineral tang of \lore{Ash} under both, and went to the fire before she went to anything else. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went through the floor four, four and a gap, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came. Above it the boards kept their other count, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two places under one roof.

\person{Tarn} was at the yard end with the small knife back on its sill and Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line. He held his broom in both hands and did not sweep with it. He said “Yes, house” to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the water had come and the paying was in the yard. His broom went over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and his head did not turn at the note a carried vessel gave past the door. The loan that can hear was worth more than the debt that held him, and the sentence that would call it lay under the roof where it had lain for six years. Six years were counted; the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He did not look toward the basket, and the boy kept his silence beside his own without letting the two touch.

Garl was kept by his objects. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and no hand had taken it down. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on the left that nobody filled. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and went around in the talk at the door as a woman goes around a pot set down in the dark, a careful circle with no word for the thing circled. Sella said his sayings to the pot and the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing,” she said to the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The pot had no crack in it. The saying had outlived the thing it was true about and cost more now than it had when there was a man to say it, and the boy set the proverb and the pot with no crack in it on two places and did not join them. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear and the empty place beside it went through another day without a name. He wanted the crust and wanted what had been taken out of the fold and could lift a hand to neither.

Dusk came into the room through the finger's width of open door and went nowhere else. Sella lifted the basket level and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals, and told the fire it was the thaw. She had not touched the mark, because there had been no opening, and she did not say the house had been saved.

Through the gap of the door came the low note of the water standing off the wall, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap, and the two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, given in a dark room where no fire was lit and taken on trust; the heard bell at \place{the Cross}, under which nobody crossed himself; the early note out of the stone under the falls at \place{the Singing Sill}; the kiln's missing breath — four low things in four places, and the district's new note going on underneath all four, enormous and unnamed, and the thing the water had done to the line lying on a place of its own and not joined to any of them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh had come. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid. The word that belonged to the shape of a double turn stayed a saying on its own tray, and was not spoken in this room. He did not lay one of these on another. A likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anyone put one there, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can do with an answer he wants too much.

In the corner the clean dark line stood where it had always stood, and the new grey lay beside it finer than the old line and unswept, and the water stood off the wall a hand's breadth short of both. Nobody had said the house was saved. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the corner and came down. The count went on.
