\daymark{255}{Smoke 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay where it had been put down, against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and the room under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof took it back the way a bench takes back a tool laid down at the end of work. \person[Corin]{the boy} did not wake to any number. The two-rhythm crossing that had closed the day before had been remembered into its place and not done again; he put it away with the same hand that had put away the others, without making a reach of it, a hearing, a gift, or a rule for a morning to obey. What had crossed a wall that does not touch was over. It lay where the next hand would find it without looking. Two hundred and fifty-four had stood exact at the going of the dark. In the dark it became two hundred and fifty-five, six months and seventy-four days became six months and seventy-five, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} went on giving its fifth unpaid day the payer it had given all the others, a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-five days. Smoke 16 would be earned by the morning's own light, when there was light enough to earn it; Smoke 20 stood four mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. There was no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on a Sixthday.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow, the dry smell of a kiln that had been fed and left to work. Under that came wet wool, sour with the breath of a body that had lain long in it. Under that, finer and more persistent, came the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, a smell that had been in the room so long it had stopped being an event and become a condition of the air. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall --- four low pulls, and the silence where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell. Nothing in this house struck four, four and a gap and called it a summons; the heard bell stayed in the square where it had sounded, beside the told bell, and was not folded into the morning's work.

There were three counts in three places and the boy kept them in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept its missing fifth. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, going on with its own business and never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts, and none rose into another. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them; a knot is not a nail, a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. He had learned the difference between an account and a likeness at a cost he could not enter anywhere. He kept the three where they were.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh had come and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The ownerless question stood at five askings on its own place with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no witness and no row named; five askings are not one witness and do not make a sixth, and a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, while none stood and none could be asked. He did not take any of these up in the dark and set them beside the three counts to see whether they would stand in a line. They were objects on separate trays, and the space between trays was part of the keeping. To lay them edge to edge would have been to ask what stood under them all, and nothing under any roof in Sill had ever answered to that question with a name.

The inventory came in the order the facts arrived, because that was the order the surgeon had been taught to trust. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even, for a third in that fold would be the beginning of an account and this house gave without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen hours since and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the severe face cold wears, the face that looks like composure and is not. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, and that if the warming came it must come from outside and slowly, and that the patient would seem better before he was worse. He knew all of it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder; the child's body had no vote and still it voted, and the surgeon took the facts in their order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of them, not even where he kept the count.

He could not lift the crust two inches to his mouth. He could not wipe the thread from his own lip. A man who had once set a shoulder back into its socket in the dark by feel alone lay under a fold of wool and could not move a hand to his own face; the small soft wrists outside the blanket had their own exact reach, and it ended at an inch. He took the humiliation as a fact rather than as a punishment, which was its own smaller defeat, and the taking cost him less than the wanting did. The afterdrop had a shape he knew without having to be told it: a patient in a cold room would seem to settle, the shivering would ease, the face would compose itself, and all the while the middle of him was going down. The hand at the wrist was small and pale and did not belong to him in any way he could use. He wanted the crust. He had wanted it on two hundred and fifty-five mornings and had never learned to want it less, and the wanting went on under the exactness the way a coal goes on under ash.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. The fire was low and the woman was quieter than the fire. She told it the thaw, as she had told it on the mornings before, turning the coals with the back of a broken pot so that the ash broke over them in a fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- as if she were setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. Her hand did not go to it. A shelf is not an opening, a well is not an opening, a rack, a road, a basket, a morning and a dusk are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and this morning's kiln had been opened and fed before any of it. When she spoke it was to the fire, the pot, the wool and the weather, never to the basket. The instruction went past him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

She had touched the mark once before the first firing of Smoke, held it one breath, let go, and touched it at no hour since. There had been no opening in the days between and so no reason to touch it; a rule held is still only a rule, and she did not make a virtue of holding it. When the coals had taken she turned the back of the pot over on its edge and set it down by the hearthstone, the burn closing on the handle and opening as she let go. \emph{There}, she told the pot, and then nothing more, because the pot had been told and the fire had been told and the morning had its instruction. She did not look toward the bed. She had not looked toward it while she worked and would not look toward it when she went out; the boy understood the refusal perfectly and had long since stopped taking it personally, which was not the same as not wanting her to look.

\scenebreak

On the flat stone near the fire the dressed grain lay under its wool. She lifted the wool for damp, put her fingers into the grain to the knuckle, and spread it thin so the air could take what the grain had held through the night; then she turned it, and gathered it by the handful, and set it under the fold flat again. The arm that did it knew what a handful weighed and not what it cost. She counted in glances and handfuls, and in the space between one heap and the next, and entered nothing on any slate. The grain had changed state three times before the boy's eyes and gained no figure. The figure that had once been spoken over it, twice, in a square where two hundred people leaned in, knew the wool and the cover and not the grain beneath; and the space between what that figure said and what the grain had cost had no signature on it. Smoke's first arithmetic lay locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, where it answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or for the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The second reading waited four mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. She put the wool down smooth and went to the shelf.

Turn it under wool before the damp takes it; that was the whole saying, and the handful that lifted it knew what it weighed and not what it cost, while the slate knew the figure and not the grain. The boy had heard the saying over the dressed grain in the middle tend of Smoke and had kept it as an instruction about order, not as a proverb with a lesson inside it. A thing not entered is not therefore free. That, too, was a sentence with a payer missing from it, and he left the payer missing rather than supplying one from the yard. The wool lay over the grain in a low pale ridge. Under the wool the grain was a handful and then another handful and then a covering, and that was the whole of the arithmetic he would allow it; there was no figure in it, and the absence of a figure was not a figure kept back.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kiln mouth} she set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. Two inches of air were the whole of the boy's reach; the distance was not cruelty and it was not mercy, but a measurement, and he had learned to take it without flinching and without thanks. At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath it stayed unread, and moved her lips along the two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on the empty air; then she took her hand away and the jar stood where it stood. There were two gaps in the row and one crust in the fold and an empty place beside the crust, and the boy kept each on its own tray with a finger between every pair. Four absences are four and are not an account until somebody stands under them to pay. Nobody did.

\scenebreak

Then he put the first question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; he had long since learned to ask the way a man tests a tool before he trusts it. Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of water over water, low and lower, and the wall between that sound and the bed did not touch anything but itself. An inch was a measurable thing. He had never asked the hand for more than an inch, because more than an inch in this body was a wish and not a procedure, and a wish put to a hand is a way of asking the world to make a thing that has not been paid for. The question he put now was small enough that a no would have cost him nothing; the answer, when it came, would cost him nothing either, and that was the whole of its value. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and came down at once on the river's next one, down on the note and not past it, not held, not carried across into the little silence afterwards, not made to finish a row that had no fifth stroke. It was an answer and not an asking. It was eighty-five, filed on the sixth place beside eighty-four with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. The hand came down at once and lay back against the fold. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept its fifth. Two hundred and fifty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath, six months and seventy-five days told by nobody, and the morning went on over the house without a boundary drawn anywhere in it.
