The morning went on over the house and did not stop at noon, because there was no boundary in it to stop at. Afternoon came out of it the way smoke comes out of a chimney that has been fed and left: not by ending, but by thinning until the eye can hardly find where it began. The heat settled behind the pot at \place{the hearth}, under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay. It was the dry heat of the second firing, a warmth with no flame in it, and it had been in the room so long that the room had gone on being warmed without taking notice of the warming. He took it as he took the rest of the day, as a fact with no speaker and no payer.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third had come into the fold to make the row come out even; a third there would have been the beginning of an account, and this house gave without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen hours since and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the face that looks like composure and is not. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, and that the warming, if it came, must come from outside and slowly, and that the patient would seem better before he was worse. He knew all of it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even where he kept the count.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the afternoon was a room of kept things. \person{Garl Vail} was not on stage and had not been for two hundred and fifty-five days, yet the house was so much the shape of him that a man might have drawn him from what was left. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door and kept a broader shoulder's outline in the dark, the crease at the left shoulder still where a man had set his weight against a bench to reach a high shelf. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, as though a body had only just risen from it and would come back to it. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over corners where the repair had been rubbed smooth: \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the house and around it, circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for it on Smoke 16, and none had gone down. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under the roof for two hundred and fifty-five days, and the boy had no word in which to speak it even had he wanted to, which he did not, because a name spoken here would be a name the house could enter.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around seven things, each with a look and a step that left room for a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no number spoken over any of them. The clean dark line at the Vail corner lay where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the finer grey hairline the yard called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, a thing left in place and therefore still kept. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them and entered them into nothing. On the ground by the racks the shallow clay pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say where the pan had stood. Beside \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather by everybody who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it. Out past \place{the terrace} on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place stood where a field should have stood, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was gone around as wide as any of the rest, with a look and no number and no name. Seven in a row are seven; they are not an account until a payer stands under them, and none stood. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon or a dusk, because none of these was an opening. A shelf is not an opening. A well is not an opening. The instruction had gone to the fire before the basket that morning and would go to it again at the going of the light, and between the two it went to the wool, the pot, the broom and the weather and never to the basket; the boy understood that a basket set down is a pot set down and left, and had long since stopped taking the refusal personally, which was not the same as not wanting her to look.

At the well \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee, and the boy was set down at the edge of the work where no slate lay. Clay was dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. A woman with a pot asked what the firings of Smoke had cost. Ilva's hand stayed in her sleeve, and the short strip of birch bark notched along one edge stayed where it was, not taken out, not read, not asked about, not joined to the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood or to any other private keeping. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.} She went on with her water and nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. The boy filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its own tray with a finger between it and every other keeping, nine objects and not a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. No figure came off the stick; the firings stood in wood where no clerk's thumb could read them.

A neighbour come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool, and Sella sent her to the pot and the weather with \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, and the woman went off level with her question still on her. A man come down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve; the figure arrived nowhere, and its not arriving was not a missing figure to be supplied by anybody standing by. The boy had learned to read a closed fist the way he had once read a chart turned face-down: the shape of the paper was not the writing under it, and a figure that arrives nowhere has not therefore been hidden from a man who wants it. He did not supply the missing figure, because a supplied figure is a forged one and the space where it would have stood was part of the keeping.

At \place{the kindling} the broom was given its instruction before anybody gave it to a person. \person{Tarn} took it up as a man takes up a tool already named and said \emph{Yes, house} to the early thaw and the late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and looked at it with the flat of his hand. Beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence about the man who had turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there, with no figure and no name. It was the ninth stopping, remembered and not made anew; nine stood on the tray and a tenth had not been stood up before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year had still not given. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, something settled and was still; the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and cold long since, and not tried is not forgiven. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. The stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped and nobody entered it.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the door of \place{the back room} kept its finger's width of open, and low voices had gone into it before first grey and gone again; the boy had the room only as a direction and a sound and followed neither, and no name was given to the men, and nobody said the room was cold. \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip} from the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them the way the boy had once parted clot from vessel on a towel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} To the pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, a knowing and not a power. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, the two tellings of the room shut in the war at two, the sayings about a pot at three with the premature note from Smoke 14 on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; no pot had been lost and no name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Sella's voice went low where the basket was set down near the fire, and the sentence did not finish: \emph{The fire's low. It wants---}. Before the end of it could arrive at the pot Jorra's bent finger had gone into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, taken the cold out of the fingertip, held while the rest of the sentence came to its end, moved a hair's breadth and been left standing. One piece of iron, two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man. There was no Grathic word for both mercies, and the boy filed them as one object joined to no kiln, worth nothing at two inches of air. The household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and neither man said what had been kept back.

The yard's discipline had been to go around absences with a look and a wide step; the house's discipline was to keep things back and let the work arrive at the pot instead of at a person. The boy lay under the roof with both disciplines in him and joined neither. Two silences stood side by side under the roof and no word was put between them, as it had not been put for years, and his private work was to hold them there without turning either into the other.

\scenebreak

Sella carried him up \place{the clay road} at the going of the light and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. She told the fire the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- as if she were setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals and she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, the little-door burn closing on nothing and opening again to the work. Then she went to her own bed. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-five days. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them.

In the small hours he put the second question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; a man tests a tool before he trusts it, and the testing is the whole of the work. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door, took the borrowed count from the room that does not touch his and the little silence that followed it after the count had ended, and came down before the letting-go --- down on the river's next note and not past it, not held into the silence, not carried across to make a fifth stroke in a row that had none. The two intervals were held apart in the inch and nowhere else; crossing them would have wanted a payer under both and none stood. It was an answer, not an asking, and not a seventh of the full note. It was eighty-six, filed on the sixth place beside eighty-five with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. The hand came down before the letting-go and lay back against the fold like a tool put away where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and fifty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and seventy-five days were told by nobody. The fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured, the widow's mark hung where it hung, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in it and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 20 stood four mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. The hand rested against the fold, and answer eighty-six lay on the sixth place beside eighty-five, an object and not a power.
