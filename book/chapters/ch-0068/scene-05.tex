\daymark{257}{Smoke 18, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

No number woke him. The hand lay where the day before had laid it, against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and the room under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof took it back as a bench takes back a tool within reach of the hand that will next want it. \person[Corin]{the boy} had come to the small hours on nothing but dry heat and the weight of wool. In the dark two hundred and fifty-six went the way all his days went, into two hundred and fifty-seven, and six months and seventy-six days became six months and seventy-seven, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to the payer it had given every day before it, a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-seven days. Smoke 18 would be earned by the morning's own light, when there was light enough to earn it; Smoke 20 stood two mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. The first firing of Frost stood far off and was not brought into a day that had not earned its heading. There was no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on an Eighthday.

Hot clay came first under the tallow, the dry breath of a kiln fed and left to work; under it wet wool, sour with the long lodging of a body in it; under that, finer and more lasting, the mineral sourness of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, which had left off being an event years ago and become a condition of the air. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall --- four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell. Under the floor the fire kept its four and its missing fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, going on with its own business and never numbered among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He left them in three places, because agreeing any two would want a payer set under both and no payer stood to any of them; a river is not a fire and a knot is not a nail. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh had come and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The ownerless question stood at five askings on its own place with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no witness and no row named; five askings are not one witness and do not make a sixth, and he did not open a sixth to see whether the wound had changed its mind.

The inventory came in the order the facts arrived, as it had on other mornings and would again. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the face that looks like composure and is not. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He knew all of it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon took the facts in their order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of them, not even where he kept the count.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. The fire was low and the woman was quieter than the fire. She told it the thaw, turning the coals with the back of a broken pot so the ash broke over them in a fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- as if she were setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once before the first firing of Smoke, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning or dusk since; the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, and an Eighthday morning is no opening. When she spoke it was to the fire, the pot, the wool, the bucket and the weather, never to the basket. The instruction went past him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

\scenebreak

The dressed grain lay where the day before had set it away, on the cool stone at \place[the drying racks]{the rack end} beyond the hearth's warmth, gathered by the handful and covered under wool, changed in state and given no figure. She lifted a corner of the wool for damp, put her fingers under it to the knuckle, turned the covered heap once and drew the wool back smooth, so that the pale ridge stood as it had stood. There was no gathering by the handful on this morning; that work had been done for the last time before Smoke's second reading, and a fifth turning would only have been a way of asking the covered thing to confess a number. The arm that had known the weight of each handful knew it still and gave nothing away. She counted in glances and in the space between one heap and the next, and entered nothing on any slate. \emph{Turn it under wool before the damp takes it} was the whole of the saying; she had heard it over the dressed grain in the middle tend of Smoke and kept it as an instruction about order, not as a proverb with a lesson folded inside it. The handful knew what the grain weighed and not what it cost; the slate knew the figure and not the grain.

The figure once spoken over it, twice, in a square where two hundred people leaned in, knew the wool and the cover and not what lay beneath. The space between what that figure said and what the grain had cost had no signature on it. Smoke's first arithmetic lay locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or for the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out; the second reading stood two mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. A thing not entered is not therefore free, and a thing covered is not therefore paid for. He held the two sentences side by side without laying either across the other, because a crossing would have wanted a payer and none stood to either.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kiln mouth} she set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. Two inches of air were the whole of his reach; the distance was not cruelty and it was not mercy but a measurement, and he had learned to take it without flinching and without thanks. The half-beat was its own small fact, a pause between the hand's arrival and the hand's going away, and he kept it where he kept such facts and did not call it anything. At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and moved her lips along the two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on empty air, then took her hand away; the jar stood where it stood. Two gaps in the row, one crust in the fold, an empty place beside the crust, and the field's empty place far off: he kept each on its own tray with a finger between every pair. Four absences are four and are not an account until somebody stands under them to pay, and nobody did.

A neighbour come up from \place[the worked strips]{the strips} with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool. Sella did not turn from the coals. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, she told the pot and the weather, and the woman went off level with her question still on her, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. A man come down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt stopped short of \place{the yard well}. He brought a number up out of a fist toward the water, looked at it as a man looks at a thing he has already decided not to give, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere, and its not arriving was not a figure hidden from somebody who wanted it. He read the shut fist by its shape as a man reads a paper laid writing-down: the shape was not the figure under it, and a figure that arrives nowhere has not therefore been withheld. He supplied nothing to fill the space, because a supplied figure is a forged one and the space where it would have stood was part of the keeping. No name was spoken over any of it and no row was made to come out even.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \lore{frost-bloom} had written itself in white fronds on the stone lip of the yard well, where the Warm had lately gone out of the stone. It was a map of a leak, the boy knew, the shape a vessel makes when the heat in it has gone somewhere it was not meant to go; he had kept that knowledge without ever being told it and without a word in \lore{Grathic} to speak it. He kept the white lip now on a place of its own and gave it no cause. The rope went over the writing and the bucket knocked the heads off it; by the time the bucket had gone around twice the stone was grey again under the wet, and the yard had called it weather and gone on drawing. Beside the well \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather by those who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it.

A woman letting the bucket down wet a finger in the water and tasted it. \emph{It tastes different}, she said, and did not say what it had tasted like before. A man waiting his turn said the ice had been honeyed under the mud in Ilmen, which lay a long day's walk off and a whole month behind them. The two sayings passed each other without an owner, the way two pails go different ways on one rope; nobody took either up, and neither woman nor man went home obliged to own the morning. The argument ended where the bucket-round always ended, in the one word that grants a person permission to stop looking, and the boy took no side in it because there was no side to take and no payer standing under either saying. He had watched the frost come and go on this lip before and had written no cause across it then. The white lip was not a hearing and not a seventh of the full note; it was not a payer, not one of the held changes, not the widow's mark --- a well is not an opening --- and not the first firing of Frost, which stood far off and unbrought. A well is a well. The cold in it had gone somewhere, and the yard drew water and called it weather, and the two facts went past each other without meeting, as they had on other mornings and would again.

Sella gave the instruction to the bucket and the fire and never to the basket. She went around the dark trough with a look and a wide step and no number, carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate, and told the wool to keep the river wind off his ears as though the wool had been a person and the wind a bill neither of them had asked to see. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said to the fire, not to him. He had long since stopped taking the refusal personally, which was not the same as not wanting her to look.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he put the fifth question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; a man tests a tool before he trusts it, and the testing is the whole of the work. Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of water over water, low and lower, and the wall between that sound and the bed did not touch anything but itself. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and held for one rhythm, from a note to the note following it; then it took the little silence after a count that had ended in the room that does not touch and carried that one rhythm across the silence, crossing no second count against it, borrowing no house count and filling no empty interval to make a fifth stroke in a row that had none. An inch was a measurable thing and the silence was an empty thing, and he kept both as they were. The hand came down on the river's next low note, down on it and not past it. It was an answer and not an asking. It was eighty-nine, filed on the sixth place beside eighty-eight with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. The hand came down and lay back against the fold like a tool put away where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and fifty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and seventy-seven days were told by nobody. The fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured away from the hearth; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in it and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 20 stood two mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. The hand rested against the fold, and answer eighty-nine lay on the sixth place beside eighty-eight, an object and not a power.
