\scenebreak

Afternoon thinned without ending. It lost its edges by degrees, the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals, and the work of the yard went on inside the thinning light as though the day had never been asked to account for itself. The dry warmth of the kiln travelled into the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and settled there behind the pot, a small residue that would outlast the light and be gone before morning, and nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof gave it a name. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay where he had been laid, carried and silent, and took the afternoon in the order it arrived, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the facts present themselves: light, heat, wool, the small refusal of his own body to be useful. The light went. The heat stayed. The wool kept the shape of a body that had been in it a long time and smelled sour with the lodging.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hardened past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went forward nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He knew all of it and he wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder, and he entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

\person{Garl Vail} was not on stage and had not been for two hundred and fifty-seven days, yet the house was so entirely the shape of him that a man might have drawn him out of what was left, the way a potter draws a missing vessel from the ring it left on the bench. The salt-stiff coat kept a broader shoulder's outline on its peg behind the door, the crease at the left shoulder still where a man had set his weight against a bench to reach a high shelf. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, as though a body had only just risen from it and would come back to it before the coals were cold. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over corners where the repair had been rubbed smooth: \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the house and around it, circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for it on Smoke 16, 17 or 18, and none had gone down it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under the roof for two hundred and fifty-seven days, and the boy had no word in which to speak it even had he wanted to, which he did not, because a name spoken here would be a name a house could enter.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him. She took the basket up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, the way a woman carries a pot filled to the brim and does not look into it to see whether it has slopped, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The light was going off the terrace in long flat strips and the road kept the last of it in the ruts. She came back down through \place{the Clay Yard} and went wide around seven things, each with a look and a step that left room for a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no number spoken over any. The clean dark line at the Vail corner lay where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the finer grey hairline the yard called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, a thing left in place and therefore still kept. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them and entered them into nothing; the shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring on the ground, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say where the pan had stood. Beside \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather by those who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it. Out past \place{the terrace} on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place stood where a field should have stood, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was gone around as wide as any of the rest, with a look and no number and no name. Seven in a row are seven. They are not an account until a payer stands under them, and none stood.

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it was, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and no instruction had ever been given about the thread. Then she went to \place{the hearth}. She told the fire the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot so the ash broke over them in a fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- as though she were setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. Then she went to her own bed.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once before the first firing of Smoke, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since; the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is no opening. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again, made into no lesson, no sign, no mending and no payment, with no payer named to any of it. The next real opening was the first firing of Frost, far off and unbrought. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in it, and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong by anybody in the yard.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the broom was given its instruction before anybody gave it to a person. \person{Tarn} took it up as a man takes up a tool already named and said \emph{Yes, house} to the early thaw and the late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot anywhere, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and looked at it with the flat of his hand. Beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence about the man who had turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there, with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stood on the tray and a tenth had not been stood up before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year had still not given. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it out, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, something settled and was still; the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and cold long since, and not tried is not forgiven. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. The stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped and nobody entered it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay beneath the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the room kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices, which arrived at the basket as a direction and nothing more. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. Three shapings stood on their own place and stayed three sayings about a pot; the premature note from Smoke 14 stood beside them on a place of its own and no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause. \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. No name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot was lost in the yard. When Sella's sentence went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing: one piece of iron and two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned the file out as a surgeon turns instruments out on a towel at the beginning of a case, each thing laid where the next hand will find it without looking, and found the whole of it exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. There was no hand to enter any of it and no slate within reach, and the towel lay on nothing but the fold. He went through it in its order, and the order was the whole of the keeping; a thing laid on the wrong tray is a thing made to answer a question nobody had asked it.

Six hearings stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, with air under them and a wanting in them, and beside them three shapings of the pot saying with the premature note from Smoke 14 on its own place; no fourth likeness had stood up to call itself a cause, and the pot had not been lost. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three, though the damper in the shed held its double bite under one piece of iron and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. The heard bell stood beside the told bell, its four, four and a gap not laid across the fire's four and missing fifth or the boards' four nails and thumb-width knot. The told figure stood beside the paid figure, and Smoke's first arithmetic lay locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing this towel could hold and nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year had still not given; the Seed 16 breath lay unspent and the loan uncalled.

The Day 161 fraction at the damper lay on its own place; the Day 166 held beat beneath the loaf shelf lay on a second; the flagstone half-check of Tallow 22 on a third. The small dry scrape of a pot's foot from the loft sill lay on a fourth, the pale line of the carried man on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with its honest answers standing on it. Six places and a finger between every pair, and nothing laid across two of them, for a crossing would have wanted a payer set under both and no payer stood to any. Nine record-first hands stood on their own places with a finger between every pair, and the ninth was \person{Ilva}'s, taken from the firing work and not from any house or Court; her notched strip of birch stayed in her sleeve, unread, unasked about, not taken out and not joined to the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood, and no tenth hand was filed from a hand that did not reach. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over either. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool away from the hearth, changed in state and carrying no figure; the figure that would be read over it stood two mornings off and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The pan's gone ring, the fold's one crust and its empty place, the shelf's two gaps and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. The returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 was gone around and not named.

The ownerless question stood at five askings on its seventh place with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no witness and no row named. Five askings are not one witness and do not make a sixth; he had not supplied a sixth to make the row come out even, and had not opened the wound a sixth time to see whether it had changed its mind. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none had stood. The towel held the whole of it and the whole of it was worth nothing at two inches of air; that was not a loss entered, for a thing worth nothing is not therefore free, and a thing kept is not therefore settled. He kept the two sentences side by side without laying either across the other.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, low over lower, going on with its own business and never numbered among the hearings. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} held at four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-seven days. The wall between the note and the bed did not touch anything but itself, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house and no fire-sitter standing over the coals, so the only silence he borrowed was the little one after a count that had ended in the room that does not touch.

He put the sixth question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and held for one rhythm; then it took the little silence after the count that had ended in the other room and carried that one rhythm across the empty interval as an object in its own right, neither filling it to make a fifth stroke in a row that had none nor crossing a second count against it, borrowing no house count and making no two counts agree. An inch was a measurable thing and the silence was an empty thing, and he kept both as they were. The hand came down on the river's next low note, down on it and not past it. It was an answer and not an asking. It was ninety, filed on the sixth place beside eighty-nine with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it. The hand came down and lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and fifty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and seventy-seven days were told by nobody. Ninety honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid the same number of days and its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. The file lay open on the towel with every tray parted and every gap left gap. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn, and the count went forward into Smoke 19, a day that had not earned its heading; Smoke 20 stood one morning beyond that and was not reached for, and the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The hand rested against the fold, and answer ninety lay filed on the sixth place, an object and not a power.
