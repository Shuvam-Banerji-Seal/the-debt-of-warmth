\daymark{256}{Smoke 17, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

No number woke him. The hand lay where the day before had left it, against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and the room under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof took it back as a bench takes back a tool within reach of the hand that will next want it. \person[Corin]{the boy} had not woken to a count. In the dark two hundred and fifty-five went the way all his days went, into two hundred and fifty-six, and six months and seventy-five days became six months and seventy-six, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to the payer it had given every day before it, a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-six days. Smoke 17 would be earned by the morning's own light when there was light enough to earn it; Smoke 20 stood three mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. There was no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on a Seventhday.

Hot clay came first under the tallow, the dry breath of a kiln fed and left to work; under it wet wool, sour with the long lodging of a body in it; under that, finer and more lasting, the mineral sourness of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, which had left off being an event years ago and become a condition of the air. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall --- four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell. Under the floor the fire kept its four and its missing fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He left them in three places, because agreeing any two would want a payer set under both and no payer stood to any of them; a river is not a fire and a knot is not a nail.

The inventory came in the order the facts arrived. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if a line had been written out for it and no farther. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the face that looks like composure and is not, and he knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He knew all of it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon took the facts in their order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of them, not even where he kept the count.

He could not lift the crust two inches to his mouth. He could not wipe the thread from his own lip. A man who had once set a shoulder back into its socket in the dark by feel alone lay under a fold of wool and could not carry a hand to his own face; the small pale wrists outside the blanket had their own exact reach and it ended at an inch. He took the humiliation as a fact rather than as a punishment, which was its own smaller defeat, and the taking cost him less than the wanting did. The afterdrop had a shape he knew without being told it: a patient in a cold room settles, the shivering eases, the face composes itself, and all the while the middle of him is going down. He had wanted the crust on two hundred and fifty-six mornings and had never learned to want it less, and the wanting went on under the exactness the way a coal goes on under ash.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the fire the thaw, turning the coals with the back of a broken pot so the ash broke over them in fine grey dust and the heat came up again with no flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- as if she were setting tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a morning or a dusk, because none of these was an opening. It had been touched once before the first firing of Smoke, held one breath, let go, and touched at no hour since; a rule held is still only a rule and she did not make a virtue of holding it. When the coals had taken she set the back of the pot on its edge by the hearthstone and told the pot \emph{There}, and that was the whole of the instruction. The instruction went past the basket the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. She did not look toward \place{the bed}, and had not looked toward it while she worked.

\scenebreak

The dressed grain lay under its wool on the flat stone near the fire, and though the work of it belonged to \place{the drying racks}, the damp of the night had to be taken out under the roof before the day's heat settled. She lifted the wool, put her fingers into the grain to the knuckle and spread it thin so the air could take what the night had left in it; then she turned it, and gathered it by the handful, and set it under the fold flat again. The arm that did it knew what a handful weighed and not what it cost. She counted in glances and handfuls and in the space between one heap and the next, and entered nothing on any slate. \emph{Turn it under wool before the damp takes it} was the whole of the saying; the handful knew the weight and the slate knew the figure, and neither knew the other's work. In the old life he had weighed provisions by the handful in a room with a tile floor and a clock on the wall, and the handful had been honest there too; he kept that thought in the place where such thoughts were kept, without letting it into the morning. The grain had changed state three times before his eyes and gained no figure.

The figure once spoken over it, twice, in a square where two hundred people leaned in, belonged to \person{Berr Clay}, who had wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing and had gone up \place{the clay road} with the box locked and the true subtraction inside. That figure knew the wool and the cover and not the grain beneath, and the space between what it said and what the grain had cost had no signature on it. Smoke's first arithmetic had changed the district's figures for the first time in Smoke and could not be changed back; the second reading waited three mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. A thing not entered is not therefore free, and a thing covered is not therefore paid for. He had learned to hold the two sentences side by side without laying either across the other, because a crossing would have wanted a payer and none stood to either.

Under the roof \person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects, as he had been kept for two hundred and fifty-six days --- salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, wheel-seat hollow on that side, proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack and said now over corners where the repair had been rubbed smooth. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the house and around it, circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up the clay road for it on Smoke 17 and none had gone down, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under the roof for two hundred and fifty-six days and the boy had no word in which to speak it, which was no loss, because a name spoken here would be a name the house could enter.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kiln mouth} she set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. Two inches of air were the whole of his reach; the distance was not cruelty and it was not mercy but a measurement, and he had learned to take it without flinching and without thanks. The half-beat at the setting was its own small fact, a pause between the hand's arrival and the hand's going away, and he kept it on the tray where he kept such facts and did not call it anything. At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and moved her lips along the two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on empty air, and took her hand away; the jar stood where it stood. Two gaps in the row, one crust in the fold, an empty place beside the crust, and the field's empty place far off: he kept each on its own tray with a finger between every pair. Four absences are four and are not an account until somebody stands under them to pay, and nobody did.

\scenebreak

A neighbour come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool. Sella sent her to the pot and the weather with \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, and the woman went off level with her question still on her, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. A man come down the clay road with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere, and its not arriving was not a figure hidden from a man who wanted it. He had learned to read a closed fist the way he had once read a chart turned face-down: the shape of the paper was not the writing under it, and a figure that arrives nowhere has not therefore been withheld. He supplied no figure to fill the space, because a supplied figure is a forged one and the space where it would have stood was part of the keeping.

\scenebreak

The turning of the grain came to its end in the ordinary way, with no word spoken over the ending. She had turned it under wool for damp, spread it thin, turned it, gathered it by the handful and set it flat again on other mornings; on this one she gathered it in by the handful for the last time before Smoke's second reading. She did not say that it was the last time. She put her fingers into the grain to the knuckle, lifted it, felt the weight of it in the cup of her palm, and laid each handful under the fold flat as she had laid the others. The arm knew the weight and not the cost, as it had known it every other morning, and nothing in the arm's knowledge changed when the work ended. When the last of it was gathered she drew the wool up over it and set the covered heap away from the hearth, on the cool stone at the rack end, where the day's warmth would not come at it. The pale ridge of wool lay there with no figure in it and no figure over it.

On Smoke 20 the district's arithmetic would be read over grain that carried no account of what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be spoken and the price paid for the grain would stand unsigned. The arithmetic would not know the cost; that was all, and it was enough. Smoke 20 stood three mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned its heading. No loaf passed, no slate opened, no gap came into the two-gapped row to make a third, and the one crust in the fold kept its unnamed empty place. Nothing was made a lesson of the covering and nothing was made a payer of it. The boy kept the difference between the figure and the cost on the place where he kept such differences, and did not name it, and did not hand it to the yard to be entered. The house's arithmetic moved on over the covered grain as water moves over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the keeping went with it.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he put the third question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; a man tests a tool before he trusts it, and the testing is the whole of the work. Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of water over water, low and lower, and the wall between that sound and the bed did not touch anything but itself. He had borrowed a house count on other mornings and taken the little silence after it; on this morning there was no house count to borrow and no silence from a room that does not touch. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and held for the river's own interval, from one note to the note that followed it, taking nothing from the hearth, nothing from the coals, and nothing from the lips that had moved along the two-gapped row. An inch was a measurable thing and the interval was a length of water going over water, and he kept the two apart. The hand came down on the river's own next note, down on it and not past it, not held into the silence afterwards, not carried across to make a fifth stroke in a row that had none. It was an answer and not an asking. It was eighty-seven, filed on the sixth place beside eighty-six with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh of it, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. The hand came down and lay back against the fold like a tool put away where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and fifty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and seventy-six days were told by nobody. The fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured away from the hearth; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in it and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 20 stood three mornings beyond the close and was not reached for. The hand rested against the fold, and answer eighty-seven lay on the sixth place, an object and not a power.
