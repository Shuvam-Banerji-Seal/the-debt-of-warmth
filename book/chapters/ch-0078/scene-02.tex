The river's note stayed quiet through the first of the light and came back low under the finger's-width door as the day went on, and by the middle of the afternoon it had thinned to the temperature of a pot lifted off the coals and set upon a cold stone. It did not go all at once. Heat drew back from the fold and sat behind the pot, keeping a little on the side that had faced the fire. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} with that small remainder about him, and took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and five days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down from \place{the Vail kiln-house} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, out of the dry cold under the eaves and into the yard's thin light. She carried him like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and she gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody: \emph{Go around}, she told it, and the bristles went where she had told them, and nobody's slates lay where she set him down. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and her hand was never once given an instruction to the basket. Then she went around the places the yard goes around, with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither, though the broom was in her hand. She went as wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack}, where the broken birth-slates lay, as a woman sets a pail of hot water where a child might knock it. She went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word left in it. She went around \place{the trough} skinned over at \place{the yard well}, whose water the yard called weather. She went around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out.

Seven were seven and not an account. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain lying covered under wool at \place{the rack end}; it went on its own tray with a finger between it and every other private keeping, and the boy did not make three absences into a cause. What he wanted to know was what a hand keeps to itself, and the rule that a keeping is not his to read checked the knowing before it went any further. He filed the check as the exact order of another person's reaching, nothing more. It was not envy and it was not a virtue; he had learned to distrust both of those, because each of them wants a payer put under it and none stood here.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of the well past the going of the light and into another afternoon, and the yard called it weather. The rope stiffened in the hand and gave its small dry rasp; the water in the trough kept its skin. The plate of ice that had been lifted off the trough stood where it had been set on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it, and nobody moved it and nobody took a figure off it. The surgeon's reading of a covering laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4; no sign, lesson, cause, mending or payer was made of either whiteness, and no fourth likeness of three was made a cause. The yard kept its count of the firings in wood, where no slate could take a figure off it, and the boy kept that wooden keeping on a place of its own too, unopened and unread.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and the hollow on that side that nobody filled. The clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it and neither was swept. The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled; a debt a man can go around is a debt that never stops standing in the ruts. His proverb had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and it came back now out of Sella's mouth to the coals as it had come a hundred times: \emph{Feed the fire before you feed yourself}. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-five days. No collector came up \place{the clay road} for the tiles and none went down it, and the keeping of him was maintenance, not a story, and no lesson was made of any of it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill, with six years counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood what he told it every day, in the tone of a man stating the weather. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.}

To those who wanted the thaw early he said \emph{Yes, house}, and to those who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house}, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot anywhere between them. He kept the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts and had stood there since the water went. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach he drew and did not spend, and his chest was tight as it had been since the dark.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and there it stopped, with no figure and no name. It was a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, and it was still ungiven. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with the broom. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped, joined to no cause and to no ear. Up in \place{the Debt Loft} a sound had a direction and never a room he was taken into; the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiveness, and the small dry scrape of the sill lay on a place of its own, not laid across the carried man's pale line.

At the edge of the wheel-shed \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, moving the tongue a hair's breadth and leaving it standing so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot. The iron took the warmth out of his fingertip. The boy read one iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

\place{The back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices. Men unnamed had gone low into it before first grey and had been given no name, and the room kept its width and its sound and was to the boy a direction and never a room he was taken into. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over \place{the boards} and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place apart, and no fourth likeness had been made a cause.

No sixth asking came over the boards. The ownerless question stood at five askings with one further shaping, a finger between every pair and no witness standing under any and no row named; the silence was a keeping and not a sixth witness. No sixth hearing came either, and the empty seventh place kept its emptiness and was not filled to make the row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three counts stayed in three places --- \lore{the carried fire}'s four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should stand, its payer unnamed; the river's low thing over lower; the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot --- and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, went around the places the yard goes around, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. \emph{It was the thaw}, she told the fire, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on the broken pot and then on nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with that wrist so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and gave no instruction at any hour to the basket. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at an afternoon or a going of the light, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Two private keepings went on side by side under this roof and neither was opened: the charcoal on the underside of \place{the loaf shelf}, which her finger kept on top of the front edge so it stayed unread, and the count the yard kept in wood, which no slate could take a figure off. He wanted very much to know what a hand keeps to itself, and he did not read either one, and the not-reading was the exact order of another person's reaching filed apart, not a virtue and not a loss.

In the small hours the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed, and the room whose floor does not touch his went on with its low counting --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy took from it only what the hand could take. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch. It took the small intake of one counted breath from that room and the hold of the next, set them side by side with a finger between, left the letting-go between them where it was, and came down on the river's next low note of \lore{water over water}. It was two intakes held apart as objects on the sixth place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Neither was made to agree with the carried fire's missing fifth, and no house count was borrowed and no silence crossed that belonged to another's breathing. It was kept as an honest coordination; it was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at setting one sound against another was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

One hundred and forty-six stood beside one hundred and forty-five. Two hundred and eighty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and five days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. He made nothing of the number and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Beyond the finger's-width door the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and the plate of ice stood where it had been set, clouded where the silt rose into it. Frost 20 (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood two mornings beyond the close and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The boy lay with the crust two inches off and its empty place beside it unnamed, the hand set down on the hundred and forty-sixth answer with the letting-go between the two intakes left where it was. The river's low note went quiet after the hand came down.
