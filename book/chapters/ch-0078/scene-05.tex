\daymark{287}{Frost 18, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours took the heat the way a cold stone takes a pot set down upon it, drawing it out of the fold and holding what was left on the side that had last faced the fire. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and beyond the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, low over lower, a sound that goes on whether a room is listening to it or not. Frost the eighteenth had come on its own morning and had not been poured into the day behind it; it came as the light came, earned by that light and not by any count kept under a roof, and there was no market, no gathering at \place{the Cross} and no bell on an Eighthday. He came into it two hundred and eighty-seven days old, six months and one hundred and seven days, and nobody in Low Kiln, Sill or Grath said so. The number was exact and complete, set edge to edge against a house that kept its days in loaves, in glances and in the unnamed space where a loaf should have stood, and it bought him nothing he could spend.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy, and the order had not changed in two hundred and eighty-seven mornings. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and seven days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-seven days; at the finger's-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not folded into the three and was not heard as a seventh of the full note, but stayed a heard bell beside a told bell.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket and gave the instruction to the fire and the pot and the wool and never to the thing she carried. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I} and \emph{the boy} where another woman would have said \emph{my son}, and she never finished a sentence that would have required her to say what she felt, which was not the same as not knowing she had left it unfinished. She stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; the half-beat was the whole distance between the thing a hand is told to take and the thing it is told not to take, and she gave it to the pot and never to the boy. When she put the fold right at his ear and under his chin she did not look at his face, and she left the thread frozen where it lay, because nobody had told her about the thread and she had told nobody about it either. With her back to the basket she kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread, uncopied and unfigured; she moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat at a morning, a hearth and a rack, because none of these was an opening and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, sign, mending or payer made of it, and the next opening stood in a later month's first firing, far off and not hoped for.

On the cool stone at \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool, away from the hearth and the fine grey dust of \place{the drying racks}, and she turned it once for damp with a touch lighter than the morning before and lighter again than the morning before that, and gave it no figure. The arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 did not know what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it and nobody had put a hand there to sign it. Frost's first arithmetic stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln}, the printed one in twelve unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the reading was not looked at again this morning as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment. Nothing came off the road back under this roof to stand where the loaves stood or where the empty space stood; the district's figures had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and the boy kept the told figure beside the paid figure with a finger between them and made no third of the two.

She carried him down from under the roof at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and in the yard she went around the seven places the yard goes around, with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither though the broom was in her hand. She went as wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack}, where the broken birth-slates lay, as a woman sets a pail of hot water where a child might knock it. She went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word left in it. She went around \place{the trough}, skinned over at \place{the yard well}, whose water the yard called weather; she went around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool at the rack end; it went on its own tray with a finger between it and every other private keeping. Her broom went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} by \place{the kindling} without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped since Seed 22; neither she nor the yard made a figure of it.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of the well into another morning, and the yard called it weather; the rope stiffened in the hand and gave its small dry rasp, and the water in the trough kept its skin. The plate of ice lifted off the trough stood where it had been set on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. None of it was entered, and no sign, cause, lesson, mending or payer was made of any of it; the surgeon's reading of a covering laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, and no fourth likeness of three was made a cause.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and went now to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{feed the fire before you feed yourself}. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-seven days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story; no collector came up the clay road and none went down it, and the boy made no lesson of the uncalled debt. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to him only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

The Frost 14 well-saying stayed on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand with a finger between: one saying, with no second shaping on this morning, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. A woman waiting her turn at the yard well had said it to the boards and the well-roof three days back, that the runner who carried Berr Clay's slate once carried a figure that named a person she knew; she had said it the way the yard says the weather and gone on with her bucket, and no owner of the voice stood anywhere to be asked. It was not joined to the strip in a sleeve at the well, nor to a thane's twice-wiped thumb, nor to the clerk's inward slate, nor to the charcoal under the shelf, and it did not make Hessa a reader of what she had carried for years. \person{Ilva}'s ninth stayed nine on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, her notched strip still in her sleeve and unread, and nobody stood in the yard to be asked.

The held interval and the empty interval lay side by side on the sixth place with a finger between, and neither they nor the saying was made to stand up and call itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stands to any of them. The ownerless question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness and no row named; the nine stoppings stood with no tenth; the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth of either; the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust and its empty place, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place stayed each on a tray of its own with no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause. He kept the house's not-doings on one place and his own gaps on another with a finger between, and neither stood up and called itself a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next opening stood in a later month's first firing, far off and not counted toward. Frost the twentieth (Day 289), the month's second Account-day and market, stood one morning beyond the close of this day's work and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; the boy made nothing of the nearness and would not let it stand up as a sign.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note of water over water. It took the small intake of the river's own going and let it go on the same note without holding, and came down, the letting-go kept whole. It was not an ending made early, not a freedom and not a gift, for a freedom wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything; it was an honest coordination, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon's clean observation of how a rhythm ends was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue, and the two went on without a doctrine standing up out of them.

One hundred and forty-nine stood beside one hundred and forty-eight. Two hundred and eighty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and seven days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. He made nothing of the number and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Beyond the finger's-width door the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and the plate of ice stood where it had been set, clouded where the silt had risen into it. The boy lay on his bed under the roof with the grain still covered under wool at the rack end beyond the door, the hand come down on the letting-go with the river's note going quiet after it, the letting-go left whole.
