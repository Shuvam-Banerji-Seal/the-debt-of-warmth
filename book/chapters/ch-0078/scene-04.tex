The river's note had gone quiet when the hand came down, and it came back low under the finger's-width door as the day went on, the way a sound returns to a room after the kettle has been carried out of it. By the middle of the afternoon the light had thinned to the temperature of a pot lifted off the coals and set upon a cold stone. Heat drew back from the fold and sat behind the pot, keeping a little on the side that had faced the fire. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, with that small remainder about him, and took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and six days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-six days; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on Frost 10, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not folded into the three and was not heard as a seventh of the full note, but stayed a heard bell beside a told bell.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down from \place{the Vail kiln-house} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, out of the dry cold under the eaves and into the yard's thin light. She carried him like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and she gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody: \emph{Go around}, she told it, and the bristles went where she had told them, and nobody's slates lay where she set him down. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and her hand was never once given an instruction to the basket. Then she went around the places the yard goes around, with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither, though the broom was in her hand. She went as wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack}, where the broken birth-slates lay, as a woman sets a pail of hot water where a child might knock it. She went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word left in it. She went around \place{the trough} skinned over at \place{the yard well}, whose water the yard called weather. She went around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain lying covered under wool at \place{the rack end}; it went on its own tray with a finger between it and every other private keeping, and the boy did not make three absences into a cause.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of the well into another afternoon, and the yard called it weather. The rope stiffened in the hand and gave its small dry rasp; the water in the trough kept its skin. The plate of ice lifted off the trough stood where it had been set on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. None of it was entered, and no sign, cause, lesson, mending or payer was made of any of it. The surgeon's reading of a covering laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, and no fourth likeness of three was made a cause. The yard kept its count of the firings in wood, where no slate could take a figure off it, and the boy kept that wooden keeping on a place of its own too, unopened and unread.

At the yard well \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. She was of the firing work and not of a house, and went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all. The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay, and watched the order of her reaching the way he had once watched a hand go past a chart to a signature.

A woman with a pot asked what the month's firings had taken, now that the first reading had gone up \place{the clay road} and the district's arithmetic had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of birch bark with its notches was not taken out, not read, not asked about, not given a figure off, not joined to the charcoal under the shelf, the cord, the knots, the pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line. Her eyes went to the asker before her mouth opened, and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

She said no figure and gave no name to the thing she kept, and put the pail back to the rope and went on with her water while nobody turned to look at her twice. The bucket knocked the stone lip and took up a little of the stayed white on the north side; the rope gave its dry rasp and the water in the trough kept its skin. Nothing came off the stick into the yard's speech, and no figure went up the road behind the reading to stand under the two hundred that had changed.

The boy read the order: hand toward the record, thumb over the notches, eyes to the asker, and only then the mouth. He filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed nine on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it; the notches stayed in her sleeve, not his to read, and she did not know she had been read. What he wanted to know was what a hand keeps to itself, and the rule that another person's keeping is not his to read checked the knowing before it went any further. The check was not envy and it was not a virtue; he had learned to distrust both, because each wants a payer put under it and none stood here. He took the order of the reaching and nothing else, and let the woman go back to her bucket.

The Frost 14 well-saying stayed on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between: one saying, with no second shaping on this afternoon, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. A woman waiting her turn at the well had said it to the boards and the well-roof three days back, that the runner who carried \person{Berr Clay}'s slate once carried a figure that named a person she knew; she had said it the way the yard says the weather and gone on with her bucket, and no owner of the voice stood anywhere to be asked. The saying was not joined to the strip in a sleeve a pace from the well, nor to a thane's twice-wiped thumb, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate, nor to the charcoal under the shelf or any private keeping, and it did not make Hessa a reader of what she had carried for years. Nobody stood in the yard to be asked, and the silence under the boards was a keeping and not a witness.

The shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} had been kept as it was since Seed 22, and the broom stroke went over it without the half-check, the stones still keeping the place where it had stopped. The small dry scrape of the loft sill lay on a place of its own and the carried man's pale line on another, and neither was laid across the other, because a likeness laid across a thing is the beginning of a cause and no cause stood to either.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside it lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt, and the two kept company without either being entered. He took the broom only after it had been instructed, said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts. To the wood he said, in the tone of a man stating the weather, \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; a tenth standing up now to make a row come out even would be a stopping forged. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and his chest was tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the bright patch without the half-check and did not turn along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. No collector came. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} had the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her unfinished sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing; the boy read one iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \place[the back room]{The back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices; men unnamed had gone low into it before first grey and were given no name, and he did not say the room was cold. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three pot sayings stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place apart and no fourth likeness made a cause. At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. With her back to the basket she kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal stayed unread, moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. The pot rim she stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; the half-beat was the whole distance between the thing a hand is told to take and the thing it is told not to take, and she gave it to the pot and never to the boy. Every instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat at a going of the light, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

In the small hours the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed and he walked the shorter inventory in its order, because a thing hurried is a thing entered wrongly. The five askings stood with one shaping and no witness; the nine stoppings stood with no tenth; the nine record-first hands stood with a finger between the ninth and the eighth and nobody standing anywhere to be asked; the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth of either; the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust and its empty place, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place stayed each on a tray of its own with no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause. The well-saying stood where it had been said, one saying on a place of its own beside Hessa's eighth hand, and the strip in a sleeve a pace from the well stayed unread on a place of its own; a voice that carried a figure it would not name and a hand that kept a figure it would not give lay a pace apart and were not joined, and the boy's refusal to join them was the same refusal that kept nine hands nine.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note. It took two intakes from the room whose floor does not touch his, small and counted, set them apart with a finger between, and bridged them over the river's next low note of water over water. The bridge was laid down as an object on the sixth place and not crossed as a span; no house count was borrowed for it, no fifth was supplied to the kiln, no silence that belonged to another's breathing was crossed to fetch a figure for it, and the well-saying was left where it was said. It was an honest coordination, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at setting two intakes over a third sound was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

One hundred and forty-eight stood beside one hundred and forty-seven. Two hundred and eighty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and six days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. He made nothing of the number and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved.
