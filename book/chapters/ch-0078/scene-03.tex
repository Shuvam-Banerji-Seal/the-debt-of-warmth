\daymark{286}{Frost 17, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

Frost came on its own morning and was not brought into the day behind it. It came the way cold comes into a house that has been warm all night, by degrees and from the outside, laying its small hand first on the iron latch and then on the wool at the fold, and nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof said it had arrived. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay carried at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, with the hundred and forty-sixth answer put down behind him the way a pot is set on a shelf where nobody will knock it. The count had gone from two hundred and eighty-five to two hundred and eighty-six the way a man lays one board beside another without measuring the seam. Two hundred and eighty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}; six months and one hundred and six days were told by nobody; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. There was no market, no gathering at \place{the Cross} and no bell on this Seventhday, and the reading of the tenth was not looked at again as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment. Frost the twentieth (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood three mornings ahead and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. The house kept its days in loaves, in glances and in unnamed spaces, and gave nobody a number to carry.

The small hours came in with their smells before they came with their light. Hot clay cooled under tallow was the first, and under it wet wool, and under that the faint sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and behind the wall the kiln went on answering in fours with the gap where the fifth should have stood, and it was not a bell. He had learned to tell the kiln's breathing from the river's turning the way a man learns two voices in a room he cannot enter, each kept in its own place and neither asked to explain the other. There was no fire-sitter standing over the coals and no house count borrowed to fill the gap; the fifth pull stayed where it was, and the silence where it should have stood stayed with it, two things kept carefully in one place and neither of them a witness.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. The knowing was exact and bought him nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and six days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-six days; at the finger's-width door the river went low over lower; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not folded into the three and was not heard as a seventh of the full note, but stayed a heard bell beside a told bell.

In the room whose floor does not touch his a woman went on with her low counting --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy took from it only what the hand could take and left the letting-go where it was. It was her breath and her count and not his, and he had long ago stopped thinking that taking the one gave him any claim on the other. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at setting one sound against another was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue, because a virtue wants a payer put under it and none stood here either.

First light came up the yard without announcing itself, the way heat comes back into a room that has been cold all night, by degrees and from the outside, and with it came \person{Sella Vail}. She went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she had gone on every morning since the boy came under the roof, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. \emph{It was the thaw}, she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on the broken pot and then on nothing, and she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with that wrist so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the dark. The fire answered four low pulls and kept its silence where the fifth should have stood; she neither praised it nor supplied the pull, and made no lesson of the first firing of Frost now that it stood behind her. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule. The next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. Every instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket.

At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at a morning, a hearth and a basket, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She took the wool at his ear between two fingers and drew it up, telling the wool and not the boy what it was for. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she said, in the flat practical voice she kept for inanimate things, and her burn shut on the wool and then on nothing. She set the bed right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. The pot rim she stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, as she had stood it on every other morning and would stand it again. The half-beat was the whole distance between the thing a hand is told to take and the thing it is told not to take, and she gave it to the pot and never to the boy.

Beyond the finger's-width door the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth, and a corner of the wool was lifted and the grain turned once for damp with a touch lighter than the morning before, and set back where it had lain with no figure going with it. The arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. Frost's first arithmetic stayed locked up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}; the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Nothing from the reading had come back under this roof as lesson, figure, mending or payment. The boy took the small lift of cloth and the small settling of it back through the gap in the door as a hand's two movements, one to the grain and one away from it, entered nowhere.

Through the same narrow frame she went around the places the yard goes around, with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called the thaw, and swept neither, though the broom was in her hand. She went as wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack}, where the broken birth-slates lay, as a woman sets a pail of hot water where a child might knock it. She went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word left in it. She went around \place{the trough} skinned over at \place{the yard well}, whose water the yard called weather. She went around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain lying covered under wool at the rack end; it went on its own tray with a finger between it and every other private keeping, and the boy did not make three absences into a cause.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of the well past the going of the light and into another morning, and the yard called it weather. The rope stiffened in the hand and gave its small dry rasp; the water in the trough kept its skin; the plate of ice lifted off the trough stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. None of it was entered, and no sign, cause, lesson, mending or payer was made of any of it. The surgeon's reading of a covering laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, and no fourth likeness of three was made a cause. The yard kept its count of the firings in wood, where no slate could take a figure off it, and the boy kept that wooden keeping on a place of its own too, unopened and unread.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and the hollow on that side that nobody filled. The clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it and neither was swept. The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled; a debt a man can go around is a debt that never stops standing in the ruts. His proverb had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and it came back now out of Sella's mouth to the coals as it had come a hundred times: \emph{Feed the fire before you feed yourself}. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-six days. No collector came up the clay road for the tiles and none went down it, and the keeping of him was maintenance, not a story, and no lesson was made of any of it.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed and he walked the shorter inventory in its order, because a thing hurried is a thing entered wrongly. The five askings of the ownerless question stood with one shaping and no witness and no row named, a finger between every pair; it was not a hearing, not a seventh and not a cause, and no sixth asking had come over the boards. The nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; a tenth standing up now to make a row come out even would be a stopping forged. The record-first hands stood at nine with a finger between the ninth and the eighth and nobody standing anywhere to be asked; \person{Ilva}'s ninth was unread in her sleeve and no tenth was filed here; nine were nine and no hand reached to make them ten. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either and no second look joined to the first. The shelf's two gaps kept their place above the charcoal, the fold's one crust kept its empty place beside it, and the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out stayed each on a tray of its own. No third gap came to make a row come out even, and no fourth likeness was made a cause. Keeping by \lore{not-doings} held the house the way a clamp holds a seam: the fifth pull unsupplied, the charcoal unread, the debt uncalled, the grain unfigured, the thread unwiped, the crust unlifted. He kept the house's keeping on one place and his own gaps on another with a finger between, and neither stood up and called itself a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them.

The Frost 14 well-saying stayed on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between: one saying, with no second shaping on this morning, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. It was not joined to the strip in a sleeve a pace from the well, nor to a thane's twice-wiped thumb, nor to the charcoal under the shelf nor to any private keeping, and it did not make Hessa a reader of what she had carried. Nobody stood anywhere to be asked, and the silence under the boards was a keeping and not a witness.

In the small hours the room whose floor does not touch his went on with its counting, and the boy took from it only what the hand could take. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note. It took the small intake of one counted breath from that room, held it past that count's letting-go and through the little silence that came after it, and came down on the river's next low note of water over water. The little silence stayed whole and empty; it was not filled to make a row come out even, and no silence that belonged to another's breathing was crossed to fetch a figure for it. The held intake and the empty interval after the count lay side by side on the sixth place with a finger between, two objects and not one finding joined to another. It was an honest coordination, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at holding one sound past another and landing it on a third was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

One hundred and forty-seven stood beside one hundred and forty-six. Two hundred and eighty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and six days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. He made nothing of the number and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Beyond the finger's-width door the dressed grain lay still covered under wool at the rack end, with the arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 not knowing what it cost and the space between the figure and the price still unsigned; the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and the plate of ice stood where it had been set, clouded where the silt had risen into it. Frost the twentieth (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood three mornings beyond this one and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. The boy lay on his bed under the roof with the crust two inches off and its empty place beside it unnamed, the hand laid down on the hundred and forty-seventh answer with the little silence after the count left whole and empty. The river's low note went quiet after the hand came down.
