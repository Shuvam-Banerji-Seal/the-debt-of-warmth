The afternoon came down off the yard walls by small gains no hand entered, the light going from the colour of old brass to the colour of wet ash along the wall, the small sounds of the tend rising out of it as the large ones went away --- a pail set where the pail always stood, a rope settling into its own knot, a loaf pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped there with nobody calling the stopping a figure, barley passed from one end of the wall to the other without ever becoming a number. \person[Corin]{The boy} took the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. The basket sat level out of the draught where the heat came to him sideways, and from its low angle \place{the Clay Yard} was a floor and a wall and the stone lip of \place{the yard well} far off, and beyond that the working of the place going on without an audience and without an owner. He took the yard as he found it and left it as he found it.

He went around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice. There was no arithmetic in the going, and the wide step was a keeping and not a sign; a figure set against it would want a payer standing under it and none stood. At \place{the returning-line place} the step went widest, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back out, and the wideness was not a mourning, not a diagnosis and not a door left open, but the room a man gives a thing he will not step across. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} kept its finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years, and a neighbour with a rake looked at it, raised the rake, and set it down with nothing done and nothing said. The boy kept the called-gone grey beside the left-in-place ridge with a finger between, for there is a word in the yard for grey put down and another for grey gone, and no agreement had been made in this yard to stop looking at this one.

Costs came in as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man come down \place{the clay road} brought a number up out of a shut fist toward the well, held it where the light could see it, and put it back into his sleeve. It arrived nowhere. It was a direction and then it was weather, and the boy did not ask what it had been for, because a number that wants a man put under it wants a payer standing and none stood. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got no figure from it; she went away with the answer the pot had given her, which was a note and not a price. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone away from the hearth, and the arithmetic read over it on Ilmen 10 had not known what the grain cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and nobody set one there. He had learned to let such things go without making a door of them, for a door wants a room behind it and there was no room under any roof here for a figure nobody had paid. He had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and he knew the difference between a field and a crowd: a crowd wants somebody counted, and a field does not. The yard at the going of the tend was a field at rest. Its small sounds came to him in the order they arrived, and he took them in that order and made no row of them, because making a row of a field is the first way a man turns what he has seen into what he can charge for.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before the basket at the going of the light, as she had come at both ends of every day since he came under the roof, and told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned them with the back of a broken pot. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps; a small thing done toward nothing, the way a woman breathes before a lift she has already decided not to make. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the board, and the hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and went back to the pot. At her throat \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched: a hearth, a shelf, a well and a going of the light are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a day that had earned only its thirteenth.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side of it. \person[Garl Vail] was in that hairline, and in the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on its peg behind the door, and in the hollow wheel-seat in the shed that nobody was allowed to fill, and not in a story anybody told. His name had not been spoken under the roof for four hundred and seven days, and the two counts --- his name and the fire's fifth --- stood side by side without becoming one. She said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration; a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves the hand showing, and a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure for the same reason a potter leaves a staple in the light. \lore{The tile debt} against a firing of tiles went on circling without a name and worse for being uncalled, and no collector came up the road on a Thirdday to call it.

At the rack end she lifted a corner of the wool and turned the dressed grain piece by piece with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, setting each down where it had lain, and took no figure from it and no handful for herself. The wool went back over the grain and the space stayed as it was. Then the pot went down on \place{the pot board} again with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and there was a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between an intention and an arrest: her hand had meant to set the pot nearer and had stopped where it stopped, and the distance cost her nothing and bought the boy nothing, and neither of them called it a gift. Under the roof nobody instructs a basket, and nobody instructs a pot about a hand.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. The seven walked-around places were gone around with a look and a wide step, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place, and he was set under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and never into \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold to the crust's empty place, and \lore{the keeping at the fold} was found where put and still a finding, no name over it and no payer under it.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and he went over the field by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended, and no door was drawn across the tend.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found and knew it cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot board's handle; wanted them without knowing that wanting them was a want, and got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor does not touch his. He knew the order in which a body gives up heat and the order in which it takes it back, the extremities before the core, the surface warm while the middle is still cold, and the knowledge was of the same order as the knowledge he had once used at a bay door and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work. A surgeon who cannot lift a crust two inches to his own mouth is a surgeon who has become a field, and a field does not dress itself. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody here would perform. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, low under \lore{the winter skin}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, found where put and not spent on a day that had earned only its thirteenth.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the covering --- neither early nor stayed --- and let it go as the note ended. Then the hand came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The crust's corner stood a hair off the wool and the thread was frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied; the first arithmetic of the year was up the road in a locked box with no key in his hand; the fifth shaping sat on its own tray with a finger between it and every other kept thing; and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept --- forward, into a morning not yet earned and not brought into this one.
