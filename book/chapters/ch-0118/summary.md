## Chronicle

Three days came under the Vail roof — Ilmen 11, 12 and 13 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: crust two inches off, fold found where put, thread stiff and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither won. He kept four hundred and five, six, then seven exact, and stood no number up as a threshold. The year's first arithmetic lay up the clay road in Berr's locked box: one reading, not an account, not a thirteenth, the figure read still not the figure paid.

Sella came to the hearth before the basket at both ends of all three days, told the coals it was the thaw, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, gave both proverbs to pot and fire and never to a person, turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure or handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat. She carried him down and up the nineteen steps, went wide around the seven walked-around places, and righted the fold.

At the kindling Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, passed his hand over blade and sill and away, telling the wood, *Six counted. The next I don't count*. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, withheld the pot's full note, and put his bent finger into the cold damper without the extra holding of Ash 26. Neither promise was given: *he's not Vail* stayed sealed, the loan uncalled.

On the twelfth a neighbour at the yard well said once that the woman who carries the slate had once carried a figure naming a person she knew. No owner stood under it, nobody to ask. The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes a field: a fifth shaping of the well-saying, on a tray of its own with a finger between it and every other kept thing. Ilva drew beside it, her hand to her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker; the strip stayed unread and she answered the question put and not the one behind it — *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account* — expressly not the speaker. No sixth shaping came due.

Answers 385 through 390 were filed on the sixth place, six honest answers and not one a doctrine. The fire's fifth was unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number of days. In the small hours the hand laid itself flat against the fold on answer 390. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept.
