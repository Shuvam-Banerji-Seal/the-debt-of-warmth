# Chapter 28 — The Word Held at the Teeth

**Days:** 123–125 (Reed 4–6, Y.H. 813, Fourthday–Sixthday). A **normal** chapter of three lived days. Day 122 is finished and is not revisited; Day 126 belongs to Chapter 29.

**Daymarks and word budget.** One `\daymark` per day, carried by the first scene of that day: Scene 1 opens Day 123 (`\daymark{123}{Reed 4, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}`); Scene 3 opens Day 124 (`\daymark{124}{Reed 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}`); Scene 5 opens Day 125 (`\daymark{125}{Reed 6, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}`). Scenes 2, 4 and 6 continue their days without a second mark. Scene 1 also carries the chapter's drop capital. Targets 2400 + 2300 + 2600 + 2400 + 2500 + 2800 = **15,000 words**; each day lives well past 800 words on the page.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** For the first time a sentence under the Vail roof comes all the way to the threshold of the word that would call the loan and stops there, held. It is filed as **an interval, not a payment** (T036/T076). Nothing is joined, named, paid or ended, and the seventh year has still not given.

## Scene 1 — A Direction in the Beds

- **Day and time.** Day 123, Reed 4, Fourthday, morning into midday, the light coming up off the river with mud in it.
- **Place.** The yard at Low Kiln, the edge of the worked strips east of it, the two unswept lines, the space beneath the lowest rack.
- **Present.** Corin (carried); Sella Vail; yard and reed-cutting walk-ons (a woman come up from the strips, a cutter with an armful, two women at the racks, a man from the sluice with wet sleeves). Nobody from the shed on stage, and no slate opened within reach of the basket.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Reed-cutting goes into the beds as the planting went into the strips. Bundles lie grey in the ruts, green at the broken ends and pale grey at the frost-bitten tips; they are judged by the arm that brings them. A cutter wipes his palms on his thighs and gives the yard its arithmetic: a bundle counted in the arm that carries it. A woman at the racks asks *And the hands that cut it?*; he goes past with another armful and the question goes down the yard unanswered (T071/T072, T077).
  2. A day's cutting is spoken of as a going-downriver and a coming-back at dusk. A man from the sluice asks what the beds will want before the week is out and gets the weather or the river, never a figure. The cost is asked in the form of a direction and answered with a direction (T058/T079).
  3. The yard goes around what it will not enter: the clean dark line, the new finer grey beside it that the thaw has been renamed after the fact, the space under the lowest rack where whole slate should have stood and the broken birth-slates lie, and the place in the worked strip where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come back with the rest on Seed 27. A look, a wide step, no number. No name, cause, accident, body or payment for the hand (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried).
  4. A woman come up from the strips, dry mud worked into her wrists, asks what the reeds will cost now the hands are out of them. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and sends her to the pot: *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.* The woman carries the unanswered thing away held level so nothing slops (T058/T079).
  5. Sella carries the boy at the half-pace from every open slate, goes wide around the rack space as though it were a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  6. The boy, one hundred and twenty-three days old and four months and three days, reads the going-around as a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody opened --- exact, complete, worth nothing --- and sets it on a place of its own. He lays no likeness across the shelf's two gaps in the next room, the one stiff crust in the fold, or the empty place at his ear (T060/T086). His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the strips and comes down.
- **Emotional turn.** A month has become work with a shape and no cost. The district's withholding is not a secret kept by one person but a thing performed in front of everybody, and the boy is the only one who can read it as a closed account. Competence without a hand to lay it down is only a more exact kind of helplessness.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet mud and cut grass off the Ilm; the thin mineral edge the river leaves on everything; green broken ends and pale frost-grey tips of reed; the clay jar cold under its glaze; the clean dark line and the finer hairline of grey beside it, both unswept; the basket carried level; the small wet sound when the head goes too far; the half-pace.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (123 kept exact), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T060/T086.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T098 gathers interest: a slate would be found and a figure made if a hand came up in a month or a year, and nothing has come up. No planting of a new thread. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The yard going around the empty place in the strip with a look and a wide step, the armfuls of reed passing it, and no number anywhere in the line.

## Scene 2 — The House Will Keep It

- **Day and time.** Day 123 continued, going of the light into the small hours, under the Vail kiln-house roof and at the loaf shelf in the next room.
- **Present.** Corin on his bed under the roof (never the Debt Loft); Sella Vail; Tarn at the edge of the yard and the fire; Jorra only as the silence and the damper's standing tongue already reported, not entering the room.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella gives the bucket, broom, pot and fire their instructions before anybody and never the basket, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*. She goes wide around the space beneath the lowest rack, sets the basket down where no slate lies, and moves her lips along the loaf row without a figure (T058/T079).
  2. The shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end that neighbours have called a thing the planting brought in, and the new palm's breadth that came in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- in a room that does not touch the fold's one stiff brown-edged crust and its unnamed empty place. No third crust, no third gap. She does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf is not an opening (T060/T086, T097 carried and unpaid).
  3. At the fire she tells it it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak pale in the dying light), sets the pot rim out and handle toward the reaching hand where it can be found in the dark, and does not look toward the bed (T058/T079).
  4. Tarn has taken the broom only after it was instructed. The small bench knife lies at the very edge of its sill, six years in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the unforged collector sentence kept beside it. His chest is tight with the breath from Seed 16 still unspent, a small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in; he says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying (T036/T076).
  5. The boy turns out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it. Six hearings of the full note stand beside and never among: five from four sources, the sixth --- the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted --- beside them and never counted with them. No seventh came today; the bell was a told bell and the pots under the roof notes he has learned to leave where they sound. Three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid; three intervals lie on another tray; the told bell sits in one place and the heard bell in another; the word forty stays unspoken with its readable double turn kept without a name (T061/T082).
  6. The carried fire takes its coal and gives back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-three days. Through the finger's-width gap comes the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing on a place of its own, never a hearing and never among the six. The boards overhead keep four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places; none becomes one (T088, T061/T082).
  7. Nobody says he is a hundred and twenty-three days old. He has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down (T071/T072).
- **Emotional turn.** The household's arithmetic and the surgeon's arithmetic stand side by side and do not meet: one kept badly in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, the other exactly, one day laid edge to edge against the next. The more exact it grows the more it isolates the only adult in the room.
- **Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and wet wool; tallow; the back of a broken pot against the hearth; blind ash; the fold put right without a look at his face; the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; cold at cheek, temple, wrist with the wool ridden back from the bare joint; the fourth-low-pull-and-a-gap of the fire; water over water beyond the gap.
- **Threads advanced.** T058/T079, T060/T086, T097 (carried), T036/T076, T061/T082, T088, T071/T072 (123).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The unforged collector sentence and the held breath are set in place for the near-saying of Scene 4; the interval tray is prepared to receive it. Nothing paid, no new thread opened.
- **Exact last image.** Three counts sitting in three places --- river, fire, floorboards --- and not becoming one, with the hand going up an inch toward the note of water and coming down.

## Scene 3 — The Bite Ahead of the Sentence

- **Day and time.** Day 124, Reed 5, Fifthday, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard, the Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (spoken of, not entered), the cold-iron damper, the flagstones before the shed.
- **Present.** Corin (carried, set at the edge of the work where no slate lies); Sella Vail; Jorra Vail; Tarn; men unnamed who come up the yard and go into the back room (walk-ons, not followed); a kiln-master come up from High Kiln (walk-on).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Men come up the Clay Yard before first grey with their collars high and speak low in the room off the wheel-shed, its door a finger's width open and not shut. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold; the boy does not follow them and the room is not entered (T035/T031).
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger --- not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel --- and keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, entered by nobody (T035/T031).
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.* He tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds it from a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it. The knowing is worth nothing anyone may take (T035/T031, T061/T082).
  4. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing there, and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The end of her instruction arrives at the pot instead of at him (T035/T031).
  5. The boy reads the iron once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object worth nothing joined to no kiln. Two men have carried the story of a counting room shut in a war into this yard and a third only mentioned it; he sets those tellings beside the iron without laying one across the other (T062, T065/T095).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction and says *Yes, house* to those who want the thaw early and those who want it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument can find a foot, while keeping the late because it puts off the paying though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went. His seventh year is pressed past the end of the third tend of the planting and still held: the knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the unforged collector sentence beside it, the breath from Seed 16 unspent with the small interval at the top of each reach (T036/T076).
  7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch where it stopped; Jorra's bent finger stands in the damper; neither man asks about the other's silence --- the ear and the room --- and neither names the other's silence. The loan that can hear is uncalled (T036/T076).
- **Emotional turn.** The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to, neither rising nor falling: one man's obedience arrives before the instruction and is also a correction nobody will name, the other's stroke goes on without the check it once made. Two kept things stand in one room with no hand joining them.
- **Sensory anchors.** Collars high against the cold; grey slate dust ground small as meal into the creases past the bent finger; bone-coloured slip kept on a separate tray; the cold iron taking the cold out of the fingertip; the tongue of the damper moved a hair's breadth; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckles the colour of the clean dark line; the pale clip-ring catching what light there is; the shallow bright patch the flagstones keep.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031/T062, T036/T076, T061/T082, T065/T095 (carried), T058/T079, T071/T072 (124).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The damper's double bite is read again as an object joined to no kiln, carrying the Greybank tellings forward without a third telling or a joining. The seventh year is pressed nearer its end and has not given. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The bent finger standing in the cold iron of the damper and the stroke going on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch where it had stopped, neither man naming the other's silence.

## Scene 4 — The Word at the Teeth

- **Day and time.** Day 124 continued, going of the light into the small hours, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the hearth and the kindling stack.
- **Present.** Corin on his bed under the roof; Sella Vail at the fire; Tarn at the hearth, the kindling and the bench; Jorra gone from the shed, present only in the standing tongue of the damper and the dust already filed.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Tarn banks the coals and sets the small knife back where it can be checked, six in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone and held, the one entry in the house the house cannot check. His chest is tight and the breath from Seed 16 is still unspent, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in (T036/T076).
  2. He speaks to the kindling and not to a person: the six are counted, the next he does not count --- and then the sentence goes on to the threshold of the word that would call the loan. It is a thing about the ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it, and what would follow if a man said whose ear it was and set a figure to it. He stops short of the word. No figure is given, no name is spoken, and the loan is not called (T036/T076, **first movement toward the calling of the loan, withheld**).
  3. He draws the breath that should go in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, the interval staying where it has stayed since Seed 16. He says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom, which is the shape his help takes when he is frightened (T036/T076).
  4. Sella gives the end of her instruction to the pot and does not ask. Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room. The two silences stay unjoined and the equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to (T035/T031, T036/T076).
  5. The boy files the stopped sentence on the interval tray beside Tarn's half-beat at the damper Seed-on and the stroke that went on without the check, and beside his own held breath from the market: objects on one tray and not a rule, an interval and not a payment, not a hearing and not a seventh (T061/T082). He writes no cause across them.
  6. The kindling keeps its old arithmetic --- four, four and an empty fifth --- and something must give at the end of the seventh year; it has not given yet. The payer of the carried fire's fifth breath stays unnamed (T088).
- **Emotional turn.** A held thing moves for the first time without being spent. The near-saying is the chapter's one real change: the loan comes to the threshold of being called and is kept there. Keeping it is both mercy and a further debt, and nobody in the room can say which.
- **Sensory anchors.** Coals banked under blind ash; the back of a broken pot; the bench knife at the very edge of its sill in a thumb-worn mark; the tight chest and the small empty interval at the top of each reach; the straw and tallow smell of the kindling; the sentence stopping at the teeth; the pot set where it can be found in the dark.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (near-saying withheld, loan uncalled), T061/T082, T058/T079, T060/T086, T035/T031/T062, T088.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No payoff. The near-saying is planted as a second interval toward the far day the loan is called or the seventh year ends; it stays unfigured and unnamed. Chapter 29 will bring it once more to the threshold and stop it again (T036).
- **Exact last image.** The kindling stacked four, four and an empty fifth, and the sentence standing at the threshold of the word with no figure under it and no hand laying a line across it.

## Scene 5 — The Cord Goes Back Inside

- **Day and time.** Day 125, Reed 6, Sixthday, morning downriver at the Weir and the plank walk, then mid-morning at the stalls beyond the Cross and the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.
- **Place.** The reed beds and the Weir, the plank walk on posts, the flat stone where the hearth-maids blessed the traps in Ilmen, the outer boards of the fish-trade shade, the market stalls, the out-of-line landing.
- **Present.** Corin (carried at the edge of the work where no slate lies); the reed-cutter with the cord (unnamed, given no sheet); Orra Weir at her board and cord, present and silent, unnamed in the narration and not asked; Mira Silt at the eel boards; a weir woman with salt dried white at her wrists; an eel buyer in a coat with the river in its hem; Berr Clay (short); the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing. The foundling is offstage. Rusk is only the man who walks the traps at dusk, not named and not introduced.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed work goes on in armfuls at the beds. A reed-cutter comes along the plank walk with a bundle on his shoulder and the short length of wool or reed-fibre cord inside his coat; he sets the bundle on the flat blessing stone, straightens the cord on his thumb, turns it once so the knots stand in the small light, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb and puts it back inside his coat (T099 carried).
  2. To the weir woman who asks how many went up yesterday he says *You can ask the cord*; to *I can't read a cord* he says *No more can the Court*, and goes to lift another trap. No figure read, no knot counted, no name given and no joining made to the hand that did not come back out of the strip (T099, T098).
  3. The boy holds the cord on a place of its own beside the other second records: Mira's grey cord at her waist and the second twist beneath it, neither knot counted, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door; Orra Weir's knot-string lying beside Mira's board and not touching it; six pages in a little tin turned writing-down toward a floorboard he cannot enter; the round pewter tin above a bed nobody uses, shut for more mornings than anybody keeps count of (T099, T059/T025/T024, T023/T089). No tin or box is opened.
  4. Orra, at the Weir beside her board, gives a date instead of an answer to a weir woman's question about the levy and goes back to her knots. When the reed-cutter lifts his bundle she watches what there is to watch at the edge of the shade and gets no answer from the basket. Nobody asks her and she does not ask. The shape of the last knot is legible without its name, and the word forty stays unspoken (T024/T059/T025).
  5. At the eel boards Mira works with pale slime dried in grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been and her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin with the hands in the reeds she answers her own answer --- *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* --- moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, and puts one away. When the buyer in the coat with the river in its hem names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted, the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. She does not look into the basket; her board lies beside Orra's and does not touch it (T059/T025/T024).
  6. At the out-of-line landing the soft-cuffed clerk is still set with his cuff grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, his hand to the slate before his eyes to the stalls, every time. The market sells its reeds and eels over the top of him as though a man with a slate were a thing a landing always had, and nobody steps around him or asks what he is set there for (T090/T080). A girl goes over the landing sideways, catches the rail and goes on selling; nobody makes a fall of it (T083).
  7. Berr Clay comes up short off the north road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. His hands are large and clean; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest on it a breath too long. He says a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the two hundred, calls the levy and says it is not called by him, and to the man who brought a number up out of a fist he says there will be a slate at the watch --- *your house*, never *I*, taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. He goes off up the clay road with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he has done the district a kindness (T015/T041/T047/T077).
  8. No loaf passes, no slate is seen near the basket, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand comes. The foundling stays offstage. Dessa stays dead and unnamed; Marra is not in Sill (T033/T034 held, T002 held).
- **Emotional turn.** Every record in the district is kept in a material no Court thumb will read, and none of them touches another. The clerk's slate is the one record that is all figure and no keeping, set down before anybody called a measure. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and a number into a slate is not a number into a strip.
- **Sensory anchors.** Salt dried white where the flood had touched the posts; silt in the hollow of Mira's neck; eel slime dried grey on her sleeves; the green of tasted glaze at a corner of Berr's mouth and hot clay under it; the cord gone grey with handling and the knots standing in the small light; the soft cuff grey from washing; the out-of-line landing walked over carefully.
- **Threads advanced.** T099 (carried, uncounted), T059/T025/T024, T090/T080, T083, T015/T041/T047/T077, T061/T082, T023/T089 (carried), T071/T072 (125).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T099 gathers interest toward the far convergence of the district's private records; the cord is carried and not counted. Nothing paid, no knot read, no tin opened, no new thread planted.
- **Exact last image.** The cord going back inside the man's coat with its knots unread while the market sells its reeds and eels over the top of the clerk's slate, and nobody stepping around him.

## Scene 6 — One Hundred and Twenty-Five

- **Day and time.** Day 125 continued, going of the light into the small hours, under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Place.** The clay road up from the Weir, the yard gone around at dusk, the bed under the roof, the loaf shelf through the wall, the finger's-width-open door.
- **Present.** Corin on his bed under the roof, never in the Debt Loft; Sella Vail; Tarn and Jorra present only as the kept objects already filed (knife, breath, near-saying, damper, dust), not entering the room.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries the boy up the clay road from the Weir at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, going around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it (T058/T079).
  2. She sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof has been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rests a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it goes away (T058/T079).
  3. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, stands a moment counting under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak pale in the dying light), sets the pot rim out and handle toward the reaching hand where it can be found in the dark, does not look toward the bed and goes to her own bed. She does not touch the widow's mark: a shelf, a strip, a rack, a market, a road and a dusk are not openings (T058/T079).
  4. The door stands a finger's width open. Through the gap comes the river's note of water over water, beyond the reed beds and the Weir, a sound kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing, never among the six. Through the floor under blind ash comes the carried fire taking its coal and giving back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-five days. Above him the boards keep four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places; none becomes one (T088, T061/T082).
  5. He turns out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it. Six hearings stand beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid; three intervals lie on another tray with Tarn's half-beat at the damper, the stroke that went on without the check, and now the near-saying stopped at the threshold of the word --- two objects and a stopped sentence on one tray and not a rule. The told bell sits beside the heard bell in two places that do not touch, and the word forty is unspoken with its readable double turn kept without a name (T061/T082, T036/T076).
  6. One stiff brown-edged crust lies two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the loaf shelf keeps its two gaps in a room that does not touch the fold. Two neighbours have laid the old gap and the empty place under one cause in one breath, and he has set their sayings on places of their own and made neither his own; no fourth likeness has made a cause, and no finger of his has drawn the line (T060/T086, T097 carried and unpaid). The hand that did not come back holds its place with no name, no body and no figure (T098); the reed-cutter's cord stays inside a man's coat with its knots unread (T099); the cold foot below the sill stays the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed (T093/T094/T096).
  7. Nobody under this roof says he is a hundred and twenty-five days old, four months and five days. He has stopped expecting to be told; that is not the same as not wanting it. The count goes on edge to edge against the house's loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, exact and unspent and spoken by nobody, and goes on into Day 126. The near-saying stays at the threshold; the seventh year stays held; something must give at its end and has not given (T071/T072, T036/T076).
  8. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down. In the other life it closed on clamps and on a phone with a name he would not look at; here it can close on nothing at two inches. The hand was his and small, and the count went on with it (T004 held, no present-tense break).
- **Emotional turn.** The file is exact, complete and worthless, and the fact that it is unchanged is the one thing about it he cannot file. The chapter ends not on a settlement but on a held thing one day further on: the near-saying at the threshold, the loan uncalled, the seventh year not yet spent.
- **Sensory anchors.** The half-pace up the clay road; the thread frozen white at the corner of the mouth; the pot set rim out and handle toward the reaching hand; blind ash; woodsmoke and wet wool; the river's note through the finger's-width gap; the fire's missing fifth breath under the floor; the fifth that is a knot in the boards; cold at cheek, temple, wrist with the wool ridden back.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (125), T058/T079, T060/T086, T061/T082, T088, T093/T094/T096, T097, T098, T099, T036/T076, T004 (held, offstage).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The near-saying is carried forward as an interval toward its second stopping in Chapter 29 and the later end of the seventh year; the private records gather interest toward the far convergence; the unsent call stays a shape with no present-tense break and no tagged memory. Nothing paid, named, opened, joined or ended.
- **Exact last image.** The hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water and coming down, with one hundred and twenty-five standing where one hundred and twenty-four stood --- exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, the river going over water beyond the gap and the fire missing its fifth breath under the floor.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 29

The last image is mid-action, not a resolution: a hand that can reach two inches going up an inch toward a sound it cannot touch and coming down while the count crosses into Day 126 unspent. Everything the chapter moved is still in the ground rather than brought to light. The near-saying of Scene 4 stands at the threshold of the word that would call the loan, filed as an interval beside the half-beat and the unchecking stroke; the seventh year is pressed past the third tend and has not given; the reed-cutter's cord is back inside the coat with its knots unread; the charcoal tally under the Vail shelf is not yet planted; the two shelf gaps and the fold's crust and its gap remain in rooms that do not touch; the green at a mouth has had only two glimpses and a third is owed in Chapter 29. Chapter 29 opens on Day 126, Reed 7, Seventhday, with the boy one hundred and twenty-six days old and nobody saying so, the reed beds giving their first bundles into the yard, a third green coming at the market's edge, and the sentence under the roof coming once more to the threshold of the word and stopping again. Forward, not closed.
