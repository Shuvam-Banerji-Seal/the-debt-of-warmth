The going of the light came down the clay road behind them in the order it always came, off the reed tips first and off the ruts last, so that for a while the sky held a dull pewter over a road already gone dark underfoot. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} up from \place{the Weir} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the basket level under her burn-shut hand, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, as she had given it in the morning and as she would give it on the morrow. He was one hundred and twenty-five days old, four months and five days, and the number stood in him exact as a set of instruments laid out on a towel and spoken by nobody who would have known what it counted. The road went under her feet, the reeds went past on other shoulders in armfuls judged by the arm that carried them, and nobody on the road had any use for the number.

Cold came at him in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for and went under the fold at his ear. She had carried a pot filled up this road a hundred mornings and never slopped it, and she carried the basket the same way now, level, with the little-door burn shut on its weight and the grey streak at her temple showing pale under the dull sky. The thread at his mouth had frozen white in the small hours downriver and nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put, a thing kept and not entered. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody had been given an instruction about that sound either.

The yard received them as a yard receives a basket, with the going-around. She went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it that had been renamed \lore{the thaw} after the fact, both unswept for three days and both standing where they had stood, and she went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, the broken birth-slates lying where they lay under it, entered by nobody. Beyond the racks \place{the worked strips} held their own empty place, the place where a hand had gone in with the seed and had not come out with the rest when the last hands came up on Seed 27; the yard went around it with a look and a wide step, the armfuls of reed passed it, and there was no number anywhere in the line. The boy read the going-around as he had read it for a month --- a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody had opened, exact, complete, worth nothing --- and set it on a place of its own. He laid no likeness across the shelf's two gaps in the next room, the one stiff crust in the fold at his ear, or the empty place beside it. A month had become work with a shape and no cost, and the only person in \place{Low Kiln} who could read it as a closed account could not close two inches on the basket that held him.

\scenebreak

Back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, the way a woman sets a pot down where no passing hip will find it. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and nobody had instructed her about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the way a hand rests on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot bring itself to let go of, and then it was gone and the burn on the back of it was shut on nothing. She did not look toward the bed. She had not said whose child he was in all his days and would not have known how to say it if she had wanted to.

In the next room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its days the way this house kept most of its days, badly, and in materials no Court thumb would read. Loaves pushed along the board, a glance at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall, and spaces left where no figure stood. She moved her lips along the row without a figure, her hand going past the old empty place at the row's end and past the newer palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, and closed on a loaf beyond both. Two gaps; no third. The two rooms did not touch. A shelf was not an opening, and a strip, a rack, a market, a road, a basket and a bed were not openings either; she had gone around every one of them today and had not once put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, which was touched before an opening and at no other hour.

At the fire she told it what it was. “It was the thaw.” The words went down into the coals as instructions went into the bucket and the broom and the pot, and the coals answered nothing. She turned them with the back of a broken pot, the broken edge lifting and settling, and fed them, and the house kept its warmth the way a cup keeps water with a crack in it nobody will mention.

“The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}.

Then she stood with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted something under her breath toward no opening. The lips moved; the count did not arrive anywhere; there was no slate, no door, no crossing of a threshold for it to be entered at. The boy, on his bed with the fold at his ear, read the stopping of the count as a surgeon reads a chart closed over a note nobody has opened: exact, complete, worth nothing. He had learned in two lives to tell a record that says what happened from a record that says only where to stop reading, and this was the second kind, kept in the mouth instead of on slate and more carefully for that.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, the skin inside the door puckering as the wrist turned, the grey streak pale in the dying light off the coals. She had said once that it came in the winter \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} died and had not said it again. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, the way a woman sets a cup for a man who will come in late and not look where he puts his fingers. She did not look toward the bed, and went to her own bed, leaving the fire to keep what it kept and the door standing as it stood, a finger's width open.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width gap came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir} and the long grey ranks of the cutting, a fifth low thing kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing and never among the six. It came nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, as it had come nearer every night since the flood went out of the yard, and he had never once mistaken it for a vessel's note or given it a source it did not have. Through the floor under blind ash \lore{the carried fire} took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-five days. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one. A man with an ear for vessels might have laid one gap across another and called them the same absence; he had spent two lives practising the refusal, and the refusal itself was worth nothing to anybody under this roof.

He turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, in the dark, with the count standing at one hundred and twenty-five where one hundred and twenty-four had stood the day before. Every object was where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot through a wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, which came from a person and was filed beside the five and never counted with them. No seventh had come. He had not made one to fill the empty place. The bell that had gone across the square in the morning was a told bell, and it sat in its own place with the heard bell in another, and the two places did not touch.

Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under a wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. On another tray lay the intervals, three made in the yard and one made under this roof last night: the half-beat of a check that came behind a standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from the market, and now the near-saying stopped at the threshold of the word --- two objects and a stopped sentence on one tray and not a rule. An interval was not a payment and a near-saying was not a hearing, and neither was a seventh. The word that would call the loan had come up through the counting as water comes up through a crack and had stood on the threshold of the teeth with no figure under it and no name on it, and had gone back where it came from; here it lay as an object among objects, the same iron as the standing tongue of \lore{the damper}, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. He wrote no cause across any of them. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and he had no hand that could reach two inches and no voice to say the thing that would make the holding plain. He kept them apart by knowing what each one was, which was the only instrument he had ever had and the only one he had never been able to lay down. The word forty stayed unspoken with its readable double turn kept without a name, a shape on a place of its own in a night that had a great many figures in it and not one he could enter.

The knife kept its six in a worn thumb-mark with the seventh at the very edge and still held; the breath in a man's chest stayed unspent with the unforged collector sentence beside it; the near-saying stayed at the threshold and something must give at the end of the seventh year and had not given yet. He did not join them. He had spent two lives learning that a held thing is a kept thing and a kept thing is owed, and that the owing does not go away because nobody has said what it is.

\scenebreak

One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold, past smelling of anything, uneaten, unlifted and unnamed, with its empty place beside it still without a name and no third come to lie between them. The loaf shelf in the next room kept its two gaps in a room that did not touch this one, and the empty place at his ear was not the old gap and not the new one and was not laid across either by any finger of his. Two neighbours had laid the old gap and the empty place under one cause in a single breath and gone off with their pots, one naming the planting and one a child's appetite, and he had set their sayings on places of their own and made neither his own. No fourth likeness had made a cause. No finger of his had drawn the line. The line was a thing a Court hand draws at the foot of a slate, and he had declined to become that hand in two lives and would decline it again in the dark.

The hand that had gone in with the seed held its place with no name, no cause, no body and no figure, and a slate would be found and a figure made if a hand came up in a month or a year, and nothing had come up. The reed-cutter's cord stayed inside a man's coat with its knots unread, a tally in wool or reed-fibre gone grey with handling that no Court thumb would spell, and it lay on a place of its own beside the grey cord at a woman's waist and the second twist twisted twice beneath it, her board beside another woman's knot-string and not touching it, six pages turned writing-down toward a floorboard, a round pewter tin above a bed nobody used with its thumb-bright lid shut. No tin was opened. No box was opened. The cold foot below the sill stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed, and the boy did not lay it across the other absences, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause and there was no hand in this house to hold them apart but his, and his could not reach two inches.

Nobody under this roof said he was a hundred and twenty-five days old, four months and five days. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The count went on edge to edge against the house's loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, exact and unspent and spoken by nobody who would have known what it counted if they had said it, and went on into the day that would come after this one. The near-saying stayed at the threshold. The seventh year stayed held. Something must give at its end and had not given yet, and the loan that could hear was uncalled.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. In the other life it had closed on clamps and on a phone with a name he would not look at; here it could close on nothing at two inches, not on the crust, not on the pot set rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, not on the fold, not on a slate, not on a hand. The hand was his and small, and the count went on with it. The water went over water beyond the gap, the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor, the boards kept their knot where a fifth nail should have stood, and one hundred and twenty-five stood where one hundred and twenty-four had stood the night before, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. The dark went on keeping what it kept, and the count went on into \place{Sill}'s next morning with him.
