The light went out of the yard in the order it always went, off the tops of the racks first and off the flagstones last, so that for a while the sky held a dull pewter over a floor already dark. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the dark came in through the door standing a finger's width open and lay along the boards in the low places, the way water lies where it is let. \person[Corin]{the boy} had been carried in from the edge of the work and set on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the fold was right at his ear and under his chin, and the thread at his mouth had frozen where nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread. He was awake. One hundred and twenty-four days stood in him, four months and four days, exact as a set of instruments laid out on a towel and spoken by nobody who would have known what it counted. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

\person{Sella Vail} was at the fire. She had given the pot its instruction and the pot had taken it, and now she gave the end of the same sentence to the coals, because a sentence begun to a thing must be finished to something or it stands about the room all night with nowhere to go. “It was the thaw,” she told the fire, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting toward no opening. The little-door burn on the back of her hand was shut on nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried it, the grey streak at her temple pale in the dying light off the coals, and set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} was at the hearth with the coals already banked. He had done it without looking at the fire, the way he had banked it for six years, pressing the blind ash over the red with the back of his hand and leaving one small place where the heat came through slowly enough to be trusted. His chest was tight. The breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16 was still unspent, and there was a small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in, an empty place in the order of a thing a man does without thinking, a breath stopped against iron and never let through. He finished the bank, wiped his knuckles on his thigh without lifting the \lore{Ash} worked into the creases, and put his hand out on the sill for the small bench knife.

The knife lay at the very edge, where a man could check it and where a man could also take it, and he moved it a hair's breadth and set it back where it could be checked. Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat, and the seventh nearly gone and never named, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The unforged collector sentence lay beside it where he had kept it since the frozen ford, and he did not enter either. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. “It's back,” he told the sill, though the sill had not asked and the yard had been told a hundred mornings running. “You can check.” Then he told the sill something else, lower, in the tone a man uses to state the weather: “Six counted. The next I don't count.”

\scenebreak

The kindling stack stood where it had stood through the planting month, and Tarn crouched to it as he crouched to all his tools. It smelled of straw and the tallow that had dripped from the house's own dish onto a split end, a dry, honest smell that asked nothing of a man. He laid a stick across and took the measure of the stack with his eyes as he had taken it a hundred evenings, four, four, and a space where the fifth should have been. The space was older than the boy. It had been kept as carefully as the breaths that came, and no hand had ever filled it and no hand had ever called it by a name.

He spoke to the wood, because the wood was what he spoke to when a thing had to be said and a person would have made of it a question. “Six counted,” he said. “The next I don't count.” The stick lay where he put it. He took up another, and the sentence went on past the counting the way a man walks on past a gate he has left open, not meaning to go far and going all the same. “An ear under this roof that can hear,” he said to the kindling, “is worth more than the debt that holds it.” The wood took it as wood takes everything, without answer and without keeping. He laid the second stick. “And if a man said whose ear it was, and set a figure to it—”

The sentence came up to the word and stood there.

He had it at his teeth. It was a short word, a plain word, a word that would have called the loan and set the whole house to answering it; he had carried it in his mouth for six years without once letting it out, and now it had come up through the counting as water comes up through a crack and stood on the threshold of his teeth with no figure under it and no name on it. He did not say it. The kindling kept its arithmetic. The dark under the roof kept whatever it kept. Somewhere beyond the finger's-width gap the river went over water in its low muttered note, and under the floor the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and a silence, and neither of them said the word for him.

The boy, on his bed with the fold at his ear, knew the shape of a stopped sentence the way he knew a breath stopped at the top of a reach --- the small empty place where the thing should have gone in. He had spent two lives learning what a word does when it is held at the teeth. In the one life a word spoken into a chart could call a number out of a man and set a hand writing; in this one a word held at the teeth was kept, and a kept thing was owed. The word that would call the loan was the same piece of iron as the damper: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. Here it had a figure cut into it that nobody had read, and where there was a figure there was a payer, and nobody under this roof had named one. He set the stopped sentence on a place of its own. He wrote no cause across it.

Tarn drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. It stood where it had stood since Seed 16, a clamp stopped against the Warm and never let through, and his chest kept the shape of it. He put the last stick on the stack, set the space where the fifth should have been where it had always been, and took up the broom. He had taken it only after it had been given its instruction, and he went around the two unswept lines and the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with the stroke that went around and entered nothing. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and to the pot, and to nobody who had asked him a thing. When he was frightened he became helpful, and in a kiln-house that meant doing three things at once and quietly putting the fourth right; the household straightened around him the way cloth straightens when a weight has been lifted, and nobody said what had lifted it.

\scenebreak

Sella gave the end of her instruction to the pot. “There,” she said to it, and left the sentence where a pot would understand it, and asked Tarn nothing about the ear. She had been within hearing of the wood and the stopped sentence and the word that had come up to the teeth and gone back; she had heard it the way she heard the fire's fourth pull and the silence after it, and she asked nothing, because a question said aloud becomes a thing a house must answer, and a thing answered is a thing entered. \person{Jorra Vail} was gone from the shed. The bent finger stood in the cold iron of \lore{the damper} where he had left it, and the fine grey dust was already ground into his forearms past the little finger and filed where the house kept such things, and the back room off the shed stood a finger's width open with whatever it kept still inside it. He did not ask about the ear. Tarn did not ask about the room. The two silences stayed where they had stayed since the night the boy came, two shapes under one roof with no hand joining them, and the equality held at the place it had moved to, neither rising nor falling. Nothing was paid, named, or ended.

The boy turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, in the dark, with the count standing at one hundred and twenty-four where one hundred and twenty-three had stood the day before. Every object was where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, beside them and never counted with them; no seventh had come. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid. On another tray lay the intervals: the half-beat of Tarn's check behind the standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from the market, and now this --- a sentence stopped at the threshold of the word, two objects and a stopped sentence on one tray and not a rule. An interval was not a payment and a near-saying was not a hearing, and neither was a seventh. He wrote no cause across any of them. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and he had no hand that could reach two inches and no voice to say the thing that would make the holding plain. He kept them apart by knowing what each one was, which was the only instrument he had ever had and the only one he had never been able to lay down.

The kindling kept its old arithmetic at the hearth, four, four, and an empty fifth, and the knife kept its six in the worn mark with the seventh at the very edge and still held. The breath in Tarn's chest stayed unspent, the near-saying stayed at the teeth, and something must give at the end of the seventh year and had not given yet. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, taking its coal and giving back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-four days. Through the gap came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing on a place of its own, never a hearing and never among the six. Above the bed the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one.

The boy's small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down. He lay with one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear and its empty place beside it unnamed, in a room that did not touch the shelf in the next room with its two gaps, and in a house that had never once said how old he was. The sentence stood at the threshold of the word with no figure under it and no hand laying a line across it. The loan that could hear was uncalled. The seventh year was held. The dark went on keeping what it kept, exact, complete, and spoken by nobody, while the woodsmoke and the wet wool and the straw smell of the kindling went up together under the roof and the count went on into the next day unspent.
