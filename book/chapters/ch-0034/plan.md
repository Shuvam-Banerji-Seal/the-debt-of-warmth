# Chapter 34 — beat sheet: *Seven at the Teeth*

**Days covered.** Days 141–143, Reed 22–24, Y.H. 813, Secondday to Fourthday. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived in full, at least eight hundred words to the day. Day 140 is finished and not revisited; Day 144, Reed 25, belongs to Chapter 35. The boy is one hundred and forty-one, then forty-two, then forty-three days old — four months and twenty-one, twenty-two and twenty-three days — and nobody under the roof says so. The reed month goes into its last week; at the chapter's close the third Account-day (Reed 30, Day 149) is six days off.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof on Day 142 Tarn brings the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt that holds it **a seventh time** — after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17 and 20 — all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there, with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray beside the six as a **seventh interval and not a seventh payment**, an object among objects and not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh hearing, refusing to let seven likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine — for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer — with no payer named to any of the seven. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given. A second gathering, not a resolution: on Day 141 a neighbour at the loaf shelf names the row's old gap and the empty place at the boy's ear under one appetite in a single breath, a further shaping of T097 held beside the earlier tellings on a place of its own, advanced and **not paid**, with no third gap, no finger laid and no joining.

**What is withheld everywhere.** No calling of the loan, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no cutting or counting of the seventh year, no collector, no payer named to any stopping, no seventh hearing of the full note, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no sixth green, no knot counted, no word forty spoken, no name or number from Dessa, no sale, no opening of tin or box, no body, cause or figure for the missing hand, no reading of either private tally, no joining of the two empty places, no third gap, no fourth likeness made into a cause, no silt-leach, cause or cure, no Fenna named in narration. T097 stays advanced and unpaid; T098, T099 and T100 stay carried and unpaid. The old life stays offstage: no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from the boy's eyes, no Court, collector, Waiting Room, back-room interior, Ulda on stage, Marra in Sill, watcher on stage, present-tense break or ending. **Seven stoppings must not stand up and call themselves a doctrine.**

## Scene 1 — *The Last Week's Bundles*

- **Day and time.** Day 141, Reed 22, Secondday, Y.H. 813, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard and the reed beds below Low Kiln; the edge of the worked strips east of the yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; reed-cutters going downriver and coming back with the last bundles; a girl with a creel; a woman at the racks; yard voices. Walk-ons only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Open on the morning of Day 141 with the reed month gone into its last week and no boundary drawn at its entering; the boy is one hundred and forty-one days old and four months and twenty-one days and nobody under any roof says so. Sella carries him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket — *Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind* (T058/T079).
  2. The last bundles go downriver and come back at dusk-shaped armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter tells the girl with a creel to lift her bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it, *and that's the whole of what it knows*; to a second question about cost he says the arm doesn't know cost and asks how heavy it is (T071/T072, T077).
  3. A woman working the racks asks what the cutters' hands will be paid now the tend is nearly out; she is answered with the weather and the river, never a figure, and shuts two fingers out of a fist as she goes (T077, T058/T079).
  4. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look, a wide step, no number (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried, not paid).
  5. The reed-cutter's grey cord goes past along the boards unread, straightened and turned once where no slate lies; it is not laid beside the charcoal under the shelf, the empty place in the line, Mira's cords or Orra's knot-string (T099 carried, not paid).
  6. The boy reads the carrying arm as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost: exact, complete and worth nothing. The surgeon's register notes the order of reaching — the hand to the record before the hand to the person — without giving him standing to enter it anywhere (T061/T082).
  7. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down against the fold; he cannot reach the bundle, the pot or any of the places gone around. One hundred and forty-one goes on without anybody's thumb upon it (T071/T072).
- **Emotional turn.** From the ordinary last work of the reed month to the exactness of a record that cannot be spent: competence here is helplessness, and the day's arithmetic stays in arms and weather where no Court thumb will read it.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet wool and woodsmoke going before Sella; grey reed ends pale where frost bit the tips; dry mud worked into wrists; river wind at the ear; the small wet sound of the wandering head; a trough by the well with a skin of ice.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (141 kept exact and unspent), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T099 (carried), T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T098 gathers another going-around with no name, body, cause or figure; T099 goes past unread another day; T077's cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The cutter goes back downriver with the bundle counted only in his arm; the reeds stand grey in the ruts; the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down.

## Scene 2 — *One Appetite, Two Rooms*

- **Day and time.** Day 141 continued, midday into the afternoon.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, the boy in the basket; a neighbour come up from the strips with a pot. Walk-on only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket low near the bench leg at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board a man standing sees only the top of; she gives the instruction to the wool, the pot, the broom and the fire and never to the basket, and tells the fire it was the thaw (T058/T079).
  2. Above, the shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word — in a room the fold does not touch (T060/T086).
  3. A neighbour at the shelf reaches past the bare board, her hand going to it before her eyes go to the boy, opens for the half-beat of an absence, closes on the loaf beyond and names the old gap as a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at his ear as a child's appetite under one appetite in a single breath, without once seeing she has made two rooms one absence (T097).
  4. The boy files the saying as a **further shaping** of T097 on a place of its own beside the two earlier tellings and the watcher's late turn — advanced and **not paid**, with no third gap, no finger laid across the two places, no joining, no name and no fourth likeness made into a cause (T097, T060/T086).
  5. Sella moves her lips along the row, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and gives nobody a figure; to the neighbour's question she says *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and the woman carries the unanswered question away level, nothing slopped (T077, T058/T079).
  6. Still standing with her back to the basket, Sella reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies — the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again — without looking and without a word, holds it a breath out and back, brings it away and does not go back to the shelf (T100 carried, unread).
  7. From the low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, press a thumb where her thumb had been, lay the underside beside the two gaps above or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or make a cause of the habit — such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two (T100, T097, T060/T086). The widow's mark hangs where it hangs and is not touched, because a shelf and an afternoon are not openings (T079).
- **Emotional turn.** A second private record is handled in front of him and stays unread, and a neighbour makes an account of two bare boards without seeing the other room: care that hides itself tells more by the hiding than the writing would, and the boy refuses to be the hand that joins either keeping to anything.
- **Sensory anchors.** The dry warm smell of the shelf crossed for one breath by the sharper smell of charcoal from under the board; the cold iron of the bench bracket at his cheek; the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the clay jar cold under its glaze; one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear.
- **Threads advanced.** T097 (further shaping, not paid), T100 (carried, unread), T060/T086, T058/T079, T077, T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T097 advances toward the far-off finger that will lay the shelf and the fold together and call them an account; T100 gathers toward the far-off day the district's private records are laid together. Nothing read, counted, copied, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The shelf keeps its two gaps above and its charcoal marks below, the finger has come away without a word, and the two rooms of the house do not touch.

## Scene 3 — *Six Where the House Can Check*

- **Day and time.** Day 142, Reed 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard and the flagstones.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; Jorra Vail; Tarn; three or four unnamed men who speak low in the back room; a High Kiln kiln-master. Walk-ons except Jorra, Tarn and Sella.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey three or four men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and go low-voiced into the room off the shed, whose door keeps its finger's width of open; Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, turns no head toward the door, gives the house no name for the men and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031).
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under the lowest rack are entered by nobody (T035/T031, T009/T013/T092).
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who would call it early and make something of it (T062).
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence and the end of her instruction arrives at the pot (T062).
  5. The boy reads the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object worth nothing joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut counting room stand beside it with no third (T062, T065/T095 carried).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction and wants the late thaw because it puts off the paying; his seventh year stands nearer its end than it has ever stood, the knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the unforged collector sentence beside it and the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach (T036/T076).
  7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check while the stones keep the shallow bright patch where it stopped; neither man asks about the ear or the room, and the two silences stay unjoined. The boy files the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat with no cause across them (T036/T076, T061/T082).
- **Emotional turn.** The household's equality holds at the place it moved to by fractions: an iron that is both mercy and mutilation, a dust that names nothing, a year the debt cannot bring himself to count, and two silences that are not joined.
- **Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal worked past the bent finger and under the nails; bone-coloured slip on clean forearms; cold iron of the damper taking the cold out of a fingertip; wet clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on the kiln-master's coat; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line; the shallow bright patch on the flagstones.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, dust, broken birth-slates unentered), T062 (damper's double bite filed as object), T065/T095 (two tellings carried, no third), T036/T076 (seventh year pressed nearer, breath unspent, unchecking stroke), T009/T013/T092, T058/T079, T061/T082, T071/T072 (142 kept exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T035 points at the Broken Slates without a name; T062 keeps the double bite for the far-off day it is understood rather than merely read; T065/T095 carry the two shut-room tellings with no third owed; T036's year is pressed toward its end without giving. Nothing paid, named, entered, called or joined.
- **Exact last image.** Tarn's stroke goes on over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it stopped, and the door off the shed standing a finger's width open.

## Scene 4 — *Seven at the Teeth*

- **Day and time.** Day 142 continued, afternoon at the Cross and the market stalls and below the north bridge, then the going of the light into the small hours at the hearth and kindling under the Vail roof.
- **Place.** The reading block and market stalls at the Cross; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge; the hearth and kindling stack under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, the boy in the basket; Berr Clay in one short appearance; the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing; a stall-keeper, an eel buyer, market goers. Walk-ons except the three household figures and Berr.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Berr comes up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth; he wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low, calls the levy and denies it with *your house*, never *I* (T015/T041/T047/T077).
  2. The figure goes into the air and not into any house; the true subtraction stays in the locked box up the clay road. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid. At the stalls he says *A number goes into a slate, not a bundle*, pays two marks short for two pair of eels, and the stall-keeper puts the marks in her palm without counting them where anyone can hear; he goes off up the road with the cord folded and the box locked, believing he has done the district a kindness (T015/T041/T047).
  3. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm with the writing inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; nobody takes him off (T090/T080/T083).
  4. Under the roof, with the coals banked and the small knife set back where it could be checked, Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and sets the knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot (T036/T076).
  5. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes **a seventh time** — after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17 and 20 — all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there with no figure and no name. He draws the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom (T036/T076).
  6. The chapter's one meaningful change: the boy files the seventh stopping on the interval tray beside the six as a **seventh interval and not a seventh payment** — an object among objects, not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh hearing. He refuses to let seven likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; no payer has been named to any of the seven (T036/T076, T061/T082).
  7. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room, and the two silences stay unjoined. The seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given; the loan stays uncalled. The carried fire keeps its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty-two days (T088, T058/T079).
- **Emotional turn.** A thing stopped seven times is still a held thing, not a settled one: the chapter's change is a further interval, bought by a breath a man does not spend and recorded by a child who cannot spend it either. Seven likenesses must be held apart by hand or they will stand up by morning as an account with a payer.
- **Sensory anchors.** Hot clay over the smell of nothing at all; green glaze at a mouth corner; a thumb wiped twice on an apron before touching nothing; a slate held unopened to a forearm; banked coals under blind ash; the small knife's worn thumb-mark at the edge of the sill; the pale clip-ring at Tarn's left wrist; the small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in.
- **Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077, T090/T080/T083, T036/T076 (seventh stopping withheld, loan uncalled, seventh year held), T061/T082 (seventh interval as object not payment), T088, T058/T079, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T036 advances the loan one interval further and something must still give at the end of the seventh year, but not here and not by a counting of the knife; T041 keeps the true subtraction in the box; T090 holds a Court presence set ahead of something. Nothing paid, named, opened, called, spent, cured or ended.
- **Exact last image.** The seventh stopping lies on the tray among six others and none of the seven has a payer; the fire gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and the loan stands uncalled with the year one tend from its end.

## Scene 5 — *Neither Knot Counted*

- **Day and time.** Day 143, Reed 24, Fourthday, Y.H. 813, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Weir, the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone and the lower boards; below the sill at the fish-trade shade; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the reed-cutter; Orra Weir; Mira Silt; the soft-cuffed clerk; a weir woman who cannot read a cord, a buyer, a woman winding wool about a child's foot and a neighbour with a pail. Walk-ons except Sella, Orra and Mira; the reed-cutter stays unnamed.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle and the short length of grey wool or reed-fibre cord gone grey with the oil of a working thumb; he sets the bundle on the flat stone, straightens the cord, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat, drawing the coat closed over it with the flat of his hand (T099).
  2. To the weir woman who asks what the cord holds he says *You can ask the cord*; to *I can't read a cord*, *No more can the Court*. No figure, knot, name or joining; the cord is not laid beside the charcoal under the shelf, the empty place in the returning line, Mira's cords or Orra's knot-string (T099 carried, not paid).
  3. Orra stands at the lower boards with her board on its side and her hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, salt white at her wrists, and pulls one knot down with her thumb. To the woman who asks what the levy will take she gives a date — *The ninth of Ilmen. The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them* — and to *That isn't the levy*, *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* The forty-first double turn stays legible with its name unspoken (T024).
  4. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch; asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer — *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* — and flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, putting one away (T059/T025).
  5. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child's knot untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's and does not touch it (T059/T025/T024).
  6. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard and a neighbour with a pail. The surgeon's reading — the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform — works perfectly and buys nothing; no silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it (T093/T094/T096).
  7. The soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the landing for the uncalled measure (T090/T080). Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies, gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket, and carries the boy home at the going of the light (T058/T079).
- **Emotional turn.** Three private records stand within a dozen paces of the basket — a cord in a coat, a knot-string on a board, a second twist beneath an apron — and every one is visible and none is enterable; the boy keeps them side by side and not across.
- **Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried in grey strokes; silt pale in the hollow of a neck; the grey cord oiled by a working thumb; the small flat knock of a knot pulled down; river silt and cold fish and tallow smoke; three turns of wool around a foot that stays dark; salt white at Orra's wrists.
- **Threads advanced.** T099, T024/T059/T025, T093/T094/T096, T090/T080, T058/T079, T071/T072 (143 kept exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T099, T024 and T025 gather with the charcoal under the shelf, Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin toward the far-off day a hand not his lays the district's private records together; T096 carries the cold foot uncured toward Arc 3. Nothing counted, read, named, cured, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The buyer goes off with the eels, Mira's thumb comes out from under her apron, and the reed-cutter's cord is back inside his coat with its knots unread while the child's foot stays dark in three turns of wool.

## Scene 7 — *One Hundred and Forty-Three*

- **Day and time.** Day 143 continued, the going of the light into the small hours.
- **Place.** The clay road up from Low Kiln; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle.
- **Present.** Sella Vail and the boy only at the last; the boy alone at the final line.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, and gives the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  2. She goes around a pan set out to dry and the trough by the well with its skin of ice, and goes wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it (T009/T013/T092).
  3. Under the roof she sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, lifting him out of the basket without looking into it and setting him down as one sets a pot down where it will not be slopped; she puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and lets her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it goes away (T058/T079).

She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale, sets the pot rim out with its handle toward the reaching hand — the handle standing two inches beyond the hand that will not close on it — and neither looks toward the bed nor touches the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening (T058/T079, T079).

  7.   8. Cold arrives in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. - **Emotional turn.** The file comes back exact, complete and worthless — the whole of an inventory that cannot be lifted to any table — and the keeping of the things apart is the only honest work in the room and the one no slate will enter.
