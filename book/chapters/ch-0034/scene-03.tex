\daymark{142}{Reed 23, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday)}

Before the first grey the yard in \place{Low Kiln} had the look of a pot stood out overnight: the same shape in the same place on the ground, with the warmth gone out of it and a skin drawn over the top. \person{Sella Vail} came down from under the kiln-house roof with the basket held level against her hip and the fold tucked in with her thumb, and gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the morning --- Keep it off his ears; it had come all the way down in the dark and would go all the way back up --- and then to the wind that got in under the eaves and went looking for the cracks, and to nothing that sat in a basket. It was a carried pot and she carried it as one, with the weight on the forearm nearest the wall and the basket square, so that nothing slopped and nothing in it was asked what it wanted. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay with his cheek to the wool and the small wet sound coming and going as his head wandered and was not set right. The wool had ridden back from the small bare joint of his wrist, and the cold had got in there in its known order, wrist before temple, temple before cheek, and he had stopped being surprised by the order some weeks ago and had not stopped wanting it to be different. One hundred and forty-two days old. Four months and twenty-two days. The numbers lay edge to edge with all the others, exact, complete and unspent, and there was no thumb in the district that would have known what to do with them.

Three or four men came up out of the river road and across \place{the Clay Yard} with their collars high against the early air. They did not come as a group comes to a door in daylight, with a hand raised and a word said; they came the way water comes at a stone, each one arriving a little behind the one before, and the last of them put his hand to the door-lip before his eyes went to the yard. It was a small thing and the boy saw it and kept it on the tray where such things lay: the hand to the record before the person, whether the record was a slate, a cord, the underside of a shelf or the edge of a door held a finger's width open. He did not lay it across Berr's twice-wiped thumb or the soft-cuffed clerk's slate or the finger that went beneath the loaf shelf. Hands went first in this district because the district kept its accounts in places a face could not reach; it was a habit and not a doctrine, and he had no standing to enter anybody's hand anywhere.

The men went low-voiced into \place[the back room]{the room off the shed}, and the door of it kept its finger's width of open as it had kept it on other mornings, as though a door left so could still be counted as shut. Sella carried past at the half-pace. She turned no head toward the door. She gave the house no name for the men and did not say the room was cold, and the low voices went on behind the finger of open without arriving at the basket as anything that could be repeated. A thing not named is not therefore a thing not kept; she kept it by not naming it, the way she kept the two lines in the corner by not sweeping them, and the household went on around the keeping as a yard goes around a stone.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} before the men's voices had gone out of the air. The dust was ground as small as meal and worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, grey and fine, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He held his forearms a little away from his coat as he came through, so the dust should not brush off on the door, and kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray the way a potter keeps two slips that must never be mixed. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate, because asking either would have made the dust into an entry, and the dust was carried where it was carried and named nothing. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} at the yard's edge the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, gone around and entered by nobody. A grey powder on a working forearm is a record as exact as any slate, and this one told what had been handled and not what it had cost, and the boy put it on the same place as the carrying arm and left it there.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone, before Sella, before the pot he took off the bench, and the words went to the wheel as a man's words go to an animal he has worked beside and has no need to flatter. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot, low, with no welcome in the voice and no unkindness either, he said, “You'll do.” The pot went on the wheel. He knew \lore{the full note} that was in it before it left his hands --- knew it three turns ahead, as he had known it on other mornings --- and kept the knowing behind his teeth, with a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} standing not two paces off in wet clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} on his coat. The kiln-master said the pot was singing before its time, and that a pot that sings early generally sings itself out early, and Jorra said nothing at all. He did not say it because saying it would call the note early and make something of it, and he had spent sixteen years watching men make something of notes. The boy put the withheld knowing on the tray of near-full answers, advanced and not paid, and did not add it to the hearings. He had six of the full note on a place of their own and no seventh; a knowing kept behind a man's teeth is not a sound, and he did not promote it into the empty place to keep the row tidy.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low, Jorra, the fire's --- ” The words were still going toward the pot when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand, found the tongue by heart, moved it a hair's breadth and left it standing where he had put it. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave a little of it back in reverse, the way a clamp gives back to the hand that sets it. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: Sella shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot, the broom found its line again, and the kiln-master went on talking about early singing to a man who had already answered him with his hands. Nobody said the room off the shed was cold. Nobody said the men had come in with their collars high. The door kept its finger's width of open, and the low voices went on behind it, or stopped and left the space as a shape in the air, and from the basket one was the same as the other.

The boy read the iron again as a man reads an instrument he has used and put down a thousand times. It was one piece of metal. Its bite held a vessel whole, and its bite could take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that carried both mercies in one breath; the house had one word for the holding and another for the taking and kept them on two shelves, as it kept two slips from mixing. He had set such a bite on a forearm in a country whose rooms he would never have described to these people, and had watched the Warm back up behind it like meltwater standing against a jam of ice, and had known exactly what he was stopping and what it would cost if he left it a moment too long. That knowledge was a tool and a clamp was a clamp; it was not a doctrine, and he would not have asked it to be one. Here it was one iron with two bites, filed as an object worth nothing and joined to no kiln. He did not carry it to the shut counting room mentioned over buckets in the yard; he did not carry the shut room to the iron. The two tellings stood on their own tray --- breath through the nose, breath in the throat, both saying not the war's hand --- and this morning's men went into a room with a door a finger's width open and gave no third telling to anyone. The shut room of the two tellings and the room off this shed stood where they stood, and neither had been made the other. He kept them apart by hand, the way another man keeps three slips from mixing, and the keeping was the whole of the work and could not be entered anywhere.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave it to the broom before she gave it to him --- Round it. Go around --- and he took it up as a man takes up a tool already named, saying “Yes, house” to \lore{the thaw} early and late, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the Vail corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. He moved quickly, as he always moved, and stopped moving altogether when anybody told him he had done well, which nobody had done this morning and which he had arranged his morning to avoid.

His seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood, and was held. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six counted in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh where it could not --- the one entry in this house the house could not see --- and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it, unentered, as it had lain for six years. His chest had been tight since the dark. The breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16 stood behind the small interval at the top of each reach, a gap in the breathing shaped like a held thing, and he worked around it as a man works around a stone he will not lift. Something must give at the end of the seventh year. It had not given this morning. The knife stayed where it could be checked, the six stayed in the worn mark, and the seventh stayed where nobody but the house's own debt could stand and look at it.

\place{the flagstones} before the kiln-house kept the shallow bright patch where the stroke had stopped on an earlier morning, a place nobody entered and nobody swept around. His stroke went on over it without the half-check. The bristles passed the place and went toward the road a man takes down when a house wants a collector, over and over, and he did not once turn his head along it. Jorra at the wheel did not ask about the ear. Tarn at the broom did not ask about the room. The two silences went on under one roof in the shape they had taken, and the boy set the unchecking stroke on the tray beside the earlier half-beat and laid no finger across them. A stroke that went on was a stroke that went on; a finger standing in cold iron was a finger standing in cold iron; there was no cause that would carry from one to the other, and if he had had one he would not have spent it. He had been given no standing in either silence and had not asked for any, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled with the year pressed toward its end and not yet given.

The door off the shed kept its finger's width of open. One hundred and forty-two went on without anybody's thumb upon it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water that came through the gap and came down against the fold, exact, complete and worth nothing, and the stroke went on over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped.
