## Chronicle

On the twenty-second day the reed month went into its last week and Sella Vail carried the boy down at the half-pace from every open slate, instruction to the wool and wind, never the basket. The bundles came up judged by the arm that carried them; a cutter told a girl with a creel that her arm knew the bundle, and a woman who asked what the hands would be paid was answered with the weather and the river. The yard went around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, and the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. A neighbour under the roof named the old shelf-gap and the boy’s empty ear under one appetite in one breath, not seeing she had made two rooms one absence. Sella sent her to the pot and reached a finger beneath the board where the charcoal lay, without looking or a word.

Before first grey on the twenty-third day men with collars high went low-voiced into the room off the wheel-shed, and Sella carried past with no name for them. Jorra came through with slate dust into both forearms, spoke to the wheel before anyone, told a pot it would do and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella’s unfinished sentence. At the Cross Berr Clay read one in twelve, denied the levy with your house, never I, paid two marks short for eels and went off believing he had done a kindness. At the hearth Tarn told the kindling Six counted. The next I don’t count, brought the ear-sentence a seventh time to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. The boy laid the stopping beside the six: seven intervals, not seven payments; he would not let seven likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

On the twenty-fourth day the reed-cutter turned his grey cord on the flat blessing stone, tied one low with his teeth and told a woman who could not read it that no more could the Court; Orra Weir gave a date where a figure was asked, the ninth of Ilmen and the boat with six oars and the man at the stern who never touched them; Mira Silt answered a thin run with Ask the river and kept her thumb under her apron to a second cord. A child’s foot stayed dark in three turns of wool. In the small hours he turned out his file and found every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else. One hundred and forty-three days old, he wanted the crust and the number spoken by somebody who would know what a number was, and got neither. His hand went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold.

  - **Sella Vail**, 33, keeper of the fire --- gone to her own bed after carrying him up the clay road at the half-pace, going around the pan set out to dry, the trough with its skin of ice and the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie, lifting him out of the basket without looking into it, setting him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, putting the fold right without looking at his face, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed, telling the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turning the coals with the back of a broken pot, saying \emph{The house will keep it}, counting under her breath toward no opening, pushing coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak pale off the coals), setting the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and neither looking toward the bed nor touching \lore{the widow’s mark}, because a dusk is not an opening. On Day 142 she gave the house no name for the men who went into the back room and did not say the room was cold. She gave nobody a figure and answered cost-questions with the pot and the house.
  - **Tarn**, 22 --- last seen on Day 142 evening at the hearth and kindling with the coals banked, the small knife set back where the house could check it at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark and the seventh where the house cannot check, the unforged collector sentence beside it. He told the kindling \emph{Six counted. The next I don’t count} and brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it a **seventh** time --- on Reed 23 after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17 and 20 --- all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name, drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with the broom. By day his stroke had gone on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and he did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. His seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given; something must give at the end of it. \lore{Ash} is worked into his knuckle creases, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is, and his chest has been tight since the dark.
  - **Jorra Vail**, 16 --- seen on Day 142 morning and again in the evening coming through from the shed with the day’s dust still ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip, keeping dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before anyone (\emph{It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady}), told a pot \emph{You’ll do}, knew its full note three turns ahead and withheld it from a High Kiln kiln-master who said it was singing before its time; before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and left it standing, and the household straightened around his silence. He told the evening pot it would do and did not look toward the bed or ask about the ear. \emph{He’s not Vail} stays sealed; the promise of the foundling’s name to the men who burn slates is ungiven and not withdrawn.
  - **Berr Clay**, 48, Thane of Clay --- one short appearance on Day 142 afternoon at the Cross and the eel stalls: red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat, green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one corner of his mouth, hot clay before him and the smell of nothing behind. His hands are large and clean; he wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passed a finger over the figure, read \emph{one in twelve, as the notice says}, called the \lore{levy} and denied it with \emph{your house}, never \emph{I}. He said \emph{A number goes into a slate, not a bundle. If your house wants it again, there’ll be a slate at the watch}, took two pair of eels and paid two marks short into the stall-keeper’s palm without her counting where anyone could hear, let a low voice about the levy fall on the boards, and went off up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded, the slate under his arm and the box locked with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The figure he read did not know the hand that had not come back.
  - **Mira Silt**, 27 --- seen on Day 143 at the lower boards working eels, with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run would be thin she answered her own answer --- \emph{Thin? Who’s to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river} --- flicked eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, moved two from hand to hand and put one away. To the buyer who named a figure: \emph{Two marks the pair and two short. You’ve said it twice and I’ve counted it twice. There’s nothing in the sentence that wants an answer.} Her thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord and stayed longer than the figure required; a shifting creel showed the **second cord twisted twice** beneath it, never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child’s knot untouched. Her board lay beside Orra’s and did not touch it. She said something low about a boat that had come up on the flood and nothing about the hand that had not come out of the strips; the boy could not hear the low part and did not try.
  - **Orra Weir**, 34 --- seen on Day 143 at the lower boards with her board on its side and a grey cord at her waist, her hands like old rope, the pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, salt white at her wrists, the grey streak first through the black hair and the nasal half-song of the Fen in her voice where she was tired. She pulled one knot down with her thumb and set it among the others with a small flat knock. To the woman who asked what the levy would take she gave a date --- \emph{The ninth of Ilmen. The collector’s boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them} --- and to \emph{That isn’t the levy}, \emph{It’s a date. The river takes what the Court doesn’t. You can shout at either one. I’ve tried}. Her thumb went to the cord again at the end of the sentence; the boy could not tell whether that was a lie or the habit of counting and made no cause of it. The forty-first double turn, tail tucked, left over right with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in, stayed legible with its name unspoken, and the knot-string went back upriver to the \place{Eel House} where no clerk could spell it.
