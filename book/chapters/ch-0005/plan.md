# Chapter 5 — “Nineteen Steps” — beat sheet (private)

**Days:** 7–9 — Stone 13 (Thirdday), Stone 14 (Fourthday), Stone 15 (Fifthday), Y.H. 812. Three lived days, no gaps, no passage mode. Day 6 is finished and is not revisited as a scene.
**Arc:** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — “a warm stone, already going cold.”
**POV:** Daniel Marchetti (Corin), close third-person past tense, inside a seven-, then eight-, then nine-day-old body carried everywhere. Present tense only for one or two sentences of Chicago breaking through in Scene 4, with no voice tagged. No recap, no Court scene, no departure from Dan.

## Carried in from Chapter 4 — guardrails

- **Body.** Seven days old at first light of Day 7, nine at dusk of Day 9. Cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin: twelve to fifteen embers at best, flaw unnamed, rate of loss unmeasured. He drools; the fold will not stay under his chin; the head goes where it wants; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward a pot, a bench, a cord, his own chin, and comes down. Carried in a basket or against a hip for every scene; cannot roll, stand, speak, read Grathic, reach a cup, or lift a hand to take part in anything.
- **The ear.** Five hearings of the flat note from four sources: a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed, the district's slate twice at the Cross, a dying man under the collector's tube at the Drawing — the fifth the first from a person. **This chapter adds no sixth hearing.** Day 7 gives an *anticipated* note that does not come: the object for the file is the interval where it should have been, not a sound. Tarn's ear is present for the third time (after Ch 2 and Ch 4) and is witnessed by the reader only; nobody under the roof names it. What is new in this chapter is that **Tarn sees the boy turn toward the pot before it is set down** — the first time anyone under that roof has witnessed his ear, and the first time he has been in a position to witness Tarn's. Two ears, one house, not one word between them.
- **The private file.** Four objects already filed and not joined: the Vail Ash ridge (near two thumbs wide), the death-house ridge (thicker by a finger's width, turning the corner without a break), the charred edge of a slate in the kiln ash, the barrow of grey that went toward the north gate. This chapter adds the death-house bench's count, the missing interval, the cord, the eel-bones and Dessa's nineteen steps — still not joined. Joining would be a kind of deciding he has no standing to make.
- **Count.** Four, four and a gap: kiln breaths, floorboards, bell, death-house bench. New counts in this chapter are added beside the gap, never into it. The gap is the part he keeps.
- **Withheld throughout.** No explanation of the flat note, the bell, the birth-flower, the widow's mark, the charred slate, the debt against the tiles, or why Sella took him. No Sella roll, no Berr's box, no Alda's tin opened, no word *forty*, no name Marra, no Court term in narration, no one in three. Mira never says she knows the boy is Dessa's son. Dessa never looks into the basket and still believes him spent among the levy. Fenna is a silhouette with no dialogue. The three pauses stay three; the Drawing collector's hand stays separate and without a pause. Low Kiln still says three days to the Five and will not say what it counts.
- **Costs and payers.** The gruel, the crust, the eels, the barley and the warmth of the hearth all come from someone's fire and are entered in no slate. The house that paid twice and then took a measure out of the district goes on keeping the boy without saying so. Every kindness in this chapter has a payer who can be named and a recipient who cannot refuse it.

## Scene 1 — “The Note That Did Not Come”

- **Day and time.** Day 7, Stone 13, first light, before and at the opening of the kiln.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house and the threshold into the Clay Yard; the wheel-shed seen through the doorway.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail (in the shed), Garl Vail as seat, coat, ridge and proverb.
- **Beats.**
  1. Open on the coals holding under their ash, the door at its finger's width of failure, the cold coming in along the floor to the grey ridge. Not on a thought. The boy is awake under the fold with a thread of drool at his mouth that nobody wipes; Sella puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face.
  2. Sella's hand goes to the widow's mark at her throat before she opens the kiln and at no other time. She holds it a moment longer than the touch requires, then gives the instruction to the fire and the pot, never to the basket. The rule holds; the boy already knows the rule and does not know what it is for.
  3. She feeds him from the house's own dish — barley gruel thinned with milk, warmed at the fire, spoon wiped on her sleeve — holding his head with two fingers behind the ear because he cannot hold it himself. She says the house, never I. The bed under the roof and the loft above the shed are both still as they were; the arithmetic is performed again without anyone calling it triage. He reads it with the part of him that once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and has none of the standing to act on it.
  4. A pot waits on the bench in the shed. **Before anyone sets it down, the boy turns his head toward it** — the expectation of a note, not the hearing of one. It is set down softly and gives nothing. He keeps the interval where the note should have been. Do not count it as a hearing; do not name the sound in Grathic.
  5. Tarn comes up the yard with his broom. The small bench knife is already back on the sill where it can be checked. He goes through the motion of checking it with his eyes, lowers his head over the broom, and stops for half a stroke at the soft set-down — listening for something that does not come. He has seen the boy turn his head before the pot was set down. He says nothing, and the boy says nothing. The reader sees the second ear a third time; nobody under the roof sees either ear spoken of.
  6. Jorra is at the wheel with bone-coloured slip dried up both forearms, the bent little finger, no slate dust today. He tells a pot it will do, shifts the damper before Sella has finished, and does not look at the boy. The absence of dust is as much a fact as its presence was on Stone 12; the boy notices the difference and cannot ask which it means.
- **Emotional turn.** From the safety of a rule kept perfectly — mark, fire, fold, dish — to the sharp small danger of being seen. The boy has spent seven days being the only person in a room who notices; now someone else under the roof has noticed him noticing, and has chosen silence. That is not safety and it is not yet a threat. It is an equality he has no word for.
- **Sensory anchors.** Coals banked under ash; the smell of woodsmoke, wet wool and skimmed-milk Ash; the door's finger's width of wind; the little-door burn at Sella's hand closing first on nothing and then on the spoon; the widow's mark, thumb-length of lead-glazed clay on its cord; barley gruel thinned with milk; bone-coloured slip on Jorra's forearms; the soft, unstruck set-down of a pot that gives no note; Ash in the creases of Tarn's knuckles and the pale ring at his wrist.
- **Threads advanced.** T076 (two ears in one house; Tarn witnesses the boy's ear for the first time), T036 (the knife back where it can be checked; the sixth year held), T061 (a non-hearing: the interval kept, count unchanged), T071/T072 (the count extended to the bench and the missing interval), T060 (food and fold entered nowhere), T058/T079 (the rule of the mark, the wall held), T065 (the kiln's fours and a gap).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: Tarn's silence over what he saw becomes the value someone will one day pay for (toward ~330, his refusal); the expected-but-absent note teaches the boy that a thing can be kept without happening (toward the third thing, ~1300). Paid off: nothing. No thread is joined or closed.
- **Exact last image.** The boy lying under the fold with his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the bench and coming down; Tarn's lowered head over the broom; the pot on the bench silent; the coals taking; the door not quite shutting.

## Scene 2 — “The Uncounted Year”

- **Day and time.** Day 7, Stone 13, mid-morning through dusk.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed, the yard and the Debt Loft ladder.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Garl Vail as absence.
- **Beats.**
  1. The working day: clay dust and slip, the wheel's rhythm, pots set on the drying rack, the kiln breathing in fours and missing the fifth. Sella counts loaves and bits and days; the number of days to Kindling is gone around by somebody who came back from looking at the ice and does not want to give it. The boy registers the withheld number as a shape, not a value.
  2. Tarn's wage: six bits a term, a year of the seven left, and a count he has kept every day and will not count the next. He agrees with everything so thoroughly that there is nowhere for an argument to go. When Sella tells him he has done well, he stops altogether, then begins again. The boy reads the stopping the way he once read a resident who would not meet a senior's eye.
  3. Jorra speaks to the pots and to the wheel, not to Sella and not to the boy; the sentence about his not being Vail is still unspoken and still sealed. He goes quieter when the levy is spoken around, quieter than Sella, and the yard straightens up around it.
  4. The boy extends his private file: the death-house bench with four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb; the interval where the pot's note did not come; beside the two Ash ridges, the charred slate and the barrow of grey. He sets them side by side and does not join them. The surgeon's eye keeps finding the same architecture — bench by door, draught under it, pot on the bench, gap in the count — and makes nothing of the repetition.
  5. The body's humiliations continue without comment: the drool freezing at the corner of the mouth, the head that goes where it wants, the small wet sound when it goes too far, the hand that cannot reach the bench two paces off. The dry joke lives in the exactness, never in pathos.
  6. Dusk: coals banked with the back of a broken pot, fold put right without a look, wind at the door's finger's width. Nobody has said what was taken two doors down on Stone 12, and the day has gone on being ordinary.
- **Emotional turn.** From the morning's small tension of being seen to the larger, quieter recognition that the whole house is an arrangement of things nobody will enter: a bed, a loft, a seventh year, a number, a ridge. The boy's competence is useless and his attention is the only possession anyone cannot tax. The turn is not despair but a colder, more exact attention.
- **Sensory anchors.** Slip dried to old bone; wet clay; the wheel's wet turning; cold ash underfoot; the smell of a corner not swept; six bits counted in a man's head; loaves and a broken pot used as a coal-shovel; the small wet sound of a head going too far; grey ridge turning the corner without a break; the hollow wheel-seat and the salt-stiff coat on its peg; Garl's proverb said to the pot — *leave the crack showing*.
- **Threads advanced.** T077 (the number withheld and gone around), T071/T072 (count extended to the death-house bench and to the missing interval), T009 (Garl's objects and the uncalled debt spoken around), T013 (the ridge left unswept), T031 (the charred slate still in the kiln ash), T035 (Jorra's silence and the unnameable back room — dust absent today), T036 (the seventh year not counted), T058 (Sella's wall), T060 (fire fed and food entered nowhere), T081 (the same room repeated, not recognised).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: the uncounted seventh year toward Tarn's refusal (~330); the withheld number toward the true deduction (~640); the repeated architecture toward the Weighing Hall (~640) and the Court room. Paid off: nothing.
- **Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with the fold at his chin, the coals holding under ash, the grey ridge turning the corner without a break, the hand outside the wool going up an inch and coming down, and Low Kiln saying three days to the Five without saying what it counts.

## Scene 3 — “Eels at the Fishstairs”

- **Day and time.** Day 8, Stone 14, before dawn breaking into morning.
- **Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge, Sill.
- **Present.** Corin in the basket, Sella Vail, Mira Silt, Berr Clay (short exchange), eel-sellers and gulls as walk-ons.
- **Beats.**
  1. Sella carries the basket down from the Clay Yard to buy eels; the boy is carried because there is nobody to leave him with. Arrival in layers: gulls, wet rope, salted perch, eel slime, the cold off the water, the north bridge overhead. The Ilm lies under its skin of ice; the Singing Sill is downstream and not explained.
  2. Mira Silt in her own element: slime dried pale on her sleeves, the top of her left ear gone — a hook, she says; the scar says a knife — her head held a little to the right against the cold where the ear was. She flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, and puts one away when she decides.
  3. The six-year war: she short-weights Berr Clay's levy order by two eels and he has paid her two marks short for the same six years. Neither acknowledges it. Berr wipes his thumb before touching a slate, speaks in the district's passive grammar, and is reasonable. She asks him the eel price and answers it herself so that nothing in the sentence wants an answer. Keep it short — a market exchange, not a speech.
  4. Someone asks about the foundling in the basket. Mira answers with a question and then her own answer: he is warm; that is all she will say on a slate, and she is saying it off the slate. She does not look into the basket. She will not sit where a slate is open. The boy puts her not-looking beside the other kinds he has learned to read — one priced him, one kept him — and has no word for either.
  5. A number is spoken — the eel price, or the two marks short, or the days to the Five given out by a voice that has counted what it has. Mira's hand goes to a cord at her waist and her thumb finds a knot. The boy sees the movement and not the knots. Do not show the knot-string plainly; do not name it in narration; do not let her say why she touches it or what the cord holds.
  6. Changes: a second kind of keeper is on the page — one whose accounts are carried on her person, in a material no Court can take, and whose kindness arrives briskly before anybody can make a speech about it. The boy cannot read any of it and notices all of it.
- **Emotional turn.** From the house's wall of unentered kindness to a woman who makes a small, visible performance of not telling — and who is therefore, to the boy, the first person in the district whose keeping looks like a craft rather than a wall. He does not trust her; he cannot place her; he watches.
- **Sensory anchors.** Gulls crying; salted perch; eel slime dried pale on sleeves; wet rope and cold river stones; the missing top of a left ear and the scar that contradicts the hook story; eel-bones flicked against a thumb and put away; two marks short; the soft cuff and lead-glaze green at a Thane's mouth; grey wool cord touched at the waist; the Ilm under ice.
- **Threads advanced.** T025 (the cord touched when a number is spoken; barley arrangement still unshown), T077 (the number given out and not given), T060 (brisk kindness), T071/T072 (the count extended to the Fishstairs), T081 (the Fishstairs as another room with its bench, draught and gap, not recognised), T041/T047 held quiet (Berr's care with the slate, his passive grammar, still a good reason).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: the cord and the eel-bones toward the district's private accounts (~1400); Mira's missing ear and her question-answer idiolect toward the quarrel in which she will tell Jorra the truth (~160); Berr's two marks short as the small arithmetic of a large theft (~640). Paid off: nothing.
- **Exact last image.** Mira's thumb coming away from the grey cord at her waist as she turns back to the eels, the boy in the basket at the edge of the steps with his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the cord and coming down, and the gulls going on over the ice.

## Scene 4 — “Steps Down to the Water”

- **Day and time.** Day 8, Stone 14, later the same morning and the walk back up to the Clay Yard.
- **Place.** The Fishstairs, the north bridge, the road down to the Clay Yard.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Mira Silt (still at the stall), a collector crossing the bridge as a walk-on, market voices.
- **Beats.**
  1. Rumours are already in the air at dawn though they are sold at noon: which boats on the Ilm carry something other than fish, which collectors take their ale before a collection and which after, which households are warm and which are cooling. The boy hears figures he cannot spend and a language he cannot read; the market is an account read aloud in a tongue he does not have.
  2. Mira sells, answers, and moves eel-bones from hand to hand; a figure under her breath, another bone moved. The boy watches the counting and then the cord again, trying to make the two agree, and cannot. Attention is the only thing he has ever been able to keep, and it has never once given him a hand to act with.
  3. A collector crosses the bridge with a flat satchel and a soft cuff. **Two sentences of Chicago break through in the present tense:** a hospital cafeteria at four in the morning, the fluoro hum, a paper wrapper he finished to the last crumb because waste offended him, and his hand going to a pocket that held a lit phone that night and holds wool in this one. No voice, no name, no date. Then the river and the gulls close over it. Do not tag a `\memory{}` and do not explain the seam.
  4. He counts the steps down to the water and the landing that is not a step — four, four, and a place where a step should be — and files the gap beside the kiln's and the bell's. The hand goes to the place where a pocket should have been and finds wool.
  5. Sella buys eels, says nothing about the collector or the cord or where they have been, and carries him home with the bucket swinging at her knee; the smell of river silt goes up the Clay Yard with them. She puts the fold right at his ear when they reach the door and does not look at his face.
- **Emotional turn.** The old life's thrift and the new life's hunger turn out to be the same appetite — a thing to be finished because waste offends — and the boy is briefly, uncomfortably at home in a cafeteria he cannot name. The return to the Clay Yard is a small humiliation: he has been given a whole morning and can carry none of it out.
- **Sensory anchors.** River silt and salt; gulls; ice on the Ilm; the soft washed cuff crossing the bridge; fluorescent light and a crumpled paper wrapper (present tense, Chicago only); the small cold rectangle that is not there; the landing that is not a step; the bucket swinging at a knee; wool smelling of woodsmoke over river silt.
- **Threads advanced.** T004/T044 (the pocket-place reached once more, the name not looked at, the boat carried as absence), T071/T072 (the Fishstairs steps and the landings gap), T025 (the cord seen twice), T075 (the collector's ordinary kindness, not addressed), T081 (the market as a room repeated).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: the cafeteria's finished wrapper as the old life's version of the crust and the stone — warmth taken in and entered nowhere (toward ~1500). Paid off: nothing. No voice is tagged and the past life is not explained.
- **Exact last image.** Sella's knee swinging the bucket up the Clay Yard, the boy under the fold with the river smell on the wool and his hand outside it closing on nothing, the Vail door not quite shutting.

## Scene 5 — “Nineteen Steps, One Knot”

- **Day and time.** Day 9, Stone 15, morning at the market's edge (an ordinary morning's fish-trade at the edge of the Cross, not an Account-day market).
- **Place.** The edge of the market below the north bridge; the Fishstairs and the Cross within sight.
- **Present.** Corin in the basket, Sella Vail, Dessa Silt, Mira Silt, Fenna Clay as a silent silhouette across the market, market voices and gulls.
- **Beats.**
  1. Sella carries the boy down again; he is set at the edge of the trade, in a patch of shade that is not where a slate lies open. Dessa Silt stands at the end of her nineteen steps, shoulder against nothing, thin and worked older, slip-grey nails, wet clay at the hem.
  2. The cough comes up out of the pits and she calls it weather. The boy reads what he can read without guessing and without a diagnosis: a vessel going thin, the cough, the pale scar under her thumb, the way she holds herself against the cold. He says nothing. Nobody in the district says anything else either. Do not name the pits as a lie in narration.
  3. The count: nineteen steps from pit to drying rack, nineteen back, every day for six years; now the count has gone out of her feet into her thumb and runs there without her legs. She gets specific — loaves, bits, the day of the month — because specifics are a handle on a thing too large. The door-shut smile comes down. The boy is carried to the edge of that count and does not know he is inside it.
  4. She does not look into the basket. The boy takes the not-looking in with the cold air: the kind that keeps, not the kind that prices. She will not say the number and will not say the name and has not said either for three years. Keep the Forty unspoken and the lie unexposed.
  5. Across the market, a girl with green at the corner of her mouth stands a silhouette and says nothing. The boy registers the green and the fact that she is looking at the pots, not at him. No dialogue, no name on the page, no recognition, no tapping. The plant is visual only.
  6. Mira's knot-string is glimpsed: a cord of grey wool at her waist, touched when a number is spoken; a second cord kept apart, which she has never taken the length of the river to Nine Pots and never knocked on. Show the cord and the thumb on a knot; do not show the knots plainly, do not count them, do not explain either cord. The boy watches a woman keep accounts in a material no Court can confiscate and has no words for the knowing.
  7. Mira gives him a crust quickly, briskly, into the fold at his side before anyone can make a speech about it — brown-edged Vail-smoked barley, a little smoke, a warmth that lies against him through the wool. **He cannot eat it.** He can only smell the barley and the smoke and feel its warmth through the fold while his hand outside the wool fails to close on it. A kindness entered nowhere that he cannot refuse and cannot even take. Do not have an adult feed him bread; keep the infant body exact.
- **Emotional turn.** From watching a woman count to discovering he is one of the things she counts without knowing it — and from being given a warmth he cannot reciprocate to the plain, funny, awful fact that a nine-day-old cannot even lift a crust. The recognition of the cord is the chapter's largest inward movement and it arrives without a single word of explanation.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet clay at a hem; cough from the pits called weather; the pale sieve-wire scar under a thumb; slip-grey nails; nineteen counted in a thumb; grey wool cord and a second cord kept apart; eel-bones flicked; brown-edged barley crust, smoke, warmth through wool; green at the corner of a girl's mouth; the market bell striking four, four and a gap somewhere back toward the Cross.
- **Threads advanced.** T008/T073 (the lie still held; the number and the name still unsaid; Dessa does not look), T025 (the cord glimpsed, the knots withheld), T059 (the separate cord for the child in Nine Pots, never knocked on), T060 (brisk kindness, entered nowhere), T071/T072 (nineteen steps witnessed at last, and the cord), T078 (the bell's four-four-and-a-gap in the market), T081 (the market's edge as another room with its bench, draught and gap).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: the cord and the separate cord toward ~1400, where both are read as the district's own accounts; Dessa's cough toward Day 38 and the uncollected death; the green at a girl's mouth toward Fenna's Ch 40 speaking debut and the glaze answer at ~700. Paid off: nothing. Fenna does not speak; Mira does not say she knows; the word forty is not spoken; the cough is not diagnosed.
- **Exact last image.** The crust lying warm in the fold at the boy's side while his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward it and comes down; Mira's thumb coming away from the grey cord; Dessa counting nineteen into her thumb and smiling the door shut at nothing; green at a girl's mouth across the market.

## Scene 6 — “The File Beside the Gap”

- **Day and time.** Day 9, Stone 15, dusk into night.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard at dusk; House Silt three doors down as a threshold glimpsed in the angle.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Garl Vail as objects and proverb. Dessa is not on stage; she is the count he carries.
- **Beats.**
  1. Return under the Vail roof. Sella banks the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *that'll hold* to the fire, puts the fold right without looking at his face, and says nothing about the market or the crust or the eels. The hand does not go to the widow's mark at the bank — that is for the hour before the kiln opens. The wall holds.
  2. Tarn sweeps and puts the small bench knife back where it can be checked, announcing it unasked to the yard. Jorra comes in with slip, not slate dust, speaks to a pot, shifts the damper, and does not take up the silence about the back room. Garl's coat keeps its salt-stiff shape on its peg; his seat keeps its hollow on the left; the ridge turns the corner without a break.
  3. The boy files the three days' new objects beside the old ones, exactly and without joining: the grey cord and the second cord, the eel-bones, Dessa's nineteen steps, the death-house bench's four-four-and-a-knot, the interval where the pot's note did not come — beside the death-house ridge, the Vail ridge, the charred slate and the barrow of grey. Each is useless; none is named in Grathic; the joining would be a deciding he has no standing to make.
  4. The count remains four, four and a gap — kiln, floor, bell, bench, the landing that is not a step, the space between a woman's thumb and her scar. The gap is the part nobody has thought to enter and the only place in the house where nothing has been written down. He cannot write there either.
  5. Close with forward motion, not rest: the household has begun, without saying it, to count what it has against the days before Kindling; Low Kiln still gives three days to the Five; Account-day and the Cross are ahead, and with them another reading of the slate in the neat hand whose pause he has seen three times and not joined. Alda's notes are still turned down where nobody will read them. The drawing is behind him and nothing has been explained.
- **Emotional turn.** The private file has grown in three days into something a whole district could not read, and it belongs to nobody. The boy's attention is the one thing no institution can tax — and he still cannot lift a hand to the crust in his fold, or ask a single question of anyone in the house. The turn is a colder, more exact resolve to keep counting, not a discovery.
- **Sensory anchors.** Banked coals under ash; the back of a broken pot; the little-door burn shut on nothing; wind at the door's finger's width; woodsmoke and river silt on the wool; Ash in Tarn's knuckles; the small knife on the sill; bone-coloured slip on Jorra's forearms; salt-stiff coat, hollow seat, uncalled debt spoken around; grey wool cord and brown-edged crust remembered in the fold; the count four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced.** T072 (attention as the untaxable record; the file grows), T009 (Garl's objects and proverb, ridge unswept), T013 (the ridge left where it lies), T031 (the charred slate untouched), T058/T079 (the wall and the rule of the mark held), T060 (the crust entered nowhere), T077 (the number and the days ahead), T071/T072 (the gap kept).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Planted: the growing file toward the second records' convergence (~1400) and the third thing (~1300); the coming Account-day toward the fourth sighting of the neat hand in Ch 6, still unjoined. Paid off: nothing.
- **Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with the crust lying warm in the fold at his side, the coals holding under their ash, the grey ridge turning the corner without a break, his hand outside the wool going up an inch and coming down — four, four and a gap — and Low Kiln saying three days to the Five without saying what it counts.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 6

The last image is deliberately the same furniture as the first chapter's — coals, fold, ridge, door's finger's width, hand that cannot reach — but with a file that has grown by a cord, a crust, a count and a silence witnessed by another person. Nothing has been explained and no thread has been joined. What pulls forward is arithmetic: the household has begun to count fuel and bread against the days before Kindling, Low Kiln still withholds its number, and Account-day is five days off. Chapter 6 opens on that counting — stores measured, Alda Reed coming with her tin and her exact dates, the Cross again in deep cold with a fourth sighting of the neat hand still held apart from the other three — and the boy carrying the cord and the nineteen steps into a room with a bench, a draught and a gap. Forward, not closed; no ending, no epilogue.
