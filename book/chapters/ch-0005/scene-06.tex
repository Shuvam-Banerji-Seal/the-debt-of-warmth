By dusk the wind had gone round and the gulls had stopped crying over \place{the north bridge} and gone to roost in the stone of it. \person{Sella Vail} lifted the basket off the shaded boards with the hand that carried the little-door burn and settled it against her hip the way she settled a filled pot, and started up \place{the Clay Yard} with the eel bucket knocking at her knee. The boy lay under the fold with a crust warm against his side that she had not put there and would not take out. The smell of the river went up the yard with them: salt and silt and the iron cold off \place{the Ilm}, and under it the woodsmoke that had been in the wool since the first morning of his nine days. She said nothing about the market and nothing about the woman at the eels and nothing about the crust, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing that wants an answer, and she had arranged the whole of her life around not wanting one.

The warmth lay against his ribs through the wool, a small flat warmth with no weight in it that he could neither lift nor push away, and he had learned in nine days that a kindness which cannot be answered cannot be refused. He had once been the man who decided who would be warm by morning and who would not; he was now a basket at a woman's hip with another woman's barley against his side and no hand on the end of either arm that could close on anything. Both women had paid for the warmth and neither had entered it, and he was the place where the two payments met and went quiet. The gulls were gone. The bucket knocked. The yard rose under the basket in the ordered steps he had counted on the way down and could not count now, because a carried boy counts nothing that is done to him.

Back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she put the fold right at his ear without once looking at his face, and set the basket on the bed under the roof that was not the loft above the shed. The arithmetic of the two beds had not changed in three days and would not change tonight: the foundling under the roof, the debt above the wheel. Nobody called it what it was. The door did not quite shut, as it had not shut on any night since he came under it, and the wind came in at a finger's width and went along the floor to the corner where \lore{the Ash} lay.

The first thing she did was to the fire, not to him. She took the back of a broken pot from the ash heap and used it as a shovel, drawing the coals together and laying the ash over them in a smooth blind layer, pressing the edges with the back of the pot so no air got in at the sides. “That'll hold,” she said to the fire. Her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat. That was for the hour before the kiln opened and for no other hour; the rule held as exactly in the dark as it had in the morning, and the little-door burn closed on nothing and opened and went to the bench.

Then she counted. It was not counting for anybody who could hear it. She moved her lips along the shelf where the loaves stood and the dish where the bits lay, and came back to the same place twice, and the boy watched the returning from under the fold. There was a number of days to \lore{the Five} that the district had gone around and would not give out, and she would not give it either; she had arranged the loaves so that the number was not in them but the shape of it was, which was enough for a woman who had kept one house through one Long Cold and meant to keep this one through another. He had once counted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and decided which of them would be breathing by morning. He could not lift his hand to the dish, and nobody had asked him to.

\person{Tarn} came in behind her with the broom going, bent low over it the way he bent over everything, grey Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles and the pale ring at his left wrist showing where the iron clip had sat six years ago. He swept the threshold and swept it again and set the broom against the wall. Then he took the small bench knife out of his pocket --- the boy had not seen him take it, only seen the place on the sill where it had not been --- and put it back where it could be checked.

“I took the small knife. I put it back. You can check,” he said to the yard and the bench and the fire, and to nobody who had asked him. Six years of a seven he had counted and would go on counting; the seventh he held back, the one entry in the house the house could not check. He said nothing about the note that had not come three days ago, when a pot was set down softly on a bench and gave nothing. He had said nothing about it for three days and would say nothing tonight, and under this roof there were now two people keeping the same silence and neither of them able to call it by name. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said in the house and lay in it all the same, as the Ash lay in the corner.

“Yes, house,” he said to Sella's back. “I'll sweep it again.”

\person{Jorra Vail} came in from the shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the little finger of his left hand bent the way it had been since he was eleven. There was no slate dust on him tonight. The boy looked at the clean creases above the bend of the finger and could not ask which the absence meant, any more than he could ask about the presence of the day before.

“You'll do,” Jorra said to a pot on the bench, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He went to the damper and moved it before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, and the fire took the instruction and went on. He did not say the back room was cold. He had said it yesterday and nobody had taken it further, and tonight he did not say it at all, and the silence about the room stood where he had left it, which was the only answer he would give and the only one he would take.

The coat kept its salt-stiff shape on its peg behind the door and the hollow seat in the shed kept the lean of twenty years and the ridge along the skirting board ran to the corner and turned it without a break. Nobody swept it. Nobody had swept it since the Long Cold and nobody would sweep it before the Last Day. \person{Garl Vail}'s proverb went to the pot as if the pot were a person who could hear: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. It was said to the clay and not to the boy, and the boy took it into the file with the rest of the things said to pots. Three doors down the yard the threshold of \place{House Silt} stood in the angle of the wall, a dark line where a woman had stood that morning with her shoulder against nothing and counted nineteen into her thumb.

He set the day's new objects beside the old ones in the file he could not open and had no hand to open, and did not join any of them.

There was the grey cord at a woman's waist, the colour of a wet morning, with a second cord twisted twice under it that had never gone up the river to \place{Nine Pots} and had never been lifted at a door. There were the \lore{eel-bones} moving hand to hand across a board in \place{Sill}, each movement a figure said under the breath and then put away. There were nineteen steps from a pit to a drying rack and nineteen back, now run in a thumb without any legs to carry them, and the pale scar under the thumb that the thumb went to whenever a truth came near enough to be almost said. There was the death-house bench with four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the interval where a pot on a bench under this roof had been set down softly and given nothing.

Beside these lay the older things: the ridge along the Vail skirting board near two thumbs wide, the ridge in the death-house thicker by a finger's width and turning the corner without a break, the charred edge of a slate in the kiln ash with the writing gone off the end, the barrow of grey going out through \place{the north gate} toward \place{the Bone Orchard} in the ordinary work of a market-day. He set them side by side exactly, in the order they had arrived, with nothing added and nothing taken away, the way he had once laid out a trauma note before anybody came in to make it mean something. The habit had been the whole of his trade. Here it was the whole of his nine days, and it bought him nothing he could spend.

None of the objects had a name in \lore{Grathic}. He had no word for the cord and no word for the difference between the two cords, no word for what a woman kept in a thumb, no word for the sound that had not come from the pot and therefore no word for the interval where it should have been. To name a thing in this district was to hand it to somebody who could enter it; he had watched a man with a soft cuff lay a hand on a slate before he looked at a dying man and say the verbs with the people taken out, and he had learned what a name does when it is written in a neat hand. So the file stayed wordless, and the boy lay silent, and the knowing grew in him without anybody's permission and without a single entry another person could read.

Joining the objects would have been a deciding, and he had no standing to decide. He had learned that in the first days, in a room where a woman reached for a slate before she reached for a child: a thing written down is a thing handed over. The surgeon who had once sorted twenty people in ninety seconds and sent some to a warm bed and some to a cold one had no appetite left for deciding which of these objects was the same object wearing a different face. Two ridges, a charred slate, a barrow of grey, a bench with a knot, a gap in a count, a cord at a waist, a crust in a fold: they lay side by side and he let them lie. The suspicion that they were one thing seen seven ways was a thing he kept where he kept the gap, which was the only part of any count he had ever been allowed to have.

The count stayed four, four and a gap. Four breaths of the kiln and the breath it missed. Four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb. Four strokes of \lore{the bell} back toward \place{the Cross} and the stroke that was not there. Four nails on the death-house bench and four and the knot. Four steps down to the water and a landing that was not a step. Nineteen and nineteen in a woman's thumb, which did not fit the shape and would not be made to fit. And always the space between her thumb and the pale scar, which was the same gap as the others and the only one that had a person in it. The gap was the part nobody had thought to enter and the only place in any room he had been in where nothing had been written down. He could not write there either. He had no hand, no slate, no word in the language of the house, and no standing; but the gap was his in a way the warmth in the fold was not, because nobody had paid for it and nobody could take it and nobody had even thought to look at it.

Forward, not rest. The household counted, without saying it, what it had against the days before \lore{Kindling}. Sella had counted the loaves twice and come back to the same place; Tarn held the seventh year back as he had held it for six years running; Jorra moved the damper as if the fire owed him an answer it would not give. \place{Low Kiln} still said three days to the Five when it said anything at all and would not say what it counted. \lore{Account-day} and the slate at the Cross were ahead of them, and with them another reading of the neat hand whose pause he had seen three times and had not joined to the pause at the flare or the pause on the birth-slate, and would not make a fourth until the room told him to. Alda's notes lay turned down where no mother and no officer would read them. The Drawing was behind him and nothing had been explained.

The boy lay on his bed under the roof with the crust warm in the fold at his side, the coals holding under their ash, the grey ridge running to the corner and turning it without a break, and the door standing a finger's width open to the wind. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. Four, four and a gap. Somewhere out in the district a voice that had counted what it had gave three days to the Five and would not say what it counted, and the fire went on holding, and the fold went on not staying under his chin.
