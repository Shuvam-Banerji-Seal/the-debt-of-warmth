\daymark{33}{Ilmen 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

The \place{the Vail wheel-shed} smelled of wet clay and tallow and, when the wind came wrong off \place{High Kiln}, the sweetish edge of lead glaze. \person{Corin} was carried in under the fold before the light had properly reached the bench and set at the edge of \place{the drying racks} in \place{the Clay Yard} where the baskets went, a pot set down where a pot would not be slopped. He was thirty-three days old. The fold sat at his ear. Cold came in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wheel was turning already under hands he could not yet see.

Carried, he was a thing among things. The basket's handle knocked against a hip and the fold slipped and his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound it made when it went too far; two fingers came behind his ear without a face looking down and put the head back. He read the order of the shed the way he had once read the order of a field --- where the light came from, where the cold collected, which way a body would fall --- except that the field was a room and the body was his own and small. The wheel stood in the middle of it with its seat hollow on the left, and the shed was built around that hollow the way a wound is built around the thing that made it. He counted what he could, the wheel's tread, the boards under the basket, the hands going to the damper, and let the rest stand as the household stood it, unentered.

He heard \person{Jorra Vail} before he saw him: not the step, which was quiet, but the small catch of breath a man makes when he goes out of a cold room into a warm one and decides not to notice the cold. Then Jorra was in the shed, the light behind him, grey dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of the little finger.

It was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Slip dried the colour of old bone sat on a thrower like a second skin and cracked at the knuckles when the hand opened; a boy could read the morning's work in it the way he had once read a cast left out too long, by the lines the stuff had settled into. This was finer. It went into the creases and stayed, grey as the ridge the neighbour had burned and called dealt with, the colour of a thing that has been in the corner of a room for a year and is still in the room though the corner has been cleaned. Jorra said nothing about where he had been. Nobody asked him. The men whose names were not spoken had names --- the boy knew this as he knew a chart has a name at the head of it even turned face-down --- and knew that in this house a name left off a slate was the same as a room left off a roll: present by the shape of the absence, and kept the safer for it.

He kept the two states of the same forearms on two places in the private file, the forearms with grey dust in the creases and the forearms with clean bone-coloured slip. They were the same arms and the same morning and he filed them as two entries, because a face that refuses to report a thing still leaves the report on the sleeve. He wanted to know which slate had been broken. He wanted to know whether it had been a birth-slate. The wanting was exact and went nowhere; a question asked at thirty-three days comes out as a wet sound and is taken for hunger, and hunger is answered with the dish and not with the slate. So he kept the two questions where he kept the others, in the place questions are allowed to last for years, and went on watching the forearms he could not reach.

\scenebreak

“She'll want a thinner slip,” Jorra said to a pot on the bench. He did not look toward the basket; he had not once looked toward it, not on any morning, and the not-looking was as regular as the damper. “The wheel doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.”

The wheel agreed, going over on its steady hands. Jorra put a palm to the pot to feel the shoulder of it, and the boy knew a thing nobody in the shed had told him and that Jorra would not have said if they had: \lore{the full note} was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel. Jorra could hear it in the wet clay while it was still turning, the way a man reads the level of a measure by the sound it makes going into the pot. He would not say so before a kiln-master. A kiln-master would call it early and make something of it, and a thrower who heard a note before anybody tapped it was a useful thing to a house and a dangerous thing in a district. Jorra kept the knowing for the pots. The boy kept his knowing of the knowing where questions can last for years, which was nearly the whole of the room he had.

Once while the wheel turned the boy felt rather than heard a note come up through the boards into the basket --- a pressure in the wicker under his back, the way a vessel's note goes through a table before it goes through the air. He kept it as a pressure and not as a hearing. A sound felt through the floor was not a sound taken under a knuckle; the count was a count of hearings, and the count was five. He had learned to be exact about that difference in a room where being nearly right had once cost a life.

“That wants opening,” he said to the damper, and the bent little finger of his left hand went in ahead of his hand and moved it.

\person{Sella Vail} had got as far as “The fire's ---” before the damper moved. She let the rest of the sentence stand where it was, the way a woman sets a dish down and leaves it, and went to the fire with her own hand. “Feed it before you feed yourself,” she said to the fire. “It'll still be there in the morning. You won't.” It was Garl's saying and she had improved it in the keeping, and she said it to the fire as she said all his sayings, never to a person and never to the basket. She did not take up the silence about the grey; she never had. The damper was the one thing in the shed that got moved before anybody finished a sentence, and the household had long ago stopped finishing them.

\scenebreak

Under the drying racks there was a space where whole slate should have stood. The boy could not see it from where he lay --- the rack's shadow went down to the floor a hand's breadth past his reach and the space was under the far end of it --- but he had learned the yard by the way work went around a thing, and work went around this. A woman setting a pot to dry shifted her weight and set it down two boards over. A man with a barrow of slip took the long way past the end of the rack rather than the short one through it. Nobody swept under there and nobody said why. The door off the shed stood a finger's width open and nobody pushed it wider, and the open dark of it was another thing the yard went around, as a woman goes around Ash left in a corner. The space kept its shape the way a hollow in a wheel-seat keeps the shape of a man who has sat in it for twenty years.

He planted nothing. He had no hand to plant with and no slate to plant and no word he had heard for the thing that had stood there. But he set the shape of the space on the tray beside the grey dust and the two questions and left it there toward a day when the thing might be named, without naming it now, without a slate shown or a man in the back room described. A room could be present in a house only as dust on a sleeve. A broken thing could be present under a rack only as the shape of the work that went around it. He knew the difference between a likeness and a cause; he had made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it for the rest of a life, so he set the dust and the space side by side and did not let them touch.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} swept near the bench. He had come up from \place{the Debt Loft} with the broom and the small knife already back on its sill, and he had told the empty shed where it was, though nobody had asked him and nobody was there to check. Six years of the seven stood counted; the seventh he held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles the colour of the clean dark line where the ridge had turned its corner and been called gone, and no scrubbing lifted it.

“Yes, house,” he said to the ground, though no house had spoken. “I'll sweep it. I swept it this morning. I'll sweep it again.” It was not a joke and there was no invitation to laugh in it; it was a statement of fact made in the tone that asks another person to supply the laugh so that the speaker need not. The boy had known the shape of that voice in a room with better light. Tarn agreed with everything so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot, and the broom went on.

The broom passed the bench. Somewhere in the yard a vessel had given its sound --- or had not; the boy would not have sworn to which --- and Tarn's head did not turn and his eyes did not go to the basket, and he said nothing about what he had or had not heard. The boy did not turn either. Two ears kept the same silence through a third morning, and the house went on turning as though two people holding the same sound were an ordinary thing, which perhaps it was, in a house where the ordinary things were the ones nobody entered in any roll.

A woman from the racks set a pot down on the bench beside the basket. It gave what it gave --- not the flat answer, so the count was safe --- and the boy filed whatever it gave on the tray with the objects that were not hearings, and did not add a sixth to the five. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for what it gave and no word in the other language exact enough either. A pause was not a hearing. A thing that did not happen left a shape, and a shape was not a hearing either. The tray held its old objects, the pot turned toward on the bench that gave nothing, the half-heard note that went past the door, the pot that cracked in the yard and the told bell kept beside but never on the heard bell, and the new note went among them without joining them. The five stood at five from four sources. He had kept the count exactly all morning while the household kept its numbers badly in fists and mouths and weather, and the keeping had bought him nothing he could lift a hand to.

\scenebreak

Sella moved the damper again after Jorra had already moved it, a small settling of the hand on the iron that was neither an order nor an obedience, and did not ask about the grey in the creases or about the room it came from. She had her own way around the room: she went around it as she went around a name, arriving at the edge of it and turning without ever putting a foot in. The keeping was a wall with no door in it. She had built it out of silences and loaves and the space where a fifth breath should be, and she had never asked him to climb it and never left a way down.

At the basket she put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face. To the fire she gave the instruction, not to him. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” The wool and the fire were the two things in the shed that could be told what to do. His head wanted to go everywhere at once and the fold held it in the world of the bench. The drool at his chin froze a little in the draught and nobody wiped it, because a basket set at the edge of a board was a pot set down and left. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the turning wheel and came down.

\place{the Vail kiln-house} kept \person{Garl Vail} as it had kept him all year: the wheel-seat hollow on the left worn by twenty years of a man's leaning, and nobody filled it; the salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door keeping a broader man's shape, and nobody took it down. The proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to the basket. Leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The crack had shown all year in a grey line along the wall and now the line was a clean dark board and the proverb went on being said to the pot, as though a thing left in plain sight could not be made to disappear by a woman with a pail of water. The debt against the firing of tiles lay where it had lain through another turning of the year, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and the house went around the word for it as Sella went around the room off the shed.

The yard went around the back room too. Work moved to the far end of the racks and came back again, and the space under the rack where whole slate should have stood kept its shadow with nobody in it and nobody entering it, and the boy lay there at the edge of the drying racks with the fold at his ear and two states of the same forearms filed in two places he could not point to. Clean slip and grey dust were kept apart as a house keeps what it will not enter in any roll it shows, and the door stood a finger's width open and nobody went in. The wheel went on turning under steady hands. Behind him the fire took its breath in fours and missed one, and the yard went around the space and went around the room, and entered neither.
