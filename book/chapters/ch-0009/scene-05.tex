\daymark{35}{Ilmen 6, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

The yard had come awake by the time the basket was set down, which was a different thing from being awake in it. Bristles moved on boards somewhere down the racks and a woman worked with her hands full, and the morning lifted the grey off the poles strip by strip and laid light along the ground where the pots stood in rows. \person{Corin} was thirty-five days old. He had been carried out under the fold and set at the edge of \place{the drying racks} in \place{the Clay Yard} where the baskets went when the women wanted their hands for other things, a pot set down where a pot would not be slopped. The fold sat at his ear and the two crusts lay where they had been put, one gone past smelling of anything and one that gave a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turned. Cold came in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and he had stopped expecting anybody to do anything about it.

A woman at the racks lifted a pot off the board and set it down again a hand's breadth to the left, to get it out of the way of her own shadow. It was a common motion, made ten thousand times in this yard and in every yard along the row. She did not knock it. She did not listen to it. “That slip's thick at the shoulder,” she said to the pole she was working, and pushed her sleeve higher up her arm, and went on with what she had been doing. A stripe of wet clay lay across one forearm the colour of old bone.

The pot answered her.

It answered under nobody's knuckle, while her hands were already away and her attention back at her work. The answer came up out of the wet clay of its own accord, as a pot will answer when it is set down on a board that knows it --- a small sound in the throat of it, round and going toward full. Almost full. A thumb's breadth of air still in it. It was not the flat answer of a cracked vessel, that dead knock a coin makes on wet boards, heard five times from four sources and filed without a sixth. It was not the silence of pots turned toward on a bench that gave nothing and left the space where a note should stand empty as a door opened into a cold room.

It was a third sound. He lay with his ear under the fold and listened to it the way a man listens in the dark to a thing he has been waiting thirty-five days to hear and did not know he was waiting for.

He knew the difference between nearly and full before he knew that he knew it, the way the body gets there first and the mind comes along afterward to write it down. Full closed; the note went all the way down and stopped, with nothing left over. Nearly left air under the note, a looseness just below the rim where the sound had not settled. It was a note with a want in it. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the want, no word for nearly, and no hand to put on the pot to ask it to say the thing again. He could not have lifted the fold, could not have turned his head toward the woman and said set it down once more. He was thirty-five days old, and the ear that would have been a gift in another house was a thing that merely happened to him here, in a yard where nobody had asked it to hear and nobody would have known what to do with the answer if it had.

There had been a space in his file since Stone 13 --- an interval with the shape of a sound in it, filed on the morning a pot turned toward on a bench and gave nothing, an absence with the shape of a presence and not entered among the hearings. He had carried it through the Five and across the turn of the year, and nothing in the world had offered to match its shape. Now a pot set down out of a woman's shadow had come almost up to it. Not up to it. Almost. He set the two together and did not let them join, because a thing that almost answers is still an answer that has not come, and he had learned in a better-lit room what it cost to make a cause out of a likeness. Advanced, not paid. Not the third thing. He knew enough to keep it on its own tray, and the keeping was its own small temperature, a warmth with no heat in it he could not share.

The surgeon in him wanted the observation written down --- the exact interval, the air left under the note, the whole anatomy in a thumb's breadth between a vessel filled to the brim and one filled just short of it. The infant in him wanted the breast. Both were true and the second was louder, and the second had no words in it. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and here could not lift a hand an inch to tap a pot giving him the first answer anyone had given him in thirty-five days. The joke was as dry as the boards under the racks, and he had learned to find it funny without laughing, which cost less and kept the same blade in it.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was sweeping the flat boards under the far end of the rack. He had been at it since before the light. The broom made its usual dry sound, and then the sound changed. His head went a degree toward the pot where it stood out of the woman's shadow --- a small turn, no more than a man gives a door heard moving in another room, and it came before the broom stopped, so the turning was first and the sweeping followed. Then the broom went on. “I swept it this morning,” he said to the boards. “I'll sweep it again.” He did not look at the basket. He did not call the sound a sound or say what it was he had turned his head toward, and if anyone had asked him he would have said he was sweeping, which he had been, and would be again.

The boy kept the turning beside his own hearing and did not join them. Two ears under one roof had kept the same silence for thirty-five days, and the roof was far off but the silence had come into the yard as a thing carried. Tarn had turned at a flat note past a door before this and said nothing; he had stopped his broom at an empty set-down and said nothing about that either. Now he had turned at a nearly full note in open light and said nothing, and the boy had heard it and said nothing, and the two not-sayings stood in the same place without a word. It was nearer an equality than anything the house had a name for. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said under the roof and lay under it all the same, and he did not drag it out into the yard to stand beside this. He left the turning on its own tray, as he left everything a face refused to report.

\person{Jorra Vail} came out of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger of his left hand going in ahead of the rest of him. He did not come to the pot because of the pot; the morning's work ran that way and the pot stood where the work ran. He looked at it as a thrower looks at a thing off his own wheel --- not at the shape first but at the shoulder, where thick slip cracks in the firing --- and said to it, “You'll do.” He said it flat, with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and then went past the woman toward the damper and had his hand on it before she had finished telling him the fire was low. The bent finger went in first, as it always did.

The boy knew what Jorra knew. The full note was in a Vail pot before it left the wheel; Jorra could hear it in the wet clay under his hands and would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it. He had never said it to him, had never looked toward the basket, and the boy watched the knowledge the way he watched the dust that came and went on the forearms --- kept and unreported, entered nowhere and not to be taken up. He kept it where questions can last for years. He could not ask Jorra to tap the pot, or to say what he heard before the kiln-master came. He could only lie under the fold and listen to a young man tell a pot it would do, in a voice that would not have changed if he had known which ear was listening.

\person{Sella Vail} stood with her hand at the mouth of the kiln and the little-door burn shut on nothing. There had been no opening since the fire came up the yard on Kindling and lay down under its ash; the coals had been woken before light and the pot would go in when ready. She put her thumb to the little clay tag on its cord at her throat --- a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it --- and held it the space of one breath, and let go. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} went back under her shift. She lifted the lid, and the heat came out of the kiln in a breath that smelled of woodsmoke and old firing and swallowed the cold at the door of \place{the Vail kiln-house} as a mouth swallows.

The rule held. She touched the mark before an opening and at no other hour. She had not touched it at the bank the night before, though the coals were going under ash; she had not touched it when \person{Alda Reed} came under the roof with dates, or when the grey was burned in the yard and called dealt with, or on the morning of the Opening of the Slates when there had been no opening at all. She had not touched it for a pot, or for a note, or for a nearly full sound set down in the yard. The hand had gone to the mark for the lid and for nothing else, and it went back to the fire now and told the fire something about the draught, and the mark stayed where it was. The boy watched the sequence as he watched everything in this house --- not as a story but as an order, the same order every time, and the order was the thing kept. A woman who kept a mark for a kiln and a foundling for a house kept the same account in two hands, and neither hand had ever asked him what he thought of it.

The kiln took its breath behind them. Four, and four, and a place where the fifth would stand if there were a fifth, and then the gap, and then four again. The boy had counted it under the roof every day of his life and counted it here with cold at his wrist, and the count did not change because a pot had answered nearly full. The missing breath was its own object. The empty fifth in the kindling firelight was its own object, six days old now and carried up the yard in a dish nobody had paid for out loud. The nearly full note was a third. He set them in three places on a flat board that was not a tray and could not be seen, and did not let the three touch. A likeness was not a cause. He had once put three things into one sentence in a room with better light and spent the rest of his life being sorry for it; here the room was a clay yard and the sentence had not been said at all. He kept the three apart as he kept the told bell beside but never on the heard bell, and the count went on, four, four and a gap, taking the house's heat and leaving the space where the fifth should stand for anybody who wanted to enter it.

\scenebreak

The kiln took the pot when the pot was ready, and the woman at the racks went back to her pole, and the yard went on being the yard with one more sound in it that nobody had entered. The boy lay at the edge of the racks with the fold at his ear and listened to the work going on around him --- bristles, clay, a ladle set down, a voice telling a pot something practical --- and kept the nearly full note where nobody could take it up. It was his and it was not his. He had no more right to it than the woman who had set the pot down, and less use for it, because she had hands and he had none. He had heard a thing nobody else had heard and could not tell anybody what it was, and the telling was not the loss. The loss was the hand to ask the pot to say it again, to learn whether the thumb's breadth of air under the note had been the same on a second answer or had changed while he listened. He wanted that second answer the way he wanted the breast, with the small animal impatience of a body that had not eaten in some hours, and he knew he would not get either.

Sella came for him when the light had gone round far enough to take the shadow off the basket. She lifted him with the little-door burn closing first on nothing and then on his weight, put her other hand under his head because the head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and did not look at his face. She said something to the wool about the river wind. The two crusts went with him in the fold, neither eaten, neither lifted, neither named, and no third had come into it in six days. There was barley in the house and milk, and the smell of the fire came up the yard ahead of them as they went, and the warmth was the house's and not hers and had a payer who was never said. He had been carried this way thirty-five times and the way had not changed and neither had the fold. He lay with his ear against the wool and felt the note go with him, a small round almost that had waited since Stone 13 for a sound to match its shape and had come at last from a pot set down out of a woman's shadow. It had come almost. That was the whole of what it had done. It was not a failure of the full note; it was a note of its own, with a thumb's breadth of air under it and a want in it that was not a crack and not a silence. He filed it on its own tray between the hearings and the intervals, where nobody could take it up and nobody could enter it, and the file went on being the only thing in this house he had ever been allowed to keep exactly.

The kiln was breathing behind him when they came under the roof. Four, and four, and a gap. The clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} had turned the corner for a year and two days went along the skirting board and held nothing and was not swept; \person{Garl Vail}'s coat kept its salt shape on its peg and the hollow seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years, and the proverb went to the pot and the fire and not to the basket. Sella put the fold right at his ear without looking at the face under it and set him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft} above the wheel, and went back to the fire. He lay with a sound nobody else had heard filed where nobody could take it up, the five hearings of the flat note standing at five from four sources and no sixth among them, the count going on into a year the district had entered and had not entered him. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down.
