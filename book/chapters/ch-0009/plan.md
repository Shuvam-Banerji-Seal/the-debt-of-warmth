## Scene 1 — The Water Tastes Different

- **Day and time:** Day 31, Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813, Secondday, before first light at the yard well into the ordinary morning.
- **Place:** The yard well and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln; the Vail kiln-house and the Slip Brook behind the racks. The Ilm spoken of, not reached.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-one days old, carried in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Mira Silt (brief, come up from the eels), a woman at the well who lets the bucket down and a man come up from the Ilm (walk-ons); Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Open on the well, not on a thought and not on a recap: a woman lets the bucket down and says the water tastes different. A man come up from the Ilm says the ice is honeyed under the mud. Nobody says the word for what is coming, and the boy notes that a thing everybody can hear in the water has no name anyone will give it (T085, T083). Do not write the word *the Breaking* in narration.
  2. The small physical signs arrive in a row: the oiled paper at the Vail window no longer lifts quite so hard; the Slip Brook behind the racks has lost its stiff skin and moves under a thin grey film; the yard calls it a warm spell and then the weather, the word that lets a person go home without owning a day of it (T085, T065).
  3. Sella draws water and gives the instruction to the bucket and the wool, not to the basket; she says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, and does not say where the warmth in the household dish came from. Her little-door burn closes first on the rope and then on nothing (T060, T058).
  4. Mira comes up from the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in her neck and her head held to the right against the cold where the top of her left ear had been. Asked whether the eels run early, she answers with a question and then her own answer. When a figure is spoken at the well her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays while she decides; shifting the basket's weight shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never taken upriver to Nine Pots and never lifted at a door. Neither is counted (T025, T059, T077).
  5. The boy is thirty-one days old and a month old tomorrow. He is carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, with the fold at his chin and the two crusts in it --- one stiff since Stone 15, one still giving a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns --- neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third given (T086, T060).
  6. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the swinging bucket and comes down. The surgeon in him reads the shape of the draw --- head, rope, the give of the full bucket --- and the infant body cannot reach the water he can already hear changing (T071/T072, T085).
  7. Garl's coat keeps its salt shape on its peg and his seat its hollow on the left; the ridge under the skirting board is a clean dark line where the grey was burned and called dealt with, and the proverb goes to the pot as though a thing left in plain sight could not be made to disappear by a woman with a pail of water (T009, T013).

- **Emotional turn:** A whole district can taste the change in the water and will not say the word for it; the boy, who has no word either, is the only one keeping the exact shape of what is not being said. The month turns under a fold he cannot lift.
- **Sensory anchors:** cold water coming up over the stone lip of the well; the small sour taste of it spoken aloud; honeyed ice under mud; oiled paper that has stopped lifting hard; thin grey film on the Slip Brook; eel slime and river silt on Mira's sleeves; an eel-bone flicked and put away; grey wool cord under an apron with a second cord twisted twice beneath it; tallow, wet wool, woodsmoke; the two crusts in the fold.
- **Threads advanced:** T085 and T083 (thaw in small things, hairline carried into it), T077 (a figure spoken and not given), T025 and T059 (Mira's two cords, knots uncounted), T060 and T086 (warmth entered nowhere, two crusts, no third), T071/T072 (bucket's draw filed in the private count), T009 and T013 (coat, hollow seat, ridge now a clean line), T058, T065.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the honeyed ice and the hairline toward the Breaking of Silt; plants the number nobody gives toward the true figure; plants Mira's two cords toward the knot-string read years hence; plants the rising brook toward the day it takes Dessa. Pays nothing; joins nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The bucket coming up cold over the well's stone lip, the water tasting different to everyone but nobody saying the word, and the boy's hand outside the wool going up an inch toward it and coming down.

## Scene 2 — One Year of Seven

- **Day and time:** Day 32, Ilmen 3, Y.H. 813, Thirdday, before light under the Vail roof and through the yard. The boy is a month old today.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the bench and the fire, the Clay Yard at the place where the broom checks.
- **Present:** Corin (one month old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The boy is a month old. Nothing in the house marks it: no number is spoken, no loaf set apart for it, and the district has already entered a year on its slates that does not contain him (T071/T072, T077).
  2. Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the empty yard where it is, unasked: *I put it back. You can check.* Six years of the seven are counted; the last year is held back and will not be counted, the one entry in the house the house cannot check (T036).
  3. He agrees with everything so thoroughly that there is nowhere for an argument to go --- with Sella about the fire, with Jorra about the damper, with the ground about the sweeping --- and the boy remembers a man who did the same in a room with better light and a file in his voice (T047, T036).
  4. The boy's hand outside the fold goes up an inch toward the broom going past and comes down. Tarn's broom checks half a stroke at the place by the bench where no note has come, and he does not turn his head or name it. The two silences under one roof keep their equality, which neither man has a word for (T076, T082, T061).
  5. Sella feeds him barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and two fingers behind his ear holding his head where it wants to go, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*. She gives the instruction to the fire and the wool --- *keep it off his ears; it's the river wind* --- and does not wipe his mouth or say who paid for the warmth in the dish (T060, T058, T044).
  6. Ash is worked into the creases of Tarn's knuckles that no scrubbing lifts; he scrapes at it with the knife and the grey comes up the colour of the ridge the neighbour called gone. He still wants a late thaw, because a late thaw puts off the paying, and now the thaw is coming on anyway (T036, T085, T013).
  7. Garl's proverb goes to the pot and the fire and never to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The crack has been showing all year in a grey line along the wall, and now the line is a clean dark board and the proverb goes on being said (T009, T013).
  8. At the bank she turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the mark, because a bank is not an opening. The fire behind the boy takes its breath in fours and misses one (T079, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** A man with one year of his term left will not count it, and the boy who is a month old today has no entry anywhere either; the two of them keep their accounts in the same refused place under one roof, without a word passing between them.
- **Sensory anchors:** the small knife set on its sill in the half-light; Ash the colour of the burned ridge in a clay-hand's knuckles; a broom checking half a stroke at an empty set-down; barley gruel thinned with milk and a spoon wiped on a sleeve; two fingers behind an ear holding a head where it wants to go; the little-door burn closing on the rope and then on nothing; the kiln's four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T036 (six counted, seventh held), T076 and T061 (two ears, two silences, no sixth hearing), T082 (the empty set-down kept as an interval), T060 and T058 (unentered warmth, instruction given to the wool and the fire), T044 (pocket-place and the unanswerable gift), T009 and T013 (coat, seat, proverb, ridge now a clean line), T085 (late thaw wanted and coming), T079, T065, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants Tarn's kept seventh year toward his later refusal of a whole return; plants the two silences toward the loan that can hear becoming a loan that can be called; plants the late-thaw wanting toward the levy in the thaw. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof a month old and unentered, the knife back on its sill, the fire behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the broom and coming down.

## Scene 3 — The Back Room

- **Day and time:** Day 33, Ilmen 4, Y.H. 813, Fourthday, morning at the Vail wheel-shed and the drying racks, into the afternoon.
- **Place:** The Vail wheel-shed, the back room off it (not entered), the drying racks outside, the bench where a pot is set down.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-one days old, carried in and set at the edge of the racks), Jorra Vail, Tarn, Sella Vail; the men whose names are not spoken (offstage, unnamed); Garl Vail as hollow seat and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Jorra comes in from the back room with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of the little finger --- not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel --- and says nothing about where he has been. The men whose names are not spoken are not named (T035).
  2. He tells a pot it will want a thinner slip, tells the wheel it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady, and goes to the damper before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low, the bent finger going in ahead of his hand. He knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it (T035, T061).
  3. The boy keeps the forearms with grey dust and the forearms with clean slip on separate trays in the private file, as he keeps everything a face refuses to report. He wants to know which slate was broken and whether it was a birth-slate, and cannot ask (T035, T031, T071/T072).
  4. Under the drying rack is the space where whole slate should have stood, which the yard goes around without entering. The seeds of the broken birth-slates are planted there toward the day they are named, without being called by that name now, without a slate shown or a man in the back room described (T035, T031).
  5. Tarn sweeps near the bench and does not say what he heard; his head does not turn and he does not look toward the basket. The boy does not turn either. Two ears keep the same silence through a third morning (T076, T036).
  6. A pot is set down on the bench and answers what it answers; the boy files whatever it gives with the objects already on the tray and does not add a sixth to the five hearings from four sources (T082, T061).
  7. Sella moves the damper after Jorra has already moved it and does not ask about the dust or the room. She puts the fold right at the boy's ear without looking at his face and gives the instruction to the fire; the keeping stays a wall with no door in it (T058, T060).
  8. Garl's seat keeps its hollow on the left; nobody fills it, and the coat keeps its salt shape on its peg while the yard goes around the back room the way Sella goes around a name (T009, T013).

- **Emotional turn:** The house keeps a room it does not enter in any roll it shows, and the boy learns that a room can be present in a house only as dust on a sleeve. The third ear in the yard says nothing about what it hears, and the house goes on turning as though two people keeping the same sound were an ordinary thing.
- **Sensory anchors:** fine grey slate dust in the creases past a bent little finger; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone on other mornings; the wheel turning under steady hands; a damper moved before a sentence is finished; the open dark of a room not entered; the bench's full note or its flat answer, unnamed; wet clay, tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a bad wind.
- **Threads advanced:** T035 (dust, back room, broken birth-slates planted), T031 (space under the racks), T076 and T036 (two ears and two silences, knife unsaid), T061 and T082 (no sixth hearing, intervals kept apart), T058 (the wall with no door), T009 and T013 (hollow seat, coat, proverb), T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the broken birth-slates under the drying rack toward the day Jorra hands a boy's name to men who burn slates; plants Jorra's knowledge of the full note toward the kiln-masters' discovery; plants the dust toward the unnameable room. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy set at the edge of the drying racks with the fold at his ear, clean slip and grey dust filed in two places he cannot point to, and the space under the rack where whole slate should have stood going around by everybody and entered by nobody.

## Scene 4 — Dates Where Wonder Should Stand

- **Day and time:** Day 34, Ilmen 5, Y.H. 813, Fifthday, mid-morning under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** The room under the Vail roof; the bench where the birth-slates are laid writing-down; the fold at the boy's shoulder.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-one days old), Alda Reed, Sella Vail; Dessa Silt offstage three doors down, alive, her cough weather; Garl Vail as the name not spoken under this roof.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Alda comes smelling of soap and warm milk, sets the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, and reaches for the slate before the child in the order the boy has learned to read. Where a question should stand she gives an exact date (T007/T008, T023).
  2. She presses the fold flat at his shoulder too long, touches the little tin once inside her bodice and comes away without opening it, and crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower at his wrist before she knows she has done it in front of him. She covers the sign with the same unasked-for date she gave Dessa --- Stone seven, the account up on the eighth, before the paper went out --- and goes on with dates after the question has stopped (T007, T001, T008).
  3. She takes up a narrow slate and writes what she will not leave lying, in the second hand no Court physician has seen, then turns it writing-down into her shawl toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name. A fourth hidden note goes with the others into the tin, still unread (T023).
  4. Sella stands a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing and does not ask what was written or why; the arrangement between the two women holds without a word passing, a door in it that neither opens (T058).
  5. The boy can read none of the writing, cannot ask which house holds the floorboard and cannot hand the gesture back. He keeps the sign and the date laid over it beside the turned slate, as he keeps the told bell beside the heard bell in two places, and writes no cause where he has only noticed a likeness (T082, T071/T072).
  6. To Sella's *he lies quiet* she says *Warm and breathing --- that's all I've set down*, which is exact and not true. Three doors down a cough goes up from the pits and is called weather; the lie told for mercy is still a fact in a woman's face (T008, T073).
  7. Under this roof Garl's name is not spoken; Alda lays a date where a name should stand and the house goes around the word as Sella goes around Ash in a corner. The boy files the silence with the other gaps, not with the hearings (T073, T082, T009).

- **Emotional turn:** A kind woman writes down what she cannot say and covers what she cannot write with a date nobody asked for; the boy sees both gestures and can no more hand them back than he can lift a hand to the slate. Exactness is the shape a lie takes when it is told for mercy.
- **Sensory anchors:** soap and warm milk; birth-slates laid writing-down on the bench; a little tin touched once inside a bodice and not opened; two fingers at a red edge; the cooper's seventh in Mud End and a potter's third child in Stonemouth arriving as dates after the question stopped; a narrow slate turned face-down into a shawl; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the smell of another house's cough spoken of as weather.
- **Threads advanced:** T023 (fourth hidden note, tin unopened, floorboard unshown), T007/T008 (unaware sign, exact unasked date, lie standing), T001 (birth-flower crossed), T073 (name left out, date in its place), T058 (half-pace and turned slate), T082 and T071/T072 (sign and date filed apart, no cause), T060, T009.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the fourth note toward the tin read years after Alda is gone; plants the unaware sign and its covering date toward the Second Want record; plants the exact lie toward Dessa's death tomorrow and the day Jorra uses her name as a weapon. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The child under the Vail roof with a date laid where a name should stand, the tin shut inside a woman's bodice and a fourth note turned face-down toward a floorboard he cannot name, the two crusts where they were put.

## Scene 5 — Nearly Full

- **Day and time:** Day 35, Ilmen 6, Y.H. 813, Sixthday, morning among the drying racks in the Clay Yard, into the opening of the kiln.
- **Place:** The drying racks and the Clay Yard; the Vail kiln-house fire and the bench.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-one days old, set at the edge of the racks), Jorra Vail, Tarn, Sella Vail, a woman at the racks who sets down the pot (walk-on); Garl Vail as hollow seat and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. A woman at the racks sets a pot down and it answers nearly full under nobody's knuckle. She does not tap it and does not hear it; she says something practical about the slip and goes on with her work (T082, T061).
  2. The boy hears the difference between nearly and full. It is neither the flat answer of the five hearings from four sources nor the silence of the pots turned toward on a bench that gave nothing. He files it as a new object: an answer to the interval filed on Stone 13, which has waited all this time for a sound in the world to match its shape (T082, T061, T071/T072). It is advanced, not paid --- it is not the third thing, and he knows enough now to keep it on its own tray rather than make a cause of it.
  3. He has no hand to tap the pot and no Grathic word for nearly; he cannot ask the woman to set it down again or tell anyone what he heard. The ear that would be a gift in another house is a thing that happens to him in a yard where nobody asks it to (T082, T061).
  4. Tarn's head goes a degree toward the pot as it is set down and he goes back to his broom without naming the sound or looking at the basket. The boy keeps this turning beside his own hearing without joining them; two ears and two silences keep their usual shape (T076, T036).
  5. Jorra tells the pot it will do and moves the damper before Sella has finished; the full note is in a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel, which he will not say before a kiln-master. The boy knows he knows it and keeps that where questions can last for years (T035, T061).
  6. Sella touches the widow's mark before she opens the kiln and at no other hour, holds it the space of one breath and lets go; at no point in the morning does the hand go to the mark for a pot, a note or a nearly full sound (T079).
  7. The kiln takes its breath in fours and misses one; the boy sets the nearly full note beside the missing breath and the empty fifth in the kindling firelight, and still does not let the three touch (T065, T071/T072, T082).
  8. The two crusts lie in the fold, neither eaten nor named; the boy is carried back under the roof with the new sound filed where nobody can take it up (T086, T060).

- **Emotional turn:** For the first time something in the world answers the silence the boy has been keeping since Stone 13, and the answer is almost --- nearly full, never quite. The ear that has been useless for thirty-five days is given one exact thing to hold and no hand to hold it with, and the boy learns that almost is a note of its own and not a failure of the full one.
- **Sensory anchors:** a pot set down on a bench answering nearly full under nobody's knuckle; the flat board of the yard under the drying racks; slip the colour of old bone on a thrower's forearms; a head turning a degree and a broom going on; the widow's mark under a thumb before the lid and at no other hour; the kiln's four, four and a gap; the two crusts in the fold.
- **Threads advanced:** T082 (first earthly answer to the Stone 13 interval, advanced not paid), T061 (no sixth flat hearing; nearly is a different note), T076 and T036 (Tarn's head, unsaid), T035 and T061 (Jorra's known full note), T079 (mark before the opening only), T065, T086, T060, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the nearly-full note toward the third thing --- a vessel that holds nearly full and never quite --- which belongs far off; plants Tarn's turned head toward the day the ear makes him valuable and unsafe. Pays nothing; T082 is advanced, not closed.
- **Exact last image:** The boy carried back under the Vail roof with a sound nobody else heard filed on its own tray between the flat hearings and the intervals, the kiln behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Scene 6 — The Hairline in the Ice

- **Day and time:** Day 36, Ilmen 7, Y.H. 813, Seventhday, at the yard well and the edge of the Ilm below the sill.
- **Place:** The yard well, the Clay Yard, the edge of the Ilm under its ice; Slate Row spoken of as a direction, not entered.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-one days old, carried), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Berr Clay (one short appearance), a man come back from looking at the ice and neighbours who argue the thaw (walk-ons); Garl Vail as coat and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The ice on the Ilm shows a hairline crack, carried forward into the thaw from the shard with the dark hair that ran in from one edge and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the other. The boy sets this hairline beside the missing step at the Fishstairs and the gap in the told bell and keeps the three in separate places --- a likeness is still not a cause (T085, T083, T078, T071/T072).
  2. A man come back from looking at it has a number shut in his fist with the thumb tucked under; he says it has come a hair further and thinks better of the rest. Sella answers with the fire and the weather, and the number goes around the yard as a shape that nobody gives out (T077, T085).
  3. Berr Clay is glimpsed coming back from the river with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, passes a finger over a figure on no slate the boy can read, has a number he does not care to give, and goes off toward his house with the box still locked. He looks nowhere near the basket (T041, T077, T015, T047).
  4. The Singing Sill gives its low note under the water a little earlier than it should and the yard calls it weather. A woman come up from High Kiln says the thaw will come early; the neighbours who want it late contradict her, because a late thaw puts off the levy and a late thaw gives a body its weeks. Both sides end in the word that lets them go home without owning the day (T085, T077, T065).
  5. One in twelve still stands printed where it stood last year and nobody challenges it; the true subtraction that would put a figure on what a house has lost is spoken by nobody and goes past the basket shut and unopenable. The boy cannot spend, correct or hand back a figure he cannot read (T015, T077).
  6. Tarn agrees with everybody so thoroughly that the argument has nowhere to go and keeps his seventh year held back; he still wants the late thaw because a late thaw puts off the paying, and now the ice itself is going against him (T036, T085).
  7. The boy lies with the fold at his chin and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the water he cannot reach and coming down. He has kept the count exactly all seven days while the household has kept its number badly in fists and mouths and weather (T071/T072, T085).
  8. Under the Vail roof at dusk the coals go under blind ash and the mark is not touched, because a bank is not an opening. The carried fire from Kindling is still in the house, under ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four (T079, T088, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** The river is breaking in plain sight and the whole district's answer is a word that lets it go home; the boy sees a cracked vessel with an enormous capacity and a slow failure and has no name for it and no hand to point. Even the man who could put a figure on the days will not give one, and the number goes around as an absence.
- **Sensory anchors:** a hairline in grey ice held up to the tallow light; cold coming off a man's boots; a fist shut with the thumb tucked under; red cord folded under a coat and green at the corner of a mouth; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; the Singing Sill's low note coming up through the boards; the printed figure nobody challenges; a banked fire missing one breath in four.

## Scene 7 — Nineteen Steps into the Thumb

- **Day and time:** Day 37, Ilmen 8, Y.H. 813, Eighthday, morning to dusk at House Silt three doors down the Clay Yard and along the Slip Brook.
- **Place:** House Silt, the doorway at the end of nineteen steps, the Slip Brook behind the drying racks; the Vail kiln-house at night (reached at the close).
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-seven days old, carried in the basket), Dessa Silt, Mira Silt (brief), Sella Vail; Alda Reed offstage; Garl Vail as coat and proverb. The collector's clerk at the Fishstairs is not present and is named by nobody.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Dessa stands at the end of nineteen steps with slip-grey nails and wet clay dried at her hem, her shoulder against the jamb as if the jamb were the only thing holding her up. The cough comes up out of the pits in a low short way and she puts her hand over her mouth; when it passes she says it is weather --- *It's the pits. Comes up in the cold. Weather* --- to the ground. It is worse than it was on Stone 28 and she calls it the same thing (T008, T073, T085).
  2. Because a truth has come near she gets specific: nineteen out and nineteen back, four loaves on the seventh of Stone in eight hundred and twelve, three to the levy and one eaten with her feet up, and bits to the woman who came with a basket. Her thumb goes once over the pale sieve-wire scar up the inside of her left forearm and the door-shut smile comes down over her face (T008, T073, T060).
  3. The nineteen steps go out of her feet and into her thumb, counted there because the count keeps a larger thing out of the room. The boy reads the pulse under the smile and the hand over the mouth and the way the shoulder holds the jamb up --- a vessel going thin that the whole district has entered nowhere --- and cannot name what he sees in her face (T073, T071/T072, T060).
  4. She does not look into the basket going past her shade and does not say the number or the name. She believes him spent among children she has never seen, and the belief has the weight of a fact because she has laid it down so often (T008, T073).
  5. Mira comes up from the eels with silt in her neck and passes her sister without looking at her either; a crust is not given and no third comes into the fold. Two not-lookings of the same shape are opposite things and the boy keeps them apart (T059, T025, T086, T060, T071/T072).
  6. The Slip Brook behind the racks runs a little higher than it should and clay-heavy, under a thin film that no longer holds. The boy files it beside the honeyed ice and the hairline, still three objects on three places, and does not make a cause of them (T085, T083, T082).
  7. As Sella lifts the basket past the doorway, the shape of Dessa's hand on the handle --- thumb along the wicker, the small quick grip of a woman who has carried clay and children --- reaches for something in the old register: a hand in a grey sleeve going into a coat pocket for a small cold rectangle with a name on it, a sentence about a boat on cold water nine promises old. It comes in past tense, untagged, and the name is not looked at. Nothing crosses back that a baby's hand can carry (T044, T004, T060).
  8. The sixth hearing of the flat note and the paying of the hand-on-the-basket detail belong to tomorrow, when the woman goes into the clay-heavy rise of the brook uncollected and the boy is held on the bank. Hold both. Tonight she is alive, the cough is weather, and the boy is carried up the yard under the Vail roof with the two crusts still in the fold (T087, T008, T086, T071/T072).

- **Emotional turn:** The woman who believes her child dead is failing in front of him and calls it weather, and the boy has an surgeon's knowledge of exactly how a vessel goes thin and a baby's hand that cannot reach the sleeve. The old life's unsent sentence arrives as the shape of a grip on a basket handle and is not answered; the chapter closes with the debt of a woman who could not be thanked becoming another account the boy cannot settle.
- **Sensory anchors:** slip-grey nails and wet clay dried at a hem; a hand over a mouth and a cough that goes on too short; the pale sieve-wire scar under a thumb; the door-shut smile coming down; nineteen counted into a thumb; eel slime and silt on Mira's sleeves; the Slip Brook running clay-heavy a little too high; a thumb along a wicker basket handle; the two crusts in the fold.
- **Threads advanced:** T008 and T073 (lie standing, name and number unsaid, cough called weather), T060 (love as maintenance and the unanswerable gift), T071/T072 (nineteen into the thumb, three objects kept apart), T044 and T004 (pocket-place, boat promise, name unlooked-at, past tense untagged), T085 and T083 (brook higher, hairline toward the thaw), T059 and T025 (Mira's two cords, two not-lookings), T086 (no third crust), T087 (held toward Day 38).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants Dessa's going into the brook tomorrow, the small flat knock from a person, the hand on the basket handle the boy will remember, Alda's arrival after the vessel has given, and the collector's clerk at the Fishstairs --- all for Chapter 8's promised payoff on Day 38 (T061/T087), not paid here. Plants the unsent call's shape toward the last account carried rather than paid. Pays nothing; nothing joined.
- **Exact last image:** The boy carried up the Clay Yard under the Vail roof at dusk, thirty-seven days old, with the two crusts in the fold, the Slip Brook running clay-heavy a little higher than it should behind him, the count four, four and a gap going on into a year the district has entered and has not entered him, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Pull into Chapter 10

Day 37 closes with Dessa alive but visibly going thin, her cough still called weather, the number and the name still unsaid, and the Slip Brook running clay-heavy a little higher than it should. The boy has heard a pot answer nearly full for the first time, filed a fourth hidden note and a new hairline in the ice, and learned that almost is its own kind of note; nothing has been joined, paid or explained, and five hearings of the flat note still stand at five from four sources.

Chapter 10 opens on **Day 38, Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813, Ninthday**: Dessa drowns in the clay-heavy rise of the Slip Brook, uncollected. The boy, thirty-eight days old, is held on the bank and hears the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through --- the **sixth hearing and the second from a person**, the payoff of the plant from Stone 25. The collector's clerk is at the Fishstairs; Alda arrives after the vessel has given. He cannot speak, cannot move, cannot look away, and will remember the exact shape of her hand on the basket handle. No visit to the bank becomes a quest. Forward, not closed.
