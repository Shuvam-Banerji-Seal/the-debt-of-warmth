## Chronicle

On Ilmen 9 the clay came up behind the drying racks in the night and took Dessa Silt from the end of nineteen steps. The yard lifted her out with their hands and no words, and the boy, thirty-eight days old, held on the bank, could not move or look away. As the men lifted, a small flat knock came out of the body --- the sixth hearing of the full note, the second from a person, beside the five from four sources and never among them. A hand in a grey sleeve went into a coat pocket and came back with wool; nothing crossed into the basket. Alda Reed came after the vessel had given and went to her slate before the body, laying dates where a wonder should have stood, and a clerk was set at the Fishstairs for an uncalled measure.

On Ilmen 10 the market went on as markets do. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve as the notice says, and put himself out of his own sentence while the true subtraction went over the basket; the number for the thaw went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and was never given. On Ilmen 11 no collector came for the woman in the brook, and nobody named it. Sella fed him from the house's own dish, Tarn set his knife on the sill with six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back, and the clean dark line where the Ash had turned was measured by a thumb and left.

On Ilmen 12 Mira Silt came up from the eels, answered a question with her own answer and showed a second cord twisted twice beneath the grey, the knots on neither counted; she brought no crust. On Ilmen 13 Sella carried the basket three doors down, and the boy saw, from the low angle of the basket, a tin above the empty bed with a pale fold at its seam and a thumb's bright rub on the lid. He did not know what it held and entered it nowhere.

On Ilmen 14 the blessing went upriver. A woman with green at one corner of her mouth looked at pots and was gone, unnamed; Orra Weir kept her accounts in knots of grey wool and would not sit where a slate lay open. On Ilmen 15 the hearth-maids blessed the traps on the flat stone and the eels ran as they always ran. Orra tied a forty-first knot she could not account for and said the word forty low, into the basket, and watched what the boy would do with it. He did nothing; he kept the shape of the knot and the word on separate trays. At dusk Sella carried him up the Clay Yard and set him on his bed, forty-four days old, the two crusts still in the fold, uneaten, unnamed, and the fire behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** The boy lies on his bed under the Vail roof, forty-four days old, the fold at his ear and the two crusts in it, carried home at dusk from the Fish Blessing at the Weir. Sella set him on the bed, not in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right without looking at his face. Behind him the carried fire keeps under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the clean dark line where the Ash turned its corner is unswept; his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down. Nothing has been joined, explained or ended.
- **Day and time at the end.** Day 44, Ilmen 15, Y.H. 813, Fifthday, from the blessing at the stone through the afternoon and the carrying-home at dusk. Chapter 11 opens Day 45, Ilmen 16, Sixthday, at the Fishstairs.
- **Seven lived days, one dated entry each.** Day 38 Ilmen 9, Day 39 Ilmen 10, Day 40 Ilmen 11, Day 41 Ilmen 12, Day 42 Ilmen 13, Day 43 Ilmen 14, Day 44 Ilmen 15. No day poured into another, no recap, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no rank change. The old life surfaces once, on Day 38, in past tense and untagged: the hand in the grey sleeve going into a coat pocket for a small cold rectangle of light with a name on it, the boat on cold water nine promises old not said, the name not looked at, the hand coming back with wool. No present-tense break.
- **Where everyone is now.** Corin: on his bed under the roof, carried everywhere, mute, drool unwiped, cold at cheek–temple–wrist, hand up an inch and down. Sella Vail: at the fire and the bed, having carried him home from the Weir; retrieved a clay jar from House Silt on Day 42 and set it on the shelf by the wall; still says *the house* where another woman says *I*, gives every instruction to the fire, the pot and the wool, touches the widow's mark only before an opening, keeps her private roll unshown, and has named neither the payer of the warmth nor the woman in the brook. Tarn: knife back on its sill and announced to the empty yard each morning, six of the seven counted and the seventh held back, Ash in the knuckles no scrubbing lifts; on Day 40 his broom went over the road to the Fishstairs without turning and he noticed that he had noticed; he still wants the thaw late because a late thaw puts off the paying. Jorra Vail: in and out of the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip and no grey dust on Day 40, moving the damper before Sella finishes; still wants the foundling outside the name Vail and has not once looked toward the bed. Alda Reed: came after the vessel had given on Day 38, reached for her slate before the person and laid dates where a wonder should stand, tin touched once inside her bodice and not opened; at the Weir on Day 43 she and Orra let the number and the third day change into weather; offstage at the close. Berr Clay: at the Cross on Day 39, thumb wiped twice, one in twelve read as the notice says, speaker taken out of his own sentence, green of glaze at one corner of his mouth, box still locked; not at the Weir. Mira Silt: at the Fishstairs on Day 41 and the Weir on Days 43–44, two cords under her apron with the knots on neither counted, no crust brought, no look into the basket; still has not said she knows the child. Dessa Silt: dead since Day 38 in the clay-heavy brook, uncollected, and stays dead; her number stayed in her thumb and her name under the sieve-wire scar, and the mercy told her became a fact nobody alive can correct. Orra Weir: onstage from Day 43, rope-handed, eel-scarred, smelling of silt and tallow, accounts in knots of grey wool she will not set beside an open slate; tied a forty-first knot on Day 44, said the word forty low into the basket and watched. Garl Vail: absent, his coat, hollow seat, proverbs and clean dark line still arranging the house. Orra's cord of knots now hangs in the Eel House; the collector's clerk at the Fishstairs is filed apart, not Marra and not the grey-fingered Drawing collector.
- **Items gained, lost or used.** Gained: the sixth hearing of the full note, from a person, filed beside the five and not among them; the paid detail of the thumb on the wicker, kept where things with no hour are kept; the pewter tin above the empty bed in House Silt, with its pale fold and thumb-rubbed lid, seen but not opened; the clay jar Sella fetched from House Silt and set on the shelf; Orra's knot-string with its forty-first knot and the shape of the double turn tied left over right; the word forty and the weight of the thumb that set the knot; the green at a mouth and the looking (Berr on Day 39, the unnamed pot-looker on Day 43), filed apart; the weak board in the plank walk and the Weir's early low note, filed as gaps. Lost: Dessa; a woman is dead and stays dead. Unchanged: both crusts in the fold (one stiff past smelling since Stone 15, one still giving a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns), neither eaten, lifted or named, no third; the small pale river stone on the abandoned bed in House Silt, unmentioned; the bench knife on its sill; the charred slate in the kiln ash; basket and wool lent without a word; Alda's tin shut in her bodice and her fourth note toward a floorboard; Sella's roll unshown; Berr's box locked; Mira's two cords with knots uncounted; the clean dark line left where it lies; the carried fire under blind ash.
- **Money and figures.** No money reaches the boy. One in twelve stays printed and unchallenged and the true subtraction goes over the basket unspoken; the thaw's cost goes from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and is never given; the levy and bits and the figure in Dessa's thumb went into the water with her; Mira sells six eels for five marks or five for six and makes nothing of it; a number goes around the Weir from mouth to shoulder and changes into weather. The word forty is spoken once, low, and watched, and he does not know it is a count of children or that he is one of them. Every figure goes past him unspendable, uncorrectable, unreturnable.
- **Powers and cost.** No usable power, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. Cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift. The ear now holds six hearings: five from four sources (a cup across the birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at the Cross, a man giving at the Drawing) and the sixth from a person, the small flat knock out of Dessa as the men lifted; not a seventh, not joined, the nearly full note of Day 35 still on its own tray, advanced and not paid. The word forty is filed as a saying, not a sound of a vessel, and does not go near the six. The surgeon's register notes the knot's shape and the weight of a thumb and can spend neither. No Grathic word for the flat note or for nearly, and now no word of his own for the thing Orra put in the cord.
- **Facts, lies, promises, debts.** Dessa is dead and nobody calls the omission; no collector came and no word was found for what had not been done --- the first edge of the later heresy, that warmth may be left where it fell and the morning still comes. The lie told for mercy stands as a fact nobody can correct. Berr takes the speaker out of his own sentence and sends a house to a slate at the watch; Mira answers a question with her own answer; Orra ties a knot when she lies and gives a date where an opinion should stand; the blessing has a number tied to it that nobody gives out; a kind of keeping enters nothing a Court could read. Garl's debt against the firing of tiles stays neither paid nor settled nor done with. No new promise is made; the boat on cold water is not said, the name in the pocket is not looked at, and the hand comes back with wool.

- **Foreshadowing planted.** The tin above the bed toward the convergence of private accounts no assessor can read; Orra's knot-string, the knot tied over a story of a small warm bundle in the year of the flower, and the watched reaction toward the day the cord is read and the long vigil it begins; the green-mouthed silhouette toward the ear that will be spoken of and the lead-glaze answer; the weak board in the plank walk toward the Silt flood; the word forty and the withheld number toward the deduction and the whole return; the two crusts toward the day one is gone and nobody says who took it; the uncollected woman and the unremarked omission toward the later refusal to collect; the clean dark line left where it lies toward the keeping of a thing gone. Pays the sixth hearing and the hand-on-the-basket detail; joins nothing and explains nothing.
- **The next chapter must not forget.** Day 45, Ilmen 16, Sixthday, opens at the Fishstairs after the Blessing: the forty-first knot already tied and nobody able to say what it counts; the boy carried below the north bridge at dawn; a brine-gull going off with a coal not yet cold; eel-bones moving from hand to hand while Mira decides whether to lie; a crust will go missing and nobody will say who took it. The cord is readable in shape if not in name, the two cords stay side by side and unjoined. Dessa stays dead; no collector was called and the omission stays unnamed; nothing has been joined, paid or explained except a sound, a shape and a word. No visit to a bank becomes a quest; no recognition of the mother, no name from her, no number from her thumb, no one in three, no Marra, no explanation of the Forty, no ending. Keep the count six from four sources and one from a person, four, four and a gap, and forward momentum.
