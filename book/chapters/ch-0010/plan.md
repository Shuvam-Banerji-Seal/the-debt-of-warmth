## Scene 1 — The Sixth Hearing

- **Day and time:** Day 38, Ilmen 9, Y.H. 813, Ninthday, before first light at the Slip Brook behind the drying racks, through the morning and the carrying-back at dusk.
- **Place:** The Slip Brook behind the drying racks in the Clay Yard; House Silt three doors down (the tin's room kept for Day 42); the Fishstairs below the north bridge, reached later in the morning; the Vail kiln-house at night.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-eight days old, held in the basket), Dessa Silt (dead from the opening; dies in the first pages and stays dead), Sella Vail, Alda Reed (arrives after the vessel has given), Mira Silt (brief, at the brook's edge, no crust), the collector's clerk at the Fishstairs (walk-on, not Marra, no dialogue to the basket); rack-women and men lifting (walk-ons). Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and proverb. Tarn and Jorra can be kept offstage or given one silent yard-beat each; keep them to the background.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Open on the bank, not on a thought and not on a recap: the clay-heavy rise has come up in the night and taken the woman who stood at the end of nineteen steps. Wet clay, silt, the thin film gone, water going over more water. The boy, thirty-eight days old, is held on the bank in the basket with the fold at his chin and cannot speak, cannot move, cannot look away (T085, T071/T072).
  2. He hears the small flat knock of a body cracked all the way through --- the **sixth hearing of the full note and the second from a person**, the payoff of the plant from Stone 25. Write the sound plainly and file it without explaining it: it goes on the tray with the five from four sources, not among them, not a seventh, not an answer (T061, T087, T082, T071/T072).
  3. The lifting and the trying are brief, clumsy, undignified: hands under a body heavy with water, an arm that will not do what an arm should, the interval of dragging and breathing and wiping clay off a face that will not be wiped clean. Plain clauses; no choreography, no elegance. The boy reads what a body does when its walls give way and has a baby's hand that cannot reach it (T060, T082).
  4. The held detail is paid: the shape of Dessa's thumb along the wicker --- small, quick, the grip of a woman who carried clay up nineteen steps and children down them --- arrives in **past tense, untagged**, as the shape he is given to keep and never to spend. A hand in a grey sleeve goes into a coat pocket for a small cold rectangle with a name on it; the sentence about a boat on cold water nine promises old is not said; the name is not looked at; the hand comes back with wool. Nothing crosses into the basket that a baby's hand can carry (T044, T004, T060).
  5. She does not say the number and does not say the name; she dies believing him spent among children she has never seen. The number stays in her thumb, the name under the sieve-wire scar, and the lie told for mercy is now a fact nobody can correct. Pay the first edge of her lie and of the withheld name only; the full cruelty is years away (T008, T073).
  6. Alda arrives after the vessel has given: soap and warm milk gone cold under river silt, her hand going to the slate before the person, exact dates laid where a wonder should stand, the tin touched once inside her bodice and not opened. She does not say the woman's name. The boy sees the order of reaching and files it with the record-first hands, but not as the same hand as Berr's twice-wiped thumb or the Drawing collector's hand with no pause (T023, T007/T008, T080, T073).
  7. The collector's clerk is at the Fishstairs this morning with a slate and a soft cuff; word comes up with the neighbours that he has been set there for a measure nobody has called for. When Sella carries the basket down past the Fishstairs edge the boy sees the hand go to the slate before the eyes go to the stalls. He files the order on its own tray --- not Marra, not the grey-fingered Drawing collector, not joined to the woman at the flare (T080, T084, T047). **No visit to the bank and no quest.**
  8. Mira comes up from the eels with slime on her sleeves and her head held right; she does not look at the water or into the basket, brings no crust, and answers a silence with her own answer (T059, T086, T060).
  9. At dusk Sella carries him up the Clay Yard under the Vail roof and sets him on his bed; the two crusts are in the fold, one spent and one still giving a thin breath of barley smoke, neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap (T086, T088, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** The woman who could not say his name is gone into the water, and the only thing the boy can keep of her is the weight of a thumb on a wicker handle. A surgeon's knowledge of exactly how a vessel gives way has never been more exact or less useful: he is held on the bank and cannot look away. The hearing the world would not give him comes at last --- out of a person he loved without knowing her.
- **Sensory anchors:** clay-heavy water going over water; silt in a hem and under nails; the small flat knock; a grip that stays one breath and lets go; the grey sleeve and the cold rectangle not looked at; the boat on cold water; soap and milk gone cold; a soft cuff turned back twice and a hand to a slate; the two crusts in the fold; the banked fire's four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T061 and T087 (sixth hearing, second from a person --- paid), T008 and T073 (first edge of the lie and the unsaid name), T044 and T004 (hand-on-basket detail paid, untagged, nothing crossing), T085 (the rising brook takes her), T060, T080 (clerk filed apart), T082 and T071/T072 (the new hearing filed, not joined), T023, T084, T086, T088, T065.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the sixth hearing planted from Stone 25 and held since Chapter 7; pays the hand-on-the-basket detail held from Ilmen 8; pays the first edge of Alda's mercy-lie. Plants Dessa's tin (seen on Day 42) and the number in her thumb as an account nobody thinks is an account; plants the clerk's ordinary order toward the grey fingers at the collector's table years away. Joins nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof, thirty-eight days old, the two crusts in the fold, the shape of a thumb on wicker kept where things with no hour are kept; the sixth hearing lying beside the five from four sources without joining them, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the banked warmth and coming down.

## Scene 2 — The Figure Goes Over the Basket

- **Day and time:** Day 39, Ilmen 10, Tenthday, Account-day and the market at the Cross, from the walk down to the carrying-back in the afternoon.
- **Place:** The Clay Yard and the road down to the Cross; the moot-post's shade at the edge of the market; Slate Row spoken of as a direction.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-eight days old, carried in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), Jorra Vail or Tarn (one silent carrying beat), market crowd and the collector's clerk at the Court's table (walk-ons); Garl Vail as the uncalled debt spoken around.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella carries him down at a half-pace from every open slate and sets him in the moot-post's shade, the same care she uses at the edge of the light; she gives the instruction to the wool and says *the house* where another woman would say *I* (T058, T060, T084).
  2. The market goes on: pigs weighed, eels and pots and turnips, the levy called for the thaw, the number for what the thaw will cost going from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and never given out (T077, T085).
  3. Berr Clay wipes his thumb twice before he touches nothing, reads one in twelve *as the notice says* in the voice that stops the shouting, and puts himself out of his own sentence --- *there'll be a slate at the watch* --- while the true subtraction stays unspoken. The figure goes over the top of the basket (T015, T041, T047, T077).
  4. At the Court's table the clerk's hand goes to the top slate before his eyes go to the woman waiting; the soft sentence with no edge is entered and the woman goes off with her fist shut. The boy keeps this order on the tray of record-first hands, not joined to Alda's reaching or Berr's thumb (T080, T047, T084).
  5. Garl's debt against the firing of tiles is spoken around without a name and left where it lies; the proverb goes to the pot and not to the basket (T009, T013).
  6. The boy keeps the count exactly --- four ways out of the square, the bell's four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the sixth hearing kept beside but never among the five from four sources --- while the yard keeps its number badly in fists and mouths and weather. No new hearing, no sixth sighting of the neat hand, no joining of the pauses (T071/T072, T061, T078, T082).
  7. Sella carries him up the yard and puts the fold right without looking at his face; she says nothing about where they have been or what was read over him (T058, T060).

- **Emotional turn:** A day after a woman goes into the brook uncollected, the district's arithmetic resumes exactly where it left off. The boy is carried through the account that priced his kind and cannot spend, correct or hand back a single figure.
- **Sensory anchors:** a half-pace kept from every open slate; the moot-post's shade; pigs going over the weighing board; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; one in twelve read twice; a soft cuff and ink in two fingers; a fist shut at the Court's table; the bell's four, four and a gap; the fold put right without a look.
- **Threads advanced:** T077 (number withheld, passed hand to hand), T015 and T041 (one in twelve, thumb wiped, box still locked), T047 (passive grammar as market and Court speech), T084 (the not-lookings), T080 (clerk filed apart), T058 and T060 (unanswered keeping, warmth entered nowhere), T009 and T013 (uncalled debt, proverb), T061, T078, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants nothing new; advances the withheld number toward the printed deduction against the true figure and the clerk's order toward the collector's table. Pays nothing; no joining, no recap of the figure.
- **Exact last image:** The boy under the Vail roof with the fold put right at his ear, the sixth hearing lying beside the five from four sources and the count going on four, four and a gap into a year the district has entered and has not entered him.

## Scene 3 — No Collector Called

- **Day and time:** Day 40, Ilmen 11, Firstday, morning through dusk under the Vail roof and along the Clay Yard.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house (bench, fire, hollow seat, clean dark line), the drying racks, the yard well spoken of; the death-house ridge two doors down measured by thumb.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-eight days old, carried and set at the edge of the racks), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; neighbours at the well (walk-ons); Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, proverb and uncalled debt.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella does not send for a collector for the woman in the brook, and nobody remarks on it. The omission is not entered, not named and not argued; the yard goes on with its buckets and slip as though warmth left where it fell were an ordinary way for a body to end. Plant the first edge of the later heresy --- warmth can be left where it is and the world does not end --- without writing the doctrine or any Grathic word for it (T009, T088, T060).
  2. The boy notes the absence the way he notes a sound that did not come: an interval with the shape of a thing that should have happened. He keeps it as an object on a tray, not as a cause and not as a decision (T082, T071/T072).
  3. Sella measures the clean dark line where the Vail Ash ridge turned its corner against the death-house ridge by the width of a thumb, and leaves both where they lie. The coat keeps its salt shape, the seat its leftward lean, and the proverb goes on being said to the pot after the crack is gone (T013, T009).
  4. Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the empty yard it can be checked, six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back. He agrees with everybody so thoroughly that there is nowhere for an argument to stand, and still wants the thaw late, because a late thaw puts off the paying (T036, T085, T047).
  5. Jorra's bent finger leads at the damper, which he moves before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low; bone-coloured slip stands to old bone up both forearms today and there is no grey dust, no back room mentioned, and no look toward the basket (T035, T061).
  6. Sella feeds the boy barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, two fingers behind his ear holding his head where it wants to go, the spoon wiped on her sleeve, his mouth not wiped. She gives the instruction to the fire and the wool, never to the basket, and says nothing about the payer of the warmth (T060, T058, T044).
  7. At the bank she turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the widow's mark, because a bank is not an opening. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap (T079, T088, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** The household answers a death by performing none of the rites the Court would book, and the boy recognises the shape of an omission before he has a word for what it means. The first heresy arrives as something that is simply not done.
- **Sensory anchors:** a bucket going round with no slate following it; a clean dark line measured by a thumb; a small knife set on its sill; Ash worked into knuckles no scrubbing lifts; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone; gruel from the house's own dish; a damper moved before a sentence is finished; coals turned with a broken pot and a mark untouched.
- **Threads advanced:** T009 and T088 (first heresy planted as an omission; warmth left where it fell), T082 (an absence filed as an interval), T013 (ridge left where it lies), T036 (six counted, seventh held, late thaw wanted), T035 (no dust today, damper moved early), T060 and T058 (unentered warmth, instruction to the fire and the wool), T079, T065, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the first heresy --- a body left where it fell and no collector coming --- toward the refusal to collect in Arc 5; plants the clean dark line left in place toward the later understanding that a thing left where it is is still kept. Pays nothing; no doctrine, no word, no explanation.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with an omission filed on its own tray beside the intervals, the clean dark line where the ridge was left where it lies, and the carried fire behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one.

## Scene 4 — The Second Cord Twisted Twice

- **Day and time:** Day 41, Ilmen 12, Secondday, dawn at the Fishstairs and back along the Slip Brook into the yard.
- **Place:** The Fishstairs below the north bridge; the eel stalls; the Clay Yard and the Slip Brook behind the racks.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-eight days old, carried and set in the Fishstairs shade), Mira Silt, Sella Vail, weir women and a stall-keeper (walk-ons); the collector's clerk can be glimpsed at the board's edge without dialogue; Dessa absent and dead. Garl Vail as proverb only.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Mira comes up from the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in her neck, her head held to the right against the cold where the top of her left ear had been, the small smile kept on her face like a door on the latch (T059).
  2. Asked whether the eels run early she answers with a question and then her own answer --- *They run when they run. Ask the river* --- and when a figure is spoken at the stall her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord at her waist and stays while she decides (T059, T077).
  3. Shifting the basket's weight shows the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey one, the cord never taken upriver to Nine Pots and never lifted at a door, the knots uncounted. The boy keeps the two cords side by side on separate boards and does not let them touch (T059, T025, T071/T072).
  4. Eel-bones flick against her thumb and one is put away when the deciding is done; the movement is entered nowhere. She sells a woman six eels for five marks or five for six and makes nothing of it (T059, T047).
  5. **No third crust.** The spent crust and the smoky one still lie in the fold, neither eaten, lifted or named; Mira brings nothing and does not look into the basket. Do not muddy the crusts toward their Day 46 going (T086, T060).
  6. The weir women speak of the blessing tomorrow and the number that goes with the eels, then end in weather; the number goes around as a shape nobody gives out and the boy cannot spend it (T077, T085).
  7. Behind the racks the Slip Brook runs clay-heavy a little higher than it should; the boy sets it beside the honeyed ice and the opened hairline and keeps three objects in three places, making no cause of them (T085, T083, T082).

- **Emotional turn:** The aunt who knows keeps her account in wool where no Court thumb would think to look, and the boy sees two cords that are both records and cannot ask what either counts. A second kind of keeping stands beside the household's silences, unreadable to any officer.
- **Sensory anchors:** pale eel slime dried on sleeves; silt in the neck; a head held to the right; a smile like a door on the latch; eel-bones flicked and put away; grey wool with a second cord twisted twice beneath it; a number that goes from mouth to shoulder; the two crusts in the fold; the brook running clay-heavy behind the racks.
- **Threads advanced:** T059 and T025 (two cords, knots uncounted, Nine Pots unvisited), T077 (number withheld at the stall), T086 and T060 (no third crust, neither named), T084 (not-looking), T085 and T083 (brook, honeyed ice, hairline kept apart), T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants nothing new; keeps the two cords side by side toward the knot-string read years hence and the crusts toward the day one is gone and nobody says who took it. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy set in the Fishstairs shade with the fold at his chin, a second cord twisted twice under a grey one that he cannot see from where he lies, the two crusts still in the fold and no third, and the count four, four and a gap going on.

## Scene 5 — The Tin Above the Bed

- **Day and time:** Day 42, Ilmen 13, Thirdday, mid-morning three doors down at House Silt and back under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** House Silt --- the room with the board-bed, the bench, the door a finger's width open and the tin on a shelf above the bed; the Vail kiln-house.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty-eight days old, carried in and set just inside the door), Sella Vail, a neighbour who opens the door and goes about her work (walk-on); Alda Reed offstage; Dessa absent and dead. Garl Vail absent.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella carries the basket three doors down and sets it just inside a door standing a finger's width open --- the room where a measure was taken on Stone 22 and where a woman now lies dead elsewhere. She sets it where a pot would be set, level and out of the draught, and does not look into it (T084, T060).
  2. A tin on a shelf above the empty bed holds a scrap of cloth from a birth. The boy notices the object from the low angle of the basket --- the angle at which shelves are highest --- knows neither what it is nor that it was carried warm from another fire, and enters it nowhere. He cannot reach it, cannot ask and cannot read the writing if there is any (T074, T060, T071/T072).
  3. The board-bed, the bench by the door, the pot on it and the draught going along the floor to the corner make this another likeness of the birth room, the kiln-house, the death-house and the Court's room heard through a doorway. He keeps the five rooms in five separate places and writes no cause where he has only noticed a likeness (T081, T082).
  4. Nobody says the woman's name and nobody says the number in her thumb. The lie she believed stays where she left it: a child spent among children she never saw, which was a mercy when it was told and is a fact now that she cannot correct it (T008, T073).
  5. Sella's private roll stays unshown behind its wall; she does not open a slate, go to a bank or turn a visit to the collector into anything. Neither woman makes a visit to the bank into a quest (T058, T057).
  6. The boy comes back under the Vail roof with the tin filed as one more object no Court can enter --- a second record kept in a material no assessor would think to read --- beside Alda's notes under a floorboard, Mira's cords and Orra's knots, and keeps it where things with no hour are kept (T071/T072, T023, T059).
  7. At the bank the coals go under blind ash and the widow's mark is not touched, because a bank is not an opening (T079, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** Dead, the woman keeps one thing of her child that nobody entered and nobody will read; the boy sees the object without knowing what it is, which is the truest picture of all his keepings. A room can hold an account no slate can confiscate.
- **Sensory anchors:** a door standing a finger's width open; a shelf high above a board-bed; a tin that holds a scrap of cloth; a bench by the door and a draught felt at the ankles; a low angle at which shelves are highest; silt and wet clay gone cold in a room without its woman; the little-door burn closing on nothing.
- **Threads advanced:** T008 and T073 (lie standing, name and number unsaid), T074 (the mother's warmth kept at home and entered nowhere), T060, T081 (the room as another likeness, no cause), T082, T058 and T057 (Sella's wall and roll unshown), T079, T065, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the tin above the bed as the second record no Court can enter, toward the convergence of private accounts late in the saga; plants nothing that pays. Does not open the tin, does not name its contents to the boy, does not turn Dessa into a quest.
- **Exact last image:** The boy back under the Vail roof with the tin on its shelf filed where things with no hour are kept, the five like rooms held in five separate places, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Scene 6 — Before the Blessing

- **Day and time:** Day 43, Ilmen 14, Fourthday, at the Cross market and along the river road upriver toward the Weir, into evening.

- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Preparations for the Fish Blessing go upriver: eels salted and hung on the eel racks, the eel traps lifted and set, the blessing stone on the north bank made ready. Orra's cords of knot-accounts are in the Eel House, where she keeps them rather than on any slate (T024, T059).
  2. Orra is slow and half-sung in the Fen way; she tells a story sideways and gives a date where another person would give an opinion. When she is lying she ties a knot, which is invisible to anyone who does not know the cord. She never answers a question about the levy directly, but will tell which night the collector's boat came and how many oars it had (T024, T077).
  3. The boy sees her cord and her rope-hands and the eel scars she never covers, and keeps the grey wool beside Mira's grey wool without letting the two touch (T024, T059, T071/T072).
  4. Across the market a woman with green at one corner of her mouth looks at pots as a woman looks at things she can price, and is gone. No dialogue, no name on the page. The boy files the green and the looking in silence, years before the ear is spoken of (T050, T051).
  5. Alda and Orra do not discuss the third day. They speak of the weather and the river, and when the number comes near one of them touches something and the subject changes (T008, T024, T073).
  6. The Weir reads as another room --- a bench, a pot, a draught, a gap where a board in the plank walk will not bear a man's weight in Silt --- and the boy keeps it apart with the others. He does not recognise it; he only files it (T081, T083).
  7. The Ilm runs a little higher under its thinning skin and the yard calls it weather; the honeyed ice, the opened hairline and this new rise stay on three places. The Singing Sill's low note comes up through the boards early and is called weather too (T085, T083, T071/T072).

- **Emotional turn:** A woman who keeps a son in knots brings her account onto the page without telling anyone what it counts, and the boy is brought to the Weir without being told why. The silent ear in the market is filed beside the other keepings and says nothing.

## Scene 7 — The Forty-First Knot

- **Day and time:** Day 44, Ilmen 15, Fifthday, the Fish Blessing at the Weir, from the blessing at the stone through the afternoon and the carrying-home at dusk.
- **Place:** The Weir: the blessing stone on the north bank, the plank walk, the eel traps, the Eel House; the Vail kiln-house under the roof at night.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-four days old, carried and set in the shade with the fold at his chin), Orra Weir, Sella Vail, hearth-maids and weir women at the blessing, market and eel voices (walk-ons); Mira Silt can be glimpsed with her cords; Alda Reed offstage; Berr Clay not present. Dessa dead and stays dead; no visit to the bank becomes a quest.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The hearth-maids stand on the blessing stone and bless the weir and the eel traps, the crossed mark cut into the stone drawn but not explained. The eels run whether anybody blesses them or not. The boy is set in the shade out of the draught, with the fold at his chin; he is thirty-eight days old in the morning and the count goes on regardless (T060, T071/T072, T081).
  2. Orra keeps her accounts in knots on grey wool and will not sit at a table where a slate is open, because a written name is a name somebody else can read. She tells the story of the traps --- an eel goes in and cannot find the way out, which is all a trap is, and she tells the boy not to make more of it than that (T024, T059).
  3. She ties a forty-first knot she cannot account for: the one tied the night she saw Sella carry a covered bundle up the Clay Yard, small and warm with a flower on it. The boy cannot know any of this; he sees only the shape of the knot and the way her thumb goes to the cord (T024, T001, T059, T071/T072).
  4. She says the word **forty** within his hearing --- the first time the number has been spoken where he can hear it --- and watches what he does with it. He is too young to answer and does not forget the shape of the cord. The word goes on its own tray, unread, the first edge of a number that will open years later. **Do not explain the number, the deduction, the Forty or the boy's return** (T024, T077, T082, T071/T072).
  5. The boy is a cracked, low-capacity vessel, below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named, in a district that has just spent a woman and is blessing eels. He cannot speak, cannot read Grathic, cannot lift a hand to the cord. The infant body drools and wants the smoky crust; the surgeon's register notes the shape of a knot and the weight of a thumb (T060, T086, T071/T072, T082).
  6. Mira's grey cord and Orra's knot-string stand side by side in his private file and do not join; the two records of the district's private account stay unread, and the girl who kept them has not said what she knows (T059, T025, T024, T082).
  7. At dusk Sella carries him up the Clay Yard under the Vail roof and sets him on his bed, not in the Debt Loft. The two crusts lie in the fold, neither eaten nor named; the carried fire keeps under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Nothing has been joined, explained or ended; the word *forty* lies in the private file with the shape of a knot and a thumb on wicker he cannot spend (T086, T088, T065, T044, T071/T072).

- **Emotional turn:** A woman who has kept a son in knots for three years says the number aloud to a baby who cannot answer, and watches anyway. The boy receives a word he cannot spend and keeps its shape, another entry in an account that outlives everyone who can read it. A district blesses its eels over a woman it did not collect.
- **Sensory anchors:** a flat stone with a crossed mark; salt on the eel racks and green smoke from the tallow; a rope-hand tying a knot; grey wool and the thumb going to it; the word *forty* spoken low and half-sung; a low angle at which a basket sees the plank walk; the two crusts in the fold; a fire under blind ash keeping four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T024 (forty-first knot and the word spoken, paid and opened), T081 (the Weir as another room), T059 and T025 (two cords side by side, unjoined), T060 and T086 (warmth entered nowhere, two crusts), T088 and T065 (the carried fire, four, four and a gap), T077, T082, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the long-withheld number into the boy's hearing for the first time (first edge of T024; the cord read as a chart and the full account remain years away); plants the forty-first knot toward the day it is read, and the watched reaction toward Orra's long vigil. Joins nothing, explains nothing, closes nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof, forty-four days old, the two crusts in the fold and no third, the shape of a cord with a knot that cannot be accounted for filed beside the thumb on wicker and the sixth hearing, the carried fire behind him keeping four, four and a gap, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Pull into Chapter 11

Day 44 closes with the forty-first knot tied and the word *forty* spoken for the first time where the boy can hear it, with no answer from him and nothing explained; Dessa is gone into the brook and stays gone; no collector was called for her and nobody called the omission anything; the two crusts still lie in the fold and one will be gone before Chapter 11 is out. Nothing has been joined, paid or explained except a sound, a shape and a word, and the count still stands four, four and a gap.

Chapter 11 opens on **Day 45, Ilmen 16, Y.H. 813, Sixthday**, at the Fishstairs after the Blessing: the forty-first knot already tied and nobody able to say what it counts; the boy carried below the north bridge at dawn; a brine-gull going off with a coal not yet cold; the eel-bones moving from hand to hand while Mira decides whether to lie. The cord is an object he can now read in shape if not in name, the two cords stay side by side in his private file, and a crust will go missing that nobody will say who took it. Forward, not closed.
