\daymark{40}{Ilmen 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

No collector came for the woman in the brook. It was a fact made out of nothing: no knock at the yard gate before light, no soft cuff coming up \place{the Clay Yard} with a slate under the arm, nobody sent down the road to ask for the man who took measures and said them into a book. \person{Sella Vail} went to the fire instead. She lifted the lid without putting a hand to the cord at her throat, because there had been no opening, and she fed the coals and said to the kiln what the house always said to the kiln. The kiln took its breath in fours and missed one. \person{Corin}, forty days old, lay on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof and heard the gap as he had heard it on every morning of his life, and heard, around it and under it and after it, the thing that had not happened.

A district that collects its dead has a shape for it, and the boy knew the shape. A man comes with a slate and a soft sentence; there is a pale round place left on an arm where the iron has been; the account stands as entered and the household has nothing in its fist that it can spend. He had heard that sentence said over a neighbour in a room with a bench and a pot and a draught under the door, and he had watched the number go around the well in fists and mouths afterward. Two days ago a woman had come up out of the water behind the racks with the clay set in the creases beside her mouth, and the shape had not appeared. Nobody went to fetch it. Nobody said that it had not appeared. The yard took up its buckets where it had laid them down and went on with its slip, and \lore{the Court} entered neither the woman nor the omission in anything it showed him.

Warmth could be left where it fell and the morning came anyway. That was what the day laid out, though nobody was teaching it and he did not know he was being taught. The fire held; bread was cut; the kiln took its breath in fours; no one had gone down the road with a slate. A man under this roof had been left where he fell two winters ago and the house had gone on with its fire and its roll and its hollow seat, and a woman had been left in the water two days ago and the yard went on with its buckets. He kept the two left things in two places. They had the same shape, and a same shape was where a mistake began if a man had a hand free enough to follow it, and he had made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried it here. He wrote no cause beside either. He did not call the leaving a decision, because nobody had decided anything in front of him. He did not call it mercy, because a district that calls things mercy has a use for the word. He did not call it a refusal, because there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold it still long enough to look at. He called it nothing and kept it on its own tray.

He had learned early that a thing which does not happen leaves a space with the outline of a sound that did not come. He had kept such spaces before: the half-heard note that had gone past a door in the old year, the silence where this house's dead man's name should have stood, the fifth stroke the bell never struck. This one went on a tray of its own, beside the intervals and not among the hearings. It was not the sixth hearing of \lore{the full note}, which had lain where it was put two days ago beside the five from four sources, not joined to them and not a seventh. It was not nearly full, not a note, not an answer to any interval he had carried. It was an absence with the outline of a collection, and the outline was all he had. No hand to draw it, no word in Grathic with which to ask for it, and a baby's voice that would have turned the question into a wet sound. The tray touched none of the others.

At \place{the yard well} the morning went the way mornings go when nothing has been written down. A woman let down a bucket and said the water tasted of the thaw; a man come up from \place{the slip pits} said the pits had been worked early and the clay would want turning; somebody said \place{the Ilm} only did what rivers did in Ilmen and that let everybody go home without owning the day. The bucket went round and no slate followed it. A woman scouring a pot two doors down had something to say about the water behind \place{the drying racks} and ended in the weather instead, and the girl carrying slip beside her said nothing at all, and the words they found instead were about buckets and clay and the price of eels, which were the words they would have said on any Firstday of any Ilmen. The boy could not have said what word was missing from the scouring woman's sentence. He knew only that the sentence had arrived somewhere other than where it started, and he kept the shape of its ending on the tray with the absence.

The cold came in its known order, cheek and temple and wrist. His head went where it wanted and made its small wet sound and came back, and the thread at his mouth froze a little and was not wiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and cannot be set down again for a chin. He wanted the smoky crust in the fold and could not reach it, and the wanting was louder than the knowing, as it had been on every morning he had ever had.

Mid-morning Sella went out with the thumb of her right hand already laid flat, the way a woman goes to look at a thing she has measured before and does not need to measure again. Where \lore{the Ash} had turned the corner of this house for a year and two days there was now a clean dark line in the plaster, near two thumbs wide if a man laid his thumb in it and called it near. She laid her thumb in it. Then she walked two doors down the yard to \place[the Drawing house]{the death-house two doors down} and laid the same thumb in the line at that corner, which was a finger's width wider, and walked back without saying which was which and without fetching a broom for either.

The boy watched from the edge of the racks, where she had set the basket level and out of the draught and not looked into it afterward. Her little-door burn shut on nothing as the hand came back to her side. In the house the salt-stiff coat of \person{Garl Vail} kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and the hollow wheel-seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years, and the proverb went on being said to the pot after the thing it was about was gone: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. There had been a grey crack in the wall all year; then the line was burned on the first morning of the year and called dealt with by a neighbour with a pail of water, and the proverb went on being said to the pot. A saying that outlives its subject is not the same thing as a subject that outlives its saying, and the boy kept the two facts in different places, as he kept the clean dark line and the ridge that had stood in it.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} had the small bench knife on its sill before the yard was properly light. He set it down as a man sets down an instrument that has been in his pocket all night and is not there now, and told the empty yard where it was, as he told it every morning: “It's back. You can check.” Six years of the seven were counted; the seventh he held back in the space between the count and the word for the count. He swept the place by the bench where no note had come. He swept around the corner where the clay-heavy water had come up behind the racks two days ago, and he swept the road that went down toward \place{the Fishstairs} without once looking along it. That was the road a man went down when a house wanted a collector, and the boy knew it from the times one had come into the yard for a neighbour; Tarn's broom went over it and over it and did not turn.

A neighbour at the gate wanted the thaw early because the pits would have to be worked whatever the river did; a woman at the tubs wanted it late because the levy fell in the thaw and a late thaw was a longer week before the paying. Tarn agreed with each so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot. “Yes. That's right. I'll sweep it again,” he said to both, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying and a clay-bound man can want a thing without owning the wanting. Nobody sent him down the road this morning. He noticed that he had noticed it, and went back to the broom. The boy kept the not-looking where he kept the other not-lookings and wrote no cause beside any of them.

\person{Jorra Vail} came in from the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and no grey worked into the creases past the bend of his little finger today, and the bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper. Sella had opened her mouth to tell him the fire was low. He had moved the damper before the sentence got as far as the fire, and the fire came up, and she shut her mouth on the rest of the instruction and gave it to the pot instead. “You'll do,” he said to a pot on the bench, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He told the wheel it did not care whose hands were on it, only if they were steady. He did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the basket at the edge of the racks.

The boy read the forearms from the low angle of the basket. Grey dust was one entry and clean slip was another, and today there was only clean slip, and an absence is a fact like any other if a man has learned to count absences. He put the clean forearms on their tray and did not ask what room they had not been in, because the question would have come out of him as a wet sound and nothing more. He had once stood in rooms where a wrong entry could be corrected by speaking; here he was the only one who had noticed the thing not written down and the only one who could not say it.

Sella fed him in the doorway, where the light was good enough to see the spoon and the draught came along the floor to the corner. The dish was the house's own, barley gruel thinned with milk, and the warmth in it had come from a fire nobody in the yard had paid for in front of him. She sat with two fingers behind his ear holding his head where it wanted to go; the spoon went in and came out wiped on her sleeve, and his mouth was not wiped after. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She never said it to the basket. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and gave every instruction to the fire and the pot and the wool, and said nothing at all about the payer of the warmth, which the house kept as it kept its roll: entered nowhere and not for show. He wanted the breast and got the gruel and wanted the smoky crust and could not reach it, and ate what was given, which was the whole of his arithmetic and more than he could say for the yard.

At dusk she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A bank was not an opening; she had carried the rule through a year and into this one without explaining it to anybody, least of all to the child at her hip, and the mark stayed where it was, at her throat on its cord, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it; the ridge that had recorded another man's leaving was a clean dark line in the plaster. The carried fire that had come up the yard on \lore{Kindling} lay under blind ash and the broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The two crusts lay in the fold where they had been put, one gone past smelling of anything since Stone 15, the other still giving a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turned off the yard, neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third.

She put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face and set him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. He lay with the omission on its own tray beside the intervals, and the clean dark line left where it lay here and two doors down, and the fire behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one. No collector had come for the woman in the brook and nobody had found a word for what had not been done, and the yard had gone on with its buckets as a yard goes on. He was forty days old and the only adult in the room and the least use in it. His hand outside the wool, after a consideration that was not a decision, went up an inch toward the warmth behind him and came down.
