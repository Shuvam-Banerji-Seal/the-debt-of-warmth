\daymark{329}{Bone 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

The thirtieth of Bone came on under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof as its two predecessors had come, in the small hours, without a boundary drawn and without a voice to say what had been reached. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and twenty-ninth day with nobody under \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} to tell him what the number counted; six months and one hundred and forty-nine days were told by nobody. Three hundred and twenty-eight stood exact behind him and the day ahead was edge to edge against it, each a small vessel poured into no other. Nothing from the twenty-eighth or the twenty-ninth crossed the threshold as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The fingers at the rack end that had gone in and come out empty were not made to close on this morning; the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh at the kindling stayed one object on its own place and was not made again, because a morning had not earned the making of it and no payer stood under any of these. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and that silence had stood three hundred and twenty-nine days with no payer named beneath it. \person{Garl Vail} stayed where he had been kept, in objects and not in a story. Above, \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went on as a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, not numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood, so the three stayed where they were.

He took the body in its old order, as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in on the thirtieth to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, the ease cold puts on a face that is no ease, the patient who seems better before he is worse because a cold vessel cannot clot and the blood stands where it should not; such a patient must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict and without a witness, and the argument was the work.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold, and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said I. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold with the pale puckered skin inside it. The twice-mended pot came up from \place{the pot board} with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and only then did the weight come on the handle; both proverbs went to the pot and to the fire and never to a person, and the crack was left showing. The dressed grain turned under wool on the cool stone away from the hearth with a still lighter touch and no figure, and no handful was lifted; her fingers went in and came out and did not close. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} was touched at no hour, because a morning was not an opening and five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the sixth stood far off at \place{Ash} 2 and was not brought near. The thread lay where it lay. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap, and two hundred and thirty-two honest answers stood on the sixth at the opening of the day.

Bone 30 was the month's third and last Account-day and market, and it was earned by its own morning and not by any that had gone before. In the direction of \place{the Cross} \place{the reading block under the moot-post} was a direction still, and the bell and the thane with his box were a direction and nothing more, as the road had been on the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth. Five arithmetics of the year rode locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space between, five readings five and not one account; five true subtractions sat inside beside the printed lie and were spoken by nobody. The reading stood one morning's walk away and was not brought into the small hours. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and the told figure stood beside the paid with no signature between. Bone went on without a boundary drawn. The older women named it by the weather that had not come and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse.

\scenebreak

In the afternoon Sella carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the way a pot is carried filled to the lip that must not be slopped. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she told the fold, and told the wind nothing, for the wind was the one thing in this district that had never asked anybody to be told anything. The small jolts of her walking came up through the straw into his ribs; out in \place{the Clay Yard} the seven places went by with a look and a wide step and no number, the clean dark line and its finer grey hairline unswept, the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack, the round ring on the well's stone lip with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, the skinned trough, the field's empty place, the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. No eighth likeness stood up among them and no cause was laid across any two. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet and was called weather and entered nowhere, and the three coverings kept a finger between every pair. The dressed grain lay turned under wool and given no figure. None of this was a sign of what the day would bring; a yard goes around its own places on an Account-day as on any other, and the boy entered it as he found it.

They went up \place{the clay road} toward the square where the market had already begun, the clay-heavy air of it coming down the road ahead of the noise, and the noise coming after in a low spread that was not yet a crowd. Sella set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, the narrow strip of shadow falling across one corner of the straw and not across his face, and stood half a pace off through the reading and the bell. From there the reading block under the moot-post stood at a little angle, and the pot board further along caught the light along its rims with the dust standing in the hollows. He took the square as a field is taken before the light, by the small sounds first. Coins went into a palm without being counted where anyone could hear. A woman set a bowl down and set it back. Somebody coughed behind the rail. Two hundred people leaned in the wrong direction and then corrected themselves, the way a crowd breathes before it listens.

Berr Clay came up short off the clay road at the reading block with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward. A finger went over the figure and his eyes rested on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the two hundred. \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says}. He sent the figure twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the late households that had not heard it the first time; he did not look into the basket and never had.

He called the levy and denied it in the same breath, as he had called and denied it in months behind him. \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me}. And to a man who brought a number up out of a fist and would have put it into the square: \emph{Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch}. Your house, never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field clean and the hand clean and nobody to say whose field it had been. To the block he said something more low about kindness being entered, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs; the block answered nothing, being a block. Then he went off up the clay road with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Bone's third true subtraction riding inside beside the five that had gone before, believing he had done the district a kindness.

Six arithmetics of the year now stood behind one printed figure. Six were six and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid, no signature stood on the space where the price should have stood, and a rule made from six wanted a payer put under all six and none stood, so no rule stood up, though two hundred people had heard the figure twice and gone back to the pigs. The boy kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it: not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, not a rule. He did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool and read over by a number that did not know its cost, the four held changes on their four places, the handful let fall back at the rack end, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching. The finger went between. The reading answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and did not come out, nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, nothing for the grain under wool, and nothing for the silence where the fifth pull of the carried fire should have stood. An account that answers none of these is still an account, and the boy entered it where he entered such things, and supplied no payer to make it hold.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road toward \place{Greybank} before noon, edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve, the second strap let out for the long stretch and the dried pale scurf of road-mud standing unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. Her hand arrived at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Two women by the rail asked what she carried. \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}, she said, answering the question put and not the one behind it; and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}. She went on with the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate; no fourth shaping was made of the well-saying; she was not made a reader of what she carried and did not become a witness, a cause or a confession. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on its own place with a finger between it and every other, and the boy filed no tenth. She was not \person{Marra} with her neat dark hand, not the soft-handed man of \place{Slate Row}, not the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing, not the woman who kept a count in wood in a sleeve, and he joined her to none of them. Two likenesses were not a cause, and six arithmetics were six, and a rule wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

The bell went four, four, and a gap, longer than a church bell and with the gap where the fifth should have stood; nobody crossed himself. It stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night and was not fetched in to make a fourth count and was not heard as a seventh of the full note. Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk sat set for a measure nobody called, with his hand to his inward slate before his eye went up to the stalls; the market sold over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. His measure stayed where it was, entered inward and uncalled. At the pot board a woman with green at the corner of her mouth turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, and at the market's edge a shape in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one stood where the light did not fall squarely on it. The boy read the small shoulders as a fracture set in the wrong position and the green as a colour, and filed each on its own place as the seventh of its kind with no eighth, never putting a name to either, and joining neither to loaf, slate, sale, hearing, cause or romance. No eighth came at this market. No collector came up the clay road or went down it, and the thane going up was not a collector and was not joined to one.

\scenebreak

In the moot-post's finger's width of shade, with the basket set where no slate lay and Sella standing half a pace off and giving nothing in the direction of his face, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and thirty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the eighth hand on the road, the ninth hand in the sleeve, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the finding on its own place nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and thirty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The district's arithmetic had changed a third time in Bone and could not be changed back, and the boy's care was to keep the sixth reading from standing up as a doctrine where two hundred people had heard it and gone home with it in their pockets as a kindness. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it. The infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without a verdict under the thin shade of the moot-post while the market went on around them.

Three hundred and twenty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the thirtieth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. Bone went out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right, and the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do; it had bought nothing at two inches of air and was not for sale, and it went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. Berr went up the clay road with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, six true subtractions riding inside under one unsigned figure; Sella stood half a pace off in the moot-post's shade with the basket where no slate lay; and outside the wool the hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and thirty-three while the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door of a house not yet reached and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
