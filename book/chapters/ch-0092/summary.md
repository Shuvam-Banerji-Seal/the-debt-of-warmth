## Chronicle

Bone went out as it had come on, without a boundary drawn, the boy setting right neither of the district's two habits of naming its months. On the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, left both proverbs with pot and fire, turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure, and set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number; the Cold Acre hare was answered with the weather and the cracked jar sent to a pot that said nothing. At the well on the twenty-ninth Ilva told a woman who asked what the firings had taken, *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, her hand flat on the sleeve where the notched strip lay unread.

On the thirtieth the month's third and last reading came to the Cross. Berr Clay came up off the clay road with the red cord of his office folded under his coat and the sweet green of glaze at one mouth-corner, wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, read *One in twelve, as the notice says* twice over the basket --- once for the moot and once for the late households --- called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never I, and went off up the road with the box locked, believing he had done a kindness. Six arithmetics now stood behind one unsigned figure, six and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer. Hessa Rale carried the slate up toward Greybank before noon and would not read what she bore: *I don't read it. I carry it.* The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself; no collector came up the road or went down it.

Each day gave two answers: a small intake let go whole on the river's note, the hand coming straight down as a tool put away where the next hand will find it. Six were entered, two hundred and twenty-nine through two hundred and thirty-four, each worth nothing at two inches of air. At the close three hundred and twenty-nine stood exact and spoken by nobody, six months and one hundred and forty-nine days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl Vail's name unspoken the same number. Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door while Bone went out without a boundary drawn.
