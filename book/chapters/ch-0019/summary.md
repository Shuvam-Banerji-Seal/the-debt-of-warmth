## Chronicle

On the second day of Seed the work went east of Low Kiln onto the strips the boy had carried for a month only as a direction. Brooms went around what the sweep had left; seed came out of a fist in handfuls nobody counted, and nobody put a figure to hands or strips. Berr Clay came down with his office cord folded under his coat, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, and told the yard a number goes into a slate, not a strip, that the levy was called but not by him.

At the well Sella Vail left the empty place at the row's end with no figure and told a woman to ask the pot what a wet thing costs; the house had said it. She gave the bucket its instruction before the women and never to the basket. The boy kept ninety-one exactly against the house's loaves and glances.

On Thirdday Jorra Vail came through the finger's-width-open door with grey dust past his bent finger, told the wheel it cared only for steady hands, and put that finger into the damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence. He read the damper as the bite that holds a vessel whole and can take a hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both. Tarn set his knife on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held, told the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and swept his broom over the collector's road without turning. No collector came.

At the Fishstairs Mira Silt answered a question about the run with *Ask the river* and put her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a buyer named a figure, shifting a creel to show the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. The steps came back four, four and a landing out of line that a market walked over without a fall; word came that the weak board at the Weir was gone or trodden flat and nobody had gone down to see which. The soft-cuffed clerk still sat for a measure nobody had called.

Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower before she knew it and covered the sign with a dead woman's date. *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down*, she said, exact and not true. Her tin stayed shut; her sixth slate stayed face-down, six pages unread. At dusk Sella set him on his bed, put the fold right without looking at his face and left the thread frozen. The river's note came through the door and the fire's gap through the floor, in two places. He was ninety-three days old; nobody had told him so. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Dusk of Day 93, Seed 4, Fourthday, under the Vail kiln-house roof. Nothing in the action is mid-stroke: Sella has lifted the boy level, carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, gone around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, set him on his bed under the roof (not the Debt Loft), put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it; the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for ninety-three days. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note from the Ilm comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot --- two sounds in two separate places as for ninety-three days. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; no third came. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water going over water and comes down. The count is ninety-three, unspent. Only Corin is present at the last line (Sella has withdrawn); nothing joined, paid, explained or ended.

- **Day and time at the end.** Day 93, Seed 4, Fourthday, Y.H. 813, at the going of the light (dusk). - **Where everyone is and their condition.** Corin/Dan: on his bed under the roof, ninety-three days old (three months), carried during the three days to the strips east of Low Kiln, the Clay Yard, the well, the wheel-shed door (back room not entered), the Fishstairs and House Silt; awake, silent, drool frozen, head wandering, hand outside the wool up an inch and down; cannot roll, stand, speak, read Grathic, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. Sella Vail: at the fire, the broom, the well and the bed; gave every instruction to the wool, the fire, the bucket and the broom and never to the basket, said *the house* (on Day 91: *the house has said it*), went around the two lines and the space under the rack, told the fire it was the thaw, and touched the widow's mark at no hour in three days because a strip, a broom, a basket, a well, a market, a door and a bed are not openings. Tarn: in the yard and under the roof, his small knife on its sill with six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held, Ash worked into his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his wrist, broom over the collector's road without turning, head not turning at a vessel's note on Days 91 and 92. Jorra Vail: in the wheel-shed and the back room (not entered); grey slate dust past his bent finger on Day 92, unnamed men in the back room since first light, spoke to the wheel, told a pot *You'll do*, moved the damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, did not look toward the bed or the basket; *he's not Vail* still sealed. Alda Reed: came short three doors down on Day 93, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay under them, soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt, mercy-lie curdled further against a death nobody alive can correct. Mira Silt: at the eels below the north bridge on Day 93, cords uncounted, board beside Orra's not touching. Berr Clay: came down from the north road to the strips on Day 91, office cord folded under his coat, box locked, true deduction inside, gone off toward High Kiln believing he had done a kindness. Orra (offstage, watching, board beside Mira's), Ulda, Fenna, Marra, Dessa (dead), Rusk and the wider-world figures all offstage.

- **Items, money, powers.** Nothing gained that he can use; no money. **Held/unchanged:** the one spent brown-edged crust, stiff and smell-less, with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third; the small bench knife on its sill; the charred slate in the kiln ash; the basket and wool lent by Sella; the clay jar on the wall shelf; Garl's salt-stiff coat and hollow seat; the uncalled debt against the firing of tiles; the clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept and called the thaw; the boards' four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb. **Subtractions entered in no account:** the smoky crust gone since Day 46, the small pale river stone gone from the fold in House Silt (only the hollow remains), the weak board gone or trodden flat at the Weir with nobody gone down to see which, the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack entered by nobody, and the coal gone up with the brine-gull on Day 45. Alda's little tin is in her bodice, touched on Day 93 and unopened; her **sixth** narrow slate is turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name, six pages all unread, **no seventh written and T023 not paid again**; the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright rub unopened, and the two private records in two materials stand apart in one room, read by nobody and carried to no door. Berr's box stays locked; Mira's grey wool cord and the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched; Orra's knot-string; Sella's private roll unshown. **Powers:** none usable --- no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no recognition. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift; the surgeon's register keeps working perfectly and buying nothing. Cost each day: the body humiliates him in its known order (frozen drool, wandering head making the small wet sound, cold at cheek--temple--wrist with the wool riding back, the hand up an inch and down), and the reading of the cold foot below the sill has the usefulness of an instrument behind glass.

- **Facts, lies, promises, debts.** **Meaningful change:** the direction carried for a month became worked ground --- seed went into the strips east of Low Kiln with brooms, fists and looks, still with no figure spoken --- while the exact count ran 91, 92, 93 against the house's arithmetic in loaves, glances and a space nobody names. **Facts:** the boy is ninety-three days old and nobody has told him so; the strips want hands and nobody says how many; the carried fire's payer has gone unnamed for ninety-three days. **Lies/self-removals:** Alda's *warm and breathing, that's all I've set down* remains exact and not true, and her date stands over the sign where no living person can take it off; Berr's *a number goes into a slate, not a strip* and *the levy is called. It is not called by me*; Sella's *the house has said it* and *the thaw*. **Promises held, not paid:** Jorra's promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn and *he's not Vail* stays sealed; Tarn's seventh year is nearly gone and still held; Sella's private roll unshown; Garl's debt neither paid nor settled. **Debts:** the passed warmth from the fire has a payer beyond the yard, unnamed; the warm loaf from the man from Slate Row gathers interest in an account the boy cannot read. No new debt paid, none incurred that a slate could read.
