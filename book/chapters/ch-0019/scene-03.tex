\daymark{92}{Seed 3, Y.H. 813}

Before the light the room off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} stood a finger's width open, the same width it had kept through all the dark hours, and the dark lay in that width like water held behind a sluice. Men had come in since the first grey and spoken low behind the door, and \person{Corin} knew they had come not by any name spoken aloud but by the dust that came out after them and by the particular quiet that settled on the house while they were inside it --- a quiet with a held quality, as though a vessel had been covered with a cloth so the note could not get out. \person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for them. He did not say the room was cold. There was nobody in the house who would have asked for a name and nobody who would have taken one if he had given it, so the names stayed where they had been spoken, behind the gap, and the boy, carried at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} in a basket that was not his and under a fold that was not his, heard the low talk only as a change in the air, the way a man in a dark room hears that a window has been opened without seeing which way the cold comes from.

He was ninety-two days old and three months and could not hold up his own head for the length of a sentence. The head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and the wool had ridden back from the wrist so the cold arrived in its known order, cheek and then temple and then the bare place at the wrist, and the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. A thread of drool had frozen at his mouth in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the life that was not this one and here he could not lift a hand to his own chin; he had learned to find the joke without laughing, which cost less and kept the same blade in it. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came, and the cold got on with its work around him while the house got on with its keeping.

Then Jorra came through the finger's-width-open door.

The dust was worked into both forearms past the bend of the little finger, grey and fine, ground into the creases the way silt grinds into a seam, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. On the clean mornings the slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the bent finger led at the damper and nothing grey came past the bend; on this morning the grey had come. The boy kept the two states on one tray as two states of one man's mornings and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate, because a question spoken makes an object of an answer and there was no answer here that could be carried. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where whole slate should have stood, and \person{Sella Vail} and every woman working the racks went around the space as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and nobody entered it and nobody swept it. The space kept its shape the way a missing tooth keeps a shape in a man's mouth, and the yard went on around it all morning.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. That was the order of the house and had been since the boy could notice orders: the wheel first, the pots after, the people last if at all, and the basket never. “It doesn’t care whose hands,” he said to the wheel. “It cares if they’re steady.” The low note of the wheel went on under the word, and a pot turned on it with the thrower's weight settled into the hollow his father had worn on the left side over twenty years of leaning. \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and nobody took it down; the seat kept its lean and nobody filled it, which was a distinction the house had never explained and the boy had never asked it to.

“You’ll do,” Jorra said to the pot, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel; the boy knew that he knew, the way he knew the bent finger and the short hair cut so \lore{Ash} could not nest in it. Jorra would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it, and the boy kept the knowing where questions can last for years, on a place of its own with no name written across it. \lore[the full note]{The full note} itself was a thing he could not make and could not spend: six hearings of it stood in his file and stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman who had been warm that morning, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh came into the shed with the dust and no seventh went out of it with the pot. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on their own tray, and three intervals stayed on another, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25, shapes of sounds that had not been sounds. The word forty was a saying with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and it was not spoken this morning. Two tellings of a room shut in the last war stayed on their own tray joined to no kiln in \place{Low Kiln}, likeness kept and difference kept --- breath through the nose against breath made in the throat, both men saying \emph{not the war's hand} --- and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Below the sill a child's foot was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, wrapped in wool by one hand and gone past by a dozen, uncured and unnamed. He knew the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the knowing was exact and had the usefulness of an instrument held behind glass.

She had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the damper ahead of her sentence. The instruction had been going to the fire first, as every instruction in that house went to the fire or the wool or the bucket or the broom and never to the basket, and the person came last if at all; it never reached him. The cold iron took the cold out of his fingertip as his finger went in, drawing it out of the flesh the way a poultice draws, so that the hand that moved the damper was warmer at the point of contact than the hand that had come into the room. He did not look at her. The end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted from it: the wheel kept its note, the women at the racks went back to their pots, and Sella shut her mouth on the rest of the instruction and gave it to the fire.

He had not answered her with a word and had not looked at her, which was how he obeyed her instantly. The boy, carried at the edge with the cold going on at his wrist, read the damper as any iron that stops a thing from running. It was a gate set across the way the heat went, a bite that held the house's warmth in and let it out by degrees, and the same bite that kept a vessel whole could take the hand off a man: close it and the room held; close it wrong and the breath went out of it. He had once held such a bite between his own fingers and known it for mercy and mutilation in one piece of iron, and here there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held both mercies in one. The house had one word for the thing and it was the damper, and the word carried only the holding, never the taking, which was why the men who came into \place{the back room} could speak of a counting room shut with the damper closed and make a curiosity with a date of it, eighteen leagues east, while under this roof a woman who had lost a husband to that danger told the fire it was the thaw. The two tellings stayed on their tray joined to no kiln. The boy kept the reading to himself. It was an anatomy of the house's danger, exact and worth nothing to anyone who might have had the use of it.

He did not look toward the bed or into the basket. The basket might as well have been another pot set down at the edge of the floor, and the bed might as well have been the hollow seat. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn; a man can hold two opinions as neatly as two tools and use neither, and Jorra had held these since the hour he spoke the sentence and the boy was carried in under the roof. He wanted the wheel to stay in the house, the boy outside the name, and the hollow seat for himself, and had said none of the three. The wanting sat in him with the dust in his forearms. Sella, who had never taken the coat down, asked for nothing and was not asked; the keeping went around the wanting the way the yard went around the space under the rack, and neither of them made a thing of it.

\scenebreak

Out in the yard \person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill before the light and told the sill first and the yard after where it was, six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh never cut, nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, and never once turned along it. He had been sent down it once in \lore{the Long Cold}, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, with neither entered in any account that could be read.

No collector came. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence that would call it was not said under this roof; two ears and two silences kept their equality and something that must give at the end of the seventh year did not give here. The carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash with its payer unnamed, warming the boards by degrees, and the planting month went over it the way water goes over a stone without stopping to be counted.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; its payer had gone unnamed for ninety-two days and the coal the brine-gull took upriver was entered in no slate and missed in none. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because no opening had come and a damper moved ahead of a sentence is not an opening and a room spoken of over a bucket is not one either; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay where it had always lain and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw and would go when it went. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety-two days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. The place at the end of the loaf row and the place at his ear lay in two rooms and did not touch. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was unspoken, the two tellings stayed on their tray, the loan stayed uncalled, and the cold foot stayed the cold. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. The boy was ninety-two days old and nobody had told him so, and the count stood at ninety-two, unspent, with the wheel quiet under the roof and the damper where his bent finger had left it.
