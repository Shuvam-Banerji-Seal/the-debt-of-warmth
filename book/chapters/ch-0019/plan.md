## Scene 1 — A Direction, Not a Number

**Day and time.** Day 91, Seed 2, Secondday, before light through midday.
**Place.** The strips east of Low Kiln on the barley fields; the Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house at the return.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; Berr Clay (short); field and bucket-round voices as walk-ons. Jorra, Alda, Mira, Ulda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open without recap on the last image of Chapter 18, continued into the planting month: the mud the flood left set into a brown skin holding a boot's print, and the work after the sweeping lying east of Low Kiln in strips the boy carried until now only as a direction. The first planting work begins on that ground — brooms and glances, no harvest, the barley still a direction on the far side of the yard.
2. The yard counts seed in handfuls and looks and no figure is spoken. A man says the strips want hands; a woman says the weather will have the last word; nobody puts a number to the hands or the strips. The boy sets the strips on a place of their own, a direction still not a number, and keeps ninety-one exactly against a house that keeps its days badly in loaves pushed along a shelf, glances at the clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody calls a figure.
3. Berr Clay comes down to look at the strips, short: red wool cord folded under his coat, green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, smelling of hot clay and nothing under it. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, holds a figure he will not give, says the number goes into a slate and not a strip and there will be a slate at the watch if the house wants it, and takes himself out of his own sentence with *the levy is called; it is not called by me*. The true subtraction goes unspoken; a figure read aloud is not a figure paid. He goes off toward High Kiln with the box locked in his house and the true deduction inside, believing he has done a kindness.
4. Sella gives the broom its instruction before she gives it to anybody, and gives none to the basket. She goes wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner, the new finer grey called the thaw and the space under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, sweeping none of them. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees with its payer unnamed; the coal the gull took is entered in no slate and missed in none.
5. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The body humiliates him exactly: frozen drool unwiped, head going where it wants and making the small wet sound, cold arriving cheek-temple-wrist with the wool riding back from the wrist. He wants the crust, the breast and the bucket's rim two inches off and can reach none; the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water going over water behind the racks and comes down. He wants his number said by somebody who would know what it counted and nobody says it.
6. At the turn of the wind Sella lifts the basket level, carries him up at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen. The count crosses into the afternoon of Seed 2 unspent.

**Emotional turn.** From a direction carried for a month to ground under a broom: work begins, and still nobody will put a figure to the hands doing it. The boy's exact keeping sits against the district's arithmetic without either touching. Forward, not rest.

**Sensory anchors.** Brown skin of mud holding a boot print; seed counted into a fist; broom bristles going around the two lines and the space under the rack; wet wool, woodsmoke, tallow; green glaze at a mouth and a thumb wiped twice; the cold iron rim two inches off; the stiff, smell-less crust; a frozen thread of drool; the fire's missing fifth breath coming through the floor.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (91 exact against bad arithmetic), T077 (figure read aloud not figure paid; strips want hands, nobody gives the number), T041/T047 (Berr's locked box, self-removal, slate at the watch), T009/T013/T092 (two lines unswept, thaw; direction not a number), T058/T079 (instructions to fire, wool, broom, never the basket), T060/T086 (crust and unnamed empty place, no third), T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The strips now worked but still unfigured, held toward the day hands go in and a fist will not say what it holds; Berr's box and the true deduction published years hence; the two unswept lines and the empty place carried further; the count that will reach one hundred.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with one stiff crust in the fold and the empty place still unnamed; the new finer grey unswept beside the old clean dark line; the carried fire at four, four and a gap under blind ash; the river's note through the finger's-width-open door and the fire's gap through the floor in two separate places; the count at ninety-one, unspent.

## Scene 2 — Ask the Pot

**Day and time.** Day 91, Seed 2, Secondday, midday at the yard well and drying racks; dusk under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Place.** The Clay Yard — the yard well and the drying racks — then the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; yard women and a bucket-round of walk-ons. Berr gone toward High Kiln; Jorra heard but not seen at the wheel; others offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. At the well a woman with wet sleeves asks what the planting will cost now that the seed is in the ground. Sella moves her lips along the loaf shelf in the kiln-house doorway, glances at the clay jar on the wall, and leaves the place at the row's end where a loaf stood and stood no longer; she puts no figure to the place.
2. *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,* she says, and to the woman, *The house has said it.* She gives the bucket its instruction before anybody — keep the silt out, the water came up thin twice and will not bear a hard hand — and the bucket goes down and comes up and sets itself on the stone with the care of a pot filled that must not be slopped. Instruction to the bucket, then to the women, then nothing to the basket, and the person last if at all.
3. The boy, carried at the edge, wants the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted and gets no number; in the life that was not this one he could have told the hour of a burn and the term of a debt, here he cannot lift his hand to his own chin. The joke has never once been funny enough to say aloud.
4. He checks the private file as a man checks instruments laid out on a towel: six hearings of the full note stay six — five from four sources and the sixth, the flat knock, from a person — filed beside and never among, no seventh; three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid; three intervals (Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25) stay on another tray; the word forty remains a saying with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; four low things stand in four places — told bell, heard bell, early note of the Singing Sill, the kiln's gap — with the river's note of water going over water a fifth thing and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut counting room stay on their own tray joined to no kiln, likeness and difference kept (breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both saying *not the war's hand*); the loan that can hear stays uncalled; below the sill a child's cold foot is still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured and unnamed. He knows the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform, and the reading buys nothing.
5. Tarn's knife is on its sill and his broom goes over the collector's road without turning; no collector comes. From the wheel-shed comes the low note of the wheel and a word to a pot, *You'll do*, with no figure coming onto the yard. A vessel gives its note past the door and Tarn's head does not turn and he does not name the sound; the boy does not call it a seventh hearing.
6. Sella tells the fire it is the thaw and turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and feeds it; the fire answers with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. She does not touch the widow's mark at the well or at the bank, because no opening came. At the going of the light she lifts him level, goes around the space under the lowest rack, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen.

**Emotional turn.** The house turns the cost of planting into a question put to a pot; the boy keeps an exact file that no one will read and that buys him nothing. Quiet accumulation, not arrival.

**Sensory anchors.** Wet sleeves at the well; loaves moved along a shelf; a glance at the clay jar; an empty place at the row's end with no number; the bucket set down like a pot that must not be slopped; the stiff, smell-less crust; a frozen thread of drool; the muttered note of the Ilm nearer at the going of the water; the fire's missing fifth breath through the floor.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (91), T077 (no figure for the planting cost), T060/T086 (crust, unnamed empty place, no third), T092/T013 (two lines unswept, called thaw), T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer, gull's coal), T061/T082 (six hearings, three near-full answers, intervals, no seventh), T065/T095 (two tellings unjoined to any kiln), T076/T036 (two silences, loan uncalled), T093/T094 (cold foot uncured), T058/T079 (instructions never to the basket, mark untouched).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The exact file kept without a hand to spend it; the two tellings held for the day they join to the breath under the Vail roof; the cold foot carried toward the silt-leach years; the loaf row and the fold's empty place held apart for the new plant of Chapter 20.

**Exact last image.** The boy under the Vail roof at dusk with one stiff crust and the unnamed empty place; the river's low note through the finger's-width-open door and the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor in two separate places; his hand outside the wool up an inch toward the water and down; the count at ninety-one, unspent, crossed into Seed 3.

## Scene 3 — Grey Past the Bent Finger

**Day and time.** Day 92, Seed 3, Thirdday, before light through midday; dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and its damper; the Clay Yard; the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Jorra Vail, Sella Vail; Tarn in the yard; the two back-room men present only as dust and low talk already ended.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before light the room off the wheel-shed stands a finger's width open; men came in since first light and spoke low in it, and Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room is cold.
2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray as two states of one man's mornings; the boy asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
4. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the damper ahead of her sentence, cold iron taking the cold out of his fingertip, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He reads the damper as any iron that stops a thing from running: the same bite that keeps a vessel whole can take the hand off a man, and no word in Grathic holds both mercies in one.
5. He does not look toward the bed or into the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed as a lid nobody has tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn. He wants the wheel to stay in the house, the boy outside the name and the hollow seat for himself, and has said none of it.
6. Tarn's knife is on its sill; his broom goes over the collector's road without turning. At dusk Sella sets the boy on his bed under the roof, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen; the count reaches ninety-two unspent.

**Emotional turn.** A household straightens around a silence rather than a word, and the boy reads an iron bite as both mercy and mutilation with no word for either — a private, useless anatomy of the house's danger.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust past a bent little finger; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone on the clean mornings; the low note of the wheel; cold iron of the damper taking the cold out of a fingertip; a pot's full note known and unsaid; Garl's salt-stiff coat holding a broader man's shape; the clean dark line unswept.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (dust, unnamed men, broken birth-slates under the rack, damper ahead of the sentence), T061 (full note known and unsaid before a kiln-master), T057/T084 (name ungiven, *he's not Vail* sealed, two not-lookings), T058/T079 (instructions to fire and wool, mark untouched), T071/T072 (92).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The unnameable back room and the birth-slates under the rack pointing toward the Broken Slates; Jorra's promise of the foundling's name still ahead; the clamp read as mercy and mutilation in one bite; the hollow seat waiting for his weight.

**Exact last image.** The damper where his bent finger left it and the knife back on its sill where it can be checked; the boy on his bed under the roof with one crust and the empty place; the carried fire at four, four and a gap under blind ash; the count at ninety-two, unspent.

## Scene 4 — Six Counted

**Day and time.** Day 92, Seed 3, Thirdday, before light through dusk.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the road down toward the Fishstairs, and the floor under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Tarn Vail, Sella Vail; yard voices arguing the thaw. Jorra at the wheel, heard and not seen.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before the light Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the sill first and the yard after where it is, six years standing in a mark his thumb has worn almost flat and the seventh never cut, nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. To the kindling: *Six counted. The next I don't count.* He says it as a man says the weather and goes on holding the breath he has decided not to spend, though his chest is already tight with it.
2. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back.
3. The yard argues an early thaw against a late. He says *Yes, house* to both, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went.
4. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. He was sent for one once, in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done; he keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year and neither is entered. No collector comes.
5. In the afternoon a vessel gives its note past the door. Tarn's head does not turn and he does not name the sound; the boy does not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences keep their equality under one roof, and the loan that can hear stays uncalled — a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence has never been said under the roof where it is truest. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it does not give here.
6. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash with its payer unnamed. At dusk Sella lifts him onto his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen, and tells the fire it is the thaw. The door stands a finger's width open; the river's note comes through the gap and the fire's gap through the floor, kept apart. The count crosses into Seed 4 unspent.

**Emotional turn.** A man holding one breath he has decided not to spend while his term runs out under him: the seventh year presses to its edge, is held, and the equality of two silences under one roof stays unbroken. Tension carried forward, not released.

**Sensory anchors.** The small knife and the worn thumb-mark; Ash in knuckles the colour of the dark line; the pale circle of the clip-ring; a chest held tight against a breath; the road a house sends a man down for a collector; a vessel's note past the door with no turn of the head; the fire's missing fifth breath through the floor.

**Threads advanced.** T036 (six counted, seventh nearly gone and held — **do not pay**), T076 (two ears, two silences, loan uncalled), T009/T090 (broom over the collector's road, no collector, the Long Cold sentence kept), T061/T082 (note past the door, no seventh), T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer), T058/T079, T071/T072 (92).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The end of the seventh year and what must give; the unforged collector sentence kept beside it; the ear worth more than the debt and therefore unsafe; the loan held for the day it is called.

**Exact last image.** The knife on its sill where it can be checked and the seventh year held where it cannot; the boy under the roof with one crust and the empty place; the river's note through the door and the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor in two places; the count at ninety-two, unspent, crossed into Seed 4.

## Scene 5 — The Landing Out of Line

**Day and time.** Day 93, Seed 4, Fourthday, dawn market through afternoon; dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge and the edge of the fish-trade; the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Mira Silt; a weir woman with salt white at her wrists, an eel buyer and the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (walk-ons). Orra present only as her board lying beside Mira's and not touching it.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the fish-trade shade where no slate lies, keeps her half-pace from open slates and gives the instruction to the wool and the water, never to the basket.
2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers the question and her own: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away when the deciding is done.
3. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey wool cord and stays while she decides. Shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots and never lifted at a door; neither knot is counted and the knot for the thin child upriver is untouched. Her board lies beside Orra Weir's knot-string and does not touch it; Orra stays offstage, watching still, and will get no answer from this bed.
4. The steps below the bridge have come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it. Word comes up that the weak board in the plank walk at the Weir is gone or trodden flat and nobody has gone down to see which; the boy sets the gone board beside the gone step and the stopping hairlines in three gaps entered in no account.
5. The soft-cuffed clerk still sits at the landing with his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, his cuff grey from washing and turned back twice, his hand going to the slate before his eyes go to the stalls. He is filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector and the Court-table clerk, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand comes. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of a death.
6. The boy sets Mira's not-looking — a keeping — beside Berr's not-looking — a pricing — in places that do not touch, and has no word in Grathic for either. At dusk home, Sella puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen; the count reaches ninety-three unspent.

**Emotional turn.** Necessary kindness done briskly in a trade the market will not read, held against the market's small administrative cruelty — a clerk posted for a measure nobody called and a bad step everyone walks carefully over. The boy keeps the two kinds of not-looking apart.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried pale on sleeves; salt dried white at a weir woman's wrists; silt in the hollow of a neck; the missing top of a left ear; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; a grey wool cord and the second twisted twice beneath it; an out-of-line landing; a cuff grey from washing; a slate held to a forearm.

**Threads advanced.** T059/T025/T024 (both cords, uncounted knots, knot for the thin child untouched, board beside Orra's without touching), T090/T080 (clerk still set for the uncalled measure, record-first hands filed apart, no sixth sighting), T083 (out-of-line step and the gone board at the Weir), T058/T079 (instruction to wool and water, half-pace), T071/T072 (93).

**Foreshadowing planted / carried.** The knot for the thin child in Nine Pots left untouched; the private accounts in wool and knot-string converging years hence; the clerk posted ahead of a death; the weak board held for the Breaking and for the room with the gap left showing.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with one crust and the unnamed empty place; at the Fishstairs the soft-cuffed clerk's hand still going to his slate before his eyes go to the stalls; Mira's second cord twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted and her board beside Orra's not touching; the count at ninety-three, unspent.

## Scene 6 — Warm and Breathing

**Day and time.** Day 93, Seed 4, Fourthday, midday three doors down in House Silt; dusk under the Vail kiln-house roof.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every place a slate might lie; she opens no slate and turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row, and stands a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing.
2. From the basket's low angle the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed keeps its pale fold at the seam and thumb-bright rub, unopened, and the fold of grey wool below holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone once lay. Nobody says either is gone.
3. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay still under them. She lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold flat at his shoulder a breath too long, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower before she knows it and covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa — the seventh of Stone, the account up on the eighth — a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and uncollected, which no living person can take off.

5. Her hand goes once to the little tin in her bodice and comes away without opening it. Her sixth narrow slate stays turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name — written in the second hand on Day 64, six pages all unread, no seventh written and T023 not paid again. At dusk home the private file is checked and unchanged: six hearings stay six beside and never among, three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid, the word forty is unspoken, the two tellings stay on their tray joined to no kiln, the loan that can hear stays uncalled, one brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third comes. ## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 20

The last image of Chapter 19 is a hand going up an inch toward the note of water going over water and coming down, with the count at ninety-three unspent and Seed 4 closing over worked ground. Chapter 20 opens on Day 94, Seed 5, Fifthday, in the Clay Yard: the seed is in the ground east of Low Kiln and the yard goes around the last of the flood's mud with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken, the boy ninety-four days old and carried at the edge, keeping the count exactly. On Seed 6 (Day 95) the loaf row at the house's shelf will stand with two empty places where one used to stand — one beside the loaves nobody calls a number, one in the fold at his ear — and nobody will name either or lay them together. Nothing in the first planting week has settled anything: the new grey lies unswept, the crust and its empty place stay unnamed, six hearings stay six, the private records stay shut, the clerk stays set, the cold foot stays the cold, and the loan that can hear stays uncalled. The planting goes on under the same arithmetic and the count goes on. Forward, not closed.
