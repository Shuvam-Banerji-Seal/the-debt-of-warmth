\daymark{93}{Seed 4, Y.H. 813}

The dawn market had come up out of the river before there was light enough to see whose hands were selling, and \person{Sella Vail} carried the basket down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, under \place{the north bridge} and out onto \place{the Fishstairs}, and set it at the outer edge of the fish-trade shade where the stalls thinned and no Court slate had ever reached. She set it the way a pot is set down on a board that has been chosen for it: not in the draught, not where a man might step, not where any writing lay. There was no writing here. She put the fold right between her fingers without looking into it, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the wool and then on nothing.

The instruction went to the wool and to the water and never to the basket. To the wool: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. To the water, which had come up over the steps in Silt and gone down again leaving them a hand's breadth out of line: go where you're going and leave the step as you found it. She gave the river this instruction every morning and the river had never once taken it, and she gave it anyway, the way a woman tells a pot not to be set near the door when the door is already standing open. The basket got none. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a pot set down is not told what it is holding.

\person{Corin} was ninety-three days old and three months and could not hold up his own head for the length of a sentence. The head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and a thread of drool had frozen at his mouth in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek and then temple and then the bare place at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. He kept ninety-three exactly while the house went on keeping its days badly, in loaves pushed along a shelf and glances at a clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody called a figure. The two counts went down the road together and neither turned to look at the other.

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels at the outer boards with pale slime dried on her sleeves and silt dried into the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch. A weir woman come down from \place{the Weir} with salt white at her wrists and at the cuffs of her sleeves stood over the creels and asked whether the run would be thin now that the planting had begun.

“Thin?” Mira said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.”

She said it to the boards and went on with her knife, and the eel-bones came out against her thumb while she decided whether to lie: one, then two changing hands, then one put away when the deciding was done, the deciding and the document under the same small smile. A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem came down the steps and named a figure for a pair. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord and stayed there while she decided, and the answer she gave came out of her mouth and not out of her thumb. Shifting a creel to reach a deeper eel showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey, the one that had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door. The knots on neither were counted by any thumb but her own, and the knot for the thin child upriver was not touched. Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and did not touch it. Orra watched from where she watched and got no answer out of this basket on this morning, as she had got none on the last market of Silt; the board lay where it lay and the knot-string lay beside it and nothing passed between them.

The boy, carried at the edge of the shade with the wool riding back at his wrist, watched the hand and not what the hand was said to mean. He had read hands all his life before he had read this one, and an instrument laid out on a towel had never told its own use either. Mira did not look into the basket. She had not looked into it on the twenty-ninth or the thirtieth of Silt, or on any morning since, and the not-looking was a keeping. He set it where it belonged and let nothing else be laid beside it yet.

\scenebreak

The steps below the bridge had come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line. The market went over them as it had gone over them for weeks, carefully and without remark: the buyer in the wet coat with his creel against his hip, a woman with a basket balanced high, a man with a barrow who took the landing at a small angle and went on down as though the angle were the road. Nobody made a fall of it. In the life that was not this one a bad step would have had a rail and a notice by noon and a man with a form to fill in; here a whole market agreed on the angle without a word spoken between them, and the agreement was entered nowhere and was not called an agreement.

Word came up from the river road that the weak board in \place{the plank walk} at the Weir was gone, or had been trodden flat by the flood and left under the silt, and that nobody had gone down to see which. A man with salt at his wrists said it to the eel boards and went on to his creels, and the eel boards took it the way a bench takes a hand laid on it: it bore the weight and gave back nothing. The boy set the gone board beside the gone step. He set beside them the hairlines that had run into the ice and stopped short of the edge in three places, entered in no account, three gaps in three places and no hand sent to any of them. A thing nobody has gone down to see is a thing nobody has made an accident of. The four low sounds in the world kept their four places and \place{the Singing Sill} kept its note under the fall, and none of them was made a fifth hearing.

The soft-cuffed clerk still sat at the landing with his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called. His cuff had gone grey with washing and been turned back twice, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every market, in the same order as a man might say a grace he no longer listened to. He did not speak and nobody asked him what he waited for. The boy filed the order on a tray of its own, apart from the grey-coated hand at the flare light, apart from the grey-fingered man who had found the way into a neighbour's forearm at the Drawing, apart from the neat upper line at the Court's table at the Cross. Four hands that reached for the record before the person, four places, four trays, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came to join them. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of a death; he knew the shape of that and had no word in which to say it and no standing from which to say it to anybody.

\scenebreak

Mira's not-looking was a keeping; \person{Berr Clay}'s not-looking at the block on the last market of Silt had been a pricing. The two faces had been turned the same way, toward the basket and not into it, and the turning had meant opposite things. He had once watched two men turn their faces from the same table in a room that was bright enough to hurt: one turned because he could not bear what lay on it, and one turned because he was deciding what the rest of it would cost. The faces had been alike and the acts were not, and here there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for either. He kept them in places that did not touch, the keeping on one tray and the pricing on another, with no cause written across the space between them, and if a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, then the only hand available was his own and it could not lift a thread from his chin.

The joke had no words in Grathic either. It lived in the exactness of the reading: he could tell a woman's smile was a latch from the way the eel-bone went into her pocket after the lie was decided and not spoken, and he could not lift his hand to his own mouth to wipe the drool off it. He let the joke have what it cost and went on keeping the count.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for ninety-three days, and the coal the brine-gull took upriver was entered in no slate and missed in none. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a market and a landing and a basket and a bed are not openings and no opening had come into this house on Seed 4; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself.

The carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees. \lore{Ash} had been worked into the creases of a man's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw and would go when it went. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word forty was unspoken, the two tellings of the shut room stayed on their tray joined to no kiln, the loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and below the sill a child's foot stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured and unnamed. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. The place at the end of the loaf row and the place at his ear lay in two rooms and did not touch.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety-three days. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. The boy was ninety-three days old and nobody had told him so, and the count stood at ninety-three, unspent, with the clerk's hand still going to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls and the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted and Mira's board lying beside Orra's and not touching.
