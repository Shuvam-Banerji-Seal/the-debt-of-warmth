The afternoon came into \place{the Clay Yard} without a mark of its coming, the way water goes over a stone without stopping to be counted. The light shifted off the basket and onto the stone lip of \place{the yard well}, and the women at \place{the drying racks} kept the work they had in their hands. \person{Corin} was carried at the edge of it, ninety-one days old and three months, under a fold that was not his and in a basket that was not his, with the cold iron of the handle two inches beyond his hand. He had watched seed go into the strips east of \place{Low Kiln} on a fist and a look and no figure spoken. Now the work had come back to the well, and the question came with it.

A woman with wet sleeves let the bucket hang on the rope and asked what the planting would cost, now that the seed was in the ground. She did not ask anyone in particular. She asked the yard the way people ask a pot, with the question left lying where it fell.

\person{Sella Vail} stood in the \place{the Vail kiln-house} doorway with the little-door burn shut on nothing. She moved her lips along the loaf shelf and did not say what her lips were counting. Loaves stood where loaves had stood, and at the end of the row was the place where one had stood and stood no longer, and she came back to that place twice the way a man comes back to a figure he does not trust, and gave it no figure at all. Her eyes went to the clay jar on the wall and stayed there one breath and came away. The jar had been fetched from a house three doors down and set on the shelf by the wall, and it was the house's, and nothing in it was entered against anybody.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. To the woman she said, “The house has said it.” And that was the whole of the answer.

The woman with the wet sleeves took it as an answer, because it was the kind of answer a yard can carry home. She let the bucket down. Sella gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody, as she gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to the woman who took it, and as she had given it to the wool that morning and never once to the basket. “Keep the silt out,” she said to the bucket. “The water came up thin twice and it'll not bear a hard hand.” The bucket went down on a slack rope and came up with its belly full and its lip level, and the woman set it on the stone with the care of a pot filled that must not be slopped, the water going quiet in it by degrees. Then Sella gave an instruction to the women, and after that to nobody, and to the person in the basket last if at all. A basket had no ears. A thing set down was a thing set down.

The boy wanted the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted. He had no voice to ask and no standing to be told, and there was nobody in the yard who would have known what the number meant if they had spoken it. In the life that was not this one he could have told the hour a burn was dressed and the term a debt had left to run, and both of those numbers had been useful and neither had been kind. Here he could not lift his hand to his own chin. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and he lay under a fold of wool with a crust beyond his reach and a drool freezing at his mouth. The joke had never once been funny enough to say aloud, and he had learned to find it funny without laughing, which cost less and kept the same blade in it.

He checked the file as a man runs a thumb down a row of instruments laid out on a towel, feeling for the one that has moved, though no instruments had been laid out and there was no thumb to run. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six — five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a wall, the district's own reading slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at the Drawing — and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman who had been warm that morning, from a person. The sixth lay beside the five and never among them. No seventh came into the yard with the bucket or went out of it with the question. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, neither hearings nor the third thing, and no hand had come to pay them. Three intervals stayed on another tray, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25, objects with the shape of sounds that had not been sounds. The word forty remained a saying on a place of its own, unspoken this afternoon, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both. It had been said once, low, by a woman who watched, and watching was not the same as telling, and the shape of a knot was not the name of one.

Four low things stood in four places: the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, the heard bell at the Cross, the early note out of the stone at \place{the Singing Sill}, and the gap under \lore{the kiln's breath} where the fifth pull should have been. The river's own note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. He had learned to keep the five things from becoming six by the simple expedient of not joining them, which was the only procedure in this country he could perform with his hands where they were. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their own tray joined to no kiln in Low Kiln, the likeness kept and the difference kept — breath drawn through the nose against breath made in the throat, both men saying \emph{not the war's hand} — and neither was turned into the other, though two men had now carried the same danger into the same yard and gone home with their bags and their road-mud, and one woman under this roof had lost a husband to it and told the fire it was the thaw. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. Below the sill a child's foot was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, wrapped in wool by one hand and gone past by a dozen, uncured and unnamed. He knew the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the knowing was exact and had the usefulness of an instrument held behind glass.

\person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill before the light and told the sill first and the yard after where it was. It lay there now where it could be checked, six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh never cut, nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, and never once turned along it. He had been sent down it once in \lore{the Long Cold}, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, with neither entered in any account that could be read.

No collector came.

From the wheel-shed came the low note of the wheel and a word spoken to a pot. \emph{You'll do}, the word said, and no figure came out of the shed onto the yard. A vessel gave its note past the door a moment after, whole and round enough that the boy felt the shape of it before he placed where it had come from, and Tarn's head did not turn and Tarn did not name the sound, and the boy did not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences kept their equality under one roof, and the sentence that would call the loan was not said. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and a man whose ear is worth more than his debt is a man a house might one day learn to count; but the yard went on with its buckets and the shed went on with its wheel, and nothing in either place had been joined to the thing under the roof that was not said.

Under the Vail kiln-house roof the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Sella told it it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of the broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for ninety-one days. The coal the brine-gull took upriver weeks back was entered in no slate and missed in none; one warmth less had gone out of the world with no account the poorer, and the planting month had come over it without noticing. Every warmth in the house had a payer and none of them stood in the room to be thanked, so the boy had learned to ask who paid before he asked what a thing was worth, which was a surgeon's question in a country that had no use for it.

She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. She had not touched it at the well and would not touch it at any bank before an opening that did not come, because a bucket and a question and a basket are not openings, and she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. The clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw and would go when it went. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the kiln's breath, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety-one days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. The woman at the well had put no figure to the place at the end of the loaf row, and nobody had put a name to the place at his ear, and the two places lay in two rooms and did not touch. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was unspoken, the two tellings stayed on their tray, the loan stayed uncalled, and the cold foot stayed the cold. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. The boy was ninety-one days old and nobody had told him so, and the count crossed into the evening of Seed 2 unspent.
