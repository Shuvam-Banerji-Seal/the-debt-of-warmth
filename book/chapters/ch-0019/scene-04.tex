The two sounds came into the dark under the roof as they had come for ninety-two days, the river’s low muttered note through the finger’s-width-open door and the carried fire’s gap through the floor, in two places that did not touch; and between them lay the equality the house kept without naming it, two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called. Something must give at the end of the seventh year. It had not given yet. Before the light went down into the yard, while the dark lay in the door’s width like water held behind a sluice, \person{Tarn} set the small bench knife on its sill and told the sill first and the yard after where it was. He told the empty yard the way another man tells the weather, with no listener and no need of one: it was back, it could be checked, it was where it had always been.

Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat, and the seventh had never been cut, nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. To the kindling he said it the way he said everything that cost him something: “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” The words went to the wood, and the wood took them and gave back nothing. He went on holding the breath he had decided not to spend, and his chest was already tight with it, the small muscles at the neck standing out under the skin in the dark. \person{Corin}, carried at the edge and looking up out of the fold, read the tightness as he read everything, by its shape and by what it cost. He had once laid a hand on a chest that held like that and known the man was keeping pain out of his breathing; here the keeping was of a breath, and the breath was a year. The joke had no words in \lore{Grathic}. It lived in the fact that he could read a man’s held breath across a yard and could not lift a hand to wipe his own chin.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of Tarn’s knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; there was grey in the nail-beds and along the fold of the thumb, the same grey the yard went around at the Vail corner and called the thaw. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, a small circle that had sat there since Y.H. 806 and had outlasted four of the six years he had counted. He did not look at it now. He put his hands to the broom.

He was ninety-two days old and three months and could not hold up his own head for the length of a sentence. The head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and a thread of drool had frozen at his mouth in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek and then temple and then the bare place at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward nothing and came down. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. He kept ninety-two exactly while the house went on keeping its days badly, in loaves pushed along a shelf and glances at a clay jar and a space at the row’s end nobody called a figure.

\scenebreak

By the time the light got into \place{the Clay Yard} the argument had started, as it started most mornings, about whether the thaw would come early or late. A man from the pits wanted it early, so the strips east of \place{Low Kiln} would want hands sooner and the work would be done before the levy; a woman at the tubs wanted it late and said a late thaw put off the paying and a body wanted its weeks. They put the case to the boards and the well and to nobody in particular, the way the yard put everything to the pot before it put it to a person.

Tarn said “Yes, house” to the man and “Yes, house” to the woman, and agreed so entirely with each that no argument could find a foot to stand on. He went on wanting the late one. It put off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went, and had stood there through the turn of the month and would stand there when the seed was in the ground. The boy set the two opinions on one tray as he set everything that would not settle, and beneath them the wanting, which was a third thing and was not entered.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Round it,” she said to the broom. “It cost enough to come in. It’ll cost more to take out.” Then she let Tarn take it, and gave no instruction at all to the basket. She went wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the new finer hairline of grey beside it, unswept and called the thaw that would go when it went, and around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it; nobody entered the space and nobody swept it. The yard went on around all three with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken. Seed was counted into a fist and let out again in looks, and the strips east of the town wanted hands and nobody said how many hands the strips wanted.

His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, and never once turned along it. The road went down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, and a man who had work in the yard could keep the road in the corner of his eye all morning without ever looking down its length. Tarn had been sent down it once, in \lore{the Long Cold}, with the ice at the ford and the river shut; he had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, with neither entered in any account that could be read. The boy, carried at the edge of the work, knew the shape of a sentence that had been told instead of done --- he had spent his whole second life beside people who handed a figure to a slate and took the speaker out of it --- and set it on a tray of its own with the seventh year, which was also kept and also not entered. No collector came down the road that morning. The yard went around the mud the flood had left, and the omission had no name and made no doctrine of itself.

\scenebreak

In the afternoon a vessel gave its note past the door.

It came from a woman carrying a pot across the yard, a sound with a full body to it, and it went past the open door and into the shed and out again the other side. Tarn’s head did not turn. He swept the stroke he had been sweeping, and he did not name the sound, and he did not supply a Grathic word for it, because there was a word for a pot and a word for a crack and no word that held both what the pot had said and what the man had not. From the wheel-shed came the low note of the wheel and a word to a pot, “You’ll do,” and no figure came out onto the yard; \person{Jorra Vail} was heard at the wheel and not seen. The boy did not call it a seventh hearing.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood in his file and stayed six: five from four sources, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman who had been warm that morning, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. The three intervals stayed on another, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25. The word forty was a saying with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and it was not spoken. Four low things stood in four places --- the told bell, the heard bell, the early note of \place{the Singing Sill}, the kiln’s gap --- with the river’s note of water going over water a fifth thing and not a hearing; and the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed on their tray joined to no kiln, likeness kept and difference kept, breath through the nose against breath made in the throat, both men saying \emph{not the war's hand}. Below the sill a child’s foot was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, wrapped in wool by one hand and gone past by a dozen, uncured and unnamed. He knew the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the knowing was exact and had the usefulness of an instrument kept behind glass.

The two ears under the roof kept their equality. An ear that could hear was worth more than the debt that held it, which made it dangerous and unsellable and worth keeping quiet; a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence that would call it had never been said under the roof where it was truest. Tarn kept the silence because it was the one thing he owned that the house had not entered, and the boy kept it beside his own because there was nobody to tell and no word in which to tell it. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; the term pressed against its edge all afternoon and did not give here.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down. She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for ninety-two days, and the coal the brine-gull took upriver was entered in no slate and missed in none. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because no opening had come and a held breath and an unforged sentence and a pot’s note past the door were not openings; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself.

The carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw and would go when it went. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. The small knife lay on its sill where it could be checked and the seventh year lay where it could not, and the household went on around both as it went around the Ash.

The door stood a finger’s width open. Through the gap came the river’s low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap held under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety-two days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. The place at the end of the loaf row and the place at his ear lay in two rooms and did not touch. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, the three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word was unspoken, the two tellings stayed on their tray, the loan stayed uncalled, and the cold foot stayed the cold. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. The boy was ninety-two days old and nobody had told him so, and the count stood at ninety-two, unspent, crossed into Seed 4 with the knife on its sill and the damper where a bent finger had left it.
