The market thinned the way an Account-day market thins, not at a stroke but one thing going after another: the eight pigs off the wet boards, the men who had laid them, the two hundred who had leaned in to \place{the reading block under the moot-post} and leaned back again, and after them the stalls that had been set before light. What was left went on selling over the place where the reading had stood the way water goes over a stone and does not stop to name it. \person{Sella Vail} lifted the basket out of \place{the moot-post's finger's width of shade} with the whole of her attention on its level, as if the thing to be got right were the balance of a pot on a shelf and not the figure that had been sent twice over its top. She put the fold right under his chin with the back of one knuckle, gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind --- “Keep it off his ears,” and “it's the river wind” --- and never to the basket, and went down toward \place{Low Kiln} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate.

She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of what had been read over the wicker. That was not a refusal to tell him. There was nobody under any roof who told a basket what a figure meant, and a story told to a child who could not answer back would have been a thing said to please the speaker; she was not that woman. She carried him as a pot is carried that must not be slopped, and the half-pace was the pace of a full pot crossed carefully through a room where somebody might have set down a slate and expected a number off it. \place{the clay road} went up behind her toward \place{High Kiln}, a direction with a box going away in it, and she did not look along it. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay two inches under the wool and did not make the direction into a road he was expected to walk; the distance between a road and an instruction to walk it was a distance he had learned to keep without being told.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the light had gone off \place{the terrace} and the yard stood at its last. She went wide of \place{the Vail corner} with a look and a step and no number, the clean dark line with its finer grey hairline called \lore{the thaw} lying unswept both, and came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. The order was the order of the house and had been the order for three hundred and forty-nine days: fire first, basket after. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and the small flat sound of the pot's back on the ash was the first honest sound in the room, because it came from a thing being moved and not from a thing being said.

“The house will keep it,” she said to the fire, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so that the charcoal tally beneath its front edge stayed unread; her lips moved once along a row with two gaps in it and figured neither; she glanced at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. Then she lifted the twice-mended pot from \place{the pot board} with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” and “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” The hairline of the original break showed dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hid the hand that had handled it; a mended thing left that hand showing, and no amount of kindness under this roof would rub the line out.

She turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than the touch before, and her fingers went in and came out without closing, so no handful was lifted. Whatever arithmetic had gone up the road that morning in a locked box would not know what the heap had cost, and the space between that figure and its price had no signature; that was not a thing a kitchen could mend by looking at it. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing. He took it apart as he took everything apart, by what was there and what was not: a hand that set a pot down, a hand that did not bring it nearer, no look toward the child, no giver and no gift. A gift wants a giver and nobody had given.

She carried him to the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf and set him under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. There was a sound above and behind --- a foot, a board, the house going on with its own business --- and no interior was entered and no name given to the sound. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped. Nobody under any roof in \place{Grath} had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched: a going of the light and a bed are not openings; six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on \place{Ash} 2 without a name, and no seventh made due.

The crust at his ear had gone stiffer and browner in the dry kiln heat and stood two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third; cold had come cheek, temple and the small bare joint where the wool rode back and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, and a hand to close on the pot handle that stood beyond his reach. Both were true, the second louder, neither won. The argument was the work.

\scenebreak

What had changed in the district that morning was not in the room and never would be. \person[Berr Clay] had gone up \place{the clay road} with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat and the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner, and under the hot clay of him the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. The narrow slate lay under his arm with the writing inward; the box was locked; the key went up in a coat pocket and did not come back down. Inside the box rode Ash's second true subtraction beside the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone. Eight arithmetics of the year now stood behind one printed figure. Eight were eight and not one account.

The printed figure stood where it had stood all year: one in twelve, unchallenged. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Two hundred people had stood where the arithmetic changed and could not be changed back, and the boy had lain under a slate while it happened and had not been asked and had not asked. He had watched the order of the thing --- wipe, wipe, slate to the forearm, finger before eyes, the extra breath at the end, the speaker taken out of his own sentence --- the way a surgeon watches a field being prepared, and had taken away no instrument and left no mark. There had been no hand put to the wicker, no household named under the figure, no payer standing. Only a box going away, and a man who believed he had done a kindness. It was the absence of anger in the man that made the field hard to read; a man with a good reason and clean hands is harder to argue with than a man with a knife.

He filed the finding on a place of its own with no payer. He did not lay it across the house's not-doings: not across the two unswept lines at the Vail corner, not across the widow's mark untouched since Ash 2, not across the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf kept unread beneath a finger, not across the fifth pull of \lore{the carried fire} left unsupplied under the floor, not across the loan of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} kept in objects on this roof and not in a story --- salt-stiff coat on its peg, hollow wheel-seat in the shed, twice-mended pot with the hairline showing, unforged collector sentence at the kindling --- whose name was unspoken the same three hundred and forty-nine days as the fire's missing fifth, not across the crust in the fold not lifted. He did not lay it across the dressed grain lying covered under wool with its empty fingers, nor across the five held objects on their five places, nor across the pot-crack resting of Ash 9, nor across the held breath at the knife-sill from Ash 14 which had been checked that evening and found where it was put and not made again, nor across the Ash 5 joining of the burned slate to the shut room, nor across the Ash 17 direction that stood beside that joining as a further shaping, nor across the fourth shaping of the well-saying, nor across the turned writing that had gone up the road on Ash 6 turned to its carrier's sleeve, nor across the strip of notched birch bark in a sleeve that belonged to somebody else, nor across his own kept gaps.

The edges came near to touching. They came so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt and set a payer under both: eight readings beside five not-doings, a locked box beside a covered heap, a twice-told figure beside a missing fifth, a burned slate beside a shut room, a hand that did not come back out of a strip beside a hand that went up an inch and came down. The finger went between. It was the same finger that went between every pair on the towel --- not a decision, not a doctrine, only the placing of a small clean interval where an interval was due. He had learned that a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Eight readings were eight readings and not a rule, and no doctrine stood up out of them, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Nothing in the reading answered the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, or the man carried out on Day 188 with the cost entered nowhere, or the dressed grain under wool read over by a figure that did not know what it cost, or the carried fire's missing fifth, or the word that had gone up \place{the terrace} on the seventeenth, or the room shut with \lore{the damper} closed. There was no answer in the box and no answer in the crowd that had gone back to the pigs. The three counts stayed in their three places and none rose into another: the carried fire's four low pulls and its silence under the floor, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-nine days; overhead \place{the boards} keeping four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; the river's \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, which was never numbered among the hearings. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. The heard bell --- four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} with nobody crossing himself --- stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside that unforged sentence, and the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. He passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching what little light there was and giving it back, and the hand went away when the checking was done. The held breath he had left at the sill on Ash 14 was where it had been put: checked again at the going of this light, found, and not made again, with a finger between it and the pot-crack resting and no payer standing under it. He told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” cutting nothing, counting nothing, calling no loan, in the tone of a man stating the weather. His ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of the reach and the loan stayed uncalled. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed on the flagstone's own place with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 beat and the Frost 5 beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. He said nothing about eight arithmetics going up the road, and wanted the late thaw still because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing with the early and the late so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. Ash stood worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low; his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \place{Grathic} word for both mercies. He had grown quieter since the direction went over the boards on the seventeenth, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order from anybody. The burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no sixth; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and the claim went on gathering interest against it. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. He did not look toward the bed.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found: eight entries set down on eight places and not averaged into one account, every gap left gap, every instrument laid back where the next hand would find it without looking. It was a cold satisfaction and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that smelled of disinfectant and had since reduced none, and could not lift a crust two inches; the joke arrived on time and was not funny and cost the same. He did not spend the eight readings on himself. He did not make them a lesson, a grievance, a sign or a power. They waited.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under \place{the finger's-width door}, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly with the whole of the attention and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch its, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; the note's own finishing ended the interval and not any decision of the hand.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-four: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-four honest answers stood on \place{the towel} at the bed's head with every gap left gap, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and forty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and sixty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twentieth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road since the slate went up before noon, and no collector had come up or down the clay road; there had been a market and a Cross and a bell, and they belonged to the twentieth and not to any day that had not earned them. Ash kept its twentieth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood ten mornings ahead, named only as a date, and he did not spend it in a night that had not earned it.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-four; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap and the eight arithmetics held behind one unsigned figure on a place of their own with no payer; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; and the river went water over water under the finger's-width door.
