\daymark{349}{Ash 20, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

The twentieth came in the dark before first light, and it came earned. There would be a market at \place{the Cross} on the twentieth and a slate read under \place{the moot-post} and a bell, and none of those things had stood on the nineteenth, so none of them could be carried back into a finished day to make a lesson of them. Three hundred and forty-eight had closed exact behind him; three hundred and forty-nine had opened and would stand exact at the going of the light, six months and one hundred and sixty-nine days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor of \place{the Vail kiln-house} \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name the same number of days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had been unspoken the same number. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and took the dark as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under \place{the finger's-width door}. The crust at his ear had gone stiffer and browner in the dry kiln heat and stood two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in the district had ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold had come cheek, temple and the small bare joint where the wool rode back and stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, and the pink that comes back to the surface while the core stays cold, and that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, and a hand closed on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true; the second was louder; neither won. The argument between them was the work, as it was every morning, and he had long ago stopped expecting a verdict.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down before first light at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the basket level under her arm and the instruction going where it always went --- to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire that would be banked while the house was empty --- and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, and “it’s the river wind,” to the wind, and there was no I in either sentence and no instruction for the thing in the wicker. The half-pace was not slowness but a measurement, the balance a tray is balanced with across a room where somebody might have set down a slate and expected a figure off it. The way down was the way down: the flagstones cold enough to take the warmth out of a sole through a shoe, \place{the pot board} passed without a look, the door on its finger of open air, and the river wind coming up off the sill with the taste of iron in it. A few stalls were already set along the Cross, and men were laying boards for the pigs, and the boards were wet, and the two hundred had not yet come but the shape of the two hundred was already there in the way the goers-by left room at the block.

At \place{the reading block under the moot-post} she set the basket down in \place{the moot-post's finger's width of shade} where no slate lay, the way a pot is set down out of the draught, level, with the whole of its weight on the flat of the ground. The shade was a thin thing, no wider than a finger, and it lay where the post's own edge cut the light; she chose it as she chose the cool side of the hearthstone, without thinking about choosing. His cheek took the cold iron of the post through the basket's wicker, and the iron was colder than the river wind, and the iron did not give. She put the fold right under his chin without looking into the basket, straightened the wool with the back of one knuckle, and went half a pace off and stood there through everything that followed. Half a pace was far enough that a man could read a figure over him and near enough that the house could answer for him if a hand were laid on the wicker. She did not look into the basket then, and she did not look into it at any hour of the reading, and if anybody had asked her why she would have told them to ask the pot.

By the time \person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} the two hundred had leaned in to the block and were leaning back again, and eight pigs had gone over the wet boards without anybody counting them aloud. The pigs went over with the small clumsy scrambling sound pigs make on boards, and a man with a pole kept their heads down, and the market went on over the noise as water goes over a stone. He came up short the way he always came up short, stopping before he reached the block so that the crowd had to turn to him, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one corner of his mouth. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath: a man who smells of nothing has had the sweating and the dust and the ordinary evidence of a body taken off him by somebody whose accounts are neat. He did not raise his voice. The crowd leaned in to hear him, which was why he never raised it.

His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward, and passed a finger over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. The boy watched the order of it from two inches of air: the wipe, the wipe, the slate kept to the arm, the finger before the eyes, the extra breath at the end. It was the order of a man approaching a field and exposing only what he intended to cut, and it did not vary, and nothing in it was an accident of manner that could be taken for a weakness. A surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and does not shake the hand afterwards where anybody can see it. Berr took himself out of his sentences in the same way.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said low into the goers-by. He sent the figure twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the late households, and his eyes passed across the top of the wicker and did not go into it. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.” To a man who had pushed a number up out of a fist and held it there, he said, “If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch” --- your house, never I, the house standing in for the man who had spoken and the man who had signed. The crowd took the figure the way a crowd takes a figure, with a sound like a held breath let go in many places at once, and went back to the pigs.

He said something more to the block, low, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs: kindness was entered, he said, or a kindness had been done and entered, the words too small to carry past the block and the post's cold iron. The block answered nothing. It was a block and had no answer in it, and he had not expected one, and he went off up the clay road with the cord still folded square under his coat and the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, \place{Ash}'s second true subtraction riding inside it beside the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone. Eight arithmetics of the year now stood behind one printed figure. He believed he had done a kindness. There was no anger in his face and no triumph in it and nothing a crowd could have fastened on if it had wanted to, and by the time the pigs had been weighed he was a direction up the road and not a man standing anywhere to be asked.

The bell went through the market, four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself. The gap was where a fifth stroke would have stood and no fifth stroke came, and the bell went on being heard after the sound of it had gone, the way a cold hand goes on being cold after it has left iron. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, not folded into the three counts and not made a seventh of \lore{the full note}. The three counts stayed in their three places --- the carried fire's four and its silence under the floor, the boards back under the roof with four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not, and the river's \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, which was never numbered among the hearings. Agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

\scenebreak

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road toward \place{Greybank}, and took it at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. The second strap was let out for the long stretch and she settled it without appearing to settle it; the dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for the road, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. The slate lay edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd, and the small flat correction of the strap came before both, and her mouth opened after them when there was a question to answer. To a woman by the rail who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the woman who wanted more she said, “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” and went on, leaving the rest of the conversation standing where it had been set down, without rudeness and without apology. No figure came off the slate. She was a runner and not a reader, and the eighth order of reaching stood where it had stood, eight with no tenth, on a place of its own and joined to none --- not made a reader by the fourth shaping of the well-saying, not a witness, not the speaker of any voice that went over \place{the boards}, not the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out.

Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called, his slate turned inward and his hand to the slate before his eye to the stalls, the cuff grey from washing and the grey of it worked into the seam at the wrist. The market sold over him as water over a stone, and nobody took him off, and he did not take himself off, and a measure nobody had called stayed unentered in an inward slate that nobody read. He was a shape in the boy's file on a place of its own, and nine record-first hands were nine and not a rule, and no tenth came on the twentieth any more than it had come on the nineteenth.

At the pot board a silent woman with a green at one corner of her mouth turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the colour staying a colour and nothing coming off it. At \place{the market's edge} a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver and went dull again, the sliver staying a shape and nothing coming off it. Each was a seventh with no eighth, unnamed, not joined to Berr's green or to each other, not joined to a loaf, a slate, a sale, a hearing, a cause or a romance, and no second look was given to either. The foundling's small half-turn went where it went and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; the colour and the shape waited on their own places for a hand not his and not yet come.

The boy took the reading as a surgeon takes a field. There was a direction up the road, a figure said twice, a thumb wiped twice before nothing was touched, a finger passed over writing, a sentence with its speaker taken out of it, a box going away locked with a subtraction inside that would change the district's arithmetic where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back. There was not a signature on the space between the figure and the price, not a household named under the figure, not a hand put out to the wicker, not a payer standing under any of it. He had been carried under a slate all morning and no figure had known his name and he had not asked one to. He filed nothing yet, because the finding was not his to spend on a morning that had earned only its own events, and he did not pull the ninth arithmetic into the twentieth any more than he had pulled the twentieth into the nineteenth. Eight were eight and not one account; an eighth stood offstage nowhere and unnamed, a date at the end of a month that had not yet earned it. The crowd had leaned in and gone back to the pigs. The quiet exactness of the field he had read was the whole of what he took away, and it was worth nothing at two inches of air, and that was its honesty.

\scenebreak

In the small hours of the reading night the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under \place{the finger's-width door}, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch its, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note's own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; the note's own finishing ended the interval and not any decision of the hand.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Nothing in the reading answered the hand that went in on Seed 15, the man carried out on Day 188, the dressed grain under wool, the carried fire's missing fifth, the word that had gone up \place{the terrace} on the seventeenth or the room shut with the damper closed. No doctrine stood up out of eight readings, and the eight waited on their own places with a finger between every pair for a hand not his and not yet come. The infant underneath took none of the surgeon's satisfaction and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle; both were true, the second louder, neither winning.

Three hundred and forty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and sixty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twentieth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road; there had been a market and a Cross and a bell, and they belonged to the twentieth and not to the nineteenth. \place{Ash} kept its twentieth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood ten mornings ahead, named only as a date, and he did not spend it in a night that had not earned it.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-three; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; Berr was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the sweet green at his mouth-corner, the clerk still set at the landing with his slate turned inward, and the bell's four, four and a gap stayed in its square beside the told bell while the river went water over water through the finger's-width door.
