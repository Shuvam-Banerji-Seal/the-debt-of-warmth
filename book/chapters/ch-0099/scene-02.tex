\person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down to \place{the Clay Yard} in the afternoon with the basket held level and the instruction going where it always went --- to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire that would be banked when they came back --- and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool; “it’s the river wind,” to the wind, and there was no I in either sentence and no instruction for the thing in the wicker. The half-pace she kept from every open slate was not slowness but a measurement, a basket balanced the way a tray is balanced across a room where somebody might have set down a slate and expected a figure off it. He went under her arm with his cheek out of the wind, and the cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and stayed frozen; nobody under any roof in the district had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Behind them, under the floor of the house, \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Three hundred and forty-eight days stood behind him exact and unspent, six months and one hundred and sixty-eight days told by nobody, and would stand exact at the going of the light. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted. That was not the same as not wanting it.

The yard lay open under a sky the colour of wet ash, and the afternoon had laid its thin light across the rows of pots standing with their bellies full of it, each holding its small private cold until a hand came to take it. She went around the place as she went around it every day, with a look and a wide step and no number. At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line of the old \place{Ash} ridge lay where it lay, and beside it the finer grey hairline that somebody had called \lore{the thaw} lay where that lay too, unswept both, a line left where it is being a line kept. Under \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay in their own dark and were entered by nobody, and she went wide around them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The gone pan had left a round ring in the dust with no scrape in it, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place{the trough} had a skin on it that would break under a pail and close again; out on \place{the worked strips} was the field’s empty place, a space in the row where nothing had been set and nobody had supplied a thing to set there. On \place{the flat stone by the well} the silt-clouded plate of ice stood out of the path of feet, and in the returning line, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, the yard went around a place that had no figure and no name and never acquired one. Seven places, gone around with a look and a wide step. He entered none of them, because a thing entered wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Three coverings stayed weather, entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair and no fourth likeness made a cause: the gone white frost on the north lip of the well, the skin on the trough that broke and closed, the plate of ice lifting its clouded face a little out of the path. Each was a covering and each was weather, and the three were three, and if a man had made them one thing he would have had to put a name under it, and no name stood.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay under wool away from \place{the hearth}. She lifted a corner with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, turned the heap once and gave no figure. Her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer. Whatever arithmetic the morning would put upon the heap would not know what it cost; the dressed grain would stand under a figure that had never felt the weight of it, and the space would have no signature.

The yard’s hunts and repairs came and went as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and never as figures. A man down from Cold Acre shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month’s mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came. Two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early, and said it would be late, and \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to the one and “Yes, house” to the other, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

At \place{the yard well} \person{Ilva} drew with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Ash cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and people noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A woman waiting her turn, with a pot on her hip and her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Ash’s firings had taken.

Ilva’s hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, to the cord, to the knot-string, or to any saying that had arrived nowhere. No figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat in the gap between the looking and the word. “Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.” She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

He took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart, by what was there and by what was not: the hand to the record before the eyes to the person, the eyes before the mouth, the half-beat in the interval where a lesser observer would have put an intention. He filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand in the district, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}’s eighth. He filed no tenth, made no figure of the slate’s nearness, made her no reader of her own keeping and joined her to none. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice; her well gave no witness, no fifth shaping came, and the direction that had gone over the boards on the seventeenth stayed on its own place beside the Ash 5 joining with a finger between, advanced and not paid.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, beside \person[Garl Vail]’s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. He passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” He checked the thumb’s resting of Bone 26 and found it where it had been put and did not make it again. He checked the held breath he had left at the sill on Ash 14 and found it where it had been put too: hand to the sill, over the knife as the house checks, away when the checking was done, no breath held at the top of the reach. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. It was not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not a sixth held object, not made again, not joined to the handful, the thumb’s resting, the pot-crack resting or any other keeping, and there was no payer standing under it. His chest had been tight since the dark, and Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it.

His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check. The returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed on the flagstone’s own place with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 beat and the Frost 5 beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule; neither the handful nor the thumb’s resting nor the pot-crack resting nor the held breath at the sill had been made a fifth. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there --- a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach, the loan uncalled.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day’s meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said, and told a pot “You’ll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any \place{High Kiln} master who would call it early and make a song of it; the knowing stayed on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair’s breadth and was left standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. \emph{“He’s not Vail”} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy’s name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn; the claim went on gathering interest against it.

The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing. The shut-room tellings stood at two and the row at five askings with no sixth, and no ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} this afternoon to make a sixth telling or a third shut-room telling; the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 direction stayed held apart, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, and no sixth was made due on a day that had not earned one.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace, wide around the seven places, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it. At the hearth she told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and to nobody else, and left the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. \lore{The widow’s mark} hung where it hung and was not touched; a going of the light and a bed are not openings, and six openings in six months are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash and no seventh made due.

The yard’s work had appeared to be mere repetition --- seven places gone around, the same grain turned under wool, the same six kept where the house could check --- and then the three keepings stood up in his attention and would not lie down together. The held breath at the knife-sill was a thing Tarn kept that the house could not enter; the hand going toward the sleeve and staying was a thing Ilva kept that no slate could take a figure from; the bent finger standing in the cold iron was a thing Jorra kept that had no word in the district’s speech for the two bites of it. Three people, three small things, each kept where no house could enter. His noticing found an equality among them and refused to turn the equality into a cause, because a cause wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have laid them across one another and called the touching a debt, and the finger went between.

He turned out the file as a surgeon turns out instruments on a towel, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else. Seven arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure, seven and not one account; the figure stood one in twelve, unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid, with no signature on the space between. Ash’s first rode locked and unread up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}’s box toward High Kiln. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across the house’s \lore{not-doings}, the covered grain, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction or his own kept gaps. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, not folded into the three counts. Under the floor the carried fire kept four and a gap; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger’s-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river’s note under the door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch its, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note’s own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note’s own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-two: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell’s gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb’s resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle set within reach. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the nineteenth. Three hundred and forty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and sixty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire’s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the nineteenth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road; there was no market, no Cross and no bell. Ash kept its nineteenth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district’s two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. The twentieth stood one morning ahead, named only as a date, and he did not spend it in an afternoon that had not earned it.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-two; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; and under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-eight days.
