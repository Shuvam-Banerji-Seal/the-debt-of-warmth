\daymark{350}{Ash 21, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and seventy-fourth answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist worth speaking of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and had since learned the exact weight of a barley crust at two inches. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Corin]{the boy} lay awake on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under \place{the finger's-width door}. Three hundred and forty-nine had stood exact behind him; three hundred and fifty had begun and would stand exact at the going of the light, six months and one hundred and seventy days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for the same number of days. On its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and fifty days.

\place{Ash}'s second arithmetic had gone up the road the day before in a locked box and stood behind the printed figure with the rest. Eight arithmetics of the year now stood behind one figure: the three of \place{Frost}, the first, second and third of \place{Bone}, Ash's first and Ash's second. Eight were eight and not one account. The box had gone and the key had gone up in a coat pocket and did not come back down, and nothing inside it had been read under this roof. It bought no lesson here and answered none of the held questions. Nothing from the twentieth crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment, and he did not go back into a finished day to fetch a meaning out of it. A day lived is a day set down.

Ash kept its twenty-first day without a boundary drawn. It was a Firstday and there would be no market, no Cross and no bell, and no collector up or down \place{the clay road}. The going of the light would come as an ordinary going of the light, measured in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. Neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- would be set right or left wrong in so many words this morning; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. There was nobody under this roof who could have told him what the number counted, and he had given up asking without ever quite giving up the want of an answer.

The crust lay two inches from his ear in the dry kiln heat, gone stiffer and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; no third had come in all the days behind to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease that is no ease, the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile. A cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes; and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. The knowing arrived in the old order, the way instruments arrive on a towel, and he took it because it was there and because there was nothing else in the room to take.

He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set within his reach. Both were true, the second was louder, and neither won. The surgeon's register took the small hours in its old order; the infant underneath went on being cold and made his wants known without words, which was the whole of his vocabulary and the whole of his power. A man who had reduced a thousand shoulders could not lift a barley crust two inches, could not wipe his own mouth, could not keep a fold of wool at his own chin. He played it for its exactness and not for pity; there was no pity in it worth the name, only the dry joke that lived in the discrepancy, and the joke cost him nothing because he was the only one in the room who knew there was a joke. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict. It was the work, and he did it.

\person{Sella Vail} came in before the basket and went to \place{the hearth} before she went to him. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and, under them, the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which had not been swept in two years. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear; the small flat sound of it went across the room and was the first honest thing said in it. “The house will keep it,” she said, in the tone of a woman setting a cup where a cup belongs, and another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting small intake, hold and letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure: the coals took breath, held it, let it go, and the account she kept of it was written nowhere and was not an account.

“Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she said to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light and went out again.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man who had first said them, which did not stop them being true. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing; repair stays apart from restoration, because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing.

Near the hearth the dressed grain lay under wool. She lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the touch before, turned the heap once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer. She set the pot rim down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed.

He took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand's intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given. He wanted the rim under his fingers and got the space where it would have been, and the space was not entered. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. Letting it lie was not the same as forgetting it was there; she had never once been given an instruction about the thread, and she had never once given one.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched. A morning, a hearth and a bed are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months --- Tallow, Ember, Smoke, \place{Frost}, \place{Bone} and Ash --- are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash without a name and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this morning for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire's missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. Eight arithmetics of the year --- the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash's first and Ash's second --- stood behind one printed figure, eight and not one account. The figure stood one in twelve, unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between. Both of Ash's subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the covered grain, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt, and the finger went between. Eight readings were eight readings and not a rule, and no doctrine stood up out of them, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Nothing in the day before had answered the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, or the man carried out on Day 188 with the cost entered nowhere, or the dressed grain lying under wool read over by a figure that did not know what it cost, or the carried fire's missing fifth, or the word that had gone up \place{the terrace}, or the room shut with \lore{the damper} closed. Two hundred people had stood where the arithmetic changed and could not be changed back, and the boy had lain under a slate while it happened and had not been asked and had not asked. He did not set the reading and the keeping of this morning under one cause, for a cause is an account with a payer standing under it and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell --- four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} with nobody crossing himself --- stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of the full note.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. The private tray was checked and left as it lay: nine record-first hands with no tenth, nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the row at five askings with one shaping and no sixth, the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 direction held apart with a finger between every pair, none spent, named, joined, promoted into a witness or a rule. The loan stayed uncalled and the Seed 16 breath unspent. The far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

Three hundred and fifty was not a round number anybody under this roof would speak and not a threshold; it had no more shape than any other day in the row. He had long since stopped expecting to be told what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. Ash 30 stood nine mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing or sixth held object invented to make the row come out even.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the note's own finishing, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it --- eight entries set down on eight places and not averaged into one account, every gap left gap, every instrument laid back where the next hand would find it. It was a cold satisfaction and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-first. He did not spend the eight readings on himself, did not make them a lesson, a grievance, a sign or a power. They waited.

Three hundred and fifty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twenty-first had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or down the clay road, and there was no market, no Cross and no bell. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-five; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; and through the finger's-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 30 stood nine mornings ahead and was named only as a date. Forward, not closed.
