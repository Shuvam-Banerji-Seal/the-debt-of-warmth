\daymark{353}{Ash 24, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty, and the dark went on holding the room the way a lid holds a pot that has been set down to cool. At the very end of the twenty-third it had come up an inch, taken its small intake, held the interval whole on the river's own finishing and come straight down; now it rested with the backs of the fingers against the wool and the thumb a little apart, a tool put away where the next hand will find it without looking. Nothing in the room had moved in the interval. The coals kept their small dry settling under the ash; the wool breathed at his ear; a dry tick came out of the cooling clay at the same place in the wall and at the same point in the silence as on the two nights before, and under all of it the river kept its low note, a thing so constant that at first it was not a sound at all but the absence of one. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and read the room the way a trauma bay is read before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings for want of a source standing. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

It was the twenty-fourth of the dark month and no boundary had been drawn around the change. The day had not announced itself; days under this roof never announced themselves, but arrived the way heat arrives in a pot set near a fire, by degrees and without asking anybody's leave. Three hundred and fifty-two had stood exact at the going of the light; three hundred and fifty-three stood exact now, in the small hours, and told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}. Six months and one hundred and seventy-two days had become six months and one hundred and seventy-three. The carried fire's fifth was unpaid three hundred and fifty-three days and its payer had no name. On its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and fifty-three days, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. Three hundred and fifty-three was not a round number and not a threshold. It was one more in a row nobody counted aloud, and he had long ago stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it.

The file lay where it had been laid and he did not open it again to see if it had changed. A thing checked and found exact is not checked a second time to make sure it is still exact; the second checking is where habits come from, and habits want a payer. Two hundred and eighty honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power. Eight arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure --- the three of the first frost-month, the first, second and third of the hunting month, and the first two of \place{Ash} --- and both of the dark month's subtractions rode locked and unread up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. One in twelve stood printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Eight were eight and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth: \person{Ilva}'s strip stayed unread in her sleeve and \person{Hessa Rale}'s writing stayed turned toward herself and unread on the road. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the loan was uncalled. Five held objects lay on their own places, the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that, with a finger between every pair and no sixth and no seventh. The burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at four shapings. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood apart on their own places with a finger between every pair, the last of them come over these boards on the twenty-third from the direction of \place{Slate Row}, standing nowhere to be asked, still gathering and still unspent, with no witness, no sixth telling, no third shut-room telling, no sixth asking and no fifth shaping. Ash 30 stood six mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The surgeon's register took the body in its old order, as a wound is taken in the order the layers lie, and found it altered only by the length of the cold. The crust came first: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself, and no third had come in these three days to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Low Kiln, in Sill, in Grath, had ever been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, and the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile, and the pink that comes back to a surface while the core is still cold. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it and he could not lift his own head.

Under the knowing the infant wanted, and the wanting was louder. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. He wanted the thread wiped. He wanted the hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work. The body humiliated him in its exact small ways: the drool frozen with no thumb to wipe it, the head useless on the fold, the crust gone leathery past the point any child would put teeth to it, the handle two inches beyond a hand that had once closed on clamps in the dark. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and he could not turn over. He had held pressure in a cavity with both hands and now he could not keep a blanket on. The joke lived in that and cost him nothing and was not, in the end, funny.

Before first light \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth}, and the fire came before the basket. Only one set of feet had crossed the floor; the yard had not yet begun its work and no other person came under the roof. Her forearms were the colour of dried \lore{slip}, and the grey streak at her temple showed where the lamplight touched it; she smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under that the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which she had not swept in two years and did not intend to sweep. Her hand went to the coals first. She told them it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and the fire and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool; “it's the river wind,” to the wind. To the pot she gave a practical sentence and to the coals another, and to the thing in the wicker she gave nothing at all.

She stood a moment over the coals counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, the cord of her widow's mark oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted. She left the hairline showing, so that repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Near the hearth she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave.

The boy took the setting apart by what was there and by what was not: a hand, a pot, a rim, a pause; no figure spoken, no look toward the bed, no instruction to the basket. He filed the pause where it belonged and made no more of it. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the sixth stood behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash, held without a name and not spent, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire's missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected. None of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The last tend of the dark month put its cold against the door as the light came, entering nowhere. Out in the yard the skin on \place{the trough} had thickened to where it would hold a poured spoon; he knew it from the going of the light and from the small dry report the wind made of it at the door, and left it where it lay, a covering and weather, with no figure taken off it and no fourth likeness made a cause. Under the finger's-width door the river's note came lower than it had come at the close of the twenty-third, water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, and the carried fire under the floor kept four and a gap. Behind the wall the fired kiln stood cold from its last firing and nobody went out to look at it; the cold had gone into the brick and stayed there the way water stays in a pot set down. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or gone down the clay road; there was no market, no \place{the Cross} and no bell. Ash kept its twenty-fourth day without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse.

He turned out the whole file as a surgeon turns out instruments on \place{the towel}, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else, checked and left as it lay. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources, and the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it. The heard bell --- four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross with nobody crossing himself --- stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between, and eight arithmetics stood behind it, eight and not one account, both Ash subtractions locked and unread up the clay road. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row at five askings, the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, none spent, named, joined, promoted into a witness, a sixth telling, a third telling of the shut room, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a hearing, a cause, a payment or a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The edges came near to touching --- near to Ilva's unread strip, to Hessa Rale's turned writing, to Berr Clay's locked box, to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate, to the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf, to the covered grain, to the handful let fall at the rack end --- and the finger went between every pair. No voice came over the boards on the twenty-fourth. The direction of the twenty-third stood where it had been put and did not repeat, and bought no lesson under this roof. The far-off hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

The last clean morning of Ash arrived with nothing due and nothing answered, and the keeping went on because there was nothing else for it to do. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The absence of change was itself an exact result: a day that offered no sixth telling of the burned slate, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth asking of the row, no fifth shaping of the well-saying, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no seventh opening, no ninth arithmetic and no payer standing under anything, and had offered none because none had been earned. The infant underneath took none of the satisfaction and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle; both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-fourth. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Nothing on this day had yet been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch its, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and eighty-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and eighty-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and fifty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Ash 30 stood six mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping invented to make a row come out even. Three counts in three places did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood; the bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-one; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third come in these three days to make the row come out even; the thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped. Through the finger's-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Forward, not closed.
