## Chronicle

Ash kept its last three days as it had before — without a boundary drawn, one tending day, an ordinary day, another, each lived in full and none spent to make a lesson of the next. Under the Vail roof three mornings and three afternoons came and went with no market, Cross or bell and no collector on the clay road. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I. She went wide around seven places with a look and no number, turned the dressed grain under wool without a handful, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first at the wire and left the hairline showing, and set the rim two inches out with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. At the well Ilva answered *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, hand to sleeve and the notched wood unread. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, and stopped short of the calling word. Jorra spoke to the wheel before any person and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished.

On the twenty-third a word came over the boards from the direction of Slate Row — a room shut with the damper closed, a slate burned over a man nobody named, and, lower, that the room was shut against the cold and the slate burned so a count would not go up. No room was named, no hand stood at any flame, no figure, no date, nobody to be asked. Weather answered first, a wrong wind off High Kiln carrying the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards to the fold; the price of barley answered after it and the word went up the terrace. The boy set it on a place of its own beside the joining of Ash 5 and the direction of Ash 17, a finger between every pair: a further shaping, not a sixth telling of the burned slate, not a third telling of the shut room, not a witness, not a payment; nothing under that roof was spent on it and no payer stood. On the twenty-fourth it did not come again.

Each night the hand went up an inch, took a small intake, held it whole on the river's finishing and came straight down — answers 277 through 282, exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. Two hundred and eighty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, objects and not powers. Three hundred and fifty-three days stood exact and spoken by nobody; the fire kept four pulls and a gap, the boards four, four and a knot, the river low over lower. What the days cost was attention, which no institution can tax, and the keeping of a number nobody speaks.
