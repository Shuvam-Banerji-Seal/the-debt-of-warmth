\daymark{352}{Ash 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-eight, and the dark went on holding the room in place the way a clamp holds a wound until somebody comes with a dressing. Nothing in the room had moved in the interval. The coals kept their small dry settling under the ash; the wool breathed at his ear; a tick came out of the cooling clay at the same place in the wall and at the same point in the silence as it had the night before, and under all of it the river kept its low note, a thing so constant that at first it was not a sound at all but the absence of one. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and read the room as a trauma bay is read before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather.

It was the twenty-third of the dark month and no boundary had been drawn around the change. The day had not announced itself. Days under this roof did not announce themselves; they arrived the way heat arrives in a pot set near a fire, by degrees and without asking anybody's leave. Three hundred and fifty-one had stood exact at the going of the light; three hundred and fifty-two stood exact now, in the small hours, and told by nobody. Six months and one hundred and seventy-one days had become six months and one hundred and seventy-two. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name these three hundred and fifty-two days. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings for want of a source standing. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

The file lay where it had been laid and he did not open it again to see if it had changed. A thing checked and found exact is not checked a second time to make sure it is still exact; the second checking is where habits come from, and habits want a payer. Two hundred and seventy-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power. Eight arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure --- the three of the first frost-month, the first, second and third of the hunting month, and the first two of \place{Ash} --- and both of the dark month's subtractions rode locked and unread up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. One in twelve stood printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Eight were eight and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth; \person{Ilva}'s strip stayed unread in her sleeve and \person{Hessa Rale}'s writing stayed turned toward herself and unread on the road; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the loan was uncalled. Five held objects lay on their own places, the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that, with a finger between every pair and no sixth and no seventh. The burned slate stood at five; the shut-room tellings at two; the row at five askings with no witness; the well-saying at four shapings. The Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 direction stood apart on their own places with a finger between, advanced and not paid, gathering toward a day and a hand that were both far off. \lore{The widow's mark} had not been touched since the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due. \place[Ash]{Ash 30} stood six mornings ahead as a date and nothing more, and he did not bring it into a day that had not earned it.

He had stopped expecting anybody to speak the number aloud. The stopping was not the same as not wanting it.

The surgeon's register took the body in its old order, as a wound is taken in the order the layers lie, and found it altered only by the length of the cold. The crust had gone stiffer and browner in the dry kiln heat: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, its corner standing a hair off the wool, the empty place beside it unnamed and no third come in these three days to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, had ever been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, and the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile, and the pink that comes back to a surface while the core is still cold. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it and he could not lift his own head.

Under the knowing the infant wanted, and the wanting was louder. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. He wanted the thread wiped. He wanted the hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work. The body humiliated him in its exact small ways: the drool frozen with no thumb to wipe it, the head useless on the fold, the crust gone leathery past the point any child would put teeth to it, the handle two inches beyond a hand that had once closed on clamps in the dark. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and he could not turn over. He had held pressure in a cavity with both hands and now he could not keep a blanket on. The joke lived in that and cost him nothing and was not, in the end, funny.

Before first light \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth}, and the fire came before the basket. Only one set of feet had crossed the floor; the yard had not yet begun its work and no other person came under the roof. Her forearms were the colour of dried \lore{slip}, and the grey streak at her temple showed where the lamplight touched it; she smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under that the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which she had not swept in two years and did not intend to sweep. Her hand went to the coals first. She told them it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and the fire and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool; “it's the river wind,” to the wind. To the pot she gave a practical sentence and to the coals another, and to the thing in the wicker she gave nothing at all.

She stood a moment over the coals counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, the cord of her widow's mark oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted. She left the hairline showing, so that repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Near the hearth she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave.

The boy took the setting apart by what was there and by what was not: a hand, a pot, a rim, a pause; no figure spoken, no look toward the bed, no instruction to the basket. He filed the pause where it belonged and made no more of it. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and the widow's mark hung where it hung and was not touched. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the sixth stood behind at the kiln mouth on the second of Ash, held without a name and not spent, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire's missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the woman carried out of the brook clay-heavy and uncollected. None of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

The last tend of the dark month put its cold against the door as the light came, a harder cold than the day before, entering nowhere. Out in the yard the skin on \place{the trough} had thickened to where it would hold a poured spoon; he knew it from the going of the light yesterday and from the small dry report the wind made of it at the door, and left it where it lay, a covering and weather, with no figure taken off it and no fourth likeness made a cause. Under the finger's-width door the river's note came lower than it had come at the close of the twenty-second, water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, and the carried fire under the floor kept four and a gap. Three coverings in the yard stayed weather entered nowhere --- the gone white frost on the well's north lip, the skin that broke under a pail and closed, the silt-clouded plate lifting its face a little out of the path of feet --- with a finger between every pair. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector had come up or gone down the clay road; there was no market, no \place{Cross} and no bell. Ash kept its twenty-third day without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse.

Nothing new had come under the roof in the small hours and nothing new had been owed to it. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The absence of change was itself an exact result: a day that offered no sixth telling of the burned slate, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth asking of the row, no fifth shaping of the well-saying, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no seventh opening, no ninth arithmetic and no payer standing under anything, and had offered none because none had been earned. The keeping went on because there was nothing else for it to do. The infant underneath took none of the satisfaction and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle; both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-third. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Nothing on this day had yet been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch its, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and seventy-nine: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and seventy-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and fifty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-two days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number, while the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. Ash 30 stood six mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping invented to make a row come out even. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and seventy-nine; the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. Through the finger's-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Forward, not closed.
