The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat went off the high air first and stayed longest in the low places, under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where the one leathery crust lay two inches from the ear of \person[Corin]{the boy} and kept its empty place beside it unnamed. No third came to lie with them. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as though it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. A roof that stops where a hand stops is a roof of a particular kind, and he lay under it and did not ask it to be another.

The thread of drool at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, because nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried to save and cannot: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. All of that knowledge, exact and complete, lay two paces from a pot handle he could not close his hand upon.

In \place{Low Kiln} the kiln month's last tend went on into the afternoon with no boundary drawn under the mornings behind it. In the district's mouth Tallow was the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither name was corrected under this roof, because a house that keeps its days in loaves and glances has no use for a name that tells what a month is for. The kilns stood at their heat beyond the wall and the yard gave off the dry smell of clay surrendering its water. A week counted by tens would have closed on a tenth day; a household that counts by loaves lets a week run out the way water runs out of \place{the trough}, by going down and not by being emptied. He set the two ways of ending beside each other and did not make a likeness of them, because two endings standing in a row will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and whatever lay beneath the board lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, and the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it. Then the hand went back to its work. The household straightened around the quiet of her as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire that \lore{the thaw} was come, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every afternoon of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no hearth, shelf, well, rack, basket, yard, afternoon or dusk since. A dusk was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening; there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken. The instruction had gone to the fire and the pot and the wool and had not once gone to the child lying under the roof receiving it, and the boy, who had spent years in rooms where a man is named before he is treated, filed the order of it without approving it and without calling it a slight. It was the household's grammar. A pot was told what it wanted; a child was told nothing, and the warmth arrived all the same.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. Seven years had been measured out on that sill; six were counted and entered, the seventh stood at the lip with its edge over the dark and nothing under it. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

To the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it at the same time is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it. The boy filed the two answers on two places. A man who says yes to both sides has not chosen a side, and a house that calls a debt by the weather has not called the debt at all.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever stood and had still not given, and the word waited on the other side of his teeth; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} that afternoon. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

\scenebreak

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the \lore{full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld it from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

So the seventh year stood at the lip of a sill and the boy lay under a roof in a room that did not touch the sill, and neither of them had been paid. A breath held at the edge of a count and an inch held toward a sound are the same shape seen from two rooms, and the boy did not lay them together. Two likenesses standing on two places will call themselves a pair if a man leaves them long enough to want a third, and a pair with a payer under it is an account. He kept a hand between them, which was the only instrument he had.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, as she had given it on every going of the light since the boy came under the roof. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing without taking each other's colour. The arm that had carried him two hundred and three mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end --- a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark trough whose water was called weather, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. The yard swept around its absences the way a hand goes around a wound it does not mean to touch, and entered none of them.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without looking at his face and left the thread frozen. \place{the carried fire} answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, and its payer had had no name for two hundred and three days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of water over water. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places. They did not become one, and the two rooms did not touch. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; he had spent two hundred and three days refusing to supply one, which was not a power and not a conclusion but only the order in which he laid the things down. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh came and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, and the second reading had gone up the road in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside and was still not the figure paid, and \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve while the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the landing for a measure nobody called. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft scrape on a fourth and the pale line on a fifth. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap, no fourth likeness, no finger drawn. The charcoal lay unread beneath the shelf, the stone cold and unreconciled in its loft pot, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the loan uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out entered nowhere. The sixth green was still a colour and the foundling still a shape. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

Then he put the question to his hand.

It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It had gone up an inch on other days and come down at once, or come down before the counted breath finished, or held for the length of the small intake only, or stayed through a whole counted breath and come down with the breath; these were the answers the body had given and he had filed each on its sixth place and laid nothing across them. Now the hand went up and stayed up through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch his, and this time it did not come down with her breath. It kept the small height through the little silence after her count had ended and came down on the river's next low note.

It was a coordination with a second rhythm, the one through the door, which nobody counted and which had never been given to him. It was not a longer reach, not a stronger hand, not a hearing --- water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings --- not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named to it and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He entered it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true; the inch was worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The market did not get to read it and no slate in the district would take it. The answer had arrived wearing a rhythm nobody gave him, and that was the whole of its value and the whole of its smallness: the river did not stop for it.

The hand came down against the fold on the river's note. The river went on without noticing that an inch had ridden one of its low notes for the length of one breath. Two hundred and three stood exact in the dark, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and three days; the mark had been touched once, before the kiln on Ember 1, and at no other hour; the stone lay cold in its loft pot; the charcoal lay unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled; the seventh year stood nearer its end than ever and had not given. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and nothing was now. The fold took the hand without a word and the count went forward.
