\daymark{201}{Ember 22, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} outside the wool lay where the fold had taken it, still and small and unasked. In \place{Low Kiln}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, the small hours went on around it and the house went on with its ordinary keeping, and nobody drew a boundary under the dark or under whatever had stood before it. The kiln month had entered its last tend, and the tend had opened the way every tend opens --- the same cold on the same sill, the same coal banked under the same pot --- so that a man looking for a beginning would have found nothing to mark one with. He had been two hundred days old in the dark and was two hundred and one days old in the morning, six months and twenty-one days, and nobody under any roof said either number. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same thing as not wanting it; the wanting had gone down into the body and lived there as the body's own business, alongside the hunger and the cold. The first smell was hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and beneath all of it the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner, which the house called \lore{the thaw} and had called it so for a year without once sweeping it. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap behind it, a brick breath taken and held and let go and then the little silence where a fifth pull should have stood --- not a bell. Bells belonged to readings and to the district's arithmetic; this was a kiln doing what a kiln does, and the boy had learned to tell the two apart before he had learned to hold up his head.

Two hundred stood in him exact, complete and unspent, the ledger carried out of the dark and not yet opened; the morning would make it two hundred and one and nobody under any roof would say either number. No hand had come to spend it. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold, stiff enough in the dry heat of the kiln month to keep its own shape against the wool, and its empty place lay beside it without a name, and no third came to stand where the second had been. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and stayed frozen, a small bright line nobody had been told to wipe, for a basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a pot set down and given no instruction about its thread is a pot that keeps its thread. Cold had come as far as his cheek and his temple and the bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register in him took the night as a man takes a chart held at the wrong end: heat going off the high air and staying in the fold, a thread left where nobody had been told to wipe it, cold that stopped at a joint and went no further, and a small hand outside the wool with an inch of possible motion in it, filed separately from all of it, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The file was carried in the mornings and laid out only in the small hours, the way a man lays instruments on a towel when the case is shut and the work is done; there was no towel under this roof and no case and no instruments, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline, and he would not have it done in the daylight where a glance could fall on it and call it a habit. Six hearings stood beside and never among, the sixth out of a person and not a source; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the told bell lay beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid. None of it was laid out now. It went with him under the wool as a ledger goes in a coat, exact, complete and with no hand to spend it. He wanted the crust, and wanted the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted; he got none of the three, and the wanting of them went on without hope in it and without end.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for two hundred and one days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and had never been asked to stand up and be one; it went on with its note the way water goes on over water, with no regard for the ear under the roof or for the hand lying still against the fold. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four on the next board, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places: four pulls and a gap under the floor, a fifth low note through the door, four nails and a knot in the boards above his head. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them, and the boy kept his hand between them such hand as he had. He had learned early that a row of like things left standing long enough will call itself an account, and an account will want a figure, and a figure will want somebody to have paid it; he had spent two hundred and one days refusing to supply one, which was not a power and not a conclusion but only the order in which he laid the things down. The fire went on missing its fifth, the river went on keeping its fifth, the boards went on keeping their four and their knot, and the morning came up over Low Kiln without reconciling any of them.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came in from \place{the Clay Yard} with the day's dust held in her apron and gave the instruction before she gave it to anybody, and it went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. The basket was a pot that had been filled and set down, and a pot set down with no instruction given to it is a pot left where it stands. She kept her half-pace from every open slate as she came under the roof, the same half-pace she had kept going up in the sun and would keep coming back in the dark, so that no slate could take a figure off her passing and no figure could take a name off the child in the basket. At \place{the loaf shelf} she stood a moment with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row that stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it, and then the hand went back to the row. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. The household straightened around the quiet of her as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted; nobody had been told what the grey was and nobody asked.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every other morning of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, two fingers behind his ear, with the spoon wiped on her sleeve and not on his mouth; the little-door burn shut on the dish's edge and opened again empty, and the corner of the mouth she had fed went on carrying its line of gruel, which she did not wipe. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches between the handle and the hand were not a distance anybody in this house had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them. \lore{the widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath and let go, and at no shelf, no hearth, no well, no rack, no basket and no bed since. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening; there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the threshold with dry mud-scurf on her hem, the sort of scurf that comes off a yard that has been walked dry and not yet watered, and stood with her hands tucked into her apron. “What will the firing want now the last tend's in?” she asked, and asked it of no one in particular, which in this district is the way a cost-question is asked when the asker does not want to be the one who owns it. Sella did not turn from the pot. “Ask the pot,” she said. “Ask the weather. The pot knows what it wants and the weather knows what it'll take, and the house has said it.” The neighbour stood a moment with the question still in her mouth, the way a woman stands with a cup she has not been given a place to set; then she took the answer away level, with nothing priced in it, nothing refused and nothing entered, and went off down the step with her dry hem and her unspent figure. She had not been told a number and had not been denied one; she had been sent to the pot and the weather, and a cost sent in that direction arrives nowhere and stays nobody's. The surgeon's register in him marked the shape of it without approving it: a cost asked as a direction is an answer from which no payer can be read, and a household that keeps its warmth by never pricing anything has a way of staying warm that no slate can enter. He had spent years in a room where every cost was entered before the dressing went on and every hand was counted at the end, and here the whole district did its arithmetic in loaves and glances and went on warm without once writing down who had paid. It was a method and it worked, and he did not call it honest and did not call it a lie; he kept it on its own place, as he kept everything, with his hand between it and the next thing. Up \place{the clay road} and out of hearing, \person{Berr Clay}'s box had gone locked toward \place{High Kiln} with the true subtraction inside it, and \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve; the printed figure stood at one in twelve and had not been challenged under any roof, and a figure read twice over a basket was still not the figure paid. None of it came through the finger's-width door or under the Vail roof, and the boy did not lay the neighbour's level going-away beside any of it.

Before first grey, three or four men came up the yard with their collars high and went low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and the house gave them no name and did not say the room was cold. The boy heard the small dry sounds of their going --- a boot set down carefully on the stone outside the shed, the brush of a sleeve against the jamb, the low placing of voices in a room where voices must be placed quietly --- and heard them as a man hears a door shut in a room he cannot enter: the sound had a direction and no interior. The door kept its finger's width of open after them. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war, with the men inside dead of the breath, had come into this district in other years, one on Ilmen 26 and one on Silt 25, and a third voice at the bucket-round had only mentioned such a room; none had been joined to this room and no third telling came now. The men went in and the morning went on, and no word under the roof said what they were or why they had come before the light.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof the boy lay with the fold at his ear and the crust two inches from it, and put the question to his hand. He did not put it aloud; there was no need and no ear in the house to hear a question if he had had the voice to make one. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, went up as it had gone up on other mornings and in other small hours, and came down. It came down at once, with no holding in it at all, down against the fold before the small intake at the top of a breath could have been counted and before any hold had begun. The asking was not always answered; this was one of the mornings when it was not, and there was no failure in the refusal and no success in the asking, only the inch that had gone up and the inch that had come down. He filed it on a place of its own beside the others: beside the three held changes on their three places, the small dry scrape from \place{the Debt Loft} on a fourth, and the pale line at the gum of the man carried out on a fifth. It was not a hearing and not a seventh, not a stopping and not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule; no payer was named to it and nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true, and the inch was worth exactly what an inch was worth in a district that kept its accounts in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. The market did not get to read it. The hand came down against the fold, the fold took it without a word, and two hundred and one stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. The carried fire missed its fifth, the river kept its fifth, the boards kept their four and their knot, and the last tend had opened as an ordinary morning with no boundary drawn under anything. The river went on with its note and did not stop for the inch that had gone up and come down without riding it for so much as the length of a breath.
