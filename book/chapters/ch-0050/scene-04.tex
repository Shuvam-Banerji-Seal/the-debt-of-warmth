The afternoon thinned without ending. The heat went off the high air first, the way warmth leaves a pot's shoulder before it leaves the belly, and stayed longest in the low places, under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold at his ear. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. \place{Low Kiln} called this month the kiln month and had called the last one the firing month, and neither name was corrected under this roof, because a house that keeps its days in loaves and glances has no use for a name that tells what a month is for.

The one brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear. Beside it the empty place kept its own shape, unnamed, and no third came to lie with them. The thread of drool at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He had learned the order of it the way a man learns the order of a thing he has tried to save and cannot: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. All of that knowledge, exact and complete, lay two paces from a pot handle he could not close his hand upon.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the loaf shelf} with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it; then the hand went back to the row. She kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and whatever lay beneath the board lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, and the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. The household straightened around the quiet of her as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every afternoon of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not on his mouth, and left the corner of his mouth with its line of gruel still on it, for the instruction had gone to the spoon and the dish and not to the boy's face.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, basket, yard, afternoon or dusk since. An afternoon was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening; there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken. The instruction had gone to the fire and the pot and the wool and had not once gone to the child lying under the roof receiving it, and the boy, who had spent years in rooms where a man is named before he is treated, filed the order of it without approving it and without calling it a slight. It was the household's grammar. A pot was told what it wanted; a child was told nothing, and the warmth arrived all the same.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” To the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late he said “Yes, house,” agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever stood and had still not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} that afternoon. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the \lore{full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld it from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} had come up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open back room before first grey and he had given them no name and had not said the room was cold; the two tellings of the shut counting room stayed two with no third, planted toward a far year that no man in this yard had seen. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there until the sentence reached the pot and the end was given to it. Then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other two places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

So two silences kept under one roof: the ear that heard and said nothing, and the hand that waited at the iron and said nothing. The boy lay at the edge of them and put no word between them, because two silences standing side by side will call themselves an equality if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer, and an equality is an account that has found a payer. He kept them apart with a hand he could not lift, which was the only instrument he had.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road} at the half-pace, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket; the basket was a pot that had been filled and set down, and a pot set down with no instruction given to it is a pot left where it stands. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars, and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}, and the two passed without taking each other's colour, as two women pass at a well with a pot between them and neither asks the other what she carries. The arm that had carried him two hundred and two mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end --- a record in muscle that knew weight and did not know cost. It had carried a child of her own once, and a hundred loaves since, and had entered neither in any slate, and the boy, who had once counted the instruments on a towel before he touched them, read the record in the shoulder as a man reads a face he has been looking at too long to see.

She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the wall, the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called weather, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the empty place in the returning line, and the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing and not entered, each with a look and a wide step and no number. A yard that goes around a thing is a yard that keeps it; a yard that measures it has begun to pay it, and this yard had kept its absences in loaves and glances for two hundred and two days without once entering a figure. The half-pace held all the way under the roof.

\scenebreak

She set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without looking at his face, and left the thread frozen. The two rooms did not touch. Above, a man's few possessions sat in an unmarked clay pot and the warm stone in them was cold and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving; below, a child lay under wool and heard a sound from that room with a direction and no name and gave it a place of its own. A small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, heard from that room on a day earlier in the kiln month, lay on a fourth place beside the three held changes and had not become a cause, because a cause would want a payer and no payer stood to it.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for two hundred and two days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and had never been asked to stand up and be one. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; he had spent two hundred and two days refusing to supply one, which was not a power and not a conclusion but only the order in which he laid the things down. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file again the way a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh had come in two hundred and two days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, the one in twelve unchallenged, the box gone up the road locked with the true subtraction inside, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate unread at \place{Greybank}, the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for an uncalled measure, a figure read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth, the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach, the pale line at the gum of the man carried out on Day 188 stood on its own place, the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place stood on trays that did not touch, the charcoal under the shelf was unread and the stone in its loft pot cold, and the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. No finger of his had drawn the line, and absences standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a hand between them.

Then he put the question to his hand.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It went up as it had gone up on other mornings and in other small hours, an inch and no more, and this time it stayed. It stayed through the small intake and the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch his, and came down with the breath rather than before it. It was the old coordination remembered, the shape the hand had kept on the night the count reached two hundred, not a longer reach and not a stronger hand and not a change made again. It was not a hearing; water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. It was not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule; no payer was named to it and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He entered it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them. The river went on without noticing that an inch had ridden one of its low notes for the length of a breath.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true; the inch was worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The market did not get to read it and no slate in the district would take it. The fold took the hand without a word. Two hundred and two stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof, and nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
