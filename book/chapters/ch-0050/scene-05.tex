\daymark{203}{Ember 24, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

The dark under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had the shape of a pot kept under ash --- heat down in the low places, cold at the edges, and no hand anywhere to say which of the two had been given an instruction. In \place{Low Kiln} the kiln month's last working week went on into its third day with no boundary drawn under the second, and \person[Corin]{the boy} came into his two hundred and third day at six months and twenty-three days, and nobody under any roof said either number. The wanting to be told had gone down into the body with the hunger and lain there without a word, the way a tool put aside at dusk lies where it was set until somebody reaches for it in the morning. A tool does not ask to be reached for. He had stopped expecting the reaching. He had not stopped wanting it, which is a different thing, and he kept the two on separate places rather than let one stand up and call itself the other.

Hot clay came first under tallow, and wet wool under that, and beneath all of it the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner, which the house called \lore{the thaw} and had called so for a year without lifting a broom to it. Then the kiln drew in fours with a gap behind it: a brick breath taken, held, let go, and the little silence where a fifth pull should have stood. Not a bell. Bells belonged to readings and the district's arithmetic; this was a kiln doing what a kiln does, and he had learned to tell the two apart before he had learned to hold up his head. \lore{Ash} lay in the corner in a clean dark line with a finer hair of grey beside it, and the two lay together without either becoming the other, as the day's two rooms lay together without touching.

\place{the carried fire} gave four low pulls through the floor and kept the silence where the fifth should have closed, and its payer had had no name for two hundred and three days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never asked to stand up and be one. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; he had spent two hundred and three days refusing to supply one, which was not a power and not a conclusion but only the order in which he laid the things down. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

Two hundred and three lay in the wool as an exact thing, complete and unspent, and he had no use for it and could not put it down. The district kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf and glances at a jar and a space nobody named; he kept his in a count that nobody had asked for and that no slate in Sill would take. It was an old habit, this laying of numbers edge to edge --- a surgeon's habit of knowing how long a thing had been wrong before anybody else had thought to ask --- and here the thing that was wrong was the room itself, and the count had no hand to spend it. He did not call it a gift. A gift is a thing given, and this had been carried in by himself and left with him.

One brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear. Beside it the empty place kept its own shape, unnamed, and no third came to lie with them. The thread of drool at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it the way a man knows the order of a thing he has tried to save and cannot: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. All of that knowledge, exact and complete, lay two paces from a pot handle he could not close his hand upon.

The last tend had run on into its third working day. In the district's mouth Tallow was the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither name was corrected under this roof, because a house that keeps its days in loaves and glances has no use for a name that tells what a month is for. The kilns were at their heat behind the wall and the yard was full of the dry smell of clay giving up its water. Nobody had drawn a boundary under the tend that went before, and nobody drew one now. A week that is counted by tens would end on a tenth day; a household that counts by loaves lets a week run out the way water runs out of a trough, by going down and not by being emptied. He set the two ways of ending beside each other and did not make a likeness of them, because two endings standing in a row will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came in before first grey and gave the instruction where it always went --- to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, never to the basket. The basket was a pot that had been filled and set down, and a pot set down with no instruction given to it is a pot left where it stands. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and kept her half-pace from every open slate even in a room where no slate lay open, because the half-pace was a habit of the hand and not a thing done for an audience. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it. Then the hand went back to its work. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

At \place{the loaf shelf} she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and whatever lay beneath the board lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, and the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. The household straightened around the quiet of her as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not on his mouth, and left the corner of his mouth with its line of gruel still on it, for the instruction had gone to the spoon and the dish and not to the boy's face. He took what was given and wanted what was not given and kept both without complaint, the way a man at a long table keeps his place. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every morning of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no hearth, shelf, well, rack, basket, yard, morning or afternoon since. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening; there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken. The instruction had gone to the fire and the pot and the wool and had not once gone to the child lying under the roof receiving it, and the boy, who had spent years in rooms where a man is named before he is treated, filed the order of it without approving it and without calling it a slight. It was the household's grammar. A pot was told what it wanted; a child was told nothing, and the warmth arrived all the same.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a question about the firing, now the last tend was in, and asked what it would want before the kiln month was out. Sella did not answer with a figure. She told the woman to ask the pot and ask the weather, and the woman went away with her question still in her hand, level and unspilled, carrying nothing priced and nothing entered. There was a trick to it that the boy had watched for two hundred and three days and had never once seen fail: a cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction, and a direction has no figure in it, so nothing can be written down. The district kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and the space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The house had stayed warm on that trick for a year, and had never once said what the warmth cost or who had stood under it.

At the yard's edge a woman wanting the thaw early and a man wanting it late put their case to \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} as they had put it to the boards and the weather before him. He said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it at the same time is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it. The boy filed the two answers on two places. A man who says yes to both sides has not chosen a side, and a house that calls a debt by the weather has not called the debt at all.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. Seven years had been measured out on that sill; six were counted and entered, the seventh stood at the lip with its edge over the dark and nothing under it. Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever stood and had still not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} that morning. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

Three or four men unnamed had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, their collars high and their voices down, and the house gave them no name and did not say the room was cold. \person{Jorra Vail} came through the shed door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed two with no third, planted toward a far year no man in this yard had seen. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the \lore{full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld it from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached the pot and the end was given to it, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other two places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

So the seventh year stood at the lip of a sill and the boy lay under a roof in a room that did not touch the sill, and neither of them had been paid. A breath held at the edge of a count and an inch held toward a sound are the same shape seen from two rooms, and the boy did not lay them together. Two likenesses standing on two places will call themselves a pair if a man leaves them long enough to want a third, and a pair with a payer under it is an account. He kept a hand between them, which was the only instrument he had.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof the morning came down the walls without arriving anywhere, the way heat comes down the underside of a shelf and stops at the edge of a reaching hand. He put the question to his hand.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It went up as it had gone up on other mornings, an inch and no more, and this morning it held for the length of the small intake only and came down before the letting go. That was the answer the body gave on this morning: neither the down-at-once of a morning two days back nor the whole counted breath of the small hours, but an inch kept for one small intake and given up when the intake ended. It was not a shorter reach or a longer one. It was the body keeping its own time, as it had kept its own time on other days and would keep it again, and there was no failure in the early letting go and no triumph in the holding. He entered it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them. It was not a hearing; water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. It was not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule; no payer was named to it and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true; the inch was worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The market did not get to read it and no slate in the district would take it. The seventh year at the lip of the sill was a held breath that had not been spent, and the hand under the wool was an inch held for one small intake that had not been spent either, and both were kept exactly where they were, neither a payment nor a thing that could be paid. The river went on without noticing that an inch had ridden one of its low notes for the length of a breath.

The fold took the hand without a word. Two hundred and three stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof, and the count went forward into the afternoon.
