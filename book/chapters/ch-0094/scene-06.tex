The afternoon came on grey and ordinary, with no market, no Cross and no bell on it, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down to \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. He took the yard as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry rasp of a rope stiff with cold, the small flat knock of a pail set down, the talk of the boards going on over the price of barley. Nothing from the fifth of \place{Ash} crossed the afternoon toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer; the month kept its middle days without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming it --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words, and the boy set neither. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at the Vail corner lay where it lay, and the finer grey hairline of \lore{the thaw} beside it lay unswept too, and neither was swept today; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} kept its broken birth-slates, which the yard went around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place{the trough} by \place{the yard well} kept its skin; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips} kept its shape; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out kept its place in the line with nobody putting a figure to it. Seven places with a look and a wide step. No eighth likeness was made of them, no cause laid across any two, no payer stood under any. Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair: the stayed frost gone off the well's north lip on Bone 3 while the yard looked elsewhere, which was not a sign; the skin on the trough breaking and closing under a pail's knock; and the silt-clouded plate of ice standing on \place[the flat stone by the well]{its flat stone} out of the path of feet.

The hunts and repairs of the afternoon went as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and not as figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} with a hare across his shoulders shifted the weight and answered a woman's question about the price with the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward \place{the clay road} with nobody weighing the hare and nobody opening a slate. A man with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and would be late. \person{Tarn}, who had taken the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said *Yes, house* to each of them and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying; he agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The boy took each of these as a chart left blank where the number should stand, and entered none of them. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from \place{the hearth} with a lighter touch and no figure, and Sella's fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted.

Then the road brought the teller back, and it was not the voice from yesterday and not a collector. \person{Hessa Rale} came down the clay road in the afternoon with the writing of \person[Berr Clay]'s narrow slate turned toward herself for the first time in the boy's knowing of her. On Account-days she went up that road toward \place{Greybank} before noon with the writing to her sleeve and the face of the slate turned away from everybody; here she came down it, and the face was turned toward her own forearm still edge-on, the writing looking at her sleeve and at nobody, the thumb lying along the grain of the frame with nothing pressing where the writing was. The second strap had been let out for the long road and the dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the yard, and the small flat correction of the strap came with the turn of the wrist that brought the edge round, and nothing of the slate's face was offered to anyone going by.

A woman by the path asked what she carried. “Ask the road,” Hessa said. “It knows the way and I know the weight. That's the whole of what either of us knows.” She answered the question put and not the one behind it, and was done with the conversation when the road was done with it, without rudeness and without apology. She went on with the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking, and no figure came off the slate, and no choosing was made.

The boy took the turning of the writing toward herself apart as he takes everything apart, by what was there and by what was not: a face turned inward, a thumb along the grain, no pressure where the writing lay, no reader's eye, no figure coming off the stone. He filed it on a place of its own beside the fourth shaping of the well-saying from yesterday, with a finger between. A voice had said the runner had come back down the road with the writing turned toward her, and here a runner came down the road with the writing turned toward her; but a voice standing in no doorway is not a prophecy fulfilled, and a sighting is not the payment of a saying, and he refused to join saying to sighting, to make a fifth shaping of it, to make Hessa a reader, to make the sighting a payment or to pay the claim. The joining said over the boards on the fifth of Ash and the fourth shaping at the well stayed each on its own place, with a finger between them and between them and \person{Ilva}'s strip in her sleeve, Berr's twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate and the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}; neither made Hessa a reader nor \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra} a payer. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on its own place with no tenth, and she was joined to none --- not to \person{Marra}, not to the soft-handed man of Slate Row, not to the clerks, not to Ilva, not to the green-mouthed woman, not to the Drawing collector and not to the woman whose voice had come over the boards. She was a runner and not a reader of anyone's private keeping.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept six where the house could check, in the worn thumb-mark, and the seventh at the lip beyond it where the house could not, and beside the seventh lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man could check it and where a hand could also slip it off. He told the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count* in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the Seed 16 breath was drawn and not spent. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back; his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the place where it had stopped kept itself. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot and he did not try to reconcile it, which is not forgiveness. His thumb's resting on the uncut seventh of Bone 26 was checked and found where it had been laid, and was not made again on the fourth, the fifth or the sixth of Ash: not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not a payment, not a fifth held change, not a hearing, not a mending and not a rule, with no payer standing. He put the knife back where it could be checked and went on with the broom.

Jorra came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} at the shed's edge with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and would not say it before a master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make a song of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing, and the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the quiet without an order from anybody. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. *He's not Vail* stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing, and the joining said over the boards on the fifth of Ash was held apart on a place of its own; no sixth telling came, no third shut-room telling came, and no seventh opening was made due on a day that had not earned one. The claim went on gathering interest against a promise he neither paid nor refused.

The house kept Garl without a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder hung on its peg behind the door and held a broader man's shape; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and Sella let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with the finer grey of the thaw beside it, and neither had been swept. The twice-mended pot stood on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and both of his sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled* and *If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one*. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them, and repair stayed apart from restoration because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and thirty-five days, and no lesson had been made of any of it.

The whole tray was checked and left as it lay. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; an empty place kept empty was a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh hearing. The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between; one in twelve was printed, read and unchallenged, and had not been paid by anybody who stood where the boy could see. Seven was a colour and seven was a shape, with no eighth of either. Nine record-first hands stood with Hessa's eighth and Ilva's ninth apart and no tenth filed anywhere, and the six arithmetics of the year rode locked up the clay road in Berr's box toward High Kiln, figure read still not figure paid, six readings six and not one account; the finding that six are six sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across \lore{the not-doings}, the covered grain, the five held objects, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand or his own gaps; the edges came near to touching and the finger went between.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, went wide around the seven walked-around places, and set him on \place{the bed} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. She said nothing of where they had been, and gave the instruction to the fire, the wool and the pot and never to the basket, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched, because a going of the light and a bed are not openings and six openings in six months are six and not a rule.

She went to \place{the hearth} before the basket and told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-five days, and she did not supply the pull or look toward the floor to see whether anything stood there to supply it. Her finger lay on top of the front edge of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a number; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and went on to the next thing. She lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person, and left the hairline of the original break showing. At the rack end of the day's work the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth; she lifted a corner with a still lighter touch and turned it once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out and did not close, so that no handful was lifted. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The boy took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand's intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The finding that six are six and not one account stayed on a place of its own with no payer, and was not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the five held objects, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping, Hessa's turned writing or his own kept gaps; the edges came near to touching and the finger went between, and no doctrine stood up to say why, for an account that has found no payer has no doctrine to stand on.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall at the rack end, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh at the kindling and the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} sat each on its own place with a finger between every pair; none was made again on the sixth of Ash, none was a habit, a way, a fifth held change, a hearing, a gift or a rule, and no payer stood under any. Tarn's thumb on the seventh notch was found where it had been left; Ilva's ninth hand stayed in her sleeve a pace from the open slate and Hessa's eighth stayed on its own place, and no tenth hand was filed anywhere. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled; five askings of the ownerless question stood with no sixth and no row named. The burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing, and the joining said over the boards on the fifth of Ash stood beside it on a place of its own, a first joining of the burned slate to the shut room said aloud by nobody standing, not a sixth telling, not a third shut-room telling, not a hearing, not a cause, not a confession and not a rule. The well-saying stood at four shapings with no fifth and no witness, and the fourth shaping stood on its own place beside it: a runner come back down the road with the writing turned toward herself, said by a voice that stood in no doorway and had not become a prophecy because a sighting had followed. The two sayings of yesterday and the turning of the writing today lay on separate places with a finger between them and between them and Ilva's strip in her sleeve, Berr's twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate and the charcoal beneath the shelf; neither made Hessa a reader nor Jorra a payer, and neither was spoken of under the roof.

In the small hours, with the river's note low and going lower under the finger's-width door and the house quiet about him, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and forty-six: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping, Hessa's turned writing nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and forty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the small hours. Nothing on the sixth of Ash had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. Three hundred and thirty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and fifty-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Ash kept its middle days without a boundary drawn and neither habit of naming the months was set right; the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the runner's writing was turned toward herself and no figure had come off the slate; and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door. The tenth of Ash stood four mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and Ash's first arithmetic belonged to its own morning. Forward, not closed.
