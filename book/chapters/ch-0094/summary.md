## Chronicle

Ash kept its middle days without a boundary drawn. On the fourth morning the boy came into his three hundred and thirty-third day, the hand laid down on the two hundred and fortieth answer as a tool put away where the next hand will find it. One crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed, the thread frozen and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I, then went wide of seven places in the yard.

On the fifth, over the price of barley on the Clay Yard boards, a voice with nobody behind it said the slate that burned was burned in the room with the damper shut, a Greybank saying of a shut counting room spoken without a date or a man standing to be asked. The boy set the joining on a place of its own: not a sixth telling of the slate, not a third telling of the room, not a hearing, a cause or a rule, with no payer under it. Sella gave the voice no name; Jorra grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet. At the well Ilva kept her strip in her sleeve and answered *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*; a second ownerless voice gave the old saying a fourth shape --- the runner had come back down the road with the writing turned toward her. The boy set the shaping beside the saying with a finger between.

On the sixth the road brought the teller back, and it was neither yesterday's voice nor a collector. Hessa Rale came down the clay road with the writing of Berr Clay's narrow slate turned toward herself for the first time, still edge-on, with nothing pressing where the writing lay. To a woman who asked what she carried she said *Ask the road. It knows the way and I know the weight.* The boy filed the turning beside the fourth shaping with a finger between and refused to make of it a fifth shaping, a reader or a payment.

Each morning and close the hand went up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake and let it go on the same low note. Six answers were entered, two hundred and forty-one through two hundred and forty-six. At the close three hundred and thirty-five stood exact and spoken by nobody, six months and one hundred and fifty-five days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl Vail's name unspoken the same number. Nothing had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap and the river's low note went quiet.
