\daymark{334}{Ash 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold where the two hundred and forty-second answer had put it down, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist to speak of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and had since learned the weight of a barley crust at two inches. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Corin]{the boy} lay awake and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under the finger's-width door. Three hundred and thirty-four stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and fifty-four days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-four days; the salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door kept the shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. Nothing from the fourth of \place{Ash} crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The sixth opening stood behind the wall inside the morning's ordinary heat, with nobody under the roof naming it; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The older women named the month's middle days by a weather that had not done what it promised and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right in so many words.

The inventory came in its old order, because an order is the one thing a field will give a man who cannot move in it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder through the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in the small hours to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to wipe is a thing that stays. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a face that is no ease; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through every morning of his life in this house: the surgeon taking the inventory exactly and entering it nowhere, the infant underneath taking none of it and wanting, the two of them quarrelling in a body that could not so much as lift its own chin from a fold.

First light came grey and ordinary, with no market, no Cross and no bell waiting on it. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she had gone before every basket since the boy came under the roof, and gave the instruction to the coals and not to a person. “It's the thaw,” she told them, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the pot's edge drawing the ash into a small dark line that she did not smooth. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the little-door burn on the back of her left hand opened and shut on nothing as the hand worked. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. The fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she did not supply the pull or look toward the floor to see whether anything stood there to supply it. One grey streak at her temple showed pale in the first of the light and she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and the burn closed on the hair and then on air. Nothing in the room turned toward \place{the bed}. The boy heard the handle lift and the small sound of the wire taking the weight before the light showed him the hand that had lifted it, and took both sounds as findings: the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather.

Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge as she passed, so that the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a number. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beside the shelf; her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air, and went on to the next thing. From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, and had improved in the keeping in the three years since a man who mended pots and would not let anyone mend him had last said them, and were very likely not as good as he had said them. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing; repair and restoration were kept on separate places with a finger between. At \place{the rack end} of the day's work the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth; she lifted a corner with a lighter touch than the day before and turned it once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out and did not close, so that no handful was lifted. The handful let fall on Bone 22 stayed one object on a place of its own; these empty fingers went beside it as a keeping and not as a second object, and the finger went between them. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. He took the half-beat apart as he took everything apart, by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand's intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched. A morning and a hearth are not openings, and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the fired kiln behind the wall stood cold after Ash 2 and nobody went out to look at it or spoke of what it had done. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held behind the household without a name and not brought out into the morning to stand up as a sign. Nothing had been opened, joined or paid in the night. She went about the room with her hands never still and never speaking, and gave the instruction to the wool, the pot, the fire and the river wind and never to the basket.

The afternoon came down off the terrace without a boundary drawn upon it, the light going thin over \place{the Clay Yard} as a pot goes slack when it is lifted from the fire and set where the heat can leave it in its own time. Sella carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, one hand under the basket's base and the other at the fold, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” The wind came up off the water with clay in it and a colder edge under the clay, and went through the yard without asking anybody's leave, and the fold stood between it and the ears as a field dressing stands between a wound and the air. He took the yard as he took every field, by the small sounds first: rope stiff with dry cold at the well, the small dry rasp of it as a pail came up, the knock of a pot set down wrong and then set down right, a broom going around something it did not touch. Over \place{the boards} the price of barley went from mouth to mouth with the weather, the way the yard said everything that had no owner standing behind it --- flat, going off up the terrace while the work went on, with nobody leaning in to take it down.

Then, on the same breath as the price of barley and the weather, a voice with nobody behind it said that the slate which had burned was burned in the room with \lore{the damper} shut. It was the \place{Greybank} saying about the counting room shut in the war, which the yard had always taken as history eighteen leagues east, spoken here without a date and without a man standing anywhere to be asked. There was no room named, no hand at the flame, no owner and no hour; the voice went off up the terrace with the barley price after it, and the yard went on around the two sayings as a broom goes around a stone it does not touch, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the sentence apart as he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. What was there: a burned slate, a shut room, the joining of the two in one breath, said low over boards and not into any basket. What was not: a witness standing in a doorway, a room named, a hand at a flame, a date, a figure, a payer, a name under it. He set the joining on a place of its own with a finger between it and everything else. The burned slate stood at five and stayed five, a finger between the fifth and the four; the shut-room tellings stood at two and did not become three. This was not a sixth telling of the slate and not a third telling of the room, for neither voice had stood anywhere to be asked and a saying that arrives nowhere cannot be carried into any chart a clerk would keep. It was not a hearing, not a seventh of \lore{the full note}, not a stopping, not a payment, not a cause, not a confession and not a rule; no doctrine stood up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The claim went on gathering interest against a promise neither given nor withdrawn, the betrayal that waited to be called not called here, and the yard's price of barley went on over the boards as though nothing had been said into it.

Sella heard it as she heard all the yard's sayings, with her hands going on doing what they had been doing. She had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself, not to the fire and not to the boards; she gave no name to the voice and no figure to the saying. When they came past the shed \person{Jorra Vail} was coming through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person, and to a pot he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He grew quieter as the saying went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody gave him an order to stop. When Sella had not finished telling him that the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. One piece of iron had two bites in it, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn. No joining was made to the two shut-room tellings beyond the one filed on its own place, and no third telling came.

The going of the light came without a bell and without a Cross. Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a step and no number, and set him on his bed under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. There was a sound from above and behind, a foot on the loft stair going up and then still, and no interior was entered and no name given to the sound. The instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She went to the hearth before she lifted a cup and told the coals it was the thaw, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the fire answering four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-four days. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; an empty place kept empty was a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh hearing. The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap: the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth}, the burned slate at five, the two shut-room tellings, the two bites of the damper, the ninth hand kept in a sleeve and the eighth kept on its own place, and now the joining said over the boards on a place of its own with a finger between it and every pair. Nothing had moved. Six arithmetics of the year rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, six readings six and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, and was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool and read over by a number that did not know its cost, the five held objects or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. No doctrine stood up out of any of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

In the small hours, with the river's note low and going lower under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and forty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the burned slate at five, the joining said over the boards nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and forty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found, and the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the dark.

Nothing on the fifth of Ash had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended; the joining said over the boards had been said by nobody standing and was filed where a thing said by nobody must go. Three hundred and thirty-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-four days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Ash kept its middle days without a boundary drawn and neither habit of naming the months was set right; the keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place[the flat stone by the well]{its flat stone} out of the path of feet; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand lay back against the fold on the two hundred and forty-third answer; and the boards were quiet with the price of barley going on over them, the burned slate still five on its place and the joining on a place of its own with a finger between. Ash 5 stood five mornings short of mid-month, and went on.
