The afternoon came down off \place{the terrace} without a boundary drawn upon it, the light going thin over \place{the Clay Yard} as a pot goes slack when it is lifted from the fire and set where the heat can leave it in its own time. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, one hand under the basket's base and the other at the fold, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” The wind came up off the water with clay in it and a colder edge under the clay, and went through the yard without asking anybody's leave, and the fold stood between it and the ears as a field dressing stands between a wound and the air.

He took the yard as he took every field, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: rope stiff with dry cold at \place[the yard well]{the well}, the small dry rasp of it as a pail came up, the knock of a pot set down wrong and then set down right, a broom going around something it did not touch. The crust in the fold lay two inches from his ear, stiff and leathery in the dry heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, for nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through every carried afternoon of his life, the surgeon taking the inventory exactly and entering it nowhere, the infant underneath taking none of it and wanting. He knew that a vessel which cools while everybody is kind to it does not announce the cooling, and he knew the order in which the small sounds go quiet. He could not lift his own chin from a fold.

Sella went wide around the yard's seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. Past \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay under the afternoon like a seam that had been closed and left to show; beside it the finer grey hairline that the district called \lore{the thaw} ran on unswept, and a broom in somebody's hand went around the two of them and across neither. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, entered by nobody and asked about by nobody, with grey rack dust settled in the seams between them. The round ring where a shallow clay pan had once stood held no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word to say what hands had lifted it away. The \place{trough} kept its skin. Far out on \place{the worked strips} a field's empty place stood among the planted rows with nothing in it and nobody putting a figure to what was missing. And at the returning-line place a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and the place was gone around as the others were gone around. Seven places, a look, a wide step, no number: no eighth likeness made among them, no cause laid across any two of them, and no payer standing under any.

Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, and a finger went between every pair. The frost that had gone off the well's north lip on Bone 3 while the yard looked elsewhere was not a sign, and she did not look twice at the bare stone to make it one. The skin on the trough broke under a pail's knock and closed again over the dark below it, and closed as though nothing had been opened. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, lifted there by a hand that had told the weather where to stand; no boot went near it and nobody called it anything but weather. She gave each covering its own place in the passing and kept her hands off all three.

The hunts and the repairs went around him as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} with a hare across his shoulders shifted the weight off one foot when a woman asked what the carrying had cost and told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward \place{the clay road} with nobody weighing the hare and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want, and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing, and its silence was not an answer entered anywhere. A woman with a cost-question got the weather for hers and went away with it as she had come. The boy read each exchange as a chart left blank where the number should stand, and entered neither the blank nor the going-away. A direction was not a price, and a payer who had not stood up could not be named to a figure.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth. She lifted a corner with a lighter touch than she had used in the morning and turned it once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out and did not close, so that no handful was lifted. The handful let fall on Bone 22 stayed one object on a place of its own; these empty fingers went beside it as a keeping and not as a second object, and the finger went between them. The arithmetic that would be read over the covered grain in six mornings was a figure that did not know what the grain had cost, and the space between that figure and its price had no signature. Nothing under the wool was entered into it.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} when they came past, with the small knife lying at the very edge of its sill where a man could check it and where a man's hand could also slip it off. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check; the seventh stood at the lip beyond it, beside the unforged collector sentence that \person{Garl Vail} had left there with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. “Six counted,” he said to the wood. “The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, and did not look toward the basket. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up as far as the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the loan uncalled and the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach still unspent. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the place where it had stopped on another day stayed where it was. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot and he did not try to reconcile it with the debt, which was not forgiveness. His thumb's resting on the uncut seventh of Bone 26 was checked and found where it had been left and was not made again on the fourth of \place{Ash}: not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not a payment, not a fifth held change, not a hearing, not a mending and not a rule, and no payer stood under it. “Yes, house,” he said to Sella, though she had given him no instruction, and went on with three things at once.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened. It was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would have called it early. When Sella had not finished telling him that the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot; the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites in it, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. He grew quieter and the yard went on around him without an order from anybody. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing anywhere to be asked, no hand named at any flame and no sixth due on a day that had not earned one; the shut-room tellings stood at two and not three; the two bites of the damper were one object joined to no kiln. “You want me to say it,” he said to no one in particular. “I'm not saying it.” \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of a name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and went on gathering interest against a quiet that was not yet a refusal.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a hand had once pulled it straight; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and nobody filled it. The clean dark line and the finer grey beside it were unswept, and the twice-mended pot that would come to the hearth at dusk had the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. Repair and restoration were kept on separate places with a finger between. The debt against the firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for three hundred and thirty-three days, and no lesson had been made out of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance, and maintenance was not a story.

The going of the light came without a bell and without a Cross. Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going wide again around the seven places with a look and a step and no number, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. There was a sound from above and behind, a foot on the loft stair going up and then still, and no interior was entered and no name given to the sound. The instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She went to \place{the hearth} before she lifted a cup and told the coals it was the thaw, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the edge of it drawing the ash into a small dark line she did not smooth. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said to a woman at the door, and gave the house no figure. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was figured; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of it closing on air. She lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and let the weight come on the handle only after the thumb had felt what it had to feel. The crack showed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched, because a going of the light and a bed are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The fired kiln stood cold behind the wall and nobody went out to look at it.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-three days. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty; the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told; the told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap: the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth}, the burned slate at five, the two shut-room tellings, the two bites of the damper, the ninth hand kept in a sleeve and the eighth kept on its own place, with a finger between every pair. Nothing had moved. Six arithmetics rode locked up the clay road in a box, six readings six and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the five held objects or his own gaps. No doctrine stood up, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Three hundred and thirty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and fifty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number.

In the small hours, with the river's note low and going lower under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach, the intake not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river and the note not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and forty-two: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the burned slate at five nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and forty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine.

The fourth of Ash had gone without an opening, a joining, a name, a payment or an ending, and nothing stood anywhere to be asked about it. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found, and the infant underneath took none and went on wanting the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the dark. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the burned slate stood at five on its own place with no sixth standing beside it; and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door. Ash 4, one morning short of mid-month, went on.
