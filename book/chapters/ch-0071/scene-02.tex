The hand lay where the hundred and third answer had left it, against the fold at the low angle of the shelf, and the afternoon came on over \place{the Clay Yard} the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals --- not all at once, not with a boundary drawn, but drawing back behind the pot and settling into the wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay carried, awake and spoken of by nobody. He had stopped expecting anyone to tell him the number. The wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, giving out nothing and going out not at all. Two hundred and sixty-four stood exact and unspent; six months and eighty-four days were told by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer without a name. Smoke 25 would not end by a boundary. Smoke 30 stood five mornings ahead and was not reached for, and there would be no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on a Fifthday.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow, a kiln fed and left to work; under it wet wool sour with the long lodging of a body carried since before first grey; under that, finer and more persistent, the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}, \lore{Ash} so long in the air that it had ceased to be an event and become a condition of the room. Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, going on while he listened and going on when he stopped. Above him \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. The afternoon gave these back in the order it had taken them, and nothing in the yard made one of them answer another.

He took the body in the order the facts arrived, as a surgeon takes a field. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, neither going forward nor going away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder in a body of two hundred and sixty-four days; the surgeon entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

The house kept \person{Garl Vail} in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where the wheel had leaned into him for twenty years; the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- as though a man's whole life could be a saying addressed to an uncracked pot. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled more carefully each year for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and sixty-four days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of any of it. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on Smoke 25 and none went down it; a man's debt can go around a house for a decade without ever arriving at the door it belongs to.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate. She went wide around seven things and gave each a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line where the old ridge turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called the thaw; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, crumb, print or word to say what had gone; \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} beside it bare and dark and its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven in a row were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand that had gone in was carried and not named, not paid, not joined to the shelf's two gaps in the room that did not touch or to the one crust in the fold; he kept it on its own place as he kept the others, and the absence of a joining was itself kept. She lifted him over the ground as a woman carries a pot filled that must not be slopped, and gave every instruction to the wool, the broom and the water. None came to the basket.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} she gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to any person, and \person{Tarn} took it up as a man takes up a tool already named. A neighbour wanted the thaw early and another wanted it late, and he said \emph{Yes, house} to both, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because a late thaw puts off the paying though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the Seed 16 reach he drew and did not spend, a small interval showing where it should have been; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and set the small knife at the very lip of its sill --- six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, the seventh beyond where it could not, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. Beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man who had turned back and no figure on the debt; he had been that man. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stopped there, no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He would not stand one up to make the nine into a rule, because a rule wants a payer under it and none stood. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked clay pot, two years gone and cold long since, not tried and not forgiven. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the collector's road without a turn of the head.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened; he kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Three or more unnamed men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and got no name from him and no word that the room was cold; its sound came to the basket as a direction and nothing more, and the boy did not follow it into a room. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it. He withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, and no seventh hearing came and none was invented to fill the empty place. He did not look toward the bed and the boy did not look at him.

The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. The promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. When Sella's sentence went low --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip and held there until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot; the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites in it, mercy and mutilation, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both, and he joined it to no kiln. No slate burned under any roof on Smoke 25 and no witness stood.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, went wide around the seven places, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and went to her own bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung, touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or going of the light on Smoke 25, because an afternoon and a bed are not openings. The one opening before Smoke 1 stayed behind the household, made into no lesson, no mending, no sign and no payment; the next real opening stood far off in the first firing of Frost and was not brought into a day that had not earned its own light.

In the small hours he turned the file out on the towel and found it, as he found it every time, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; the seventh place stood empty and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid, and beside them three shapings of the pot saying on their own place with the premature Smoke 14 note apart and no fourth likeness made a cause. Four low things stood with water over water a fifth; told bell stood beside heard and told figure beside paid, with Smoke's second arithmetic gone up the road in a locked box and unread under this roof. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth; three held changes on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth and the pale line of the carried man on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with a hundred and three answers. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth on its own tray and her strip unread in her sleeve; the covered grain lay unfigured; the pan's gone ring and the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one crust with its empty place lay on trays that did not touch. The ownerless question stood at five askings with no witness and no row named. The house's not-doings were kept on a place apart from his own kept gaps with a finger between and no joining, because a joining would want a payer and none stood. He made nothing of any of it.

Then he put the question to his hand. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its low note; under the floor the fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; over his head the boards kept their nails and their knot. Three counts in three places and none rose into another. In the room that does not touch, a count had ended some time before and a little silence stood after it, whole and empty on a place of its own, an object and not a miss, never crossed to make a stroke come out even.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and carried one rhythm across the little silence --- not borrowing a second count, not filling the interval, not making a stroke in a row that had none, but taking the empty interval as an object in its own right --- and came down on the river's next low note. It was one hundred and four, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and three with no payer named, an honest coordination, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of the likeness and would not let it stand up as a gift.

Two hundred and sixty-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and eighty-four days were told by nobody; one hundred and three honest answers had become one hundred and four and none was a reach. The carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid two hundred and sixty-four days and its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung; the grain lay covered and unfigured with the arithmetic of Smoke 30 still five mornings off and not knowing what it cost; the ownerless question stood at five askings with no witness and no row named; the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 was carried and not named. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two month-names was set right or left wrong. The little silence behind the river's note stayed whole and uncrossed on its own place. The file lay open on the towel with every tray parted and every gap left gap, and the hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.
