## Chronicle

On the twenty-fifth of Smoke the house took the hand back as a bench takes back a tool, and the boy came into his two hundred and sixty-fourth day. Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot and said the house would keep it. She kept her finger above the charcoal, set the pot handle two inches out, and lifted a corner of the wool. Fire, river and boards kept three counts that did not agree, for agreeing any two would want a payer and none stood. The hand held for the small intake and came down on the next note (one hundred and three); in the small hours it carried one rhythm across the silence after a count in the other room (one hundred and four).

On the twenty-sixth she drew the wool wholly back, turned the grain piece by piece in her palm for damp and rot, set each piece where it lay and covered it without a figure or a word. The boy watched as a surgeon watches a wound turned and kept the finding that a thing may be handled closely and still enter nothing --- not a lesson, not a grievance, laid across nothing. At the well Ilva drew with the pail against her knee and answered, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account; her notched birch stayed in her sleeve and no tenth hand was filed. A wrong wind off High Kiln brought the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards and took it out. Mid-afternoon a voice with no owner reported over the boards that somebody had been asking which row went quiet; the weather and the price of barley answered, and nobody leaned in. The hand held through one whole interval of the river (one hundred and five), then laid two rhythms apart (one hundred and six).

On the twenty-seventh the week went out as it had come in. The grain was turned once under the wool with a lighter touch and learned nothing; the pot handle stood two inches out; a fist went up toward the well and back into its sleeve. Tarn brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped as a remembered ninth stopping; Jorra held his bent finger in the cold damper ahead of an unfinished sentence. The hand came down before the letting-go of a counted breath (one hundred and seven). Then, in the small hours, it held past the breath and past the next note to the one after, across two intervals with the silence between kept empty and no second house count borrowed (one hundred and eight). He made nothing of it and would not let it stand as a gift. Two hundred and sixty-six stood exact, unspent and spoken by nobody; the file lay open with every gap left gap, and the hand lay back against the fold.
