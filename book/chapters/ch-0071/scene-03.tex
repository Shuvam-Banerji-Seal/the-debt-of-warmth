\daymark{265}{Smoke 26, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the small hours went on in their own manner, and the hand lay against the fold where the hundred and fourth answer had put it down --- an inch taken toward a low note, one interval held whole, the coming-down on the note after it, a tool returned to the place where the next hand would find it without looking. In the dark two hundred and sixty-four became two hundred and sixty-five; six months and eighty-four days became six months and eighty-five. Nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so, or knew there was a number to say. \person[Corin]{The boy} had stopped expecting anyone to tell him, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer who had gone without a name for two hundred and sixty-five days. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung, touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, rack, well, road, basket or bed since, because a roof and a rack and a bed are not openings.

Smoke 26 came on its own morning, earned out of its own light, and was not carried back into a day that had already spent its own. There would be no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on a Sixthday; Smoke 30, the third and last reading of the firing, stood four mornings ahead and was not reached for. The first smell was hot clay under tallow, a kiln fed and left to work; under it wet wool sour with the long lodging of a body carried since before first grey; under that, finer and more persistent, the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw} --- \lore{Ash} so long in the air that it had ceased to be an event and become a condition of the room. The first sound came up through \place{the kiln mouth}: four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, a kiln drawing in fours with a gap. It was not a bell. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and over his head \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Four and a gap here, water over water there, nails and a knot above him: three counts in three places, and nothing in the house made one of them answer another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to either.

He took the body in the order the facts arrived, the way a surgeon takes a field. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, neither going forward nor going away; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder in a body of two hundred and sixty-five days; the surgeon entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. She told it the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} in the tone another woman might have used for \emph{I}, and stood a moment counting something under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on \place{the loaf shelf}, and shut her hand half over nothing. At the shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread, and moved her lips along a two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. Then she took up the basket and carried him out at the half-pace from every open slate, giving every instruction to the wool and the wind and none to the basket. The fold at his ear went right without a look at his face, and the thread stayed frozen. None of it entered a slate, and no number was spoken over the morning.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Clay Yard} the morning went around its walked-around places the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. Sella gave the broom its business before she gave it to anybody, and went wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline beside it, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print or word to say what had gone, \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} bare and dark beside it and its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. She gave each a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. He had been carried past these places often enough to know the shape of the going-around, and had learned to expect nothing from it and to note everything.

At \place{the drying racks} the dust lay meal-fine on the lower rails, and the yard went on with its work without turning toward the basket. Cold had come back to his cheek in the open air and sat there while Sella set the basket down where no slate lay. At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone, set away from the hearth, waiting for the arithmetic that Smoke 30 would make over it. The wool was the colour of old tallow and the stone under it held the chill of a thing that had never been near the fire. She stood over it a moment, with the little-door burn shut, and then she drew the wool wholly back.

What she did next she had not done on the mornings before, and the boy watched it with the attention a surgeon gives to the turning of a wound. She laid the wool aside and put her hand flat among the pieces of grain, and turned them over one by one --- not lifting them away, not counting them, but rolling each piece in the flat of her palm the way a potter turns a cup in both hands to find the thin place in the wall. Her fingers felt for damp and for rot, for the soft spot that would go through a firing and spoil its neighbours, and found what they found and said nothing about it. Each piece went down again where it had lain, in the order it had lain, so that the grain was in its old place with nothing taken from it and nothing added. There was a dry smell off it, of barley and stone and the dust of the rack, and a small sound as the pieces moved against one another, like a handful of dry beans shifted in a dish. She did not speak a number over it. She did not write one. Then she laid the wool back over the grain and smoothed it with the edge of her hand until the whole of it was covered as it had been, carried the clay dish away from the hearth and set it where it belonged, and went back to the fire.

The arithmetic that would be read over the grain on Smoke 30 would know the cover and the dish and the place on the stone. It would not know what the grain had cost. The space between the figure and its price stayed unsigned. She had examined the thing as closely as a hand can examine a thing, and had entered nothing, and the not-entering was as complete as the examining.

He kept the handling on a place of its own. A surgeon turns a wound to see the part that was hidden, and learns no more than the wound is willing to show, and puts the finding where findings go without imagining that the finding has made the wound better. He had watched a woman turn grain for damp and for rot and set it down again where it lay, and he kept what he had seen as one more object: that a thing can be handled closely and still enter nothing. He did not make a lesson of it. He did not make a grievance of it, or a doctrine, and he did not lay it across the house's other not-doings --- the unswept line, the untouched mark, the unread charcoal, the missing fifth pull, the loan left uncalled --- or across his own kept empty intervals, for a joining would want a payer standing under both and none stood to either. The finding stayed where it was. The absence of a joining stayed where that was. Neither was entered, neither was spent, and he made nothing of either.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee, and the boy was set down at the edge of the work where no slate lay. The grey lip under the bucket had gone the colour of the rest of the stone, and the yard called the whole of it weather. Clay was dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she had not scrubbed it out. A woman with a pot asked what the firings of Smoke had cost. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there; the short strip of notched birch was not taken out, not read, not asked about, and not joined to the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood, or to the knots, the six pages, the tins, the charcoal under the shelf, the missing hand, the pale line where a ring had been, or any other private keeping. Her eyes went to the woman who had asked, and only then did her mouth open, and it answered the question put and not the one behind it.

\emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.}

She went on with her water, and nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. The boy filed no tenth hand-shape. The ninth stayed on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The count of firings stayed in wood where no clerk's thumb could take a figure off it. He neither read the strip nor wanted to, and did not join her to any of the others he had filed apart, and did not ask what she kept for she would have answered the question put and not the one behind it.

A neighbour hitched a pot against her hip and put a cost-question to it --- what the keeping and the storing would take now the grain lay under wool and the visits were coming --- and Sella sent her to the pot, and she went off level with the question still on her and nothing slopped. A man come down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it as though to satisfy himself what it was, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere and none was supplied to take its place. The boy read it the way he read a chart turned face-down: not a figure withheld, but a figure that had not arrived, and nothing on the page to copy. He entered neither, and supplied nothing the yard had not given.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, went wide around the seven places, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without a look at his face, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it}, counted under her breath toward no opening, and went to her own bed. The thread at his mouth stayed frozen. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong.

In the small hours he turned the file out on the towel and found it, as he found it every time, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with a seventh place kept empty and none invented to fill it; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; told bell stood beside heard and told figure beside paid; nine stoppings stood with no tenth; the held inch stood on a sixth place with a hundred and four answers; nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth on its own tray and her strip unread; the ownerless question stood at five askings with no witness and no row named; and the finding of the morning lay on a place of its own with a finger between it and the house's not-doings and a finger between it and his own kept gaps. Every tray was parted from its neighbour and every gap left gap. He made nothing of any of it.

Then he put the question to his hand. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its low note; under the floor the fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; above him the boards kept their nails and their knot. Three counts in three places and none rose into another. He took the low note as he took the others, as an object and nothing more.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and held through one whole interval of the river's own going --- not the small intake, not a borrowed count from the room that does not touch, but the river's own interval, held as a thing holds a breath it has no need to spend --- and came down on the river's next low note. It borrowed no house count and supplied no stroke to make a row come out even. It was one hundred and five, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and four with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of the likeness and would not let it stand up as a gift.

Two hundred and sixty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and eighty-five days were told by nobody; one hundred and four honest answers had become one hundred and five and none was a reach. The carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid two hundred and sixty-five days and its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay back under the wool on the cool stone at the rack end with no figure on it, and the arithmetic of Smoke 30 stood four mornings off and would not know what it cost; the figure that had come up out of a fist had gone back into the sleeve and no figure had been supplied. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn. The file lay open on the towel with every tray parted and every gap left gap, and the hand came down on the river's next low note with no stroke supplied and lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.
