The afternoon thinned without ending. It went the way a coarse cloth goes at the fold, thread by thread, until the light had no edge to it and lay on the floorboards as water lies on a flat stone, shallow and going nowhere. Heat came down out of the day and settled under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with his ear two inches from the crust. It pooled there the way warmth pools in the hollow of a seat that has been sat in for twenty years, and he had nothing to do with the making of it and could not have told it from any other warmth. A body of two hundred and twenty-eight days takes heat the way a vessel takes it: without thanks, without a figure, without the least notion of who had paid to put it there. The surgeon's register set the finding down in the order he had learned to set things down --- what it was, heat; where it had come to rest, in the fold behind the pot; what it asked of him, nothing --- and the finding stood, exact, complete and worth nothing, the way a body read at two paces is worth nothing without a hand to carry it.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. An empty place kept empty is a finding; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and he had set that down on other days without its needing to be said again. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and he knew that a vessel which has cooled cannot clot and must not be rubbed too fast; neither knowing bought him the use of his own hands. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in a body of two hundred and twenty-eight days the wanting was the louder.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder that a wheel-keeper had leaned into for twenty years; the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left that nobody filled; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay along the skirting board and the new finer hairline beside it was called \lore{the thaw} and neither was swept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, a saying about honest repairs that had outlived the thing it was true about, and cost more for being said to a pot that had no crack to leave showing. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, a space in a sentence shaped like the hollow of the wheel-seat. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and twenty-eight days, and no lesson had been made of any of it, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Grain 19.

The boy took the objects apart the way he took everything within two paces, in the order of what they were and where they had stood and what they asked of him. A coat was a coat kept stiff with salt; a seat was a hollow worn by a weight; a line on a wall was a line left where it was, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. In the kiln ash a charred slate lay with the writing gone off the end, swept around for a year and never picked up, and he put it on its own place beside the coat and the line and did not lay a finger across the three. The keeping of the man was maintenance and not a story, and maintenance has no figure and no ending. He did not lay the clean dark line across the crust's empty place or across the shelf's two gaps or across the dressed grain lying covered near the hearth; three keepings in three places are three keepings and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. The grain had come off the strips as cut stalks, gone under wool, been beaten out and winnowed and spread and poured back under its fold, three changes of state and no figure over it anywhere, and the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight and not what the grain would have cost to buy back. Grain 20 stood two mornings ahead with its second reading and was not brought early into an afternoon that had its own keeping to do.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said. To the wood he said, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that the house could not check. Beside it lay the unforged collector sentence --- a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford --- and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man, and his own name was not spoken with the sentence either.

Then the ear-sentence came to his teeth as it had come on other afternoons: an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name; a remembered stopping and not a new one. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the ninth stopping stood at nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year at its very end had still not given. The loan was not called. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it on Grain 19. His chest had been tight since the dark; \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed, in \place{the Debt Loft} that reached him only as a sound with a direction, an unmarked clay pot held the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter the house's man died, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving.

He said “Yes, house” to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. He did not ask about the room and nobody asked about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

The boy kept the stopping where it lay, an object on a tray and not a rule. The ear under this roof was worth more than the debt, and the sentence had come to the word and stopped, and the stopping was an interval and not a payment, a thing the house had done and not a thing the house had said. It stood beside the other eight on their own places with a hand between it and every one of them, and no tenth stood up to join them and call itself a doctrine. He did not join it to the two ears keeping the same silence, or to the nearly full answer under the shed wall, or to the pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which was a saying and stayed a saying. Nine objects are nine objects and not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under them, and no payer stood. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and something must give at the end of the seventh year, and it had not given here.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and got no name from him, and he did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three shapings of the pot saying stood on their own place and stayed three sayings about a pot: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. None was promoted into the hearings, no cause stood up, no lesson was made, no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot was lost in this yard. \lore{The burned-slate word} had come a fourth time over the boards from \place{Slate Row}, a direction arrived nowhere and joined to no cause, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the dust or the pot or the men.

He grew quieter as the burned-slate word went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off one corner; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room.

When \person{Sella Vail}'s voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth}, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence and stood there. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back. He held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; the boy read it as he had read it before, one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. He did not pair it with the fraction held on an older day or the beat beneath the shelf or the half-check on the flagstones. Three places were three places and not a rule with a payer standing to any of them.

At the hearth Sella told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. The little-door burn opened on the pot and shut on nothing when the pot was set down. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because no opening had come in forty-eight days and an afternoon and a shelf and a pot are not openings. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the broom and the running water and the fire, and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

At the going of the light she lifted the basket level and carried him up \place{the clay road} at the half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. She went wide around the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw, neither swept; around the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood; around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called weather; around the field's empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number: no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. The absence gathered what interest it had by being left where it was, and he did not lay it across the covered grain or the shelf's two gaps or the crust's empty place. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that knew weight and not cost, and it carried him up the yard and set him on his bed under the roof and not into \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face and left the thread frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. From above came the small sound of the loft settling, a direction and nothing more, and the warm stone in its unmarked pot stayed cold.

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Sella had gone to her own bed before the dark was old and left the coals to keep themselves; there was nothing in his room to hold a rhythm against, and he borrowed none from the room that did not touch his. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-eight days; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with no seventh; the three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; the three sayings stayed sayings; the burned-slate word stood at a fourth telling and the shut-room tellings at two; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid, with the true subtraction locked in a box going up the road and answering nothing. All of it lay where it lay with a hand between one place and the next, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a payer.

He put the question to his hand the second way of the chapter. The hand went up an inch and he held it to the small intake only, the first drawing of a breath that had no count anywhere in the house to hold it against, and brought it down before the letting go. Not held past the intake, not carried through the counted breath, not kept to the river's own low interval and down on the next note, not down at once under conditions that had sometimes answered --- the second way, an object on the sixth place with no payer named. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not one of the three held changes, not a payment, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Thirty-two answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he set the hand down where a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of the work, not watched, not named, not asked to stand up and say what it was.

The hand came down before the letting-go and lay against the fold while the fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, neither stopping for the ear.
