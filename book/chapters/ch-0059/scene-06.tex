The voice came back over the boards in the afternoon, when the light had gone thin and flat on the paving and the heat under \place{the loaf shelf} had settled for good into the fold. It had no owner. It had no room, no hand at the flame and no cause to stand under it. It was a voice that had come up off the terrace on a wrong wind, gone around the yard with the price of barley behind it, and come back now to ask a plain thing low into the boards, the way a man asks the hour of a day he has no intention of keeping.

“Which row did the pot go quiet on?”

No slate answered. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in a body of two hundred and thirty days, six months and fifty days, and nobody under any roof had told him a number had been reached. The question went over the boards and not into the basket. It was not put to the wool, not put to the pot, not put to the fire or the running water or the river wind; those had their instructions and the basket had none, and a question asked across a yard is not an instruction given to a thing that cannot answer. The weather answered first and the price of barley answered after it --- \lore{the thaw} had come late and grain would be dear before the month was out --- and the voice that had asked went off up the terrace, and the row stood where it had stood. A direction had come into the yard and gone out of it, arrived nowhere, with nobody leaning in to take the answer down.

He took the question apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: what it asked, where it stood, what it would want before any man could answer it, and who would have to stand under the answer to pay for it. It asked for a row. A row is a place; a place wants a witness; a witness wants a day when somebody stands somewhere and can be asked. It offered no cause and gave no name and set no hand at the foot of it. He set it down on a place of his own with a finger between it and the three shapings of the pot saying, and a finger between it and the fourth telling of the burned slate that had come over these boards out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, and a finger between it and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, and a finger between it and the six hearings of \lore{the full note} standing beside and never among, and a finger between it and the count.

It was not a fourth shaping of the pot saying. It was not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a cause, not a name, not a fifth telling of the slate. The saying about a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw was a proverb about pots and stood with the other two as a saying; this was a question about a row, and a proverb and a question are different instruments and he kept them on different trays. Three sayings stayed three. The burned-slate word stayed at four. The shut-room tellings stayed two. He did not go looking for a witness and did not supply one; he had no hand to stand under the question and no voice in \lore{Grathic} with which to ask it, and the seed lay where he had put it, waiting on the far-off day a witness stood somewhere and could be asked.

The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat settled under the low angle, behind the pot and into the fold, where one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The place beside it kept its namelessness; no third had come to stand there and make the row come out even, and an empty place kept empty is a finding while a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no farther and it did not go away, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it and he wanted the crust. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and thirty days old the child's wanting was the louder.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder; the seat kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept beside the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, and neither had been touched by a broom. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and thirty days. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and no collector went down it on Grain 21; the ruts held what the ruts held, and the district's arithmetic had changed twice in Grain and the road stood where it had stood. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of any of it. A line left where it is is still a line kept.

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with its two unnamed gaps and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closes on everything that comes within reach of it. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stands between the two, unspoken. “The house will keep it,” she told the fire, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in fifty days, because an afternoon and a shelf and a basket and a bed are not openings. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said. To the wood he said, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that the house could not check. Beside it lay the unforged collector sentence of Garl Vail --- a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford --- and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man.

Then the ear-sentence came to his teeth as it had come on other evenings: an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name; a remembered stopping and not a new one. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the ninth stopping stood at nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year at its very end had still not given. The loan was not called. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it on Grain 21; the road stood where the road stood. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed, in \place{the Debt Loft} that reached him only as a sound with a direction, an unmarked clay pot held the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving.

He said “Yes, house” to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. He did not ask about the room and nobody asked about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

The boy kept the stopping where it lay, an object on a tray and not a rule. It stood beside the other eight on their own places with a hand between it and every one of them, and no tenth stood up to call itself a doctrine. He did not join it to the nearly full answer under the shed wall or to the saying about a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which was a saying and stayed a saying. Nine objects are nine objects and not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under them, and no payer stood.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and got no name from him, and he did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The one saying that the wrong wind had let stand over the boards --- a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- he let stand again as a saying and no more, taking it apart and setting it back where it stood. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No lesson came out of it. No name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot was lost in this yard. \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at a fourth telling, a direction come over the boards from Slate Row and arrived nowhere; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two, and he joined them neither to the dust nor to the pot nor to the men.

He grew quieter as the burned-slate word went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off one corner; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth}, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence and stood there. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back. He held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies.

At the going of the light she carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. At every open slate she kept the half-pace and went wide. She went wide around the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it called the thaw, neither swept; around the rack space beneath the lowest of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; around \place{the trough}, bare and dark, whose water was called weather; around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number --- no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no figure and no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under them, and none did. The absence went on gathering its interest on a place of its own, advanced and unpaid, and he did not lay it across the covered grain or the crust's empty place.

She set him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen. Then she went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house: nothing in his room to hold a rhythm against, and he borrowed none from the room that does not touch his. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof; the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name; through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low thing; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot.

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirty days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood the three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month has not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. The burned-slate word stood on its own place at a fourth telling, a direction over the boards from Slate Row with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood beside it with a hand between the three and stayed two. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid, the true subtraction gone up the clay road in a box that answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 and nothing for the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come back. The nine stoppings stood at nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the Seed 16 breath was unspent. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction in a wall, the beat beneath a shelf, the half-check in a yard --- with the small dry scrape from the loft sill fourth, the pale line at a gum fifth, and the held inch sixth. And on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between it and the hearings and the sayings and the tellings and the count, lay the seed of the question about the row: no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, no name and no payer, waiting on the far-off day a witness stood somewhere and could be asked.

Nothing had been joined, named, opened, spent or ended. Exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.

Then, with no breathing number anywhere in the house, he put the sixth question of the chapter to his hand. The conditions were the conditions that had sometimes answered: an inch of air between the hand and the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, a room with no count in it to borrow, and the river keeping its own low interval on the far side of the wall. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed. He took it through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended and brought it down on the river's next low note. Two rhythms, one held against the other across a wall that does not touch, remembered, not a change made again. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now he could not close two inches on the pot handle set out for him, and the two facts lay in the same small hand without either buying the other.

It was the sixth way of the chapter and the thirty-sixth answer, filed on the sixth place with no payer named to it --- not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Thirty-six answers stood on that place, each exact and worth nothing. The surgeon's register laid the whole of it down at two paces and asked the dark what it was for; the dark gave back the river's low note and nothing that could be entered.

Two hundred and thirty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. In \place{Low Kiln}, under the Vail roof, the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty days; the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over them. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with no figure over it, and the place in the returning line went around with a look and a wide step and still no number. The question about the row stood over the boards with nobody to own it --- no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, a direction arrived nowhere with a witness it would need and no witness standing yet. Grain 30, Day 239, the third and last Account-day and market, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought early into this night.

The hand came down on the river's next low note, and the count went forward exact and unspent into Day 231, Grain 22, Secondday, while the question stood over the boards with nobody to own it and the dressed grain lay covered with no figure over it.
