\daymark{229}{Grain 20, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

The day came to him first as a smell and only afterward as a gap in a sound, and he lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} and let it arrive without asking it to hurry or stay. Hot clay under tallow, tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season's name in place of a cause. Then the kiln drew in fours beyond the wall, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short, and the gap came where the fifth pull should have stood --- not a bell, for a bell is rung and counted and answered and this was none of those, only a fire keeping itself the way a man keeps his own breath without knowing that he counts it. He was two hundred and twenty-nine days old and six months and forty-nine days, and nobody who could have known what either number counted said a word about it in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, and there was no slate in the district with a place to enter either. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and twenty-nine days with no payer named to it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. Grain's second Account-day stood off in the morning and had not been brought early into the small hours; the market would come first as boards on a road, the way it always came, and only afterward as a reading.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold of grey wool and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third had come to stand there and make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and it had stopped there as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no farther and it did not go away, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it and he wanted the crust, and both were true and the child's wanting was the louder of the two. The surgeon's register took the reading at two paces, set down what it was and where it had come to rest and what it asked of him, and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone, following the bone through the swelling until the joint gave and the man's arm came down quiet at his side; he could not now lift a crust two inches to his mouth.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and twenty-nine days and none would be invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings and no rule, and a fourth likeness had not stood up among them to call itself a cause; \lore{the burned-slate word} stood at a fourth telling on a place of its own beside \person{Jorra Vail}'s dust and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, with a hand between the three, and it had come from \place{Slate Row} as a direction and not as a visit. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket on Grain 10 and still not the figure paid, with the true subtraction locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up the road and answering nothing. Nine stoppings stood as objects with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, which had come and had still not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth with thirty-two answers on it, each filed on a place of its own and none standing up to call itself a reach, a key, a power or a rule. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and not brought into this one; no eighth of either had come and neither had been named or joined. The year's first grain lay dressed and stored under its covering near \place{the hearth}, counted in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, which remembered weight and not cost, and entered on no slate. The place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back had been gone around again with a look and no number, advanced and still unpaid, and he did not lay it across the covered grain or any other emptiness. He laid nothing across any of it and named no payer.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the hearth before she came to the basket, the way she came to everything that wanted keeping: the back first, the face withheld, the hand going where it had gone ten thousand times before. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. \emph{The house}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and told the fire it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and the fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and she did not look toward \place{the bed} to see whether the boy had heard the gap. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. The dressed grain lay under its fold near the hearth and she counted it in glances and handfuls as a woman counts what she must keep and not what she could sell, and no figure came off it. When a neighbour at the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool, she sent the question to the pot and the weather with “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” and the woman went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-nine days, because a morning and a shelf and a basket are not openings.

Then she lifted the basket level and carried him up \place{the clay road} at the half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars and the river wind coming behind with the taste of water over water. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and twenty-nine days. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, neither swept; around the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called weather; around the field's empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number --- no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no figure and no payment --- and seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. She stopped short of \place{the reading block}. She set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay and stood a half pace off, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” The cold iron of the post waited at his cheek, and the boy lay two inches short of every hand that might have told him what the figure over his head was counting.

\scenebreak

The square went on assembling itself out of boards and noise. The reading block stood wet at one end where the boards had been washed, a plank on two trestles under the post's thin shade, and \place{the weighing board} stood ready with eight pigs waiting to go over it; the slap of wet hooves went into the boy's file as a direction and not as a number addressed to him. Stalls were going up across the square with the price of barley going over them and the weather going with it, two sayings travelling together without either paying for the other. Two hundred people would lean to the block and straighten and go back to their pots before the morning was out. It was a machine that read a number over a basket and moved on, and the boy lay at the edge of it with the cold iron at his cheek and his hand down against the fold, and the machine did not know he was there.

Berr Clay came up short off the clay road as he had come up short off it a hundred mornings, stopping before the reading block with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it. He smelled of hot clay and, under that, of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. The green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth, a small sweetish stain the colour of a thing that had been put in and taken out again; he had tasted it before he came and would taste it again before he went, and the masters read the habit as thoroughness and Sella read it as nerves. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned inward where the crowd could not take a figure off it. Then he passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, and only then raised them to the crowd. The order of reaching --- thumb, slate, finger, eyes --- went down into the private file as an old shape on an old place, and he did not file it twice and did not let it stand up beside the other record-first hands and call itself a rule.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” Berr said low into the top of the crowd, and the crowd leaned in because a low voice in a moot makes people come close rather than fall quiet. The figure went twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that had come late, and went nowhere that the boy could enter. “The levy is called,” he said. “It is not called by me.” To a man at the rail who had brought a number up out of a fist and wanted to know what the month's firing would want now the harvest had been dressed and stored, he said that a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, and that if \emph{your house} wanted it again there would be a slate at the watch --- \emph{your house}, never \emph{I} --- taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere. He never looked into the basket, and went off toward High Kiln with the cord folded square and the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb and the finger over the figure on a tray of their own and did not join them to any other record-first hand in the district, for a row of hands is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it and no payer stood.

The district's arithmetic changed for the second time in Grain and could not be changed back. It changed where two hundred people stood, in the air over a wet plank, and the printed one in twelve stood where it had always stood, unchallenged. A figure read aloud was still not the figure paid; the true subtraction stayed in the locked box going up the road and answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, whom no figure, collector or slate had ever cost. The dressed grain lay covered under the Vail roof and was read over by a figure that did not know what it had cost to bring it in from the strips, what hands had gone into it, or what it would take to buy it back. The reading answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back with the rest; that absence went on gathering its interest in a place of its own, advanced and unpaid, with no name, no cause, no body, no figure and no payment, and the boy did not lay it across the printed figure or the covered grain or any other emptiness. Nothing reached back under the roof. A reading is an account going into the air; it does not go down into the ground after a man and come back with an answer.

\scenebreak

The bell went four, four and a gap. It struck longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself at it, not at the reading and not at the stroke that was missing, and the heard bell lay down beside the told bell of the Long Night on a place of its own where the two could be looked at separately and not mistaken for one ringing. Four was four in the fire under the floor, four in the nails overhead, four in the bell, and the gap stood where the fifth should have stood in each of the three places and rose up in none of them. The boy let the counts lie. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any.

\person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. Her hand arrived at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd; the small flat correction of the strap came first, then the turn of the wrist that brought the edge round, and only afterward did she take in the square. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Two straps crossed her chest, the shorter one let out for the long road. When a woman at the rail asked what she carried, she said, “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the woman who wanted more she said, “Cross to \place{Greybank}. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” and went on with the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. She answered the question put and not the one behind it, and she was done with the conversation when the road was done with it, without rudeness and without apology. No figure came off the slate. The boy kept her order of reaching on its own place and did not file it twice and did not make her a reader because she carried a figure she would not look at; a runner who does not read is not the same object as a clerk who does, and her hand stayed the eighth such hand on its own place, advanced and not paid, joined to none.

Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk sat still set for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, and the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, and a buyer's number went into it before he looked up to find the man who had said it. Nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. The market sold its pots and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the stone does not rise and does not ask what it is set there for. He was no one the boy had been given a name for, not the thin child at the market's edge and not the man carried out on Day 188, and he was joined to none of them. The boy set the inward order of that hand apart from the runner's inward order and kept the two apart from Berr's twice-wiped thumb; two records going inward are two records going inward, and a row of them will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer from it, and no payer had been named to either.

Across the square \place{the pot board} stood where it stood, shallow and dusty in the hollows, and kept the little print where a cup had been turned a quarter and set back, and gave nobody a figure. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings; the boy kept them where they had been laid and did not bring either across the square to stand in this one. No eighth of either came to market, no second look was joined to the first, no loaf passed within reach of the basket, no slate came near it and no sale was made. He gave the woman no name and the shape no name, and neither was promoted into a hearing of the full note, a seventh of it, a tenth stopping, a cause, a name or a romance. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; he had put none under either row.

\scenebreak

He took the morning apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, separating what lay next to what without pretending the two had grown together. The square was a machine that read a number over a basket and moved on: the block, the bell, the twice-wiped thumb, the narrow slate going up unread, the clerk set for an uncalled measure, the board where a cup's print was kept. Not one of them could be entered by a child who could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him. He kept the printed figure on its own place and the figure read over the basket on a place that did not touch it, and kept beside both, on a third place, the knowledge that a figure read aloud was still not the figure paid. The reading had gone into the air and did not touch the person it counted; it did not touch the man carried out on Day 188 and it did not touch the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and come back with the rest missing. Nothing reaches back under a roof from a square. The file lay where it lay, exact, complete and worth nothing, and nothing in it had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

At the market's edge he put the third question of the chapter to his hand. The conditions were the conditions that had sometimes answered: the square making its low machine-noise over the top of him, pigs going over the wet board, the crowd leaning and straightening, a gap hanging where the fifth stroke of the bell should have stood. He took the hand up an inch toward that gap and held it through the small intake and the hold, the letting-go still ahead of it. It was the third way of the chapter: not down at once under conditions that had sometimes answered, not held to the small intake only and down before the letting-go, not carried through the whole counted breath and kept past the end of the count and down on a second rhythm. It went on the sixth place as the thirty-third answer, an object with no payer named to it --- not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Thirty-three answers stood on that place, each exact and worth nothing, and the surgeon's register laid the whole of it down at two paces and asked the square what it was for; the square went on selling its pots and its pigs and gave back only the price of barley and the weather.

Sella lifted the basket level at midday and went back at the half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water, the carrying arm a record in muscle that knew weight and not cost. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Berr's box went up the clay road locked with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 and nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. The bell's gap hung in the square with nobody crossing himself, and the boy's hand came down before the counted breath, with the hold still ahead of it, and lay against the fold where a man lays a tool on a bench at the end of the work.
