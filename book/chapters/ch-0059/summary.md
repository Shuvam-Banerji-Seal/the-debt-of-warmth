## Chronicle

Ninthday came under the Vail roof without a bell, only the kiln drawing in fours and stopping where the fifth should have stood. Hot clay came first under tallow, wet wool under that, and beneath all three the sour breath of the corner the house called the thaw. The boy lay at the loaf shelf's low angle, two hundred and twenty-eight days old by no slate's reckoning, with one crust two inches from his ear. Fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places; agreeing them would want a payer and none stood to any. His file was exact, complete, worth nothing.

On Grain's tenth day Sella set the basket in the moot-post's thin shade. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road, wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over the figure and read one in twelve low into the crowd, the figure going twice over the basket; the levy was called, and not by him. He went off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Hessa carried the slate up before noon unread, and the bell went four, four and a gap. The district's arithmetic changed for the second time in Grain and could not be changed back: the figure read was not the figure paid.

Back under the roof cost-questions went to the pot and the weather, and a fist's number went back into a sleeve. A wrong wind off High Kiln carried the sweet edge of lead glaze over the fold and out again, and a voice let a pot saying stand; he took it apart and set it back, a saying and no cause. Then a voice with no owner asked which row the pot had gone quiet on. The weather answered, and the price of barley after it, and the row stood where it had stood.

Through three days Sella gave no instruction to the basket, Tarn kept his seventh where the house could not check, and Jorra withheld his note. The hand answered six times --- down at once, held to the small intake, down before the counted breath, kept to the river's own interval, down at once again, and at the last an inch carried through a breath counted in a room that did not touch his and down on the river's next low note: two rhythms across a wall, remembered, not a change made again. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now he could not close two inches on the pot handle.

Two hundred and thirty stood exact, unspent, spoken by nobody. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with no figure over it. The returning-line place went around with a look and no number, and the question about the row stood over the boards with nobody to own it. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
