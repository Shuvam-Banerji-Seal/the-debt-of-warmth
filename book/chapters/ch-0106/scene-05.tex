\daymark{371}{Stone 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813, the Hearth Drawing}

The hand lay flat on the fold where it had been put, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy exact. It did not carry itself across the edge of the night; \person[Corin]{the boy} carried it, as he carried the room's small sounds and the crust's hair-lifted corner, without deciding to and without telling anybody. Three hundred and seventy-one days old, six months and one hundred and ninety-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid three hundred and seventy-one days with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the same number of days. \lore[The Hearth Drawing]{The Drawing} stood on its own morning now and would earn its own telling. Nothing behind it had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and the arithmetic gone up the road in a locked box did not come down into a day that had not earned a second figure.

The surgeon took the body in the old order, as a man takes a field before the light is good enough to work in it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and lay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a face composed by cold from a face composed by choice, and knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. The infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before the basket. “It's \lore{the thaw},” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Then she lifted him, the weight taken before the handle, the basket level and unslopped, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” She told the basket nothing; no basket under that roof had ever been told anything. She carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and nowhere on the way did a slate lie open.

\place{The Drawing house} stood two doors down \place{the Clay Yard} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, in the same clay-daubed row as the Vail house and the birth house, with a bench by the door and a pot on the bench and a draught under the door going along the floor to the corner. She did not put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark}. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a Drawing was not an opening; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on this morning. She carried him in past the bench, past the pot, and set the basket at the edge of the light where he could see and not join, level and out of the draught and never where a slate lay open. Then she went half a pace off and stood there with her hands gone still, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand shut on nothing. She would not be the one to tell a basket what the room was doing. The boy took the room by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie: a pot set down carefully, wool unwinding, a man's breath held and let go without haste, and under all of it the thin scrape of a chair moved on a trodden-ash floor.

\scenebreak

The man wore a cuff soft from washing and folded back twice, the grey of the wash worked into the seam at the wrist. A flat leather satchel lay open on the bench beside him, and from a roll of wool at his elbow came a cold iron tube, grey at the light and darker where his fingers had it. His fingertips were grey at the tips, the colour of skimmed milk gone off. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the bed; that was the order of him, and the boy filed it without having to be told, on a tray of its own with a finger between it and the other trays, and made no tenth of it and no rule of it. When the man spoke it was low and once, to the slate and not to the room. “The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded.” The hand that wrote it was an ordinary hand with no pause in it, going on from word to word as a hand goes on turning a wheel. The boy took that hand apart by what was there and what was not: no catch at the second sentence, no pressure at the third, no look up to see whether anyone had heard. He filed the ordinary hand on a place of its own and did not join it to the neat dark hand, to \person{Marra}, to the clerk at the Court's table or to the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing, though all of them had reached for a record before reaching for a person. Nine record-first hands standing in a row might call themselves a rule if a hand stood to lay them together, and none stood; the collector's hand stayed where it had been put, and no tenth stood up in the empty place.

A neighbour lay on \place{the drawing pallet} against the far wall with his hands crossed on his belly, a man past helping and past pretending he was not. The collector found the way in the neighbour's forearm by touch, feeling along the inside of the arm with two fingers as a potter feels for the flaw in a wall before he cuts, and held the tube against it without pressing. He did not hurry. He did not say what he was doing and nobody asked him. There was a reason in the way he held the iron, and the boy read it the way he read all reasons in this world, from the outside: the man believed he kept the world alive by taking what was already going. That reason was the part of him that would be remembered against him. No name came with it and no interior was given, and the boy did not go looking for one; he had learned by now that a reason found in a gesture is a thing seen from a distance and not a thing entered.

\scenebreak

The neighbour gave under the tube with a small flat knock.

It was not a wound. There was no blood. When the iron came away there was a pale round place on the arm where it had been, the colour of the inside of a shell, and the man's hand went on lying crossed on his belly as though nothing had asked anything of it. The boy heard the knock. He did not file it as a seventh hearing. A hearing wants a source standing, and this room was seen from a basket and not entered; a knock out of a body on a pallet was a thing heard from a place he could not stand in, and it went on its own place, not among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} and not into the empty seventh either. Six hearings stood beside and never among, and the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for or filled to make a row come out even. The pale round place stayed where it was: not a wound, not a payment, not a sign, not a hearing, with no payer standing under it and no hand to enter it. The surgeon in him noted the small flat sound a vessel gives when it has cracked all the way through, and noted that here the sound had come out of a man and had been answered by nothing. The infant in him wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won.

\scenebreak

At the other side of the hearth, on \place{the folded quilt}, a child three days out of a thin return had grown worse on the third day. The room called it a \lore{graft} settling. The boy looked at it as he had been looking at bodies since he was old enough to hold up his head, and what he saw was not settling. Grey stood at the fingertips, a grey finer than the collector's and nearer the bone. A red mark had come up at the hairline where the skin was thin and the light found it. The child's face had colour in it, more than it had had in the morning, and the women about the quilt took that for the turn and spoke a little quicker because of it.

The surgeon knew the order in which a body takes heat back and the order in which it gives it up, and the two orders were the reverse of each other, and the giving-up came sooner than anybody liked. Pink returning to a face was the surface still cold and not the core; a face could be pink while the small joints were going and the core was giving way behind it. Extremity before core, always; the fingers first, then the wrists, then the great vessels and the heart; and a rewarming patient who gets worse before getting better is a patient whose afterdrop is waiting on the far side of a warming that must be slow and from the outside, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew what was required. Heat back by degrees, the outside first, the whole of the attention on the small joint and the rate of it, no rubbing of a cold extremity, no pressing of a warm thing hard against a cold skin, no putting the body close enough to the hearth to cook the surface while the core went on failing. He knew the look of a patient composed by cold and the look of one composed by choice, and this child's face was the first. He had once held pressure in a cavity with both hands while somebody counted out loud behind him, and he knew the difference between a hand that holds heat in and a hand that drives it out, and he had no hand at all.

He could not speak. He could not reach the child. He could not say *leave it*, which was the sentence he had spent two lives learning how to say, and he could not say *warm it slowly*, which was the sentence that came before it. He could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth. The composed face was not composure. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this room would perform in the order it wanted, and the room called the waiting a graft settling, and the boy sat in a basket at the edge of the light with the surgeon's register open and no hand to enter anything in it. Both registers were true. The infant's wanting was louder. Neither won.

\scenebreak

The child's mother held a cup of warm milk she never drank. It stood in her two hands at her breast, and the steam went off it and into the dry cold, and she turned it a quarter and turned it back without lifting it once to her mouth. “Will he know me,” she said, “if he comes through?”

A death-house woman with her sleeves pushed up answered from the hearth side. “A child knows warmth before it knows a face.”

An older woman, the one who had the quilt corner in her hand, said, “Warmth goes in slow. You can't hurry a graft. My mother knew it and her mother knew it.” She said it to the cup and not to the mother, in the tone in which a woman tells a pot not to boil over, and then she put the corner of the quilt back where it had been, with the flat of her hand and no pressing.

They corrected him without knowing he had spoken. He had not spoken; the surgeon's register had said its sentence inside the basket, and the room had answered it as rooms do, by going on with its own work. He took the three sentences apart by what was there and what was not. Warmth before a face was true; warmth going in slow was true; nobody in the room had said what to do when the warmth was going the other way, and nobody had said what a graft was or why it settled, and the words my mother knew it and her mother knew it carried no figure and no name and no hand that stood to be asked. The room had not said hurry, and it had not said leave it, and the boy supplied neither.

A warmed stone lay at the child's belly under the quilt where the hearth-maids had set it, and a pot stood between the quilt and the door, and neither had been moved since the women came in. One of them lifted the stone at the edge of her apron and put it back, testing its heat with the back of two fingers rather than with the palm, the way a woman tests a pot before she lets a child near it. The child's hands lay outside the quilt. The grey at the fingertips had gone no further up the wrists in the last hour, which the room took for holding and the boy took for the cold going inward where nobody could see it. The mother's cup turned a quarter and turned back. Nobody set the pot nearer the fire. Nobody rubbed the small cold hands. Nobody said what the red mark at the hairline meant, and the boy did not supply it: a thing not said in a room is not a thing withheld from that room, and a basket at the edge of the light has no standing to accuse anybody of withholding.

There was a bench by the door and a pot on the bench, and a draught under the bench going along the floor to the corner, felt at the ankles before the cold was felt anywhere else. The room's nails kept four on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. The Ash ridge along the skirting board was thicker here by a finger's width than the ridge under the Vail roof and turned the corner by the door without a break. And in the figures of whatever the room kept, by the bench, by the pot, by the pallet, by the quilt, there was a place where a line should have stood and did not, the same every time the room was read. The boy set the room on a place of its own beside the birth room in \place{House Silt}, beside \place{the Vail kiln-house}, beside the room where the slate was read on market-day, with a finger between every pair, and wrote no cause across any of them. Four likenesses were four likenesses and not one account; an account wants a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand stood in that room and no such payer stood. He did not join the room to the Greybank saying, to the shut counting room of that saying, to the burned slate of \place{Slate Row}, to the Ash 5 joining or to the Ash 17 and Ash 23 directions. He supplied no sixth telling of the slate, no third telling of the shut room, no witness, no name, no room beyond this one, no second hand, no figure and no payer. The finding gathered interest against a hand not his and not yet come and was not paid on this morning. The cup went on turning a quarter and turning back, and the mother did not drink, and nobody made a lesson of the cup.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate. She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of the room, the tube, the child or the cup, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread frozen and unwiped. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift; a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, and a hand on the pot handle. The surgeon knew a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won.

In the small hours the file came out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel when a case is done and before the next is called. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty, and the Drawing's flat knock stayed on its own place outside them, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure beside the paid figure with ten readings behind it and no signature on the space; nine record-first hands with no tenth and the collector's ordinary hand filed on a tray of its own and joined to none; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; five held objects with the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-sill breath and the holding in the cold damper beside those, each on its own place; the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, the row at five askings with no witness; the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction each where it had been put and not one of them repeated or made to buy a lesson; the four rooms with the same gap held apart with a finger between every pair; the river's winter skin beside \lore{water over water}, found where put and not spent on this day; the promise under the wheel-shed neither given nor withdrawn and the loan uncalled. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under \place{the finger's-width door}, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself back flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. He filed it as answer three hundred and seventeen: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the child's grey fingertips, the mother's cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the two bites of iron, the river's winter skin, the widow's mark nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and seventeen honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine. The crust kept its hair-lifted corner and its empty place with no third; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back; the Afterdrop waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. Through the finger's-width door the river went low over lower under its skin, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Count three hundred and seventy-one exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-one days told by nobody, with the Drawing behind the household and no lesson made of it. Forward, not closed.
