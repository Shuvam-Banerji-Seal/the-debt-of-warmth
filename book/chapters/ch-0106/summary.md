## Chronicle

On the tenth day of the deep-winter month the district's arithmetic changed for the tenth time where two hundred people stood. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road with the red cord of his office folded under his coat and, under the hot clay of him, the smell of nothing at all. He wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over the figure through his sleeve, and read one in twelve twice over the basket --- once for the moot, once for the late households --- denying the levy with *your house*, never *I*. The block answered nothing; he went off with the box locked and Stone's first true subtraction inside beside the nine, believing he had done a kindness. Hessa Rale took the slate up toward Greybank before noon with the writing to her sleeve, answering that she carried it and did not read it. The bell went four, four and a gap, and nobody crossed himself.

The boy in the basket kept the count nobody under any roof kept: three hundred and sixty-nine days, then three hundred and seventy, then three hundred and seventy-one, six months and one hundred and ninety-one days told by nobody. Ten readings stood behind one unsigned figure and ten were not one account; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no doctrine came out of ten. On the eleventh morning Ilva drew at the well and answered what Stone's firings had taken with *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, her hand to her sleeve before her eyes to the asker and the strip of birch unread.

On the twelfth morning Sella Vail carried him two doors down to the Drawing house and set the basket at the edge of the light, where he could see and not join. The collector, cuff soft from washing and fingertips grey as skimmed milk gone off, set a cold iron tube against the neighbour's forearm by touch without pressing. The neighbour gave under it with a small flat knock and was left with a pale round place, not a wound and no blood. On the other side a rekindled child went worse on the third day, grey at the fingertips and red at the hairline, while the room called it a graft settling and the mother held a cup of warm milk she never drank. The surgeon knew that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core; he could not speak, reach or warm. Under the Vail roof the house kept four pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. She carried him home and said nothing of where they had been, and the hand outside the wool came down on the three hundred and eighteenth answer, laid where the next hand would find it without looking.

- **Day and time at the end:** Day 371, Stone 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813, the very end of the day, after the Hearth Drawing of that morning and the going of the light. - **Corin/Dan:** 371 days old, carried to the Drawing and set at the edge of the light where he could see and not join, carried home and set on his bed under the roof, never in the Debt Loft. Crust two inches off with its hair-lifted corner, its empty place unnamed and no third; thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint; Afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody performs. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle; both true, the second louder, neither won. New this chapter: he read the rekindled child's third-day worsening in the surgeon's register --- grey at the fingertips, red at the hairline, a pink face that was surface still cold and not the core, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a slow outside warming --- and knew he could not speak, reach, warm or say *leave it* or *warm it slowly*. No new wound, no rank change.
- **Sella Vail:** carried him at the half-pace from every open slate on all three days, instruction to the wool, the river wind and the fire, never to the basket; hearth before basket at both ends, coals told the thaw and turned with a broken pot's back, *The house will keep it*. Widow's mark untouched on a market-day, a well, a yard and a Drawing, for none was an opening and no eighth was made due. Twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, both proverbs to pot and fire; dressed grain turned under wool with no figure and no handful; charcoal unread beneath her finger on top of the shelf; lips on a two-gapped row figured neither way; pot rim two inches out with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; her hand rested the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away and left the thread where it lay. At the Drawing she stood half a pace off and told the basket nothing.
- **Tarn:** on Stone 10 and 11 afternoons at the kindling --- six in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check, the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, knife at the very edge of its sill, hand over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach. *Six counted. - **Jorra Vail:** meal-fine grey slate dust white in a fist-crease off the wheel-shed; three or more men unnamed gone low into the back room before first grey and no name and no word that the room was cold; dusty forearms parted from clean as clot from vessel; spoke to the wheel before any person (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*) and told a pot *You'll do*; withheld the full note three turns ahead from any High Kiln master; bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, one iron with two bites, taken out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. *He's not Vail* sealed; promise under the shed neither given nor withdrawn.
- **Berr Clay:** on stage only on Stone 10 morning at the Cross --- tenth arithmetic read one in twelve twice over the basket, levy called and denied with *your house*, never *I*, kindness said low to the block, box locked with Stone's first true subtraction inside beside the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone and Ash. - **Ilva:** on stage only on Stone 11 morning at the yard well, pail against her knee, rope stiff in the cold, clay pale in her nail crescents and grey rack dust in one thumb seam, hand to sleeve before eyes to asker and mouth after both. - **The Drawing collector (walk-on, no name):** on Stone 12 at the Drawing house, cuff soft from washing and folded back twice, flat leather satchel, cold iron tube out of a roll of wool, fingertips grey the colour of skimmed milk gone off; hand to the slate before eyes to the bed; said once to the slate *The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded.* His ordinary writing hand with no pause in it filed on a tray of its own, not joined to the neat dark hand, to Marra, to the Court-table clerk or to the soft-cuffed clerk, and no tenth hand stood up. He believed he kept the world alive by taking what was already going; no interior, no name. The neighbour on the drawing pallet gave under the tube with a small flat knock and a pale round place left on the arm, not a wound and no blood. The rekindled child grew worse on the third day; the mother held a cup of warm milk she never drank and asked whether he would know her; a death-house woman answered that a child knows warmth before a face and an older one that warmth goes in slow and a graft cannot be hurried. The boy supplied no cause, no name, no witness and no lesson.
