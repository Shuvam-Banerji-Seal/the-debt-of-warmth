By midday the dry heat had got into everything. It lifted the oiled paper at the windows and took the water out of the clay faster than the women could set their pots to it, and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind and went over the yard like a hand passed over the rim of a bowl. A kiln at its heat will give a wet pot a note before its time, a small bright sound with air under it, and the woman who set the pot down would not hear it; nobody under any roof had been given an instruction to tell her. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the basket at the edge of the light with the fold at his ear and the crust two inches off, and the kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, with the gap where the fifth should have been. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never invented to stand up as a seventh. He filed the early note as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer, and no room was changed by it and no pot was lost.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the rope came up dark to the last strand and laid itself on the stone lip with a small slap. A neighbour with a pot come out of the tubs set it down level and asked what the firing would want now the kilns were hot. She asked it of the pot first and of \person{Sella Vail} second, in the order the yard uses when it wants an answer from a thing that cannot refuse.

“The weather's set it,” Sella said. “Ask the pot. It's had the heat and kept it. The river'll take what's over.”

She gave no figure. The neighbour stood with her mouth open on the next part, then took up her pot by both handles and carried it off level as she had brought it, nothing slopping, nothing priced, nothing refused. The question went with her and arrived where such questions arrive, in the air over the ruts and not in any slate the boy could see. He had known a man put a question to a chart and get the neat margin back for an answer; here the margin was the weather, the chart was the yard, and the answer was a direction to a pot. It was exact. At two paces it bought him nothing.

A man with clay on his wrists came up and said the morning was a cold. A woman at the bucket said it was a cold and a wet one under it. The man said the wet had gone off in the night and that was \lore{the thaw} come early; the woman said it would go when it went, which was what her mother had said before her. There the argument ended, as such arguments end, in weather. A thing called weather belongs to nobody, and a man can carry it home without owning the day or entering it anywhere. Sella gave the bucket its instruction and did not say which of the two had it right. Her arm had carried the boy a hundred and seventy-one mornings and kept the weight in muscle and not the cost; it was the whole of the record that arm kept.

\scenebreak

At \place{the loaf shelf} she set the basket low near the bench leg, at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof. From that low angle the dark waiting under the front edge came into his view: the charcoal marks, some pressed hard, some faded nearly into the grain, waiting the way a record waits in a language nobody has asked him to read. He did not read them. He did not count them or copy them. A mark is a record only when a man knows what it is a record of, and nobody under this roof had told him whose marks these were or what a stroke of them stood for. The marks waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.

Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along her own row, and the row stood with two gaps in it: the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. Her finger did not go beneath the board this day. On the morning of Day 166 it had gone under and stayed a counted breath without a look or a word, and that beat stood where it had been left, a change held and unspoken. It was not repeated by being remembered. A hand held under a board once and a hand kept away from it on another morning are two states of the same woman, and the boy kept them on separate places rather than make the second stand in for the first. There was no instruction under this roof for saying why either had happened, and Sella gave none to the shelf, the row, the board or the wool.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The two shelf gaps stayed in one room and the crust's gap stayed in another. The shallow pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own. Out in the yard the field's empty place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, was gone around with a look and a wide step and no number. He had kept the absences apart by hand, the way an instrument goes back into its own hollow with nothing resting across it. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line. There is a saying of the loaf-shelves of \place{Low Kiln} that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together, and that an account then wants a name; no finger under any roof had laid these together, and the account had no name and no payer.

\scenebreak

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else.

Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among: five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, filed beside the five and never among them. There was no seventh, and he did not invent one to fill the empty place.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra}'s withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings; a knowing kept back from a kiln-master was a different object from a note that had actually sounded, and he did not let the one stand in for the other. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check. Beside them \lore{the damper} lay as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful.

On the widened tray lay nine stoppings of the near-saying --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. A tenth had not stood up out of the nine; the seventh year had not given; the loan that can hear was uncalled; the breath he had drawn on Seed 16 was still unspent at the top of every reach. He did not call the loan. He did not stand a tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned, because a tenth stood up early would be a debt invented to make the nine into a bill.

Beside these, on a place of its own, lay the morning's third movement of the equality: a stroke checked for half a beat behind a finger standing in the iron while a sentence was allowed to finish, with both men looking elsewhere and neither naming the other's silence. Beside it, but not beneath it and not upon it, stood the fraction from the morning of Day 161, the hand that had waited for a sentence to finish. Beside that, on a third place touching neither, stood the held beat beneath the loaf shelf from Day 166. Three held changes, and he would not let them stand up and call themselves a pair, still less a doctrine. A doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of the three. The told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself; the kiln's missing fifth behind the wall and the boards' knot the width of a thumb overhead were two other counts in two other places, and the three did not become one.

He had learned, in rooms whose walls were cleaner than this one, what a finding costs when it is carried into a chart with a signature at the bottom: three likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a cause if a man leaves them standing and wants an answer badly enough. The refusal was the keeping. He laid no finger across the three changes and wrote no cause over them, and the file went back into its hollows, exact and worth nothing at two paces.

\scenebreak

At the hearth Sella glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and did not touch it; her hand, which had started toward it, shut half over nothing and went back to the pot in her own hand, the little-door burn closing on the rim and then on air. The jar was the house's own cold record of what a wet thing set down in the wrong place would cost by the thaw; she did not open a record she meant to keep.

“It was the thaw,” she told the fire. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-one days.

At the going of the light she set him on his bed under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and she had never told anybody.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. In the other room her own bed waited, and the two rooms did not touch.

He wanted the crust, the breast, the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got none of them, which was not the same as not wanting them. One hundred and seventy-one stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its gap; through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note; under the floor the fire missed its fifth with its payer unnamed. Three counts in three places, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. None rose up and became the others. The charcoal under the loaf shelf waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come, and the two rooms did not touch.
