At midday \place[the yard well]{the well} had the yard’s wet work in it. The rope came up dark to the last strand and laid itself on the stone lip with a small slap, and the women spoke to the bucket and not to one another, because a bucket can be told a thing and go on being a bucket. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the basket at the edge of the light with a fold of wool at his ear and the crust two inches off, and the note of water going over water came through the finger’s-width door behind him and went on coming, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note}. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down against the fold. It could go no further. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb, and he would count them again when the light went.

A neighbour came up with a wet pot held level in both hands, water in the hollow of it slopping at nothing and going still as she stopped. She set it on the stone lip where the rope had lain and looked at \person{Sella Vail} with the look of a woman who has already decided what the answer will be and means to have it said aloud.

“What does a firing cost, set down in this house?” she said. “I’ve a pot come out of the tubs and it wants the kiln before the week’s out.”

She said it again, louder, to the wet pot rather than to the woman. “What’ll a firing want, set down here?” The pot gave back its own small wooden sound and nothing else. The boy had once watched a man put a question to a chart and wait for the margin to answer; the chart had answered in its own neat hand, which was to say not at all. This was that again, in a yard that smelled of clay giving up its water to the dry cold.

Sella did not answer the woman. Her eye went once to the clay jar on the shelf by the wall, cold under its glaze, and came away; her hand, which had started toward it, shut half over nothing and went back to the pot in her own hand, the little-door burn closing on the rim and then on air. She had not touched the jar. She had not needed to. The hand had gone to the record before the hand went to the person, as it goes in other rooms before a face is looked at, and the gesture arrived nowhere and left no entry. The glance had gone to the jar because the jar was the house’s own cold record of what a wet thing set down in the wrong place will cost by the thaw; the hand did not touch it because a record is not opened by a woman who means to keep it. That was his reading of it, and it was exact, and at two paces it bought him nothing.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

The neighbour stood a moment with her mouth open on the next part, then took up her pot by both handles and carried it off level as she had brought it, her elbows tight to her sides, nothing slopping, nothing priced, nothing refused. The question went with her and arrived where such questions arrive, which was in the air over the ruts and not in any slate the boy could see. The surgeon’s register in him read it as a direction sent to an instrument and brought back without a figure: an order given where a number had been asked, and the number neither entered nor denied. He had known charts closed over a finding with the neat margin left showing; this was the same grammar in a clay yard, with wet rope for ink.

Sella’s arm had carried him a hundred and seventy mornings and kept the weight in muscle and not the cost. That was the whole of the record it kept. She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Go around,” she said to it. “It cost enough to come in; it’ll cost more to take out.”

\person{Tarn} took it up as a man takes up a tool already named. “Yes, house,” he said to the ones who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and again to the ones who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at \place{the kindling} where the house could check; the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge with \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}’s unforged collector sentence beside it. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch where an older stroke had stopped, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came.

A kiln-hand come up on the wrong wind let the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} go past him from \place{High Kiln} and said to the boards that the kilns were at their heat and a pot set down wrong would sing before its time. Nobody made a lesson of it. The boy filed the saying as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer, and kept the early note on its own place. The danger gathered without a hand to spend it, as heat gathers behind a screen while a man stands at the gate with a towel he will not use.

Carried past \place{the drying racks} at the half-pace, he watched the yard go around what it would not enter. The women went wide around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that was called the thaw, and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went around with a look and a wide step and no number, as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. Dry slip dust lay in the rack shadows and came up under the women’s nails and went back to the boards with their pots. The space under the lowest rack kept its broken slates and entered nobody; the field’s empty place kept its shape on a place of its own and was not laid across the shelf’s two gaps, the crust’s gap, or the round ring the shallow pan had left when it went off the wall. A yard that goes around an absence is a yard that keeps it without entering it, which is an account kept in feet rather than ink. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

At the loaf shelf she set the basket low, near the bench leg, at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof. From that low angle he could see the front edge of \place{the loaf shelf} and the dark waiting under it where the charcoal marks lay. They waited there as a record waits in a language nobody has asked him to read. He could have read a chart in a room he remembered; he could not read six strokes of charcoal under a board because nobody had told him whose strokes they were. A mark is only a record when a man knows what it is a record of. Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along her own row, and the row stood with two gaps in it: the old space at the end where a loaf had once stood, and the palm’s breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. A rack woman with dry slip dust in the creases of her wrists reached past the bare board with her hand before her eyes found the basket and asked what the row would want now the kilns were hot; Sella told the pot not to be set near the door and said it would pay for that in the thaw, and the woman went off with her pot and no figure.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The two shelf gaps stayed in one room and the crust’s gap stayed in another, and the pan’s gone ring stayed on a tray of its own, and the field’s empty place stayed out in the yard, and none of them touched another. He did not lay them together and call them one absence, because a man who lays four absences together has already chosen which hand to believe. He did not read the charcoal, did not count it, did not copy it. His finger stayed inside the wool. The marks waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. Sella’s finger did not go beneath the board this day; the held beat from beneath it stood where it had been left, on a place of its own beside the fraction at the cold \lore{the damper}, and the two did not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to either. The told bell stood beside the heard bell, and the kiln’s gap under the wall was not laid across either.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without a word as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the place where the shallow pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, around the bare dark \place{the trough} at the well, and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is carried level and not told why it turns. He had no name for the turns and did not ask for one.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and she had never told anybody. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. In the other room her own bed waited, and the two rooms did not touch.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy days. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its own gap; through the finger’s-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places, and none became the others. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and the knot the width of a thumb.

One hundred and seventy stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. He lay with the crust two inches off and the empty place beside it unnamed, and wanted the crust and the breast and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got none of them, which was not the same as not wanting them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. The pot rim stood out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand; the fire missed its fifth under the floor; the two rooms did not touch.
