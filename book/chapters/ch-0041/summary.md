## Chronicle

The last working week opened with the kilns brought up to heat and the boy one hundred and seventy days old under a roof where nobody counted him. Hot clay came in before the light, under tallow and wet wool, and the kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours with a gap. Under the floor the carried fire answered with four low pulls and the same silence; its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy days. Sella Vail gave her instruction to wool, fire, pot, broom and running water, never to the basket, said *the house* where another woman would say *I*, kept her half-pace and left the widow's mark where it hung, for a morning and a shelf are not openings. She set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed.

Costs went where costs go in that house. A neighbour with a wet pot who asked what a firing cost was sent to the pot with *The house has said it*; she carried the question away level, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused. At the racks the yard went around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the broken birth-slates and the empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15, with a look and a wide step and no number. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze; Sella glanced at it once and did not touch it.

On the second morning men went low into the finger's-width-open back room and got no name. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust ground small as meal on both forearms, told a pot it would do, knew its full note and withheld it from a High Kiln kiln-master who said a pot singing early would sing itself out early; no pot was lost. Then, while Sella's unfinished sentence waited and Jorra's finger stood in the cold iron of the damper, Tarn's broom checked half a beat behind it on the flagstones and went on without a word or a turn of the head. He set the movement beside the fraction at the damper and the held beat beneath the loaf shelf --- three held changes and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer.

On the third day Tarn told the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, kept the seventh where the house could not check it and held the sentence about an ear under that roof at his teeth without saying the word that would call the loan. No collector came. In the small hours the file lay exact and worthless: six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings with no payer named. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent. The fire missed its fifth; the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
