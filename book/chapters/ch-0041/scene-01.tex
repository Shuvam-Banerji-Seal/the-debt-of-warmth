\daymark{170}{Tallow 21, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay} came into the room before the light did, under tallow and wet wool and the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years; it was the smell of the yard giving up its water to the dry cold, and it was the first thing \person[Corin]{the boy} knew of the last working week. Under the smell came a sound that was not a bell. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, and there was a gap where the fifth breath should have been, the way a sentence can carry a hole in it that everybody hears and nobody fills. He lay on \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in \place{Low Kiln}, one hundred and seventy days old and five months and twenty days, and nobody under any roof said so, because there was no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number.

The fold was at his ear and under his chin, wool gone damp where his breath had settled and frozen again in the dry heat. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery since the planting, with an empty place beside it that had no name, had never needed one, and would not call a third to keep it company. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and there was no instruction for that sound either. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. He had counted them in the dark. He would count them again.

Cold had come in its known order in the night, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. Want had come up small and importunate behind it, for the crust two inches off and for a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and had got neither. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted; that was not the same as not wanting it. The man who had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and known the exact order in which a body fails lay in a fold of wool and could not lift his own head, and the two of them argued in the small room without a word: the surgeon's dry register, the infant's hunger. The hunger was louder.

\person{Sella Vail} came in with the light and put the day in order without once putting it into words. She gave the wool its instruction, and the fire its instruction, and the pot its instruction, and the broom its instruction, and the running water at \place{the yard well} its instruction; she did not give the basket one. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the pot: “Don't set that near the door. You'll pay for it in the thaw.” To the fire, later, when she had turned the coals with the back of a broken pot: “It was \lore{the thaw}. The house will keep it.” She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and \emph{the boy} where another would have said \emph{my son}, and had not said either in all his days. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing as she reached for the wool and shut half over the fold of it when she smoothed it, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals where she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with that wrist.

She stood a half-pace from every open slate, though there was no slate open under the roof, and went around the clean dark line in the corner as she went around a pail set where a child might knock it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go; then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was the only thing in the room that admitted anything. She did not look toward the bed.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for one hundred and seventy days. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its own gap, and through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and had never been invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places --- the fire through the floor, the kiln behind the brick, the river through the door --- and overhead the boards kept their four nails, four, and the knot. None of them rose up and became the others. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

Carried at the half-pace past \place{the kiln mouth}, he watched the yard go around what it would not enter. The women went wide around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that was called the thaw, and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went around it with a look and a wide step and no number, as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. At the well \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its skin of ice gone, and the water in it was called weather. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze; Sella glanced at it once and did not touch it. The shallow clay pan that had stood mouth-up against the wall through Reed was gone these many mornings, and its round empty ring kept the exact width of its absence on a tray of its own, not laid across the shelf's two gaps, the crust's gap, or the field's empty place. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. He came up over \place{the flagstones} with his chest tight from the dark, his narrow shoulders bent, and Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, so that no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. “Yes, house,” he said to the early thaw and again to the late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark on the sill at \place{the kindling} where the house could check; the seventh stood at the very lip where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge of its sill with \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it. He had brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it all the way to his teeth on other mornings and it had gone no further; the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of each reach, and the sentence was not said. In the clay pot in the loft above \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold. He could no more reconcile the stone with the debt than the house's kindness with the account he kept of it, and had stopped trying, which was not forgiving.

His stroke went over the flagstones and past the shallow bright patch where an older stroke had stopped, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came. Nine stoppings stood on the tray where they had stood, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine, and the seventh year had not given. He told the kindling what he told it every morning: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” The broom went on.

The danger of the firing month gathered in the dust ground small as meal on the wrong wind and in the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} carried up from \place{High Kiln}. At the racks a woman set a pot down on the boards and went on with the pot in her other hand; the pot gave a high thin note before its time, a small bright sound with air under it, the note of a vessel with a hairline gone all the way through or a wall too thin for the heat it would be asked to hold. The woman did not turn. Another woman near her turned her head a degree and went back to her work, and nobody said what the sound was or made a lesson of it. The boy filed it as a danger and not as a fourth near-full answer, and no room was changed by it and no pot was lost that morning. A note early was a note spent before its time; a note known and held back was a different thing on a different tray, and he kept the two apart.

He read the firing the way he had once read a room where heat was kept and counted behind a screen while he stood at the gate with a towel in his hands: a theatre he could not enter, with heat held behind a wall and counted in fours, a gate of two bites, and a cost paid by a man whose name was not spoken under any roof. The heat was kept. The cost was kept. The keeping and the cost stood in different places and did not add up to an account that any man would read aloud. His file was exact and worth nothing at two paces. An inventory is not a key. A list is not a hand. He was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it, with a child's wrist and a crust two inches out of reach.

The fraction from the morning of Tallow 12, when a hand had waited at the cold \lore{the damper} for a sentence to finish, stood where it had been left. The held beat from beneath \place{the loaf shelf} stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it. He did not repeat either, did not lay one across the other, did not let the two of them stand up and call themselves a pair, still less a doctrine. Two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell. Nothing under the roof said \lore{Grathic} over any of it; the surgeon's register coloured the noticing and kept out of the way of it, as a man keeps a hand off a field he cannot enter. Three counts in three places, and none became the others.

At mid-morning the broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and went over it again, without once turning along it. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its gap; through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note, never counted among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire missed its fifth, its payer unnamed for one hundred and seventy days. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. One hundred and seventy stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof.
