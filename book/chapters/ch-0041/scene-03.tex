\daymark{171}{Tallow 22, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

Before first grey the men came up \place{the clay road} with their collars high and went low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}. There were three of them, or four; \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the basket at the edge of the shed's shadow and did not count them, because a number given to men who had been given no name would be a number with no place to stand, and he had learned to leave such numbers where they lay. They went in stooping, the way a man goes into a room the door has promised to be higher than it is, and the door kept its finger's width of open after them, as it had kept it for three years. \person{Sella Vail} carried past at the half-pace with the basket level and the little-door burn shut on the handle, and turned no head toward the door, and did not say the room was cold. She had said it once, years back, to nobody in particular, and the saying had gone into the yard and come back without an answer, and she had not said it since. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning was not an opening. Two tellings of a room shut up in the war with the men inside dead of the breath stood side by side in the boy's file, one from a man with road-mud on his boots, one from a second man with a bag, each eighteen leagues east and each dated, and no third came this morning to stand beside them.

Under the broken pot the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been; its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-one days. Behind the wall the kiln drew breath in fours with its own gap, and through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Three counts in three places, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb; none of them rose up and became the others. The sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind and went over the yard like a hand passed over the rim of a bowl, and under it the clay gave up its water to the dry cold. That was the month's danger and it carried no lesson. A pot set down wrong would sing before its time in a dry heat like this, and the woman who set it down would not hear it; the boy filed the saying as a danger of the yard and not as a fourth near-full answer, and the early note stayed on a place of its own.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed before the light had settled where it meant to fall. Fine grey slate dust was ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; it had come off something scraped or burned, and it had been worked in by the same hands that would presently take up wet clay. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray in the boy's file, two states of the same morning, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, where the yard went around them and entered nobody; a whole slate has a place, and a broken one has a place that is gone around, and nobody under this roof had ever told him which was which. He wanted to know. He had wanted to know for a hundred and seventy-one days and could not ask, which was its own way of keeping a thing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to it. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel gave back its small wooden complaint under his palm, and he set a pot up and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. His hands went in with the bent finger a little ahead of them, and the clay came up damp and obedient on the edge of the wheel. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands; the knowing stood in the boy's ear as a fact and went onto the near-full tray, beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

The boy watched those hands from under the wool and read them the way he had once read a resident's hands at a bedside: not what they said but what they had been taught to do before the thinking caught up with them. The bent little finger led because a splinter at eleven had taught it to lead; the dust lay past the bend because the dust had gone on working after the finger had stopped. Two facts a forearms' width apart, and neither the cause of the other. He had learned to keep facts in separate pockets, the way an instrument goes back into its own hollow on the towel with nothing resting across it. If he laid the dust and the bent finger and the withheld knowing together and called them a finding, he would have a man he could enter, and a man entered in that fashion is a man who can be spent. He kept the three apart. The wheel went on turning and the dust went on lying where it lay.

A kiln-master come up from High Kiln on the wrong wind stopped with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs and listened to the pot under Jorra's hands. “That one's singing before its time,” he said. “A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early.”

Jorra's eyes went to the door and came back without reporting. He said nothing. The kiln-master took the silence for the agreement men take silence for and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost in the yard that morning. The boy kept the withheld knowing where it was, on the tray with the other near-full answers, and made no fourth out of it and no hearing out of the early note.

\scenebreak

Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to the man. “Go around,” she said to the broom. “Take the road last.” Then to the fire, with her voice going down the way it went down when a sentence wanted a word she would not hand it: “The fire's low. It wants---”

Jorra's bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and stopped. It went in ahead of his hand as it had gone in on Tallow 12 and again on Tallow 15, but this time it did not move the tongue at once. The iron took the warmth out of the fingertip and gave a little back in the reverse of the core, the two bites of it shut about nothing. The boy read it as he had read it a hundred mornings: one piece of iron with a bite that holds a vessel whole and a bite that can take the hand off a man, and no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. He filed it as an object joined to no kiln. Jorra held the iron while Sella finished her sentence.

“---feeding,” she said, and gave the end of it to the pot instead of to him.

Then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. He gave nobody a word about the waiting. Nobody under the roof spoke of it. No payer stood to the fraction and no slate was entered.

\person{Tarn} had come up the yard with the broom after it had been given its instruction, and had started his stroke on \place{the flagstones} before the sentence was ended. The bristles went over the shallow bright patch the stones had kept since Seed 22, the place where an older stroke had stopped and that nobody had entered. Behind the standing finger, with the sentence just finished and the iron still holding, the stroke checked for half a beat. It was not a stop. A stop would have been a thing that anybody could see; this was the small hesitation a man makes when his hand remembers a place his head has told him to forget. Then the broom went on toward the road without a word and without a turn of the head. The stones kept the shallow bright patch where this stroke too had stopped.

Neither man named the other's silence. Jorra did not look up from the wheel; Tarn did not look toward the damper. The household straightened around the two silences the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and no word went into the air because there was no instruction under any roof for saying it.

To the surgeon's register the half-beat had a shape and a length: the small distance between a hand's intention and a hand's arrest. He measured it without a clock, as he had once measured a pulse against his own breath, one interval and no figure written beside it. It was the sort of thing a man notes in the margin of a chart and never carries into the body of the note, because writing it there would make somebody answer for it. Here nobody had been given an instruction to answer, so the margin stayed a margin and the note stayed clean.

The boy felt the thing before he understood it. He had seen the half-check once, on Seed 22, and it had not come back in all the days between; and now it had come back changed, in the very breath of a sentence allowed to finish and a finger standing in the iron, with both men looking elsewhere. It was not a reconciliation. It was not a hearing and not a payment. No slate was entered, the loan that can hear was uncalled, and the nine stoppings stood where they had stood, nine objects on the interval tray and no tenth among them. He had known a chart to close over a finding with the neat margin showing, and had learned that the margin could be right and the finding worth nothing at two paces. This was that again, in a yard that smelled of hot clay and wet rope. What had widened was not the account but the room the account could not enter. Two things had been held at once under one roof without either man asking to be told what the other held, and the holding was the whole of it.

\scenebreak

He set the morning's movement on a place of its own. Beside it, but not beneath it and not upon it, stood the fraction from the morning of Day 161, the hand that had waited for a sentence to finish; and beside that, on a third place touching neither, stood the held beat under \place{the loaf shelf} from Day 166, a finger kept beneath a board for one counted breath without a look or a word. Three held changes, and he would not let them stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. He did not call the loan. He did not stand a tenth stopping up to join the nine or invent a seventh hearing to fill the empty place where no seventh had come; the six hearings stayed five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, filed beside the five and never among them. The told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself, and the kiln's gap under the wall was not laid across either. Three likenesses in a row will call themselves a finding if a man leaves them standing in a chart, and he had spent a life learning what such a finding costs when it is carried into a room with a signature at the bottom. He left the three where they were and drew no line between them.

Tarn's broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and went over it again, and did not once turn along it. The bristles dragged and lifted on the stone, and the grey meal of clay dust went up and settled back into the seams between the flags. He swept the same stretch twice and came at it a third time from the other side, and the road kept its ruts and its silence. No collector came. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at \place{the kindling} where the house could check; the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge with the unforged collector sentence beside it. The breath he had drawn on Seed 16 was still unspent, a small interval standing at the top of every reach, and no tenth stopping stood up before the seventh year had been earned. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He said “Yes, house” to \lore[the thaw]{the thaw} early and late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and missed its fifth, its payer unnamed for a hundred and seventy-one days. The damper lay beside the file as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln, read once more as the holding and the taking, and the two shut-room tellings stood beside it with no third. In the shed the wheel went on turning under Jorra's hands, and on the stones Tarn's broom went on over the road and back; the two strokes went on under one roof without a word between them, and the flagstones kept the shallow bright patch where one of them had stopped. The boy set three held changes on three places and drew no line between them. One hundred and seventy-one stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold.
