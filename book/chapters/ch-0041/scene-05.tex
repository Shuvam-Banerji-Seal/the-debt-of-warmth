\daymark{172}{Tallow 23, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday)}

The last working week came into its third day with the kilns already at their heat, and the yard had the smell of a thing held at a temperature it would not stay at. There was no bell for it and no word spoken over it; the hot clay came up under the tallow smoke and hung in the still air, and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} rode on a wrong wind off \place{High Kiln} and went over the tubs and the ruts and the basket like a hand passed over the rim of a bowl. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the edge of the light with the fold at his ear and the stiff crust two inches off, and \person{Sella Vail}'s arm had carried him a hundred and seventy-two mornings and kept the weight of him in muscle and not the cost, which was the whole of the record that arm had ever kept. She gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and the pot and never to the basket, and carried him at the half-pace past every open slate, and did not say that the week was in its third day or that the boy was a hundred and seventy-two days old and five months and twenty-two days. Nobody under any roof said it. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, with the gap where the fifth should have been, and the carried fire under the floor answered four low pulls and kept its silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-two days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never invented to stand up as a seventh.

The danger of the month was in the dust ground small as meal, in the sweetish wind and in the kiln's breath in fours with a gap, gathering the way heat gathers under blind ash toward an edge somebody would pay later and nobody would pay now. A wet pot set down at \place[the drying racks]{the racks} gave its high thin note before its time, a small bright sound with air under it, and the woman who set it down did not hear it; nobody under any roof had been given an instruction to tell her. He filed the sound as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer, and no room was changed by it and no pot was lost. He had learned, in rooms whose walls were cleaner than this one, that a thing watched closely enough gives up its order before it gives up its name; the danger had an order and no name here, and an order without a name is not a thing a man can carry into a chart. At two paces he could no more enter it than he could lift the crust two inches from his ear.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} came up the yard with the broom down at his side and his chest tight from the dark. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction; a tool that had not been named was a tool a clay-bound hand could be accused of taking, and he had learned to wait for the word before the reach. Sella gave it to the broom and the yard and not to him, and he took it up the way a man takes up an instrument already laid out for him, saying, “Yes, house.”

At \place{the kindling} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} he set the small knife back at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked, and told the split wood what he told it every morning of his term. “Six counted. The next I don't count.”

Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, an entry the house could check by looking at it; the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where the house could not check, and he did not give it a figure. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man being sent for a collector and turning back at the frozen ford, neither entered nor forgiven, kept where he kept the seventh year. He had his own collection of small things in a clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft} and a warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold long since; he could no more bring the stone and the debt into one account than he could bring the house's kindness and the account he kept of it into one, and he had stopped trying, which is not forgiving. The stone lay in its pot above \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} and he had not looked at it in a year and would not.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line where the ridge had turned, and no scrubbing lifted it; a man could scrub until the skin came up raw and the grey would still be sitting in the creases when he had done. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was under the roof and gave it back, a small bright circle on a narrow wrist that had been bound in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} six years ago and would be unbound at the end of the seventh or not at all. He held the ring up to the light without appearing to look at it and let it go. His chest had been tight since the dark; there was a small interval at the top of each reach where a breath from Seed 16 should have gone in and had not, and the interval had stood there for ninety-six days with nothing in it.

A neighbour at the tubs wanted the thaw early and another wanted it late, and Tarn said “Yes, house” to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot. He went on wanting the late one all the same, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and had not moved out of them in three months. A man can agree with a thing and still want it; the two are not the same entry, and he kept the wanting where the house could not check it, as he kept the seventh year.

His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up into his teeth and no further, the same sentence that had come to him before and had stopped at the word, and he did not say the word that would call the loan. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and no tenth stopping stood up out of the nine before the seventh year was earned, because a tenth stood up early would be a debt invented to make the nine into a bill. The near-saying stayed where it had stayed. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled. Something must give at the end of the seventh year, and the end was not here.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} a woman with a pot just out of the tubs set it down level and asked what the kilns would want now the last week was in. She asked it of the pot first and of Sella second, in the order the yard uses when it wants an answer out of a thing that cannot refuse.

“The kilns have had the heat and kept it,” Sella said. “Ask the pot what a firing wants set down there. The river'll take what's over.”

She gave no figure. The woman took up her pot by both handles and carried it off level as she had brought it, nothing slopping, nothing priced, nothing refused, the question going with her into the air over the ruts and not into any slate the boy could see. He had known a man put a question to a chart and get the neat margin back for an answer; here the chart was the yard, the margin was the weather, and the answer was a direction to a pot. It was exact. At two paces it bought him nothing.

The yard went around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned, and around the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw}, and around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody went in, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number. \place{the worked strips} lay beyond and were not entered. Sella gave the broom and the running water their instructions and went wide of all of it as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gave no explanation of the turns to the basket.

\scenebreak

At the hearth Sella told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning and a shelf are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

Under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-two days. The surgeon's register read the held breath as a clamp left too long on a vessel that would not bear the taking off, and the near-saying stopped at the word as a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin showing, everything in its place and nothing in the field; he had known such charts, and had signed one or two, and knew what a finding cost when it was carried into a neat hand with a signature at the bottom. Here the reading was exact and worth nothing at two paces. The clamp was a clamp and not a doctrine; the margin was a margin and not a key; the finding was a finding and not a hand that could reach the crust two inches from his ear. A list is not an instrument, and a man who cannot lift the instrument is still the man who knows what it is for.

He did not call the loan. He did not stand a tenth stopping up to join the nine, for the seventh year had not been earned, and nothing under this roof had been given an instruction about spending the breath that stood at the top of every reach. The near-saying stayed where it had stayed, held at the word the way a hand holds a clamp on a vessel it cannot leave and cannot close. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; he knew it the way he knew the afterdrop, and the knowing bought him nothing, because the end had not come and the only thing he could do with a knowing was keep it.

One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. He wanted the crust, the breast, the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got none of them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The dry heat had gone leathery into the crust; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen days ago and had not been wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Cold would come in its known order when the light went, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and would stop as if it had reached the end of its instruction. The kiln behind the wall took its breath in fours with the gap; the boards overhead kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb; the river through the door kept its fifth low note. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others.

The small knife went back to the very edge of its sill where it could be checked, six in the worn mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, Garl's unforged sentence beside it and the Seed 16 breath still unspent. Under the floor the fire missed its fifth, and its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-two days.
