At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} home up \place{the clay road}, the woodsmoke of the yard going before her and the river wind coming up the road to meet it, the two passing each other on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without a word spent on either. She carried him at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and keeps a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and the arm that had carried him a hundred and seventy-two mornings kept the weight of him in muscle and gave no account of the cost. The basket sat in the crook of her elbow with the fold drawn over its rim; the instruction went to the wool and to the wind and to the road under her feet, and never once went to the thing in the basket, because a basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and is not asked what it wants.

The road went down between the tubs and came up past the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was not standing now, and she went wide of that place as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, looking for no scrape and finding no word. At \place[the yard well]{the well} the \place{trough} stood bare and dark with its water called weather, and she went around it; beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody went in, and she went around that too, wide as the turning of a wheel; and in the returning line there was the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and she went around it with a look and a wide step and no number. She gave none of the turns to the basket. He lay at the edge of the light with the fold at his ear and the crust two inches off, and the carried weight went up and down with her step in a rhythm older than any word he had in this country, and he kept the count of those steps without deciding to, as he kept everything that would not leave him alone.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen days ago and she left it where it was, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to wipe is a thing that stays. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and had never told anybody --- and then the hand was gone and the fold lay as it lay.

At \place{the hearth} she told \lore{the carried fire} it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She went to her own bed without explaining the count, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among: five from four sources, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, from a person and not from a vessel, filed where a thing from a person must go and never counted among the four sources. There was no seventh and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. He had seen a neat hand close a chart over just such a finding and leave the margin showing, and the margin was not the finding and the finding was not the hand.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing \person[Jorra Vail]{the wheel-keeper} had kept from a kiln-master who came up on the wrong wind --- knowing the full note a Vail pot would give before it ever left the wheel and saying nothing of it, the knowing going among the near-full and never among the hearings. A man who knows and does not say has given the sound to nobody but himself, and a knowing kept in a mouth is a thing no ear can enter and no slate can take, which was why it stood advanced and unpaid and would not be spent here. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke that went on over the bright patch, his own held breath from a market morning, each in its own hollow and none laid across another.

On the widened tray lay nine stoppings --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the nine. A tenth had not stood up out of them. The seventh year was nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled; and the breath that stood at the top of every reach was still unspent, with its small interval where a breath from Seed 16 should have gone in and had not. He knew the shape of a thing that must give before it gives, the way he knew the afterdrop, and the knowing sat in its own hollow with the rest and bought him nothing.

Beside the nine lay \lore{the damper} as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln --- the bite that holds a vessel whole on its way to the fire, and the bite that can take the hand off a man shut in a room with the breath going out of him, both in the same tongue of iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry the two mercies in one mouthful. He had known a clamp that did the same work in a room whose walls were cleaner, and it had been a clamp and not a doctrine there too; here the word carried only the holding, and the taking went nameless, because a house gets to name one of its mercies and not both, and a thing with no name is a thing nobody can be made to answer for.

The morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left --- the hand that had waited for a sentence to finish before it moved the iron --- held and never spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered; and the repetition of it had not made it a change twice over. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own and not upon the first, a hand that goes under without looking knowing the board by heart or having been told not to look, the reason standing between the two and never settled. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man's broom checked half a beat behind it on \place{the flagstones} and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any of them and no payer named: he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The shallow bright patch on the flagstones where the stroke had stopped was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either. Three places, and he kept them with his hands full and unspent.

The two tellings of the room shut in the war stood side by side with no third --- breath through the nose and breath in the throat, both saying it was not the war's hand, one carried downriver with a date eighteen leagues east and taken by the yard for history, the other kept under this roof and spoken around --- and the told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself, the telling from the Long Night in its own place and never added to the hearings of the full note.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under the front edge for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. The shallow pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own, and the field's empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was where the yard had gone around it; neither was laid across the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust and its gap, and the two rooms did not touch. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, kept offstage and unspent. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew a line between any two of them. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and seventy-two days taking them down again, one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-two days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. \lore{Ash} lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line, and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, and neither was swept at this hour, and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about either. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction, the extremities going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast that had never come under this roof, the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. He got none of them. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the two things stayed in their own hollows as everything else did. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that was not a key and a list that was not a hand; the reading was exact and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it, with no case to begin and no instrument he could lift. That was the whole finding, and it went into its own hollow and stayed.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and unspent; the payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and seventy-two days; and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
