\daymark{374}{Stone 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof came on in the order they always came, and \person[Corin]{the boy} took them in that order, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry rasp of a coal settling under the ash at \place{the hearth}, the small tick of cooling clay along \place{the loaf shelf}, the low note of the river coming under its winter skin through \place{the finger's-width door}. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and twenty-two, where it had been laid when the last day closed, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-three exact, carried forward whole and not spent and told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \place{Stone} had kept its fourteenth day and this one came on without a boundary drawn to mark the change, and nothing from the thirteenth or the fourteenth crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. A day was a small vessel and would not be poured into another. He let the new morning take the old count as a cup takes what is poured into it and keeps its own shape, and did not set the number up as a threshold for anybody's foot to cross. \place{Low Kiln} lay under \place{Sill} in \place{Grath} with the dry cold on it, and the room smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and tallow, with under those the faint sour mineral tang of the unswept ridge, which had not been swept in two years and was not swept now. He took the room as a trauma bay is taken before the light, by the small sounds first, so that nothing in it would have to be explained to him by somebody who did not know what ailed it.

Then the surgeon's register took the body in its old order, without being asked and without leave. The crust was a stiff leathery barley crust gone browner and harder in the dry heat, lying two inches from the ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew, with the dry exactness that had once been the whole of his working life, that the order in which a body gives heat up is the reverse of the order in which it takes it back; that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core; that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside, slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew all of it and could do nothing with any of it. The body was allowed to humiliate him. A trauma surgeon who could reduce a shoulder in the dark could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the knowing stood in him like a tool held in a hand that would not close, and the hand that would not close was his.

The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where the fingers could reach it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work. He had stopped expecting the quarrel to end, which was not the same as being used to it.

\scenebreak

Before she came to the basket \person{Sella Vail} came to the hearth. She said to the coals what she said to them at both ends of every day. “It's the thaw,” she said, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and then, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure, so that the count went into the room and not into a slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” That was all she said to anything that could hear her, and she did not look into the basket. The light came thin through the finger's-width door and did not reach the shelf's low angle; he lay where the warmth came sideways and not full, and the pot board stood at the reach of his eyes and beyond the reach of his hand.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle, and the proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” And: “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” They were improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as \person{Garl Vail} had said them, who had said them to a pot and never to a person; he was present in the house in objects and not in a story, in the salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door, in the hollow of the wheel-seat nobody filled, in the clean dark line at the corner with its finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the month's dry cold and smelling faintly of \lore{Ash}. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She lifted the pot as a wound is lifted that must not be torn wider, and set it down again with the crack left where a man could see it. He had once set a staple he knew was false and had carried the shape of it two lives; the crack left showing was the honesty of the house, and the surgeon in him trusted the full note a mended vessel gave under the knuckle and distrusted the one that had been made whole again for show.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and seventy-four days and nobody lifting the floor to supply the pull. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and lifted no figure and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf's front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung, untouched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due. He took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart --- hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention --- and filed it where he filed such things, on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other.

\scenebreak

The empty place beside the crust was left exactly as it had been left on the two mornings before: no hand at it, no third crust put there, no name spoken over it. It was kept, and a keeping was not a sign, and a kept thing did not become a payer by being kept. The boy held it on a place of its own beside the loaf shelf's two gaps in the room that did not touch this one, the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood on \place{the flagstones}, and the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, with a finger between every pair. No third gap came to make a row come out even; no fourth likeness was made a cause; he did not lay a finger across any of them, though the finger would have gone down easy and the laying would have cost him nothing but the exactness. An account wants a hand to lay places together and a payer under them, and no hand stood and no payer stood. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off and was not his. He had learned to tell the difference between a line drawn by weather and a line drawn by a hand, and this one had been drawn by neither: it was a place where something had been, entered nowhere, and it would not stand up and call itself a want.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over that, from bank to bank, lay the winter skin the cold had put on and the cold would take off, found where put on the first day of Stone and not made again, not spent on this morning and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. The skin sat on its own place beside the note and not upon it, a covering put on by the weather and entered nowhere, and he kept a finger between it and the note it muffled. The widow's mark hung where it hung and was a morning's business of nobody's; the ridge at the corner was left where it had been left; the grain lay covered and unfigured and gave up no handful. The house kept itself by not-doings, and the warmth of it was maintenance and not mercy, and he knew the difference by what it cost him: nothing, and therefore not peace.

Stone kept its fifteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held, and neither of the district's two habits of naming the months was set right or left: the older women named it by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and in the shape a room takes when a weight is lifted out of it, and could not have said a number if a number had been the price of it. He kept three hundred and seventy-four exact without standing it up as a threshold or a line for anybody's foot to cross, and told nobody what the number counted, because there was nobody under the roof who would have known what it counted and a number spoken to such a person would have been a number spent. Six months and one hundred and ninety-four days stood complete in the only register that kept them, and the register was his and was worth nothing at two inches of air.

\scenebreak

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed and the hand did not stay; the note's own finishing under the covering ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his. He filed it as answer three hundred and twenty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the two bites of iron, the river's winter skin, the widow's mark, the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and twenty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a prayer; it was the small motion a hand makes in a field it has learned, and the field had not been his to change.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

Count three hundred and seventy-four exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-four days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number. The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and twenty-three; the stiff crust kept its hair-lifted corner two inches from the ear, and the empty place beside it was still unnamed and with no third. Cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and the Afterdrop waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. Stone kept its fifteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held, and the count went on.
