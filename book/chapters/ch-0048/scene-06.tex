The afternoon thinned without ending. The heat went off the high air first, as water goes off a board when the sun leaves it, and stayed longest in the low places: under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with one leathery crust two inches from his ear and its empty place beside it still unnamed. There the warmth collected and would not spend itself, a small kept thing in a house that kept everything and entered nothing. It came down the shelf's underside in a slow slant and stopped at the edge of his reach, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. Nothing crossed that edge. He lay with the fold against his cheek and watched the slant end where his fingers ended. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was frozen still; nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to move stays where it is put. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

\person{Sella Vail} stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, so that the days stood as a shape and no figure came out of it. The row had two gaps in it: the old space at the end where a loaf had once lain, and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave neither a name. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing without touching it, the little-door burn closing on the air and opening again; the jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month for a reason nobody had told her to give, and its glaze was cold, and she left it so. Her finger, going to the shelf's front edge, stayed on top of the board and did not go beneath, for the charcoal tally lay under that edge set there by a hand that was not hers and read by nobody under this roof, uncounted, uncopied. Once she had held a finger beneath that board for a counted breath without looking; the beat stood where it was left, and an afternoon was not the same as a finger under a board, and she did not repeat it.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and the fire took the coal under its blind ash and answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-seven days. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening: the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. No opening stood at the end of the count, and she had not looked for one; a woman who counts toward a thing she does not want to find will keep her count exact and let it arrive nowhere. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. It had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no other hour since; an afternoon and a shelf are not openings, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She did not look toward \place{the bed} at the setting. She never looked toward the bed at the setting.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it --- the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark; \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, the small interval standing at the top of the reach where the breath should have gone in and had not, and said, “Yes, house,” to the fire, and went on with the broom. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped, joined to no cause and to no ear, and neither man named the other's silence.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} gave the men who had gone into \place{the back room} before first grey no name and did not say the room was cold. He came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}, and he kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone: “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from the man who had come up on a wrong wind with his saying about a pot singing before its time. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; no pot was lost, and the knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and unpaid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held while she finished with feeding and gave the end of the sentence to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. He did not look toward the bed or the basket, did not ask about the ear, and gave nobody a word about the waiting. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, “it's the river wind,” and the basket was carried without a word said over it. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, and the two passed without taking each other's colour, as a clean hand passes a stained one on a stair and neither leaves a mark. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-seven mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end --- a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the low wall, around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} bare and dark with its water called weather, around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, around the empty place in the returning line and the place the carriers had crossed. Each got a look and a wide step and no number.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the roof} she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. The coals went under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the fire keeping four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and ninety-seven days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. The two rooms did not touch. She did not touch the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening and there has been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Each thing went into its own hollow and nothing rested across anything else, for a row of like things left standing will call itself an account and an account will call itself a rule, and a rule is an account that has found a payer.

Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the Vail shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}; and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in a hundred and ninety-seven days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: the pot that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, the rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost.

The nine stoppings lay where they had lain, each in its own place; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, and \person{Berr Clay}'s box had gone up the road locked with the true subtraction inside while the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged and \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with no figure coming off it. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction from the iron on Day 161, the beat beneath the shelf on Day 166, the half-check on the stones on Tallow 22 --- and the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on loft stone stood on a fourth and the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand on a fifth. The crust's one gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line. The warm stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot in the loft, two years gone and cold, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving. He laid every thing down and kept his hand between them, and took them up again in the same order, exact, complete and worth nothing.

\scenebreak

Then he put the question to his own hand.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, and did not come down at once. It stayed up through the whole of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch this one: the small intake at the top, the hold, and on into the letting go. It came down with the breath rather than before it, against the fold. The longest holding yet, held into the letting go and let go with it --- an inch of a small soft hand lifted toward a sound and left there, counted against a breath he could not hear and a fire he could not see. The surgeon who had once kept pressure in a cavity with both hands until the vessel took was keeping nothing now but one inch of a small soft hand, and the keeping cost him nothing and bought him nothing.

On the two mornings before, the hand had come down early or come down at once; here it came down with the breath, neither early nor late, arriving at the end of the count as a held thing arrives at the place it was held for. It was not a greater reach and not a stronger hand. It was a coordination, small as the turn of a wrist at a step's end: for the first time the hand came down with a rhythm in a room he could not enter, and the rhythm went on without him and did not stop to notice. He filed it on a place of its own beside the others and never laid it across them --- not a reaching, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named and no doctrine stood up out of it. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true.

The hand came down against the fold and the fold took it without a word. One hundred and ninety-seven stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. \lore{The carried fire} had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-seven days; the charcoal under the shelf was unread by a hand that was not hers and for a day that had not come; the mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the stone was cold in its loft pot; the Seed 16 breath was unspent at the top of every reach; the loan was uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out while the month was young was entered nowhere. The river kept its fifth low note, the fire missed its fifth pull under the floor, and the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb, three counts in three places and none rising up and becoming the others. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The count went forward.
