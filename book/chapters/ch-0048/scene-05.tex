\daymark{197}{Ember 18, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

Before first grey the kiln month had a smell \person[Corin]{the boy} had come to know before he had a word for it: hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner where the grey went on gathering and was called dealt with. He lay in \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} with the fold at his ear and one leathery crust two inches from that ear, its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was frozen still; nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to move stays where it is put. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back --- the small joint first, the core last, and the core holding on after the extremities have given out --- and the knowing bought nothing at two paces, and the two paces were the whole of his reach.

First sound the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, the fourth pull ending and the fifth not beginning; first the river through the finger's-width door with its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards keeping four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed, and its payer had been without a name for a hundred and ninety-seven days. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He laid them where they lay and kept his hand between them, such hand as he had. A row of like things left standing will call itself an account, and he would not have one.

He was one hundred and ninety-seven days old and six months and seventeen days, and nobody under any roof said so. The kiln month's second working week drew toward its end with no boundary drawn, the district going on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and neither corrected. His count had grown exact enough to be useless, edge upon edge of days set against a house that kept its days in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, and he had no voice in which to offer it and no hand to lay it down. He wanted the crust, wanted the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it.

\person{Sella Vail} came in ahead of the light the way a woman brings a lamp into a room and sets it where she means to work. The instruction went first to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, “it's the river wind,” and the basket was carried without a word said over it. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and kept her half-pace, and moved through the morning as a woman moves around a thing she has decided not to step over. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on the fold and opened again, and she fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not his mouth. The gruel was thin and hot and tasted of the fire that had kept it, and she did not say whose fire, and he did not ask.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing without touching it, the burn closing on the air and opening again. The jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month for a reason nobody had told her to give, and its glaze was cold, and she left it so. Her finger, going to the loaf shelf's front edge, stayed on the top of the board and did not go beneath, for the charcoal tally lay under that edge set there by a hand that was not hers and read by nobody under this roof, uncounted, uncopied. Once she had held a finger under that board for a counted breath without looking, and the beat stood where it was left. It was not repeated on a morning; a morning and a shelf are not the same as a finger under a board.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no other hour since. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; it had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance --- not a great gulf and not crossable; he could no more close it than lift his chin to the crust or speak a number. She did not look toward the bed at the setting. She never looked toward the bed at the setting.

\scenebreak

Before first grey three or four men with their collars high went low-voiced into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and got no name from anybody under this roof. The boy heard the small sound of their going the way a man in the next bed hears a door in a passage --- a direction and a number of steps and nothing entered. \person{Jorra Vail} came through later with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The dust said nothing of the men and Jorra said nothing either; the two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the boy did not join them to the men or to the kiln or to the glaze-room hand carried out while the month was young.

At the shed's edge Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who stood a moment at the edge and said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. “It does,” the man said, having taken the silence for the silence of a man at work. “You'll find.” He went off up the yard with his saying and the saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson. No pot was lost. The knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and unpaid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held while she finished with feeding and gave the end of the sentence to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kiln mouth} the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind and went out on the same wind. It put a false sweetness in the mouth and went; it was a direction and not a second edge paid, and nobody in the yard named a cause for it in Grathic, because there is no word in Grathic that will carry what a man would need it to carry and still leave the thing a direction. No collector was called for the glaze-room hand carried out of High Kiln while the kiln month was young and up \place{the clay road} breathing, and the pale line at his gum stayed on a place of its own, an exact reading worth nothing at two paces --- the hand curled, the nail beds, the short breath with the pause at its top, the care with which a dipper had been set down when the fingers had stopped telling the man where it was. No offstage fate was invented for him, for a breath kept is not an ending. The sweet edge had paid its one small edge and went on being spoken of as the yard speaks of its own weather, and no second edge was paid so soon after the carrying-out.

At the yard's edge and at the well neighbours asked what the firing would want now the account had gone up the road. “Ask the pot,” Sella said. “The house has said it. Ask the weather.” The questions went away level, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered; a cost asked as a direction arrives nowhere, and that is the whole of the house's method for staying warm. A woman with dry mud-scurf on her hem put her question a second time and got the pot a second time, and went off carrying the answer as she had come carrying the question, with nothing added to either.

The figure had gone up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, locked, with the true subtraction inside, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged where it had been read twice in the square. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve and no figure coming off it, and the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody had called. All of it stood beyond the boy's reach and had not come back under this roof, and he kept the shape of each and made no rule of them. A figure read aloud is still not the figure paid; the arithmetic of the district had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and nothing under the Vail roof had been paid by the reading. The box, the slate and the inward measure went on gathering on places of their own, and he laid no finger across them.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its absences as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Around the clean dark line in \place{Low Kiln} where Garl Vail's ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, neither swept; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round empty ring on the low wall where a shallow clay pan had stood through Reed, gone with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark with its water called weather; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; around the place the carriers had crossed. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up as a cause, and no finger of his drew the line. The field's empty place and the pan's gone place and the shelf's two gaps and the crust's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, and a row of absences left standing will call itself a doctrine if no hand is kept between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

\scenebreak

Mid-morning he put the question to his own hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, and came down at once against the fold. There was no holding in it on this morning. He had asked it to stay and it had not stayed; the asking is not always answered, and an inch that comes down at once is as much an object in the file as an inch that stays. The morning offered three ways to spend a question --- pot, weather, direction --- and none of them cost a figure, while the one question he could put had no answer in it at all.

The surgeon who had once kept pressure in a cavity with both hands until the vessel took could not keep one small soft hand in the air for the length of a single breath on this morning. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true. He filed the miss on the place where the other holdings lay, beside them and never laid across them --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not made into a rule and not joined to the wrong wind or the dust or the pot's early note or the fire's gap. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these; he kept his hand between them, such hand as he had, and it went up and came down when it came down.

The hand had gone up once and come down at once, and the fold took it without a word. One hundred and ninety-seven stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-seven days; the river kept its fifth low note; the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot, and none of the three rose up and became the others. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed, the pot handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, and the number remained unspoken. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
