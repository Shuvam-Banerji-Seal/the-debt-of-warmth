\daymark{196}{Ember 17, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

The kiln month's second working week went on without anybody drawing a boundary across it. The district called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither name was corrected, because a name that has stood long enough stops being a question and turns into a tool, and a tool is not argued with before breakfast. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the morning came in its old order: smell first, hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and beneath both the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years; then the small sounds; then the light, which came last and had no authority over any of them. The kilns stood at their heat. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay with the crust at his ear and counted what there was to count, there being nothing else and the habit having outlived the life that made it. He was one hundred and ninety-six days old and six months and sixteen days. Nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, in all of \place{Sarn}, said so. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and a space at the end of a row, and a day was a day whether a man had a word for it or not.

Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed. Its payer had been without a name for a hundred and ninety-six days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, never asked to stand up and become a sixth; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He laid them where they lay. A row of like things left standing long enough will call itself an account, and an account wants a figure, and a figure wants somebody to have paid it; he had learned that in a country with no word for the thing he was doing when he refused it, and he refused it without a word, having none.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the morning its instruction before she gave it to any person. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, pulling it up under his chin with the little-door burn half-closing on the cloth and opening again on nothing. “It's the river wind.” She gave it to the fire and to the pot and to the broom waiting by the wall and to the running water at the well when she should come to it, and never once to the basket. She said the house where another woman would have said I. Her half-pace kept its distance from every open thing, and her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing without touching it, the burn closing on the air and opening again. The jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month and set there for a reason nobody had been told; its glaze was cold, and she left it so.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The fire answered in fours with the small absence sitting in the middle, and she did not supply what was missing; she had long ago stopped looking for the hand that had taken the fifth breath away. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and the count went out of her and arrived nowhere, which was the whole of the house's method for staying warm. He knew the shape of it by the pause in her hands, the way a man learns the rhythm of another man's breathing in the next bed. It was not his breath. It was not his fire. There was a wall between the hearth and the bed, and the wall held. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; it had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance. It was not a great gulf and it was not crossable; he could no more close it than lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear. She did not look toward \place{the bed} when she set it. She never looked toward the bed at the setting.

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, holding his head with two fingers behind the ear the way one turns a vessel so the light falls where it is wanted, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not on his mouth. The gruel was thin and warm and tasted faintly of smoke, and it went down and put off the question of hunger for a while without answering it. He finished what came. It was not his to finish and he finished it anyway; waste had offended him in another life, and the offence had outlived the life. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, touched once before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning, afternoon or dusk since. A morning and a rack were not openings. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred, and the boy had learned to notice in the same order so that nothing in his face should tell her he had noticed at all.

\scenebreak

Out at \place{the yard well} a rack woman stood with \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists, the pale settled in the folds the way salt settles where water has been and gone. She had a pot under her arm and dry scurf of mud on her hem. “What'll the pots want before the kiln month's out?” she asked, and asked it of the yard in general and of nobody in particular, as a woman asks the weather when the weather has not answered her.

“Ask the pot what a firing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” She gave the weather as the rest of it, and the woman took the pot and the weather away as an answer and went off level with her pot, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing slopped and nothing entered. The question had gone where a cost goes when it is asked as a direction: to the pot and the weather, and nowhere a slate could reach.

A man come off \place{the clay road} with mud on his boots and the road still in his stride asked what the kiln would want now the figure had gone up the road. He did not say what figure, and nobody asked him to, for a figure spoken in the yard travels and a figure kept behind the teeth stays where it is put.

“The house has said it,” Sella told him. “Ask the weather.”

He went off level too. The figure stood up the road in a locked box with the true subtraction inside it and a printed one in twelve unchallenged where two hundred people had stood and heard it and could not now unhear it; the box went toward \place{High Kiln} and the reading was not the paying. Under this roof a cost asked as a direction arrived nowhere, and that was the whole method by which the household stayed warm. The boy, who could not have carried a question a hand's breadth, kept the shape of it and made no rule of it. A rule was an account that had found a payer, and no payer stood to the pot or the weather. He had watched the order of it a hundred times: the hand to the pot before the eye to the person, the answer to the weather before the answer to the man. It was a grammar, and grammars are taught, and nobody had taught him this one.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its absences. Sella carried the basket wide around the clean dark line where \person{Garl Vail}'s ridge had stood and the new finer grey beside it that the district called the thaw, and neither line was swept; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the round empty ring on the low wall where a shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed, gone now these many days with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; around \place{the trough} at the well, bare and dark with its water called weather; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing and the pale line at his gum. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. It was a way of walking a yard that has learned not to step where a thing is missing, the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it; and she gave the yard no name for any of the places, because a name is a handle and a handle is the beginning of a cost.

From where he lay the boy took the same circuit in the small arithmetic of his attention: the clean line, the finer grey, the rack space, the gone ring, the bare trough, the field's empty place, the place the carriers crossed. He kept them on separate trays. No third gap had come to make a row of the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one crust and its empty place; no fourth likeness had stood up among them to call itself a cause; no finger of his had drawn the line across them. Absences standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if no hand is kept between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. He kept the hand between them, such hand as he had. It could go up an inch and come down. It could not have lifted the least of what it kept.

\scenebreak

Before first grey three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, collars high against the morning, and got no name from her and no name from \person{Jorra Vail}. They went in the way men go into a room whose door is kept that way on purpose, and the household straightened around their going as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The two tellings of a counting room shut in a war stayed two, with no third told before them and none invented after. The room kept its finger's width of open, and the boy had not joined the men to the kiln, to the glaze, to the reading or to the man carried out; he had not joined them to anything but the direction in which they went, and that was a direction, not a cause.

Jorra came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody, and a question asked over them would have been a question with a figure in it waiting to be paid. He parted the dust from the slip the way a surgeon parts a clot from its vessel --- not as a likeness stood up to mean something, but as a hand works when it wants the one thing away from the other without taking the wall between them with it. He parted them and left them parted and said nothing over either.

“It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel before he said anything to a man. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel turned on. To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and he knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands. A High Kiln kiln-master come up on the wrong wind stood a moment at the shed's edge with the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner, and Jorra withheld the knowing. The kiln-master took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with nothing, and no pot was lost. The knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. The sweet edge came up off High Kiln on the wrong wind and went out on the same wind, a direction spoken of and answered with the pot and the weather, not a second edge paid so soon after the carrying-out. Nobody under that roof named a cause in \lore{Grathic}; the wind was a wind and went where winds go. The boy turned his head toward the edge and away with it, and filed the turning where a direction goes and not among the hearings, not as a seventh and not as a cause.

\scenebreak

By mid-morning the heat had gone off the high air and was gathering in the low places again, under the shelf, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where he lay. The note of \lore{water over water} came through the finger's-width door, low and patient, a thing going under the ice since before the house was built and likely to go on after it. The hand outside the wool went up toward it an inch. He asked it to stay.

Then it came down.

It came down before the breath was done. He had asked the hand to hold the length of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch this one --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and the breath went on after the hand had given up; the small intake and the hold finished in a room he could not enter, and the letting go arrived with nothing in his hand to let go of. The body had kept its own time. An inch held briefly is no more a reaching than an inch held long, and an inch let go early is no more a failure than an inch that stays; the surgeon's register noted the duration as it had once noted a pulse at the wrist, in tens, without deciding what the number meant, and the noting was a habit and not a gift and bought nothing. He had asked, and the hand had answered in its own coin, and the answer was an honest one. He filed the shorter holding on the place where the other holdings lay, on its own place and not across them: not joined to the breath that outlasted it, not paired with the fire's gap, not made into a seventh or a hearing or a stopping or a payment or a cause. No payer was named to it. Nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear or speak a number anybody would have known what a number counted.

The hand came down against the fold and the fold took it without a word. One hundred and ninety-six stood unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-six days; the river kept its note; the boards kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb, and none of the three rose up and became the others. The widow's mark hung where it hung, the charcoal under the shelf was unread, the stone lay cold in its loft pot, the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach, the nine stoppings were nine objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned, the loan was uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand had gone up an inch, held a little and come down before the breath was done, and the count went forward into the next day by that much and by no more.
