\daymark{195}{Ember 16, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of \lore{water over water} that came through the finger's-width door, and came down again against the fold. It had done this every morning of his life and did it now without triumph or instruction, the small bare joint at the wrist riding back as the arm rose, the fingers opening on nothing and closing on wool. It was not a reaching. A reaching would have had a thing at the end of it. This had only the sound, and the sound was a note the river made going over itself under its skin of ice, and the hand went up and came down the way a man's eye goes to a light and away again when he has other work. The count at one hundred and ninety-four went whole out of the dark behind him into this morning, exact, complete and unspent, a thing laid out on a towel that did not exist and could not now be put down. He was one hundred and ninety-five days old and six months and fifteen days. Nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, in all of \place{Sarn}, said so. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and the space at the end of a row, and a day begun was a day begun whether anybody counted it or not. The fold took the hand down and did not move.

Smell came before sight, and sight before any sound that could be named: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. The kiln drew in fours with a gap where the fifth should have closed. It was not a bell. A bell would have said a thing and gone on saying it; this drew and stopped and drew again with the small absence sitting in the middle of it, the way a man's breath sits when he has not let the last of it go. The crust lay leathery two inches from his ear, brown-edged and stiffened by the dry heat of the kiln month, with the empty place beside it still without a name and no third thing to fill it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was frozen still, unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it too had come to the end of an instruction and had no authority to go further. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had been without a name for a hundred and ninety-five days. Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings, never made a sixth, never asked to stand up and be something it was not. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them where they lay. A row of like things left standing long enough will call itself an account, and an account will want a figure, and a figure will want somebody to have paid it; he had learned that in a country that had no word for the thing he was doing when he refused it. He did not refuse it aloud. He had no voice for it. He simply laid the four-and-a-gap beside the four-and-a-gap beside the four nails and the knot, and let them lie.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the morning its instruction before she gave it to any person. She gave it to the wool first, pulling it up under his chin with the little-door burn half-closing on the cloth and opening again on nothing. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” She gave it to the fire next, and to the pot, and to the broom waiting by the wall, and to the running water at the well when she should come to it, and never once to the basket. She said the house where another woman would have said I. She kept her half-pace from every open thing, which was a way of walking that had long ago stopped being a thing she had to think about. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it. It was a jar fetched from three doors down in another month and set on the shelf for a reason nobody had been told, and the glaze on it was cold, and she did not touch it.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- the way she had counted at every going of the light and every morning since the kiln month came in, and never once toward a thing that came. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. It had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died. She would say no more than that.

She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance. It was not a great gulf and it was not crossable; it was two inches of board between a small soft hand and a pot handle, and \person[Corin]{the boy} could no more close it than he could lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear. She did not look toward \place{the bed} when she set it. She never looked toward the bed at the setting.

\scenebreak

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, holding his head with two fingers behind the ear the way one turns a vessel so the light falls where it is wanted, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not on his mouth. The gruel was thin and warm and tasted faintly of smoke, and it went down and did what gruel does, which was to put off the question of hunger for a while without answering it. He finished what came. It was not his to finish and he finished it anyway; waste had offended him in another life and the offence had outlived the life. Two fingers behind the ear, spoon on sleeve, mouth left as it was. The instruction had gone to the spoon and the dish and the fire that had warmed the dish, and never to the boy, who was carried and fed and discussed in the third person and knew every word of it.

\lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning, afternoon or dusk since. A morning and a shelf were not openings. There had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred, and the boy had learned to notice in the same order so that nothing in his face would tell her he had noticed at all. He lay with the crust at his ear and the thread frozen at his mouth and the mark behind her coat, and the two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a boy who could not close two inches and a number nobody would say.

Out in the \place{Clay Yard} the kilns stood at their heat in the second working week of the kiln month, and the district went on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and neither name was corrected. A neighbour come to the threshold with dry scurf of mud on her hem, the mud left from the flood and dried pale in the creases of the cloth, asked what the firing would want now the account had gone up the road.

“Ask the pot what a firing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.”

She gave the weather as the rest of it, and the neighbour took the pot and the weather away as an answer and went off level with her pot, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing slopped and nothing entered. The figure had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box with the true subtraction inside and a printed one in twelve standing unchallenged where two hundred people had stood and heard it and could not now unhear it. Here under the Vail roof a cost questioned as a direction arrived nowhere, and this was the whole method of the house for staying warm. The boy kept the shape of it and made no rule of it. A rule was an account that had found a payer, and no payer stood to the pot or the weather.

The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind, sweetish and metallic under the clay smell, and went out again on the same wind. Sella answered it as a direction and not as a second edge paid. Nobody under that roof named what had ailed the man carried out on Day 188, no collector was called for him, no figure given, no slate opened, and the pale line at his gum stayed on a place of its own at two paces, exact, complete and worth nothing. No cause was spoken in \lore{Grathic}; the wind was a wind and went where winds go. The boy turned his head toward the edge and away with it, and filed the turning where a direction goes and not among the hearings, not as a seventh and not as a cause.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof the note of \lore{water over water} came through the finger's-width door, low and patient, a thing that had been going under the ice since before the house was built and would go on after it. The hand outside the wool went up toward it an inch.

This time he asked it to stay.

It was the asking that was new and not the inch. The inch was an old small fact of muscle, a thing the body did without being told; the asking was the surgeon coming back into the room he had left years ago and putting his hand where the hand already was, as if a hand could be given an order and kept to it. He asked it to stay the way he had once asked a nurse to hold pressure in a cavity until the vessel took --- not with words, for there were no words in this mouth that any ear in this house would have understood, but with the whole inward address of a man who knows that a thing held is a thing that has been told to hold. He had held pressure in a cavity for twenty minutes once, in a white room with the blood going out under the drape, and had not moved and had not let anybody else move, and when the vessel took he had taken his hands away and gone on to the next body. That was the register he brought to one inch of a small soft hand.

The hand stayed.

It was a small physiological thing, the keeping of it. The muscle at the shoulder took the weight and did not let it drop; the small bare joint at the wrist held where the wool had ridden back; the fingers stayed open on the air the way they would have stayed open on a thing handed up to them. Nothing in the hand was strong. An infant's hand lifting an inch against no resistance is not a feat of strength, and a man who had once reduced a shoulder in the dark would have been the first to say so. What was held was not a weight but a duration, and duration was the thing the surgeon's register had been trained to note --- the pulse counted in tens, the interval between the injury and the arrival, the minutes a clamp stood before the vessel took or did not. He noted this interval as he had noted those, without deciding what it meant, and the noting was a habit and not a gift. It bought nothing. It would not close two inches or make a mouth speak. He noted it and put it down.

It stayed for the length of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch this one --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- a rhythm he could not hear and a fire he could not see, counted against the note that came through the door. It was an asking made before and an answer given before, an inch held and let go, and an inch held twice might start in time to look like a reaching, which was a thing he would not let it become. He filed it on a place of its own beside the others: not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. No payer was named to it. Nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and he laid it away anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of what they had once held.

Then the hand came down against the fold, and the fold took it without a word. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear or speak a number anybody would have known what a number counted. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch at the same time, and both were true. Nobody had seen the holding. It was worth nothing. It had been asked and it had been answered, and that was the whole of it, and he kept it exactly, complete and unspent.

One hundred and ninety-five stood exact in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, the thread was frozen and unwiped, the crust lay leathery two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The carried fire missed its fifth with no payer for a hundred and ninety-five days; the river kept its note of water over water; the boards kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. The widow's mark hung where it hung, the charcoal under the shelf was unread, the stone lay cold in its loft pot, the Seed 16 breath stood unspent, the loan was uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand had gone up an inch, held, and come down, and the count went forward into the next day by that much and by no more.
