The afternoon thinned without ending. The heat went off the high air first, as though a hand had lifted it from the upper boards and set it down again lower, and it stayed longest in the low places: under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with the crust at his ear. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. He lay under the roof of the shelf under the roof of the house, two roofs, and neither reached past the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back. Cold had come that far in the small hours and no farther. Between the cold and the warmth there was the width of a joint, and that was the whole of his country.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row. It was not a song and not a count anyone could have taken down. The loaf at the end stood where a loaf had stood; the old space at the row's end had kept its place through the firing month; and a palm's breadth of bare board had come in on the night of Seed 6 and had not been filled since. Two gaps stood in the row. She gave neither a figure. A figure spoken over a shelf would travel, but a figure kept behind the teeth stayed where it was put, which was a thing a woman could do with a gap as with a pot: set it down and leave it standing. She had set both down. Her lips went along them and came back to the same place twice, the way her hand went to the same place twice when she counted fuel, and she gave nobody the thing she counted.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall. Her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it, the little-door burn closing on the air and opening again. The jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month and set on the shelf for a reason nobody had been told. Its glaze was cold. She left it so. Her finger, going to the shelf's front edge, stayed on top of the board and did not go beneath. Beneath that edge lay the charcoal tally, put there in another month by a hand that was not hers and read by nobody under this roof, uncounted, uncopied. Once, years ago, she had held a finger under the board for the length of a counted breath without looking, and that beat stood where it had been left, on a place of its own in the boy's keeping. It was not repeated now. She straightened the board with the flat of her palm and turned from the shelf, and the two gaps stayed in the other room, where a shelf's absences do not touch the one crust lying two inches from a boy's ear.

\scenebreak

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw. She said it to the fire and not to the room and not to the basket, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the pot's back going in under the ash and lifting and letting the heat come up through the gaps. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The fire answered as it had answered every morning and every going of the light since the kiln month came in: four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-five days. She did not supply one. A man who wanted the four to agree with the fifth would have had to put somebody under them, and no payer stood to the fire's gap, and she had long ago stopped looking for the hand that had taken the fifth breath away.

Then she stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening. The small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She had counted this way at every going of the light and every morning through the kiln month, and never once toward a thing that came. The count went out of her and arrived nowhere, and this was the whole of the house's method for staying warm: a cost asked as a direction, an answer given to the pot and the weather, nothing entered. The boy, who could not see her face from where he lay, knew the shape of the count by the pause in her hands. He had learned it the way a man learns the rhythm of another man's breathing in the next bed. It was not his breath. It was not his fire. There was a wall between the hearth and the bed, and the wall held.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. It had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died; she would say no more than that. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance. It was not a great gulf and it was not crossable. He could no more close it than lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear or speak the number that stood in his head. She did not look toward \place{the bed} when she set it. She never looked toward the bed at the setting, and the room that held the pot and the room that held the boy went on not touching each other, as they had touched in no hour of his life.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six years standing in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that no house could enter. Beside it lay the unforged sentence of Garl, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. It lay there as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man, and nobody under the roof had forged it in all the boy's days.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. He did not take it when it was merely lying there; a tool that had not been spoken to was a tool with no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who took up a tool before its work was named was a hand that would be entered for it. Sella gave the instruction to the broom, and only then did his fingers close on the handle. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the kindling what it had been told so many times that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to.

A neighbour wanted the thaw early; another, coming off \place{the clay road} with mud on her hem, wanted it late. He said “Yes, house” to each, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot between them, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon. He swept the length of it as a man sweeps a step he does not mean to descend, and kept his eyes on the bristles.

His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it out; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure in it and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach --- the breath that had stood unspent since Seed 16 with its small interval where the going-in should have been --- and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood where they had been set, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments; no tenth stood up among them before the seventh year was earned, the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that could hear was not called. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch kept there from another day without the half-check, the stones holding the place where the stroke had once stopped and nobody entering it, and he did not look toward the bed or ask about the room.

In the shed's edge, where a finger's width of glass let the afternoon in thin, \person{Jorra Vail} spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel turned on. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention, the grey worked small as meal past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who stood a moment at the shed's edge with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner; the man went off up the yard with his silence taken for the silence of a man at work, and no pot was lost. The knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished the sentence with feeding and gave its end to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, the same holding he had made on older days, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the flagstones and not stood up with either into a rule. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind before it went anywhere near a person, and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she said, and the wind went on doing what it had been told and the basket was carried without a word. Woodsmoke lay in long low bars before her, laid one above another across the falling air, and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}. The two passed without taking each other's colour, the smoke going up and the water going over, and she walked between them at her half-pace as a woman walks between two things she has been given no instruction to join.

The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-five mornings kept its record without a word said over it. The height of the handle, the give of the shoulder at a step's end, the small turn of the wrist that set the basket level again --- these were held in the muscle the way a surgeon's hands hold the feel of a cavity they have packed, a record of weight and not of cost. The arm knew what it had lifted and had never been told what the lifting was worth. It did not arrive anywhere. It went on carrying.

She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the wall's low place, where nothing had stood for a month and no scrape, crumb, print or chip had come; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark with its water called weather; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks}, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing and the pale line at his gum. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. It was a way of walking a yard that has learned not to step where a thing is missing, the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it; and she gave the yard no name for any of the places, because a name is a handle and a handle is the beginning of a cost.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand resting a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat. A dusk was not an opening, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under that roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken. She told the fire it was the thaw, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot, and went to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire, a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a boy who could not close two inches and a number nobody had spoken. The wall between them kept both.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, with no towel and no instruments and no case, but the whole order of the laying. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the sources. No seventh came and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid. Nine stoppings lay as objects and not a rule; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} missed its fifth with no payer, the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach, and the loan was uncalled.

Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at the iron, the beat under the shelf, the half-check on the stones --- and no hand of his laid them across one another. The small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on loft stone stood on a fourth place; the pale line at a gum, read at two paces and worth nothing at two paces, stood on a fifth. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, with no third gap come, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawn across them. And now the held inch lay on a sixth place of its own, the asking and the answer of this morning, taken down before it could stand up and call itself a reaching. Each thing was laid in its own hollow and nothing rested across anything else, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. He had no muscle to lift the least of what he laid out, and he laid it out anyway in the dark.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water that came through the finger's-width door. He asked it to stay. It held for the length of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch this one --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and came down against the fold. It was the same holding as the morning's and worth no more. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust at his ear or speak a number anybody would have known what a number counted. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch at the same time, and both were true.

One hundred and ninety-five stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-five days; the river kept its note; the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb, and none of the three rose up and became the others. The mark hung where it hung, the charcoal under the shelf was unread, the stone lay cold in its loft pot, the Seed 16 breath was unspent, and the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand had gone up, held and come down, and the dark kept what it had been given until another day should ask for it.
