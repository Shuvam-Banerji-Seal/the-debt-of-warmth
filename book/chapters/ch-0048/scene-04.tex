By afternoon the heat had drawn off the upper boards and gone into the low places, the way a woman takes a lamp down from a shelf and sets it on the floor where she means to work. It lay under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay; it gathered in the hollow of \place{the bed} and did not climb back. The kiln month had ground everything small. A dry dust sat on the sill and in the creases of every hand in the house, the grey of slate and the paler grey of \lore{slip}, and nobody swept it before its time. Dust left lying was dust kept, and dust kept was a thing a house could go around.

Under the shelf's underside the boards made a roof that ended at the reach of a small hand. The warmth came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped where the roof stopped, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. He lay beneath two roofs, the shelf's and the house's, with the leathery crust at his ear and its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was frozen still; nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to move stays where it is put. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed. Its payer had been without a name for a hundred and ninety-six days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them. He laid them where they lay and kept his hand between them, such hand as he had, which could go up an inch and come down. A row of like things left standing will call itself an account, and he would not have one.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row. The loaf at the end stood where a loaf had stood; the old space at the row's end had kept its place through the firing month; a palm's breadth of bare board had come in on the night of Seed 6 and had not been filled since. Two gaps stood in the row and she gave neither a figure. A number spoken over a shelf travels; a number kept behind the teeth stays where it is put. She set both down and went about her work.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing without touching it, the little-door burn closing on the air and opening again. The jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month for a reason nobody had been told; its glaze was cold, and she left it so. Her finger, going to the shelf's front edge, stayed on the top of the board and did not go beneath. Beneath that edge lay the charcoal tally, set there by a hand that was not hers and read by nobody under this roof, uncounted, uncopied. Once she had held a finger under that board for a counted breath without looking, and the beat stood where it was left. It was not repeated now; an afternoon and a woman's back turned are not the same as a finger under a board. She straightened the board and turned away, and the two gaps stayed in the other room.

\scenebreak

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the pot's back going in under the ash and lifting and letting the heat up through the gaps. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The fire answered as it had answered every morning of the kiln month: four low pulls and the small absence sitting in the middle, and she did not supply what was missing. She had long ago stopped looking for the hand that had taken the fifth breath away. A man who wanted the four to agree with the fifth would have had to put somebody under them, and nobody stood to the gap.

Then she stood with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. The count went out of her and arrived nowhere, which was the whole of the house's method for staying warm: a cost asked as a direction, an answer given to pot and weather, nothing entered. The boy knew the shape of it by the pause in her hands, as a man in the next bed learns another's breathing. It was not his breath. There was a wall between the hearth and the bed, and the wall held.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; it had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance. It was not a great gulf and it was not crossable; he could no more close it than lift his chin to the crust or speak a number. She did not look toward the bed when she set it. She never looked toward the bed at the setting.

\scenebreak

Out at \place{the drying racks} a woman set a pot down wrong. It was a small thing, the kind of set-down a woman makes a hundred times an afternoon with her mind on something else; the pot's foot went on the board's edge and the pot gave its high thin note before its time. Then it went on giving it, fainter, and sang itself out early, the way a note will when the vessel that made it has no more to give. The woman did not stop. She went on with her hands, lifted the pot, set it square, swept around the place where the fault had been and went back to her work. She made no lesson of it. She said nothing of the note to the yard and nothing to the pot, and the note went where a note goes when nobody takes it up: into the dirt under the rack and out of the afternoon.

The boy turned his head toward the sound, as he turned toward all such sounds before anybody tapped or spoke, and filed it where a danger goes and not among the hearings. It was not a seventh. It was not a cause. A pot out of season is a fault in the handling, and a yard sweeps around such faults and goes on; it is no more a sign than a cracked pot is a sermon. No collector was called, no figure given, no slate opened, and no second edge of \lore{lead glaze} was paid so soon after the carrying-out. The sweet edge had come off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind in the morning and gone out on the same wind, a direction answered with pot and weather. Two things put down wrong in an afternoon are two things put down wrong, and he kept his hand between them, such hand as he had. He had no name for the keeping and did not want one.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six years standing in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house no house could enter. Beside it lay the sentence of Garl Vail that had never been forged --- the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford --- lying as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction; a tool not spoken to has no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who lifts one early is a hand that will be entered for it. Sella gave the instruction to the broom, and only then did his fingers close on the handle. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the kindling what it had been told so often the words had worn as smooth as the thumb-mark: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure in it and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach --- the breath that had stood unspent since Seed 16 with its small interval where the going-in should have been --- and did not spend it. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom. Nine stoppings stood where they had been set, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments; no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned, that year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that could hear was not called. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones holding the place where it had once stopped and nobody entering it.

\scenebreak

In the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, where a finger's width of glass let the afternoon in thin, \person{Jorra Vail} spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel turned on. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention, grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip; and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who stood a moment at the shed's edge; the man went off up the yard with the silence taken for the silence of a man at work, and no pot was lost. The dust said nothing of the men who had gone before first grey into \place{the back room}, and Jorra said nothing either; the two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, the dust joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished the sentence with feeding and gave its end to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting. He gave the men no name, did not say the room was cold, and did not look toward the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn.

\scenebreak

Above the shed in \place{the Debt Loft} the warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot, two years gone and cold. Tarn had taken it out on an afternoon in another month, held it, set it on the sill in the light, looked at it a long while and put it back. The debt had not moved by the width of a hair; he had not forgiven the stone, for he had stopped trying, and stopping is not forgiving. From below the boy had heard only the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, a sound with a direction out of a room he could not enter, and filed it on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not laid across the three held changes or the pale line and not made into a cause, for a fourth likeness would call itself a cause and a cause would want a payer. He kept the scrape where it lay, a sound with a direction and nothing said over it.

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind before it went anywhere near a person, and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she said, and the wind went on doing what it had been told and the basket was carried without a word. Woodsmoke lay in long low bars before her and the river wind came behind with the taste of water over water; the two passed without taking each other's colour. She walked between them at her half-pace. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-six mornings kept its record without a word said over it --- the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist that set the basket level --- a record in muscle that knows weight and not cost, and it arrived nowhere and went on carrying.

She went wide around the clean dark line where Garl Vail's ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it called the thaw; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of the drying racks where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round empty ring on the low wall where a shallow clay pan had stood through Reed, gone with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark with its water called weather; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, left the thread frozen, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand resting a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. The widow's mark hung where it hung; a dusk was not an opening and there had been none since before the kiln on Ember 1. She told the fire it was the thaw, banked the coals with a broken pot, and went to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, with no towel and no instruments and no case, but the whole order of the laying. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh came in a hundred and ninety-six days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid.

Nine stoppings lay as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the Seed 16 breath stood unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at the iron, the beat under the shelf, the half-check on the stones --- and no hand of his laid them across one another. The scrape of a pot's foot on loft stone stood on a fourth; the pale line at a gum, worth nothing at two paces, on a fifth; the held inch on a sixth, taken down before it could call itself a reaching. Each thing lay in its own hollow, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch; no third gap came, no fourth likeness stood up as a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

On its own place beside the others lay the holding of this morning, the inch that went up and came down before the breath was done. He did not set it down as a failure or set it up as a sign. An inch let go early is no more a defeat than an inch that stays; the body had kept its own time, and a man who once held pressure in a cavity until the vessel took now held an inch of a small soft hand for part of a breath he could not hear, and both were true and neither bought anything. He filed it where the other holdings lay and took it down before it could call itself a hearing.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door. He asked it to stay. It held a little and came down against the fold, and the fold took it without a word. One hundred and ninety-six stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody. The fire's fifth had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-six days; the river kept its note; the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb, and none of the three rose up and became the others. The mark hung where it hung, the charcoal under the shelf was unread, the stone lay cold in its loft pot, the Seed 16 breath stood unspent, the nine stoppings were nine objects with no tenth, the loan was uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand had gone up, held a little and come down; one hundred and ninety-six stood exact in the wool, and the fire's fifth had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-six days.
