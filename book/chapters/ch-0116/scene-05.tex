\daymark{401}{Ilmen 7, Y.H. 814, Seventhday}

The small hours had a sound the day would not have: the house settling into itself, coals breathing under the banked ash, a beam taking its weight and holding it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, where the heat came sideways and the wind did not come at all, and took the body in its old order because there was nothing else worth doing with the dark. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth had never been driven; he had counted them so many mornings that the count had gone quiet in him, the way a pulse goes quiet when a man is listening for something else.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear. It had gone harder through the dry night, and its corner stood a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place held the fold a hand had put there on the fifteenth, found where put in the small hours and at every going of the light since, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name over the place and no hand to own the keeping. However many hands found it, it remained a keeping found and not made; a second placing would make a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer standing where none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and gone stiffer through the night, and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He had learned the order of a body in the old life and kept it still: airway, breathing, circulation, the nerves, the whole field taken in by the place of each thing before a hand touched it. He had no field now but this basket, and took it by the same order anyway, because the order was the only thing of the old life that had followed him without being asked. He knew a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose --- slow, from the outside, never hurried, never rubbed --- and could not ask for it. He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, that pink returning is the surface still lying over a cold core, and that a cold patient cannot clot. He had once said \emph{leave it} over bodies in a white room and been right often enough to trust the sentence and wrong often enough to fear what it said about him; here he could not say it at all, and the sentence went on gathering interest in a chest that could not lift a crust. That was a joke with a blade in it, and he found it funny without laughing, which cost less.

Under the register the body wanted. It wanted the crust lifted two inches from the ear and set where a mouth could reach; it wanted the thread at the mouth wiped; it wanted a hand on the handle of the pot on \place{the pot board}. The belly wanted too. It had wanted since the dark and would want until something was put into it, and the wanting had no words and no patience and did not care that a man of forty was listening inside it. Both registers were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them went on being the work the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. He judged neither. Judging would have been a kind of standing-up, and a thing stood up wants a payer under it.

Four hundred days stood behind him; this was the four hundred and first, and he kept the number edge to edge against the house's arithmetic without laying it alongside as a reproof or a rule. The house kept its days badly. It kept them in loaves pushed along a shelf, in a glance at the clay jar under lead glaze beyond the board, in a space at the end of a row that nobody called a number. From the basket's low angle, through \place{the finger's-width door}, the yard went about its small account: a hand pushed a loaf along a board, a neighbour glanced at a clay jar set in the shade and did not touch it, and at the end of a row a space stood where a loaf had stood and was not counted because it had no name. A woman said to nobody that the batch had come out light, and a voice from a door answered that the pot would have to answer for it, and the pot said nothing. The house could not have told you what day it was by any of these things, and did not try. He kept four hundred and one exact against them and did not stand the number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure. A number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “It's the thaw,” she said to the coals. “You'll want more than that come the drying.” There was no answer from the coals and she did not wait for one. She gave a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured and neither made to stand for a number, and kept her finger along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread. Her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody had numbered, and came back to the hairline at the wire. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and \emph{the boy} where another would have said \emph{my son}, and never finished a sentence that would have required her to say what she felt, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, left the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the new binding, and said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person. “Don't set that near the door. You'll pay for it in the thaw.” That to the pot. “The fire doesn't know you're sorry. Feed it.” That to the coals. Repair was kept apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves the hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and with no figure over it and no handful lifted, and said to the grain what she would not have said to a person, “Lie down. You'll keep better there.” Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The instruction went to the wool and the pot and the fire and the banked fire and the river wind, and never to the basket.

At her throat \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung; she touched it before she opened the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning was not an hour for it. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred, and the boy under the shelf had learned to notice the mark last too and to say nothing of it. A morning, a hearth, a shelf and a basket are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a Seventhday.

The twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 stood unchanged behind one unsigned figure, twelve and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of twelve. Their twelve true subtractions rode locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up \place{the clay road}, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down, and the boy had no key. The printed figure stood unchallenged under this roof. He had read twelve true subtractions once and they had been true, and truth had not bought a signature; he kept the shape of the lock without asking for the key. He did not lay the finding across the face filed on its own tray, the promise under the wheel-shed, the degree held as a shape or any other kept thing; likenesses stood up want a man put under all of them and no man stood.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its giver without a name for four hundred and one days now, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it. \person{Garl Vail}'s name was unspoken the same number. He had been a big man with clay in the creases of his hands and a laugh that ended in a cough, who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him; the house kept him in a salt-stiff coat with a crease at the left shoulder on its peg, in a wheel-seat worn hollow on one side that nobody was allowed to fill, and in the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with its finer grey called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} where no broom had gone in two years. The two silences stood side by side in the house's keeping without becoming one keeping --- one the silence where a pull should be and one the silence where a name should be, each on its own place with a finger between. He gave the missing pull no cause and the silence no source, for a source is an account that has found a payer and none stood under the floor.

\lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put and stayed a finding. \lore{The three coverings} and the note of \lore{water over water} under \lore{the winter skin} were found where put and unspent on a tend-day; \place{the river} kept its low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering. He gave the note no source and spent nothing on it, for a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold is entered nowhere, is not a fourth covering and is not a sign.

\scenebreak

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled; \place{the Drawing house} kept its flat knock outside the six on its own place. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap in two places, its gap not folded into the fire's four and a gap under the floor, into the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the twelve arithmetics behind one figure. Three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. The bell from \place{the Cross} was a bell he had heard; the bell of \place{Weighing} was a bell told from mouth to mouth and passed from one house to another the way a coal is passed in a dish, and nobody said where the count began. The silence after the name spoken over the yard on \place{the Remembering} sat on \lore{the gap tray} beside a clean voice's missing name and the empty name the neighbours had left in their story, each with a finger between every pair, none filled, none made a witness or a reader.

The four rooms that keep the same four things with the same gap stayed four with a finger between every pair: the birth room two doors down, this kiln-house, the room where a slate had been read on market-day, and the drawing room two doors the other way, each holding a bench, a pot, a draught and a board worn smooth where a woman had stood. No Court room was brought under the roof as a fifth. The cold foot below the sill stayed uncured and unnamed, and \lore{the second chill} kept its withheld cause, carried through the morning and not spent; no silt-leach, cure, recognition or treatment was named over it.

He went over the field by the place of each thing as the file came out, and left every gap a gap: the crust's gap with its hair-lifted corner two inches off, the shelf's two gaps, the gone pan's round ring, \place{the field's empty place} out beyond the last house, the fold found where put, the told bell's gap in its square beside the heard bell, the silence after the name, the empty name in the story, the ridge at the Vail corner left where it lay beside the neighbour's called-gone grey, the promise under the wheel-shed neither given nor withdrawn, the degree held as a shape and not repeated, and the face on its tray with no word passing. Each lay where the morning had left it, advanced and not paid, and none stood up and called itself a doctrine.

The hand came down on answer three hundred and seventy-seven, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the river's skin, the widow's mark, the clerk's inward slate, the promise under the shed, the degree held as a shape, the face on its tray nor the keeping at the fold. The answers stood on the sixth place and not one of them was a doctrine, and three hundred and seventy-seven stood since the first. Count four hundred and one exact, six months and two hundred and twenty-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl's name was unspoken the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one. Ilmen 10 stood three mornings off with the year's first arithmetic waiting there, a reset and not a signing, and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. He did not lay his twelve old findings across a new figure to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand that has no key is a row made even on paper, and paper is not a payer.

The hand outside the wool came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The dressed grain lay covered under wool with no figure over it; the pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with the half-beat still in it and no look toward the bed. Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate had been opened within reach of the basket, no figure given for the boy, no sale of the waiting, no witness of the men, and the year's first arithmetic waited three mornings off where it had been left.
