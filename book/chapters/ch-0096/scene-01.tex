\daymark{339}{Ash 10, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and fifty-second answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist worth speaking of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal and had since learned the exact weight of a barley crust at two inches. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Corin]{the boy} lay awake and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under the finger's-width door. The room smelled of wet wool and tallow and, under them, the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which had not been swept in two years. Three hundred and thirty-nine stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and fifty-nine days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-nine days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Nothing from the seventh, eighth or ninth of \place{Ash} crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The pot-crack resting of the ninth sat on a place of its own beside the five held objects and was expressly not a sixth; an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and he had kept rows honest longer than anybody under this roof could count, which was not a thing he could say to anybody. The ear gave a man the truth before the eye did, because the eye is always being told what to see and the ear is not; he had learned that in a room where nobody had ever thought to tell him what he was hearing.

On \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} the inventory came in its old order, because an order is the one thing a field will give a man who cannot move in it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder through the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the small hours to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to wipe is a thing that stays. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping for a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face that cold puts on a man, the composed face that is not composure, the ease that is no ease. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; he knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes, and that the pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through every morning of his life in this house: the surgeon taking the inventory exactly and entering it nowhere, the infant underneath taking none of it and wanting, the two of them quarrelling in a body that could not so much as lift its own chin from a fold. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and could not lift a crust; the joke cost him nothing and was not funny. When he turned his head past a point a small sound came up out of him that was not a word and would not be commanded; he had once told a junior that a patient making that sound was already in trouble, and now the sound was his own and there was no junior to hear it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She was a broad woman who took up room by being impossible to move, with forearms the colour of dried slip and a burn across the back of her left hand shaped like a little door --- two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside it puckered and pale. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear. The ash over them was the colour of a day-old loaf and broke where she turned it, showing a red that went out again almost at once. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the breath went in, stood, and went out, and she did not call the space between the two ends of it anything. She counted the way another woman might count spoons on a shelf, where the count is a way of knowing the room is as it was and not an entry in anybody's book. The little-door burn shut on nothing after the coal turned, and nothing in the room corrected it. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air. She did not look at the bed. She had one grey streak at her temple and she pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket.

From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot, and her thumb went to the wire where it crossed the crack as it had gone on every morning for three years. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, and had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man who had first said them. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and here the hand was left showing in the dark hairline between brass and wire. He read the mending as a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, by what the hand had done and by what it had left undone, and kept the two readings apart: a seam that holds is not a seam that never broke. The dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth, and she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the morning required and turned it once and gave no figure; her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. Wool over turning grain gave its small dry rasp, and the smell of the yard came up through it. It was the lightest touch she used all day, lighter than the setting of a fold, and it told him nothing he could carry out of the room because there was no number on it. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. The room held the three the way a body holds three old injuries, each with its own weather, and none of them would agree to be one pain. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; an empty place kept empty was a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh hearing. The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap: the staying at the inch of Bone 15, the early letting-go of Bone 17, the handful let fall at the rack end on Bone 22, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh on Bone 26 and the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} on Ash 2, five objects on five places with a finger between every pair and none made again; the pot-crack resting of the ninth stood beside them and not among them, with a finger between it and each, and no payer stood under any of them. He did not look at the sixth place longer than it took to know it was six, for a finding stared at too long starts asking to be promoted.

Six arithmetics of the year --- the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone --- rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space, six readings six and not one account. Ash 10 was a date that had arrived and must earn its reading; Ash's first arithmetic belonged to its own morning, to go up in the locked box and stand with the six behind one unsigned figure, and was not carried into the small hours. The finding, once the morning had earned it, that seven are seven and not one account would be kept on a place of its own with no payer, not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps; the edges would come near to touching and the finger would go between. Ash 20, the month's second Account-day, stood ten mornings ahead and was named nowhere under this roof; it belonged to its own day and was not brought into one that had not earned it. Berr had wiped his thumb twice on his apron the last time and taken himself out of his own sentence, and the boy had filed the taking-apart as a hand drawn from a field before the dressing goes on; nothing from it was due this morning, and no eighth arithmetic was owed on a day that had not earned one. The box had a lock and the lock had a key that went up the road in a coat pocket and did not come back down, and the boy had no key and made no figure out of a reading he had only heard.

In the small hours, with the river's note low and going lower under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The satisfaction was not comfort. Comfort wants a giver standing, and none stood, so the feeling had to be taken small and without a name. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and fifty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 9 pot-crack resting or his own kept gaps. Two hundred and fifty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the small hours. Three hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the seventh, eighth or ninth of Ash had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended, and nothing had crossed into the tenth to make any of them a sign. The first reading of the dark month was coming up the clay road without anybody under this roof having made it happen, and no hand here would go down to meet it as a question. The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and fifty-three, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap, the twice-mended pot standing on its board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, the fired kiln standing cold behind the kiln mouth, and the tenth morning at the door with its arithmetic still unread. Forward, not closed.
