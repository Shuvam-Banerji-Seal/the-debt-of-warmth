\daymark{340}{Ash 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

In the small hours under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} the hand outside the wool lay where the last one had been laid, against the fold on the two hundred and fifty-fourth answer, a tool put away where the next hand would find it without looking. It came up for the two hundred and fifty-fifth. Three hundred and forty stood exact behind it, six months and one hundred and sixty days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty days; on its peg the salt-stiff coat kept the shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under that roof the same number. Nothing from the tenth of \place{Ash} crossed the night as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The reading had gone up where readings go up and had answered nothing under this roof; the basket had come home under the fold with the same two inches of air in it it had gone out with, and the figure had gone off up the road in a box with a lock and a key in a coat pocket that did not come back down.

He took the room as a field is taken before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the tick of clay going on cooling behind a wall, the settle of a coal, the thin rasp of the river under its skin of ice, the small dry scrape of a foot somewhere above and behind that was not coming down. The dark month had turned over its first Account-day and the house had kept its grain under wool without a number, and the boy had kept the keeping without a figure, which was not the same thing and never had been.

It was a Firstday with no market and no Cross and no bell, an ordinary day of the kind the district kept in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces; the yard would go around its seven places in the afternoon and the pot would say nothing to anybody's cost-question, and there was no arithmetic due under this roof before its own morning had earned it. The quiet was not a decoration set on the figure and it was not an answer to it. It was a house going on being a house, which is the most exact thing any house does.

\person[Corin]{The boy} took the body in its old order, because a field is checked the same way every time or the check is worth nothing. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, that the ease cold puts on a face is no ease, that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes; he knew that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core, and that a man who mistakes the one for the other will lose the patient in the hour he thinks he has saved him. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set where his own hand could reach it. Both were true and the second was louder. Neither won. The argument was the work, and he had done it every morning of his three hundred and forty days, which was longer than any man in this house had kept anything except a grudge or a fire.

The body was allowed to humiliate him and did, every morning, without malice and without an audience: he had reduced shoulders in the dark and pinned femurs by feel and now he could not keep a fold of wool on his own ear, could not lift one leathery crust two inches, could not wipe a thread that had frozen where nobody had thought to tell him a thread could freeze. He had once been the man other men called when a room went quiet in a particular way. Here he was the quiet in the room. The joke had cost him something every time he found it funny, and he found it funny every morning.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the breath went in, stood and went out, and she did not call the space between the two ends of it anything. The three acts stayed on her own side of the room with a finger between them and his three, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}.

Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally under it stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap, the way a person counts loaves and leaves the empty place at the row's end empty. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air and staying closed after there was nothing in it to hold. One grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist. When her hands had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child --- and when they had done counting they put the count nowhere at all and kept the shape of the day in loaves and glances instead. The boy had watched her do it for three hundred and forty days and had never once seen her enter a figure against him.

From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot. Her thumb went to the wire where it crossed the crack as it had gone on every morning for three years, and the hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire and she left it showing. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person; they had been improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man who first said them had said them, which did not stop them being true. The dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth and she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the morning required and turned it once and gave no figure; her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects with his name unspoken three hundred and forty days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow wheel-seat nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both, the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and the unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh notch with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The debt against a firing of tiles remained neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. No lesson was made of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

\scenebreak

Seven arithmetics of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and Ash's first, gone up the road the morning before in a locked box. Seven were seven and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid and no signature stood on the space between. The finding sat where findings sat, on a place of its own with no payer under it. He did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool at the rack end, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting beside them on its own place, the handful let fall, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. The finding was a keeping and not a payment, and he did not let it stand up beside the house's not-doings and call itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. No eighth arithmetic was due on a morning that had not earned one; the second of the month belonged to its own day and was not brought into this one.

The reading answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out, nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, nothing for the fifth pull under the floor with its payer unnamed. It had not been put there to answer anything, and its going up where readings go up had not brought it one step nearer to any of them. He kept that where he kept the rest, on a place of its own, without making a cause of the nearness or a payment of the silence.

The three movements that had come nobody's stood each on its own place with a finger between and were not made again: the joining over the boards of the burned slate to the room with the damper shut, the fourth shaping at the well of a saying that had arrived nowhere, and the turning of a runner's writing toward herself upon the road. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing; the shut room stood at two tellings and not three; the well-saying stood at four shapings with no fifth and no reader; the row stood at five askings with no witness and no sixth; nine record-first hands stood with a finger between every pair and no tenth; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall at the rack end, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh and the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} stood each on its own place with a finger between every pair and between them and every other object there; the pot-crack resting sat beside them, expressly not a sixth. None was a habit, a way, a sign, a hearing, a mending, a cause, a payment or a rule, none had a payer standing under it, and none had come due with the reading or had been made due since.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. The heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh of the full note. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space. Ash 20, the month's second Account-day and market, stood nine mornings ahead and was named nowhere under this roof, a date and not a promise, belonging to a day that had not yet earned it.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. It did not reach; the inch was not a reach and he had long ago stopped calling it one. It was the distance a hand may move in a field without disturbing the dressing, and he took it the way he had once taken a hand into a cavity, slowly, with the whole of the attention and the knowledge that most of what a hand does in a field is wait. The note under the finger's-width door went low over lower, a thing over a thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, and let the intake go on the same low note. It came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and fifty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the joining over the boards, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the wood kept in a sleeve nor his own kept gaps.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both true, the second louder, neither winning. Two hundred and fifty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Three hundred and forty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixty days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Seven arithmetics rode locked and unread up \place{the clay road} behind one printed figure, the figure read still not the figure paid. The morning after the figure was ordinary to the last detail, and his keeping of that ordinariness, the finger going between every pair, was the whole of what had changed in him: another answer, another object worth nothing at two inches of air, held where no clerk could take a figure off it. The hand lay back against the fold, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap, and the eleventh day of the dark month one morning old. Forward, not closed.
