\daymark{341}{Ash 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

In the small hours under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} the hand outside the wool lay where the last one had been laid, flat against the fold on the two hundred and fifty-sixth answer, a tool put away where the next hand would find it without looking. It came up for the two hundred and fifty-seventh. Three hundred and forty-one stood exact behind it, six months and one hundred and sixty-one days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-one days; on its peg the salt-stiff coat kept the shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under that roof the same number. Nothing from the eleventh of \place{Ash} crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The yard had gone around its seven places the day before and had answered with directions and the weather; the pot had said nothing to anybody's question about a cost; a woman had kept a count in wood and had not let a figure off it. None of it came into the small hours. A day that has been lived is a day that has been set down, and he did not go back into it to fetch a meaning out of it.

He took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the thin tick of clay going on cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the dry rasp of the river under its skin of ice coming through the finger's-width door, and, above and behind, the small scrape of a foot on the loft stair that was not coming down. Something about the cold had tightened the house overnight, so that each sound stood separate, the way instruments lie separate on a towel. It was a Secondday with no market and no Cross and no bell, an ordinary day of the kind the district kept in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces. There was no arithmetic due under this roof before its own morning had earned it, and the quiet was not an answer to anything. It was a house going on being a house, which is the most exact thing a house does.

\person[Corin]{The boy} took the inventory on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, in its old order, because an order is the one thing a field will give a man who cannot move in it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder in the dry kiln heat than it had been at noon, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to wipe is a thing that stays. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping for a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the face that cold puts on a man, the composed face that is not composure, the ease that is no ease. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; he knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes, and that the pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through every morning of his three hundred and forty-one days. He had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal and could not lift one leathery crust two inches to his own mouth; the joke cost him nothing now and he found it funny every morning, which was a way of laughing that kept the blade in it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She was a broad woman who took up room by being impossible to move, with forearms the colour of dried slip and a burn across the back of her left hand shaped like a little door --- two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside it puckered and pale. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear. The ash over them broke where she turned it, the colour of a day-old loaf, and showed a red that went out almost at once. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the breath went in, stood and went out, and she did not call the space between its two ends anything. The three acts stayed on her side of the room with a finger between them and his three, and she did not look toward the bed.

Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap, the way a person counts loaves and leaves the empty place at the row's end empty. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air and staying closed after there was nothing in it to hold. One grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist. When her hands had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child --- and when they had done counting they put the count nowhere at all and kept the shape of the day in loaves and glances instead. He had watched her do it for three hundred and forty-one days and had never once seen her enter a figure against him.

From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot, and her thumb went to the wire where it crossed the crack as it had gone on every morning for three years. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person; they had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man who had first said them, which did not stop them being true. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and here the hand was left showing in the dark hairline between the brass and the wire. He read the mending as a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, by what the hand had done and by what it had left undone, and kept the two readings apart: a seam that holds is not a seam that never broke. The dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth and she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the morning required and turned it once and gave no figure; her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. Wool over turning grain gave its small dry rasp and the smell of the yard came up through it, and it was the lightest touch she used all day, lighter than the setting of a fold, and there was no number on it to carry out of the room. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects with his name unspoken three hundred and forty-one days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow wheel-seat nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both, the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and the unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The debt against a firing of tiles remained neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. No lesson was made of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

First light came without nudging the dark, the way it comes in the dark month: a grey thinning at the finger's-width door, not a beam, the river under its ice still holding its low note underneath it. The house took the light the way a patient takes a warming, from the outside and slowly. Nothing in the room was made new by it. The crust kept its corner standing a hair off the wool, the thread kept its frozen place, and the cold kept the line it had written at the wrist, because light is not a hand and nobody here had been given an instruction about any of it.

Seven arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and Ash's first, gone up the road the morning before in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box. Seven were seven and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid and no signature stood on the space between. The finding sat where findings sat, on a place of its own with no payer under it. He did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool at the rack end, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting beside them on its own place, the handful let fall, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. The finding was a keeping and not a payment, and he did not let it stand up beside the house's not-doings and call itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. No eighth arithmetic was due on a morning that had not earned one; Ash's second belonged to its own day and was not brought into this one.

The staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall at the rack end, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh and the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} stood each on its own place with a finger between every pair and between them and every other object there; the pot-crack resting of the ninth sat beside them and not among them, expressly not a sixth, with a finger between it and each, and no payer stood under any of them. The three movements that had come nobody's stood each on its own place with a finger between and were not made again: the joining over the boards of the burned slate to the room with the damper shut, the fourth shaping at the well of a saying that had arrived nowhere, and the turning of a runner's writing toward herself upon the road. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing; the shut room stood at two tellings and not three; the well-saying at four shapings with no fifth and no reader; the row at five askings with no witness and no sixth. The ninth record-first hand and the eighth stood on places of their own with a finger between them and no tenth; the count a woman kept in wood stayed in her sleeve with no figure off it, and a runner had carried a figure up the road without reading it and was not made a reader by the carrying. None was a habit, a way, a sign, a hearing, a mending, a cause, a payment or a rule; none had a payer standing under it, none had come due with the reading, and none was made due now.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; an empty place kept empty was a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh of the full note. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap.

Ash 20, the month's second Account-day and market, stood eight mornings ahead and was named nowhere under this roof, a date and not a promise, belonging to a day that had not earned it. No eighth arithmetic, no sixth telling, no third telling of the shut room, no fifth shaping, no tenth hand and no tenth stopping was due on the twelfth of Ash. The boy kept the count the way he kept the rest: as an object on its own place, with a finger between it and the next, and with no hand standing under it to be asked who had paid. He had stopped expecting to be told what any of it counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. It did not reach; the inch was not a reach and he had long ago stopped calling it one. It was the distance a hand may move in a field without disturbing the dressing, and he took it the way he had once taken a hand into a cavity, slowly, with the whole of the attention and the knowledge that most of what a hand does in a field is wait. The note under the finger's-width door went low over lower, a thing over a thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, and let the intake go on the same low note. It came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and fifty-seven: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the joining over the boards, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the wood kept in a sleeve nor his own kept gaps.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both true, the second louder, neither winning. Two hundred and fifty-seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Three hundred and forty-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixty-one days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Seven arithmetics rode locked and unread up \place{the clay road} behind one printed figure, the figure read still not the figure paid. The twelfth morning was exactly like the mornings before it, and his keeping of that sameness, the finger going between every pair, was the whole of what had changed in three hundred and forty-one days: another answer, another object worth nothing at two inches of air, and Ash's second reading still locked in a box up the road. The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and fifty-seven, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap, Ash 12 one morning old and Ash 20 eight mornings ahead, named only as a date. Forward, not closed.
