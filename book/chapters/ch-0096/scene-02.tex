\person{Sella Vail} carried him down before first light. \place[the Clay Yard]{The yard} was a room with no light in it yet and the river wind had not yet come up off the water; the clay underfoot held the cold of the night and gave it back in the small dry rasp of a boot. She went at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the basket level on her hip and the fold laid over his ears against the wind, and the instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. \person[Corin]{The boy} took the carrying apart as he took everything apart, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the settle of her breath under the load, the rasp of the pail rope at \place{the yard well}, the tick of clay going on cooling behind a wall. Three hundred and thirty-nine stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and fifty-nine days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor of the house he had left \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed the same number of days; on its peg the salt-stiff coat kept the shape of a man whose name had not been spoken under that roof the same number.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept; the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; the skinned \place{trough}; the field's empty place out on the worked strips; the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven places, and not one of them given a figure. The three coverings at the well stayed weather entered nowhere. Nothing from the seventh, eighth or ninth of \place{Ash} crossed the yard with them as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The pot-crack resting of the ninth sat on a place of its own beside the five held objects and was expressly not a sixth; an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and he had kept rows honest longer than anybody in this yard could count, which was not a thing he could say to anybody.

At \place{the Cross} she set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, with his cheek turned toward the cold iron and his ear to the wool. Two hundred people were already leaning toward \place{the reading block under the moot-post}, and the pigs were being driven over the wet boards, eight of them, though nobody in the shade counted them aloud. She gave no instruction and no reason to the basket. She stood half a pace off and did not look into it, and the half-pace was not a distance she had chosen for feeling; it was the distance at which no man could read a figure over him without her seeing the hand that read it. The market came up around them in mud and straw and the smell of wet pig and turnip. The cold iron of the post put its own small cold into the air beside his cheek, the third cold of the morning and the one that had not come from inside him.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road}. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward. A finger went over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and his eyes rested on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. The boy took the order apart as he took apart a field before the dressing went on: hand to the record before eye to the person, eye before mouth, and no hand at all to the basket. He filed the twice-wiped thumb on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other hand he had ever filed, and filed no tenth.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” Berr read low into the goers-by, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs. He sent the figure twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the late households, and the figure went over the top of it as water goes over a stone lying where a stone lies. He did not look into the basket. He never did. “The levy is called. It is not called by me,” he said. A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and let it stand there; Berr looked at the fist, did not take the number, and said, “Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” Your house. Never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the work clean and the hand that did it nowhere in it. Then he said something more low to the block about kindness being entered, in the same cup-setting tone, and the block, being a block, answered nothing.

There was no anger in the reading and no triumph in it and nothing in the face of the man who read it that a crowd could have fastened on. That was the terrible part, and the boy had learned early to be afraid of the banal before he was afraid of the cruel: a reasonable man with a ledger and a good reason is the most frightening thing in \place{Sarn}, and the reason is the part that will be remembered against him. He had once stood in a white room and watched a man say a number over a body on a table and go on to the next body, and the number had been right and the hand that wrote it clean. Here the number was one in twelve and the print had not been challenged in any of the year's readings. Seven arithmetics of the year now stood behind that one printed figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and now Ash's first, going up in the locked box. Seven were seven and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid and no signature stood on the space between.

The finding came where findings came, on a place of its own with no payer under it. He did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool at the rack end, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting beside them, the handful let fall, the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh, the two small objects of the hand or his own kept gaps. The edges of these things came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. No doctrine stood up, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The reading answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out, nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, nothing for the carried fire's fifth with its payer unnamed. It answered nothing for the crust two inches from his ear or the thread frozen at his mouth, because it was not put there to answer anything.

Berr went off up \place{the clay road} with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Ash's first true subtraction riding inside beside the six that had gone before. He believed he had done the district a kindness. The district's arithmetic had changed for Ash and could not be changed back, and no payer stood where the boy could see. The box had a lock and the lock had a key that went up the road in a coat pocket and did not come back down; the boy had no key and made no figure out of a reading he had only heard. Ash 20, the month's second Account-day, stood ten mornings ahead and was named nowhere in the square; it belonged to its own day and was not brought into one that had earned only this.

\person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road before noon. It came to her hand first: the small flat correction of the strap, the turn of the wrist bringing the edge round, the thumb along the grain of the frame with nothing pressing where the writing was. Only then did her eyes go to the crowd. The writing lay turned to her sleeve, the slate's face edge-on to her forearm, and it was not concealment but the manner of carrying a slate so that the writing was not rubbed and no goer-by took a figure off it without a hand to put there. The second strap was let out for the long stretch and dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for the wet. She moved at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking.

A woman by the rail asked what she carried. “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which,” Hessa said, answering the question put and not the one behind it, and went on up toward \place{Greybank} with no rudeness and no apology and no figure coming off the slate. She was a runner and not a reader. Her hand to the slate before her eye to the crowd stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, eight and not a rule, with no tenth and no figure made of the slate's passing. The Ash 6 turning of her writing toward herself stayed a sighting on its own place beside the fourth shaping of the well-saying, with a finger between; the reading of a figure that had named a person she had once known did not make her a reader of it, a witness, a cause or a payment. She was not joined to any voice standing in no doorway, not to Marra, not to the soft-handed man of Slate Row, not to the clerks, not to \person{Garl Vail}, not to the woman with the count in wood, and the far-off day a hand like hers would lay the district's separate keepings together was not brought one step nearer by the morning's arithmetic.

The bell went four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The boy kept the heard bell in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap longer than a church bell, and did not fetch it in to make a fourth count or a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. An empty place kept empty was a finding, and nothing the bell did this morning invented a seventh to fill it.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing}, where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His slate lay inward to his forearm and his hand went to it before his eyes went to the stalls; the market sold over him as water over a stone, and nobody took him off. The boy put the order of his reaching on a place of its own and made no figure of it and no joining to the runner's eighth hand or to Berr's twice-wiped thumb. Three record-first hands in one morning and still no tenth and still no rule; a rule wants a payer put under all of them and none stood.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a woman stood with clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the sweetish green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, and there was a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. She said nothing. At the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver at its throat and went dull again. The boy called the one a colour and the other a shape and gave them each a place of their own, a seventh of each with no eighth, and nothing more. He did not name either. He did not join the green at the pot board to the green at Berr's mouth, or either of them to the sliver, or the woman to the shape, or any of them to a loaf, a slate, a sale, a hearing, a cause or a romance. No second look was given and none was made into a saying; a sighting is a sighting and becomes a witness only when somebody stands up behind it to be asked, and nobody stood.

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him home. She went at the half-pace and wide around the seven walked-around places, and the returning-line place where the hand had gone in and not come out was gone around with a look and no figure, as it had been gone around going down. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. She said nothing of where they had been or what had been read over the basket. The instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket.

She went to \place{the hearth} before the basket. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the breath went in, stood and went out, and she did not call the space between the two ends of it anything. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot, and her thumb went to the wire where it crossed the crack as it had gone on every morning for three years; the hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire and she left it showing. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person. The dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth, and she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the evening required and turned it once and gave no figure; her fingers went in and came out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because a reading is not an opening; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had earned a reading and nothing else.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects with his name unspoken three hundred and thirty-nine days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow wheel-seat nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both, the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. The debt against a firing of tiles remained neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. No lesson was made of any of it. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

Through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure, and now seven stood behind it, and seven were seven and not one account, the finding kept on its own place with no payer and not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand or the boy's own kept gaps; the edges came near to touching and the finger went between, and no doctrine stood up because no payer stood. The seven rode locked and unread up the clay road; the printed figure had not been challenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. In the small hours of the morning the hand outside the wool had come up an inch toward the river's note. In the going of the light it came up again, an inch and no more, toward the low note under the door. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The inch was not a reach, the intake not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river and the note not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and fifty-four: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the wood kept in a sleeve, the pot-crack resting nor his own kept gaps.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning. Two hundred and fifty-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Three hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Ash's first arithmetic had gone up the road in a locked box and answered nothing under this roof; Ash's second stood ten mornings ahead, named nowhere, and was not brought in. The hand lay back against the fold, the file open on the towel with every gap gap, the twice-mended pot standing on its board with the hairline of the break showing between brass and wire, and the tenth day of the dark month at its close with its arithmetic still unread. Forward, not closed.
