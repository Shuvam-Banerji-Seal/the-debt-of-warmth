## Chronicle

On the tenth day of Ash, the dark month's first Account-day, \person{Sella Vail} carried the basket down before first light at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and set it in \place{the moot-post}'s shade. Two hundred people leaned toward the block; eight pigs came over the boards, uncounted aloud. \person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, passed a finger over the figure, and read *One in twelve, as the notice says*, sending it twice over the basket. “The levy is called. It is not called by me,” he said, and spoke of *your house*, never I; then something low to the block about kindness being entered, and the block answered nothing. He went off with the box locked and Ash's first subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Seven arithmetics now stand behind one printed figure, and seven are seven and not one account.

\person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road before noon, her hand to it before her eyes to the crowd, the writing to her sleeve. To a woman who asked what she carried she said, “I don't read it. I carry it.” The bell went four, four and a gap; nobody crossed himself. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for an uncalled measure, the market selling over him as water over a stone. At the pot board a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back; at the market's edge a shape in cast-offs caught light on a green sliver. Each was a seventh with no eighth, and neither was named.

On the eleventh and twelfth the house kept its ordinary days. \person{Ilva} drew at the well with the pail against her knee; to a woman asking what the reading had taken she answered, “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account,” her hand toward her sleeve and staying, the notches unread. \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, and said *Yes, house* to the early thaw and the late, keeping the late because it puts off the paying. \person{Jorra Vail} spoke to the wheel before any person and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished.

At each going of the light Sella lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and left the hairline showing. The figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space. The hand came up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake, held it whole and let it go on the same low note, and two hundred and fifty-eight honest answers stand, each an object and not a power.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** the chapter stops in the small hours at the very end of Day 341 under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, with \person[Corin]{the boy} on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, the hand outside the wool laid back against the fold on answer **two hundred and fifty-eight** as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. No one else is in the room at the close; \person{Sella Vail} has set him down, gone to the hearth and gone. “Forward, not closed.”
- **Day and time at the end:** Day 341, Ash 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813, the small hours after the going of the light — the third of three fully lived days, each with its own `\daymark` (339 Ash 10 Tenthday, 340 Ash 11 Firstday, 341 Ash 12 Secondday). He is three hundred and forty-one days old and six months and one hundred and sixty-one days, told by nobody.
- **Where everyone is now:** Corin/Dan on the bed, awake, silent, cold, unable to move; Sella Vail at the hearth and about the house, having carried him up at the half-pace and left the thread frozen; Garl Vail absent three hundred and forty-one days and kept only in objects (salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease, hollow wheel-seat, clean dark line and finer grey called the thaw, twice-mended pot on the pot board with the hairline showing, unforged collector sentence at the kindling). Tarn and Jorra Vail last seen in the Clay Yard that afternoon — Tarn at the kindling with six counted and the seventh at the lip, Jorra at the wheel-shed edge and the cold damper; both came home ahead of the boy. Berr Clay went up the clay road on Ash 10 with the box locked and Ash's first subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness; Hessa Rale went up the road before noon that morning carrying the slate unread. Ilva was at the well on Ash 11 with the strip still in her sleeve; no runner was seen on any road on Ash 11 or 12. All farther-off characters (Mira Silt, Orra Weir, the reed-cutter, Alda Reed, Rusk, Marra, the soft-handed man of Slate Row) are unchanged and offstage; Dessa Silt stays dead.
- **Items, money, powers:** nothing gained he can use; no money, purchase, sale, bond, loaf passed or slate opened within reach of the basket on any of the three days. The twice-mended pot, the broken pot used to turn the coals, the dressed grain under wool, the charcoal tally beneath the loaf shelf, the clay jar under lead glaze and the widow's mark are all where they were. No rank change, no experiment, no usable power, no speech; counting remains an old-life habit, not a gift. The body's one movement — the inch toward the river's note, the small intake, the interval held whole, the letting-go on the note's own finishing — made six answers (253–258) worth nothing at two inches of air.
- **Facts, words, promises, debts:** Berr read *one in twelve* and denied the levy with *It is not called by me* and *your house*, never *I*, and said something low to the block about kindness being entered. Hessa said *I don't read it. I carry it.* Ilva said *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.* Tarn said *Six counted. The next I don't count* and *Yes, house*, keeping the late thaw because it puts off the paying. Jorra said *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* and *You'll do*. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn; the tile debt and the loan that can hear remain uncalled; no payer stands under any finding.
- **Threads advanced, nothing paid (T015/T041/T047):** Ash's first arithmetic went up in Berr's locked box and **seven arithmetics of the year now stand behind one unsigned figure — seven are seven and not one account**; the figure read is still not the figure paid, no signature on the space, the finding held on a place of its own with no payer and not laid across the not-doings, the covered grain, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand or his gaps; edges came near to touching and the finger went between; no doctrine stood up. Also carried: T080 (Hessa on Ash 10 a runner and not a reader, eighth hand no tenth; Ilva on Ash 11 ninth hand, strip unread, no tenth, no ownerless saying, no fifth shaping); T009/T013/T092 (pot-crack resting not made again on 10, 11 or 12 and expressly not a sixth object); T058/T079 (widow's mark untouched, six openings not a rule, no seventh made due); T077, T036/T076, T060/T086, T061/T082, T083/T085, T101, T090, T050/T051, T033/T034, T078, T098 — all held, none spent. No eighth arithmetic, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, fifth shaping, tenth hand, tenth stopping or eighth sighting came due.
- **Next chapter must not forget:** Day 339 was Ash 10, **Tenthday** (not Firstday), the first Account-day and market; Days 340 and 341 were ordinary Firstday and Secondday with no market, Cross or bell, and no collector on the clay road on any of the three days. Answers 253–258 are Chapter 96's six; **258 honest answers** stand on the sixth place. **Ash 20 (Day 349), the month's second Account-day, stands eight mornings ahead and is named only as a date** — do not bring it into a day that has not earned it. Nothing from Ash 10, 11 or 12 crosses forward as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The finding that seven are seven and not one account, the seven finding's lack of a payer, the runner unread, the count kept in wood, the five held objects plus the pot-crack resting beside them (not among them), and all nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end carry forward unchanged and unspent.
