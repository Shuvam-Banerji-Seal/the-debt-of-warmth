The going of the light had come down off the square and into the clay road before she lifted the basket. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears. It had come all the way down with them and it would go all the way back up. In \place{Low Kiln} the doors were shutting and the ash-coloured light lay along the ruts with the mud dried pale in the hollows of it; a pan set out to dry on a flat stone caught the last of it and held it, and a trough by the well's edge had a skin of ice that had formed since morning and would be gone by morning and was called weather by every woman who stepped around it. She went around the pan and the trough as she went around everything a person might knock over, and the half-beat in her walk was the same half-beat she had kept in the square when the market sold its reeds over the clerk and over the missing step. Nothing in the going of the light had changed the pace.

The carrying arm kept its account in muscle, registering every lift of the day from \place{the Weir} up to the shade and back again, what had been held and how long and how the weight had shifted at the crossing; it was exact and complete and told nothing of what any of it had cost. He had learned that in the last third of the reed month, and the knowledge was worth nothing he could hand to anybody. Under the wool his small hand outside the fold went up an inch toward the note of water that came up from the river below and came down again, not toward the bundle or the pot or any of the places gone around, but toward the direction of the water, which was the only direction he had. The count went on going without anybody's thumb upon it.

At \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} she went around the space beneath it as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The broken birth-slates lay where they had lain through the planting and through the reed cutting, gone around and entered by nobody, and the space kept them the way a closed hand keeps what it will not show. A woman taking in a strip of reed lifted her elbow to let the basket pass and did not look at the place under the board. The boy was carried past at the half-pace and saw only the dark under the lowest rail and the pale frost-bitten ends of the reeds beyond. He had learned in a hundred and thirty-seven days that a place gone around is a place kept, and that a thing kept in the ground under a rack is not a thing entered on a slate; the two are different orders of keeping and he had no standing to lay them together. He did not lay them together.

Under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, lifting him out of the basket without looking into it and setting him down as one sets a pot down where it will not be slopped. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and the thread of drool at his mouth froze where it lay, because nobody under this roof or any roof in the district had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and that half-beat was the whole of what she gave him that could not have been given by somebody who said what it was. She did not say it and he did not ask, not having the words and not having the breath. The one brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold two inches from his ear with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third, and it had gone from warm to cold to past the smelling of anything and nobody had lifted it or called it by a name. The shelf kept its two gaps in the other room and the two rooms did not touch.

She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot so that one small place among them came through with the heat and the rest lay under blind \lore{Ash}. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving the way they had moved along the loaf row at midday when the two gaps stood side by side and nobody gave them a figure. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, and the handle stood two inches beyond the hand that would have reached for it and would not have closed on it. The \lore{widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord against her throat and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and a shelf, a strip, a rack, a basket and a bed are not openings. She gave no explanation of the count she kept and went to her own bed.

In the small hours he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before he begins, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else. He had kept six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at the Drawing --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh had come in a hundred and thirty-seven days and he had not invented one to fill the place where a seventh would stand if there were sevenths to stand. The told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap in each, and the two neither agreed nor disagreed, being two objects in two places with nobody crossing himself at either. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: the cup, the pot, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, each answered so nearly that the want under the note could be felt, and none of them filled.

The interval tray held its older intervals, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger at \lore{the damper}, the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check, and the breath a man drew at the top of a reach and did not spend. And now a fifth stopping lay among them beside the first four, an object among objects: the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it, brought all the way this day to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent. Five likenesses lay on that tray and he held them apart, for a man who lets five likenesses stand up together will find they have stood up and called themselves a doctrine by morning, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer had been named to any of these five. The fifth stopping was an interval and not a payment, an object and not a rule, a thing that had been stopped and not a thing that had been settled. The loan that could hear was still uncalled and the seventh year had not given. The damper's double bite was filed as an object joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies and no standing to improve either. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side with no third, breath through the nose and breath in the throat, both saying not the war's hand, and the room off the shed kept its finger's width of open and gave up nothing.

\scenebreak

In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, and no third came; a saying of the loaf-shelves held that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name, and no finger in \place{Low Kiln} had laid these together and no account had wanted a name. The charcoal marks under the board lay on a place of their own, short clusters of strokes pressed hard and faded nearly into the grain, and the finger that had gone beneath the front edge at midday had come away without a word and told him nothing of what the marks said; he did not lay the underside beside the two gaps above it or beside the crust in the fold, and left the reason of the hand where it stood, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place beside it unnamed, and the two rooms did not touch.

The word forty stayed unspoken with the readable double turn beside it --- tail tucked, tied left over right, the small weight of a thumb pressed in --- and the knot was a shape and not a figure; it had been tied this morning on the lower boards and the name of it had not been said there or anywhere else. The reed-cutter's grey cord had gone back inside his coat drawn close over it, knots unread, no figure, no name and no joining to the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out. The second cord beneath \person[Mira Silt]{Mira}'s apron kept its twice-twisted turn with neither knot counted and had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door. The thane's red cord had gone folded under his coat up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln} with the true subtraction locked in his box, and his thumb had been wiped twice before he touched nothing and his finger had gone over a figure before his voice went to a buyer; a number had gone into a slate and two marks short had gone into a palm without a count, and the two went on being two. Four greens had stood at four mouths this day and five shapes of the colour if a thane's own glaze was counted, and none of them had stood up and said anything, and no fifth had come to a mouth to make the count come out even. The cold foot below the sill stayed the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused, unnamed, with the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. Below the north bridge at \place{the out-of-line landing} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called, his hand going to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls; nobody had taken him off since the month turned, or somebody had put him back in the dark. \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages lay turned toward a floorboard three doors down in \place{House Silt} with her little tin shut in her bodice, and \person{Dessa Silt}'s round pewter tin above the abandoned bed kept its pale fold and thumb-bright lid shut; neither tin was opened and neither page was read, and no name and no number came up out of the water.

Every object was in its place and nothing was laid across anything else. It was the whole of his inventory and it was worth nothing. He was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it: a man who had once laid out instruments on a towel and known every one by the wear on its handle, now a child who could not close two inches of his hand on the rim of the basket two paces off, with a tray of records he could not lift to any table and no table that would have taken them. He had spent a hundred and thirty-seven days learning that a likeness is not a cause and that five stoppings do not make a payment, and the keeping of the things apart was the only honest work in the room and the one no slate would ever enter. Exact, complete, worth nothing.

Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and the Weir on \place{the Ilm}, a fifth low thing never counted among the full note; through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-seven days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one. A man who lays three counts together and calls them one account has made a cause out of a room, and there was no room under this roof for another cause.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint so the cold found the bare place and went under the fold where nobody had been given an instruction to put a hand. One hundred and thirty-seven days old, four months and seventeen days, he was told by nobody, and if somebody had said the number aloud nobody under this roof would have known what it counted, and he would not have known either what it counted, only that it was his and exact and unspent. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. It went on being exact in the dark while the house kept its three counts in three places. In the next room the row of loaves kept its two gaps and under the shelf the charcoal waited where it had been put, pressed hard and faded nearly into the grain, for a hand not his and a day that had not come.
