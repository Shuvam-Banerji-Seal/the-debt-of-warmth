\daymark{135}{Reed 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he reed month} went into its last third on a morning the colour of wet wool, with the sky the grey of the reeds below it and no line drawn between the two. \place{Low Kiln} came up into the light the way a pot comes up from under a bench --- the shadow first, the rim after it, the whole belly last --- and the yard received it without remark. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} out at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going wide around the stones and the laid-down pans and anything set out to dry, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears; it had come down with them and would go back up with them, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing. Woodsmoke went before her down the yard and wet wool went with her, and under both the faint sour mineral tang of the \lore{Ash} that had gathered in the corners of this house for two years and would not be swept. He was one hundred and thirty-five days old, four months and fifteen days, and nobody in the yard said so, and nobody under any roof in the district knew there was a number to say.

She set him down at the edge of the work where no slate lay, as she had set him down every morning of the cutting, and stood a half-pace off with the burn shut on nothing. The basket sat in the thin shade at the rack's end with the wool up around his ears and the grey coming through at his cheek. A woman at a tub lifted her head as they passed, saw a basket and the woman carrying it, and went back to her work without appearing to have seen either. That was the whole of the welcome the yard had for them and the whole of the harm it meant them; he had long since stopped looking for more in it. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind again, low and practical, and went nowhere near the basket. It had never occurred to anybody under this roof to give the basket an instruction, and a thing with no instruction was a thing nobody had taken responsibility for, and so it was left where it was set.

The reeds stood in the ruts where the barley had stood a month before, in grey ranks going down toward \place{the reed beds}, the broken ends green under the frost and the tips pale as old bone. Nobody counted them. They came in on the shoulders and in the crooks of arms, a going-downriver at first light and a coming-back at dusk, and a bundle standing in the mud was judged by the arm that had carried it and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter going down past the racks shifted his load from one shoulder to the other and said to a girl with a creel who had asked him the weight of it, “Lift it. Your arm'll tell you what went into it, and that's the whole of what it knows.” He went on with the bundle, and the girl lifted it, and the arm under it gave her its answer and kept the rest. The girl shifted the bundle on her hip and asked what it would cost, and the cutter, already a dozen paces down with another armful, said over his shoulder, “It doesn't know cost. Ask it how heavy it is.” The girl did not ask again. By noon the bundles would stand in the ruts where the barley had stood, each one a shape nobody had numbered, and the arms that had brought them would go back downriver for more. A judge could have set a figure on the whole day's cutting in the time it takes to lift a pot; no judge was set here, and the reeds went on standing for the arm that had carried them and for nobody else. The boy, carried at the edge of the work where no slate lay, watched the shoulders go down the yard and come back up it and noted the order of them without deciding to: the hand went to the load before the eyes went to the road, and the road got no more of a look than the load got a name.

A woman working the strips at \place{the drying racks} straightened from her work with dry mud worked into the creases of her wrists and asked the cutter what the hands would be paid now the reed had gone into its last third. It was a question about a figure made to sound like a question about the weather, and the yard heard it as both. The cutter did not stop. “The weather and the river,” he said. “Ask them. They'll tell you when they've had enough.” The woman kept her eyes on his retreating shoulders a moment longer than the shoulders required, and then went back to her strips with the answer standing where he had left it. No figure went with her. Nothing had been entered. She shut two fingers out of a fist as she went back to her strips, the way a woman shuts a count she will not say aloud, and the gesture went down into the mud with her and came up nothing. The cutter had given her a direction because a direction was the only answer the yard kept in stock; a figure would have had to be made, and a made figure wants a maker, and the maker would have had to stand where the hands were and be named. Nobody in the ruts wanted that standing. The boy watched the two fingers close and noted them on a place of their own, beside the shoulders and the load and the road, and did not make a sum of the three. The reed would be cut because the reed was there and the river was low enough to cut it, and when the hands had had enough they would stop, and the stopping would not be written down either. Account-day stood four days off at Reed 20, and nobody in the ruts had yet said the date.

\scenebreak

\place{the Clay Yard} went around its own absences the way water goes around a stone it cannot move. Sella carried him past the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and past the new finer hairline of grey that had gathered beside it and been called \lore{the thaw}, with a care that looked like not seeing either one. She did not sweep them. A line left where it was was still a line kept, and the house said the grey would go when it went, and he had learned by now that a thing a house said would go was a thing a house had decided not to carry out. At the racks she went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered it no more than she had entered it on any of the hundred and thirty-four days before. Then they came to the edge of \place{the worked strips}, and there was the other place.

It was not a hole and it was not a mark. It was a space in the returning line where, on Seed 15, a hand had gone in with the seed and on Seed 27 had not come out with the rest. Sella went around it with a look and a wide step and no number, the way the yard had gone around it every morning since, and the boy felt the half-pace change by the width of a hand as she shifted the basket away from it. No name went with the step. No cause, no body, no figure, no payer. If a hand came up out of the ground in a month or a year a slate would be found and a figure made to stand at the foot of it; until then the space would go on being gone around, and the month would go on standing short. A cost with no figure was still a cost, and the arm that had carried the seed in that morning had known the weight of it and knew nothing of what it had cost.

He set the going-around on the place where it already lay, beside and never among the shelf's two gaps and the crust in the fold two rooms away, and drew no line through them. A saying of the loaf-shelves held that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name, and a neighbour had once laid a planting and a child's appetite under one cause and gone off with her pot without seeing she had joined anything. The keeping of the places apart was invisible, exact, and was the only work in this yard that nobody had asked him to do.

He thought about the arm that carried him as a man might think about an instrument he had once known by the wear on its handle. She had carried him out every morning of his life and would carry him back at dusk, and the arm had kept its record in the muscle --- the reach to the wicker, the settling of the weight, the half-pace kept from every open slate, the width of the step around the place where the hand had gone in. The record was exact. It told what had been carried and how it had been carried and where the carrier had chosen to go around. It told nothing of what the carrying cost. There was no nerve in the muscle that could enter a figure, and no Court thumb would ever read the writing of it, and the arm would have given the same account if it had been carrying a pot or a sack of clay or his own small useless weight. He had spent a hundred and thirty-five days learning the difference between a record and a price, and the arm kept its record on a place where nothing could be entered against it: complete, faithful, worth nothing anyone could pay. He could have drawn the arm the way a man draws a field before the instrument comes down --- the reach, the pressure, the direction of the give, the place where the tissue had been taught to do the same thing a thousand times and now did it without being told. What he could not draw was the cost, because the cost had never been put into the muscle in the first place. It lived in a different room: in a woman counting under her breath toward no opening, in the grey at her temple, in the half-beat her hand rested longer than the setting needed before it went away. That room was not this one and he had no standing to enter it. He kept the arm's record and the other room on separate places, as he kept everything, and the keeping was the only honest work available to a child who could not hold a cup.

The order of reaching was the same here as it had been in every room he had ever been carried through. The hand went to the record before the hand went to the person. It had gone to the wool and the pot and the fire and the broom before it came to him, and here it went to the load and the line and the space in the returning line before it came to the child in the basket. He noted the order as a man notes a sequence he knows the cost of and cannot touch, without standing to change it and without a word in \lore{Grathic} to ask for a different one. A man who could once reduce a shoulder in the dark could not now close two inches of his hand on the rim of the basket two paces off. The competence was in the noticing and the helplessness was in the hands, and the two sat side by side and did not become one.

\scenebreak

Beyond the racks and the worked strips \place{the Ilm} went on with its note of water over water, a low fifth thing that the yard had never counted among \lore{the full note} and never would. The river wind brought it up to the ear under the fold and took it away again, and the boy's head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the sound, and nobody in the yard had been given an instruction about the thread at his mouth, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Morning went into midday over the racks while he lay at the edge of the work and listened. The note went on coming up out of the water below the reed beds and going off again on the wind, a low thing with no figure in it and no payer named, the fifth thing that was never counted among the others. His head went toward it and made the small wet sound and came back, and the hand outside the wool went up an inch and came down, as it had gone up and down on every morning since the month turned. Reach and note and count stayed in three places and none of them became one.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down against the fold. He could not reach the bundle going down the yard, or the pot, or the space beneath the lowest rack, or the place in the returning line where the hand had gone in and not come out. One hundred and thirty-five days old, four months and fifteen days, he kept the count exact and unspent and spoken by nobody. The woman at the racks went back to her strips with the weather in her mouth. The reeds stood grey in the ruts beyond the racks, uncounted, and the arm that carried them knew what it had carried and not what it had cost.
