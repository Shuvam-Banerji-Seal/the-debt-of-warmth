By the last of the afternoon they were down at \place{the Fishstairs}, where the light lay flat on the wet boards and went out on the water before anybody could keep it. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and set him where the shadow of \place{the north bridge} fell across the landing and no slate lay open. The steps went down four and four and came out on a flat place a hand's breadth out of line, where a fifth step should have stood and did not, and the whole market went over the gap without making a fall of it, the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies and is not moved.

The soft-cuffed clerk was still set at \place{the out-of-line landing}. He had been set there when the month turned and he was set there now, and nobody had taken him off, or somebody had taken him off and put him back in the dark, which came to the same thing to a boy who could only watch. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by the time the sun stood over the roofs, the colour of a cloth that has been cleaned once too often and has forgotten what it was before. He held his slate to his forearm with the writing turned inward where nobody else could read it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went up to the stalls, every time, in the same order a midwife's hand goes to the writing before it goes to the child. He did not speak, and the boy had never heard him speak, and the stillness was part of the set of him, like the slate and the cuff.

A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of something, and the boy kept that as a shape and did not make a rule of it, the way he kept the record in a carrying arm that tells what was lifted and never what it cost. Three times in as many tend-days of Reed he had watched the hand go to the slate ahead of the eyes; he had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the order of reaching and no standing to enter it, and the cuff's grey was a colour on a place of its own, not a man, not a payer, not a name. When a buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure at the eel boards the clerk's hand was already on the slate before his eyes had found the man who said it, so the figure went into the slate ahead of the face. A use wants a payer, and a payer had not been named; the boy made no cause of the order and set it beside the other orders of reaching, where they lay side by side and did not touch.

The reeds came down in armfuls and went back up in armfuls, judged by the arm that carried them and by nothing set on a slate. A woman with dry mud in the creases of her wrists asked what the cutters' hands would be paid now the reed was in its last third, and the answer came back as the weather and the river, never a figure, and she carried the unanswered question away level so nothing slopped. “Ask the river,” a cutter said, and that was the whole of the answer, a direction given where a number had been asked for, leaving no entry that any thumb could read. The boy held the question on the tray where the unanswered things lay, beside the clerk's uncalled measure, and did not lay the two together, because two accounts with no figure in them are still two accounts.

The market sold its reeds and its eels over the top of him. Bundles went down and baskets came up, eels slapped wet on the boards, a woman put marks into her palm without letting the figure reach the air, and nobody stepped around where the clerk sat and nobody lowered a voice and nobody asked what he was set there for. He sat where a stone sits in a shallow and the water goes over it and does not move it. A girl with a creel took the landing sideways, one hand out to the bridge rail, her feet going where the step was not, and went on selling without looking down and without falling. The water had put the steps back out of line in the Breaking and the district had called it weather, and a girl with a creel could cross it half asleep.

The boy watched the gap and the girl's foot go over it and thought of a bone that had been set wrong and healed wrong and was walked on all the same, with no more said about it than the weather. He knew the order of a thing out of place: the gap in the steps, the space at the row's end, the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come back, the two gaps in the shelf in the other room --- absences in four places, and he kept them on four places and did not let them stand up and call themselves one cause. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water under the bridge and came down against the fold.

One hundred and thirty-six days old, four months and sixteen days, and nobody under any roof had said so. The count sat in him exact and unspent, a record kept in a place where no Court thumb would ever read it. He could not reach the landing, the slate, the cuff, the gap in the steps or the girl's retreating creel; nothing within two paces could be reached, not the pot, not the crust in the fold, not the handle standing two inches beyond the hand that would have closed on it. Sella stood a half-pace off with the little-door burn shut on nothing and gave the instruction to the wool again, and did not look into the basket to see what he had been watching.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the going of the light was measured out in small duties. Sella set the basket down near the bench leg at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof to a thing lying low, gave the instruction to the pot and the broom and the fire and never to the basket, and banked the coals with the back of a broken pot so that one small place was left where the heat came through. Woodsmoke went before her and wet wool went with her, and under both the faint sour mineral tang of the \lore{Ash} that had lived in the corners of this house for two years and would not be swept. The clean dark line where the ridge had turned lay beside the new finer hairline of grey, and neither was moved; both were called \lore{the thaw}. The fire answered four low pulls and missed the fifth, and its payer had been unnamed for one hundred and thirty-six days.

She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the end of every instruction went to the thing itself before it went to a person --- to the fire, the pot, the broom, the wool, the river wind, and last of all, if there was anyone left to hear it, to the boy. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; a bank was not an opening, and the mark hung where it hung at a rack, a landing, a basket and a bed. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals as she turned the pot, and she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn and did not look toward the bench where the basket lay. The house kept its days badly in loaves pushed along a shelf and glances at a clay jar cold under its glaze, and no figure came out of any of it.

\person{Tarn} came in with the coals already banked and the small knife already back where it could be checked. He set it at the very edge of the sill, as he had set it every morning of the boy's life, and told the empty yard where it was as though the yard might answer. “It's back. You can check.” Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh lay at the very edge and was held, six where the house could check and seven where it could not, and nothing in the room asked him which was which. The unforged sentence about a man sent for a collector in the Long Cold and turned back at the frozen ford lay where he kept it beside the seventh year, and neither was entered. He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to the last of the light for a breath and let it go, and the light came off it and went nowhere a slate could follow. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it.

He went to the kindling stack before he took up the broom and measured it with his eyes, four and four and a space where the fifth should have stood. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the kindling, and set the knife at the very edge of its sill again with the seventh pressing nearer its end and not named. His chest was tight. The breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16 had stayed unspent through four more tend-days of Reed, and a small interval stood at the top of each reach where it should have gone in, as exact and as worth nothing as the record in the carrying arm that tells what was lifted and not what it cost. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and held it a moment the way a man holds a tool that has already been named.

The boy watched the knife go to the edge of the sill and come back, and read the worn mark where the thumb had worn it as a record of the same kind as the carrying arm --- six counts deep and the seventh not cut into it, a thing that told how often a hand had passed and not what the passing cost. There was no Grathic word for a mark that kept a count and refused a name, and he did not supply one. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with the handle toward the hand that would reach for it in the dark, six in the worn mark and the seventh held.

Then the sentence came. It came the way it had come on Reed 5 and Reed 8 and Reed 11 and Reed 14, up out of the clay-bound hand and through the dark and into the room where the boy lay, and it went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there. “An ear under this roof's worth more than the debt that holds it, and---” The word that would have called the loan did not come. No figure came with it and no name, no slate at the watch and no man at the door, only the loan that could hear going on being worth more than the debt that held him, and not called. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom. The stroke went out over the flagstones and past the shallow bright patch the stones kept, and went on without the half-check, and the place where it had stopped stayed where it had stopped, a patch of light nobody swept around and nobody named.

Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. The end of it arrived at the pot, which had not been asked and did not answer, and the house went on around the stopped sentence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted and set back. \person{Jorra Vail} stood at the edge of the light with grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger, and he did not ask about the ear. Tarn did not ask about \place{the back room}. The two not-askings stayed under one roof without a finger laid between them, and the men went on with the work as though nothing had stopped anywhere. The door off the shed kept its finger's width of open, and the light through it lay on the stones like a thread nobody took up.

The boy lay with the fold at his ear and counted what had stopped. He had learned to tell the difference between a thing that had happened and a thing that had been kept from happening, and the sentence that had come to the word and stopped was the second kind, an object on the interval tray and not a hearing, not a payment, not a seventh. He had kept the first four stoppings on that tray without letting them become a rule, and now there was a fifth. Five times the same sentence had come to the same word and stopped in the same place: Reed 5, Reed 8, Reed 11, Reed 14, Reed 17. Five likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if no hand holds them apart.

He held them apart. A doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer had been named to any of the five, no figure entered, no loan called; the ear was still worth more than the debt that held it and the debt still held it, and nobody under the roof had spoken the word that would make the two agree. He laid the fifth stopping on the interval tray beside the first four and did not lay it across them, the way a man lays an instrument on a towel and leaves the others where they lie, knowing each by the wear on its handle and refusing to mistake five handles for one tool. It was a fifth interval and not a fifth payment, an object among objects.

The tray was not a rule, the rule was not a hearing, the hearing was not a seventh; five stoppings were five objects and not a doctrine with a payer at the end of it. He had learned what five likenesses could do in a better-lit room than this one, where a man who wanted an answer had let three stand up together and called the shape of them a cause; here there were five and he kept them lying down. He had kept three near-full answers on their own tray, advanced and not paid, and knew the difference between an answer that had come almost all the way and one that had come and stopped; the stopped sentence had come all the way to the word and stopped there, which was a different thing again. The loan that could hear was still uncalled. The seventh year had pressed nearer its end and had not given, and something must give at the end of the seventh year and had not given here.

Through the floor under blind ash the carried fire gave four low pulls and missed the fifth, its payer unnamed for one hundred and thirty-six days, nobody saying where the warmth came from or who had paid for it. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among \lore{the full note}, which held six hearings from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among, with no seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and no finger of his to lay them together, and the boy did not invent one to fill the empty place.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and thirty-six days, four months and sixteen days, exact and unspent and told by nobody. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. On the tray the fifth stopping lay on its own place beside the four, a fifth interval and not a fifth payment, an object among objects; the loan that could hear was uncalled, and the seventh year had not given.
