# Chapter 32 — beat sheet: *Five at the Teeth*

**Days covered.** Days 135–137, Reed 16–18, Y.H. 813, Sixthday to Eighthday. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived in full, at least eight hundred words to the day. Day 134 is finished and not revisited; Day 138 belongs to Chapter 33. The boy is one hundred and thirty-five, then thirty-six, then thirty-seven days old — four months and fifteen, sixteen and seventeen days — and nobody under the roof says so. The reed month goes into its last third; the second Account-day of Reed (Reed 20) is four days off at the chapter's close.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof Tarn brings the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt that holds it **a fifth time** all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there, with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray beside the first four as a **fifth interval and not a fifth payment**, an object among objects and not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh, refusing to let five likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine — for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year presses nearer its end and has still not given. A second gathering, not a resolution: the reed month's last third, with the arm that carries the bundles keeping a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost.

**What is withheld everywhere.** No calling of the loan, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no knot counted, no word forty spoken, no name or number from Dessa, no sale, no opening of tin or box, no body or cause for the missing hand, no reading of either private tally, no joining of the two empty places, no fifth green at a mouth, no Fenna named in narration. T097 stays advanced and unpaid; T098, T099 and T100 stay carried and unpaid. The old life stays offstage: no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from the boy's eyes, no Court, collector, Waiting Room, back-room interior, Ulda on stage, Marra in Sill, watcher on stage, present-tense break or ending.

## Scene 1 — *The Last Third*

- **Day and time.** Day 135, Reed 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard and the reed beds below Low Kiln; the edge of the worked strips east of the yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; reed-cutters going downriver and coming back; a woman at the racks; yard voices. Walk-ons only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Open on the morning of Day 135 with the reed month in its last third and the boy one hundred and thirty-five days old and four months and fifteen days. Sella carries him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket.
  2. The reeds stand grey in the ruts with nobody counting them; the bundles come in armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter's line — *Your arm'll tell you what went into it, and that's the whole of what it knows* — is the day's measure (T071/T072, T077).
  3. A woman working the racks asks what the cutters' hands will be paid now the reed is in its last third; she is answered with the weather and the river, never a figure, and carries the unanswered question away (T077, T058/T079).
  4. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look, a wide step, no number (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried, not paid).
  5. The boy reads the carrying arm as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost: exact, complete and worth nothing. The surgeon's register notes the order of reaching without giving him standing to enter it (T061/T082).
  6. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down against the fold; he cannot reach the bundle, the pot or any of the places gone around.
- **Emotional turn.** From the ordinary work of the last third to the exactness of a record that cannot be spent: competence here is helplessness, and the day's arithmetic stays in arms and weather where no Court thumb will read it.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet wool and woodsmoke going before Sella; grey reed ends pale where frost bit the tips; dry mud worked into wrists; river wind at the ear; the small wet sound of the wandering head.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (135 kept exact and unspent), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T060/T086, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T098 gathers interest with another going-around, no name, body, cause or figure; T077's cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The woman at the racks goes back to her strips with the weather in her mouth; the reeds stand grey in the ruts uncounted; the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down.

## Scene 2 — *The Jar and the Board*

- **Day and time.** Day 135 continued, midday into the afternoon.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, the boy in the basket; a neighbour come up from the strips with a wet pot. Walk-on only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket low and a little back near the bench leg, at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board a man standing sees only the top of; she gives the instruction to the wool, the pot, the broom and the fire and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  2. Above, the shelf keeps its row the way a house keeps its days badly: loaves pushed along, a glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word — in a room the fold does not touch (T060/T086, T097 carried and unpaid).
  3. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, she glances once at the clay jar and gives no figure.
  4. Still without looking down at the board and without a word, she reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies — the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again, the finger going under far enough for the knuckle to set and the thumb to come up against the grain — holds it a breath out and back and brings it away, and does not go back to the shelf (T100 carried, not paid, unread).
  5. From the low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, press a thumb where her thumb had been, lay the underside beside the two gaps above or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or make a cause of the habit — such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason is left where it stands, between the two (T100, T097).
  6. A neighbour come up from the strips with dry mud in her wrists and water standing in the creases asks what the row will want before the tend is out; Sella sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and the woman carries the unanswered question away level (T077, T058/T079).
  7. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs and is not touched, because a shelf and an afternoon are not openings; the clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and are called the thaw.
- **Emotional turn.** A second private record is handled in front of him and stays unread — care that hides itself tells more by the hiding than the writing would — and the boy refuses to be the hand that joins it to anything.
- **Sensory anchors.** The dry warm smell of the shelf crossed for one breath by the sharper smell of charcoal from under the board; the cold iron of the bench bracket at his cheek; the grey streak pale off the coals; the little-door burn closing on nothing.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (carried), T060/T086, T097, T058/T079, T077, T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T100 gathers interest toward the far-off convergence of the district's private records; the finger-under-board habit stays unexplained, held for a hand not his. Nothing read, counted, copied, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The shelf keeps its two gaps above and its charcoal marks below, the finger has come away without a word, and the two rooms of the house do not touch.

## Scene 3 — *Dust Ahead of the Sentence*

- **Day and time.** Day 136, Reed 17, Seventhday, Y.H. 813, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; Jorra Vail; Tarn; three or four unnamed men who speak low in the back room; a High Kiln kiln-master. Walk-ons except Jorra, Tarn and Sella.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey three or four men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and go low-voiced into the room off the shed, whose door keeps its finger's width of open; Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, turns no head toward the door, gives the house no name for the men and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031).
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate (T035/T031).
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who says it is singing before its time and would make something of it (T062).
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence and the end of her instruction arrives at the pot (T062).
  5. The boy reads the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object joined to no kiln, worth nothing. The two tellings of the shut counting room stand beside it with no third (T062, T065/T095).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed, says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late and goes on wanting the late because it puts off the paying; his seventh year is pressed nearer its end and still held, the small knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the unforged collector sentence beside it, the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach (T036/T076).
  7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check while the stones keep the shallow bright patch where it stopped, neither man naming the other's silence.
- **Emotional turn.** The household's equality holds at the place it moved to by fractions: an iron that is both mercy and mutilation, a dust that names nothing, a year the debt cannot bring himself to count, and two silences that are not joined.
- **Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal worked past the bent finger and under the nails; bone-coloured slip on clean forearms; cold iron of the damper; wet clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on the kiln-master's coat; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, dust, broken birth-slates unentered), T062 (damper's double bite filed as an object), T065/T095 (two tellings, no third), T036/T076 (seventh year held, breath unspent, unchecking stroke), T058/T079, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T035 points at the Broken Slates without a name given; T062 keeps the double bite for the far-off day it is understood rather than merely read; T065/T095 carry the two shut-room tellings with no third telling owed. Nothing paid, named, entered or joined.
- **Exact last image.** Tarn's stroke goes on over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it stopped, and the door off the shed standing a finger's width open.

## Scene 4 — *The Fifth Stopping*

- **Day and time.** Day 136 continued, late afternoon at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge, then the going of the light into the small hours at the hearth and kindling under the Vail roof.
- **Place.** The Fishstairs landing below the north bridge; the hearth and kindling stack under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, the boy in the basket; the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing; market goers. Walk-ons except the three household figures.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, his cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his slate held to his forearm with the writing turned where nobody else can read it, his hand going to the slate before his eyes go to the stalls (T090/T080/T083).
  2. The market sells its reeds and eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies; nobody steps around him and nobody asks what he is set there for, and a girl with a creel takes the landing sideways without a fall (T083).
  3. Under the roof, with the coals banked and the small knife set back where it could be checked, Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and sets the knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot.
  4. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes **a fifth time** all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there with no figure and no name. He draws the breath that should go in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom (T036/T076).
  5. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra, within hearing, does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room, and the two silences stay unjoined.
  6. The boy files the fifth stopping on the interval tray beside the first four as a **fifth interval and not a fifth payment** — an object among objects, not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh. He refuses to let five likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer (T036/T076, T061/T082).
  7. The carried fire keeps its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-six days (T088). The loan stays uncalled and the seventh year has still not given.
- **Emotional turn.** A thing stopped five times is still a held thing, not a settled one: the chapter's one change is a further interval, bought by a breath a man does not spend and recorded by a child who cannot spend it either.
- **Sensory anchors.** Banked coals under blind ash; tallow and wet wool; the small knife's worn thumb-mark; the pale clip-ring at Tarn's left wrist holding what light there is; the small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (fifth stopping withheld, loan uncalled, seventh year held), T061/T082 (fifth interval as object not payment), T090/T080/T083 (clerk still set for an uncalled measure), T088 (payer unnamed one day further), T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T036 advances the loan another interval toward its far calling: something must give at the end of the seventh year and has not given here. T090's measure set down ahead of something stays ahead of it. Nothing called, paid, named or ended; no seventh hearing.
- **Exact last image.** The boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold with the count at one hundred and thirty-six, the fifth stopping lying on the tray as an object and the loan uncalled.

## Scene 5 — *The Knot Inside the Coat*

- **Day and time.** Day 137, Reed 18, Eighthday, Y.H. 813, morning.
- **Place.** The Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards at the shade.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the reed-cutter with the cord; Orra Weir; Mira Silt; a weir woman who asks what the cord holds; a buyer who names a figure. Walk-ons except Orra and Mira.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed work goes on in armfuls judged by the arm that carries them as the month runs into its last days; the reeds come downriver and go back at dusk and the cost is never a figure (T071/T072, T077).
  2. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle and the short length of grey wool or reed-fibre cord inside his coat, gone grey with the oil of a working thumb the way a stitch-hole goes grey after a thread has been drawn through it a dozen times.
  3. He sets the bundle on the flat blessing stone, straightens the cord on his thumb, turns it once so the knots stand in the small light, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts the cord back inside his coat, drawing the coat closed over it (T099 carried, not paid).
  4. To the weir woman who asks what the cord holds he says *You can ask the cord*; to *I can't read a cord*, *No more can the Court*. No figure, knot, name or joining; he goes off to lift a trap with the question going down into the water.
  5. Orra stands at the lower boards with her board on its side, hands like old rope and the pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, and pulls one knot down with her thumb, setting it among the others. To the woman who asks what the levy will take she gives a date — the ninth of Ilmen, the collector's boat round the bend on the flood with six oars and one man at the stern who never touched them — and says *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried* (T024).
  6. The last knot on her cord shows its double turn, tail tucked, tied left over right with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in; the word that would name it stays unspoken, and the boy reads the shape without the name (T024/T059/T025).
  7. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in grey strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been. Asked whether the run will be thin now the hands are in the reeds she answers her own answer — *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* — flicks a string of eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, and when a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's and not touching (T059/T025/T024).
- **Emotional turn.** Three private records in materials no Court thumb can spell stand side by side and not across: the cord in the coat, the knot-string on the cord at Orra's waist, the second twist beneath Mira's apron — each kept by a hand that will not let a figure be entered where it can be read.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey cord oiled by a working thumb; river silt and cold fish and tallow smoke; salt dried white at the wrists; the nasal half-song of the Fen in Orra's voice when she is tired; eel-bones flicked against a thumb.
- **Threads advanced.** T099 (carried, unread), T024/T059/T025 (both cords and the knot-string, neither knot counted), T071/T072 (137 kept exact on the reed work), T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T099 and T024/T059/T025 gather toward the far-off day the district's private records are laid together and a figure is at last given; the word forty is watched and unread. Nothing counted, named, joined or paid; no word forty.
- **Exact last image.** The reed-cutter draws his coat closed over the cord; Orra's last knot keeps its legible double turn with its name unspoken; Mira's thumb stays under her apron and her board lies beside Orra's without touching.

## Scene 6 — *Two Marks Short and a Dark Foot*

- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Berr comes up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, hot clay over the smell of nothing at all (T015/T041/T047).
  2. His large clean hands wipe the thumb twice on the apron before touching nothing; he holds a narrow slate unopened to his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required.
  3. At the eel stall he takes two pair and pays two marks short, the stall-keeper putting the marks in her palm without counting them aloud; he says *A number goes into a slate, not a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch* — *your house*, never *I* — taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and goes off toward High Kiln with the cord folded square and the box in his house still locked with the true subtraction inside (T015/T041/T047).
  4. The green stays at his own mouth on a place of its own. No fifth green at a silent woman's mouth is glimpsed this chapter; none of the colours is named, given a face or joined to an ear or to the pale line on a hand, and none of the women is Fenna in the narration (T050/T051 held, not gathered).
  5. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard; the surgeon reads the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming he cannot perform, and the reading works perfectly and buys nothing.   6. Mira answers a question about the run with her own answer, moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie and shows the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's and not touching it; the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the landing for a measure nobody called (T059/T025/T024, T090/T080).

- **Sensory anchors.** Green glaze at a mouth corner and hot clay; a thumb wiped twice on an apron before it touches nothing; pale eel slime dried in long grey strokes; wet wool in three turns around a dark foot; river wind coming up to meet the woodsmoke.
- **Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047 (folded cord, twice-wiped thumb, two marks short, self-removal with *your house*, true subtraction in the locked box), T093/T094/T096 (cold foot, uncured and unnamed), T059/T025/T024, T090/T080, T050/T051 (held, not gathered).

- **Exact last image.** Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool while the yard calls the water weather, and the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down without reaching it.

## Scene 7 — *One Hundred and Thirty-Seven*

- **Day and time.** Day 137 continued, the going of the light and the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof.

  1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, going around a pan set out to dry and a trough with a skin of ice, and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket.
