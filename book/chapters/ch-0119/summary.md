## Chronicle

Three days came under the Vail roof — Ilmen 14, 15 and 16 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: crust two inches off and gone leathery in the dry cold, fold found where put, thread stiff and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither won. He kept four hundred and eight, nine, then ten exact, told by nobody, and stood no number up as a threshold. The year's first arithmetic lay up the clay road in Berr's locked box with no key under any roof; one reading, not an account, the figure read still not the figure paid.

Sella came to the hearth before the basket at both ends of all three days, told the coals it was the thaw, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing between staple and wire, gave both proverbs to pot and fire and never to a person, turned the dressed grain under wool unfigured, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. She carried him down and up the nineteen steps, went wide around the seven walked-around places, and righted the fold. At the kindling Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, passing his hand over blade and sill and away: *Six counted. The next I don't count*. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, withheld the pot's full note, and took his finger from the cold damper without the extra holding of Ash 26.

On the fourteenth the man from Slate Row stood again at the yard's edge, his face filed and not counted. On the fifteenth word travelled the yard that the soft-cuffed clerk below the north bridge had entered a figure on his inward slate before he looked up to find the man who gave it: hand to the slate, eye to the person after, the half-beat in the gap. Nobody saw the hand or the slate; no man stood under the figure. The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes a field and filed it on a place of its own, a finger between it and Tarn's degree and another between it and the face at the yard's edge — the next reaching toward the loan that can hear, and not the calling of it.

Answers 391 through 396 were filed on the sixth place, six honest answers and not one a doctrine. The fire's fifth was unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number of days. In the small hours the hand laid itself flat against the fold on answer 396. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept.
