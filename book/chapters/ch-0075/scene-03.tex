\daymark{277}{Frost 8, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

Frost 8 came on its own morning, earned by the light that stood at the finger's-width door, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. There was no market and no \place{the Cross} and no bell on an Eighthday; the square where the bell had struck on the last market-day of Smoke kept its four, four and a gap to itself, and the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} stayed where it had always stayed, with nobody crossing himself and no fourth sound pushed into the place that stood empty. Frost 10 stood two mornings ahead and was not brought early into this one. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the small hours smelled as they had smelled all week: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}. Rime lay heavier on the step than it had laid on the morning before. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and carried and silent, and nobody under the roof said that a number had been reached.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-seven days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now he could not close two inches on a pot handle.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-seven days, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either; there was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it struck, beside the told bell, not brought under this roof on an Eighthday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket and gave the coals their instruction, telling them it was the thaw, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. She said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She carried at the half-pace from every open slate, and stood the pot rim with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. At the shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so that the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, uncopied and unfigured, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it at a morning, a hearth or a rack, because none of these was an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket, which was a keeping and not a conversation.

The dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from \place{the hearth}, and was turned once beneath the wool for damp with a lighter touch and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it on Frost 10 did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there. Smoke's third arithmetic sat locked in a thane's box up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln}, unread under this roof; the printed figure stood where it had been printed, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. The loan against a firing of tiles stood where it stood, uncalled and worse for being uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road on an Eighthday. Two hundred and seventy-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sill and Grath; six months and ninety-seven days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed.

\scenebreak

There was a woman's low counting in the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door and came down on the next low note with no more than the small intake held. It laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

When she carried him out onto \place{the Clay Yard} the rime had not gone. The light came up over the terrace and found the step still white, and the yard went about its work with a look and a wide step at the places it went around, giving none of them a number. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say where it had been; \place{the trough} by the well; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The rope at \place{the yard well} had stiffened in the cold until it would not lie against the stone the way a rope lies when it has been wet and warmed and used, and a woman drawing made two pulls of one pull and put the pail down against her knee. Nobody gave the rope a name.

But the white was still on the lip of the well.

Frost had written it there in fronds, the way it had written on Frost 4 in the small hours and gone grey under the bucket and gone altogether by noon, so that the yard called it weather and went on drawing. On this morning the sun came up and the fronds did not go. A bucket knocked the edge of one into grey meal and the rest stood, fine and white and stacked like the little branches of a burnt tree, on the north lip where the Warm had lately gone out of the stone. A woman looked at it and said \emph{Weather}, and went on with her pail. Another said \emph{It'll go}, and did not say when. Nobody put a figure on it, and nobody asked what it would take to take it off. The boy kept the stayed white on a place of its own, and the yard's word for it on another, with a finger between, and wrote no cause across either.

Beside the well the trough had taken a skin at its edges, thin as the film that forms at the rim of a pot set off the coals and left to stand. The water under it kept the yard's name of weather. The yard went around the trough with a look and a wide step and gave the skin no name at all.

He set the white that stayed beside the white that had gone by noon on Frost 4, and beside that the sweet edge of lead glaze that a wrong wind had brought off High Kiln on Frost 2, touched the fold of the grain and went out on the wind that brought it --- a direction arrived nowhere. Three objects on three places with a finger between each pair. He wrote no cause across any two of them, for a joining would want a payer put under both and none stood; the white that stayed had not made the white that went, and the sweet edge on the wrong wind had not made either, and the cold had no hand to lay anything anywhere. The surgeon in him noted the surface laid over a depth, the fronds growing where the Warm had lately gone out, with the exactness of a man who reads a wound before he reads the story of it. The boy wanted the crust lifted two inches. The noting went on its own place and bought nothing, and the wanting was louder.

\scenebreak

At noon the white was still on the lip. In the afternoon it was still there, finer in the thin light than it had been in the dark, and the yard still called it weather. Nothing was made of it: no sign, no lesson, no mending, no cause and no payer, and no figure put to the drawing of a bucket through it. The nearness of Frost 10 did not pay it, for Frost 10 stood two mornings ahead with its reading and its market and its visits, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The household's work shifted by degrees and said nothing: the rope stiffened in the hand, the trough took its skin, and at the going of the light the coals would stand banked an hour earlier than on the days before, with nobody giving the hour a name. The boy set each shift on its own place and entered none of them.

The finding that a house keeps itself by \lore{not-doings} had a place of its own already, and his own kept gaps had another, and he set a finger between them. He did not lay the finding across the stayed frost, or across the charcoal he had not read beneath the shelf, or across the fifth pull missing under the floor, or across the loan uncalled in the ruts, or across the one crust in the fold with its empty place beside it. A joining would want a payer and none stood, and he had learned to leave the finger where it was. The not-saying was itself a keeping, and the keeping was not a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of it.

In the thinning afternoon the woman's counting came again through the floor that does not touch his: the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. The hand went up an inch toward the river's note and held one intake, and held the next intake after it, with a finger between the two and the letting-go of the first left where it had been, neither intake made to agree with the missing fifth under the floor and neither borrowed from the house. It came down on the river's next low note. One hundred and twenty-nine was filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-eight, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon felt the clean small correctness of two things held apart and a stroke set down at the right point, and the boy wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth; both were true, and the wanting was louder, and the checking was the work, done with neither hand. He made nothing of the answer and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything.

In the afternoon light the white was still on the stone lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and the hand came down on the river's note with two intakes still held apart on the sixth place. It laid back against the fold as a tool put away where the next hand will find it without looking. One crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread stayed frozen at the mouth, cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The file lay open on \place{the towel} with every gap left gap, every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Eighthday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and the frost going on toward its ninth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing.
