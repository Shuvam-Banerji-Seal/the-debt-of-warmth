The afternoon went out of the day the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals: not all at once and with no line drawn under the going, but by degrees, drawing back from the rim and sitting down behind the pot where it had always sat. By the time \person[Corin]{the boy} was carried out onto \place{the Clay Yard} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the light had thinned to its own warmth and kept it without announcement, the way a house keeps a fire it has fed and stopped feeding. Heat drew back out of the fold at his ear and went to sit behind the pot. The yard gave it back to the air a little at a time, so that there was no hour a man could put a finger on and say the afternoon ended there.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-six days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone; now he could not close two inches on a pot handle.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate, and in the yard she went around seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, the way a woman carries a full pail past a child who might knock it. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline called \lore{the thaw} beside it, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring on the ground where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say where it had been; \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, its water called weather by everybody who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool at \place{the rack end}. The rope at the well had stiffened in the cold and the trough had taken a skin at its edges; the yard went around both with a look and gave neither a name, and the boy set them on places of their own and wrote no cause across either, for a joining would want a payer and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it at a well, a rack, a shelf, a basket, an afternoon or a going of the light, because none of these was an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at the rack end, away from \place{the hearth}, and had been turned once beneath the wool for damp in the first of the light with a lighter touch and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it on Frost 10 did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there. Smoke's third arithmetic sat locked in a thane's box up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln}, unread under this roof; the printed figure stood where it had been printed, and the figure read was still not the figure paid.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story, his name unspoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-six days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder held a broader man's shape on its peg; the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, for a debt a man can go around every day of his life and never be the nearer done with. No collector had come up the clay road on Frost 4, 5 or 6, and none came on this Seventhday; the loan stood in the ruts.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying. The small knife lay at the very lip of its sill with six counted in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the edge where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have been, and his chest was as tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped. From above came the small dry scrape of a pot's foot on stone, a sound with a direction; the warm stone in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft} stayed cold and unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiveness.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the wheel-shed with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. The \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands stood on the near-full tray with air under it and a want in it, known and withheld, advanced and not paid; no pot was lost and no likeness of it was made a fourth cause. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, moving the tongue a hair's breadth and leaving it standing so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence; the boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The cold of the iron took the warmth out of a fingertip and the finger stayed where it was until the sentence finished. Three or more men went low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and got no name from the house; to the boy it was a direction and a sound of low voices, and he followed neither. The burned-slate word came from \place{Slate Row} as a direction over \place{the boards}, told four times and not a fifth; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the three sayings about a pot kept the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place with no fourth likeness made a cause. The row question stood at five askings with one further shaping beside them and no witness anywhere to be asked and no row named, and no sixth asking came over the boards on this Seventhday, the silence a keeping and not a sixth witness. The promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed.

\scenebreak

A woman come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool and the visits were coming. Sella sent her to the pot and the weather. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, she said, and the woman went off level with her question still on her, nothing priced, refused or entered. The visits were left as visits, with no date put to them and no market named, for Frost 10 stood three mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The boy read the yard's ordinary work as a whole system of refusals: a fist that let a number go toward the well and took it back into a sleeve, a row with two gaps nobody would figure, a trough whose water was weather, a hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and was carried without a name. Nothing was priced, nothing was named, nothing was called; the yard kept itself whole by not-doings. His own exactness was the same kind of keeping in a body that could not act on what it kept, and he set it on a place of its own beside his gaps with a finger between and did not lay it across the yard's not-doings, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. No grievance stood up out of it.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay; she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, uncopied and unfigured, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. No instruction went at any hour to the basket, which was a keeping and not a conversation; instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-six days, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either; there was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on the last market-day of Smoke, stayed in the square where it struck beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not brought under this roof on a Seventhday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Two hundred and seventy-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, Sill and \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-six days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed.

\scenebreak

There was a woman's low counting in the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the hold of one of those counted breaths and came down on the river's next low note while that count was still in the letting-go, leaving the letting-go and the little silence after it whole and outside the holding. It was an ending made early, kept as an object and not a freedom, because a freedom wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything; it borrowed no house rhythm, supplied no fifth pull to the kiln and crossed no silence that did not belong to the river. One hundred and twenty-eight was filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-seven, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an early ending and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon felt the clean small correctness of a thing closed at the right point, and the boy wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth; both were true, and the wanting was louder, and the checking was the work, done with neither hand. He made nothing of the answer and would not let it stand up as a gift.

The woman with the pot went off level down the yard with her question still on her and nobody turning after her, and the hand came down early on the river's note with the letting-go left where it lay. It laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The river's low note went quiet after it, the way a pot goes quiet after the knuckle has found it and put it down. One crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third to make the row come out even; the thread stayed frozen at the mouth, cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the heard bell stood beside the told and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Seventhday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and Frost going on toward its eighth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing.
