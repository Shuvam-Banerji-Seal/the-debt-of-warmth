## Chronicle

For three days the frost held where the yard had always said it would go. It came on Frost 7 with rime on the step and wrote white upon the well's north lip; on Frost 8 the sun rose and the fronds did not go, and the women drew pails through them and called it weather, and by Frost 9 the trough had skinned from edge to edge. Sella Vail went to it with the broken pot she used to turn the coals, pressed it to the skin and lifted a plate of ice clouded with silt; she set it on a flat stone out of the path of feet, drew what the yard called weather, and told it where to stand. She gave nobody a figure. The rope stiffened, the coals were banked an hour earlier, and the dressed grain was turned a second time beneath the wool because the stone had gone cold; none was entered, and the arithmetic that would read over the grain on the morrow did not know what it cost.

Through those days the boy under the wool took the body in its old order — one crust two inches off, the thread frozen and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on a pot handle; both were true, and the wanting was louder. Sella went to the fire before the basket, touched the widow's mark only before the kiln, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would say “I,” gave neither gap of the row a figure, stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and never instructed the basket. Ilva came for water and kept her count in her sleeve, answering “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account”; he did not read the notches and filed no tenth hand. Tarn held the seventh cut at the lip and stopped short of calling the loan. Jorra kept dusty and clean forearms apart and withheld his knowing. Garl stayed in objects, his name unspoken, the tile debt worse for being uncalled.

The hand went up an inch six times and came down six times on the river's note — an interval carried, an ending made early, two intakes held apart, a bridge between two breaths, a held interval beside an empty one — and each was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, not a reach, not a hearing, not a gift. At the close two hundred and seventy-eight stood where nobody had put it, six months and ninety-eight days told by nobody, the fire's fifth unpaid and its payer unnamed. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, the frost white on the well lip called weather, and the plate of ice where she had set it.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** the boy lies on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail roof, awake, carried, silent; the hand has just laid back against the fold on the **one hundred and thirty-second** answer as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Present in the house: only the boy (the narration names nobody else on stage in the final small hours; Sella has come once more to the fire, given it its instruction and gone away without looking toward the bed). The towel lies at the head of the bed with the file turned out on it, every gap left gap, every tray parted. Beyond the finger's-width door the frost lies white on the well lip where the yard left it, and the plate of ice stands on its flat stone out of the path of feet. The river's low note goes quiet after the hand comes down.
- **Day and time at the end:** Day 278, Frost 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours. - **The chapter's one meaningful change, advanced and not paid (T083/T085):** the white fronds frost wrote on the well lip on Frost 8 did not go when the sun was up as they had gone on Frost 4, and stayed past noon, past the going of the light on Frost 8, and onto Frost 9. The rope stiffened in the hand, the trough skinned over from edge to edge, and the coals were banked an hour earlier at the going of the light. On Frost 9 Sella broke the skin with the back of the broken pot and lifted a plate of ice, setting it on a flat stone out of the path of feet. The yard still calls all of it weather; no figure, sign, lesson, cause, mending or payer was made of it, and the nearness of Frost 10 did not pay it.
