\daymark{276}{Frost 7, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Outside the wool the small hours went on at their own pace under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} in \place{Low Kiln}, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay carried, awake and silent, with the hundred and twenty-sixth answer put down behind him the way a pot is set on a shelf where nobody will knock it. Two hundred and seventy-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-five days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The hand had gone up an inch toward the river's note, taken the small intake and the hold of one of the counted breaths in the next room, stayed past its letting-go and through the little silence after it, set the held interval and the empty interval side by side with a finger between, and come down on the river's next low note with no house count borrowed and no fifth supplied. That was done. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the lean quarter of its warmth stayed where it was with nobody to lift the floor and nobody to supply the pull.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. Once, in a room with no window and a lamp held in somebody's teeth, he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone. Here he could not close two inches on a pot handle, and the space between the two facts was not a fall from one life into another but a distance measured in air, entered nowhere and felt with an exactness that had no use in it.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the fire answered in fours and withheld its fifth, not a bell; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on the last market-day of Smoke, stayed in the square where it struck beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not brought under this roof on a Seventhday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\scenebreak

Frost 7 came on in its own light, earned by that morning and not brought into a day that had not earned it. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Seventhday, and the last third of the working week began the way a pot begins on a banked fire, by degrees and without announcement. The small hours smelled of hot clay cooled under tallow, of wet wool, and under both of the sour breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}; rime lay thin on the step, and the river went low over lower under the door. Frost 10, the month's first Account-day and market, stood three mornings ahead and was not brought early into this one.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket and gave no instruction to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and the pot's foot gave its small flat sound on stone and went out into the room's ordinary noise with no lesson made of it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, uncopied and unfigured, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. At \place{the hearth} the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread where it lay. Instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the thing carried in the basket, which was a keeping and not a conversation.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it. On Frost 2, before the kiln, she had put her hand to it for one breath and let go, and lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot; the fire had taken, and nobody had praised the opening or named what it mended, and no slate had been entered. At a morning and a hearth the mark hung where it hung, for neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. It had not been touched at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a yard, an afternoon, a dusk or a going of the light on Frost 4, 5 or 6, and it would not be touched at any of them on Frost 7. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, no sign, no mending anybody would name and no payer. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule, for a rule wants a payer standing under it; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for. The boy did not look at the mark again, and did not let four become a fifth by wanting one.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth, and had been turned once beneath the wool for damp in the first of the light with a lighter touch and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it on Frost 10 did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there. Smoke's third arithmetic sat locked in a thane's box up \place{the clay road}, unread under this roof; the printed figure stood where it had been printed, and the figure read was still not the figure paid.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story, his name unspoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-five days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella would let no one fill it; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and was said now to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. A debt uncalled can be gone around every day of a man's life and never be nearer done, which is the cruellest arithmetic in Low Kiln and the one nobody there will enter. No collector had come up the clay road on Frost 4, 5 or 6 and none had gone down it, and none came on this Seventhday; the loan stood in the ruts where it had stood.

Two hundred and seventy-five had stood exact at the going of the dark, six months and ninety-five days told by nobody; two hundred and seventy-six stood exact now, and six months and ninety-six days were told by nobody in Sarn, Sill and Grath. The count crossed the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam, and the fire's fifth was unpaid two hundred and seventy-six days with its payer unnamed. Nothing had been given a name at the crossing and nothing had been spent.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the morning went on doing what it had always done, and the boy lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in the room whose floor does not touch the room of the fire, carried and told by nobody what any of it counted. Sella had gone to the fire before the basket and come back to the pot; the grain lay under wool and the fire missed its fifth and the mark hung where it hung. None of it stood up and called itself a lesson. The finding that a house keeps itself whole by its not-doings stayed on a place of its own with the boy's kept gaps on another and a finger between, and he did not lay the finding across the coat, the uncalled loan, the unread charcoal, the missing fifth or the crust in the fold, for to lay any two together would want a payer and there was no payer under any of them. There was a woman's low counting in the next room --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go, and the little silence after it --- a sound with a direction and no name, and he took from it only what the hand could take and left the rest where it was. The body offered him two inches of air and a crust he could not lift; the house offered him nothing at all and kept itself whole without entering what it did. Between the two there was the one thing he could do, and it was small enough to be honest.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the small intake of the river's own going --- the low note drawing on the lower one the way a woman draws breath before a long spell at the washtub --- and carried it across the little silence after one of the counted breaths in the next room. It borrowed no house count to make the crossing and supplied no fifth pull to \lore{the carried fire} under the floor; it crossed no silence that did not belong to the river and took nothing from the row with two gaps on the shelf, or from the charcoal beneath it, or from the covered grain at the rack end. It was one house rhythm carried across the river's interval, and the river's interval stayed the river's, and the hand came down on the river's next low note with the woman's letting-go left where it had been, outside the holding and entered nowhere.

One hundred and twenty-seven was filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-six, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon in him felt the clean small satisfaction of a thing done in exact order --- field, interval, endpoint, no instrument lost --- and the boy under the wool wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and the wanting was louder, and the checking of the satisfaction was the work, done with neither hand. He did not call the checking a virtue, because naming it would have made a claim and there was nobody under this roof to take one. He made nothing of the answer and would not let it stand up as a gift; a gift wants a taker and nobody had taken anything.

The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The river's low note went quiet after it, the way a pot goes quiet after the knuckle has found it and put it down, and the crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. The thread stayed frozen at the mouth; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. Two hundred and seventy-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and ninety-six days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The file lay open with every gap left gap, every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Seventhday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and Frost going on toward its eighth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing.
