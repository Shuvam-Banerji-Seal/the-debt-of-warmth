\daymark{278}{Frost 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

Frost 9 came on its own morning and had not been brought into the day that went before it. The light earned it, thin and white over the sill, and it was a Ninthday, with no market at the Cross, no bell and no reading. Frost 10 stood one morning ahead with its account and its visits and was not brought early into a day that had not earned it, and no figure from the reading still to come reached this morning or any hour of it. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the count carried across from the night gone before it exact, complete and spoken by nobody: two hundred and seventy-eight days, six months and ninety-eight days told by nobody. \person[Corin]{The boy} had been told neither. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-eight days, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-eight days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The heard bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of the full note.

\person{Sella Vail} had come to the fire before the basket, as she came to it every morning, and gave the instruction to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket, which was a keeping and not a conversation. The rime lay heavier on the step than on the two mornings gone, and the river ran low over lower under the finger's-width door. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She stood the pot rim with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, leaving the thread frozen where it lay. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, and her hand did not go to it at a morning, a hearth, a rack or a basket, because none of these was an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the stayed frost had gone on doing what the yard called weather. Beside \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} had skinned over in the two days of it, a film laid from edge to edge as thin as the skin that settles on a pot set off the coals and left to stand. The water under the skin kept the yard's name. The women went around the trough with a look and a wide step and put no figure on the drawing of a bucket through it, and the boy read a vessel with a covering laid over a depth and set the reading on a place of its own beside the stayed white on the lip and the white fronds the frost had written on Frost 4, with a finger between each pair. He wrote no cause across any two. The reading bought nothing, and the wanting was louder.

Then \person{Sella Vail} went to the trough with the back of the broken pot in her hand --- the same pot she used to turn the coals --- and gave the skin its instruction the way she gave one to the fire. She set the flat of the pot against the edge and pressed. The skin gave with a thin, dry sound, no heavier than a crust breaking, and a hairline ran out from under the pot and crossed the water. She lifted the edge with the pot's back and brought up a plate of ice, clear at the rim and clouded where the silt had risen into it, and held it a moment with the water draining back off it in strings. She set the plate on a flat stone beside the well where no foot went, stood it where it would not be trodden, and drew what the yard called weather with the pail against her knee, telling the weather where to stand. She gave nobody a figure and said nothing to the basket.

The boy watched the lifting of the plate the way he had once watched a membrane taken off a collection, the surface laid over a depth and the depth let stand. A thing may look whole because a skin is drawn across it. He knew that as a man knows the weight of an instrument in his hand, and it was worth nothing at two inches of air, and he kept it where he kept the things he did not enter. The yard went on calling it weather. The surgeon's knowing of a surface over a depth and the yard's word for a cold morning stood on two places with a finger between them, and he did not lay one across the other.

He kept the broken plate on a place of its own beside the stayed frost and the white fronds of Frost 4. It was not joined to the two gaps above the shelf, or to the one crust in the fold and its empty place, or to the field's empty place out on the worked strips. No third gap came to make a row come out even, and no fourth likeness was made a cause, for a joining would want a payer and none stood.

The dressed grain lay under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, and that morning \person{Sella Vail} turned it a second time because the stone it lay on had gone cold under two days of stayed frost. She lifted the wool with a lighter touch than on the morning before and turned the grain for damp, and laid the wool back and gave no figure. Smoke's third arithmetic stayed unread up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s locked box; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and there was no signature on the space between what the grain would be read at and what it had cost to bring under the roof. The arithmetic that would one day count it did not know what it counted.

The first firing of Frost stood behind the household and she did not look at it again. No lesson was made of it, no sign, no mending anybody would name and no payer; four openings in four months were four and not a rule, and the next stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for. The finding that the household would not make a doctrine of four openings had a place of its own, and it was not laid across the finding that a house keeps itself by \lore{not-doings} or across his own kept gaps. A joining would want a payer and none stood, and the not-saying was itself a keeping and not a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story, his name unspoken two hundred and seventy-eight days. The salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder unfilled; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and nobody filled it; the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. No collector came up the clay road on Frost 7, 8 or 9 and none went down it. The clean dark line at the Vail corner kept the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it and neither was swept. \person{Sella Vail} carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line, the finer grey beside it, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape or crumb or print, the trough with its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the very lip where it could not, with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside it and no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He gave the instruction to the wood before he gave it to anybody: \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping there, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, his chest tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept wanting the late because it puts off the paying though the paying stood in the ruts. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

\person{Jorra Vail} stood a moment at the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger, not bone-coloured slip, and parted dusty forearms from clean ones as a surgeon parts clot from vessel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. His bent finger stood in the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of \person{Sella Vail}'s unfinished sentence, one iron with two bites and no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. \place{the back room} kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices, and the house gave the men inside no name and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood as sayings, with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one further shaping and no witness under any and no row named. He kept each where it lay and asked nothing that would join the dust on his forearms to a cause. The promise of the boy's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed.

\scenebreak

Toward the going of the morning the basket stood at the edge of the work where \person{Sella Vail} had set it, a hand's breadth inside the finger's-width door so that the river's low note came through to him and no slate lay near. The woman's counting came through the floor that does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. Out at the well the plate of ice stood on its flat stone where she had set it, out of the path of feet, and the frost lay on the north lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere. \place{the towel} lay where it had been laid at the head of the bed, and the thread stayed frozen above it, unwiped.

The hand went up an inch toward the river's note. It stayed past the letting-go of one of the counted breaths and through the little silence after it, set the held interval beside the empty interval with a finger between, and came down on the river's next low note before that note had fairly begun --- an ending made early, kept whole and outside the intakes it had crossed, no house count borrowed and no fifth supplied to the kiln. On the sixth place the early ending came down beside one hundred and thirty as an object among objects, and one hundred and thirty-one was filed there, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a freedom, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon felt the clean small correctness of an ending set just ahead of the note it answered; the boy wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth; both were true, and the wanting was louder, and the checking was the work. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything.

The plate of ice stood where it had been set, out of the path of feet, with the well behind it and the trough going on with its water called weather. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} went on with its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-eight days, and through the finger's-width door the river went on with its low note. Frost 10 stood one morning ahead and was not brought into this day. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended; only the hand was placed down, forward and unresolved, with the held interval and the empty interval side by side on the sixth place and a finger between.
