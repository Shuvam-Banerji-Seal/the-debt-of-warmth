The afternoon came round to \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and the door of \place{the back room} kept the finger's width of open it had held since before first grey. Men had come in while the yard held the small hours in its hollows and spoken low behind it. Nobody had shut the door and nobody had widened it, because a shut door is a thing a slate could enter in a report and a door standing wide is the same thing under another name. \person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for them and did not say the room had been cold. He came through at the turn of the afternoon with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, and the dust was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Slip dries to the colour of old bone and sits where it was put; slate dust goes into the creases and stays there, the colour of the clean dark line in the Vail corner, which is the colour of a thing a house has put away without naming.

Their voices had not carried past the finger's width. The boy had not seen their faces, only the way Jorra's eyes went to the door when a board shifted under somebody's weight, and he filed the going of the eyes on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it. A man who keeps a room the house does not enter will look at its door before he looks at his mother, and that order is its own kind of entry, though no slate in Sill would take it down.

\person{Corin} lay at the edge of the shed floor in a basket that was not his, with one crust stiff at his ear and the empty place beside it unnamed. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray and asked neither question the dust might have answered --- which slate had been broken, and whether it had been a birth-slate. The questions could last for years where he kept them; questions kept in that part of a house outlast the men who might have answered them. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, and the dust and the clean slip were two states of one man's mornings, not a third thing and not a cause.

Under \place{the drying racks} the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain since the year turned, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody. The space above them was swept around and not swept. A woman with a tray of wet pots carried it at arm's length past the lowest board and set the tray down on the other side, and the yard went on with its work as though nothing lay beneath it, which is how a house keeps a thing it will not enter: by walking around it often enough that the walking becomes the shape of the place.

Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and the wheel took up its low rhythm again, which was the only answer the wheel gave and the only one he asked for. The pot on the bench was thin at the shoulder and he turned it a half-turn with his wrist and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel --- knew what the clay would say under a knuckle before anyone struck it --- and would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it. The boy knew that he knew, as he knew the bent finger and the dust in its creases, and kept the knowing where no slate would reach it. No seventh hearing came out of the shed. The six stayed six beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth the small flat knock out of a person, and the wheel's low rhythm was not counted among them, being a rhythm and not a note.

His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been told to wipe it; a pot set down is a pot set down and left, and a basket is the same thing under another name. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water and came down. He was the only adult in the shed and the least use in it; he had once stood over a table and known, before the first cut, where a vessel would be thin, and here he could not turn his own head without a sound.

\person{Sella Vail} came into the shed with her little-door burn shut on the rim of a pot and gave the instruction to the fire before she gave it to anyone. “The fire's low,” she said. “It'll want ---”

She did not finish. Jorra's bent finger went into \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, the cold iron taking the cold out of the fingertip before the fingertip could take anything from the iron, and the rest of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: the tray went down level, the woman at the racks took her hand off the wet board, and the yard's small noises came back into their places, all of it done without an order and without a look from him. He had not answered her with a word. He had not looked at her. Obedience and correction were the same motion in this house, and neither of them had ever been entered in a slate.

The boy watched the finger on the iron and read it as any iron that stops a thing from running. The bite that holds a vessel whole at its crack is the bite that can take the hand off a man at the knuckle; warmth is kept in by the same gate that could keep it from getting out, and a gate that can do the one can do the other. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held both mercies. There was a word for the pot and a word for the fire and a word for the hand, but the thing between them had no name anyone in Low Kiln would say aloud, and he kept the reading to himself, an anatomy of the house's danger as exact as anything he had ever set down on a chart and worth as much here as a chart would be in a kiln. He filed it as an object and joined it to no kiln, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and there was a kiln across the yard and a room off this shed and a story told over a bucket at midday, all of them with the same cold in them and none of them the same thing.

Three people under this roof could have read that bite and kept it to themselves for three different reasons. One had lost a husband to the danger and had never described it that way, and touched \lore{the widow's mark} only before an opening. One had put his own hand to the iron before any sentence was finished and would not say why. The third lay in a basket with a crust at his ear and could not reach a cup. Keeping was the whole trade of the house, and nobody in it had ever written down what was kept.

Sella did not ask about the men and did not ask about the room. She took the pot to the bench and told the fire it was the thaw, and the fire took its instruction and went on warming the boards by degrees. On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row kept its two empty places and she moved her lips along it without giving either a figure, while the clay jar on the wall shelf stayed cold under its glaze. Her private roll stayed unshown. The door off the shed kept its finger's width; no opening had come in the day, and a door standing so is not an opening, which is why she touched the widow's mark at no hour.

The neighbour's words over the bucket at midday had been a mention and had not become a telling by afternoon. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war lay on their tray already, breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both of them saying not the war's hand, likeness and difference kept and neither joined to the chimney across the yard; the mention lay beside them without becoming a third. Yesterday's one breath that named the shelf's old space and the fold's empty place in the same sentence lay on two places still, a planting and an appetite, planted and not paid. The boy held the trays steady with the only instrument he had, which was attention, and it had no handle, weighed nothing and bought nothing.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and a strip, a shelf, a bed and a door are not openings. No opening had come in the day, so she did not touch the widow's mark; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself.

The private file was checked the way a man checks instruments laid out on a towel, and it was unchanged. Six hearings of the full note stayed six beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth the small flat knock out of a person --- and no seventh came to stand in their company. Three near-full answers stood on their tray, advanced and not paid, a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall and a rack pot in the yard; three intervals lay on another. The word forty was unspoken, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker set apart from both; four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing. The damper's double bite stayed filed as an object. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and the sentence that would have called it had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same. Below the sill a child's foot stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed.

On the loaf shelf in the next room the row kept its two empty places, the old space at the end nobody called a figure and the newer gap that had come in the night of Seed 6, and in the fold at his ear one brown-edged crust lay stiff with the empty place beside it still unnamed. No third came and no hand laid the rooms together. Every warmth in the house had a payer and none was entered: the coal gone with the gull, the hands that went into the strips, the loaves that went off the board, and the carried fire that had warmed the boards for a hundred and two days under blind ash and the back of a broken pot without anybody putting a name to who paid.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and \lore{the kiln's breath} came through the floor with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and two days. In the corner the clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the new finer grey lay beside it unswept, called the thaw that would go when it went. The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire's four, four and a gap, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for a hundred and two days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. His head went where it wanted with the small wet sound; cold arrived cheek, temple, wrist; the wool rode back and the bare place took the cold. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. One hundred and two. Three months and twelve days. Nobody under the roof had said the number, and there was no word in Grathic that would have carried it if they had.

Under the wheel-shed the damper's cold iron kept its double bite with no Grathic word for both mercies; under the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay where every foot went around them and none went in; under the blind ash the carried fire kept missing its fifth breath, its payer unnamed for a hundred and two days. The count stood unspent.
