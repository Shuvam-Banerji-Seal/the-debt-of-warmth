\daymark{102}{Seed 13, Y.H. 813}

The thirteenth of Seed came up the colour of a slate left out in the rain, and the yard took the light without giving any of it back. The mud the flood had left had set into a brown skin that held the print of a boot and gave it up slowly as the sun crossed it. East of \place{Low Kiln} the seed stood in \place{the barley fields} where the fists had let it out a week ago, worked ground that said nothing about itself, and in the Vail corner the clean dark line lay where it had always lain with the new finer grey beside it, unswept, waiting out the thaw the way a debt waits out a debtor. Under the lowest rack the space went around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and nobody went into it. \person{Corin} lay at the edge of the light in a basket that was not his, with one crust stiff at his ear and the empty place beside it unnamed. The count stood at one hundred and two in a house that kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, a glance at the clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody called a figure.

In \place{the Vail kiln-house} \person{Sella Vail} had not finished telling the broom what it was to do before \person{Tarn} had taken it up, which was the order of this house and had been the order for six years: the instruction went first to the thing that could not answer, and only afterward to the hands that could. “Go around,” she said to the bristles. “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” She said the house where another woman would have said I. The basket got no instruction and the boy got none, and the little-door burn across the back of her hand shut on the air and opened again as she reached past the bare board for a loaf. Tarn waited with his head down until the last word had gone where it was meant to go, and then took the broom by the handle, quickly, as a man takes a tool out of a fire. “Yes, house,” he said. “I'll sweep it. I swept it this morning. I'll sweep it again.”

He went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. He went from the racks to the gate and back, and over it again, and the bristles made the same small sound on the same boards every time, taking up the brown dust and laying it down again where the wind wanted it. His head stayed level with his own hands. The ruts held four places where the water had gone and a fifth where a cart wheel had stood a day, and he went over all of them and gave none of them a figure. Once a vessel gave its note somewhere past the door and the broom checked half a stroke, and the head did not turn, and the sound got no name in \lore{Grathic} or in any other tongue, and the broom went on. No collector came down the road. There was nobody in the yard that a collector could have been sent for, and Tarn swept it as though the sweeping were the errand and the errand ended when the boards were clean, which is the answer a clay-bound hand gives to a question nobody has put into words.

The small knife lay on its sill where it had been put the night before. He passed it twice with his eyes before the third time with his hand, checking what had not moved, and put it back where it could be checked and told the empty yard so, unasked. Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat; the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He had cut six and would not cut the seventh, and the account stood that way because a man who owns nothing must keep something exact. “Six counted,” he said to the kindling. “The next I don't count.” The kindling gave back nothing, and he took that for agreement, as he took everything that did not argue.

At the bucket-round two women were arguing the thaw the way the district always argued it, one wanting it early so the strips would take seed, one wanting it late so the levy would have longer to stand where it was. Tarn agreed with each in turn. “Yes, house,” he said to the early, and “Yes, house,” to the late, and agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on between them. He went on wanting the late. A late thaw puts off the paying, and the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went, brown and patient and not going anywhere. The boy knew the shape of that wanting without a word for it: a man will choose the longer road if the thing waiting at the end of it has not yet got his name. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and he scraped at it with the knife and brought nothing up. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back when he moved, a small honest thing in place since Y.H. 806 that had never once been entered in a slate. The boy knew that pale place on a wrist; on another hand it had been the mark a ring leaves. Here it was iron and a term of years, and no woman in the yard had ever asked what it was.

He had been sent for a collector once, in \lore{the Long Cold}, and had come back at the frozen ford without one. To the house he had said it had been done. The sentence sat where the seventh year sat, in the part of him the house could not check, and he entered neither. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; he knew it the way a man knows the step before a floor gives, and the sentence that would have called it had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, under the bench and the shelf and the bed, waiting for a mouth to say it and a house not to ask where it had come from.

In the basket the boy kept Tarn's silence on a place of its own beside his own, and the two did not touch. It was an equality with no word in it and no use in it, and he held it the way he held the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one: by looking at each in turn and never at both at once. One hundred and two. Three months and twelve days. Nobody under the roof had said the number, and there was no word in Grathic that would have carried it if they had. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth the small flat knock out of a person --- and no seventh came to stand in their company; the man who swept had done nothing all morning that he would call a sound, and the boy did not call it one either. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, with the wool riding back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been told to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He was the only adult in the yard and the least use in it. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and counted the beats between an injury and a decision; he could not now count the beats between his own hand and his own mouth. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water and came down.

\scenebreak

By midday the light had come round to \place{the yard well}, and the tube-water stood a finger's breadth below the lip with the skin on it broken by every bucket that had been down. \person{Berr Clay} came up the clay road from the direction of \place{the Cross}, short and heavy, smelling of hot clay with nothing under it, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. The red wool cord of his office was folded under his coat instead of worn across it. At one corner of his mouth the green of tasted glaze showed where the old habit had been at work and he had not known it; the habit had outlived the tasting, and the green went on being a sign of thoroughness to men who wanted a sign. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with a finger resting on what was written.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said to the well and the yard and the women at the buckets, not to any one of them. He passed the finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.” A woman with her bucket still in her hand put a question about the number to him, and he turned the answer so the speaker was out of it before it reached her. “A number goes into a slate, not into a strip. If the house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” He never said I. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the woman went back to her bucket with the number still in her mouth and nowhere to put it. The twice-wiped thumb was a field prepared and nothing done in it. The true subtraction stayed inside the slate, and the box in his house stayed locked in \place{High Kiln}, and he went off up the clay road toward it believing he had done the district a kindness.

The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb on a tray of its own, and the finger passing over the figure beside it, and carried neither to the midwife's hand or the clerk's at \place{the Fishstairs} or the man at the Court's table. Five record-first hands lay on five trays under his roof and none of them touched, and no sixth hand came. A figure read aloud was not the figure paid, and a figure put into a slate was not a figure put into a strip; the yard went on with its buckets as though nothing had been taken out of it, and the kindness went up the road in a coat with the cord folded underneath.

A neighbour at the bucket-round let fall, over the rim of a bucket, that there had been a counting room shut in the war with \lore{the damper} closed and the men inside dead of the breath, and said it the way a person says a thing from a distant year, with a date in it and none of the weight. Somebody said it was a long way east. Somebody else said the water had been high that year too, and the yard let the saying lie where it had fallen. It was a mention and not a telling. Two tellings of such a room lay on their tray already --- breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both of them saying not the war's hand, likeness and difference kept and neither joined to the kiln across the yard --- and no third came. The boy held the tray steady with his attention, which had no handle, weighed nothing and bought nothing, and wrote no cause across any of it.

\person{Sella Vail} came through with a pot in one hand and her little-door burn shut on its rim. She gave the men no name and did not say the room had been cold. “It was the thaw,” she said to the fire, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and joined nothing in the yard to the roof she kept. Whatever else she put away she put where nobody is shown. The fire took its instruction and went on with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the Vail roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and a strip, a shelf, a bed and a door are not openings. No opening had come in the day, so she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. Her private roll stayed unshown. On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row kept its two empty places and nobody called either a number; in the fold at his ear one brown-edged crust lay stiff with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third had come.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and \lore{the kiln's breath} came through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and two days. Every warmth in the house had a payer and none was entered; the hands that went into the strips and the loaves that went off the board were written in no slate a man could read, and he had asked who paid in his own head for a hundred and two days without once putting the question into words that a person under this roof could carry. The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire's four, four and a gap, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for a hundred and two days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

Out on its sill the small knife lay where it could be checked, six years standing in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, and the late thaw was still wanted under this roof because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and had not stirred. Under the blind ash the fire kept its four and its gap with a payer nobody had named for a hundred and two days. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. One hundred and two, edge to edge, unspent.
