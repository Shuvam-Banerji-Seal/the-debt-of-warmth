Under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} the dark went on keeping the number without a figure, and \person{Corin} went over the file once more the way a man turns out a towel of instruments before he puts his hands into a body --- not looking for anything new, but making sure nothing had been moved while his back was turned. The towel was the only thing in the room that had ever been laid out straight. Woodsmoke and wet wool came down under the roof with the dark, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the unswept corner; the boards settled by degrees as the air off the yard cooled, and the little-door burn on the back of a hand closing on nothing went on somewhere to his right. He was one hundred and three days old. Nobody under this roof had told him so, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would have carried the saying if somebody had tried. Three months and thirteen days. The house kept its account badly, pushed along \place{the loaf shelf} in loaves nobody moved except to count them back to where they had been, glanced at in the clay jar on the wall shelf, and left standing in a space at the row's end that nobody called a number; and he kept one hundred and three edge to edge against all of it, the only exact thing in a room of approximations, and could not spend it, or show it, or get one person to say it aloud.

He laid the six hearings of \lore{the full note} out first, the way a man lays out the things he has already used and knows the weight of. Five came from four sources: a cup across the birth room, a pot under \place{the Vail wheel-shed} wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at the Drawing. The sixth was the small flat knock out of a person, filed beside the five and never among them, the way a broken instrument is kept in its own compartment and not fitted to the ones that still ring. No seventh had come in three days. A pot lifted a hand's breadth too high had come up to the rim of a note and gone back down without crossing it, and he had kept that near thing on the interval tray where such near things went, because a thing that comes up to the edge of a sound is not the sound, and a man who wants an answer will make the two into one if no hand is there to stop him. His hand was outside the wool, going up an inch and coming down. It was not there to stop anything. He had once been able to tell, with a knuckle laid against a vessel, whether the note under it was honest; he could not now lift that knuckle to his own ear. The six kept their places, five from four sources and the sixth from flesh, and the near thing kept its place apart from them, and nothing crossed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot in the yard. They had come as nearly full, with air under the note and a want in it, and he had filed them as answers to long-kept intervals rather than make a cause of them; he had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, and a man who wants one badly enough will go home satisfied with the thing he has made. Three intervals lay on another tray, the shape of sounds that had never sounded. He did not lay an answer beside its interval and call the two a recovery. An answer advanced was not an answer paid, and a debt that stood where it was put was a debt nobody had settled, and the three near-full things stayed where they were and the three silences stayed where they were and nothing crossed between them. It was a dry thing to keep, an answer that could not be spent, and the joke of it had gone thin years ago into the fact underneath: he knew the note of a vessel he could not reach, and the knowing was exact and worth nothing.

The word forty lay on a place of its own, unspoken. It was a saying in this district and had not been said under this roof in all these days, and the boy had no right to it and no way to spend it. Beside it lay the readable shape of the double turn, which he had seen tied at \place{the Weir} and could not have named: double turn, tail tucked, tied left over right, with the small weight of a thumb at the end of it, and a woman who had said the word low into a basket and then watched what he would do with it, and he had done nothing she could have read. Beneath another cord a second cord twisted twice, with neither knot counted by any hand and the knot for the thin child upriver left unvisited and never carried to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door. Apart from both, set on its own square of the towel, lay the weight of a thumb on wicker, which was a hand reaching into a pocket-place in another life and coming back with wool, and a lit rectangle with a name that had not been looked at. Four low things kept their four places; the river's note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing. He kept the five apart as he kept all the others, and the word stayed a word and the shape stayed a shape and the weight stayed a weight, and none of them became the other.

The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their tray, joined to no kiln. A man come downriver with road-mud on his boots had said it twice over two bucket-rounds as a curiosity with a date, eighteen leagues east, and the yard had taken it for history both times, the way a yard takes any trouble that has a year on it and lives somewhere else; the boy had kept breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both sayings saying not the war's hand, and had kept the likeness and the difference without letting either swallow the other. Men had come in before first grey to a room standing a finger's width open and spoken low behind it, and a man had gone through it with grey slate dust worked past the bend of a finger, and neither of those things had been given a name in this house. No third telling came. The woman who kept this fire had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself, and the boy did not lay the two sayings beside her roof, though the tray they lay on was near enough to it that a careless hand might have slid one across.

The neighbour's single breath of the morning lay on a tray of its own, divided into two tellings on two places: a planting on the old space at the row's end, a child's appetite on the empty place at his ear. She had named both in one breath without once seeing that they were one absence, and had gone on with her pot; and the boy had filed the two sayings apart and written no cause across the space between them. From the pale oval on the shelf to the crust at his ear was the length of the room and a hand's breadth more, and from the crust to the old space at the row's end was the same length and a hand's breadth more again, so that a man might have drawn one line through the three places and called it one line if he had wanted an answer badly enough. He did not draw it. A line drawn through three absences is a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent his life declining to be. The witness was a planted thing, left where it had fallen, not paid and not joined; it waited for some far-off day when a finger would lay the shelf and the fold together and call the two places an account that wanted a name. He kept his own finger where it was, small and outside the wool. The holding was the whole of the work and the work produced nothing, and the nothing was the exact price of keeping the two places clean. No third gap came into either room and no hand laid the rooms together.

\lore{The damper} stayed filed as an object. A bent finger had gone into the cold iron ahead of a sentence nobody finished, and the boy had read the bite as any iron that stops a thing from running --- the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes a hand off a man are one bite, and no word in \lore{Grathic} holds both mercies --- exact and worth nothing, entered nowhere and joined to no kiln. A household had straightened around that silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and three people under one roof had been able to read the iron and had kept it to themselves for three different reasons. The loan that can hear was still uncalled; the sentence that would have called it had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, like a tool set down in a dark room where the next man will find it by his shins. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool; one woman called it the cold and a neighbour at the tubs called the water weather, and the two facts went past each other without meeting, uncured, uncaused, unnamed. He knew the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and the reading worked perfectly and bought nothing, the way a diagnosis works perfectly on a body two paces out of reach. He did not lay the foot beside the crust or the shelf or the shut pages, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and the only hand here could not have reached the wool.

In the next room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps: the old space at the row's end, worn pale by years of hands reaching past it, and the new palm's breadth within the rank that had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word to mark it. A woman's hand had passed over the new gap and opened for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there and closed on the loaf beyond and gone on with her pot, and no figure had passed. A loaf had been seen going out under a woman's arm and not seen coming back, with no slate in the yard that would have had it entered. In the fold at his ear one brown-edged crust lay stiff with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third had come. The two gaps and the one gap went on in rooms that did not touch, and the neighbour's one-breath naming went on as two tellings on two places, and the boy's finger went on being where it was. Every warmth in the house had a payer and none was entered: not the coal, not the milk, not the bread, not the hands that had carried him up the yard and set him here and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. He had learned in a better-lit room to ask who paid before asking what a thing was worth, and there was nobody under this roof who would have understood the question, and nobody he could have asked it of.

In the next room \person{Sella Vail} stood over the fire with the little-door burn on the back of her hand closing on nothing. “It was the thaw,” she told the fire, the way a woman tells a pot what it is to hold. “You'll have wetting enough before the week's out.” She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, edge first and then flat, and fed them, and the fire took the coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and three days, and the house had gone on warm all the same, the way a debt goes on standing when nobody says who owes it. Garl's hollow wheel-seat kept its leftward lean behind her and his salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg, and neither was spoken of; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept behind her heel. “Feed the fire before you feed yourself,” she said, which was his proverb and not hers, and fed the fire. She gave the instruction to the fire and the wool and the pot and never to the basket, and said the house where another woman would have said I, and did not look through the finger's-width-open door toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was touched at no hour, because a door standing a finger's width open is not an opening and a shelf, a strip, a basket and a bed are not openings either. She had lost a husband to that danger in \lore{the Long Cold} and had never once described it that way herself.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for a hundred and three days. In the corner the clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw that would go when it went. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it; he had once stood over a table and known, before the first cut, where a vessel would be thin, and now could not lift his hand to his own chin. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been told to wipe it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place, and his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. Nothing was joined, nothing was paid, nothing was named. The count stood at one hundred and three, unspent, and the dark under the roof went on keeping it without a figure.
