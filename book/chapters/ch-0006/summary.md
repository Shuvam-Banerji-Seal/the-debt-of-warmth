## Chronicle

On Stone 16 the coals held under ash and Sella Vail touched the widow's mark before she lifted the kiln lid, at no other hour. Tarn measured the kindling: four, four, a space where the fifth should be; six years counted, the seventh held back. Alda Reed came to see him, reached for her slate before she reached for him, touched the tin inside her bodice, crossed two fingers at the red edge of his wrist without knowing it, and covered the sign with an unasked-for date. She wrote a note, turned it face-down into her shawl, and went on giving dates after the question had stopped. He could not read it or hand it back.

On Stone 17 a pot on the bench gave nothing. The boy turned before the set-down; Tarn's broom stopped half a stroke. Neither named the interval. At *you've done well* he went still for two breaths, then agreed flatly. He held the pale ring at his wrist to the light, then put the knife back where it could be checked. Sella named the kiln's breath as the danger; Tarn told of a man in Mud End who died of it and was called merciful. At the well on Stone 18 a neighbour reached for her slate before her child, and for two sentences a ceiling light at four, a chart with its note turned down, a pocket with wool where a phone had lain broke through.

On Stone 19 a man came up from the Ilm with a shard of ice in which a dark hair ran and stopped short of the edge. A woman from High Kiln said the thaw would come early; everyone who wanted it late contradicted her: a late thaw puts off what is payable. A neighbour described the Court's room: a bench, a pot, a draught, a gap. The boy set it beside the birth room and death-house and made nothing of it. Sella measured the ridge: near two thumbs here, a finger thicker two doors down. Neither would be swept before the Last Day.

On Stone 20, Account-day, Sella set him in the moot-post's shade, half a pace from open slates. The bell went four, four and a gap; nobody crossed himself. Berr Clay wiped his thumb and read one in twelve to two hundred leaning people. The levy was called for the feast, not by him. He went around Garl's uncalled debt and did not look into the basket. A soft-cuffed clerk laid his hand on the slate before he looked at the woman and said it stood as entered. The upper line was in the neat dark hand; its small stop came a fourth time and lay beside the other three without joining. The crowd went back to the pigs. At dusk the coals were banked, the fold put right, the crust left cold. The Five were eleven days off; the household kept the number badly and the boy kept it exactly.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** Dusk into night of Stone 20. The boy lies on his bed under the Vail roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, with the brown-edged barley crust now cold and stiff in the fold at his side --- uneaten, unremoved, never named as a gift. Sella has put the fold right without looking at his face, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot (*that'll hold*) and not touched \lore{the widow's mark}. Tarn has set the small bench knife back on its sill and announced it to the empty yard, and his broom has stopped half a stroke at the bench where no note came. Jorra has moved the damper before Sella finished her sentence and has not said the back room was cold. Garl is coat, hollow seat, unswept ridge and proverb. Last counted thing: four, four and a gap. The fourth pause lies beside three others and has not joined them. Nobody has spoken to the basket all day.
- **Day and time at the end:** Day 14 of the new life complete; Stone 20, Y.H. 812, Tenthday, \lore{Account-day}. Morning at \place{the Cross} and the Court's table, dusk back under the Vail roof. Fourteen days old. Days 10–14 were each lived in its own dated scene --- Stone 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 --- with no day poured into another. From Day 14 the Five are eleven days off (Day 25, Kindling).
- **Where everyone is and their condition:**
  - **Corin/Dan:** carried down in the basket at dawn and back up at dusk; set in the moot-post's finger's width of shade with cold iron at his cheek; lifted home with woodsmoke and river wind in the wool. Drool freezing at mouth, head goes where it wants, hand outside the wool rises an inch toward broom, pocket-place, ice-light and warmth and comes down. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist. Crust cold through the wool. Wants the breast, the crust, a word to correct the room; gets none and cannot ask.
  - **Sella Vail:** at the fire after banking; carried him down and back keeping a half-pace from every open slate; did not touch the mark at a reading, because it belongs to the hour before an opening. Said *the house* at the threshold, moved the basket a hand's breadth out of the draught and would not have called it love. Measured the ridge with her thumb: near two thumbs at Vail, a finger more two doors down; *leave it; it'll be swept on \lore{the Last Day}*. Wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained.
  - **Tarn:** in the yard and under the roof, broom set against the wall, knife on its sill and announced twice unasked. Six years counted, seventh held back. Stopped altogether for two breaths at *you've done well*. Scraped Ash from his knuckles with the knife, held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to the light, put the knife back. Named the kiln's breath as the danger, told of the man in \place{Mud End} called merciful, wanted the late thaw (*a longer week before the paying*), offered to check the ridge. Broom stopped half a stroke at the empty set-down; did not look into the basket. Wants his exact account and still grudges the house the loan of a bed.
  - **Jorra Vail:** in and out of \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the shed} on all five days with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone on both forearms and no slate dust; bent little finger going in ahead of his hand at the damper. Said *you'll do* and *the wheel doesn't care whose hands, only if they're steady*. Shifted the damper before Sella finished. Did not say the back room was cold or look toward the bed. Wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat for himself.
  - **Garl Vail:** absent; salt-stiff coat on its peg, twenty-year hollow on the left of the wheel-seat, Ash ridge near two thumbs wide turning the corner without a break, tile debt uncalled for a year, proverbs going to the fire and to pots and never to the basket.
  - **Alda Reed:** came under the Vail roof mid-morning of Stone 16 to see the child she delivered; smelled of soap and warm milk. Touched the tin once inside her bodice, pressed the fold flat too long, made the two-finger sign at the red edge of his wrist without knowing it, covered it with dates nobody had asked for, wrote a second note on a narrow slate and turned it writing-down into her shawl --- toward a floorboard in her house he does not know. Said *warm and breathing --- that's all I've set down*. Gone by night; wants the child warm and the account in order, and has written down what she will not say.
  - **Berr Clay:** at the Cross on Stone 20 with red cord of office under his coat and green at one corner of his mouth; wiped his thumb twice before touching the slate, read one in twelve *as the notice says* and the levy *called for the feast, not by me*, passed a finger over the figure, went around the uncalled tile debt and never looked into the basket. Box still locked; gone home by dusk believing his own sentences.
  - **The collector's clerk at the Court's table:** walk-on, no name, no sheet, not \person[Marra]{Collector Marra}. Cuff turned back twice from washing, ink in two otherwise ordinary fingers, soft voice with no edge. Laid his hand on the top slate before he looked at the woman waiting; *it's entered as it stands; there's nothing in it the household need correct*. Went off toward \place{Slate Row}.

Berr read one in twelve and said the levy was not called by him; Garl's debt against the tiles lay spoken around and unnamed for another year. The clerk's *entered as it stands* finished a woman's business before it began. The Ilm has a hairline crack and the district argues about the thaw to protect what is payable in it; Sella calls the river weather. The Court's room has bench, pot, draught and a gap, and the boy keeps it in four separate places rather than write a cause where he has only noticed a likeness. Sella said the Vail ridge is near two thumbs and the death-house ridge a finger thicker, and neither will be swept before the Last Day. No promise was made to him and no debt incurred by him; he cannot enter, refuse or hand back any of it.
