# Chapter 6 beat sheet — Counting What There Is

**Days 10–14** · Stone 16 to Stone 20, Y.H. 812 · Sixthday through Tenthday · **passage mode, five dated scenes, one lived day each, no day poured into another.** Day 14 is Account-day. From Day 14 the Five are eleven days off.

**Continuity gate before the first word.** Previous chapter ended on Day 9 (Stone 15), night, under the Vail roof: coals banked under ash, grey ridge turning the corner, door a finger's width open, brown-edged barley crust warm in the fold at his side, hand up an inch and down. He is nine days old; by this chapter's close, fourteen. Cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin, mute, carried everywhere, cannot read Grathic, cannot lift a hand to his own chin. Five hearings of the flat note from four sources; Stone 13 added an *interval*, not a sixth. The three pauses (flare, birth-slate, Cross reading of Stone 10) stay unjoined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand has no pause and stays in its own place. The crust is cold now but stays in the fold, uneaten and unremoved. Do not explain the flat sound, the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate, the debt, the cords, the tin, or why Sella took him. Do not say one in three, do not say forty, do not name Marra, do not show Sella's roll, Berr's box, the knots, or the tin's contents. No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn.

**Chapter-wide texture to keep alive:** work (slip, wheel, kiln, sweeping, counting loaves), food (barley gruel, the cold crust, eels absent this chapter, the loaf shelf), weather (dry cold, Ilm under ice, hairline crack), bodies (drool, useless head, hand rising an inch, cold arriving cheek–temple–wrist), money (marks laid down and counted with eyes, figures heard and unspendable), faith (the bell, the slate read aloud, a kindness entered, the widow's mark, the levy called for the feast).

## Scene 1 — Four, Four, a Place for the Fifth

- **Day and time:** Day 10, Stone 16, Y.H. 812, Sixthday. First light into mid-morning.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln.
- **Present:** Corin (ten days old, in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed; Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, Ash ridge and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Open on the kept heat, not on a thought: coals still banked under ash, the little-door burn closing on nothing, Sella's hand to the widow's mark *before* she lifts the kiln lid and at no other hour. The pot stands on the bench where it stood on Stone 13. The crust lies cold in the fold at his side.
  2. Tarn measures the stack of kindling with his eyes while Sella moves her lips along the shelf of loaves; the boy counts the stack four, four, a place where the fifth should be, and files it beside the boards, the bench and the interval. Nobody has spoken to the basket.
  3. The household's arithmetic is performed in objects: loaves arranged so the days to Kindling are a shape and not a figure; six years counted and the seventh held back; Jorra at the wheel with bone-coloured slip and no slate dust, speaking to a pot, moving the damper before Sella has finished.
  4. Alda Reed comes to see the child she delivered. She reaches for her slate before she reaches for him; touches the tin once inside her bodice; presses the fold flat too long at his shoulder; makes the two-finger sign at the red edge of his wrist without knowing she does it and covers it with an exact, unasked-for date. On her own slate she writes what she does not leave lying about, and goes on giving dates after the question has stopped.
  5. The boy reads her order of reaching the way he once read a chart being signed: the hand to the record before the hand to the person.
- **Emotional turn:** From the ordinary comfort of a counted room to the knowledge that the gap in the count is the only place in it that has no entry --- and that a woman can write a truth down and turn it face-down in the same gesture that warms a child.
- **Sensory anchors:** cold ash under the pot-shovel; tallow and wet wool; the sweet mineral tang at the skirting board; soap and warm milk off Alda; the little-door burn's pale inside; the kiln's breath four, four and a gap; the crust gone cold and stiff against his ribs.
- **Threads advanced:** T071/T072 (count extended to fuel and the kindling stack), T044 (warmth entered nowhere, now a household pattern), T077 (the number gone around as a shape), T079 (mark touched only before the kiln), T036 (six counted, seventh held), T035 (slip, no dust), T007 (sign made unaware), T023 (second note written and hidden), T008 (date covering the sign), T060 (love as maintenance), T065 (kiln's breath), T082 (gap filed beside the interval).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants Alda's hidden note toward the floorboard and Jorra's clean forearms toward the back room; plants the kindling's missing fifth beside the bench's interval toward the nearly-full note of the third thing; pays nothing yet.
- **Exact last image:** Alda's two fingers lifted at the red edge of his wrist and covered by a date spoken to nobody, while Sella's hand comes away from the mark and the kindling stack stands four, four, a place where the fifth should be.

## Scene 2 — A Loan That Can Hear

- **Day and time:** Day 11, Stone 17, Seventhday. Before light into dusk.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard.
- **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, Jorra; Garl as absence.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Tarn sweeps before light; the small bench knife goes back to its sill and is announced to the yard unasked. One year of seven remains and he will not count it. When Sella tells him he has done well, he stops altogether for two breaths, then agrees and goes on.
  2. The pot on the bench is turned toward again and set down softly; it gives nothing. The boy turns his head before the set-down, expecting the note; Tarn's broom stops half a stroke. Neither keeps the other's silence aloud. A second interval goes into the private file beside the first (T082), with no sixth hearing added.
  3. Sella gives the instruction about the kiln mouth to the fire and to Jorra --- *don't sleep in the draught; the breath takes a man in his sleep* --- naming the kiln's breath as the danger the district lives beside. Tarn says a man in Mud End died of it and the district called it a merciful death (T065). The boy files the poison with the things that are entered nowhere.
  4. Tarn takes the knife out again to scrape Ash from his knuckles, holds the pale ring at his left wrist up to the light a moment, and puts the knife back where it can be checked. The boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the broom and comes down; Tarn does not look.
  5. Jorra comes in from the shed with bone-coloured slip, says *you'll do* to a pot, shifts the damper before Sella has finished, and does not mention the back room.
- **Emotional turn:** The boy recognizes Tarn's silence as the same object as his own, held from the other side --- which makes the loan that can hear both more valuable and more dangerous, and gives him no word, no hand and no standing to act on it.
- **Sensory anchors:** broom bristles on trodden ash; the worn mark on the knife handle; grey Ash in knuckle creases and the pale clip-ring; hot clay and the sweetish lead edge on a bad wind; the soft set-down that answers nothing; a breath of kiln air gone cold in the draught.
- **Threads advanced:** T036, T076 (two ears, two silences), T082 (second interval), T071/T072, T065 (danger named), T060, T058, T035.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the loan that can be called toward Ulda's sale and Tarn's later refusal; plants the kiln's breath toward the closed counting room of the war. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** Tarn's broom stopped half a stroke at the bench where no note came, the knife back on its sill with its mark worn almost flat, the kiln breathing four, four and a gap.

## Scene 3 — The Pocket Place

- **Day and time:** Day 12, Stone 18, Eighthday. Morning into dusk.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard and the yard well.
- **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, Jorra; a neighbour woman at the well (walk-on); Garl as objects and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. A figure goes around the yards --- days to Kindling, days to the Five --- and reaches the house as an absence. Sella keeps her half-pace from an open slate and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*. Garl's uncalled debt against the tiles is spoken around in a neighbour's sentence and never named.
  2. At the yard well a neighbour reaches for a slate before her child. For two sentences the old life breaks through in the present tense, untagged: the ceiling light at four in the morning, a chart with its note turned face-down, a hand going past it toward a signature, the hum under it. Then a pocket that holds wool where a lit phone lay with a name he did not look at. The river wind closes over it.
  3. He files the reaching hand beside the other gestures he keeps --- the midwife's sign, the clerk's hand to the slate, the man who priced him and the woman who kept him --- and does not look at any name.
  4. The surgeon who once signed a chart over a note he did not read recognizes the same grammar in a district that calls its arithmetic a kindness and its kindness entered (T047, T058/T075).
- **Emotional turn:** From watching a gesture to recognizing it as his own --- the unanswerable kindness and the unclosed account prove to be one shape, and the hand that reaches a pocket finds only wool. The knowing reduces him to the thing carried in a basket.
- **Sensory anchors:** cold stone lip of the well and a little roof dripping; a slate turned down; the wrong light and the hum of another life; wool under his fingers where cloth should be; woodsmoke in the wool; the lead smell off the door.
- **Threads advanced:** T004/T044 (pocket reached, name unlooked-at, boat carried as absence), T045 (the note not read, the signature), T058/T075 (kindness that cannot be refused), T079, T077, T071/T072, T047 (Vesci's grammar now ordinary market speech), T009, T081 (light, faintly).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the old chart's grammar into the Keeping's grammar; plants nothing that needs explaining yet. The room with the bench and the gap is only glimpsed, not recognized.
- **Exact last image:** His hand outside the wool going up toward the pocket-place and coming down with nothing in it, while the yard's closed fist of a number goes past him and Sella's little-door burn shuts on nothing.

## Scene 4 — A Hairline in the Ice

- **Day and time:** Day 13, Stone 19, Ninthday. Dusk.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house and the yard path toward the Ilm.
- **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, Jorra; a man come up from the river and a woman from High Kiln (walk-ons); Garl as absence.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. A man come up from the iced river brings word: the Ilm shows a hairline crack. A woman from High Kiln says the thaw will come early; everybody who wants it late contradicts her, because a late thaw puts off what is payable in the thaw (T077, T083).
  2. A neighbour returned from the Court's room in Slate Row describes it --- bench by the door, pot on the bench, draught under it, a gap in the count. The boy sets it beside the birth room, the kiln-house and the death-house without recognizing them as one room (T081, advance, not recognition).
  3. Sella measures the grey ridge along the skirting board against the death-house ridge by the width of a thumb; the death-house ridge is still thicker by a finger's width. Both turn the corner without a break; neither will be swept before the Last Day (T013, T009).
  4. The household keeps the days to Kindling badly, in loaves and glances; the boy keeps them exactly and cannot spend the number. The crack in the ice is read as weather, the debt as weather, the Ash as dirt.
- **Emotional turn:** From the outside world's weather to the inside room's account: the district has decided what to call both, and the only honest count in the yard belongs to a child who cannot write or speak it.
- **Sensory anchors:** river water on a man's legs; a hairline of dark in grey ice under tallow-light; a draught that goes along the floor to the corner; sour mineral Ash; the shelf of loaves rearranged twice; the kiln taking its breath in fours with one missing.
- **Threads advanced:** T077, T083, T081, T013, T009, T071/T072, T065, T060.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the early thaw toward the Breaking and Dessa's death; plants the Court's room as one more version of the same room; no recognition, no explanation.
- **Exact last image:** The hairline crack in the ice held up to the light by a man who has come up from the river, and the Vail Ash ridge turning the corner without a break while the house keeps its number badly and the boy keeps it exactly.

## Scene 5 — The Fourth Pause

- **Day and time:** Day 14, Stone 20, Tenthday, Account-day. Morning at the Cross into dusk back under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** The Cross and the Court's table at its Slate Row fringe; then the Vail kiln-house.
- **Present:** Corin, Sella, Berr Clay, the collector's clerk at the Cross (walk-on, one soft line, not Marra --- log in `_minor.md`, give no sheet); Tarn and Jorra at dusk; market voices; Garl as coat, seat, ridge and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Sella carries the boy down in the basket under the fold, keeps her half-pace from every open slate and sets him in the moot-post's finger's width of shade. The bell strikes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself at it (T078).
  2. Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron before touching the slate and reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* in the low voice that makes two hundred people lean in; *the levy is called for the feast; it is not called by me*; the figure stands as read. Garl's uncalled debt against the firing of tiles is spoken around, never named, left where it lay. He does not look into the basket (T015, T041, T047, T009).
  3. At the Court's table a collector's clerk with a cuff soft from washing lays his hand on the top slate before he looks at the woman waiting and says, softly, that it is entered as it stands and there is nothing in it the household need correct. The upper line is in the strange neat dark hand. The boy catches the small stop between two clusters **a fourth time** --- at the flare, on the birth-slate, at the Cross on Stone 10, now here --- still unread, still unjoined to the other three. The Drawing collector's ordinary hand, with no pause at all, stays filed separately (T003/T005).
  4. The crowd leans in; nothing is proved; the market goes back to the pigs. The boy hears figures he cannot spend and his hand goes to a pocket-place that holds wool (T004/T044).
  5. Dusk: Sella lifts the basket, sets the fold right without looking at his face, banks the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the mark; the crust stays cold in the fold. The Five are eleven days off. The household keeps that number badly and the boy keeps it exactly.
- **Emotional turn:** A fourth sighting does not make a pattern he can use. He has more evidence than anyone in the square and no standing, no word and no hand to enter it; the account goes on outliving the debtor.
- **Sensory anchors:** shade cold against his cheek at the moot-post; a bell longer than a church bell; a thumb wiped clean on an apron; green at the corner of a man's mouth; the soft cuff and the neat upper line; the flat knock nobody remarks; woodsmoke and river wind in the wool going home; coals hissed under ash.
- **Threads advanced:** T003/T005 (fourth sighting, unjoined), T015, T041, T047, T077, T078, T071/T072, T009, T058/T075, T004/T044, T060, T013.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the promised fourth sighting of the neat hand without joining it; plants nothing new that needs solving. The clerk is not Marra and the Box stays locked.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the roof with the cold crust still in the fold, the fourth pause laid beside three others and not joined to them, his hand going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down --- four, four and a gap, while Low Kiln will not say what it counts.

## Pull into Chapter 7

The chapter ends with the household counting what it has into the last tend before the Five and the boy keeping the number it refuses to give. Nothing has been explained: the fourth pause stays unjoined, the fifth hearing has not come, the crust is still uneaten, the roll, the box, the tin and the knots stay hidden, and the word forty has not been spoken. Chapter 7, *The Number They Give Out*, opens on Day 15 with fuel and bread counted into the tend, the boy's count extended to the kindling's missing fifth, and somebody come back from looking at the ice with a number they will not give. The district is about to change the number in the telling from three to two to whatever it decides to give out that morning; the boy will go on keeping his own. Forward, not closed.
