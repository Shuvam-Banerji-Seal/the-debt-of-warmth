The light went out of \place{the Clay Yard} in the manner of Bone light, not at a stroke but by a long withdrawing, the way heat goes out of a pot that has been set down where no foot will go. \person[Corin]{The boy} was carried down at the half-pace he was carried at everywhere in the district, with the fold over his ears against the river wind and the instruction going to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. \person{Sella Vail} gave it low, in the voice she kept for the fire and the pot and the broom; the wool took it and the wind took it, and the basket was set where it was set and told nothing. Behind them the yard went on with the work it had held all the day, because a day in Bone does not stop to be looked at for the sake of the light.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, as she had gone around them in the mornings behind and would go around them in the mornings ahead. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, the one lying against the other like two words spoken in a low voice that nobody repeats. There was the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, given its look and its wide step and entered into nothing. There was the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word to say what had stood there, a place kept by the shape it had left. There was the skinned water in \place{the trough}. There was the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no figure, and a thing gone around with a broom is still a thing kept; the house kept these the way it kept the fire, by doing the thing that left them alone.

At \place{the yard well} the stayed frost had gone off the north lip while the yard was looking elsewhere, and the trough kept its skin, and the plate of ice lifted off it stood clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all of it weather and entered none of it. The stayed frost had been a covering laid over a depth; the skinned water was the same covering over another depth; the clouded plate was a piece of the depth lifted up and set where no foot would go. The boy kept them on places of their own with a finger between every pair and made no fourth likeness of them to call it a cause, and named no payer to any. He did not bring any of it in as a lesson about the month or the boards or the voice that had gone over them the day before. The edges nearly touched, and the finger went between.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from \place{the hearth}, and she lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning's and gave it no figure. It changed state under her hand and was covered again, and no number came off it. The three readings that had gone over it in a month that did not know what it cost were not brought back as a lesson and did not follow the grain under the wool. Frost's three arithmetics, with their true subtractions, rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space between. Three readings were three and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it --- not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain or the boy's own kept gaps with the edges nearly touching and the finger going between. A box up the road could not be reached back from here, and was not.

Bone went on being the month of hunts and repairs. Men come down from \place{Cold Acre} with hares across their shoulders were the month's carrying, judged by the ache in the arm that did it and not by a figure; a cost asked of the firing was answered with the weather and a direction that left no entry. A handle mended twice either held or it did not, and the yard would have known which by lifting the pot and not by reading a slate. \lore{The carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid under the floor and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The house kept its days in loaves, in glances and in the space where a loaf should have stood, and gave nobody a number for them. Bone 10 stood four mornings off with the month's first reading in a box nobody here could open, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women named them by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days, and both went on side by side as they had always gone.

\scenebreak

\person{Ilva} was at the well once, drawing with the pail against her knee in the way women draw who have done it all their working lives, the rope running true through the palm and the bucket going down without a splash. Clay was dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the drying racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out; it would have come out with a nail and a breath of water, and she had never once done it. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft. The district would not have turned its head twice for her, and the boy lying at the edge of the work under a fold of wool was not a thing a woman drawing water would look at.

A woman with a pot asked her what the month's firings had taken. It was the question the yard asked and did not answer, and it went over \place{the boards} as other questions went --- into the air, where nobody stood under it to be counted. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of birch bark with its notches, pale where a thumb had rubbed them and dark in the uncut grain between, did not come out. It was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate lying on the stone lip a pace away, and no figure came off the wood. Her thumb passed over the place where the notches were without taking anything from them, the way a surgeon's finger may rest on a dressing without lifting it. Then her eyes went to the woman who had asked, and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word:

\emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.}

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice. The boy took the order of reaching apart as he had taken apart the voice over the boards, by what was there and by what was not: the hand to the record before the eyes to the person, the eyes to the person before the mouth, and the mouth answering the question put and not the one behind it. He filed it as the ninth record-first hand on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth, with a finger between, and filed no tenth. The ninth stayed nine. Nothing of the well went under the roof as a lesson, a figure, a mending, a cause or a payment, and he did not make her a reader of the count she kept, which she was not. The strip stayed in the sleeve where no slate could take a figure off it, and the notches stood for what they stood for and for nothing anybody had asked her to say.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill with six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh out at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside it with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stood there. He did not say the word. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back; his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it; the two ears under the roof kept their equality and the two silences their one shape, and nothing in the going of the light turned the seventh year into eight. He put the knife back where it could be checked and announced it to nobody, because the yard had already been told and a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not have said it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it.

The burned slate stood now at five tellings, the fifth come over the boards on the day before with no owner under the voice and no hand at the flame, and he put a finger between it and the four and did not supply a sixth. Five were five and not a rule; a row filled in to make it come out even would be a row forged. The two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and had not gained a third from any burning. The three pot sayings stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. When the saying had gone around the boards he had grown quieter and the household had straightened around the quiet without an order; \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The claim gathered interest in the rooms he went into and he did not pay it and did not refuse it, which in this district is a way of keeping a thing alive. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. He joined nothing on the wheel-keeper's forearms to a cause. \place{Slate Row} stayed a direction over the boards, and the fifth telling waited on the far-off day the promise would be called in, not a confession and not a payment.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story, and his name had not been spoken under the roof for three hundred and five days. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held his broader shape with the crease at the left shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with a finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- and it had improved in the keeping, as all his sayings did, and was very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said it. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; a debt circled without a name is a debt that gathers weight from being gone around. Up in the loft the stone lay cold in its pot and the debt lay uncalled, and the two were kept apart by a man who had stopped trying to reconcile them, which is a kind of keeping and not a kind of peace. Nobody made a lesson of any of it and nobody said the name.

The small keepings stayed small and were not brought forward. Ilva's strip stayed in her sleeve; the three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood beside Hessa Rale's eighth hand with a finger between every pair and were not joined to her slate, did not make a reader, a witness, a cause or a confession; the row stood at five; the seventh green stood a colour at a woman's mouth and the seventh shape a shape at the market's edge, with no eighth of either; the charcoal on the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} stayed unread beneath a finger held on top of the board; the reed-cutter's cord lay where no Court thumb had read it; the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate stayed at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. None of them was a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. None of them was brought into the going of the light to make a row come out even. He kept them as a surgeon keeps a set of instruments laid out on a towel: each in its place, none promoted into use, the space between them part of the arrangement.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. She went to the hearth before she went to the basket, because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing, as it shut on nothing whenever her hand was empty.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

Under the fold the body made its inventory in the old order, as a file is turned out on a towel before the instruments are laid back into it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone past the point where any child under any roof would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third coming in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too close to the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away, and wanted both louder than the surgeon wanted the inventory taken in its order. Both voices argued and neither won, and the argument was the work, and he did not call it a virtue. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and through the finger's-width door the river kept its \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places that did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place kept empty and none invented to fill it; the heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell, and the told figure stood beside the paid with all three Frost arithmetics locked away up the clay road. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between them had no signature on it. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row at five; nine hands with the ninth unread; the burned slate at five with a finger between; the shut-room tellings at two; the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own; the seventh green a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth. Every tray parted by the width of a finger, and nothing on any of them stood up and called itself a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

He turned out the file as he had once turned instruments out on a towel --- the mind's eye going over each place in its order --- and found every gap left gap. Nothing had moved. The held interval lay where it had been laid, the empty interval empty, the missing fifth missing, the two shelf gaps unfigured, the ninth hand nine, the fifth telling five. Nothing had been spent to make a row come out even, and nothing had been promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note, and the thing he took and laid away was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, let go whole on the same low note rather than carried into the carried fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square, or the river's low over lower. It borrowed no house count and supplied no payer. The hundred and eighty-sixth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon in him took a clean satisfaction in one sound placed against its note, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, and the two voices argued and neither won, and the checking was the work and not a virtue. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did, and the gap stayed a gap.

Three hundred and five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the sixth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth telling of the burned slate, no witness under any voice and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. Bone 10 stood four mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved. The river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door; the plate of ice stood clouded with silt on its flat stone out of the path of feet; the crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, and the want went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.
