\daymark{304}{Bone 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} as it had been laid, a tool put away where the next hand would find it without looking, and the fifth of Bone came on the way cold comes into a banked house --- not through a door opened but by the room giving up its heat a board at a time. The dry heat of \place{the Vail kiln-house} had gone thin in the small hours; the coals under their ash kept what they kept and gave the rest to the dark. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and fourth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so, and the count went from three hundred and three to three hundred and four the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam behind it. Six months and one hundred and twenty-four days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. A hundred and eighty-three honest answers stood behind him, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and four days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. Nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

Bone went on without a boundary drawn. The older women named the months by what the weather did and the slatemen named them by how many Account-days had gone into them, and the two habits lay side by side like two hands hauling on one rope, neither set right nor left wrong in so many words. Fifthday had no market, no reading at \place{the Cross} and no bell, and the district kept the face it turns to a morning when nothing is due: a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. Bone was a month of hunts and repairs, and both were directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather and never by a figure; a pot mended twice was a day's work, a hare across a shoulder was a day's carrying, and neither was entered in a slate. Repairs went on under this roof as they went on in every yard of Sill, judged by the arm that did them and the weather they had to stand, and nobody asked what a repair had cost, because the asking would have wanted a figure and no figure lived there.

On \place{the pot board} stood a broken pot whose handle had been mended twice. The repair stood visible, which was the manner of the house: two clean staples sat across the break with the clay worked smooth around them, and the crack itself had not been hidden, running from the rim a finger's length and stopping where the second staple held it. A whole pot would have rung full under the knuckle; this one carried its break in the sound of it, a small flat place in the note where the second mending had taken and the first had not. \person{Sella Vail} said the old saying to the pot and to the fire and never to a person --- if it's worth mending it's worth mending twice, the second time is the real one --- and she said the other one to the pot as well, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled. Repair was not restoration. A mended vessel carries its break; the staples held the handle on and the crack ran beside them and the pot stood on the board holding whatever was put into it. A repair that holds mends nothing anybody will name, and the house went around the fact of it the way it went around \lore{Ash} in a corner, with a look and no word.

The proverb had belonged to a man who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him, and the house had kept his sayings the way it kept his coat on its peg and the hollow in the wheel-seat, by not putting them away. He had been a big man with clay in the creases of his hands and a laugh that ended in a cough; in the house his absence had the weight of an object and not the weight of a story, and the sayings were the lightest part of it, going to the pot and the fire and never to a person. Leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled: the saying had outlived the thing it was true about, for the crack it had been said over had long since been rubbed away, and it cost more now that there was nobody to say it in his own voice. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and four days, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} or gone down it, and the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, circled without a word the way the house went around the pot's visible crack with a look and no figure.

The boy took the inventory in the old order, as he took a wound in the order it bled. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, then temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew the ease cold puts on a face is not ease, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon's knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust two inches; the two facts stood edge to edge and made no doctrine, and he did not call the wanting a virtue.

He checked the keepings in their order and left them where they lay. Three counts in three places that did not become one: the four low pulls under the floor and the silence where the fifth should stand, the river's \lore{water over water} going low over lower under the finger's-width door, and \place{the boards} overhead keeping four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood; agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the last market of Frost with not one hand in two hundred going to a face, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and no bell had come on this morning to lend an ear to. Frost's first, second and third arithmetics, with their three true subtractions, rode locked up the clay road in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between had no signature on it. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, grievance, mending or doctrine, and not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings} or across his own kept gaps; some of those edges nearly touched, and the finger went between them. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. The Frost 14 well-saying stood at three shapings beside \person[Hessa Rale]'s eighth hand, and \person{Ilva}'s ninth hand stood nine with the strip unread in her sleeve and no tenth filed. The burned slate stood at four from \place{Slate Row} and the tellings of the counting room shut in the war at two; the three sayings about a pot stood together with the Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, and no eighth of either came to make the row come out even. He brought none of it in as a lesson and made none of it a sign of the morning. Five were five and not a rule; the mended pot was a pot and not a fifth opening, and no sixth was due on a day that had not earned it.

\scenebreak

Sella went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the mark stayed where it hung. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, laid him on his bed under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, and left the thread where it lay.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note. There was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held whole and let go on the same note, and under the floor the carried fire kept its fourth low pull with the silence where the fifth should have stood. But the thing he took and laid away was the half-beat --- the little interval between the pot set down and Sella going away --- and he set it on a place of its own beside the small intake from the next room, a finger's width between them, neither spent. The half-beat was an object, the flat sound of a pot set down and the space after it before the hand withdrew, and he gave it no figure and made of it no gift. The hundred and eighty-third answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the placing of one small interval beside another with the finger brought down between, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand that could close two inches on a pot handle. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument itself was the work, and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing he had done could reach two inches.

Three hundred and four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-four days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the fifth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no bell's gap folded into a count, no fifth pull supplied, no lesson taken from the mended pot, no figure taken off a repair. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, and Bone 10 stood ahead in the dark beyond the finger's-width door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The pot stood on the pot board with its twice-mended handle turned toward the reaching hand and its crack running beside the staples, the repair standing visible and holding. The half-beat after the set-down remained unfigured, and Sella had gone away without a look toward the bed. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved. The river went low over lower under the finger's-width door, and the hand came down on its note, and the gap stayed a gap.
