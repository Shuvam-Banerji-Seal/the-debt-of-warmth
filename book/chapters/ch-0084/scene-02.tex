The morning went through into the afternoon without a line drawn across it, the dry cold holding in \place{the Clay Yard} the way a coal holds under ash, and the river keeping its note of \lore{water over water} under the finger's-width door. Somewhere past the middle of the day \person{Sella Vail} came for the basket. She lifted with the hand that carries the burn, the little-door shape of it closing first on the wool and then on the weight, and gave the instruction to the fold as she gave it at both ends of every day: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. She said it to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. The basket was a pot taken up and carried and set down, and pots are not told why.

\person[Corin]{The boy} went down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, which is the pace of a woman carrying something that must not be slopped and that must not be seen into. The sun was low and thin and did no more than show the shapes of things; the river wind came up off the sill and found the edges of the fold, and she put the edges right with a thumb that did not look at his face. He took the yard in the order it arrived, as he took a wound in the order it bled: first the smells, wet clay and cooled tallow and the sour mineral breath off a corner that had not been swept in two years, then the sounds, a pot set down flat somewhere, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water beyond the racks, then the places the feet were going to avoid. A child in a basket sees the ground first. He had learned to read a room from the floor up, and the yard was a room with no roof and a great deal of floor.

There were seven places the yard went around, and she went around them all with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned lay beside a finer grey hairline that the older women called \lore{the thaw}, and both were unswept; she set her foot past them as she would set a cup past the edge of a table, and gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them, and the space under the rack was gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. On the stone lip of \place{the yard well} the shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word on it; a thing named from the shape it had left and not from anything that remained. The water in \place{the trough} wore its skin. Far out on \place{the worked strips} a field's empty place stood where a field should have stood, and in the returning line a place stood where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. She looked at each as a woman looks at a stone in a path she knows well, and stepped wide, and entered none of them into anything.

The stayed frost had gone off the north lip of the well on Bone 3 while the yard was looking elsewhere. There was no white on the lip now, only the grey stone showing where the white had been, and the rope had lost its stiffness and gave again under the bucket. The trough kept its skin from edge to edge, and the plate of ice that had been lifted out of it stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded with the silt that had risen into the water as it froze. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them.

He took them apart as he had been taught to take a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. The frost that had gone from the north lip had been a covering laid over a depth, thin and white and gone in an afternoon without anybody seeing it go. The skin on the trough was the same covering laid over another depth, grey where the first had been white, holding its shape while the water under it went on being water. The clouded plate was a piece of the depth itself lifted up and set where no foot went, with the silt that had been in the water held in it the way a clot holds what was moving in a vessel. Three coverings in three places, and he kept them with a finger between every pair. The first was not the second and the second was not the third, and no fourth likeness was made a cause. Nothing under any of them had been paid for by anybody, and he named no payer to any of them. To call them one thing would have wanted a hand under all three, and no such hand stood in the yard.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from \place{the hearth}, spread on the cool stone in the shade the racks threw across it. She set the basket down where no slate lay, lifted a corner of the wool with two fingers and turned the grain beneath it with a touch lighter than the touch she had given it in the morning, and laid the wool back. The grain had been lifted for damp and turned and spread thin and gathered by the handful, changed in state a dozen times and given no figure on any of them. Three readings had gone over it up the road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with their true subtractions locked in, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between had no signature on it. He did not bring any of that in as a lesson. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not laid across the covered grain and not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings} or across his own kept gaps. Some of those edges nearly touched. The finger went between them.

Bone was a month of hunts and repairs, and both stayed directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather and never figures. Men came down from \place{Cold Acre} in the afternoon with hares across their shoulders, the hind legs along one collarbone and the head tucked against the other, hill frost grey in the fur at the throat. A woman with a pot asked what the month's carrying cost and was told the weather, and the men shifted the hares higher and went off toward \place{the north gate road} while the yard went on with its work. Nobody weighed a hare, nobody opened a slate, nobody turned twice to see where it went. The earlier hunter who had waited his turn at the well on Bone 1 was not among them and was not brought back for a second appearance; these were the month's trade, a direction and an arm's knowledge, not a procession, not a thread and not a payment. The boy watched the way the arm that carried a hare settled its weight higher on the shoulder without asking the other arm for help, and filed the carrying where an arm's knowledge goes and not where a cost goes. The cost asked of the month had been answered with a direction that left no entry.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where it could be checked and where it could also slip, and six years stood in the worn thumb-mark while the seventh lay at the lip beyond the mark where the house could not check it. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence about the man who had been sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it, a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped and nobody naming it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and none went down it. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into the room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her unfinished sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row} and was not made a fifth by an afternoon's work; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side that nobody filled; the clean dark line at the Vail corner lay beside the finer grey called the thaw and neither had been swept; the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it went to the pot and the fire and never to a person; the debt against a firing of tiles was circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled, for a debt uncalled can be neither paid nor settled nor done with. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and three days, and the surgeon knew a reconnection paid for by somebody and knew also that the somebody here was not named.

The going of the light came on without anybody calling it, the way all the comings and goings of this month came on, and Sella went to the fire before she went to the basket because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so that the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is not an opening. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, laid him on his bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, and left the thread where it lay.

He lay with one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Heat had drawn back from cheek, then temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew the ease cold puts on a face is not ease, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon's knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust two inches; the two facts stood edge to edge and made no doctrine, and he did not call the wanting a virtue.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note. There was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held whole and let go on the same note, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fourth low pull with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Between the letting-go and the next pull there was an empty interval, a place where nothing stood; beside it he set a held interval, a breath kept whole against its own ending. He brought the finger down between them and took it up again, leaving both where they had been. He did not fill the empty one to make a row come out even and he did not spend the held one to pay for anything. The empty stayed empty and the held stayed held, with the finger's width between, and no payer was named to either.

The hundred and eighty-second answer was a keeping and not a spending. It was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the placing of one interval beside another and the finger brought down and lifted between, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand that could close two inches on a pot handle. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument itself was the work, and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing he had done could reach two inches.

Three hundred and three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and eighty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the fourth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no bell's gap folded into a count, no fifth pull supplied, no collector on the road, no fourth likeness made a cause of the three coverings, no figure taken off the covered grain, no sign taken from the hare gone into the weather. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, and Bone 10 stood ahead in the dark beyond the finger's-width door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The hand came down on the river's low note outside the wool. On the flat stone by the well, out of the path of feet, the plate of ice stood clouded with the silt that had risen into it as it froze, and the white had gone off the well's north lip and the trough kept its skin. The empty interval stayed beside the held interval with the finger's width between, and the gap stayed a gap. \place{The boards} overhead kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved. The river went low over lower under the finger's-width door, and the small hours went on without a boundary drawn.
