\daymark{305}{Bone 6, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} as it had been laid the night before, a tool put away where the next hand would find it without looking, and the sixth of Bone came on the way the fifth had come --- not through a door opened but by \place{the Vail kiln-house} giving up its heat one board at a time. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and fifth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and twenty-five days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; the count had gone from three hundred and four to three hundred and five the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam behind it. A hundred and eighty-four honest answers stood behind him, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and five days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. Nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull.

Nothing that had gone over the boards the day before came in under the roof as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or the first of a rule. The burned-slate word stood at five tellings with a finger between it and the four, and a sixth was not due on a morning that had not earned one; he left it where it stood and did not lay another beside it to make the row come out even. Bone was the month of hunts and repairs, and the repairs were judged by the carrying arm and the weather and never by a figure. A handle mended twice either held or it did not, and the yard would have known which by lifting the pot and not by reading a slate. The house kept its days the same way --- in loaves, in glances, in the space where a loaf should have stood --- and gave nobody a number for them. A morning went on being a morning whether anybody counted it or not.

On \place{the pot board} a pot stood with its handle mended twice, and the repair stood visible. The first mend had been set with a staple the colour of old brass, the second with a wire drawn tighter and left longer than it needed to be, and the two did not pretend to be one repair. Between them a hairline of the original crack showed dark, running a little way under the glaze and stopping where the clay thickened, and somebody had once said that a thing left like that was honest. The saying had gone to the pot and the fire years ago and had never been said to a person. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.} It had improved in the keeping, as all his sayings did, and was very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said it. He had said it first to a pot with no crack in it, which was the sort of thing a saying outlives; the boy knew that about sayings without being told, the way he knew the grain of a board before he had been shown the tool that cut it. A proverb goes on being said after the thing it was true about has gone, and costs more each year, and nobody in this house was about to spend the cost aloud.

The other saying went with it --- \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one} --- and it too was said over the pot and not to anybody standing near it. The boy lay under the fold and read the two mends the way he had once read a wound closed in a place he would not have chosen: by what the hand had done and by what the hand had left. Whoever had set the wire had not tried to bury the first repair under the second, which was a kind of honesty, and the honesty cost nothing because there was nobody there to be told about it. A man who leaves a crack showing expects the vessel to be lifted again, and lifted by somebody who will feel the wire with a thumb before the weight comes on it. The house went on lifting it. Nobody praised the mend and nobody made a lesson of it for anything under this roof, and the pot held the water it was asked to hold, with the dark hairline standing where it had always stood. A repair that holds is not a vessel made whole. A mended vessel carries its break, in the thumb that knows where the wire sits and in the ear that hears the note change where the crack runs.

The heat drew back off \place{the boards} in the small hours and the body made its inventory in the old order, the way a file is turned out on a towel before the instruments are laid back into it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone past the point where any child under any roof would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third coming in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. A cold patient cannot clot. A cold patient will seem better before he is worse, and a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too close to the fire. All of it was exact, and all of it was worth nothing at two inches of air. The infant in him wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread at his mouth wiped away, and wanted both louder than the surgeon wanted the inventory taken in its order. Both voices argued and neither won, and the argument was the work, and he did not call it a virtue. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone, on a man whose chart he had not read and whose name he never learned, and now he could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him. The thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal. He lay with his small intake and his useless head and let the two voices have it out, as he had let them out on the mornings behind and would let them out on the mornings ahead.

Under the floor the fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and through the finger's-width door the river kept its \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places that did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place kept empty and none invented to fill it; the heard bell stood in its square beside the told bell, and the told figure stood beside the paid with Frost's three arithmetics locked away up \place{the clay road} in a box nobody under this roof could reach back into. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between them had no signature on it. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it --- not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's not-doings or the boy's own kept gaps with the finger going between them. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at \place{the rack end}; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the well-saying stood at three shapings beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand; nine hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread and no tenth; the burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between it and the four; the shut-room tellings stood at two; the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own; the seventh green a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth. Every tray parted by a finger's width, and nothing on any of them stood up and called itself a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

First light came without any boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; and her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing, as it shut on nothing whenever her hand was empty. When she lifted the twice-mended pot to set it down she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle, and she said nothing to the pot about the saying and nothing to any person.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. On her way past the peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat held the broader shape with its crease at the left shoulder, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow, and she let both stand as they stood, which is a way of keeping a thing and not a way of mending it. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and the debt against the firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled.

About the yard the morning went on with its work, and the sound of it came to the bed in pieces: a pot set down flat, a broom, the small knock of a thing laid where it belonged, men speaking of the weather and not of the week ahead. Under the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note, and the thing he took and laid away was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, let go whole on the same low note rather than carried into the fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square, or the river's low over lower. It was a restraint and not a payment, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He set it beside the others on the sixth place with a finger between it and them, and it did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon in him took a clean satisfaction in one sound placed against its note, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, and the two voices argued and neither won, and the checking was the work and not a virtue. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did, and the gap stayed a gap.

Three hundred and five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and one hundred and twenty-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the sixth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth telling of the burned slate, no witness under any voice and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved.

The stiff leathery crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist with the Afterdrop waiting beyond, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The pot stood on the pot board with its twice-mended handle and the dark hairline showing, and the house went on around both without a word for either.
