The afternoon came on over \place{the Clay Yard} without anybody calling it, the way a pot goes from soft to leather by giving up its water to the air and not by anybody's instruction. \person[Corin]{The boy} was carried down at the half-pace he was carried at everywhere, with the fold over his ears against the river wind and the instruction going to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. \person{Sella Vail} gave it in the low voice she used for the fire and the pot and the broom, and the wool took it and the wind took it and the basket was set where it was set and told nothing. The light thinned off the terrace and lay in long low bars along \place{the boards}, and the yard went on with the work it had been doing before the light turned, because a day in Bone does not stop to be looked at.

The boards were talking as boards do, which is to say that men and women spoke over them and not to anybody standing under them, and the talk went along the length of them and arrived nowhere. A woman with wet sleeves set a pot down and told the pot what it cost rather than telling the yard. Two men came up off the clay road with a number held shut between them and let it out only as far as the weather. The price of barley went over the boards on the same breath as everything else, and after the price of barley came the weather, and after the weather came nothing at all. It was the grammar of the district and it had been the grammar of the district for as long as anyone under the fold could remember. Nothing in it required a taker and nothing in it supplied one.

It was into this talk, and on the same breath as the barley and the weather, that the voice came. It came up from \place{Slate Row}, which the yard spoke of as a direction and not as a place anybody meant to go that afternoon. There was no owner standing under it. It went along the boards where the neighbours stood with their baskets, spoken over the boards and not to any basket, and it said that a slate had been burned on Slate Row again. No man said whose slate. No man said which room had stood around the flame, or what hand had set it there, or why a slate should be burned at all in a month of hunts and repairs. The voice did not wait for an answer and did not look for one. It went on to the price of barley as if a burning were a thing like any other thing the morning brings, and then to the weather, and then it went off up the terrace toward \place{High Kiln} and was gone, while the yard went on with its work.

The boy lay under the fold and took the saying apart as a surgeon takes a wound apart, laying it open along its edges and refusing from the first cut to let it close on itself and call itself whole. There was a place it came from, and a thing said to have happened there, and a mouth that carried it, and a row of prices it went on to. Each part went where it belonged. None of them came with a hand at the foot of it. A voice from a direction was not a witness. A word going around a yard was not a hand at a flame, and a fifth telling of a burning was not five witnesses to one fire, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and this voice stood a quarter league away and went on to the weather. He found no owner at the bottom of it and no room behind it and no reason under it, and the finding was the absence, as it had been with the other absences he had opened and found empty.

He set it on a place of its own beside the four tellings that stood behind it --- the one that had come in Ember, the telling between, the one in Grain and the one after it --- and put a finger between every pair. It was the fifth telling of the burned slate, and it was still not a third telling of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war, which stood at two and had not gained a third from any burning. It was not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a rule and not paid. Nobody stood anywhere under the voice to be asked, and a saying with no throat is not a witness however often it is said. Five were five and not a rule; a row filled in to make it come out even would be a row forged, and he did not forge it. He left the fifth where he had laid it and did not lay a sixth beside it, because no sixth had earned its place on a day that had not earned it.

The seed was held, not spent. It waited on the far-off day \person{Jorra Vail}'s promise to the men who burn slates would be called in, and it was not a confession and not a payment and would stand in neither account when that day came. Bone 10 and the month's first reading waited five mornings off and would have to carry the fifth telling unchanged, with no hand at the flame and no payer named to it. None of the afternoon went in under the roof as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. The surgeon in him had spent a life opening bodies and closing them again, and he knew the difference between a thing found and a thing put there; the infant in him wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread at his mouth wiped, and wanted it louder than the surgeon wanted the exact placing of a direction on a tray. Both voices argued and neither won, and the argument was the work, and he did not call it a virtue.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and he would not have said it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it.

Whenever the saying went around the boards he grew quieter, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. Nobody told anybody to straighten. Sella gave the fire its instruction and Tarn gave the broom its, and the two went on with their work as if the quiet were a piece of weather they had all agreed to work in. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn; the claim on him gathered interest in the rooms he went into, and he did not pay it and did not refuse it, which in this district is a way of keeping a thing alive. He had once said that a slate burns in four minutes and timed it, and he did not say it now. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The two tellings of the shut room stayed at two beside it and did not become three, and he asked nothing that would join the dust on the wheel-keeper's forearms to a cause.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill with six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh out at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside it with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back; his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone lay cold in its unmarked pot, and Tarn did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up \place{the clay road} or went down it, and nothing in this afternoon turned the seventh year into eight.

Sella carried him down the length of the yard with the instruction going to the wool and the wind, and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither; around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say what had stood there; around the skinned water in \place{the trough}; around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no figure. A thing gone around is still a thing kept, and she kept them the way she kept the fire, by doing the thing that leaves them alone.

The stayed frost had gone off the north lip of \place{the yard well} on Bone 3 while the yard was looking elsewhere, and the trough kept its skin, and the plate of ice lifted off it stood clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all of it weather and entered none of it. The stayed frost had been a covering laid over a depth; the skinned water was the same covering over another depth; the clouded plate was a piece of the depth lifted up and set where no foot would go. The boy kept them on places of their own with a finger between every pair and made no fourth likeness of them to call it a cause, and named no payer to any of them. He brought none of it into the afternoon as a lesson about the slate-burning or the voice over the boards, and the edges that nearly touched were kept from touching.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth. She lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning's, and gave it no figure. Three readings had gone over it in a month that did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and none of that came into the turning of the grain as a lesson or a mending. The arithmetic was locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward High Kiln with one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the box could not be reached back from here and was not. The carrying arm that had brought him up the yard remembered the weight of the basket and not its cost, and it set him down where it set him and gave no reason to the basket.

The going of the light came on without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words. At \place{the hearth} Sella went to the fire before she went to the basket, because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She lifted him up at the half-pace from every open slate, laid him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

In the small hours the heat drew back from the fold and the body made its old inventory in the old order: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as at the end of a line written out for it, with \lore{the Afterdrop} waiting past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the ease cold puts on a face is not ease and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon's knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fourth low pull with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the river kept its \lore{water over water} low over lower under the finger's-width door, and the boards overhead kept four nails and four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places that did not become one, and agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note. But the thing he took and laid away was the little interval between the voice over the boards and the weather's answer to it --- the empty space in which no owner had stood forward and no hand had been named at the flame --- and he set it on a place of its own and did not fill it to make a row come out even. An empty interval kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The hand did not carry it into the fire's missing fifth, or into the bell's gap left in the square, or into the river's low note; it borrowed no house count and supplied no owner to the voice. The hundred and eighty-fourth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the empty place left empty, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, and the two voices argued and neither won, and the argument was the work.

Three hundred and four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-four days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-four honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the fifth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no witness under the voice, no payer beneath the burning. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, and Bone 10 stood ahead in the dark beyond the finger's-width door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing that had moved. The voice had gone off up the terrace while the boards stood where they stood and the yard went on with its work, and the fifth telling was kept apart from the four with a finger between it and them --- five and not a rule, with nobody standing under the saying to be asked and no hand named at the flame. The hand came down on the river's note, and the gap stayed a gap.
