The afternoon gave up its light edge by edge the way a fire gives up its heat, first the far side of the yard and then the near, until the last of it lay a finger's width along the tops of the racks and the stone lip of \place{the yard well} was only a stone again with nothing written on it. \person{Sella Vail} came across when the shade had gone off it and lifted the basket as she lifted a pot filled that must not be slopped, the little-door burn closing first on the handle and then on nothing. She carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} with the basket held level, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, never between a hand and the writing it was going to, never where a figure lay with its face up. A slate stood propped against the rack end that had been open since morning. She went around it as wide as if it had been a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over, and there was no writing on the side of it that \person{Corin} could see, and that was not the reason she went around. He was forty-seven days old and went up the yard looking at the underside of her chin and at the way the smoke lay low behind her, and he kept the shape of her going, which was the shape she went around every keeping in this house.

Under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. “Keep it off him,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the fire, when she had banked it and put the pot where it would take the last of the heat: “That'll do.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and gave every instruction to the fire and the pot and the wool and never to the basket. The door did not quite shut. The room went on being the room he had known for forty-seven days, with the bench by the door and the pot on the bench and the draught going along the floor to the corner, and the gap in it that was the same gap as in the birth room and the death-house two doors down and the Court's room heard through a doorway and the room with the tin above the bed and the Eel House a league upriver. Six rooms with a bench and a pot and a draught and a gap, and he kept them in six places and had not written a cause across any of them, and he did not write one now.

\scenebreak

One crust lay in the fold where two had lain. The spent one from Stone 15 was stiff past the smell of anything and lay where it had always lain, uneaten and unlifted and unnamed, and the other was nowhere. The empty place beside it was the warmth he had most wanted in this life and the one he could least reach. It was not cold that he felt there. He knew the difference between the loss of heat and the loss of a thing, because a man who has stood over a body and known the moment it gave has no need to be told the difference; the one is a number on a slate and the other is a place in the hand. He set the empty place on a tray by itself and did not lay a cause across it. Beside it, in their own places, lay the fifth bundle the kindling did not have, the told bell kept beside and never on the heard bell, the collection that had not come for the woman the brook took with the outline of a sound that did not come, the clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} had turned its corner and been burned and called dealt with, and the shape of a thumb pressed once into wicker. None of them touched. He had gone on keeping them as he had kept the presences, gently and without use, and the surgeon in him registered the empty place with the same exactness he had once brought to a wound and could do no more with it than he could with a wound in a body two paces off.

He did not think who had taken the crust, which was not the same thing as not knowing that there were hands that might have taken it. \person{Sella Vail}'s hand put the fold right every morning and did not look at his face. \person{Alda Reed}'s hand had pressed the fold flat at his shoulder too long and gone away with her tin still shut. \person{Mira Silt}'s hand had put the crust there on Stone 28 and had done necessary things before anybody could make a speech about them. He set the three hands on three places on a tray and would not let the tray become a cause, because he had learned in a room with better light what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for a thing taken and not spoken of, and no word of his own for the taking, and the district would have called the empty place a thing or called it nothing and gone on warm either way. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen again in the afternoon and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted with the small wet sound, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the place where the warmth had been and came down. He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it, which was a joke he had learned to find funny without laughing, a way of laughing that cost less and kept the same blade in it.

Behind him the carried fire kept under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and taking its breath in fours. Four, four and a gap. It had kept that count since the year's fire came up the yard in a clay dish from hands nobody named, and it had kept it through the burning of the ridge and the opening of the slates and the death of a woman in the water, and it would keep it after this house was cold and another house had it. The coal that had gone up the river on the back of a brine-gull two mornings ago was entered in no slate and missed in no account. The fire had not missed it. The house had not missed it. One warmth less was abroad under this roof than had been abroad on the morning of the blessing, and the only person who carried the subtraction was lying on a bed with a fold at his ear and a crust he could not eat. He had once read a chart with the nurse's note turned down and signed the page anyway. He knew what it costs when nobody looks at the note. Here nobody had written a note at all, and the warmth had gone off with a bird and the morning had come anyway, and the house had asked for nothing but that the fire hold.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} came in from the yard with the small bench knife back on its sill and told nobody where it was, because the yard had already been told that morning and a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer. Six years of the seven were behind him and he held the seventh where he always held it, and Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted. His broom went over the road down toward \place{the Fishstairs} once and then again without his once turning his head along it. No collector had been called for the woman the water took and no collector came down that road now, and he did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name. \person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger, and the bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper. Sella had opened her mouth to tell him the fire was low. He moved the damper before the sentence was out and left the end of it where it was, and she gave the rest to the pot. “You'll do,” he told the pot, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and he told the wheel it did not care whose hands were on it, only if they were steady. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed. \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door and the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean and nobody filled it, and the clean dark line where the ridge had turned held nothing and was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept. One of his proverbs went to the pot and another to the fire, and neither went to the basket.

Sella went around the house's number badly, as she had gone around it every day of his life. She moved a loaf along the shelf and set another back where it had been and looked at the space between them, and her lips went around the days and the loaves and the jar on the wall shelf without letting out a figure. There was a space at the end where a loaf should have stood and there was no loaf in it, and she looked at the space twice and then at the fire, and the count in the house went on being a shape and not a number. The boy lay on his bed and kept it exactly. Four on the near row of the kindling and four behind and a space at the end. Four strokes of the bell and four and a gap. Four, four and a gap in the fire, in the boards, in the bench, in the told story, in the space between a woman's thumb and her scar. He had never been given a figure in this house and had never asked for one and had no word in the district's tongue with which to ask. At the bank she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a bank is not an opening. She had touched it once before the kiln was opened in the morning and not at any other hour, and the rule held as exactly as the count held, and neither of them was entered anywhere.

\scenebreak

Outside, the yard went on with its evening in the words it had used all day. A woman at the tubs said the ice would not hold and another said it would, and both went home with the argument unsettled and nobody owning it. \place{the Ilm} ran a little higher under its skin and gave its low note through the boards, and the house called it weather and banked its fire. The boy kept the morning's frost-bloom on the well-lip in its own place with the honeyed ice and the hairline and the higher brook, and let the four places stay four. He had written no cause across them at the well and did not write one now in the warm. A cause is a thing a man can put his hand to, and he had no hand and no word in Grathic for the failure and no standing to ask for either.

No collector had been called for the woman the brook took nine mornings ago, and nobody in this house had found a word for the not calling of one. Nobody had made a doctrine of it. Nobody had stood up in the yard and said that warmth could be left where it fell and the morning would come and that this was a thing a man might do; the yard had simply left it where it fell and the morning had come and gone back to its buckets. The clerk who sat at \place{the Fishstairs} with his slate and soft cuff for a measure nobody had called was still sitting there for all the boy knew, and a woman lay unentered somewhere east of here where the clay had not wiped out of the creases beside her mouth. Those were two facts and they stayed two facts. He did not lay the slate across the body or the body across the slate, because a man who lays three likenesses in a row and asks them to stand for a cause ends by believing the cause and forgetting the likenesses, and he had once been that man in a room with better light and a life on the table. The absence stayed as an interval with the outline of a collection, filed beside the intervals and never among them, and the six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside them and never among them, and no seventh came into the room.

The nearly full note of Ilmen 6 lay on its own tray, advanced and not paid, neither a hearing nor the third thing. Five unjoined pauses lay in five places and did not join. Two cords under a woman's apron and a \lore{knot-string} in a hut a league upriver stood on separate boards and did not touch, and the knots on none of them had been counted by anybody who could enter them. The word said low into a basket at the Weir lay on its own tray as a saying, with the shape of a double turn and a tucked tail beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and the woman who had said it was watching still and would get no answer from this bed. He had no hand to lift toward the cord and no word for the weight of a thumb and no standing to ask what any of it counted. So he kept it. That was the whole of the work available to him, and he had done it every day of his forty-seven days, and it had never once bought him a hand.

The light went off the yard and the evening came on the way it always came on, at the edges of the room first and the middle last, so that the fire was the last thing to go dark and then not dark at all but a few red eyes under the ash. The boy lay on his bed under the Vail roof, forty-seven days old, with one crust in the fold where two had lain and an empty place beside it that he had wanted more than he had wanted the truth and could not reach any more than he could reach the truth. Behind him the carried fire took its breath in fours and missed one, and the coal that had gone up with the gull was entered in no account and missed in none, and the district had entered the year and had not entered him. Nothing had been joined, paid or explained. Nothing had been solved. The count went on. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down.
