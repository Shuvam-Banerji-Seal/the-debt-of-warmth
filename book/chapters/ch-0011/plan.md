## Scene 1 — Below the North Bridge

- **Day and time:** Day 45, Ilmen 16, Y.H. 813, Sixthday, first thin light at the Fishstairs below the north bridge, the morning after the Fish Blessing.
- **Place:** The Fishstairs and the steps running down to the landing that is not a step; the shade under the north bridge; the eel stalls; the river road glimpsed up toward the Weir.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-five days old, carried in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Mira Silt (slime on her sleeves, silt at her neck, head held right, eel-bones), Orra Weir (down from the Weir with eels; rope-hands, uncovered eel scars, grey streak, silt-and-tallow smell, knot-string at her waist), the collector's clerk set for the uncalled measure (walk-on, soft cuff, slate, no dialogue to the basket), rack-women and eel buyers (walk-ons). Dessa dead and stays dead; the clerk is not Marra and not the Court-table clerk.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Open on the day, not on a thought and not on a recap: the boy is carried down the river road in the first light and set below the north bridge where a pot would be set, out of the draught, the fold turned right under his chin. A **brine-gull** goes off upriver with a coal from somebody's fire that is not yet cold. He notes the thing as a payer and a taking --- warmth leaving the carried fire's account with no slate entered --- and writes no doctrine over it (T088, T071/T072, birds motif). Opening paragraph at least three lines for the drop cap.
  2. The clerk set at the Fishstairs for the uncalled measure is at the steps with his cuff and slate; the hand goes to the slate before the eyes go to the stalls. The boy files the order on a tray of its own: not Marra, not the grey-fingered Drawing collector, not the soft-cuffed clerk at the Court's table (T090, T080, T047).
  3. Orra comes down with eels and works among them; the boy can now read the **shape** of the forty-first knot --- double turn, tail tucked under, tied left over right, the extra weight of a thumb setting it --- but not its name, and no Grathic word for it. He keeps the shape on its tray with the word *forty* still a saying beside it. **The word is not spoken again** and Orra watches him watching and says nothing further (T024, T082, T071/T072).
  4. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in her neck and her head held to the right where the top of her left ear had been. She answers a question with a question and her own answer; eel-bones move from hand to hand while she decides whether to lie and one is put away when the deciding is done. Shifting a basket shows the grey cord with the second twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted; Orra's board and Mira's board stay side by side and do not touch (T059, T025, T077).
  5. The six hearings stay six --- five from four sources and the sixth from a person --- and no seventh comes; the nearly-full note stays on its own tray, advanced and not paid (T061, T082).
  6. Both crusts are still in the fold this morning, neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third. The smoky one gives a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turns off the water; the body wants it and cannot reach it (T086, T060).
  7. The body humiliates him exactly: the thread of drool freezes, the head goes where it wants with the small wet sound, cold comes at cheek then temple, and the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth that is not within two paces and comes down (T071/T072).
  8. Sella turns the basket so the sun is off his face, gives the instruction to the wool and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*; she stands a half-pace off a slate lying open on a stall and does not set the basket near it (T058, T084).

- **Emotional turn:** He has gone from holding a shape he could not read to reading it as a shape --- the double turn, the thumb's weight --- and still cannot spend any of it. The woman who tied it watches to see what he will do with a word she will not say twice, and a coal has gone up with a bird out of the fire nobody pays for.
- **Sensory anchors:** first thin light under the north bridge; a brine-gull with a coal not yet cold; silt and cold fish and tallow smoke; rope-hands and pale eel scars; grey wool and the double turn of a knot; eel-bones flicked and put away; a cuff gone soft from washing and a hand to a slate; a thin breath of barley smoke off the water; drool freezing; the hand up an inch and down.
- **Threads advanced:** T024 (shape of the knot legible, word unspoken, watch continued), T059 and T025 (two cords on separate boards, Nine Pots unvisited), T090 and T080 (Fishstairs clerk filed apart), T088 (coal carried off unentered), T061 and T082 (six hearings, nearly-full still on its tray), T077, T086, T060, T058, T084, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Carries the coal the gull took toward the unnamed payer of the carried fire and, far off, the Hearth as a wound (T088); carries Orra's readable shape toward the cord read as a chart years hence (T024) and the watched reaction toward the long vigil; keeps both cords side by side toward the district's private accounts. Pays nothing; joins nothing; the word is not spoken twice.
- **Exact last image:** The gull gone upriver with the coal, the boy in the bridge's shade with the fold at his chin, the knot's double turn laid on its own tray beside an unspoken word, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Scene 2 — The House's Number

- **Day and time:** Day 45 continued, Ilmen 16, late afternoon and evening under the Vail roof (no new `\daymark`; the day opened in Scene 1).
- **Place:** The Clay Yard and the road up from the Fishstairs; the Vail kiln-house (bench, fire, hollow wheel-seat, salt coat, clean dark line, kiln mouth); the wheel-shed and the doorway kept a finger's width open.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-five days old, carried and set on his bed), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and proverb; rack-neighbours on the yard (walk-ons). Alda, Mira, Orra and Berr offstage.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella carries him up the yard and sets him on his bed under the roof, not in the Debt Loft, and puts the fold right without a look at his face. She gives every instruction to the fire, the pot and the wool, never to the basket, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I* (T058, T060, T084).
  2. She goes around the month's days and the household's stores the way she has always gone around a number: a loaf moved along the shelf, a glance at the jar on the wall shelf, her lips counting where no figure is spoken. Nobody says the stores are thinner than they were. The boy keeps the count exactly beside her bad keeping (T071/T072, T077, T060).
  3. Both crusts still lie in the fold --- the spent one stiff past smelling, the smoky one giving its thin breath --- neither eaten, lifted or named, and no third. Do not let either go missing yet; the going happens on Day 46 (T086).
  4. Sella touches the widow's mark once before she opens the kiln, holds it a breath, lets go, and does not touch it again at the dusk bank, because a bank is not an opening (T079).
  5. The carried fire still lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. The count is four, four and a gap. The coal the gull took that morning is entered nowhere and missed in no account (T088, T065, T083).
  6. Tarn's small knife is on its sill and he tells the empty yard it can be checked, six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back; Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifts. His broom goes over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, over and over, without once turning, and no collector goes down it. He still wants the thaw late, because a late thaw puts off the paying (T036, T009, T085, T047).
  7. Jorra comes in from the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms, no grey dust past the bend of the little finger; the bent finger leads at the damper, which he moves before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low. He tells a pot *You'll do*, tells the wheel it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady, does not say the back room is cold, and does not look toward the bed (T035, T061).
  8. Garl's salt-stiff coat keeps a broader man's shape on its peg, the hollow seat keeps its twenty-year lean, the clean dark line where his ridge turned is not swept, and both proverbs go to the pot and not to the basket (T009, T013).

- **Emotional turn:** A house keeps itself by objects and half-sentences around a boy who has counted its every breath; today one warmth went out of it with a bird and no one entered the loss. The warmth that cannot be refused is becoming a warmth nobody will name when it is gone.
- **Sensory anchors:** the fold put right without a look; a loaf moved along a shelf; a mark held one breath before the opening; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; four, four and a gap; a knife announced to an empty yard; Ash in knuckles no scrubbing lifts; a broom over the collector's road without turning; bone-coloured slip and a bent finger at the damper; a salt-stiff coat on its peg.
- **Threads advanced:** T058, T060, T086 (both crusts still present), T079, T088 (coal unentered), T065, T036, T076, T009, T013, T035, T085, T047, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** The late thaw wanted because it puts off paying, toward the levy and the Breaking (T085); the collector's road swept without turning and the uncalled debt left where it lies, toward the first heresy and Garl's account (T009); the loan that can hear going on uncalled under one roof (T076). Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with two crusts still in the fold, the carried fire behind him keeping four, four and a gap, the coal gone up with the gull unentered, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Scene 3 — The Empty Place in the Fold

- **Day and time:** Day 46, Ilmen 17, Y.H. 813, Seventhday, from the morning's fire through dusk under the Vail roof and along the yard.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house (hearth, bench, bed under the roof), the drying racks and the yard path; the wheel-shed edge; the road down toward the Fishstairs.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-six days old, carried and fed from the house's dish), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed (short: at mid-morning with soap-and-milk, birth-slates under her arm and the tin in her bodice); Garl as coat, hollow seat and proverb; rack-women and a neighbour at the path (walk-ons). Mira and Orra offstage; Dessa dead and stays dead.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The household's stores are thinner than they were and nobody says so. Sella feeds him barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, two fingers behind his ear holding his head where it wants to go, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and his mouth not wiped; she gives the instruction to the fire and the wool, never to the basket, and says nothing about who paid for the warmth (T060, T058, T044).
  2. Alda comes under the roof smelling of soap and warm milk, lays the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold flat at his shoulder too long, touches the tin once inside her bodice without opening it, lays an exact date where a wonder should stand and goes on giving dates after there has been no question to stop. She does not say the woman's name and does not open the tin. The lie told for mercy is six days old and curdles against a death nobody entered; no new note is written (T089, T023, T008, T073, T084). Keep her short and let the gestures carry her.
  3. Tarn sets the small knife on its sill and tells the empty yard it can be checked; six years are counted and the seventh is held where he always holds it. He will not count it. His broom goes again over the road to the Fishstairs without turning, and no collector goes down it. He does not say the omission has a name (T036, T009, T047).
  4. Jorra works from the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip and the bent little finger leading at the damper ahead of Sella's instruction; he tells a pot it will do, does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed or the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed (T035, T084).
  5. **One crust is gone.** The smoky one Mira put into the fold on Stone 28 --- the one the boy has wanted for weeks and that still gave a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turned --- is gone from the fold. The spent one from Stone 15 remains stiff where it lay, uneaten, unlifted, unnamed. Nobody says who took it, nobody says that one is missing, nobody says it was eaten or given to the fire or the eels. Do not solve it; do not let Sella, Alda or Mira account for it; the reader must be able to suspect every woman in the yard, including the girl who put it there. This is the first edge of T086's payoff (T086, T060).
  6. The boy wants the missing warmth with the whole appetite of a body carried all morning and fed on a dish that is not his; the empty place in the fold is felt with the exactness that has nothing to do with temperature. He has no hand to lift to it and no word in Grathic for the taking (T060, T044, T071/T072).
  7. At the dusk bank Sella turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the widow's mark, because a bank is not an opening. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap (T079, T088, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** The one warmth in the fold he could smell and never reach is gone, and nobody will name the absence. A kindness that could not be answered has become an absence that cannot be answered either; the surgeon registers the empty place as exactly as he once registered a wound, and can do nothing with the knowledge.
- **Sensory anchors:** gruel thinned with milk from the house's dish; two fingers behind the ear; a spoon wiped on a sleeve and a mouth not wiped; soap-and-milk and the tin touched once inside a bodice; a birth-slate laid writing-down; an exact date over a wonder; a knife announced to an empty yard; Ash in knuckles; bone-coloured slip and the bent finger at the damper; the empty place in the fold where the smoky crust had lain; coals turned with a broken pot.
- **Threads advanced:** T086 (first edge paid --- one crust gone, unsolved), T060 and T044 (unanswerable kindness become unanswerable absence), T089 and T023 (tin touched once, unopened), T008 and T073 (lie curdling, name and number unsaid), T036, T035, T009, T079, T088, T065, T058, T084, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Pays the first edge of the two-crusts thread planted on Stone 28 and carried through ten chapters (T086); keeps the deeper payoff --- a gift entered on no slate --- for years hence. Plants the missing crust as one more keeping no slate can enter, beside Alda's turned note, Mira's cords and Dessa's tin. Pays nothing else; solves nothing; no quest.
- **Exact last image:** The boy on his bed with one crust in the fold where two had lain, the empty place at his ear that he had wanted most and cannot reach, and the carried fire behind him taking its breath in fours and missing one.

## Scene 4 — Frost on the Well-Lip

- **Day and time:** Day 47, Ilmen 18, Y.H. 813, Eighthday, before the smoke clears of the morning fires to midday at the yard well and the drying racks.
- **Place:** The yard well and its stone lip; the drying racks and the Slip Brook behind them; the road down toward the Fishstairs and the Cross spoken of; the Weir's plank walk heard of, not reached.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-seven days old, carried and set in the shade at the well), Sella Vail, Tarn (one silent yard-beat with the broom), Berr Clay (short, with the red wool cord folded under his coat and green of glaze at one corner of his mouth), well-neighbours and rack-women (walk-ons); the Fishstairs clerk present only as word brought up with the neighbours. Dessa dead; Alda, Mira, Orra and Jorra offstage.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Frost-bloom stands on the stone lip of the well where the Warm has lately left --- a white writing no slate entered. A woman lets the bucket down and says the ice will go soft before its time; another says it will hold; the argument ends in weather, which lets the yard go home warm without owning the day (T085, T013, T077).
  2. A number goes around from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and is never given out. Berr Clay, short, wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, keeps a figure he will not give and takes himself out of his own sentence with *there'll be a slate at the watch*, saying the house where another man would say my account. He does not look into the basket and goes off with the box in his house still locked (T077, T041, T047, T084).
  3. Word comes up with the neighbours that the clerk is still at the Fishstairs with his slate and soft cuff for a measure nobody called. The boy files the order on its own tray --- not Marra, not the Court-table clerk, not the Drawing collector --- and the boy files the absence of a draw for the woman in the brook beside the intervals, as a thing with the outline of a sound that did not come (T090, T080, T009, T082).
  4. The weak board in the plank walk at the Weir is heard of from a weir woman with salt white at her wrists: a board that will not bear a man's weight when the river comes up. It is set beside the missing Fishstairs step and the hairline that stops short of the edge; T083 held against a river beginning to stir, not trodden and not made into an accident (T083, T085, T082).
  5. Behind the racks the Slip Brook runs clay-heavy a little higher than it should; the Singing Sill gives its low note before its time and the yard calls it weather. The boy keeps the honeyed ice, the opened hairline, the higher brook and this frost-bloom in four places and does not let the four places touch (T085, T083, T071/T072).
  6. Sella carries him at a half-pace from every open slate, sets him in the shade out of the draught and gives the instruction to the wool and the bucket; she says nothing about where the number went or who paid for the warmth in the well-bucket's wake (T058, T060, T071/T072).

- **Emotional turn:** The thaw writes its signature in frost on a stone where a slate should stand, and the district reads it as weather and goes on. The boy can name none of the four shapes he keeps, and the number passes him once more unspendable.
- **Sensory anchors:** frost-bloom on the well-lip; a bucket going down and a hand kept off the lip; a number from fist to mouth to shoulder; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; green of glaze at a mouth; a soft cuff and a slate for a measure nobody called; a board that will not bear a man's weight; the low note of the Singing Sill under it all; weather, weather, weather.
- **Threads advanced:** T085 (frost-bloom, ice going soft, Singing Sill early), T083 (weak board heard of, held against the stirring river), T077 and T041 and T047 (number withheld, Berr's passive grammar, box locked), T090 and T080 (Fishstairs clerk still set, filed apart), T009 and T082 (omission filed as an interval), T013, T058, T060, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** The frost-bloom where the Warm has lately left, the ice going soft before its time and the weak board held against a river beginning to stir, toward the year's Breaking in Silt and the later day the river puts the Fishstairs back out of line (T083, T085). The clerk still set for an uncalled measure toward the standing charge on a district that has already spent its dead (T090). Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The boy set in the well's shade with the fold at his chin, frost writing on the stone lip above him, the number gone past him from fist to mouth to shoulder and given to nobody, and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the bucket and coming down.

## Scene 5 — Four, Four and a Gap

- **Day and time:** Day 47 continued, Ilmen 18, afternoon into dusk under the Vail roof (no new `\daymark`; the day opened in Scene 4).
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house: the bed under the roof, the hearth and the banked coals, the shelf with the clay jar, the clean dark line, Garl's peg and hollow seat; the Debt Loft above the wheel-shed kept apart.
- **Present:** Corin (forty-seven days old, carried home and set on his bed), Sella Vail; Tarn and Jorra silent in the shed and at the damper; Garl as coat, seat, line and proverb. All others offstage; no recap of the day's visitors.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella carries him up the Clay Yard at dusk, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and puts the fold right without looking at his face. She gives the instruction to the fire and the wool and says *the house* (T058, T060, T084).
  2. The single crust lies where two had lain. The empty place beside it is the warmth he most wanted and cannot reach; he keeps it with the missing fifth of the kindling, the told bell beside the heard bell, the absence with the outline of a collection, the clean dark line and the shape of a thumb on wicker, and writes no cause (T086, T082, T071/T072, T044).
  3. The carried fire keeps under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four: four, four and a gap. The coal that went up with the gull on Day 45 is entered nowhere and missed in no account; the fire did not miss it and neither did the house (T088, T065, T083).
  4. Sella goes around the house's number badly in loaves, glances and the space where a loaf should stand while the boy keeps the count exactly; no figure is spoken in the house (T071/T072, T077, T060).
  5. No collector has been called for the woman in the brook, nobody has found a word for the omission, and nobody has turned the absence into a doctrine. The clerk's slate at the Fishstairs and the unentered body stay two facts that do not touch (T009, T090, T082).
  6. ## Pull into Chapter 12

Day 47 closes with one crust gone and nobody saying who took it; a coal gone up with a brine-gull and entered in no account; frost written on the well-lip and called weather by noon; the weak board at the Weir heard of but not yet trodden; the clerk still at the Fishstairs with a slate for a measure nobody called; no collector called for the woman in the brook and no word found for the omission. Nothing has been joined, paid or explained beyond a shape that can now be read and a taking nobody will name, and the count still stands four, four and a gap.

Chapter 12 opens on **Day 48, Ilmen 19, Y.H. 813, Ninthday**, at the yard well: a frost-bloom on the stone lip where the Warm has lately left, the oiled paper slack in its frame, and a neighbour who used to say the ice held now saying nothing at all. The boy is forty-eight days old, still carried in the basket with the fold at his chin, and his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the bucket and comes down. Forward, not closed.
