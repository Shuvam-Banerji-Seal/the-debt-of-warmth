\daymark{46}{Ilmen 17, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

The morning came on in the order it always came, cold at the edges of \place{the Vail kiln-house} and warmer in the middle where the fire kept its own small day, and \person{Sella Vail} had woken that fire before she woke anybody else. She set a dish on the bench to take its warmth and fed \person{Corin} from the house's own dish with a spoon she wiped on her sleeve, barley gruel thinned with milk that came from a house that was not his and would not be named. Two fingers behind his ear held his head where it wanted to go; when it went too far the small wet sound came out of him and the fingers brought it back, and she never once looked at his face. She gave the instruction to the fire and the wool, as she gave every instruction, and never to the basket. “Keep it off him,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and the boy, who understood every word, understood that he was not the one being spoken to, and that the warmth going into him was written in no account that any officer would ever open.

He knew the arithmetic of a body taking food before he knew the words for it. The gruel went in warm and the milk left a film on his lower lip and the film cooled while the spoon was still in the dish. He wanted more of it and could not ask for more, and the wanting was a small animal thing with no grammar in it. The surgeon in him noted the heat of the spoon against his lip and the rate at which the film went cold, and the infant in him wanted the breast and wanted the crust and had neither, and the two of them went on arguing in the same small body as they had argued every morning of his forty-six days. He was forty-six days old, could not hold up his own head, and had once reduced a shoulder in the dark. Both things were true. The second was the one that was louder.

Before she lifted the kiln lid she put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, the thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and held it for one breath and let go. It was a rule that held as exactly as any rule in the house, and the boy kept the gesture on its own tray, as he kept all the house's gestures, because a rule and the reason for a rule were two objects and he had long ago learned to keep such objects apart. She did not touch it again. She would not touch it at the dusk bank, because a bank is not an opening. The carried fire lay behind him under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and taking its breath in fours, and a coal had gone up the river with a bird yesterday and been entered nowhere and missed in no account. One warmth less was abroad in the district than had been abroad on the day before, and the boy on his bed kept the subtraction, which was the whole of what he could do with it.

\scenebreak

\person{Alda Reed} came under the roof at mid-morning with the smell of soap and warm milk on her and the birth-slates under her arm. She had come from a birth or from the washing of one; the lye sat under the soap and the milk had gone cold under river silt, and she carried the two smells the way she carried the slates, without comment and without setting either down where a body could mistake it for comfort. She laid the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold. That was the order she reached in, the record before the person, and the boy lying on his bed watched the order of the reaching and kept it on the tray where he kept the other hands that went to the slate first --- the midwife, the neighbour at the well, the clerk behind the Court table, the man who had priced him, and a woman writing in a neat dark hand at the edge of a flare light, none of them joined to any of the others.

She pressed the fold flat at his shoulder longer than a fold required. Her hand was steady, scrubbed to the quick, with clay still under the nails, and it stayed a breath too long at the place where the wool met the edge of his ear, as if she were smoothing a thing that would not stay smooth. Under the shawl her other hand went once inside her bodice and touched the little tin and came away without opening it. She did not open it. She had never opened it in company and she did not open it now. Where the wool had ridden back at his wrist the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} showed, and she crossed two fingers at the corner of it before she knew she had done it, and covered the sign with a date nobody had asked for. “Stone seven,” she said. “The account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out.”

She went on giving dates after there was no question left to stop her. The cooper's seventh in \place{Mud End}. A potter's third child in \place{Stonemouth}, now a carter. The last high water, which was the date she gave where other women gave an opinion. To Sella's “he lies quiet” she said “Warm and breathing --- that's all I've set down,” which was exact and was not true, and she said it in the tone of a woman stating the weather. She did not say the woman's name. She did not say the third day. The lie she had told for mercy had curdled all the way through to a death nobody had entered, and no living person could now correct it, and she went on laying dates over it the way she had laid them over a cradle, going on after the question had stopped because dates were a handle on a thing too large to carry. She wrote no new note this morning. She turned to go with the birth-slates still writing-down under her arm, and the boy, who had watched the order of the reaching, could not have handed any of it back.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} had the small bench knife back on its sill before the middle of the morning and told the empty yard where it was, unasked, in the tone another man would use to state the weather. “It's on the sill,” he said. “You can check.” He looked at the kindling stack without touching it, four bundles along the near row and four behind and a space at the end where a fifth should have been, and took his eyes off the space as a man takes his eyes off a thing he has no leave to look at. Six years of the seven were behind him and he had counted every one. The seventh he held back, the one entry in this house that this house could not check. Grey \lore[the Ash]{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles the colour of the ridge that had turned the corner and been burned and called dealt with, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what light there was and gave it back.

His broom went again over the road that runs down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, over and over, without once turning to look along it. That is the road a man goes down when a house wants a collector. No collector went down it this morning and nobody in the house sent one and Tarn did not go. He swept where he had swept and swept again, and when Sella told him he had done well he stopped altogether for two breaths before he said “Yes, house” and went on, because a kindness he could not enter in any account was a kindness he could not pay back, and he had stopped trying to reconcile the two. He agreed with the people who wanted the thaw early and with the people who wanted it late and kept wanting the late one all the same, because a late thaw puts off the paying. He did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name. The loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, and the boy on his bed kept Tarn's silence beside his own and did not let the two touch.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked into the creases past the bend of his little finger. The bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper. Sella had begun to tell him the fire was low; he moved the damper before she had finished and left the end of her sentence where it was, and she shut her mouth on it and gave the rest to the pot. He spoke to the pot and not to the person on the bed. “You'll do,” he said, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He told the wheel what he had told it a hundred times, that it did not care whose hands were on it, only if they were steady, and the saying had worn smooth as the seat worn hollow on the left by twenty years of another man's leaning. Of the room off the shed he said nothing, and the door of it kept its finger's width of open. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept the shape of a broader man on its peg behind the door and the hollow seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years that nobody was allowed to fill, and the clean dark line where the ridge had turned its corner held nothing and was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept. His proverbs went to the fire and the pot and never to the basket. Of the boy under the roof the house said nothing, and the sentence Jorra had spoken on the day the boy arrived was sealed as tightly as it had been on the hour it was spoken, and nobody had undone it.

\scenebreak

The wind turned off the water in the early afternoon and the smell did not come.

It had come for weeks when the wind turned that way. The spent crust from Stone 15 lay stiff where it had lain and was gone past smelling of anything, but the other one, the smoky one that \person{Mira Silt} had put into the fold on Stone 28, had given a thin breath of barley smoke whenever the air moved off \place{the Clay Yard}, and the breath had come and gone and the boy had known the shape of it as a man knows the shape of a wound he has dressed every day. He had wanted that crust with the whole appetite of a morning carried from before light and fed on a dish that was not his. He had smelled it and could not reach it, and the two paces between the warmth and his hand had been as fixed as the day he was born.

Now the wind turned and there was no breath of barley. He lay still and let the absence come to him the way he let a symptom come, without flinching from it and without dressing it in a cause. One crust lay in the fold where two had lain. The smoky one was gone. It had not been eaten beside him and it had not been lifted in his sight and nobody had said it was gone. Nobody had said it had been given to the fire or to the eels or to a hungry woman, and nobody had said who had taken it. The spent one remained stiff where it lay, uneaten and unlifted and unnamed, and no third had come into the fold. Two had become one, and the one that was left was the one that had been there longest and was worth the least.

The surgeon in him registered the empty place with the exactness he had once brought to a wound, and he could do nothing with the knowledge. He had kept the crust on its tray as a kindness that could not be answered, and a kindness that cannot be answered cannot be refused. Now the kindness had gone and the absence could not be answered either, and the two things were the same shape and neither of them had a hand he could put out to either. He kept the empty place beside the coal that had gone up with the bird and beside the fifth breath the fire did not take and beside the sound that had not come when the yard lifted a woman out of the brook, and he wrote no cause across any of them. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for a thing taken and not spoken of, and no word of his own for the taking. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the place where the warmth had been and came down.

He did not think who had taken it. That was not the same as not knowing there were hands that might have. Sella's hand put the fold right at his ear every morning and did not look at his face. Alda's hand had pressed the fold flat at his shoulder too long this morning and had gone away with the tin still shut. Mira's hand had put the crust there on Stone 28 and had done necessary things before anybody could make a speech about them. He set the three hands on three places on a tray and did not let the tray become a cause, because he had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. Whoever had taken it had taken it quietly, which was a kind of keeping, and the yard kept its keepings where no slate would think to look, and the boy kept the absence where he kept all the things that had happened to him without happening to anybody else. Nobody said the crust was missing. The house went on.

\scenebreak

At the dusk bank Sella turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a bank is not an opening. She gave the instruction to the fire and let the pot have the end of it, and she said the house where another woman would have said I, and she did not count the warmth going off up the river yesterday or the warmth gone out of the fold today, and she did not say either one was gone. The carried fire kept under blind ash and the back of the broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The gap was the part nobody had thought to enter. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner held nothing and was not swept, and Garl's coat held a broader man's shape on its peg and his hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean, and the two proverbs went to the pot and not to the basket.

The light went off the yard and the evening came on in the order it always came, at the edges of the room first and the middle last. The boy lay on his bed under the Vail roof, forty-six days old, with one crust in the fold where two had lain and the empty place at his ear where the warmth he had most wanted had been. He had no hand to lift to it and no word in the district's tongue for the taking and no standing to ask who had done it. The spent crust lay stiff where it had always lain and the smoky one lay nowhere and neither was named. Behind him the carried fire took its breath in fours and missed one, and the coal that had gone up with the gull was entered in no account and missed in none. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the emptiness and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid or explained. The count went on.
