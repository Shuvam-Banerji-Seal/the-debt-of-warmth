## Chronicle

On the twenty-first of Seed the planting entered its last tend, the hands in the strips counted in glances and handfuls, never a figure. Sella Vail carried the boy at a half-pace from every open slate and gave the bucket, broom, pot and fire their instructions, never the basket. At the loaf shelf a neighbour reached past a bare board, paused, closed on the loaf beyond and went off saying the old space at the row's end came in with the planting and this was the price of a wet thing set down wrong. She made a cause of two bare boards and never saw the empty place at the child's ear. Sella gave nobody a figure and did not touch the widow's mark.

At the going of the light he lay on his bed, the thread at his mouth frozen. He turned out his file and found it unchanged: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word forty unspoken with its readable turn, four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth, the shut-room tellings joined to no kiln, the damper's bite filed as an object, the loan uncalled. One crust lay stiff in the fold; the shelf kept two gaps in a room that did not touch it. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and said the house would keep it.

On the twenty-second men came before first grey into the finger's-width-open room off the wheel-shed, and Jorra Vail gave them no name. He came through with grey slate dust ground into both forearms past the bent finger and put that finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, so the household straightened around his silence. Tarn's stroke checked half a beat behind him and went on; the two silences stood in one room with no hand joining them. At the well Berr Clay read one in twelve and said a number goes into a slate, not a strip; a man brought a number up out of a fist and put it back in his sleeve, and it arrived nowhere.

On the twenty-third, at the Fishstairs, Mira Silt answered her own answer to whether the run would be thin and put her thumb to the grey cord when a buyer named a figure; shifting a creel showed a second cord twisted twice, neither knot counted. The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing for an uncalled measure. Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates on the bench before turning to the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower unknowing and covered the sign with a dead woman's date; warm and breathing was exact and not true. At nightfall the file was unchanged. The river's note came through the gap and the fire's missing breath through the floor. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. One hundred and twelve stood unspent, spoken by nobody.
