The light went off the yard the way it went off everything in \place{Low Kiln}, at the edges first and in the middle last, and when it had come down to a finger's width along the rack-tops \person{Sella Vail} came across to the basket at the edge of the light and lifted it as she lifted a pot filled to the lip. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed first on the handle and then on nothing, the way it closed on nothing whenever her hand had nothing to hold, and she carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, neither between a hand and the writing it was going to nor where a figure lay with its face up. A slate stood propped at the rack's end with its writing turned away; she went around it as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with the same width, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody. \person[Corin]{the boy} rode level under the fold, one hundred and ten days old and awake to every jar of the carrying, looking at the underside of her chin and at the smoke that lay low behind her with no lift in it. He kept the shape of her going, which was the shape she went around every keeping in this house.

Under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She did not say the boy, and she did not look down. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and she left it where it was, because nobody in this house had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left; a thing set down and left is a thing still kept, which is not the same as a thing tended. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody put it right and nobody made an event of the sound. Cold came in its known order, cheek, then temple, then the wrist where the wool rode back from the bare place, and he could no more alter one step of it than he could keep the wool on the wrist. He was a surgeon who could reduce a shoulder in the dark and could not keep a blanket on his own body, and the joke of it had cost somebody else from the first morning and had never once been funny enough to say aloud.

Presently, with the room gone quiet around the fire, he turned out the private file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before he opens a field: each thing in its place, handle out, edge in, nothing that could roll. He had no hands for it and no towel, but the order was the order, and an order kept without a hand is still an order. Every object lay where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood at six --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, stood beside the five and never among them. There was no seventh. He had listened for one all day in the ordinary work and in the going of the light, and the ear that held six had added nothing.

On another square of the towel lay three near-full answers, advanced and not paid, neither hearings nor the third thing; he had no hand to tap the pots and ask them to say it again. On a third lay the three intervals, Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25, each a place where a sound had stopped and not come back. The word forty lay on a place of its own, unspoken, with the readable double turn beside it --- a cord twisted twice beneath a grey one, tail tucked, tied left over right --- and the weight of a thumb on wicker set apart from both, a hand reaching into a pocket-place in another life and coming back with wool. He knew \lore{Grathic} in neither speech nor writing and had no right to the word and no way to spend it; the shape was legible to him without its name, and a word and the thing it names are two objects, and he had kept them on two places since \place{the Weir}. Four low things kept their four places --- the told bell out of the dark, the heard bell over the water, the early note out of the stone, the kiln's missing breath --- and the river's note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing. The two tellings of the shut counting room lay joined to no kiln, whatever the shape of them; the double bite of \lore{the damper} lay filed as an object, mercy and mutilation in one piece of iron with no Grathic word for both; and the loan that can hear lay where it had lain, uncalled.

Into this order he put the day without entering any of it. The neighbour's one-breath naming at the shelf went on a place beside the two other tellings and the watcher's late turn, a third shaping of the thing that waited for a far-off hand and was not his; the reaching hand that had gone to the board before the eyes went to the boy went on its own square, exact, complete and worth nothing, a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody had opened. Berr had not yet come up the clay road and the figure he would read was not yet read, so the day's arithmetic was still a direction with no figure at the foot of it; the seed stood in the strips with the hands in it counted in glances and handfuls, and the last tend went on being a tend and not a number. He added the day and spent none of it. The file lay complete and useless as a set of instruments laid out on a towel in a room with no patient and no light to work by; there was no field here to open and no person who would let him carry any of it to a door.

His body kept its own account and kept it badly. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third, and \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room kept two gaps in a room this one did not touch. Below the sill his foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the two words never let into the same sentence; he had kept them apart for one hundred and ten days and would keep them apart now, because a cold foot and a cold day laid in one sentence make a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent two lives declining to become. The dark foot was the extremity going before the core. The almost-smile that is not peace would come onto a face before anybody thought to warm it; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. He read it as cleanly as he had ever read anything across a table and could not carry the reading two paces. Perfect and worth nothing, like every other object on the towel.

In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept, and the new finer grey beside it lay unswept too, and the house called both of them \lore{the thaw} and would go when it would go. Around the space beneath the lowest rack she had gone wide with her half-pace and had not entered it, and around the clean line and the finer hairline she had gone with a care that looked like not seeing them. No opening had come in all these days, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung, untouched by any hand at a shelf, a strip, a rack, a yard, a basket or a bed; it was for the opening of a kiln and for nothing else under this roof, and there had been no opening since the coals were banked on the Last Day. Whatever she put away she put in the private roll nobody was shown, and the private roll went on not being shown. He set the not-going beside the not-figuring, two keepings that did not join.

Somewhere off the bed, on its sill in the wheel-shed where he could neither reach it nor check it, the small bench knife lay at the very edge with its blade to the wall and its handle out. Six years stood in a mark a thumb had worn almost flat, and the seventh stood nearly gone and still held; it was the one entry in this house the house could not check, and it lay where a man could find it in the dark without looking. To the kindling a voice had said six counted and the next not counted, and the breath behind that sentence had not gone in, held from Seed 16 and still held, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone. Beside it, kept on the same square and entered nowhere, lay the sentence no man under this roof had said --- the sentence that would call the loan, if it were ever said, and the loan that can hear if ever it were called. Something must give at the end of the seventh year. The year stood one day nearer its end than it had stood the morning before, and it did not give here; the knife kept its six and the breath kept its place, and the boy on the bed could no more carry either to a door than he could lift his hand to his own chin.

The fire had been going down since the light went off the yard. Sella stood over it with the back of a broken pot in her hand and turned the coals, and the ash went over them blind and smooth as a face with its eyes shut. “It was the thaw,” she told the fire, and turned the coals again. “The house will keep it.” Where another woman would have said I she said the house, and she did not look toward the bed. The fire took the coal under the ash and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had given back for a hundred and ten days; its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and ten days, and nobody under this roof had asked who paid, and the boy had asked the question in his own head every morning and had never once had a mouth to put it in.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening, the way she had stood at the shelf moving her lips along a row of loaves with two gaps in it and given nobody a figure. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals, one strand among the coal-black, and she pushed the black back with the wrist that carries the burn, the little-door shutting on nothing as the wrist passed her brow. It was told of her in the yard that she had once sat three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and the boy had heard it and had not laid it beside the dark foot below the sill; two keeps, two places, and he held his finger off both. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark and went on with the keeping, which needed no light and had never once been explained.

The door stood a finger's width open, as it had stood for a hundred and ten days. Through the gap came the river's low note out of \place{the Ilm} beyond the yard, water going over water, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; through the floor came the kiln, four, four and a gap, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot. He kept the two sounds in two places as he had kept them on the first day and every day after, and the two counts did not meet. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. One count on the ceiling, one in the floor, one through the gap in the door, and none of the three would ever add up to the number of days he had been warm, which nobody under this roof knew was a number.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and ten stood where one hundred and nine had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, while the river went over water beyond the gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor. The crust kept its empty place in the fold, the shelf kept its two gaps in the room that did not touch this one, and the count went on.
