The afternoon came down on the yard in a thin light that made nothing new and took nothing away. At \place{the yard well} the women let the bucket down and brought it up, and the rope went over a stone lip wet with the week's weather and mud ground into it to the last strand. A woman with wet sleeves stood aside to let another draw, and used the waiting to ask what the planting had cost now that hands had been in the strips. She asked it first of the ground, then of the bucket, and only then of \person{Sella Vail}, who had come to the kiln-house doorway with the basket at her feet and her hands already busy at something else.

She did not turn. She moved her lips along \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room, the way a woman reads a row she has read a thousand times and will not read aloud, and her eyes went for the space of a breath to the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and came back to the row. The space at the end of it stood where it had stood. She gave it no figure.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

She had given the bucket its instruction before she gave it to the women, and the broom its before she gave it to the man, and she gave none at all to the basket. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the edge of the work where the yard reached and its writing did not, one hundred and eleven days old and three months and twenty-one days, and the cold iron of the well's handle stood two inches beyond his hand. He could have taken a shoulder apart in the dark and he could not close two inches. The woman's sleeves were wet to the elbow and she kept them away from her body with her hands half-lifted, the way a woman keeps a wet thing she has set down wrong; chaff had got under her fingernails from gripping the rope. She went off with her question still in her mouth, satisfied with an answer that had been addressed to a pot. The bucket went on being filled. The broom went on with its instruction. The basket was not looked into.

To the surgeon's eye the answering of a question addressed to a pot was a chart closed with a neat line over a note that had not been read. The line was exact. The figure at the foot of it was exact. Neither told him what lay under the note, and a man who tried to make them tell would be a man asking a slate to agree with a strip. He kept the woman's reaching hand on a place of its own and did not lay it across the hand that had reached past the bare board at the shelf the day before, though both reached in the same way and toward the same sort of empty place, and a third likeness would have made a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it. The surgeon read a chart that way once, and the note turned down was the part that mattered, and the hand that went past it to the signature was always a clean hand. He had spent two lives declining to become that hand, and here it was in every yard, doing a kindness.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the north road at the outer edge of the well, short and solid, with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. Hot clay came off him over the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, and passed a finger over the figure on it, and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required --- the way a man checks a dish he has set down already to be sure it will not slop. He did not raise his voice. A man who does not raise his voice in a yard makes the yard lean toward him, and the women at the bucket leaned without appearing to, the ones with wet sleeves drawing back a little to give him the dry place.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said, low, into the top of the leaning yard. “A number goes into a slate and not into a strip. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.”

Your house. Not my account. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand goes out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the sentence went on standing in the yard with nobody in it. The boy had heard that grammar in a midwife's mouth and a clerk's and a neighbour's and now in this man's over the worked ground: the record before the person, the figure before the face, the hand to the slate before the eyes to the stalls. Four record-first orders on four trays, and he set this one beside them without calling any of them the same hand. The twice-wiped thumb went on a tray of its own. It was the thumb of a man who would not let a slate be spoiled by a print, which is a kind of care, and care spent on the wrong object is the most expensive kind. Berr did not look into the basket. The keeping of that not-looking went on its own place and was joined to no figure.

A man come up from the strips with a number in his fist brought it toward the well and the bucket-round, and held it out where a man might put a number, and looked at what his fist had made of it. He thought better of giving it. The fist went back into his sleeve and he went off up the road with the number still in it, and the figure arrived nowhere. It was the district's whole arithmetic done in the open air --- from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder, and nowhere --- and the women went on drawing as though a man had not stood there at all. The boy kept the shape of the fist on a place of its own beside the clay jar's coldness and the reaching hand at the shelf: a thing withheld that had never been entered and could not be, because the entering of it would have made the hand that withheld it answerable. He wrote no cause across the space from the fist to the slate. A number that arrives nowhere is still a number that was carried, and a man who carries one and sets it down where nobody will take it up has done the same work as a clerk who writes one where nobody will read it: the figure is entered in the carrying, not in the receiving. The boy had watched a figure go from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder for as long as he could remember, and had never once seen anybody hold it at the end. He did not call the man a thief or a fool, because the man had put the fist in his sleeve with the care of a man putting a tool away where it can be found, and a tool put away is a tool kept. In this district everything withheld is kept and everything kept is owed, and the boy kept the shape of the withheld thing without entering it, which was the only form of ownership available to him.

The arguers put their case to the boards and the well, as they had put it for a month, the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late, neither of them asking the bucket or the pot and both of them speaking to the ground.

“Early's best,” a woman said to the stone lip. “Get it over. A body can plan on a thing that's done.”

“Late,” said a man to the boards. “Late puts it off.”

\person{Tarn} came and took the broom only after Sella had given it its instruction, and he said “Yes, house” to the one and “Yes, house” to the other, and agreed so entirely that the argument had nowhere to put its foot. He went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and would stand there whatever the moot said. His chest had been tight since the dark and there was a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in, and the breath did not go in. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. A breath stopped against a clamp is a breath spent whether it goes in or not, and the price of it is in the hand that holds the iron, not in the vessel it holds. The small bench knife lay where it could be checked and the seventh year lay where it could not, at the very edge of the sill, and the sentence that would call the loan stayed under this roof unspoken. The boy set the held breath beside the half-check at the damper and laid no finger across the two. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and a man who keeps an ear worth more than his debt keeps it where no clerk can spell it. Two keepings under one roof had stood for a hundred and eleven days and had not yet made a third thing, and the strain of it showed nowhere in the yard except in the small interval at the top of a reach where a breath should have gone in.

Berr went off up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln} with the narrow slate under his coat and the box locked in his house behind him and the true subtraction inside it. He believed he had done the district a kindness; a reasonable man with a ledger in his hand goes home clean, and the reason he carries is the part that will be remembered against him. He had not looked back at the well and had not once said I. Garl's debt against the firing of tiles went around the space where a name might have stood, neither paid nor settled nor done with, the way the yard went around the clean dark line and the new finer grey that the house called \lore{the thaw}: a thing left where it lies is a thing kept, and a thing kept is a thing that will one day be asked for. \person{Garl Vail} had not been at the well to answer for it and could not be, and his salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg under the roof where no man had taken it down.

The boy turned out what the afternoon had given and laid it where it belonged. Berr's twice-wiped thumb went on its own tray and did not join to the neighbour's reaching hand at the shelf, or to Sella's hand moving along the board, or to the clerk's hand going to a slate before its eyes went to a person; the figure read aloud did not become the figure paid, and the figure put into a slate did not become a figure put into a strip, and a figure let fall from a fist toward a well arrived nowhere. Three likenesses on three places, and no line drawn through them, because a line drawn through three absences is a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it. The light went off the yard and the cold came up off \place{the Ilm} in its known order, cheek, then temple, then the bare wrist where the wool had ridden back from the joint, and under the roof the fold kept its one stiff crust and the empty place beside it in a room the well did not touch. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and eleven stood where one hundred and ten had stood, exact, unspent, spoken by nobody. Berr's large clean hand wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, and the narrow slate went up the clay road under his coat toward High Kiln with the box locked behind him, the figure read aloud already separating from the figure paid.
