\daymark{112}{Seed 23, Y.H. 813}

The river wind got under \place{the north bridge} before the market did and went among the outer boards of \place{the fish-trade shade} with a cold that had been in the water all night. It found the wicker of the creels, the slime drying on the boards, and the folds of wool in the basket set at the very end of the shade where the shadow thinned and no slate lay within reach of a hand. \person{Sella Vail} had set the basket there before she set anything else, and had chosen the place before she chose the shade. She had carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down from \place{Low Kiln} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and gone around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The instruction went out ahead of her hand, first to the wool and then to the water running in the gutter, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the water she said, “Go where you're going.” Only then did she set him down. A basket set down was a pot set down and left; a shade, a strip, a shelf and a bed are not openings, and she stood a half-pace from the nearest open slate as though the half-pace were a wall she had built herself and knew the thickness of.

He was one hundred and twelve days old, three months and twenty-two days, and nobody in the shade knew it and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would have carried the saying if they had. Nobody had told him. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it; the want went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under blind ash. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody turned it back because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less against the river wind than the wind wanted, riding back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that was not this one and he could not close two inches on the basket's rim.

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels at the outer boards where the shadow ended, with pale slime dried on her sleeves in the long shape of her forearms and river silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept on her face the way a door is kept on the latch: it could be shut without anybody seeing the movement. She had a knife in one hand and a string of eel-bones in the other, and she flicked a bone against her thumb while she decided. The boy watched the order of the two hands, bone first and thumb second, and set it beside the order he had watched in other rooms --- the hand to the chart before the hand to the body, the hand to the slate before the eyes to the face, the hand past the bare board before the eyes went to the child. Here the record was a bone and the account was a cord, and neither of them could be entered by any Court thumb that ever washed and went home. Two women in two districts kept two records in wool and knot, and he set the keeping on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it. Another hand going to another kept place was the third shaping of a thing he had refused to name for three days; he held it where he held the rest, advanced, exact, and worth nothing to anybody who could have paid for it.

A woman come down the steps with salt dried white at her wrists asked whether the run would be thin now the hands had gone into the strips. Mira answered the question and then her own answer, which gave her the room to choose which of the two the listener got.

“Thin?” she said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.”

She moved two eel-bones against her thumb, one over and one back, while the deciding went on behind the small smile, and put one away when the deciding was done. The deciding and the not-saying were one movement of the same hand; the boy had watched a man decide the same way once with his fist shut around a number and his sleeve waiting for it, and the figure had arrived nowhere. Here the figure was not even spoken, and nothing arrived, which was the same arithmetic done more cleanly. He kept the bone-hand and the fist-hand on two places and laid no finger across them. A third likeness would have made a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and a clean hand at the foot of a cause was the one object in this world he had spent two lives declining to become.

\scenebreak

A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure for two pair and set it down between them the way a man sets a stone down where he wants it to stay. The hem had taken the colour of the water at the step's edge, a brown that was nearly grey, and the figure was as exact as a figure read from a slate at a date, though no slate had been opened within reach of the basket all morning. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord at her waist and stayed there while she decided, and stayed longer than the figure required. The boy watched the staying of it the way he had watched a thane wipe his thumb twice before touching nothing and a midwife reach past a bare board before she looked at the child --- not to learn what the cord counted, which was beyond his reach and none of his business, but to read the order. The hand went to the cord and not to the eels and not to the buyer. The buyer's figure lay where it had been put, and neither of them entered it anywhere.

When she shifted a creel to reach another pair, the grey cord rode up and showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it, the one that had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door. The knot for the thin child upriver was untouched on its own twist, and neither knot on either cord was counted by any thumb but her own. The boy counted neither of them either, because a reading made by a hand that cannot lift itself to its own chin is a reading and not an account. He had made a life's work of readings nobody would pay for. Here it was the whole of the morning's trade.

Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and did not touch it. The woman herself was upriver at \place{the Weir} and had not come down to the shade this morning; the board was all of her the market had, and it lay where it lay with a finger's width of wet air between the two records. The boy set the two boards where he set the three gaps in the two rooms under another roof, in places that do not touch. Three likenesses in a row will send a man who wants an answer home satisfied with the thing he has made, and he had kept his finger off the shelf and off the knot-string both for a hundred and twelve days. He did not know what the grey cord counted or what the second twist held, and did not try to know. A count kept where no Court thumb will look is the only honest count this district keeps, and he had learned to read the shape of a kept thing in wool and cord the way he once read the shape of a kept thing in tin and slate and the fold of a sheet in a room he no longer stood in. None of it could be spent. That was not the same as none of it being true.

Mira did not look into the basket. It was a keeping set on a place of its own, the opposite of a pricing, and there was no word in Grathic for the difference between a woman who would not look and a man who would not look at the same thing for the same length of time. He had given up looking for the word the way a man gives up an instrument that is not on the tray. The smile did not move while the knife went on with the eels, and she said nothing about the figure and nothing about the cord and nothing about the child two paces from her knee. No loaf passed over any board that morning and no slate was opened within reach of the basket, and no head at any stall turned half a beat late after a pot was set down. The keeping went on gathering interest in a record no Court thumb would look at, and the boy held it without entering it.

\scenebreak

The steps came back as they always came, four and four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line where the fourth step should have been, and the whole market went over them carefully and without making a fall of it, the way men step over a crack in a floor they have decided not to mend. A woman with a creel on her hip took the landing sideways, caught the rail with the hand that was free, and went on selling; nobody called it an accident and nobody put a figure to the danger. The boy, who could not have stepped over the landing or the crack or anything else, set the out-of-line place beside the gone board upriver and the hairlines that had stopped short of the edge, three places entered in no account, and made no accident of any of them.

At the landing the soft-cuffed clerk still sat with his cuff grey from washing and his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, as regular as the note the river made going over water under the bridge. He had known men who sat like that in rooms he no longer stood in, men set at a desk before anybody had decided what the desk was for, patient enough to outlast the people who put them there. The grey cuff was the only part of him that said he had a body; the slate to the forearm was the part that would be entered. The boy set the washed cuff and the neat slate on two places and did not let them touch, because a man who washes his own cuff has a hunger or a habit and a man who holds a slate at the edge where nobody else can see the figure has a use. Nobody had taken him off and nobody had called the measure he was set to take. He filed the order apart from the woman in the grey coat, from the grey-fingered collector at \place{the Drawing} and from the soft-handed man at \place{the Court's table} --- four record-first hands on four trays --- and set the watcher's late turn, kept over from another morning, on a fifth place of its own. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came on Seed 23, no watcher stood at any board, and no sale was paid. A measure set down before anybody calls it is a measure set down ahead of a death; the boy kept that as a shape and did not make a rule of it, and the market sold its eels over the top of the uncalled thing the way a market will.

The boy turned out what the morning had given and laid it where it belonged. Mira's hand going to the grey cord went on the place beside the two tellings of the shelf and the watcher's late turn, and he laid no finger across any of them, though they were three shapes of one keeping and a fourth would have been a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it. He counted neither knot. The cold came up off the water into cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the joint; he wanted the crust two paces away under another roof and the breast and the bucket's lip and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what a life count was, and he could reach none of them and had stopped asking anyone. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and twelve stood where one hundred and eleven had stood, exact, unspent, spoken by nobody, while the eels went on being sold over the slate nobody had called for and the steps went back four, four and a gap that no one mended.
