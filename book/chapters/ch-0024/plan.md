# Chapter 24 — The Last Tend of Seed — beat sheet

**Days:** 110–112. Day 110 = Seed 21, Firstday; Day 111 = Seed 22, Secondday; Day 112 = Seed 23, Thirdday; Y.H. 813. No Account-day falls in this chapter (Seed 30 is Day 119). Day 109 is done and is not revisited; Day 113 belongs to Chapter 25. **Mode:** normal, three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and at least 800 words on the page. Six scenes; each day is carried by two scenes so nothing is poured into another.

**The chapter's movement.** The planting enters its last tend. T097 takes a third shape at the loaf shelf and is still unpaid. Tarn's seventh year is pressed another two days closer to the edge, and for the first time his broom checks while Jorra's finger stands in the damper — a fraction of change in the equality under the roof, still unspoken and still uncalled. The count goes 110, 111, 112, exact and unspent. Nothing is joined, paid, named or opened. Nothing ends.

**Hard withholdings (carry all three days).** No ending, no payment, no recognition; no opening of tin, box, slate, back room or widow's mark; no third gap and no third crust; no knot counted; no word *forty*; no seventh hearing; no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand; no watcher on stage; no sale or betrayal paid; no name or number from Dessa; no silt-leach, cause or cure for the cold foot; no calling of the loan; no spending of the seventh year; no finger laid across the shelf's two gaps, the fold's gap, the two tellings of the shut room or the watcher's late turn. T097 advanced again and still not paid. T023 not paid again. Marra not in Sill; Ulda offstage; Dessa dead and stays dead; Fenna offstage; Orra present only as her board. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from Dan's eyes.

## Scene 1 — The Hand Past the Bare Board
**Day 110, Seed 21, Firstday, morning.** Vail kiln-house, at the loaf shelf; the edge of the Clay Yard, where the last tend of the planting is spoken of as a direction and the seed stands in the strips. Sella Vail, Corin (carried), an unnamed neighbour with a pot (the one-breath witness), yard voices. Berr is not yet on stage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the last tend: seed up in the worked strips east of Low Kiln; the hands that went in on Seed 15 still counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. The boy is carried at the edge; his hand goes up an inch toward the direction and comes down.
2. Sella gives the instruction to the bucket, the broom, the pot and the fire before anybody and never to the basket; says *the house*; keeps the half-pace from every open slate.
3. The neighbour with a pot reaches past the bare board. Her hand passes over the palm's breadth that came in on Seed 6, opens for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, closes on the loaf beyond and goes on with her pot. Her hand went to the board before her eyes went to the boy.
4. In one breath she calls the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the new gap the price of a wet thing set down wrong. She does not see they are one absence and does not see the empty place at his ear. She goes off speaking to the board, not to anyone.
5. Sella moves her lips along the row, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, gives nobody a figure. She does not touch the widow's mark: a shelf is not an opening.
6. The boy keeps three places apart --- the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one stiff crust and its unnamed empty place --- writes no cause across the space, allows no third gap, and does not make the neighbour's joining his own. The surgeon's register reads the reaching hand as a chart closed with a neat line over a note not read: exact, complete, worth nothing; he cannot lift a hand to his own chin.
7. End on the ordinary work going on around the absence, with no event made of it.

**Emotional turn.** An absence is handled as ordinary work; the only person who notices anything is the one who can do nothing about it. The dry joke lives in the exactness: he is the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

**Sensory anchors.** Bare board worn smooth at the row's end; chaff in the creases of a hand; a loaf's hard heel; the clay jar cold under its glaze; wet wool drying without care; woodsmoke gone flat under the roof; the sour mineral tang in the unswept corner; a pot set down and left.

**Threads advanced.** T097 (third shaping at the shelf — advanced, **not paid**), T060/T086 (three absences on three places, no third), T058/T079 (instruction to bucket, broom, pot, fire — never the basket; *the house*; half-pace), T009/T013/T092 (left things kept; two unswept lines; rack space gone around), T071/T072 (110 kept exact).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Advanced toward the far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call them an account — never his hand, not this chapter. The last tend advances the month toward the day the hands come out of the strips and one is not counted (T098, planted in Ch 26). Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** The neighbour's hand closing on the loaf beyond the bare place and going off with her pot, while the boy's small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down, the fold's one crust lying stiff in a room the shelf does not touch.

## Scene 2 — One Hundred and Ten
**Day 110, Seed 21, going of the light.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof; the boy on his bed, never in the Debt Loft. Sella Vail, Corin.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him up at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft.
2. She puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped: nobody has been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.
3. He lays the private file out as instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it: six hearings of the full note beside and never among, no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; three intervals; the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn and the weight of a thumb apart; four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth; the two tellings of the shut room joined to no kiln; the damper's double bite filed as an object; the loan that can hear uncalled.
4. Add the day without entering it: the neighbour's one-breath naming, Berr not yet come, the reaching hand, the last tend. Nothing moved, nothing paid, nothing joined.
5. The body: one crust stiff in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; the shelf's two gaps in the other room; below the sill the dark foot inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core read perfectly and buying nothing.
6. The clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept and called the thaw; the space under the rack gone around; no opening came, so the widow's mark hangs untouched and the private roll stays unshown.
7. Somewhere off the bed the small bench knife lies at the very edge of its sill with six years in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held --- the one entry in this house the house cannot check. The held breath from Seed 16 is still unspent; the unforged collector sentence is kept beside it. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it does not give here.
8. Sella at the fire: *It was the thaw*; turns the coals with the back of a broken pot; *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*; does not look toward the bed. The fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and ten days.
9. She stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening; the grey streak at her temple shows pale in the dying light; she pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, sets the pot where it can be found in the dark and goes on.
10. The door stands a finger's width open. River's note through the gap; kiln's four, four and a gap through the floor; two counts in two places that do not meet. Boards overhead: four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb.

**Emotional turn.** The file is complete and useless; the day adds one more exact thing and spends none of them. The woman at the fire keeps everything by not explaining any of it, and the boy keeps the count the house has no word for.

**Sensory anchors.** Fold put right without a look at the face; frozen thread; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; four low pulls and a gap; the pale grey streak in dying light; the little-door burn shut on nothing; wet wool, woodsmoke, river silt in the gap of the door; the small wet sound when the head goes too far.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (110 exact, unspent), T061/T082 (six hearings, no seventh, three near-full answers, word forty), T036/T076 (knife at the edge, breath unspent, loan uncalled), T088 (carried fire, payer unnamed at 110), T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T060/T086, T093/T094/T096 (cold foot still the cold).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The seventh year pressed one day further toward the end that must come; T097 carried, not paid. Nothing paid, no ending.

**Exact last image.** His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down; one hundred and ten stands where one hundred and nine stood, exact, unspent, spoken by nobody, while the river goes over water beyond the gap and the fire misses its fifth breath under the floor.

## Scene 3 — The Bite That Holds
**Day 111, Seed 22, Secondday, before first grey through midday.** Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard. Sella Vail, Jorra Vail, Tarn, Corin (carried at the edge).

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before first grey men come up the yard with collars high and go into the room off the wheel-shed that stands a finger's width open. The low voices do not carry to the edge of the light where the basket is set. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, ground small as meal, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The boy keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
3. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody; the space above them is not stepped into.
4. Jorra speaks to the wheel before anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.* He tells a pot *You'll do*. He knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
5. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, the iron taking the cold out of his fingertip; the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He has not answered with a word and has not looked at her.
6. **New small movement:** Tarn, who takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction, checks his stroke for half a beat at the damper while Jorra's finger stands in the iron. He goes on without turning his head and without a word. Neither man names the other's silence; the loan that can hear is not called. The boy sets the half-check beside the bent finger and lays no finger across them.
7. The boy reads the iron once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies, and files it as an object joined to no kiln and worth nothing.
8. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn; no one looks toward the bed or into the basket.

**Emotional turn.** Two men keep the same house by not saying the same thing. The household straightens around a silence; the reader is given a fraction more than any person in the room, and the boy cannot spend it.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey dust fine as meal in the creases past the bent finger; damp clay and tallow; the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a bad wind; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; the broom's bristles checking on the flagstones; blind ash under the pot; the half-beat where a stroke should have gone on.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, grey dust, broken birth-slates, sealed sentence, ungiven promise), T062 (damper's double bite filed as object, joined to no kiln), T036/T076 (the broom's half-check; seventh year held; loan uncalled; two silences not joined), T058/T079 (instruction to pot and fire — never the basket), T061/T082 (no seventh hearing), T071/T072 (111).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The convergence of the two keepings under one roof advances toward the day the sentence that would call the loan is said; the dust and the unnameable back room still point at the broken birth-slates and the men who burn them. The damper's double bite advances toward the day the house's danger is joined to the stories of the shut counting room — not here. Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's bent finger standing in the cold iron of the damper and Tarn's broom stopped half a stroke on the flagstones behind him, two silences in one room with no word passing between them, the boy carried at the edge with the dust and the half-check laid on one tray and not joined.

## Scene 4 — A Number Goes into a Slate
**Day 111, Seed 22, afternoon at the yard well and along the clay road out of Low Kiln; going of the light under the roof.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Berr Clay (short), Corin (carried), unnamed well-neighbours and thaw-arguers.

**Beats, in order.**
1. A woman with wet sleeves asks what the planting cost now that hands have been in the strips. Sella moves her lips along the loaf shelf, glances at the clay jar, leaves the space at the row's end with no figure and sends her to the pot: *ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there*, because *the house has said it*. The bucket and the broom get their instruction before anybody; the basket gets none.
2. Berr Clay comes up short from the north road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, hot clay over the smell of nothing at all. His large clean hands wipe the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing; he holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest a breath longer than the writing requires.
3. He reads *one in twelve, as the notice says*, says *a number goes into a slate and not into a strip*, and that if the house wants it again there will be a slate at the watch --- your house, never *I*, taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand out of a field.
4. A man brings a number up out of a fist toward the well, looks at it, thinks better of giving it, puts the fist in his sleeve and goes off; the figure arrives nowhere.
5. The early-thaw and late-thaw arguers put their case to the boards and the well. Tarn says *Yes, house* to each, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest is still tight and the breath still unspent; the knife is where it can be checked and the seventh is where it cannot.
6. Berr never looks into the basket and goes off up the clay road toward High Kiln with the box locked in his house and the true subtraction inside, believing he has done the district a kindness. Garl's debt against the firing of tiles is neither paid nor settled nor done with; it goes around the space where a name might have stood.
7. The boy sets Berr's twice-wiped thumb on its own tray and does not join it to the neighbour's reaching hand at the shelf, to Sella's hand on the board or to the clerk's hand on a slate. The figure read aloud does not become the figure paid.

**Emotional turn.** A reasonable man does a kindness with a ledger in his hand and goes home clean; the district passes a number from a fist to nowhere. The boy's refusal to join the hands is the only honest arithmetic in the yard and buys him nothing.

**Sensory anchors.** Wet sleeves and the wet stone lip of the well; chaff under fingernails; red wool cord folded under a coat; green of glaze at a mouth; hot clay over the smell of nothing at all; a thumb wiping twice on an apron; a narrow slate held unopened against a forearm; the small interval at the top of Tarn's reach where a breath should have gone in.

**Threads advanced.** T058/T079 (ask the pot; *the house has said it*; instructions to bucket and broom, never the basket), T015/T041/T047/T077/T078 (one in twelve; slate not strip; self-removal; true subtraction in the locked box; fist to nowhere), T036/T076 (early/late thaw; held breath; seventh year held; loan uncalled), T097 (hand shapes held apart, not paid), T009/T013/T092 (uncalled debt, left things kept), T071/T072.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Berr's box and the true subtraction gather interest toward the day the account stands and he still cannot say what he did wrong; the withheld number and the held breath both press forward. Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** Berr's large clean hand wiping the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, then the narrow slate going up the clay road under his coat toward High Kiln with the box locked behind him and the figure read aloud already separating from the figure paid.

## Scene 5 — The Cord Twice Twisted
**Day 112, Seed 23, Thirdday, dawn at the Fishstairs below the north bridge and at the outer boards of the fish-trade shade.** Sella Vail, Mira Silt, Corin (carried), a weir woman with salt-white wrists, an eel buyer in a coat with the river in its hem, the soft-cuffed clerk (walk-on). Orra offstage, present only as her board; Ulda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket at the very end of the fish-trade shade where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket; she keeps a half-pace from every open slate.
2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. She answers a question with a question and then her own answer.
3. The weir woman with salt white at her wrists asks whether the run will be thin. Mira: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away.
4. A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem names a figure. Mira's thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure requires; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it --- never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted, the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it.
5. The steps come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it.
6. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing, cuff grey from washing, slate to forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. He is filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector, the Court-table clerk and the watcher; no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand. No foundling turns late at a pot today; no loaf passes and no secret changes hands that a slate could read.
7. The boy sets Mira's hand on the cord beside the two tellings of the shelf and the watcher's late turn, and lays no finger across any of them. He counts neither knot.

**Emotional turn.** The district keeps its accounts in eel-bones and twisted cord where no Court thumb can read them; the Court keeps its own measure set down ahead of a death. The boy sees both orders and can enter neither.

**Sensory anchors.** Pale slime dried on sleeves; silt in the hollow of a neck; the cold off the water; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; grey wool under an apron with a second cord twisted twice beneath; salt dried white at wrists; a cuff gone grey from washing; four steps, four, and a landing out of line.

**Threads advanced.** T059/T025/T024 (Mira's second cord twisted twice, neither knot counted, thin child's knot untouched, board beside Orra's not touching), T090/T080 (clerk still set for the uncalled measure, filed apart, no sixth sighting), T097 (another hand held on another place, advanced and not paid), T083 (out-of-line landing walked over without a fall), T071/T072 (112).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Mira's cords and Orra's knot-string advance toward the later convergence of the district's second records; the clerk set for an uncalled measure advances toward a death. Nothing paid; no sale, no watcher, no word *forty*.

**Exact last image.** Mira's thumb pressed under her apron to the grey cord with the second twist hidden beneath it and the knot-string on her board not touching Orra's, while the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down.

## Scene 6 — What the Fold Keeps
**Day 112, Seed 23, midday three doors down in House Silt, then going of the light under the Vail kiln-house roof.** Sella Vail, Alda Reed, Corin (carried), a scrubbing neighbour (walk-on). Dessa dead and stays dead, unnamed to the boy.

A low song comes up through the floor with its words flattened out of sense by work.

The clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept and called the thaw; the space under the lowest rack gone around; the carried fire under blind ash and the back of a broken pot at four, four and a gap, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twelve days. ## Pull into Chapter 25
The chapter closes mid-reach, not at a resolution. The count moves from one hundred and nine to one hundred and twelve without anyone under the roof knowing there is a number to move; the last tend of Seed is open and the hands are still in the strips, counted in glances and handfuls. T097 has gathered a third shaping at the shelf and remains unpaid; Tarn's broom has checked at the damper while Jorra's finger stood in the iron and the equality under the roof has tightened by that much without a word being spent; the seventh year is two days nearer its end and has not given. Chapter 25 opens on Day 113, Seed 24, Fourthday, with the boy one hundred and thirteen days old, nobody saying so, the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one still in rooms that do not touch, the word *forty* unspoken, the loan uncalled, no third crust and no seventh hearing --- the same held things one day further on, forward and not closed.
