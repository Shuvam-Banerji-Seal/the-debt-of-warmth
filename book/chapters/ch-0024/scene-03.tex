\daymark{111}{Seed 22, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the men came up \place{the Clay Yard} in \place{Low Kiln} with their collars high, and went into the room off \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} that stood a finger's width open. The low voices went in ahead of them and stayed where they had gone; the cold came out through the finger's width and went along the ground, but the voices did not carry as far as the edge of the light where \person{Sella Vail} had set the basket down. She had set it there before she set anything else, at the place the yard's work reached and its writing did not, and she gave the instruction to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with his ear under the fold and one bare wrist out of it, one hundred and eleven days old and three months and twenty-one days, and nobody in the yard or under the roof had a word in which to say what that counted. The men's boots went past at the edge of the light and he counted them without meaning to, as he counted nails, and put the number on a place of its own and gave it nobody. A count kept in a head is still a count kept, and in this district the keeping of a thing is the whole of the ownership.

Cold came to him in its known order, cheek, then temple, then the bare wrist where the wool had ridden back from the joint, and he could no more alter one step of it than he could keep the wool where it had been put. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen again in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it was, a small bright line at the corner of his lip that no hand would wipe. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under the roof had been told to put it right, so nobody did. He was a surgeon who could have taken a shoulder apart in the dark and could not keep a fold of wool on his own wrist, and the joke of it had been paid for by somebody else every morning of his life here and had never once been funny enough to say aloud. The men's boots went on past the basket; the yard's work went on around the place where they had gone; the boy kept the cold in its order and the thread in its place and said nothing to either.

\person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for them. He did not say the room was cold. He came through the finger's-width-open door with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, ground small as meal and rubbed in with the splitting of slate, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel that dried pale on top and left the creases clean. The little finger had been bent since he was eleven and it led the rest of his hand into everything, \lore{the damper}, the wheel, the bench. The boy kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, two states of one man's mornings, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. A question asked aloud in this house would have gone to the pot before it went to the man, and the pot would not have answered; he had learned to keep his questions where questions can last for years.

To the surgeon's eye the grey was a foreign thing ground small and driven into the creases of a working man's skin, the way grit drives into a field nobody has cleaned; the bone-coloured slip sat on top and dried pale and came off with washing, but the slate dust worked in past the bend of the finger and stayed. He read the difference without deciding to, the way he read the colour of a man's lips before the man spoke of pain. Two forearms were two records of one morning, and the men who had come before the light put the second record there and went back into the finger's width of open door without a name. Which slate had been broken, and whether it had been a birth-slate, were the two questions standing behind the dust; he kept them behind it, as a man keeps an instrument out of a field until the field is ready, and asked neither aloud nor in the shape of a look toward the door.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Nobody stepped into the space above them. The yard went around it with a broom and a glance and no figure, as it went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the new finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}; a line left where it is is still a line kept. The boy carried the going-around at the edge of the basket and set it beside the dust on the same tray, two keepings that did not join and did not grow into a cause. Whole slate under a rack is a thing the yard enters into nothing, and the space where it should have stood is kept as carefully as the line nobody will sweep.

Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel turned for a steady man and for an unsteady one and kept no record of which, and the dust on his forearms was the only thing in the shed that had come from somewhere the house did not name. He set a pot on the bench and looked a moment longer at the shoulder than the shoulder required. “You'll do,” he said, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel --- what it would give under a knuckle when it was whole, what it would withhold when it was not --- and he would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call the knowledge early and make something of it. The boy had watched him know it for three years and had never once heard him say it. Knowing a thing and saying it were two objects, and he set them on two places as he set the dusty forearms and the clean ones.

His quiet was a weather signal in this house; the yard had learned to read the lowering of his voice the way it read the wind off the \place{Ilm}, and when he went quieter the work went around him with a care that looked like not seeing him. From the finger's width of open door the low voices went on and came back with nothing in them that could be carried to a slate; he did not take them up and he did not give the house a name for the men. A kiln-master come up from High Kiln would have called the knowing early and made something of it, would have set a figure at the foot of it and asked the pot to agree; Jorra kept the knowing where the boy kept his questions, unspoken and unentered, and the wheel went on turning under both of them.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low. Feed it---” The end of the sentence never got as far as him. His bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, the little finger leading, and the iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave it back a little warmer. The warmth came back into the fingertip before it came back into the hand, the extremity leading the core in reverse, and the boy noted the order as he had noted it on a hundred cold mornings in a person who could not tell him what was happening to his own body. Iron does this: it takes the heat out of a thing and gives a little of it back, and a man may take the small return for kindness. The bite that closes on a vessel to hold it whole is the bite that closes on a finger to take it off, and there is no word in the yard's speech for the two mercies in one piece of metal; the house had the object and had no name for what it did, and went on using it as a woman uses a door she has never looked at closely. He moved the tongue of iron a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The end of her instruction went past him and arrived at the pot, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: the fire took its coal, the pot took its place, the broom took its instruction, and nobody gave any instruction to the basket. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing as her hand came away from the handle, and she did not look at him and he did not answer with a word. Obedience in this house arrives before the instruction that calls it, and the arriving is also a correction neither of them will ever name.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after she had given it its instruction. “The rack first,” she said to the broom. “Then the road. Leave the lines where they lie.” She said the house where another woman would say I, and she gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to the man, and Tarn came and took it up as a man takes up a tool that has already been named. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark, and there was a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in, and the breath did not go in. Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh stood nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check; to the kindling he had said six counted and the next not counted, and the saying had cost him nothing in words and everything somewhere else.

The bristles went over the flagstones and came to the damper. Jorra's bent finger was standing in the iron. Tarn's stroke checked. Half a beat, no more --- the bristles stood on the stone with a whole reach held between one sweep and the next --- and then they went on, and he went on toward the road without turning his head and without a word. The flagstones kept the place where the stroke had stopped as a thing nobody would enter. Neither man named the other's silence; the two silences stood in one room with no hand joining them, and the loan that can hear was not called. The boy set the half-check beside the bent finger on one tray and laid no finger across the two, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause, and a cause in a house like this would come with a clean hand at the foot of it. He had known two keepings under one roof for a hundred and eleven days and had not once made a third thing of them.

Tarn had turned his head a degree at a pot's note in the yard often enough for the boy to know the turn before he made it, and he had never once named the sound or looked toward the basket to see whether it had been heard. Two ears kept the same silence under one roof, and an ear that can hear is worth more than a debt that holds a man, which is why the keeping of it is unsafe and why he keeps it where he keeps the seventh year. The half-beat at the damper went on the tray beside the dust as another interval, a place where a stroke should have gone on and did not, and he wrote no cause across the space from the iron to the broom. A household can straighten around a thing it will not say and go on straight for years, and the strain of it shows nowhere except in the small places where a stroke stops and starts again.

He read the damper once more while the dust worked into his attention. It was the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, one piece of iron with two mercies in it, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held both. He filed the reading as an object joined to no kiln, exact and worth nothing, on the tray with the other exact things that had bought him nothing in two lives. The wheel gave its low note and the pot gave its note and the iron gave back no sound at all, only cold; no seventh hearing came before midday in the wheel-shed, and the three near-full answers stayed advanced and not paid on the tray where he had left them. Four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water over water was a fifth on a place of its own, and the two tellings of the room that had been shut in the war stayed joined to no kiln, whatever the shape of them. He had a file laid out like instruments on a towel and no towel, no instruments and no field to open; the hour went on without adding to it, and the fact that nothing had been added was itself an entry he did not enter.

\emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. Nobody looked toward the bed. Nobody looked into the basket. No opening came before midday, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung, untouched at a shelf, a strip, a rack, a wheel, a damper and a door, and the private roll nobody was shown went on not being shown. One brown-edged crust kept its empty place in the fold in a room the wheel-shed did not touch, and \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two gaps in a room that did not touch that one, and he carried the three absences in three places that did not join. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it did not give here, and the breath behind the man's chest stayed where it had been put on Seed 16, unspent, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in.

Jorra's bent finger stood in the cold iron of the damper, and Tarn's broom had stopped half a stroke on the flagstones behind him before it went on about the yard, two silences in one room with no word passing between them. The boy was carried at the edge of the light with the fine grey dust and the half-check laid on one tray and not joined, his small hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water beyond the yard and coming down. One hundred and eleven stood as one hundred and ten had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody under the roof, while the wheel went on turning for whoever had steady hands and the fire went on missing its fifth breath under blind ash with its payer unnamed.
