\daymark{30}{Ilmen 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

The grey went in the morning, before the light had cleared the sill. A woman at the yard mouth built a fire small against the skirting board with a pail of water by her knee, and the \lore{Ash} that had run along \place{the Vail kiln-house} wall for a year and two days went up in a thin smoke that got into the wet wool and stayed there. The ridge had turned its corner unbroken through the whole of \lore{the Last Day}; by the time the sun reached the boards there was only a clean dark line where it had been, the colour of a board rubbed with ash and not quite wiped. \person{Sella Vail} stood over the place with a broom in her hand and said nothing about what had been there. Yesterday she had laid her thumb flat into that ridge and walked two doors down \place{the Clay Yard} and laid the same thumb into another ridge a finger's width thicker, and had come back without saying which was which. This morning she did not sweep. The neighbour at the tubs did the saying for her, in the flat tone of a woman reporting the weather.

“That's the grey gone,” she said. “Burned this morning. Dealt with.”

\person{Corin} lay in the basket at Sella's hip under the fold and took the words in with the smell of the burn. A thing left where it lay had been a thing still accounted for; a thing burned and called dealt with was a thing accounted for in another way. Both ways kept the room in its shape. He held them under the same hand without putting one over the other, as he had held the ridge and the charred slate and the barrow of grey going out through the north gate, and as he had once held two columns in a ledger in a room with better light, where a line crossed out meant the account had been closed and nobody looked at the line again. The burn had taken the grey and left the smell of it in every coat in the yard. A thing called gone was not the same as a thing gone. He knew that as he knew the order in which cold came to him --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and it bought him nothing. The child in him wanted the breast. The other part of him noted that smoke clings longer than the thing that made it. Neither part had a word, and neither had a hand to put a word down with.

“That'll do,” Sella said to the fire, not to the woman. She set the broom against the wall and went in to the banked coals.

\scenebreak

The coals were still under their ash. There had been no opening of the kiln on this first morning of the new year, and so her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat; it had not gone there at the bank last night, and it would not go there for a reading or a dusk or any hour that was not an opening. The rule held across the turn of the year as exactly as it had held inside it. He had watched it now through a tend and the five named days, and the exactness of it was the whole of what he knew about the woman who kept him: that she would touch a small lead-glazed thing before she lifted a lid and at no other time, and would not explain why, and would let the whole district read the keeping as a kindness if it liked. The flame that had come into the house on \lore{Kindling} was still in the house, five days on, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot. Nothing had carried it away. It warmed the boards by degrees and missed one breath in every four.

Four, four, and a gap. The kindling's empty fifth lay on its own tray, separate from the fire's interval, and he did not let the two touch.

Outside, the yard was already speaking of \place{Slate Row} the way it spoke of the river or the north gate --- a direction, not a place anybody meant to go this morning. It was \lore{the Opening of the Slates}. The new year's accounts were being opened somewhere beyond the row of houses, in a room with a bench and a pot and a draught along the floor, and the talk came back over the boards as a shape rather than a report. One in twelve stood printed where it had stood last year and nobody in the yard challenged it; the true subtraction that would have put a figure on what a house had actually lost was spoken by nobody at all, and went past the basket the way a fist goes past a child, shut and unopenable. He had learned by now that a figure heard in the yard was not a figure he could spend, correct or hand back. \lore{The Five} were behind them. The year had opened without anybody coming to the kiln-house to do it, and the district had entered a new twelve months on its slates before the boy under the fold had been entered on any of them.

\scenebreak

He was thirty days old. A new number had been written at the head of the district's account and the sun had come up over the same yard, and the only thing that crossed from one to the other whole was the count nobody had thought to enter. It went on exactly as it had gone on in the old year: the kiln's four, four and a gap; the kindling's fifth that was not there; the bell's gap as it had been told in the dark; the missing note on the bench that had been a silence and not a hearing; the ridge that was no longer in the corner; the two crusts lying in the fold; the five pauses lying side by side in a row and not joined. A year was a change of handwriting on the same account. He had believed that once, in a life where the accounts had been kept in binders and the handwriting had changed every January, and the belief had survived the crossing without carrying any of the binders with it.

The intervals stayed on their own tray. The pot turned toward on the bench that had given nothing; the note that had gone past the door and been too far to confirm; the pot that had cracked two paces from his head and been called a pot gone wrong. The silence where a name should have stood lay with them and was not a hearing. Of \lore{the full note} there had been five hearings from four sources and no sixth, and the \lore{Drawing} collector's ordinary hand with no pause in its own place stayed filed apart from the five sightings of the neat dark hand, and the charred slate lay in the kiln ash exactly where it had lain, black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark. He kept all of it in its several places because a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anybody put one there. He had made that mistake once in a better-lit room and had carried the cost of it for the rest of a life. He could no more join the five pauses now than he could lift a hand to his own chin. He did not know \lore{Grathic}, could not have written a figure on a slate if one had been put into his palm, and was carried everywhere in a basket that was not his.

\scenebreak

Work turned toward the thaw in \place{Low Kiln}, and toward the slip pits, though \place{the Ilm} still held under its ice and had not moved for anybody's argument. \person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with bone-coloured slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms, the bent little finger of his left hand going in ahead of him at the damper, which he moved before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low. He said to the wheel what he never said to a person.

“The wheel doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.”

To a pot set down on the bench he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and went back to the shed without once looking toward the bed where the boy lay. There had been no slate dust on his forearms since the Last Day, and the boy kept the clean forearms and the dusty ones in two places, as he kept everything a face refused to report. The yard had begun to call the cold a warm spell and the warm spell the weather, the word that lets a person go home without owning a day of it; a woman come up from the pits said the thaw would be early, and a man who had looked at the ice said it was as sound as stone, and by noon both had agreed it was weather. Down below the sill the river lay under its skin of ice and did not stir.

\person{Tarn} swept before the light was properly up, with grey Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted, and set the small knife back on its sill and told the empty yard where it was, unasked.

“I put it back. You can check.”

Six years of the seven were counted; the seventh he held back again on the first morning of the new year, the one entry in this house that the house could not check. He had wanted a late thaw in Stone because a late thaw put off the paying, and now the year had turned and the paying was a year nearer and he went on sweeping as though it were the same count he had swept to yesterday. His broom checked half a stroke at the place by the bench where no note had come and went on. He did not look toward the basket. He had not said what he heard on any of the five days, and the boy kept the two silences under the one roof side by side where they had lain all year, an equality with no word on either side of it.

\person{Garl Vail}'s coat kept its salt shape on its peg behind the door and nobody took it down on the first morning of the year. In the shed the seat kept its hollow on the left, worn by twenty years of a man's leaning, and stayed hollow; the ridge under the skirting board was gone from the corner now, burned and called dealt with, and the house was not less shaped by the absence of it. His proverb went to the pot and to the fire and never to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The crack had been showing all year in a grey line along the wall, and now the line was gone and the proverb went on being said to the pot, as though a thing left in plain sight could not be made to disappear by a woman with a pail of water.

Sella put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, the little-door burn on the back of her hand closing first on nothing and then on the wool. She fed him barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and two fingers behind his ear holding his head where it wanted to go, and said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool; and to the fire, “Feed it. It'll still be here in the morning.” She said nothing about where the warmth in the dish had come from or who had paid for it, and had not once in thirty days said the word for the thing she kept. In the fold the two crusts lay where they had been put. One had been stiff since Stone 15 and one still gave a thin breath of barley smoke when the wind turned off the yard, though the wind had turned a hundred times since the smoke left it. Neither had been lifted. Neither had been named. No third crust came into the fold on the first morning of the year, though there was bread in the house and the oven had been hot five days ago.

\scenebreak

Nothing had been joined. Five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; no word in \lore{Grathic} for the sound, and no hand to tap a vessel and ask it to answer. The intervals and the silence where a name should have stood were kept apart on their tray. Five pauses lay side by side in a row, and the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in it lay in its own place, and the charred slate lay in the kiln ash where the broom had gone around it for a year. He was thirty days old, cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below \lore{Thin}, with twelve to fifteen embers at best and a flaw nobody had named. He was mute and carried everywhere, could not roll, stand, read a slate, lift a hand to his own chin or feed himself; cold came to cheek and temple and wrist in the order he knew, and the wet thread at his mouth was not wiped, and the blanket would not stay on.

He lay on his bed under the Vail roof with the fold at his ear and the two crusts in it, and the fire behind him took its breath in fours and missed one. The count went on into a year the district had already entered on its slates, unentered, untaxed, worth nothing to anybody in the house and not for sale in any market of it. Outside, the yard called the weather what it called the weather, and under the ice the river did not move. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down again.
