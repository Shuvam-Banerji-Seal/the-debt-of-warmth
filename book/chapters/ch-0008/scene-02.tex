\daymark{26}{Weighing, Y.H. 812}

The clean voice came across the yard ahead of the man who owned it, over the wet boards and the frost lying on the thatch, and it carried nothing of the yard in it. No shouting, no pig noise, no argument about the weather. It went on at the level of a spoon set down on a table, and the yard leaned toward it the way a yard leans toward any man who has a slate and the law behind his hand. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person{Corin} down in the basket with the fold at his chin, twenty-six days old and going where he was taken, and she kept her half-pace from every open slate the whole way to the fringe of \place{the Cross}, as if the slates were sleeping animals and a near step would wake them.

She set him where the reading carried and no slate lay open, at the outer edge of the boards where \place{Slate Row} begins to be spoken of as a direction, and turned the basket with her knee so his face was toward the voice and not toward the writing. Her little-door burn shut on nothing as the weight came off her hand. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, not to the basket; “it's the river wind.” She did not look toward his face. She never did, on a day when a voice had a slate behind it. The looking was a kind of entering, and the house had decided long ago what it would and would not enter. It was \lore{Weighing}, the second of \lore{the Five}, and the year's measures were being read aloud in a voice that had been washed and mended and made to fit a room.

He had heard the like before, in a market, in a man's low proverbs with the green of tasted glaze at his mouth. What came over the boards now was the same grammar wearing a temple's clothes. “The measure was banked,” the officer said, and went on without a breath for the yard to take it in: “the account stands as entered; the return was recorded; nothing is lost that we have kept.” The sentences were clean of the man who spoke them. He had taken himself out of his own sayings as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field, leaving the work done and no hand in it. It was not the shouting of a market and not the proverb of a kiln-master. It was a reading, and a reading does not need anybody to agree with it.

The surgeon in him had once sat across a table from a man who reported a death in just such a voice, with the note turned down and the chart closed over the thing it had omitted, and had heard a room accept it because the voice was level and the handwriting neat. The memory came in the old tense and went out again; there was no break, no white light over the boards, only the wet wool at his ear and a man's clean sentences arriving one after another. He had performed that report himself, once, with a signature at the end of it that made a thing done look like a thing decided. \person{Berr Clay} had spoken the district's grammar at the Cross through a tend of \lore[Account-day]{Account-days} and the boy had learned to hear the man disappear inside it. Here the disappearance had been trained. It had a uniform, a cadence and a room behind it, and it did not need to lower its voice to make two hundred people lean in, because the leaning had been done for it years ago and entered somewhere.

Cold came at cheek, then temple, then wrist, in the order he knew and could not argue with. The drool at his mouth went cold and stayed. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound it made when it went too far, and nobody put it right while the reading was on. He counted the officer's sentences because counting was the one faculty the body he was in had left intact: four measures named, four named, and a place where a closing line should have stood and did not. He set the missing line on the tray with the bench's interval, because it was nearest in shape, and kept it from the five hearings of \lore{the full note} as he kept all intervals from hearings. The count stayed five from four sources. A voice could be clean and still leave a space; he had known that in a better-lit room, where the space had been the only honest part of the report.

Then the voice altered, not in tone but in what it was made to carry. A whole \lore[the Return]{return} was set down in the air over the boards for a person who was not in the yard. The officer did not raise his voice to announce it; he had no need to, because a whole return is a thing a room receives without being told to receive it. A figure stood in the sentence, and the name of the person it belonged to stood beside the figure, and the yard took both in the way a dry board takes rain, without comment and without giving anything back. The boy heard the figure and could not spend it. He could not correct it, could not hand it back, could not ask whose it was or what had been taken to pay for it. It was somebody else's wholeness, read out over a basket that had no name of its own entered anywhere he could reach. The name that came with it was a name the household would not repeat after the reading, and he knew this before anybody under the roof had opened a mouth, because he had learned the shape of a name that gets gone around by not being said.

The officer finished the sentence and left a space at the end of it, the space that in \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail kiln} was the missing breath between four and four. The yard went on with its own business around that space. Women shifted on their feet; a man coughed into his hand; a gate somewhere at the back of the Cross went on its hinge and stopped. Nobody filled the space. The boy filled it the way he filled the kiln's gap, not with a sound but with the counting of its length, one, two, three, and a place where the fourth would stand if the fourth had been given. He had counted the space after the bell on Stone 30 when nobody crossed himself; he had counted the space on the bench where a pot had been set down and given nothing; he had counted the space where a name should have stood under a roof where names were gone around by not being said. This space went on the same tray with those, among the gaps and the intervals, and not among the hearings. It was not a sixth hearing of the flat note. It was the silence after a name, which is a different object and must be kept in a different place, or a man ends by writing a cause where he has only noticed that two absences have the same shape. Five hearings from four sources. The count did not move.

At the edge of the reading, where the boards gave onto the path toward \place{High Kiln}, \person{Berr Clay} stood with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it, as a man folds a thing he has done with for the day. The green of tasted glaze showed at one corner of his mouth. His hands were large and clean, and he wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched the slate in his hand, the way he wiped it at the moot before he read a figure to two hundred people who had to lean in to hear him. He said nothing at all to anybody. He did not look toward the basket. He passed a finger over his slate, as if to make sure the figure he had put there was still where he had left it, and went off in the direction of his own house with his face arranged in the patient expression of a man who has done the district a kindness and expects no thanks for it. Whatever he kept locked at home he left locked there, and the boy had no way to reach it and no word with which to ask. He filed the clean hands and the folded cord on a tray of their own, unjoined to the voice and unjoined to the clerk's soft cuff at the market's edge, because a man who reads a figure and a man who enters one may be the same man or two men, and the boy had learned the cost of deciding such things from a better-lit room.

The household did not repeat the name. Sella stood a half-pace off with her burn shut on nothing and her hands busy at the fold, and when a neighbour came back from the reading and said something low about a whole return having been announced, she answered with the weather and the wind off \place{the Ilm}, which still held under its ice. The name went off the yard with the reading and was not picked up again. \person{Garl Vail}'s proverb went to a pot and the fire and not to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The coat kept its salt shape behind the door of the Vail kiln-house, the wheel-seat its hollow on the left, and the ridge along the skirting board its corner turning without a break, unswept and left for \lore{the Last Day}. Nobody said whose return had been read out. Nobody said who was absent. The boy lay with the name-shaped silence in his mouth the way other men carry a bad taste, and counted it, and would not let it join anything.

\scenebreak

The reading did not stay in the yard. When the officer had done with the figures he went with them through a door, and the voice went in ahead of him, and the boy heard the last of it from where he lay on the outer boards. He was not carried through that door. He learned \place{the Court's room} the way he had learned most of the rooms in this country --- by a voice going in through its door and by what other people had said of it when they thought no basket was near. It had a bench by the door, from which the slate was read; a pot set on the bench; a draught under the door going along the floor to the corner, felt at the ankles before the cold was felt anywhere else. Those things had been described to the yard on Stone 19 by a neighbour just come back from the room, and the description had lain in the boy's private file ever since with no cause written on it. Now the voice going through the doorway gave the room its own sound, and with the sound came the other thing the neighbour had reported: a place in the figures where a line should stand and does not, the same every time. He set the room down in its own place beside the birth room in \place{House Silt}, beside the Vail kiln-house, beside the death-house two doors down where the ridge was a finger's width thicker. Four rooms. The same bench, the same pot, the same draught going to the corner, the same place where a line should stand. He kept the four likenesses in four separate places, because a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anyone put one there, and he had made that mistake once in a better-lit room and carried the correction for the rest of a life. He wrote no cause. He only set the room beside the others and left it there, the way a man lays four tools in a row and does not yet decide what they are for.

Sella lifted the basket off the boards in the late afternoon and carried him up the yard with woodsmoke and river wind caught under the fold. Nobody had spoken to the basket all day. At the door she did not go to the kiln, and because there was no opening there was no touch of \lore{the widow's mark}; the hand that had touched it before the first light of \lore{Kindling} kept its rule as exactly on Weighing as it had on any Account-day, and the little-door burn shut on nothing all the way from the yard to the bed. She put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, and gave the instruction to the fire and the wool, and said nothing about a whole return read out for somebody who was not there. In the fold at his side the two crusts lay where they had been put --- one stiff since Stone 15 and gone past smelling of anything, the other still giving a faint breath of barley smoke when the wind turned --- and neither was eaten, lifted or named, and no third crust was added. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with the spoon wiped on her sleeve and said “the house” where another woman would say I, and the warmth went in entered nowhere.

\person{Tarn} came down from \place{the Debt Loft} with Ash in the creases of his knuckles and the small bench knife back on its sill where it could be checked. “It's on the sill,” he said to the yard, which had nobody in it but the basket. “You can check.” He looked at the kindling stack where four bundles stood and four and a space where a fifth should have been, and took his eyes off it. “Six counted. The next I don't count.” Six years of the seven were behind him and counted every one; the seventh he held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. “Yes, house,” he said to the ground, and swept where he had already swept. Behind him the wheel-seat kept its hollow and the salt coat its shape, and the proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to the basket.

The silence after the name went off the yard and came under the roof with the boy and lay down beside him on the bed. He counted it the way he counted the kiln's missing breath, and filed it with the intervals and not with the hearings, and the five hearings from four sources stayed five. The officer's clean sentences had left no figure he could spend and no name he could hand back, and the room behind the voice had the bench, the pot, the draught and the gap that the other three rooms had, and he had written no cause in any of them. At the bank Sella turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch the mark, because a bank was not an opening. The fire kept four, and four, and a gap. He lay with the two crusts in the fold and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down, and nobody under the roof had said whose return had been announced, or who had been the person not in the room.
