# Chapter 8 beat sheet — The Five Cold Days

**Days 25–30** · Kindling, Weighing, the Long Night, the Remembering and the Last Day (all Y.H. 812), then Ilmen 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813 · **passage mode, six scenes, six dated entries, one lived day each, no day poured into another.** The Five have names and no tend-days, and no market is held during them; on Ilmen 1 the count begins again at Firstday and the era turns to 813. The boy is twenty-five days old on the first page and thirty at the close. Day 24 is finished and is not revisited as a new scene.

**Continuity gate before the first word.** The previous chapter ended on Day 24, Stone 30, Account-day and the year's last market, dusk under the Vail roof: the boy on his bed with the spent crust of Stone 15 and Mira's fresh crust of Stone 28 side by side in the fold, neither eaten, lifted, removed or named; the fifth pause lying with four others and not joined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause filed apart; the figure for the Five going around in fists and mouths; one day after tonight was tomorrow. He is cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin (twelve to fifteen embers at best), mute, carried everywhere, cannot roll, read Grathic, lift a hand to his own chin or feed himself; cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist. **Five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; three objects on the interval tray (the turned pot, the half-heard note past the door, the cracked pot in the yard), a fourth silence where a name should be only after this chapter; five unjoined pauses; the collector's grey fingertips, Jorra's dust and Alda's turned note on their own trays; Mira's grey wool and second cord twisted twice, knots uncounted; Alda's tin shut in her bodice; Sella's roll, Berr's box, the broken slates and the contents of the tin unshown.** Do not explain the flat sound, the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate, the debt, the cords, the tin, the Hearth-flame, the levy, the deduction or why Sella took him. Do not recap. No present-tense Chicago break this chapter: the pocket-place is reached and the boat promise felt in past tense, with no `\memory{}` tag. No experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no rank change, no power moved. Do not say one in three or forty, do not name Marra, do not show the purchaser or the boy's own return, do not count the knots, do not let the five pauses join. Dessa stays offstage, alive, her cough still weather; the small pale river stone in House Silt stays unmentioned; Fenna is not named.

## Scene 1 — The Fire Carried In

- **Day and time:** Day 25, Kindling, Y.H. 812, before first light into night. A named day, no tend-day, no market.
- **Place:** The Clay Yard and the Vail kiln-house, Low Kiln; the threshold, the fire, the bed under the roof.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-five days old, in the basket under the fold), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat, unswept ridge and proverb; neighbours handing the flame from hearth to hearth (walk-ons); the collector who takes the year's first measure is offstage, unnamed and unseen.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Open on the carried thing, not on a thought and not on a recap: before first light a clay dish comes up the Clay Yard with a coal under ash, handed from one hearth to the next, each house touching its own fire to it. Nobody says where it came from or what it cost. Do not name the Hearth-flame in narration.
  2. Sella's hand goes to the widow's mark at her throat before she opens the kiln and tips the dish in; she holds it the space of one breath and lets go. Before the opening and at no other hour, exact as a stitch (T079).
  3. The boy is carried to the threshold in the basket with the fold at his chin and watches a fire come into the house from somewhere he cannot see and cannot reach. He extends the count to the light it makes: four, four, and a place where a fifth would be if there were a fifth. File that hypothetical fifth beside the bench's interval on the separate tray; it is not a sixth hearing and he has no hand to make one (T071/T072, T082).
  4. Somewhere offstage a collector takes the year's first measure. It is entered without a figure spoken in the house; the boy learns it only from the order of a door closing and a woman's fist shut. He cannot spend, correct or hand back what he hears (T077, T058).
  5. The ordinary work goes on around the basket: Jorra at the wheel in bone-coloured slip, the bent little finger going in ahead of his hand at the damper, nothing said about the back room (T035); Tarn sets the small knife on its sill, six of seven counted and the seventh held back, and says to the kindling that he does not count the next (T036). Garl's coat keeps its salt shape on its peg, his seat its hollow on the left, his proverb going to the fire and the pot (T009, T013).
  6. Sella feeds him barley gruel from the house's own dish with the spoon wiped on her sleeve, says *the house*, never *I*, and gives the instruction to the fire and the wool. The two crusts lie in the fold, one stiff since Stone 15 and one still smelling faintly of barley smoke when the wind turns; neither is eaten, lifted or named (T060, T086, T044).
  7. At dusk she banks the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the mark, because a bank is not an opening. The kiln takes its breath in fours and misses one. Nobody has spoken to the basket all day (T065, T058).

- **Emotional turn:** A fire enters the house from an unseen source and the boy can only count the light it makes; the household takes the gift as ordinary work and enters neither its origin nor its payer. The old-life surgeon recognises a kept heat handed from vessel to vessel and is the only person in the room with no standing to ask who paid.
- **Sensory anchors:** cold ash and a coal under ash in a clay dish; tallow and wet wool; woodsmoke lifting off the yard before the light clears the sill; lead-glaze under Sella's thumb at her throat; the hypothetical fifth in the firelight; barley gruel thinned with milk and the spoon on her sleeve; the brown edge of the stale crust beside the fresh one; the kiln's four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T079 (mark only before the opening), T071/T072 (count extends to the carried light and the hypothetical fifth), T082 (a fifth that would be, filed as an interval), T077 (first measure entered offstage, no figure given), T009 and T013 (coat, seat, unswept ridge), T036, T035, T060, T086, T044, T065, T058.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the passed flame toward a source somebody pays to keep (the Hearth as a wound, far off); plants the hypothetical fifth toward a note nearly full and never quite; plants the first measure of the year toward the figure the district will not truly give. Pays nothing; joins nothing.
- **Exact last image:** Coals banked under ash with the back of a broken pot, the boy on his bed with the two crusts in the fold, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down, and the fire keeping four, four and a gap.

## Scene 2 — The Clean Voice

- **Day and time:** Day 26, Weighing, Y.H. 812, morning at the fringe of the Cross and the edge of Slate Row, back under the Vail roof by dusk. A named day, no market.
- **Place:** The Cross, the edge of Slate Row and the doorway of the Court's room (seen and heard, not entered); the Vail kiln-house at night.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-six days old, carried in the basket), Sella Vail, Tarn, Berr Clay (glimpsed, one short appearance, no explanation), an officer reading in a clean voice (walk-on, no sheet), neighbours come back from the reading (walk-ons); Garl as coat, hollow seat and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Sella carries him down with her half-pace kept from every open slate and sets him at the outer fringe, where the reading carries but no slate lies open. She gives the instruction to the wool about the river wind and does not look toward his face (T058).
  2. The year's measures are read aloud in a clean voice. It wears a temple's clothes: the same passive grammar Berr used at the Cross --- the measure banked, the account standing, the return recorded, nothing lost that we have kept --- now in an officer's cadence with the market noise taken out. The speaker is taken out of his own sentences as neatly as ever (T047).
  3. Somebody else's whole return is announced for a person not in the room. The name is read out and the household does not repeat it. The boy hears a figure he cannot spend, correct or hand back, and does not learn whose return it was (T015, T077). Do not say forty; do not let it be his own return.
  4. Berr Clay is glimpsed at the edge of the reading with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and green glaze at one corner of his mouth, wiping his thumb before he touches his slate. He explains nothing, looks nowhere near the basket, and goes off with the box in his house still locked (T041).
  5. A name is read that the house will not speak afterward. The boy counts the silence after it the way he counts the kiln's missing breath, and files it among the gaps rather than the hearings. The count stays five from four sources (T073, T082, T061, T071/T072).
  6. The Court's room reaches him only as a voice through a doorway: a bench by the door, a pot on it, a draught under the door going along the floor to the corner, a place in the figures where a line should stand and does not. He sets this room in its own place beside the birth room, the Vail kiln-house and the death-house and writes no cause where he has only noticed a likeness (T081).
  7. Back under the roof: no opening, so the mark is not touched; the fold is put right without a look at his face; the crusts stay where they were put and Tarn's knife is back on its sill with the seventh year still held back (T060, T086, T036).

- **Emotional turn:** The institution's grammar is harder to answer in a clean voice than in a market shout; the boy's only reply is to count the silence around a name the house will not say. A whole return is announced for somebody absent and the room that announces it has the same bench, pot, draught and gap as the room that kept him.
- **Sensory anchors:** the clean voice carrying over boards; red wool folded under a coat; green at the corner of a mouth; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; the flat note of a slate left where nobody tapped it for him; a draught under a door; a pot on a bench; the silence after a name; river wind caught under the fold going home.
- **Threads advanced:** T047 (passive grammar in a temple voice), T015 and T077 (figure heard and unspendable), T073 (name read and not repeated), T082 and T061 (silence after a name filed with the gaps, no sixth hearing), T081 (Weighing as another room with bench, pot, draught and gap), T058, T041, T036, T060, T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the clean voice toward Derren's grammar and the Court's borrowed temple forms; plants the whole return for somebody absent toward the Forty and the private purchase; plants the unspoken name toward Dessa's name at her death and Marra's unfinished apology; plants the doorway room toward the Weighing Hall and Ledgerhand. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The silence after a name going off the yard and the boy lying under the Vail roof counting it beside the kiln's gap, with the two crusts in the fold and nobody having said whose return was announced.

## Scene 3 — The Long Night

- **Day and time:** Day 27, the Long Night, Y.H. 812; ordinary work through the short day, then dusk into a night with no fire after dark. A named day, no market.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the wheel-shed and the Clay Yard; the dark room under the roof.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-seven days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed (among the storytellers); Garl Vail as a coat-shaped presence in the dark and a proverb said to the pot.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The short day is spent in the ordinary work --- drying, mending, sweeping, the wheel, the damper moved before Sella has finished --- while the cold sets in early and the boy's fold and crusts stay where they are.
  2. At dusk the fire is damped and no fire is relit. The house sits in the dark and tells stories of the Return: a child come back thin who knew warmth before it knew a face; a man returned with somebody else's hands, in Tarn's flat words; a hearth-maid's account from Alda in which dates stand where wonder should be, going on after the question has stopped (T007/T008).
  3. The boy listens to the voices instead of the flames and counts the gaps between them: the space between a question and its answer, the place where a name should stand, the point where a story stops short. He files all three with the intervals on the separate tray, not among the five hearings from four sources. The count stays five (T082, T061, T071/T072).
  4. In one telling of the year the bell comes back: four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody in the story crossing himself. He sets the told bell beside the heard bell and keeps them in two places, because a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anyone put one there (T078).
  5. Cold arrives in its known order: cheek, then temple, then wrist. The child in him wants the breast and the crust that is given; the surgeon notes how a room cools and how a body worsens before it settles, and the two registers quarrel without a word. Neither want is answered (T044, T060).
  6. Tarn says in the dark that six years are counted and the next he does not count, and sets the knife where it can be checked (T036). Jorra says nothing about the back room. Garl's coat holds a broader man's shape in the dark and his proverb goes to the pot, not to the basket (T009).
  7. The Long Night ends without fire and without an explanation; the boy is twenty-seven days old and still carried.

- **Emotional turn:** The house keeps warmth by telling rather than by burning, and the boy learns that a story has a gap with the same shape as a vessel's interval. Counting the dark is still counting, and still worth nothing to anybody in the room.
- **Sensory anchors:** no firelight, only tallow gone out and breath in the dark; wet wool and the fold at his ear; a voice's cadence in a room with no light; the gap after a question before the answer; the bell told rather than heard; cold at cheek, temple and wrist; the small knife set on its sill in the dark; the salt-stiff coat seen as a shape rather than a colour.
- **Threads advanced:** T082 (gaps between voices filed as intervals), T078 (bell in the telling), T061 and T071/T072 (count kept at five, file grows by told gaps), T036 (seventh year held), T060 and T086 (wanting the crust, neither crust eaten), T044 (want and the unanswerable gift), T009 (coat and proverb), T065 (fire damped and held), T007/T008 (Alda's dates where a story should have a name).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the told gaps toward the third thing, a vessel nearly full and never quite; plants the bell in the telling toward the Court's borrowed temple forms; plants Tarn's words in the dark toward his later refusal of a whole return; plants the fireless room toward the closed counting rooms of the war. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The house sitting in the dark with the fire out and the boy counting the space between one voice and the next, cold come to his cheek and wrist, the two crusts where they were put.

## Scene 4 — The Name Left Out

- **Day and time:** Day 28, the Remembering, Y.H. 812, names spoken through the yard in the morning and the Vail house keeping its own quiet through the day. A named day, no market.
- **Place:** The Clay Yard, the threshold and the room under the Vail roof; the kiln ash where the charred slate lies.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-eight days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Alda Reed; neighbours speaking names along the yard (walk-ons); Garl Vail as the name that is not spoken and the objects that hold his place.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Names of the dead pass from house to house along the yard. The boy hears them go by and does not know which of the sounds are names; he counts the spaces between them without meaning to (T071/T072).
  2. Under the Vail roof Garl's name is not spoken. The coat keeps its peg, the wheel-seat its hollow on the left, the Ash ridge its corner turning without a break, and the proverb goes to the fire and the pot and never to the basket (T009, T013).
  3. Sella goes around the name the way she goes around Ash left in a corner --- a careful circle and no word for the thing circled --- and does not say it to the fire, to Jorra or to the wool (T058).
  4. The boy counts the silence where the name should be and files it with the bench's missing note, the half-heard note past the door, the cracked pot in the yard and the bell's gap. It is a gap, not a hearing; the count stays five from four sources (T082, T061).
  5. Alda Reed comes with the birth-slates under her arm and smells of soap and warm milk. Where a name should stand she gives an exact date; she touches the tin once inside her bodice without opening it, presses the fold flat too long at his shoulder, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing she has done it, and covers the sign with the same unasked-for date she gave Dessa (T007/T008, T023, T073).
  6. The hand goes to the pocket-place and finds wool where a small lit rectangle with a name had lain. The shape of a promise about a boat on cold water comes and goes in the old life's register, in past tense, untagged; no name is looked at and nothing crosses back that he can carry (T044).
  7. The charred slate still lies in the kiln ash, black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark, swept around and never picked up (T031). The two crusts stay in the fold (T086, T060).
  8. Tarn's knife is back on its sill and the seventh year held back; Jorra comes in with grey slate dust worked into the creases past the bent little finger, says nothing about where he has been, and moves the damper before Sella has finished (T036, T035).

- **Emotional turn:** A district remembers by saying names while the house that keeps the boy remembers by not saying one; the absence has the same held shape as a seam that never rang. The women who are kind cover what they will not say with dates, and the only person who sees both gestures cannot hand either back.
- **Sensory anchors:** names passing along the yard and the spaces between them; the lead-glazed mark at Sella's throat untouched, because this is not an opening; the salt-stiff coat on its peg and the hollow wheel-seat; grey Ash turning its corner without a break; soap and warm milk and the cord of a tin under a bodice; two fingers at a red edge; a date where a name should stand; wool in a pocket-place where a lit rectangle had been; a charred edge in cold kiln ash; fine grey dust in a thrower's creases.
- **Threads advanced:** T009 and T013 (Garl's objects, the unswept ridge), T031 (charred slate untouched), T073 (name withheld and a date in its place), T007/T008 (unaware sign covered by an exact date), T023 (tin touched once, unopened), T044 (pocket-place, boat promise, name unlooked-at), T082 and T061 (silence filed with the gaps, no sixth hearing), T071/T072, T058, T035/T036, T060/T086.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the unspoken name toward Jorra's later use of Dessa's name as a weapon and toward Dessa's death on Day 38; plants the charred slate toward the spiral under the world; plants Alda's tin and turned note toward the Second Want record; plants the date-in-place-of-a-name toward the lie curdling. Pays nothing; no thread is closed.
- **Exact last image:** The boy under the Vail roof with a silence where Garl's name should be, the salt coat on its peg and the grey ridge turning its corner, the charred slate lying in the ash and the two crusts in the fold.

## Scene 5 — The Grey Left Where It Lay

- **Day and time:** Day 29, the Last Day, Y.H. 812, baking through the day into dusk. A named day, no market; the grey is still in the corners at night.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house, the oven and the fire, the Clay Yard and the drying racks; the ridge under the skirting board and the neighbour's house two doors down.
- **Present:** Corin (twenty-nine days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, Mira Silt (brisk, a few lines, no third crust), a woman at the tubs who says the grey will be burned in the morning (walk-on); Garl as coat, seat, ridge and proverb.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. Bread is baked: the house holds oven heat and the smell of barley smoke, and the brown-edged loaves come out set a little apart from one another. The boy is carried near the warmth but cannot take anything; the two crusts already in the fold stay where they were put, neither eaten, lifted nor named, and no third crust is added (T060, T086).
  2. Mira Silt comes up from the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and the top of her left ear gone, answers a question with a question and her own answer, and flicks an eel-bone against her thumb before putting it away. When a figure is spoken her thumb goes under her apron to the grey wool cord; shifting her weight shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never taken upriver and never lifted at a door. The knots are not counted (T025, T059).
  3. Sella will not let anyone sweep the Ash early. A woman at the tubs says the grey will be left where it lies until the Last Day and burned the next morning and called dealt with, as though a thing called dealt with had ever been a thing finished (T013).
  4. The ridge is measured again by the width of a thumb --- near two thumbs at Vail and a finger's width thicker two doors down where the neighbour's ridge turns its corner without a break --- and left where it lies. The boy reads a thing left in place as still accounted for, and files the thumb beside the ridge, the charred slate and the barrow of grey without joining them (T013, T009, T031, T071/T072).
  5. Garl's coat keeps its salt shape on its peg, his seat its hollow on the left, and his proverb goes to the pot and the fire: leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled (T009).
  6. The two crusts, one stiff since Stone 15 and one still smelling of barley smoke when the wind turns, lie side by side and are neither entered nor spoken of. A gift that cannot be answered is still a gift that cannot be refused (T086, T060, T044).
  7. Tarn sets the knife on its sill and says six are counted and the seventh is not; Jorra goes to the damper before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low and does not say the back room is cold (T036, T035).
  8. At the bank she turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the mark. The kiln keeps four, four and a gap, and the grey is still in the corner when the day ends (T079, T065).

- **Emotional turn:** The household bakes bread and leaves its dead where they lay; the boy reads the unswept ridge as an account kept without moving anything, and understands that a thing can be left in place and still be counted. The women's cords and the house's loaves are the same kind of record, and neither has a name written on it.
- **Sensory anchors:** oven heat, barley smoke, brown edges of hot loaves; eel slime and river silt on Mira's sleeves; an eel-bone flicked against a thumb; grey wool cord under an apron with a second cord twisted twice beneath it; a thumb laid flat into grey Ash; the salt-stiff coat; dry thatch and the last brooms; the kiln's four, four and a gap.
- **Threads advanced:** T013 and T009 (Ash left and measured, coat and hollow seat), T031 (charred slate still in the ash), T060 and T086 (two crusts neither eaten nor named), T025 and T059 (grey cord and second cord glimpsed, knots uncounted), T044 (unanswerable gift), T071/T072 (thumb and ridge into the private file), T036, T035, T079, T065, T077 (figure passing and unspent).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** Plants the two crusts toward the day one is gone and nobody says who took it; plants the unburned ridge toward the morning it will be called gone; plants the left-in-place ridge toward the later heresy that a house can be left where it is and still be kept; plants Mira's two cords toward the reading of both years hence. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** The brown-edged loaves cooling and the grey ridge still running to the corner without a break, the two crusts in the fold and the boy's hand going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Scene 6 — The Account Carried In

- **Day and time:** Day 30, Ilmen 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813, the Opening of the Slates; dawn into night. The era turns on this day and the count begins again at Firstday.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house and the Clay Yard, Low Kiln; Slate Row spoken of as a direction, not entered; the Ilm under its ice spoken of, not reached.
- **Present:** Corin (thirty days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; a neighbour who calls the burned grey dealt with (walk-on); Garl Vail as coat, hollow seat and ridge already gone from the corner.
- **Beats, in order:**

  1. The morning after the Last Day: the grey that was left where it lay is burned and called gone. The boy watches a thing accounted for by being left in place now accounted for by being called dealt with, and keeps the two ways of keeping under the same hand (T013, T009). Sella does not say it; a neighbour does.
  2. The coals are still banked under their ash. There is no opening of the kiln, so the widow's mark is not touched --- the rule holds across the turn of the year as exactly as it held within it (T079). The flame that came in on Kindling is still in the house, under ash.
  3. It is the Opening of the Slates. The new year's accounts are opened in Slate Row, spoken of from the yard as a direction and not a place anybody means to go today; one in twelve stays printed where it was and unchallenged, and the true deduction is spoken by nobody (T015, T077). No Court scene is entered.
  4. The boy is thirty days old. His private count goes on into a year the district has already entered on its slates: the kiln's four, four and a gap, the kindling's empty fifth, the bell's gap, the missing note on the bench, the ridge, the crusts, the five unjoined pauses (T071/T072, T082).
  5. Work turns toward the thaw and the slip pits: Jorra at the wheel with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading at the damper; Tarn sweeping with the knife back on its sill and the seventh year held back yet again; the yard calling the weather what it calls the weather while the Ilm still holds under its ice (T035, T036, T085). The small pale river stone lies in the fold on the abandoned bed in House Silt and is not mentioned.
  6. Sella puts the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, feeds him from the house's own dish and gives the instruction to the fire and the wool. She says nothing about where any of the warmth came from or who paid for it (T060, T058). The two crusts are still in the fold (T086).
  7. Nothing has been joined: five hearings of the flat note from four sources and no sixth; intervals and the silence where a name should be filed apart; five pauses lying side by side and not joined; the Drawing collector's ordinary hand with no pause in its own place; the charred slate untouched in the kiln ash. He is cracked, mute, unranked, below Thin, carried everywhere, unable to read Grathic or lift a hand to his own chin.
  8. Final image: the boy on his bed under the Vail roof with the fire keeping four, four and a gap and the count carried into a year already entered on the district's slates, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down.

## Pull into Chapter 9

Day 30 closes with the year opened on the district's slates and the boy's count going on unentered. The Ash is gone and called gone; the fire keeps four, four and a gap; the two crusts are still in the fold; five hearings from four sources have no sixth, five pauses lie unjoined, the charred slate is untouched, the pocket-place still holds wool, and the Ilm still holds under its ice. Nothing has been explained or joined, and the boy is thirty days old, mute, cracked and carried. Chapter 9, *The River Under the Ice*, opens on Day 31, Ilmen 2, Y.H. 813, Secondday, at the yard well: a woman lets the bucket down and says the water tastes different; a man come up from the Ilm says the ice is honeyed under the mud; nobody says the word \lore{the Breaking}. Forward, not closed.
