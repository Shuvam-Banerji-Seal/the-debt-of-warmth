# Chapter 3 — “One in Twelve”: beat sheet

**Day covered.** Day 4 only — Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday. Account-day and the last market before the Five. One day, five scenes, morning into afternoon. No recap of the taking; the boy is four days old, already under the Vail roof, and the reader has just left him there.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — “a warm stone, already going cold.” This is the chapter in which the household's private warmth is carried, for the first time, into the district that prices it. The stone is still in House Silt; it is not mentioned or retrieved.

**Chapter-level movement.** The boy leaves a house that kept him without explanation and is carried into a market that explains him as a figure. Nothing is revealed. What changes is his position: he is no longer only under a roof that will not say why it keeps him — he is physically present at the reading of the account that will carry him, and he hears that account answer flat. He acquires a third flat note and a new count. Sella's widow's mark, held privately in her house, is publicly confirmed that morning as a kindness that is entered, so that the private keeping and the Court's slate are shown to be the same shape of account. The Hearth Drawing of Stone 12 is two days off and pulls at the end of every scene.

**Voice discipline.** Close third-person past, inside Dan/Corin. Present tense only for Chicago breaking through, one or two sentences, never for anticipation. `\memory{...}` only for Ostrowski's remembered words. No character explains the deduction, the Return, the Warm, the flat sound, or the boy's origin. No game words. No modern simile in Sarn. Images from clay, slip, tallow, banked coals, grey Ash, river ice, silt, iron, lead, wet wool, salt, bone, birch bark, the slate. The body humiliates him in every scene: drool, the head, the wool, the hand that cannot reach or lift.

**Characters on stage.** Corin/Daniel Marchetti; Sella Vail; Berr Clay, Thane of Clay; Alda Reed; Mira Silt (background only, Scene 5); Lena Ostrowski (remembered voice, Scene 3); Garl Vail (absence — the uncalled debt, Scene 4); an unnamed Sill assessor and a collector's clerk (one line and one gesture each, Scenes 2 and 4 — new slugs `sill-assessor` and `collectors-clerk`; log them in `characters/_minor.md` when closing, and give either a full sheet only if it speaks a second time). **Tarn and Jorra stay offstage all chapter. Marra does not appear and is not named; the clerk is not she and must not be mistaken for her.**

## Scene 1 — The Walk to the Cross

**Day and time.** Day 4, Stone 10, first light into early morning. Dry cold after the sleet; breath shows; the Ilm under ice.

**Place.** From the Vail kiln-house across the Clay Yard, down toward the Cross. The market arriving in layers.

**Present.** Sella Vail carrying Corin in a basket under a fold of wool; the boy; unnamed yard women and market people as noise and movement. A kiln breathing behind them.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the last steps out of the Clay Yard, not on a thought. The basket under the fold, the kiln's breath going out behind him, the market arriving in strata: pigs, clay dust, the moot shouting, a bell that is not a church bell. No explanation of what the bell is.
2. He counts his steps because there is nothing else to count, and arrives at a number that is not four and four and a gap. The new number goes into the private count beside the old one. This is counting as the old life counted exits, not a gift.
3. The body's public humiliation: drool he cannot wipe, the wool folding off his face, the head that goes where it wants, the small animal sound when he turns past a point. He cannot push the fold back. Sella does it for him without looking, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*.
4. Sella does not set the basket where a slate is open. She answers a question about the boy with an instruction to the wool or the weather — a finished feeling refused in the form of practical care.
5. Weather and work arrive as texture: the oiled paper lifting, the yard well frozen at the lip, a woman at a tub, barley and tallow smoke, the smell of the pigs over the smell of clay.
6. Close as the shouting resolves into the reading; the market is now a room he is inside and cannot leave.

**Emotional turn.** From the private, object-known warmth of the Vail roof into the public cold of a crowd that has not looked at him yet. The turn is not fear — it is the surgeon's recognition that he has been moved from a room where he was kept into a room where he will be counted, and that he cannot address either room.

**Sensory anchors.** Wool with woodsmoke in it; dry cold in the nose; breath smoke; clay dust on the wind; pigs and wet straw; the not-a-church bell; the kiln's receding breath; oiled paper lifting; a frozen well-lip.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (the count extended to the walk and to the market's numbers; attention as the untaxable entry). T058/T075 (kindness in a form he cannot answer or refuse — the fold put back by a hand that says nothing). T077 (the number the district gives out, approaching). T060 (maintenance as the shape love takes here, lightly).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Plants the market as the place where the year's figures are read aloud and therefore where a child's life becomes a line in an account (paid in Scenes 2 and 5, and across Chapters 6 and 7). Plants the bell that is not a church bell — a Court object doing a temple's work, not explained. The count's new number is kept, not decoded; the gap stays a gap.

**Exact last image.** The boy in the basket under the fold of wool, the market shouting resolving around him, his own step-count a new number in his head that he cannot say and nobody has asked for. Forward, not closed.

## Scene 2 — One in Twelve, as the Notice Says

**Day and time.** Day 4, mid-morning, Account-day. The moot at the Cross before the pigs are weighed.

**Place.** The Cross, market square: two hundred people, eight pigs, one assessor, Berr Clay's reading slate on its block.

**Present.** Berr Clay; the unnamed Sill assessor (one line); Sella with the basket at the edge of the crowd; Corin; the crowd as a single leaning body.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Establish the crowd's hunger for the figure: the moot shouting, then Berr wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate. The slate is clean; the gesture is for the hand. Do not point at the meaning.
2. He says *one in twelve, as the notice says* in a low voice; the shouting stops because the crowd has to lean in. He never says *I* — he says *the district* and *the account*. He apologises in the form of an instruction.
3. The levy is read as a routine figure. The boy hears his own kind priced without being named, and cannot turn his head far enough to see the face of the man pricing him. Read the not-looking as he once read a resident who would not meet an attending's eye.
4. The unnamed assessor supplies one cold passive line — the figure stands as read, or equivalent — and nothing more. No exposition from either officer.
5. Berr sets the slate down and it gives a small flat sound. The boy hears it: the third flat note in four days, after the cup across the birth room and the pot set down in the Vail house, here the whole district's account under a knuckle he cannot lift. Set it beside the other two without joining them. **Nobody taps a slate for him. The narration does not say the sounds are the same in Grathic, and no Grathic word is given for either.**
6. Sella does not put the basket where the slate lies open. The crowd leans, hears the figure, and goes back to the pigs.

**Emotional turn.** The crowd wants a number and receives one; the boy receives a sound. The collision is between a district comforted by a printed figure and an infant hearing that figure answer dead. It is not a triumph of perception — the hearing is useless to him and he knows it.

**Sensory anchors.** Apron and clay dust; the clean slate; the moot's roar dropping to a lean; pig smell and wet straw; chalk; the small flat knock; thumb on an apron; the assessor's cold voice.

**Threads advanced.** T015 (one in twelve printed — the seed of the true figure, which is never spoken here). T008 (the levy as the language in which a child's life is priced; the boy present at his own kind's price). T077 (the number the district gives out). T061 (the flat note carried into the market — third hearing). T041 (the care Berr takes with the slate and the *as the notice says* lid over a figure; the locked box not named or shown). T047 (the grammar of the Keeping beginning to sound aloud).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Pays the Account-day setup from `bible/calendar.md`. Plants Berr's reasonable cruelty — a man with a clean slate and a good reason, the local face of the Court — for his Arc 5–6 return. Plants the deduction as a printed figure that will one day be read against a roll. The flat note is planted as a physical fact the narration refuses to interpret.

**Exact last image.** Berr's slate lying on its block with the figure on it, the crowd leaning and then dispersing toward the pigs, and the boy in the basket with a third useless flat note in his ear.

## Scene 3 — You're Back On in Ten

**Day and time.** Day 4, mid-morning, while the market goes on around the reading.

**Place.** The Cross, at the edge of the crowd, the basket on the ground beside a barrel.

**Present.** Corin in the basket; Sella nearby but not addressing him; the market's noise; Ostrowski as a voice in the room, not an arrival.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The market noise — haggling, the bell, a pig driven over the boards — and then Ostrowski's voice cuts across it with no tag and no transition: “Marchetti. You're back on in ten.” Written in the present tense for the break. Use `\memory{...}` for the remembered words only.
2. The second line arrives: the first duty is to decide, the second is to carry it, and he seems to have trouble with the second. The voice is a file taken to a man; give it the full name as an instrument.
3. For one breath the clay yard, the pigs and the trauma bay occupy the same paragraph — machine coffee, lemon disinfectant, a ceiling light, a body on a table — then the market returns. Do not explain the shift or tag the memory as memory.
4. The boy reaches for a pocket that holds no phone. The hand is a small soft wrist that cannot complete the gesture. He does not look at a name. T044 as an absence, not an explanation.
5. The clinical register gets the better of the infant for one sentence: he knows what a room does to a man who has to decide in it and then stay. Here he is not the man deciding — he is the thing being carried, and the second duty is performed on him by other people.
6. The market's ordinary life closes over the break: a crust broken, a coin counted, a pig weighed.

**Emotional turn.** From the market into the bay and back in one breath. The old life's most exact voice offers the maxim that made him and excuses him; in a Sarnish market it lands on a body that cannot decide or carry. The feeling is not nostalgia — it is dry recognition of the gap between the man the voice addresses and the infant who hears it.

**Sensory anchors.** Machine coffee and disinfectant against pigs and clay; a ceiling light against the low winter sky; a pocket with nothing in it; the bell; a coin counted; tallow and barley.

**Threads advanced.** T046 (the two duties — decide, then carry — planted in the first remembered-voice scene). T044 (the call as an absence; the phone-pocket reach). T004 (the lit phone and the name not looked at, reached toward but not seen). T047 (the institution's grammar echoing outward, begun here and completed in Scene 4's Berr). T071 (the room counted: exits, hands, the barrel, the block).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Plants the maxim that will migrate into Rusk's questions and become indistinguishable from them in Arc 9. Plants the gap between carrying and confessing that Ostrowski herself could not close — her contradiction lives in the sentence about the second duty. Nothing here explains Chicago; the machines are named only inside the memory, where they belong.

**Exact last image.** A pig driven over the boards, a coin counted twice, and the boy's small hand closed on nothing where a pocket should have been, while the market goes on without a gap.

## Scene 4 — The Neat Hand

**Day and time.** Day 4, late morning into noon, at the edge of the market near the Slate Row end of the Cross.

**Place.** The fringe of the Cross where the Court's table is set; Slate Row is spoken of as a direction, not entered.

**Present.** Corin in the basket; Sella; Berr Clay; the collector's clerk (one line, one gesture); Alda Reed with the birth-slates; Garl Vail as the uncalled debt spoken around, never named in this scene.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The collector's clerk handles a slate whose upper line is in a strange neat hand. The name was written before the face and nobody in the market remarks on it. Give the clerk one gesture — touching the slate before the person — and one line in the soft passive. He is an officer, not a monster, and has a reason. He is not Marra; do not describe a grey coat or grey fingers in a way that lets the reader join them.
2. The boy reads the pause between strokes. It is the pause he has set beside the pause at the flare and the pause on the birth-slate in House Silt. He keeps three things side by side and does not make a fourth. He cannot read Grathic, so this is the shape of a hand and the rhythm of a stop, not a name.
3. Berr comes to Sella. His line: her widow's mark is confirmed by the factor at Greybank; that is a kindness, and kindness is entered. He says it as an instruction, never as a threat, and never says *I*. Dan hears Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* inside the sentence — this is the first outward echo; in Chapter 2 the echo was interior. Do not name Vesci in the narration of Sarn.
4. Sella does not answer Berr about the mark. She answers the wool and the boy — an instruction to a thing, not a person. The uncalled debt Garl left against a shipment of tiles hangs in the space between them, spoken around and never named; Berr's care not to call it is another kindness entered nowhere.
5. Alda Reed, keeping the birth-slates, touches the tin inside her bodice once. She glimpses the edge of the flower at the boy's wrist where the wool has fallen back, makes the two-finger sign without knowing she has done it in front of him, and covers it with an exact date nobody asked for. No one comments.
6. Close on the two keepings set side by side: the house's private roll and the Court's slate, both careful hands, both entering a boy who has not agreed to either.

**Emotional turn.** The private kindness of the house and the public kindness of the Court are revealed to share a grammar — both give a thing the recipient cannot refuse and enter it somewhere. The boy's suspicion of unanswerable kindness takes a second shape; he still cannot say it or refuse it.

**Sensory anchors.** Clean chalk on slate; the clerk's cuff; the lead smell on Berr's breath; the widow's mark under Sella's collar; the little-door burn closing on the wool; tin on a cord under a wool shawl; the flower's red edge at a wrist; the date arriving too fast.

**Threads advanced.** T005/T003 (the neat hand seen a second time; the name still unread and unspoken). T047 (the first outward echo of Vesci's grammar in Berr's mouth, paid from the interior echo of Chapter 2). T075 (the unanswerable kindness, now doubled). T057/T058 (Sella's kept roll and her wall — spoken around, not shown). T007 (the crossed sign made again before witnesses, unaware). T009 (the uncalled debt spoken around; Garl's warmth left where it lay). T041 (Berr's locked-box certainty implied by the care of the slate; box not shown).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** The pause at the flare, the pause on the birth-slate and the pause in the clerk's hand are now three; the joining is a Slate Row payoff years away and must not happen here. Berr's *kindness is entered* is the sentence Dan will one day hear from Derren in a room gone cold. Alda's sign and tin continue the second-record thread toward the floorboard.

**Exact last image.** Sella's burn-shaped hand closing on the fold of wool at the boy's wrist while, a pace away, the clerk's hand closes on the slate — two careful hands entering the same child in two different accounts, and the boy unable to lift his own.

## Scene 5 — What the District Gives Out

**Day and time.** Day 4, afternoon, the market wearing out before the Five.

**Place.** The Cross, then the road out toward the north gate and the Bone Orchard as an object passing in the distance.

**Present.** Corin in the basket; Sella; Berr Clay; Mira Silt in the background; the crowd thinning; Ash carried by unnamed labourers.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The afternoon's ordinary work: stalls struck, barley and eel smells, a bargain over short weight, the last pig. Mira Silt is a background figure — eel slime dried on her sleeves, the top of her left ear gone, an eel-bone flicked against her thumb. She asks Berr a question about the eel price and answers it herself. She says nothing about the basket and does not look at it.
2. Berr reads the slate a second time and wipes his thumb again before touching it. It gives its flat answer again. The boy keeps the second hearing with the first three without joining any of them.
3. Ash is carried out in a barrow toward the north gate and the Bone Orchard — grey, ordinary, nobody reading it. The boy reads it as a fact and puts it with the ridge under the Vail skirting board. No explanation of what Ash is.
4. The boy counts the strokes of the market bell: four, four, and a gap where the fifth should be. The new market number stays beside the old household number; neither is decoded.
5. The number of days to the Five is given out as three by a district that has counted what it has and does not want to say it. The Hearth Drawing of Stone 12 is two days off — the year's first measure, unnamed as such in the boy's ear.
6. Sella lifts the basket and turns back toward the Clay Yard with no word about where they have been or what was read. The market empties behind them.

**Emotional turn.** The district's cruelty has been ordinary all along — a figure, a flat sound, a barrow of Ash going out, and everybody going home. The boy's feeling is the surgeon's: he has watched a system close a matter with clean hands, and he has performed the same gesture himself. The chapter does not let him decide anything about it.

**Sensory anchors.** Struck stalls; eel slime and salt; the eel-bone's tick; short weight argued over; grey Ash in a barrow; the market bell's strokes; the dry cold deepening; wool and woodsmoke as Sella lifts the basket.

**Threads advanced.** T061 (the flat note heard a third time in the chapter, fourth overall including the Vail pot — still useless, still unnamed in Grathic). T077 (the number the district gives out; the Five three days off). T013 (the Ash going out, read as a fact). T071/T072 (the count kept and extended to the bell). T060 (brisk, unspoken kindness glimpsed in Mira's background presence). T025 (Mira's knot-account seeded only as the way she counts — the cord itself not shown; full appearance is Chapter 5).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** The barrow toward the Bone Orchard points at the shallow graves of thin-return children and at Dessa's uncollected death on Day 38, without naming either. The Drawing is named only as a date two days off; Chapter 4 will show the cold iron tube and the small flat knock of a vessel giving way — the sound the boy has now heard from a cup, a pot and a slate will arrive in a body. The market bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work, still unexplained.

**Exact last image.** Berr's slate set down a second time on its block, its flat answer going into the thinning market noise unremarked; the Ash going out in a barrow toward the Bone Orchard; and the boy in the basket counting the bell four, four, a gap, with a hand he cannot lift and a number he cannot say. No resolution. Forward motion.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 4

The chapter ends with the boy holding a sound he cannot place and a count he cannot use, carried back up the Clay Yard under the Vail roof. The flat note has travelled from a cup to a pot to the district's own slate, and each time he has been unable to lift a hand toward it. Chapter 4 gives him the same sound in the one place it will be made by a body: on Stone 12, the Hearth Drawing, a collection witnessed from a basket at a neighbour's house — the cold iron tube, the collector's fingers, the small flat knock of a vessel cracked all the way through, which he will recognise as the sound a body makes when it gives. Between them sits the quiet day of Day 5 in the Vail house: the banked coals, Sella touching the widow's mark before she opens the kiln, Tarn's broom stopping at a pot's flat note and saying nothing (T076). The account read aloud at the Cross has become an account taken from a person, and the boy still cannot speak, cannot read Grathic, cannot reach a cup, and is still carried everywhere he needs to go. The Five remain three days off by the district's telling, and the year's first measure has not yet been taken.
