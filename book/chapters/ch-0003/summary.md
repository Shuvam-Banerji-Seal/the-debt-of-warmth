## Chronicle

On the tenth stone of the year Sella Vail carried the boy out of the only warm room he knew, a basket on her hip, wool over his face. Behind them the kiln took one breath and missed the next, and the gap went with him. She put the fold back without looking and said the house had brought him, and the house, and nothing finer. He counted a hundred and nine steps to the road and kept the number where no tax could reach it. The market came up in layers --- pigs, clay dust, a roar, a bell that was not a church bell.

At the Cross two hundred people wanted a figure. Berr Clay wiped his thumb on his apron before touching the clean slate and said one in twelve, as the notice says, low enough that the square leaned in. He did not say I; the assessor added only that the figure stood as read. He heard his own kind priced above him and could not turn his head to see who priced them. Set down, the slate gave a flat knock --- the third in four days, after a cup and a Vail pot. He joined none of the three. Nobody tapped a slate for him.

Beside a barrel, a voice arrived that had not been in the square a moment before: Marchetti, you're back on in ten; the first duty is to decide, the second to carry it. For one breath the pigs and the trauma bay shared an air. He reached for a pocket with no phone in it, and the market closed over without a gap. Here he was not the man who decided or carried but the thing carried; the grammar of the bay and the block were one grammar.

At the Slate Row end a clerk touched a slate before he looked at the woman waiting; its upper line was in a strange neat hand with a small stop, seen for the third time. Berr told Sella her widow's mark was confirmed at Greybank, a kindness, and kindness was entered; the debt against the tiles he left uncalled. Sella answered the wool. Alda Reed touched the tin at her breast, crossed two fingers at the flower's red edge on his wrist without knowing it, and covered the sign with a date. Two careful hands entered one child in two accounts; neither asked him.

In the afternoon the market came apart. Mira Silt asked the eel price and answered it herself, never looking at the basket. A second reading gave a fourth flat answer; Ash went out in a barrow toward the north gate, and the bell counted four, four and a gap. Three days were given to the Five; the Hearth Drawing was two. Sella lifted the basket and turned up the yard without a word, wool smelling of woodsmoke, the door not quite shutting. He carried back numbers he could not spend and no hand to lift toward them.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Late afternoon of Account-day: Sella lifts the basket off the iron post, puts the ridden-back fold right without looking, says something to the wind about the river, and turns back up the Clay Yard toward the Clay Yard house with its banked coals. The market empties behind her; the slate lies on its block at the Cross with its second flat answer going into the noise of stalls being taken down; the barrow of Ash goes on toward the north gate unread; the bell goes on being a bell. The boy counts four, four, a gap, his small hand outside the fold going up an inch and coming down. The house comes back into view and its door does not quite shut. No word has been said to him all day and no one has looked into the basket.

- **Day and time at the end.** Day 4 complete --- Stone 10, Y.H. 812, a Tenthday, Account-day and the last market before the Five. Morning into late afternoon of one day; no jump. Day 5 (Stone 11, Firstday) is next; the Hearth Drawing falls on Day 6 (Stone 12, Secondday), two days off by the boy's count.

- **Where everyone is now.** Corin/Dan, four days old, is in the basket under the wool, being carried back up the Clay Yard, cold, hungry, drooling, awake, with a new count and no voice. Sella Vail carries him, says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, and has given no account of where they went or what was read. Berr Clay is back toward the moot after confirming the widow's mark; the red wool cord is folded square, the lead on his breath, the uncalled debt still uncalled. The unnamed Sill assessor and the collector's clerk remain at the Cross and the Court table respectively; the clerk touched the slate before the woman and said *it's entered as it stands*. Alda Reed is at the fringe of the market with her bundle of birth-slates, the tin inside her bodice, her date spoken to nobody. Mira Silt has left the eel stall after asking her own question and shoving a loose board clear; she never once looked at the basket. Tarn and Jorra are offstage all chapter, still under the Vail roof; Marra is not present and is not named; Dessa is not seen and is still believed to have lost him in the levy.

- **Items, money, powers.** No physical possession gained or lost. The pale stone remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned bed in House Silt; no phone, no pocket, no coat; the basket and wool are Sella's, and the charred slate is still in the kiln ash. No coins change hands; the figures heard are one in twelve, Mira's *two marks the pair and two short*, the turnip prices, eight pigs, and three days to the Five. No power used and no rank change: still a cracked, low-capacity, unranked vessel who cannot move Warm, clamp, speak, read Grathic, or lift a hand two inches to his own chin. The only instruments are the old-life habit of counting and the ear for the flat note; both remain useless. The cost of the day is exposure, cold and humiliation --- drool, the head that goes where it wants, the hand closing on air --- and the knowledge that he was counted in a square where nobody addressed him.

- **Facts, lies, debts.** One in twelve was spoken aloud twice and accepted; the true deduction was not spoken and Berr's box remains unshown. Berr told Sella her widow's mark was confirmed by the factor at Greybank, that this was a kindness and kindness was entered; he left the uncalled debt against the firing of tiles where it lay, neither calling nor settling it. Sella did not answer him about the mark. Alda's covering date --- *Stone seven; the account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out* --- was given with no question in the air. The widow's mark is a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, touched only before the kiln is opened. The bell is a Court instrument doing a temple's work and nobody explains it. No promise was made and no bargain struck; two accounts (the house's private roll and the Court's slate) entered him without asking, and the warmth of the stone and the crust remains unentered.

- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (private count: one hundred and nine steps, five then eight pigs, four ways out, six hands, two hoop-strips, the bell's four-four-and-a-gap); T058/T075 (kindness that cannot be answered cannot be refused --- Sella's fold, Berr's *kindness is entered*); T077 (the number the district gives out, twice, and three days to the Five); T015 (one in twelve printed and read); T008 (a child's kind priced in the figure); T061 (flat note heard from Berr's slate in the morning as the third hearing in four days and again in the afternoon as the fourth overall --- three vessels, four hearings); T041 (Berr's care with the clean slate, box still unshown); T046 and T047 (Ostrowski's two duties, then Vesci's *that's what a chart is for* heard outward in Berr's mouth); T044 and T004 (the pocket reached for, no phone, the name still not looked at); T005/T003 (the neat hand with its stop seen a third time); T057/T058 (Sella's roll and her wall spoken around); T007 (Alda's crossed sign made unaware); T009 (Garl's uncalled debt spoken around); T013 (Ash going out in the barrow); T060 and T025 (Mira's brisk kindness; her counting seeded, cord not shown).

- **Planted and withheld.** Berr's reasonable cruelty points at his Arc 5-6 return; the printed figure is planted to be read one day against a roll; the barrow toward the Bone Orchard points at the shallow graves and at Dessa's death on Day 38 without naming either; the Drawing is only a date two days off, awaiting the cold iron tube and the flat knock of a vessel giving in Chapter 4; Ostrowski's maxim is planted to migrate into Rusk's questions in Arc 9; Alda's tin and sign continue toward the floorboard. Still withheld: forty, Marra's name and coat, one in three, any Grathic word for the flat sound, Sella's roll, Berr's box, Mira's cord, and any joining of the three pauses.

- **For Chapter 4 and after.** Keep him cracked, unranked, mute, unable to read Grathic or reach a cup; keep the two hands separate and the gap in the count unexplained. Remember that the slate gave its flat answer twice in this chapter, so the evening count stands at four hearings from three vessels, and that next the same sound must arrive from a body at the Hearth Drawing. Day 5 is the quiet day in the Vail house --- banked coals, Sella touching the widow's mark before she opens the kiln, Tarn's broom stopping at the pot's flat note and saying nothing (T076) --- and Tarn and Jorra return to the page there. Do not explain the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate or why Sella took him.
