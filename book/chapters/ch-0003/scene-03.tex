The market did not stop for the reading. That was the thing he kept coming back to, the way a man puts his tongue to a wound to find out if it is still there: the figure had been given a shape and spoken aloud, and the square had swallowed it, and now the square was about the business of being a market again as if nothing had been said over the top of it. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down on the ground beside a barrel at the edge of the crowd, and the movement was neither a putting-down nor a forsaking, only the shifting of a weight from one tired arm to the ground, the way a woman sets down a sack she means to take up again in a moment. The barrel was an ordinary thing, staves the colour of weak tea, two iron hoops gone rusty at the seam, a dry pale ring where a hundred mornings of cups had come and gone. Four days old, \person{Corin} lay against its curved side with his cheek to a stave; the wood was cold and smelled of the cooper's smoke and of barley, and through the stave the market came and went in small tremors the way a kiln wall trembles when the wheel in the shed takes a heavy piece. She did not look into the basket. She stood over it a moment with her hand on the barrel's rim, the little-door burn closing on the iron hoop and letting it go, and then turned a half-pace away toward the pigs, which was the distance she had kept all morning from anything with writing on it.

He counted the hoops. Two. A third had been mended once with a flat strip of iron that sat proud of the stave, and the iron was the colour of an old wound that has closed badly. He counted the staves up to the rim and lost the count where the light changed, and began again, because a count that is lost is not a count and he had learned to begin again rather than guess. It was the same arithmetic he had used on the floorboards of a house three doors down the yard, four and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the same he had used on the breaths of a fire through a wall, four and four and a gap where the fifth should be. \lore{The levy} had been read out on \lore{Account-day} as a figure and the figure had sat down among the pigs, and nobody in the square had looked into the basket to see what the figure might be carried toward.

The cold had got into his mouth under the wool again. A thread of drool went from his lip to the fold and stayed there, and there was nobody in that square who would wipe it and nobody he could ask to. He tried to push the fold back with the hand that had once reduced a shoulder in the dark, and the hand was a small soft thing outside the basket with milky nails that closed on air an inch from his cheek and came away. He had put a thousand shoulders back. He could not keep a blanket on his own chin. The joke of it had gone flat three days ago and the exactness had not, and the exactness was worse than the joke had ever been. The head went where it wanted, and past a certain point it took a small wet sound with it, and the sound got the wool put right by a hand that did not look and was gone again before he could be ashamed of it.

He counted the room because it was a room and rooms had to be counted before they could be borne. Two ways out toward the bridges, a steep street climbing to \place{High Kiln}, the road going north toward \place{the Bone Orchard}: four ways out of \place{the Cross} and not one of them a door he could open. Hands: the driver's hand at the lean pig's ear, a woman's hand on her daughter's head still tight from the figure, the assessor's hands that never touched anything all morning, Sella's hand on the barrel rim, a baker's hand breaking a crust at a stall. Six hands he could see and not one of them his. The slate lay open on its block three paces off with the figure turned up to the light, and he kept the block in the count the way a man keeps the one piece of furniture in a strange room he would have to climb over to reach the door.

The bell went, and the market made its small separate noises under it, a board dropped, a coin rung on a stone, a pig driven over the boards with a weight behind it that he felt travel up through the stave and into his cheek. Then a voice that had not been in the square a moment ago is in it, and it does not need to be loud: \memory{Marchetti. You're back on in ten.}

The full name is an instrument in that mouth, a small cold pressure applied at exactly the right place; she had said \person{Daniel Marchetti} for twelve years the way a file is drawn across a piece of metal, taking a little off each time, and the metal had never once learned to stop expecting it. The voice belongs to \person{Lena Ostrowski}, who is a small spare woman with soap and machine coffee about her and hands that carry the pale scars of a thousand clamps, and she is standing in a room that is not this square and has not been for years, and still the square goes quiet around the sound of her the way a bay goes quiet around a voice that knows where the bodies are.

A beat. Then the other sentence, the one that had made him and had also excused him, arriving in the same flat instrument-voice, without warmth and without apology, the way a file does not apologise for taking a little off: \memory{The first duty is to decide. The second is to carry it. You seem to have trouble with the second.}

He had heard it first at twenty-eight with his hands in a man's chest in a room that smelled of lemon disinfectant and old blood, and he had heard it a hundred times after, and every time the second sentence had landed on him as a thing he could not quite hold. Not because he did not carry. He carried. He had carried a young woman's chart out of a review with a signature at the bottom of it and let the institution call a note he had not read a systems failure; he had carried a father's crooked casket down the aisle of a church he had not been paying attention to; he had carried nine years of a boat he never bought. He carried the way a man carries. What he had trouble with, and what the woman in the file-voice had had trouble with before him, was the other thing: the thing that comes after the carrying, when a man has to say what he did in the room and remain in the room while he says it. She had asked him one careful question in a corridor and he had told her the note was not there, and she had looked at him for a long moment and let it go. She who demanded an account of every resident who came under her had withheld one from the person she loved like a son, and had never said why, and the sentence she had just spoken in the market carried that omission inside it the way a clamp carries a crack.

\scenebreak

The taste comes first: machine coffee burnt in a pot that has stood on a warmer since midnight, bitter enough to be a solid thing, and under it the lemon-sour edge of disinfectant that no mop has ever taken out of a bay. The ceiling light is a white rectangle in a low ceiling and makes no shadow, which is a thing the winter sky over the \place{Clay Yard} has never managed. The body on the table is a girl of twenty-two with a dressed scalp wound and a blood pressure that has not yet begun to fall, and the nurse holding pressure in the cavity with both hands is watching the clock and not his face, and he is already past the airway and into the question of the spleen. \person{Daniel Marchetti} stands where he has stood a thousand times, with his hands in a cavity and a resident waiting for the sentence that will tell him whether to open the chest. The bell goes on being a bell over the pigs and over the table. The clay yard is under the table and the pigs are under the ceiling light and the barley smoke goes up through a hospital that has no chimney, and none of it is a surprise and all of it is wrong. His hands are steady. His hands are four days old and shaking, small soft things with milky nails lying outside a fold of wool against a barrel stave. The resident waits. The ceiling light holds. The pig goes over the boards.

Then the square was only the square again. The light went out the way a lamp goes out when a door opens onto a yard, and the low winter sky came back over the roofs, and there was barley smoke and pig smell and the bell going on being a bell, and the boy in the basket with his cheek to a stave and the wool in his mouth. He did not tag the going. He had learned by now that the shift came and went without asking him, and that naming it would neither make it stay nor make it leave, and that the sway of it was its own kind of cold.

He reached for the pocket. It was the oldest gesture in him, older than the counting: a man going into a room where a decision waits, and the hand going to the coat to find the phone, and the phone lit in the pocket with a name on it, and the name not looked at. There had been a boat. Nine times he had promised her a day out on \place{Lake Michigan}, nine summers and nine promises, and the boat was never bought and the call was never made, and the weight of the unsent sentence was the thing that had made him portable. In \place{Chicago} the phone had been a small cold rectangle in his coat at \place{Cook County Hospital}, with the name lit on it, and he had never looked at it, not in the car park, not in the corridor, not going back into the van through the fire. Here there was no coat. There was no pocket. The hand went to the place where a pocket should have been and found the fold of wool and the curve of a stave and nothing else, and the fingers closed on nothing and came away. He had not looked at the name then and he could not look at it now, and the not-looking had not changed shape in two lives, only its size. In the bay the name had been a small lit thing in a pocket he could have reached. Here the whole world was the pocket and he was the thing in it, carried from room to room by hands that had no reason to open him and read what had been written on him.

He knew what a room does to a man who has to decide in it and then stay. It is a small knowledge and a hard one, and it is the whole of what the sentence in the file-voice had been trying to hand him for twelve years: decide, and then remain in the room afterwards, with the thing you decided still in front of you and no clean way to leave. He had decided in rooms and he had not stayed in them. He had let an institution call a chart what it wanted to call a chart and gone home to a refrigerator with four beers in it and a photograph he looked at every night without seeing. He had been good at deciding and bad at staying, and the woman with the file in her voice had known it and had said the second sentence to mend him, and had spent the rest of her life not quite mending herself with it.

Here the grammar had gone outward and the room had become a market, and the boy in the basket was neither the man who decided nor the man who carried. He was the thing carried. The first duty was performed on him by a woman who had decided without asking him and would not say what she had decided; the second was performed on him by a district that had read a figure over the top of him and set it on a slate and gone back to the pigs. Ostrowski's sentence had come into the square and found nothing in the basket it could address, because the basket held a thing that could not decide and could not carry and could not lift a hand to the place where a pocket had been. The grammar of the bay and the grammar of the block were one grammar: decide, then carry, leave the deciding unnamed and call the carrying kindness. He set the two side by side the way he set his numbers and his flat notes, and did not make a fourth, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for any of it and no need of one.

Sella was a half-pace off with her hand on the barrel rim and her face turned toward the pigs. She did not look down. When the wool rode back off his cheek she put it right without looking, the little-door burn closing on the fold and letting it go, and said something to the wind about the cold coming off the river, which was an instruction to a thing that could not answer and not a word to the boy at all. He knew the shape of it: a kindness given in a form that cannot be answered is a kindness that cannot be refused, and here it was given again, and there was no version of the world in which he could hand it back. The surgeon in him measured the pressure of her fingers through the wool, exact and unhurried, knowing what the thing would bear. The infant in him wanted the breast. Both were louder than the man who was trying to keep the count, and the count went on anyway, because counting was the only thing in the square that belonged to nobody and could not be entered on any slate.

\scenebreak

The market closed over the break the way water closes over a stone dropped in it, without a gap and without a ripple that anyone but the stone would call a ripple. A crust was broken at a stall and the soft inside of it showed steam in the dry cold. A woman called a price for turnips and another woman called a different price and neither cared that the two prices had nothing to do with the figure on the block. The slate lay where it had been set with the figure on it that would carry him whether he could read it or not, and \lore{The Hearth Drawing} was two days off, and \place{Low Kiln} said \lore{the Five} were three days off, and he kept a hundred and nine and four and four and a gap and the third flat note and a sentence about two duties, and none of it was a thing he could say out loud in a square that had already counted him.

A pig went over the boards with a weight behind it and was hauled up by the ear. A coin was rung twice on a stone. The boy's small hand closed on nothing where a pocket should have been, and the market went on without a gap.
