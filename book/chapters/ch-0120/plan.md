## Scene 1 — The Seventeenth Morning (Day 411 · Ilmen 17, Seventhday · small hours → first light → morning work)

**Place.** Small hours at the low angle of the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house; then the hearth, the pot board, the drying racks, the loaf shelf; a brief carrying-down into the Clay Yard at first light. **Present:** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Tarn (at the kindling), Jorra Vail (at the wheel-shed edge); Garl Vail in objects only. Walk-ons only at the ordinary grammar. **No** Berr, Hessa sighting, man from Slate Row, collector, officer, reading, market, Cross or bell; no slate opened within reach of the basket and no figure given for the boy.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Small hours. The towel comes out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. The boy's hand is already down on the fold on answer 396. He takes the field in the surgeon's old order: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, its corner a hair off the wool, gone past the point where a child would still eat it; the fold found where put, still a finding; the thread of drool stiff in the dry heat and unwiped because nobody has ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint as at the end of a line written out for it; the Afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody here will perform. The infant's three wants quarrel without verdict: the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle — both true, the second louder, neither winning. The quarrel is the work.
2. First light. Count **411** exact; six months and two hundred and thirty-one days, told by nobody. He does not stand the number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure. The clerk's entered figure, the degree and the face stay on three places with a finger between every pair; he does not join them and does not make a rule. The fifth shaping is found where put and not repeated or made a sixth; Hessa's eighth, Ilva's ninth and Berr's box stay where put, unread, with no key.
3. Sella comes to the hearth before the basket. She tells the coals it is the thaw in the voice another woman would use for a child who could not hear her, turns them with the back of a broken pot, gives the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening and figures neither gap. Her finger lies along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stays unread; her lips move on a two-gapped row; one glance goes to the clay jar cold under lead glaze and the little-door burn closes on air. *The house will keep it* goes to the fire and not to the basket.
4. Pot board. She lifts the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crosses the crack; the hairline shows between old-brass staple and wire. Both proverbs go to the pot and the fire and never to a person — *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled*; *If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one*. Repair stays apart from restoration. At the rack end she turns the dressed grain piece by piece under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, no figure and no handful; the pot goes down with its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed.
5. The carrying-down at first light: nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, instruction to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; once around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest at the returning-line place; set under the roof and never in the Debt Loft. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs and is not touched: a morning, a hearth, a shelf, a basket are not openings.
6. Tarn at the kindling keeps six where the house can check and the seventh at the lip; his hand goes over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach; he tells the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count*. Jorra at the wheel-shed parts dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel and speaks to the wheel before any person.
7. Answer **397** comes out and is filed on the sixth place: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. Six honest answers begin; not one is a doctrine.

**Sensory anchors.** Leathery barley crust and its hair-lifted corner; the stiff unwiped thread; dry kiln heat; coals turned with a broken pot's back; the twice-mended pot's hairline between staple and wire; tallow and low smoke; the river wind through the finger's-width door; the wide step around the returning-line place.
