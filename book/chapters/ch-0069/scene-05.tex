\daymark{260}{Smoke 21, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

The dark went off the rafters by the degrees it had gone off them every morning of the house, and in going it carried two hundred and fifty-nine away and brought two hundred and sixty in. Six months and seventy-nine days became six months and eighty. Nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, with the hand outside the wool resting where the ninety-fourth answer had put it down in the small hours, as a man leaves a tool on a bench where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not ask it to remember what it had done, and it did not remember. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer without a name, and the payer had now gone two hundred and sixty days without one. There was no bell under this roof, no market and no \place[the Cross]{Cross}. A Firstday is a day for tending, and the house had gone to its tending as though the square and its arithmetic had never opened their mouths.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow, the dry smell of a kiln that had been fed and left to work. Under it came wet wool, sour with the lodging of a body that had lain long in it. Under that, finer and more persistent, came the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, a smell so old in the room that it had ceased to be an event and become a condition of the air. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall --- four low pulls, and the silence where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell. Nothing in this house struck four, four and a gap and called it a summons. The heard bell stayed where it had sounded, beside the told, and was not folded into the morning's work.

Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last Account-day of the second firing, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The boy kept both where they were, the way a surgeon keeps a chart's turned-down note where it lies, without reading into it what the note does not say. Yesterday's arithmetic had gone up the road in a locked box and changed the district a second time, and could not be changed back; it had not come under this roof, and nothing under this roof had been settled by it. The house had learned nothing from a reading and went on. That nothing learned was itself a kind of keeping, and he kept it without making a lesson of it.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hardened past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if a line had been written out to that length and no farther; it neither went forward nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face severe cold wears, the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder in a body of two hundred and sixty days, and the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

There were three counts in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, going on with its own business and never numbered among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts, and none rose into another; agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them, for a knot is not a nail and a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell's four, four and a gap stayed beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not laid across the fire's four and missing fifth or the boards' four nails and thumb-width knot.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order since the first cold, and she did not have to decide it in the morning any more than a woman has to decide which hand takes the pail. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, saying it to the coals and not to any person, and turned them with the back of a broken pot at \place{the kiln mouth} so the ash broke over them in fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. Then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work.

Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing. It was a glance with no errand in it and no errand came of it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning at the hearth is not an opening and the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour. It had been touched once before the first firing of Smoke, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning or dusk since. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again, made into no lesson, no sign, no mending and no payment, with no payer named to any of it. The fire answered her with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she did not count the silence and did not ask who had left it there.

At the loaf shelf she kept her back to the basket and her finger on top of the front edge, so that the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread. She had reached a finger beneath that board on other mornings and brought it away without a word, and the boy had kept the reaching and the not-reading on two places with a finger between them. She moved her lips along a row of loaves standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; the old space at the row's end and the newer one beside it stood where they stood, and she did not join them to the empty place at his ear in the room that does not touch this one. Then she set the pot rim out on the shelf with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. Two inches was a measurable thing and a child's body was the whole of his reach, and the half-beat went where the half-beats went, on a place of its own with no cause and no owner.

\scenebreak

The dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, well away from the heat of the hearth, among the long rows of \place{the drying racks}. She went out to it before the yard was properly awake and lifted a corner of the wool for damp, the way a woman lifts a cloth from a sleeping child to feel whether the brow is wet, and put the corner back without looking a second time. Under the fold the grain had changed its state since the day the handfuls went in --- threshed, dressed, dried to the arm's knowledge of its weight --- and changed in state it would stay. It carried no figure. There was no slate within reach of it and no hand had entered it since it was covered, and the arm that had gathered it knew what it weighed and not what it cost.

The arithmetic read over it yesterday had not known its cost. It had known a printed number and a levy and the space where a subtraction ought to stand, and it had passed over the heap under wool as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. He kept the two figures on two places and did not average them, the way a surgeon keeps a chart beside a wound and does not write one into the other. A thing covered is not therefore paid for; a thing not entered is not therefore free. The space between the figure read over the grain and the price of the grain had no signature to lay upon it. He kept that as a finding on a tray of its own and not as a grievance with a debtor, for a grievance wants a payer standing under it and none stood. Nobody under the roof found it remarkable that a number should pass over a heap of grain and not know it. That, too, he kept.

\scenebreak

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the frost had gone out of the stone lip of \place{the yard well} under the bucket. It had stood white there in the small hours in fine fronds after the manner of \lore{frost-bloom}, written where the Warm had lately gone out of the stone; then the rope came over it with the cold in it, and the bucket knocked the heads off it, and the grey of the wet stone showed through. The yard called the whole of it weather and went on drawing. The rope took the cold and gave it to the hands that pulled, and the pail came up grey, and the water in it carried the taste of the morning and would not say what it had tasted like the day before. Whatever white had been there he kept on a place of its own with no cause --- not a payer, not the shape a vessel makes when heat goes where it was not meant to go entered into a reason, not the widow's mark, for a well is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, not the first firing of Frost, which stood far off and unbrought. \lore{Ash} had turned the corner at the Vail wall into a clean dark line a year ago and a finer grey hairline had gathered beside it, and the two lines lay unswept beside \place{the trough}, bare and dark with its water called weather. He did not lay the white lip across either line. Three absences in three places are three absences, and a row of them is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none stood.

A neighbour came up with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the keeping and the storing would take now the grain was under wool and the visits coming. Sella did not turn her head toward the drying racks or toward the basket. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” she said, and went on with the bucket. The neighbour went off level with the question still on her, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. A man came down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt and brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, the way a man brings a thing up into the light to see what it is worth. He looked at it. He put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere, and because it arrived nowhere it was not a missing figure and no answer was owed to it; a figure supplied to a fist that had shown nothing would have been a forged one, and the boy had no hand to supply one and no wish to. The river went on with its note of water over water through the finger's-width door, low over lower, and the man walked on up the road with his number where his number had been.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its low note. Under the floor the carried fire held at four with its silence where the fifth should have stood. Between the two the wall did not touch anything but itself, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house and no fire-sitter standing over the coals, so the only interval he had to borrow was the river's own --- one low note to the next --- and that was the river's and not the house's to lend.

He put the sixth question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note and stayed for one whole interval of the river, from the note to the one following it, holding to no count kept under any roof and borrowing no house number to mark the length of it. It came down on the next low note, down on the sound and not past it. There was no gap kept and nothing carried across a wall, only an inch of air and a sound's own spacing, exact as a clamp set and released on a vessel that would not tell him what it held. It was an answer and not an asking. It was ninety-five, filed on the sixth place beside ninety-four with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it. The hand came down and lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and sixty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and eighty days were told by nobody. Ninety-five honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid two hundred and sixty days and its payer unnamed; the widow's mark hung where it hung, untouched at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a morning and a going of the light, because a morning and a well are not openings; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end with a corner lifted for damp and laid back and no figure on it; the pot handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in it and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last reading, stood nine mornings ahead and was not reached for, and the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The third working week had begun as an ordinary tending and had learned nothing, and the count went forward into Smoke 22, a day that had not earned its heading. The file lay open with every tray parted and every gap left gap, and the hand rested against the fold.
