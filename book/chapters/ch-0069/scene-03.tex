\daymark{259}{Smoke 20, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

Smoke 20 came on its own morning, earned by the light that made it and by nothing that had gone before it. The oiled paper at the window took the colour of skimmed milk; the dark went off the rafters by degrees, the way heat goes off a pot set aside, and the count moved out of two hundred and fifty-eight into two hundred and fifty-nine. Six months and seventy-eight days became six months and seventy-nine. Nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and the hand outside the wool lay where the ninety-second answer had put it down, as a man lays a tool on a bench where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not ask it to remember. An inch was an inch, a low note a low note, and the borrowed silence had been an object and no more. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-nine days. No bell had sounded under this roof. The bell belonged to the square and would have to earn its morning there.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hardened past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though a line had been written out to that length and no farther; it neither went forward nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot, and knew the warming must come from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true and the second was louder, and the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

There were three counts in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any; a knot is not a nail, a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell of four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it had sounded on Smoke 10, beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth, changed in state and carrying no figure; the space between the figure that would be read over it today and the price of that grain had no signature to lay upon it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she went on every morning, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. She told it it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot at \place{the kiln mouth}, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted toward no opening, the intake, the hold, the letting-go, setting tools in order and would not have the row called a number. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a morning or a going of the light, because a market and a reading are not openings. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household with no lesson made of it, no mending and no payer named.

At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance and the hand could not close it, and the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered. Then she lifted the basket in the crook of her arm and turned toward \place{the clay road}. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she said to the wool and to the wind, and never to the basket. She carried him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, out of the yard and up the road, giving no instruction to the thing she carried and asking it nothing about the figure that would go over it before noon.

\scenebreak

\place[the Cross]{The Cross} had earned its morning and kept it badly, the way a shallow bowl holds water, spilling it on every side. Sella set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, set it down with the back of her hand the way a pot is set out of a draught, and stood half a pace off. The cold iron of the post came through the wicker to his cheek and stopped there where the order written out for cold runs out. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, the thread at his mouth stood frozen, cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody here would perform. He took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it. The square held two hundred people leaning in over \place{the reading block}, shoulder against shoulder, each carrying a figure or a fist that no slate standing in the square had been made to enter. Eight pigs went over the wet boards while the light was still coming up, nose to tail, driven by a boy with a stick who did not look at the crowd; the boards were slick and the pigs slipped and caught themselves and went on, and each gave out a small flat grunt that arrived nowhere. An assessor of Sill wrote the eight down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail. A man set a figure beside another on a heap of turnips and left them standing like two pots on a board, neither owing the other anything. It was the second Account-day and market of the second firing, and it had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road before the top of the crowd had settled. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the crowd.

\emph{One in twelve, as the notice says.}

He read it twice. The figure went over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. The boy lay under the wool with the figure going over him and did not make the figure the paid figure, because the figure read aloud was not the figure paid and there was no signature anywhere between the two. Berr did not look into the basket. The reading knew the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing; it knew no more of the man carried out on Day 188 than of the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. The surgeon opened the thing the way he would open a wound, layer under layer, and found the cost where the cost always was, under the reading and out of reach. He did not enter it. A figure spoken aloud in a crowd becomes a thing the crowd sets down and goes on keeping, exact and worth nothing, until somebody puts a payer under it, and nobody here had stood up to do it.

A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the levy would take now the kilns had been brought up and the grain was under wool. Berr took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.

\emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me. A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.}

\emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. The low voice went into the top of the crowd where the people who needed it least could lean in and hear it best. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere, and it was not a missing figure; a fist closed on a number and opened on nothing, and a sleeve took it back. Berr went off up the clay road with the cord folded square, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The kindness was entered nowhere except in the box, and the box was locked. The arithmetic of the district changed for the second time in Smoke and could not be changed back, while the printed figure stood unchallenged and the figure read was still not the figure paid. A change made in a square walks home with two hundred people and sits at two hundred tables, and not one will know there are two figures in it, one printed and one paid, with a space between nobody signed.

The bell went over the market longer than a church bell, four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood beside the told bell and he did not lay one across the other, because agreeing them would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. The gap was a gap in a bell and not a hearing, not a fifth stroke of the full note and not a seventh; the six hearings kept their places with an empty place he did not fill to make the row come out even. He kept the bell's four-and-a-gap in the square and did not lay it across the fire's four-and-a-gap under the floor or the boards' four nails and thumb-width knot overhead. Three counts in three places and a fourth sound in a square, and none of them was made to agree. The town went on buying and selling under the sound.

He put the question of the reading to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the bell and was held while the strokes came, four and four, and was held on through the gap where the next stroke did not come, an interval kept against the absence of a stroke and against no sound that stood in its place. It came down on the stroke after it and supplied no stroke to fill the gap. He filed answer ninety-three on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of the full note, not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. It was an inch of air held through an absent stroke and a down-stroke on what came after, the same honest fraction it had been in the small hours, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The heard bell stayed beside the told and was not made a seventh of the full note by a hand that had held its gap.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd at the rail; the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Two straps crossed her chest, the shorter let out for the long road, her thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing on the writing. She was of \place{Slate Row}, a carrier with working hands, and the road from the Cross to \place{Greybank} was the whole of her account. To a woman who asked what she carried she said,

\emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.}

To the woman who wanted more she said,

\emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.}

She answered the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, and Ilva's ninth stayed ninth on another with a finger between them; no tenth was filed in the square that morning. She was not joined to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate below the bridge, to the notched birch kept in a sleeve at the well, or to any other private keeping. She joined the two ends of her road without joining what was written at either, and was not made a reader here to answer what the square had left standing.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. A buyer's number went in before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody took him off. He was not the neat dark hand, not the Drawing collector, not the runner just gone up the road, not the woman at the well and not any of the hands filed on their own trays. He was a clerk set at a landing, and the measure he kept stayed where nobody had called it.

At \place{the pot board} a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the sweet green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth and a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. The colour was kept on a place of its own and no name was spoken over it; no eighth green came to the square and the pale line was not joined to the cup or to the green or to any ear. She was not Fenna in the telling of it. At the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver at its throat, grinned with a gap where a front tooth had been, and turned its head a half-beat late toward a pot set down near the eel shade. The sliver went dull when the shape turned back. It was a colour and a shape, joined to no name, no loaf, no slate and no sale; no ninth shape stood up beside it and no half-turn was made a cause. It was not Ulda in the telling of it, and nobody spoke to it and it spoke to nobody. The square went on buying and selling over both, and the bell's gap stayed unfilled under the noise.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the post's thin shade and carried him home at the half-pace, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and saying nothing of what had been read over him. The way went around the clean dark line where the ridge of \lore{Ash} had turned and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, went wide under \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape or chip or word left in it, around \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} bare and dark beside it and its water called weather, out past the field's empty place on \place{the worked strips} and past the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row were seven and not an account until a payer stood under them, and none stood. The basket went up the clay road with the reading block empty behind it.

Under the Vail roof she set him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face and left the thread frozen. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; she pushed the hair back with the burn-wrist and went to her own bed. The widow's mark hung where it hung. It had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning, afternoon or dusk on Smoke 20, because a market and a reading are not openings, and the one opening before Smoke 1 stayed behind the household with no lesson, sign, mending or payer named to it.

Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood; under the floor the carried fire stopped at four with its fifth unpaid two hundred and fifty-nine days and its payer unnamed; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it had sounded, beside the told bell, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Two hundred and fifty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and seventy-nine days were told by nobody. Ninety-three honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between them had no signature. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last reading, stood nine mornings ahead and was not reached for, and the first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought. The count went forward into Smoke 21, a day that had not earned its heading, and the hand lay back against the fold with the bell's gap still unfilled.
