# Chapter 69 — beat sheet: *What the Covered Grain Keeps*

**Frame.** Days 258–260, Smoke 19–21, Ninthday to Firstday, Y.H. 813. Normal chapter, three lived days, six scenes (two per day), each day opened by its own `\daymark` in the first scene of that day and each lived for well over 800 words. Smoke 20 (Day 259) is the **second Account-day and market of the second firing**; it is earned by its own morning and is not brought into Day 258. Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last reading, stands nine mornings beyond the close and is not reached for. The first firing of Frost (Day 271) is far off and unbrought. No recap, no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no Court, collector or back-room interior, no departure from Dan's eyes. Close third-person past tense; the surgeon's register carries the old life. The chapter's one meaningful change is public: the district's arithmetic changes for the second time in Smoke and cannot be changed back, while the covered grain under wool is read over by a figure that does not know what it cost. Nothing under the Vail roof is settled by it.

**Counts carried into the first line.** 257 exact, six months and seventy-seven days, told by nobody; ninety honest answers on the sixth place; fire's fifth unpaid 257 days, payer unnamed; six hearings beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers; three pot sayings with the premature note from Smoke 14 on its own place; burned slate at four; shut-room tellings at two; told bell beside heard, told figure beside paid; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; nine record-first hands with Ilva's ninth on its own tray, her strip unread; seventh green a colour and seventh shape a shape with no eighth; five askings of the row question with no witness and no row named; grain covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end; widow's mark touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no other hour; second reading still one morning ahead.

**Count at close.** 260 exact, six months and eighty days, told by nobody; ninety-six honest answers on the sixth place; fire's fifth unpaid 260 days; all other counts unchanged. Answers: **91, 92** on Day 258; **93, 94** on Day 259; **95, 96** on Day 260. Each is an honest coordination of the held inch, an object on the sixth place and not a reach, hearing, seventh, payment, held change, key, power or rule.

## Scene 1 — The Nineteenth Morning

- **Day & time:** Day 258, Smoke 19, Ninthday — small hours under the Vail roof, then before first grey and into the morning.
- **Place:** the Vail kiln-house: the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, the hearth, the kiln mouth, the boards, the drying racks, the cool stone at the rack end, the yard well and its stone lip, the loaf shelf.
- **Present:** Corin (carried, awake, silent); Sella Vail; walk-ons at the well and threshold (a neighbour with a pot, a man with a number in his fist). Tarn and Jorra offstage until Scene 2. Old life offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. First scene of the chapter: the only `\dropcap` and the first `\daymark{258}{...}` (Smoke 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813). Open in the small hours with the hand just laid down against the fold on the **ninetieth** answer; set it down as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, never recalled as a sign, growth, gift or power.
2. Carry the count forward from 257 to **258** (six months and seventy-eight days) exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid 258 days, payer unnamed. Do not say the number aloud in the house.
3. Three counts in three places, none rising into another: the kiln's four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should stand under the floor; the river's *water over water* through the finger's-width door; the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stands to any.
4. Body inventory in the old order: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread of drool frozen in the dry heat and unwiped, because nobody has ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint where the wool rides back; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody in this house will perform. He knows the severe face cold wears and knows a cold patient cannot clot; he wants the crust. Both true, the second louder, entered nowhere.
5. Sella goes to the fire before the basket: tells it the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the one grey streak shows pale, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shuts her hand half over nothing.
6. At the loaf shelf: finger kept on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally stays unread; lips along a two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure; pot rim set out with the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed.
7. The dressed grain lies covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end away from the hearth. She lifts a corner for damp and lays it back. It changes nothing and gains no figure, so that the arithmetic read over it tomorrow will not know what it cost. Keep the space between the figure and its price unsigned.
8. Weather: frost on the well lip has gone grey under the bucket and the yard calls it weather. The rope takes the cold. He keeps whatever white was there on a place of its own with no cause — not a payer, not the widow's mark (a well is not an opening), not the first firing of Frost.
9. Cost-question from a neighbour goes to the pot and the weather; a man brings a number up out of a fist toward the well, looks at it and puts it back in his sleeve, so the figure arrives nowhere. No figure is supplied.
10. Smoke 20 stands one morning ahead and is not brought into a day that has not earned it. Hand answer **91**: up an inch toward the low note and down at once on the river's next low note. An object on the sixth place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

- **Emotional turn:** from the held stillness of the ninetieth answer into the stubborn ordinary work of a day that must be lived; the boy registers that the arithmetic coming tomorrow will not know the grain, and that nobody finds this remarkable. No grievance with a payer, because none stands.
- **Sensory anchors:** hot clay under tallow; wet wool sour with lodging; the sour mineral Ash breath of the unswept corner called the thaw; the kiln drawing in fours with a gap; the river low over lower; white frost gone grey on the stone lip; cold iron of the well rope; the pot handle two inches out with the half-beat gone.
- **Threads advanced:** T071/T072 (258 exact, six months and seventy-eight days); T088 (fifth unpaid 258 days); T061/T082 (answer 91, file, three counts apart, six hearings no seventh); T077 (grain covered, unfigured); T058/T079 (mark untouched; no opening at a well); T100 (charcoal unread); T083/T085 (frost called weather, joined to nothing); T015/T041/T047 (figure to sleeve, arrived nowhere); T060/T086 (one crust, unnamed gap, no third).
- **Foreshadowing planted:** Smoke 20's reading stands one morning ahead; the covered grain waits for a figure that will not know its cost. Nothing opened, called or brought early.
- **Exact last image:** the covered grain under wool at the rack end with the corner laid back and no figure on it; the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand; the hand resting against the fold.

## Scene 2 — The Places Gone Around

He speaks to the wheel before any person (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note three turns ahead with air under it and a want in it and withholds the knowing on the near-full tray. Three pot sayings stay three with the premature note from Smoke 14 on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. Burned slate at four from Slate Row; two shut-room tellings at two; the promise of the boy's name ungiven, *he's not Vail* sealed. When Sella's sentence goes low the bent finger goes into the cold damper ahead of it and holds until the end arrives at the pot.

Under the roof she sets him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without a look at his face, leaves the thread frozen, tells the fire the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, goes to her own bed. ## Scene 3 — The Second Reading

- **Day & time:** Day 259, Smoke 20, Tenthday — small hours, then the morning and market at the Cross, home at the going of the light. **Second Account-day and market of the second firing.**

- **Present:** Corin (carried); Sella; Berr Clay; Hessa Rale; the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing (walk-on); the silent woman at the pot board and the foundling at the market's edge (sightings only, unnamed in narration); eight pigs and two hundred at the Cross (walk-ons en masse). Marra, Ulda and Fenna are never named and never on stage as such.

**Beats, in order.**
1. New day, new date, no recap: second `\daymark` (Smoke 20, Tenthday). Count **259** exact, six months and seventy-nine days, nobody told; fire's fifth unpaid 259 days; widow's mark untouched, for a market and a reading are not openings. Smoke 20 comes on its own morning; it was not brought into Day 258.
2. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, sets the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lies, stands half a pace off, gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Eight pigs go over the wet boards; two hundred lean in.
3. Berr comes up short off the clay road: red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat, green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner, the smell of nothing at all under the hot clay, large clean hands. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that will be on it; holds a narrow slate unopened to his forearm; passes a finger over the figure; reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the crowd, the figure going twice over the basket — once for the moot and once for the households that came late.
4. 5. The bell goes four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The heard bell stays beside the told and is not made a seventh of the full note, nor laid across the fire's four-and-gap or the boards' four-nails-and-knot.
6. Hessa takes the slate up the road toward Greybank before noon, edge-on and writing to her sleeve, hand to slate before eye to crowd. To a woman who asks she says *I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which* and *Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk*. No figure comes off it. Her reaching is the eighth hand on its own tray; no tenth is filed.
7. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing, slate inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells over him as water over a stone that lies where a stone lies.
8. Sightings only: at the pot board a silent woman turns a cup a quarter and sets it back, sweet lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth, a pale line across the back of one hand kept on its own place; at the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs catches light on a green sliver, grins with a gap, turns its head a half-beat late toward a set-down pot. A colour and a shape, with no eighth or ninth, no name, no loaf, no slate, no sale, no joining, never Fenna or Ulda in the narration.
9. Hand answer **93**: up an inch, held through the gap where the bell's next stroke did not come, down on the stroke after it, supplying no stroke to fill the gap and making the heard bell no seventh. An object on the sixth place.
10. Sella lifts the basket out of the shade at the going of the light, carries him home at the half-pace and sets him on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without a look at his face, says nothing of what was read over him.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 70

The last line leaves the hand on the fold and the count moving into a day that has not earned its heading: Smoke 22 (Day 261), where Chapter 70 opens with the hand still laid down on the **ninety-sixth** answer and two hundred and sixty exact, six months and eighty days, told by nobody. Smoke's second arithmetic has gone up the clay road in Berr's locked box and changed the district a second time, yet nothing under the Vail roof was settled by it: the grain lies covered and unfigured, the figure read is still not the figure paid, the heard bell still sits beside the told, and the space between a figure and its price still has no signature. That unresolved account is what pulls the third working week toward Smoke 30 (Day 269), the third and last reading, without being brought early. The hand will go on answering honestly — ninety-seven through one hundred and two — and the count will reach two hundred and sixty-three; the sixth hearing will not come, the tenth stopping will not stand before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, no sixth asking will be supplied, no witness will stand, no tenth hand will be filed, and no figure will come off the firing-stick. Tarn's loan stays uncalled, Jorra's promise ungiven, Ilva's strip in her sleeve, the private records apart, and the first firing of Frost (Day 271) far off. The story moves because the week moves: one more day lived, one more honest object on the sixth place, and the last reading still ahead.
