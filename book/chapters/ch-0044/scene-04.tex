The note went where such notes go when nobody catches them, and the afternoon went on around the place it had left. No hand in the yard turned to look for it. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the light came in low and stayed low, and the smaller sounds of a house that has stopped being listened to took over from the larger ones: the drag of a pot's foot, the small tick of cooling clay, the breath of the kiln behind the wall in fours with a gap. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, with his back to the fire and his face to the boards, and took the room in the only way a room can be taken from two inches of it.

He was one hundred and eighty-four days old and six months and four days, and the afternoon had no word for either. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board and in glances at a clay jar standing cold under its glaze, and in the space at the end of a row that nobody called a figure. A man could live a whole life under this roof and never once have a number spoken over him. He had now lived a hundred and eighty-four days of it, and had stopped expecting the number, which was not the same as not wanting it.

At the shelf \person{Sella Vail} stood with her back to the basket. She moved her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. Two spaces in a row are two spaces; a third standing up among them would be a row that wanted to come out even, and a row that comes out even is an account that has found a payer. She kept her finger from beneath the front edge, so the charcoal tally lay where it had lain since the night she held a finger under the board for one counted breath, unread, uncounted, uncopied. The beat she had held there on the hundred and sixty-sixth was where she had left it. An afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as that night, and nothing in the room made them one.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the coals had been banked with the back of a broken pot and the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man could check it and find it where it should be. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} told the wood what he told it every evening. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.} Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line along the skirting board, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

At the next reach the breath from Seed sixteen stood at the top of it as it stood at the top of every reach, unspent, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth as it had come on other evenings and stopped at the word that would call the loan, with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, set the wood where it would not roll, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire, and went on with the broom. The straw and tallow of the kindling kept their own smell under the sour tang of the corner, and the broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it.

The boy, from the low angle, watched the stopping and not the man. A tenth stopping had not stood up before the seventh year was earned; the nine stayed nine objects with no payer named to any of them, and the loan that could hear stayed where it had been laid, uncalled. Three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-four days taking them down one at a time. He did not take this one down or set it up. It was a breath that had not gone in, an interval, an object among objects, and it lay on its own place with no account standing at the bottom of it.

\scenebreak

The instruction went to the wool and the pot and the running water and never to the basket. Sella, within hearing of the kindling, gave the end of her sentence to the pot and asked nothing. From the shed \person{Jorra Vail} did not ask about the ear, and at the kindling Tarn did not ask about the room; the two silences kept their old shape under one roof and were not joined by any word spoken between them. The kiln breathed behind the wall and the river kept \lore{water over water} through the finger's-width door, and neither sound went over to the other.

In \place{the Debt Loft} above \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the stone lay where Tarn had put it back the day before, in the clay pot with no mark on it, cold. He had taken it out, held it, set it on the sill where the finger's-width opening let in light, looked at it a long moment, and put it back. No word had gone with the taking out and no word with the putting back, no figure, no payer, no slate. The boy had heard only a small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, a sound out of a place he could not enter, and had filed it on a place of its own. Now he filed the remembering of it as a remembering and not as a thing done again. A holding remembered is not a holding made a second time; a stone put back is not a stone reconciled, and not trying to reconcile it is not forgiving it. He did not stand the remembered holding up with the three held changes. It lay where it lay, and nobody under the roof spoke of it.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-four mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end --- a record in muscle that gave no account of the cost and would not have known how to give one.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no word. At \place[the yard well]{the well} the \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. Wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody. And around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step, no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The pan's gone round place, the field's empty place and the shelf's two unnamed gaps lay on trays that did not touch; no third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line.

\scenebreak

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock; put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face; left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread; let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. At \place{the hearth} she told \lore{the carried fire} it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing; it had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, and at no shelf, no well, no rack, no road, no basket, no afternoon and no dusk since, because the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms did not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among, with no seventh in a hundred and eighty-four days and none invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. Nine stoppings stood as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and a tenth not stood up before the seventh year was earned. Three held changes stood on three separate places --- a fraction in a wall, a beat beneath a shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, a half-check on stones in a yard that touched neither --- not a pair, not a doctrine, not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The small loft-sill sound lay on a fourth place of its own and was not laid across them. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third. The told bell kept its place beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself; the told figure stood beside the paid; the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line.

The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand: exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order. \lore[The Afterdrop]{The afterdrop} waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

One hundred and eighty-four stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed for a hundred and eighty-four days; the river kept its fifth low thing through the door and was not counted among the hearings; the boards overhead kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. The stone lay back in its pot in the loft, still cold, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
