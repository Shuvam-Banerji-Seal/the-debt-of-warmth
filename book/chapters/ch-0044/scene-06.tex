The afternoon thinned without ending. It lost its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun, slowly and without any word that it was going, so that a man looking up would have said the light was still there and gone back to what his hands were doing. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} hot clay lay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral breath of the corner no broom had been given an instruction about in two years. The work wound down not into stillness but into the smaller sounds a house makes once it has stopped being listened to: the rasp of a pot eased down on boards, the click of a tool put back where it belonged, the pull of a breath a man had not known he was holding. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, his back to the fire and his face to the boards, and took the afternoon in the only way an afternoon can be taken from two inches of it.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket. She moved her lips along a row that stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching it. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge. The charcoal tally lay where it had lain since the night she held a finger under the board for one counted breath, unread, uncounted, uncopied, and the beat she had left there was where she had left it. An afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as that night; a shelf was not an opening, and the one opening of the month lay behind the household with nobody praising it and nobody seeing what it mended.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man could check it and find it where it should be. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn}'s knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. At the top of the next reach the breath from Seed sixteen stood unspent, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not.

He told the wood what he told it at every going of the light. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.} The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it. \emph{Yes, house,} he said to the fire, and went on with his work.

The nine stoppings stayed nine objects with no payer named to any; a tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the loan that could hear stayed where it had been laid, uncalled. The seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. In the clay pot in the loft above the shed \lore{the warm stone} lay where he had put it back two days gone, cold and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving. From below, the boy kept the small loft-sill sound on the place he had made for it and did not stand it up with the three held changes, for a fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause, and a rule is an account that has found a payer.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} worked with \lore{slip} dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading, and kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one afternoon in one account without writing a word over either. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the yard went around and nobody entered. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} He said it flat and low, the way a man says a thing to an animal he means to work with, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it, and kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

A voice came up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind, a man with Ash on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner, and said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. Jorra did not answer him and did not look up, and the man took the silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying. The knowing stayed where Jorra had put it and did not become a hearing because a man in a clean apron had given the danger a name aloud. No pot was lost in the yard and nothing was paid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished in the middle of a sentence to the fire, his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and waited. He did not look at her and did not answer her. He held the iron while the sentence found its end and the end of it arrived at the pot, and only then did he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the cold. The household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named to it. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again; the men who had come before first grey got no name from him and he did not say the room was cold, and the two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third.

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-five mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that gave no account of the cost; it would have said, if a man could ask it, that the weight had been the same on the first morning and on this one, which was true, and that the cost had not been the same, which was also true and not entered.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no word. She went around \place[the yard well]{the well}, where \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. She went wide beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody entered. She went around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step, no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The cleaner dark line where Ash had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it lay unswept in the corner, neither with an instruction, and the household went around both as it went around the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line.

\scenebreak

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and would not call kindness now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure.

She told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of the month, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf and no well and no afternoon and no dusk since, because a shelf and a well and an afternoon and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name. Neither borrowed warmth from the other that anybody could enter.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}; and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and eighty-five days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. The older intervals lay at Stone thirteen, Stone seventeen and Stone twenty-five with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check, and his own held breath from a market morning. Nine stoppings stood on the widened tray --- Reed five, eight, eleven, fourteen, seventeen, twenty, twenty-three, twenty-six and twenty-nine --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, with a tenth not stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; it was joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Three held changes stood on three separate places: the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath the loaf shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, and the half-check on the flagstones in a yard that touched neither. Not a pair, not a doctrine, not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The stone's small loft-sill sound lay on a fourth place of its own and was not laid across them. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third; the told bell kept its place beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure stood beside the paid. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent, and the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its gap, the pan's gone round ring and the empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch; no third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause and no finger of his had drawn the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-five days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-five days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold had come to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; \lore[The Afterdrop]{the afterdrop} waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it.

One hundred and eighty-five stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended: the fire's fifth still missing under the floor with its payer unnamed, the nine stoppings still nine, the Seed sixteen breath still held, the three changes still on their three places, the mark touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, the stone back in its pot in the loft still cold and unreconciled, and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold, and the count went forward into Day 186, Ember seven.
