\daymark{185}{Ember 6, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

The grey came up over \place{the Clay Yard} without an edge to it, the way heat comes into a room through a wall nobody has been told to mind, and laid itself on the racks and the bare ground and the backs of the men already at their work. It was the third working morning of the kiln month's second week and it announced nothing. The kilns had stood at their heat since Tallow and would stand at it till the month turned, and there was no line in the ground where one name gave way to the other; the yard called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither saying had been corrected and neither was wrong. Under the nose of it all lay hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral tang of the corner under \place{the Vail kiln-house} that no broom had been given an instruction about for two years. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, his back to the fire and his face to the boards, and took the morning in the only way a morning can be taken from two inches of it.

He was one hundred and eighty-five days old this morning and six months and five days, and nobody under any roof had said either number, and there was no word in this house that would have carried them. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in a glance at a clay jar standing cold under its glaze, in the space at the end of a row that nobody called a figure. A man could live a whole life under such a roof and never have a number spoken over him. He had now lived a hundred and eighty-five days of it and had stopped expecting the number, which was not the same as not wanting it. It stood in him exact and unspent, the way an instrument stands clean in a drawer nobody opens.

Under the smell, under the floor, \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where a fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-five days. It lay banked under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and gave back its four and its gap as steadily as it had given them through the opened month. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing he had never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and had never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places: a gap in a wall, a note in a doorway, a knot in a board, with no account standing at the bottom of any of them. The river's note was not the kiln's gap and the kiln's gap was not the boards' knot, and the fifth breath missing under the floor was not any of them. Four things that want to be one are four things until a hand lays them together, and his hands this morning could not have laid one thing across another.

\scenebreak

The third working morning was ordinary tending, and the danger of the month kept an order and no name that could not be carried into a chart. Hot clay under tallow. Fine grey slate dust ground small as meal, sitting white in the crease of a knuckle when a fist opened and shut, not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. The sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} riding up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind. A pot set down wrong on the edge of a board. A chart wants a name at the top and a payer at the bottom, and none of these had either, and so the order went on being kept without being written, the way a man carries a full bowl across a floor he knows by heart and arrives with nothing slopped. No payer was named to any of them.

The woman at \place[the drying racks]{the racks} who set the pot down had her hands full and her mind on another, and she did not hear the high thin note it gave before its time. It went up bright into the warm air and stopped, and hung a moment where such notes hang when nobody catches them, and went where the others had gone. The boy turned his head toward it and got no further. He had no knuckle to set against the vessel, no word to tell anybody, no standing to make a lesson of a sound the woman who made it had not heard. He filed it where a danger of the month goes, on the tray beside the dust and the wrong wind, and did not promote it into a near-full answer or let it stand up as a fourth likeness. A note out of season is a fault in the handling, and a fault in the handling is not a cause waiting for a payer. No pot was lost, no lesson was made, no room was changed, and no pot singing itself out early had been paid.

At the edge of the yard \person{Sella Vail} went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither, for neither had an instruction. She went wide beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody entered. She went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no word. She went around \place[the yard well]{the well}, where \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step, no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the shelf's two unnamed gaps lay on trays that did not touch; no third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line.

She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody, the way a woman names a tool before she hands it over, and \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took it only then. He took it as a man takes up a thing already named, with no question and no word of thanks, and went to \place{the flagstones}.

\scenebreak

At the kindling the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man could check it and find it where it should be. Tarn told the wood what he told it every morning. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count.} Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. At the top of the next reach the breath from Seed sixteen stood unspent, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not.

A neighbour wanted the thaw early and another wanted it late, and he said \emph{Yes, house} to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot. He kept the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped and giving it back to nobody. It went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and no collector went down it. The nine stoppings stayed nine objects with no payer named to any; a tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the loan that could hear stayed where it had been laid, uncalled. The seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given.

The boy, from the low angle, watched the stroke and not the man. It was an arithmetic he had learned to read without being taught it: six where the house could check and the seventh where it could not, a breath not spent at the top of a reach, an ear worth more than the debt and the sentence that would call the loan kept short of the word. He did not lay these one across another. A tenth stopping stands up out of nine likenesses the moment a man wants an answer from them, and a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. Three held changes stayed on three separate places --- a fraction in a wall, a beat beneath a shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, a half-check on stones in a yard that touched neither --- and the small loft-sill sound lay on a fourth place of its own and was not laid across them. He had spent a hundred and eighty-five days taking likenesses down one at a time, and there was nobody to help him and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} worked with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading, and kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around and nobody entered. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} He said it flat and low, the way a man says a thing to an animal he means to work with, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel --- knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it --- and kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

A voice came up from High Kiln on the wrong wind, a man with Ash on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of lead glaze at one mouth-corner. He listened to the pot under Jorra's hands and said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. Jorra did not answer him and did not look up, and the man took the silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying. The knowing stayed where Jorra had put it and did not become a hearing because a man in a clean apron had given the danger a name aloud. No pot was lost in the yard and nothing was paid.

At \place{the hearth}, when Sella's voice went low and unfinished in the middle of a sentence to the fire, Jorra's bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and waited. He did not look at her and did not answer her. He held the iron while the sentence found its end and the end of it arrived at the pot, and only then did he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the cold. The household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named to it. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again: the fraction that had stood at the iron from the hundred and sixty-first day, held again on the hundred and eighty-first and remembered since, and it was not paired with the beat beneath the loaf shelf on the hundred and sixty-sixth or the half-check on the flagstones of the hundred and seventy-first. Three changes stood on three separate places, and he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule.

The boy read the iron once more from the low angle at which he knew it. It was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He filed it as an object joined to no kiln, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. A clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine, set where it is needed and not asked to mean more; he had learned that in a room where irons were laid on a towel and taken off again when the vessel had borne what it could bear. Here there was no towel and no taking off, only the iron standing where it stood with a man's finger left in it.

\scenebreak

Sella's eye went once, as it went every morning, to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching it. Standing with her back to the basket at the loaf shelf she moved her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge, so the charcoal tally lay where it had lain since the night she held a finger under the board for one counted breath, unread, uncounted, uncopied, and the beat she had held there on the hundred and sixty-sixth was where she had left it. An afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as that night, and a morning and a shelf were not openings.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and it was not touched. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred, and she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals. The one opening was behind the household, performed before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Nothing in the morning made an opening of itself, and nobody in the room said any of this. It was in the grammar of the house the way grain is in a board, and the house went on keeping it without an account the way a pot goes on holding water without anybody thanking it for the holding.

The instruction went to the wool and the fire and the pot and the running water and never to the basket. Sella gave the end of her sentence to the pot and asked nothing; Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, so the two silences kept their old shape under one roof and were not joined by any word spoken between them. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made into a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-five days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it. Cold had come to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; \lore[The Afterdrop]{the afterdrop} waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

One hundred and eighty-five stood exact and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed for a hundred and eighty-five days, the nine stoppings stayed nine and the Seed sixteen breath stayed unspent, and the three held changes stood on their three places with the small loft-sill sound on a fourth of its own. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
