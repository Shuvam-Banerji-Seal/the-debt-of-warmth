## Chronicle

The kiln month's second working week came on under the Vail roof, the boy one hundred and eighty-three days old and told by nobody. The kiln breathed in fours with a gap; the carried fire under the floor missed its fifth, its payer unnamed. Sella Vail told wool, fire, pot, broom and water, never the basket, said *the house* where another woman would say *I*, and set the pot handle two inches beyond his hand. The widow's mark hung where it had hung, for a morning and a shelf are not openings.

Tarn took the broom only after it was told its business and told the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count*. He said *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late, wanting the late because it puts off the paying; no collector came. In the afternoon he climbed to the Debt Loft and took from an unmarked pot the warm stone given him in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold. He held it, set it on the sill in the light, looked at it a long while and put it back. No word, no figure, no payer; the debt did not move and the stone was not forgiven. Below, the boy heard a small dry scrape of a pot's foot on stone from a room he could not enter, and filed it alone.

Three or four men went low into the back room before first grey, unnamed. Jorra Vail came out with slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms, told a pot it would do, knew its note three turns ahead and kept the knowing from a man on a wrong wind. When Sella's sentence hung unfinished his bent finger stood in the cold iron until the words reached the pot. A neighbour asking a cost was sent to the pot and the weather and carried her question away level. A rack-worker's pot gave its note too early; the boy filed it as a danger. The yard went around its unswept lines, the birth-slates, the pan's gone ring and the empty place from Seed 15.

On the third morning the danger kept an order and no name. Tarn's ear-sentence stopped short of the word that would call the loan; he drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, and the nine stoppings stayed nine. Sella's finger stayed from beneath the loaf shelf and the charcoal waited unread. At dusk she carried him home up the clay road, set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, and did not touch the mark. In the small hours he laid out his file: six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings as objects, three held changes and the loft-sill sound on a fourth place. He wanted the crust, the pot handle and a number nobody would speak, and got none. One hundred and eighty-five stood exact and unspent in the dark of the wool.
