\daymark{184}{Ember 5, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}

The grey came up \place{the Clay Yard} late and without a sound, the way heat comes into a room through a wall nobody has been told to mind. It found the men before it found the boards. Three or four of them went up with their collars high and their heads down, and went low through the finger's-width door of \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}; the door came back to its finger's width and kept it as a door keeps a habit, and no hand in the house gave them a name. They were boots going past a place he could not have followed and was not meant to follow, a sound with a direction and nothing in it that a basket could have carried into a room.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Corin]{the boy} lay where he had been set, his back to the fire and his face to the low angle at which the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof. Hot clay lay under tallow in the room and wet wool under that and, under both, the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. He was one hundred and eighty-four days old this morning and six months and four days, and nobody under any roof had said either number, and there was no word in this house that would have carried them. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in glances at a clay jar standing cold under its glaze, in the space at the end of a row that nobody called a figure. A man could live a whole life in such a house and never once have a number spoken over him. He had.

Under the smell, under the floor, \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where a fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-four days. It lay banked under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and gave back its four and its gap as steadily as it had given them through the opened month. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing he had never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and had never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and he had spent every day of his life here keeping them apart: a gap in a wall, a note in a doorway, a knot in a board, with no account standing at the bottom of any. The river's note was not the kiln's gap and the kiln's gap was not the boards' knot, and the fifth breath missing under the floor was not any of them. Four things that want to be one are four things until a hand lays them together, and his hands, this morning, could not have laid one thing across another.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket past the shed at the half-pace. She turned no head toward the door that had taken the men, gave the house no name for them, and did not say the room was cold. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the last war with \lore{the damper} closed and the men inside dead of the breath stood side by side in his keeping — one come downriver from \place{Greybank} and told at \place[the yard well]{the well} as a curiosity eighteen leagues east, one told a second time at a bucket-round with a date put to it — and no third telling came this morning to stand up beside them. Two were two. A third would want a cause, and a cause would want a payer under it, and no payer stood to either telling. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and the pot and never to the basket, and the basket went on being carried at the height of a vessel that must not slop.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door from the shed with the day's fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened and closed, not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, where the yard went around and nobody entered, and the boy, from his two inches of world, could not have told one slate from another or put a hand on the space where whole slate should have stood. He did not try. The dust was not the slip. He had learned to part the one from the other the way he had once parted a clot from the vessel that held it, one the material of a shut room and one the material of the work, and no word spoken in this house would have made the two one thing or explained why a man came out of a door with one and went to the wheel with the other.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} He said it the way a man says a thing to an animal he means to work with, flat and low and without welcome, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no unkindness in it and no praise either, the voice of a man stating what the thing will be. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel — knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it, an answer held and not yet spent — but he kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, and gave no sign of it to the man who came up on the wrong wind.

The man was of \place{High Kiln}, come up on a wrong wind with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner. He stood and listened to the pot under Jorra's hands and said, \emph{That one's singing before its time. A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early.} He took Jorra's silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying, and no pot was lost in the yard and no lesson was made. A pot that gives its note before its time is a fault in the handling and not a fourth likeness waiting to be promoted, and the boy filed the saying where a danger of the month goes, with no figure under it and no name on it. The knowing stayed where Jorra had put it, on the near-full tray, and did not become a hearing because a man in a clean apron had put a name to it aloud.

\scenebreak

At \place{the hearth}, when Sella's voice went low and unfinished in the middle of a sentence to the fire, Jorra's bent finger went to the cold iron of the damper ahead of his hand and waited. He did not look at her and did not answer her. He held the iron while the sentence found its end and the end of it arrived at the pot, and only then did he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the cold. The household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named to it. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again: the same fraction that had stood at the iron from the hundred and sixty-first day, held again on the hundred and eighty-first and remembered since, and it was not paired with the beat held beneath the loaf shelf on the hundred and sixty-sixth or the half-check on the flagstones of the hundred and seventy-first. Three changes stood on three separate places, and he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer.

The boy read the iron once more from the low angle at which he knew it. It was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He filed it as an object joined to no kiln, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. A clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine, set where it is needed and not asked to mean more; he had learned that in a room where irons were laid on a towel and taken off again when the vessel had borne what it could bear. Here there was no towel and no taking off, only the iron standing where it stood with a man's finger left in it.

Outside, \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the house had given it its instruction, and his stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the way it had gone on every morning since the hundred and seventy-first. The stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped and gave it back to nobody. The movement of that day was not repeated, and the three held changes stayed on their three places — a fraction in a wall, a beat beneath a shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, a half-check on stones in a yard that touched neither — not a pair, not a doctrine, not a rule, with no payer standing to any of them. The broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it, and the road kept its ruts while the broom kept its count and the two did not touch.

\scenebreak

At the racks and the well the yard went around what it always went around. The clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner for two years lay unswept, and the new finer grey beside it, called \lore{the thaw}, lay unswept with it, neither with an instruction. The yard went wide beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay. It went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust, no chip of a rim and no word. At the well the \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. And in the returning line the yard went around the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step, no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The boy watched the going-around from the edge of the floor and gave none of the turns a name. The pan's gone round place, the field's empty place and the shelf's two unnamed gaps lay on trays that did not touch; no third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line.

A neighbour come up from the tubs with a pot still wet in her hands asked what the firing cost now the kiln month's second working week was on. She put the question to nobody in particular and would have taken any answer that had a figure in it. Sella's eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching the jar. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there}, she said. \emph{The house has said it.} She sent the question to the pot and to the weather, and the woman carried it away level, with nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered. The question went off up the yard holding its shape, and the boy, who would have known what a number counted if anybody had been fool enough to give one, got no number.

A rack-worker at \place{the drying racks} set a pot down wrong on the edge of a board, and the pot gave its high thin note before its time, a small bright sound that went up and stopped. The woman who set it down did not hear it. She had her hands full and her mind on another pot and went on with her work, and the note hung in the warm air a moment and went where such notes go when nobody catches them. The boy turned his head toward it and got no further. He had no knuckle to set against the vessel, no word to tell anybody, and no standing to make a lesson of a thing the woman who set it down had not heard. He filed it where a danger of the month goes and did not promote it into a near-full answer or let it stand up as a fourth likeness. A note out of season is a fault in the handling, and a fault in the handling is not a cause waiting for a payer. No pot was lost, no lesson was made, no room was changed and no edge was paid.

The morning went on around him without asking what he made of it. The instruction went to the wool and the fire and the pot and the running water and never to the basket; Sella gave the end of her sentence to the pot and asked nothing, and neither Jorra nor Tarn asked the other about the room or the ear, so the two silences stayed unjoined under one roof. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made into a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-four days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold, with one hundred and eighty-four exact and unspent in the dark of the wool and the pot's high thin note hanging where the woman who had set it down had not heard it.
