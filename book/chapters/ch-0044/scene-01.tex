\daymark{183}{Ember 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay under tallow} came into the room before the grey did, and wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. It was the smell of the firing month and the smell of the kiln month at once, for the kilns had stood at their heat through Tallow and would stand at it through Ember without any hand in the district drawing a line where one name turned into the other. The yard called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither saying was wrong and neither had been corrected, and the heat went on behind the wall under both names the way a man's breath goes on while he is deciding what to call the weather. The smell settled into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay and stayed there, as familiar as a room come back to after somebody has moved the chairs.

He was one hundred and eighty-three days old this morning and six months and three days, and nobody under any roof had said either number, and there was no word in this house that would have carried them. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in glances at a clay jar, in the space at the end of a row that nobody called a figure. A man could live a whole life in such a house and never once have a number spoken over him. He had. He had spent a hundred and eighty-three mornings being carried and set down and folded and not counted, and the number stood in him exact and unspent, the way an instrument stands clean in a drawer that nobody opens. The grey came slowly through the finger's-width opening and reached the boards before it reached \place{the bed}, and the room kept its shapes a while longer in the half-light — the pot on the board, the fold, the small rise of the wool where a child lay, the nail-heads in the boards overhead.

Under the smell, under the floor, the kiln drew breath in fours with a gap. Four low pulls, then the silence where a fifth should have been, then four again. It was not a bell. A bell spent four, four and a gap and left a hole in the hearing that a man might cross himself over or might not, and the told bell kept its place beside the heard bell and the two had never become one; the kiln spent four and left the gap in its breathing and went on, and had never asked anybody to count it. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-three days. It lay banked under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and gave back its four and its gap as steadily as it had given them on the first morning of the month, when the woman had put her hand to the mark before the lid came up and tipped the coal under the pot and nobody had praised what was mended.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing he had never counted among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} and had never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb.

Three counts in three places. He had spent every one of these days keeping them apart: a gap in a wall, a note in a doorway, a knot in a board, and no account standing at the bottom of them. A man who lets three counts lie together long enough will wake to find they have stood up and called themselves a truth, and a truth with a payer at the bottom of it is an account, and no payer stood to any of these. He had learned that in a better-lit room, among people who wrote things down, and had brought the habit into a house that kept its days in bread and silences; it made him no friends and bought him nothing, and he kept it anyway, the way a man keeps an instrument clean when there is nobody to see it. The river's note was not the kiln's gap and the kiln's gap was not the boards' knot, and the fifth breath missing under the floor was not any of them. Four things that want to be one are four things until a hand lays them together, and his hands, this morning, could not have laid one thing across another.

The crust lay two inches from his ear. It had gone stiff and leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month, brown at the edges, the one brown-edged barley crust put into the fold on Stone fifteen, and the empty place beside it had no name and there was no third. He could smell it and could not reach it. Two inches. A man who had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark, who had held pressure in a cavity with both hands while the room emptied around him, could not close two inches on a piece of bread. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was still frozen, and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread; a basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot was told its business and left to it. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about that sound either. He turned a little and the sound came, and he turned back, and the wool took the sound and kept it.

The adult in him and the body in him quarrelled, and the body was louder. He knew the difference between a vessel cooking its own contents and a vessel going cold, knew the afterdrop before anybody in the room could have named it, knew that a child who cools while everybody is kind is a child in danger. None of it helped. He could not roll. He could not lift a hand to his own chin. He could not reach the crust, or the pot handle that would stand two inches beyond his hand when the woman came, or the frozen thread at his own mouth. The surgeon's register read the room as a field laid out and inaccessible, every instrument just beyond the reach of a hand that could not have lifted the least of them, and the infant in the field was cold and wanted the breast. Cold had come to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Both things were true. The second was louder.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came in with the light still grey at the door and gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to anybody. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” She said it to the wool and to the wind and to the fold, and not to the basket. The fire was told what it was at \place{the hearth}, the pot what it wanted, the broom where to go and the running water where to run; the basket was told nothing at all. Where another woman would have said \emph{I} she said \emph{the house}, and the house, she made plain to the fire, would keep it. Her half-pace carried her past the bed without a look into it. When her hands had nothing to do they counted — the spoons on the board, the nails in a board overhead, the breaths of a sleeping child — and when they had work they stopped counting and did the work, and neither count was ever spoken.

Her eye went once, as it went every morning, to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall. Her hand started toward it, shut half over nothing, and came away without touching. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not say where it had come from and had not for years. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was older than any instruction in the house and had never been entered anywhere.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It was not touched. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening and a basket and a bed and a fold were not openings; the one opening was behind the household, performed before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred. Nobody in the room said any of this. It was in the grammar of the house the way the grain is in a board, and the house went on keeping it without an account, the way a pot goes on holding water without anybody thanking it for the holding.

She took up the basket base first and level, the way a woman lifts a filled pot from a board, with no slop and no word, and carried him out under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and into \place{the Clay Yard} at \place{Low Kiln}. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind as she went and never to the basket. The arm that carried him had carried him a hundred and eighty-three mornings and kept the height of the handle and the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that gave no account of the cost. Through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} a pot turned on the wheel and a voice spoke to it without welcome and without unkindness, and the boy heard the wheel and did not see the hands. On \place{the flagstones} a broom went and went on, the going of a man who had taken it only after it had been told, and the boy saw the broom go and did not see the man's face. Neither voice reached the basket and no instruction went toward either man.

At the edge of the yard she went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years, and the new finer grey beside it that was called \lore{the thaw}, and neither was swept and neither had an instruction. She went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, looking for no scrape and finding no word. She went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody. She went around \place[the yard well]{the well}, where \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, and a wide step, and no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. Three absences and a gone ring lay on places of their own, and she did not lay a finger across any of them.

At \place{the kiln mouth} the fire kept its gap. Four low pulls, then the silence where a fifth should have been, then four again. The carried coal lay banked under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, keeping its warmth the way a closed hand keeps what it has decided not to look at. The danger of the month kept an order and no name that could not be carried into a chart: hot clay under tallow, fine grey slate dust ground small as meal sitting white in the crease of a knuckle when a fist opened, the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} riding up from High Kiln on a wrong wind. A chart wants a name at the top and a payer at the bottom, and none of these had either. No payer was named to any of them. No pot was lost in the yard and no lesson was made of anything, and the danger went on being carried carefully, without remarks, the way a man carries a full bowl across a floor he knows by heart. The boy turned his head toward the sound of a pot set down too hard and got no further; he had no knuckle to set against it and no word in which to tell anybody, and filed it where a danger goes and not among the near-full answers, because a note out of season is a fault in the handling and not a likeness waiting to be promoted.

She set the basket down with its back to the kiln mouth, where he could feel the heat on his face and not see the fire, and went about the morning. He lay at the low angle at which the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof and wanted what he could not ask for. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that was not a key and a list that was not a hand, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it.

One hundred and eighty-three stood exact and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire missed its fifth under the floor and its payer had no name. Through the finger's-width door the river went over its own water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made into a seventh, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb; three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold.
