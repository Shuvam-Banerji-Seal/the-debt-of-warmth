# Chapter 44 — "The Stone in the Loft" (beat sheet)

**Days covered.** Day 183, Ember 4, Fourthday; Day 184, Ember 5, Fifthday; Day 185, Ember 6, Sixthday; Y.H. 813. A normal three-day chapter, the kiln month's second working week. Every day opens with its own `\daymark` and is lived in full; nothing is skipped, poured into another or summarised. Weekday labels are checked against `bible/calendar.md` (*t* = 336 + *N* − 1): Day 183 → t = 518 → Ember 4, Fourthday; Day 184 → Ember 5, Fifthday; Day 185 → Ember 6, Sixthday. There is **no Account-day and no market** in these three days: the first of Ember falls on Day 189 (Ember 10) and belongs to Chapter 46, so there is no Cross, no bell rung, no Berr, no Hessa, no green-mouthed woman, no foundling and no soft-cuffed clerk on stage. The district keeps calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month; neither usage is corrected in narration. The boy is one hundred and eighty-three days old and six months and three days on the first morning, one hundred and eighty-four and six months and four days on the second, one hundred and eighty-five and six months and five days on the third, and nobody under any roof says so.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** On Day 183, in the Debt Loft above the wheel-shed, Tarn takes the warm stone Sella gave him in the winter Garl died out of the clay pot where it has lain two years gone and cold, not looked at in a year. He holds it, sets it on the sill where the finger's-width opening lets in light, looks at it a long moment, and puts it back in the pot. No word, no figure, no payer, no slate. He does not reconcile the stone with the debt and does not forgive it: the change is held and unspoken, a movement of **T006** (the stone) and **T036/T076** (the debt), advanced and not paid. The boy, below, hears only a small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone from the direction of the shed-loft — a sound from a place he cannot enter — and files it on a place of its own, refusing to join it to the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 held beat beneath the shelf or the Day 171 flagstone half-check, and refusing to let a fourth likeness stand up and call itself a cause, for a rule is an account that has found a payer. Nothing else is settled in the chapter: no name, no figure, no payer, no seventh hearing, no tenth stopping, no third gap, no calling of the loan, no opening of any private record and no edge paid. The dust, the wrong wind and the pot singing early go on gathering toward the small non-fatal edge owed in Chapter 45; they are not paid here.

**Continuation from Chapter 43 (not recapped).** Day 182, Ember 3, is behind and is not revisited. The count stood at one hundred and eighty-two, exact and unspent; the first firing of Ember had been performed on Day 180 with the mark touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the fire still answered four low pulls with its fifth missing and its payer unnamed; the nine stoppings stood nine with no tenth, the Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled; the three held changes stood on three separate places; the charcoal waited unread under the shelf. Scenes 1–2 in this plan carry that state forward in silence and add the stone.

## Scene 1 — "The Second Working Week"

## Scene 2 — "The Stone in the Pot"
**Day and time.** Day 183, Fourthday: afternoon into the small hours. No new dated heading (continues Day 183).

**Beat sheet, in order.**
1. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction and tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, with the small knife back at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh held at the lip where it cannot, Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it, his chest tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there is and giving it back (T036/T076).

No tenth stopping stands up before the seventh year is earned; the nine stoppings stay nine objects with no payer named to any; the Seed 16 breath stays unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not; the loan stays uncalled; the seventh year stands nearer its end than it has ever stood and has not given (T036/T076, T061/T082).
6. Sella stands at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket, moves her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, gives nobody a figure, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze without touching it and keeps her finger from beneath the front edge, so the charcoal stays unread and the Day 166 held beat stays where it was left (T100).
7. At the going of the light she carries him home up the clay road, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either; going wide around the pan's gone place, the bare dark trough, the rack space and the field's empty place with a look and a wide step and no number (T009/T013/T092, T098). Under the roof she sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen, lets her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed, tells the carried fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening — the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go — pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak shows pale off the coals, sets the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and does not look toward the bed. ## Scene 6 — "The Charcoal Waits"
**Day and time.** Day 185, Sixthday: afternoon into the small hours. Chapter close. No new dated heading (continues Day 185).

**Place.** The loaf shelf, the kindling, the hearth and the bed under the Vail roof; the yard at the going of the light; the clay road.

**Who is present.** `sella-vail`, `tarn`, `jorra-vail`, `dan-marchetti` (Corin). No walk-on speaks.

**Beat sheet, in order.**
1. The afternoon thins without ending, losing its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun; hot clay lies under tallow and wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral breath of the Ash corner nobody has swept in two years. Work winds down not into stillness but into the smaller sounds a house makes once it has stopped being listened to.
2. Sella stands with her back to the basket at the loaf shelf and moves her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6; she gives nobody a figure, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and shuts her hand half over nothing, and her finger does not go beneath the front edge, so the charcoal tally lies under the board where it has lain since the night she held a finger beneath it for one counted breath, still unread, and an afternoon and a woman's back turned are not the same as that night (T100).
3. Tarn's knife lies on the sill of the kindling at the very edge where a man could check it and find it where it should be, six years in the worn thumb-mark where the house can put a finger and verify them, the seventh at the lip where no thumb of the house has gone, Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there is and giving it back. The breath from Seed 16 stands unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. The ear-sentence comes to his teeth and stops short of the word that would call the loan — no figure, no name, no tenth standing up before the seventh year is earned, the nine stay nine, the loan uncalled, the seventh year nearer its end than it has ever stood and not given. He says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with his work (T036/T076, T061/T082). The stone stays where he put it back in the clay pot in the loft two days gone, still cold, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving; the boy keeps the remembered holding on its own place and does not stand it up with the three held changes (T006 held).
4. In the Vail wheel-shed Jorra works at the wheel with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading, keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note three turns before it leaves the wheel and keeps the knowing back from a man come up on a wrong wind, so no pot is lost; when Sella's voice goes low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger goes to the cold iron of the damper and waits while her sentence reaches the pot, then moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves the finger standing in the iron, the fraction held and unspoken and not a change made twice over. He gives the men who came before first grey no name and does not say the room is cold; the two shut-room tellings stay two with no third (T062, T035/T031, T065/T095).
5. At the going of the light she carries him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they have passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction goes to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket; the arm that has carried him a hundred and eighty-five mornings keeps the height of the handle and the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that gives no account of the cost. At the edge of the yard she goes around the round place where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and is standing there no longer, around the well where the trough stands bare and dark and its water is called weather, wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, with a look that asks for nothing and finds nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket (T009/T013/T092, T098).
6. Under the roof she sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread, lets her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, tells the carried fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go — pushes coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak shows pale off the coals, sets the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and does not look toward the bed. The widow's mark hangs on its cord at her throat where it has hung since the first firing; it was touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of the month, and at no shelf and no well and no afternoon and no dusk since, because a shelf and a well and an afternoon and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the two rooms do not touch (T058/T079).
7. In the small hours he turns out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else: six hearings of the full note beside and never among — five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross and a man giving at the Drawing — and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources, no seventh in a hundred and eighty-five days and none invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stand on their own tray, advanced and not paid; the older intervals lie at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25; nine stoppings — Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 — stand as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, with a tenth not stood up before the seventh year was earned; the damper lies beside them as one piece of iron with two bites and no Grathic word for both mercies, joined to no kiln. Three held changes stand on three separate places — the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath the loaf shelf in a room that does not touch the wall, the half-check on the flagstones in a yard that touches neither — not a pair, not a doctrine, not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stands to any of the three, with the stone's small loft-sill sound on a fourth place of its own and not laid across them. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stand side by side with no third; the told bell keeps its place beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure beside the paid; the sixth green is a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent, and the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its gap, the pan's gone round ring and the empty place in the returning line lie on trays that do not touch — no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawing the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing and he has spent a hundred and eighty-five days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see (T061/T082, T060/T086, T065/T095).
8. Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-five days; overhead the boards keep four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb — three counts in three places, and none rises up and becomes the others. Ash lies thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lies beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold comes cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and stops as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform.
9. He wants the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and gets none of them, and has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register reads the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand — exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces — and he is the only adult in the room and the least use in it.
10. Close on one hundred and eighty-five standing exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof; nothing joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the fire's fifth still missing under the floor with its payer unnamed, the ninth stopping still nine, the Seed 16 breath still held, the three changes still on their three places, the mark touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, the stone back in its pot in the loft still cold and unreconciled, and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf for a hand that is not hers and a day that has not come. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold, and the count goes forward into Day 186, Ember 7.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 45

The chapter closes in the small hours of Day 185 with the count at one hundred and eighty-five exact and unspent, the boy's hand coming down against the fold, and nothing settled: the fire's fifth still missing with no payer named, the nine stoppings still nine with no tenth before the seventh year is earned, the three held changes still on three separate places, the widow's mark touched once before the kiln, the stone back in its pot in the loft still cold and unreconciled, and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf. That forward-held inventory is the hinge into Chapter 45, which opens on Day 186, Ember 7, Seventhday, in the middle of the kiln month. The dust ground small as meal, the sweetish lead glaze on the wrong wind, a pot set down wrong giving its note before its time, and the knowing Jorra withholds from the men on the wrong wind have all been gathering here without an edge being paid; Chapter 45 is where that gathered danger can at last be paid in a small, non-fatal way — a burned hand, a pot that sings itself out early, a glaze-room hand carried out with the sweet-sickness on his breath, a room nobody enters twice — with no major character dying, no lesson made, no payer named to the keeping, and the cost entered nowhere. The stone, the count and the charcoal go on gathering with it. Forward, not closed.
