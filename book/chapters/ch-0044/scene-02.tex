The afternoon came on without the house giving it a name, the way heat came into the kiln-house through a wall nobody had been told to mind. At \place{the kiln mouth} the fire kept its breath in fours with a gap, four low pulls and the silence where a fifth should have been, and the light slid off \place{the flagstones} in a long shallow bar that got as far as \place{the kindling} and stopped. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where he had been set, with his back to the fire and his face to the low angle at which the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, and he watched what he could not reach. His window was a finger's width of door and the two inches of air between his hand and a pot's handle. Everything beyond that was a sound with a direction.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. She was already halfway across the yard, and the words went back over her shoulder to the thing standing against the wall. “Go around,” she said. “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” She did not say what \emph{it} was and did not turn her head, and the instruction was complete without a name in it, the way a pot is complete without anybody explaining the handle.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after that. He did not put his hand to a tool that had not been told its business; he had learned that in six years of being a debt with hands, and the lesson had cost him nothing he could have entered on a slate. He took the broom up as a man takes up an instrument already laid out for him, handles out and edges in, nothing that can roll, and went to work on the stones with the stroke that went around and entered nothing.

At \place{the hearth}, where the coals lay banked under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, he set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where a man of the house could put a thumb and find it where it should be. Six years stood in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat, and the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept there as an object and not entered anywhere. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark. At the top of every reach the breath from Seed sixteen stood unspent, with a small interval showing where it should have gone in and had not.

He told the wood what he told it at the going of the light. “Six counted. The next I don't count.”

He said it to the kindling, not to the house and not to the basket, and the boy, who had heard the same sentence on more evenings than he could set a number to, kept it where such sentences went: an account kept by a man who could not enter it, exact and worth nothing at two paces.

Two people at \place{the trough} had been arguing since the middle of the day about \lore{the thaw}, one wanting it early and one wanting it late, and neither put the question to a clay-bound hand, because a clay-bound hand is not asked what it wants. Tarn said “Yes, house” to the early and “Yes, house” to the late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on. He went on wanting the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning his head to look along it. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and no collector went down it. The road kept its ruts, he kept his count, and the two did not touch.

\scenebreak

Above \place{the Vail wheel-shed} lay \place{the Debt Loft}, a narrow room under the roof where the house's clay-bound hands slept, and where Tarn had slept six of his seven years. He went up the ladder without haste in the thick of the afternoon, while the yard below went on with its own noise. The boards up there held the day's warmth and the dry small smell of old straw. Through the finger's-width opening at the end of the loft a bar of light came in and lay across the sill, and the wheel below kept turning under him, and he did not go to the opening or look down.

His few things were in a clay pot with no mark on it. He had had the pot since the year he was bound and had never marked it, because a man who owns nothing has no name to put on what holds nothing. In the pot, folded in a piece of wool that had once belonged to a coat, lay \lore{the warm stone} Sella had given him in the winter Garl died. It was two years gone and cold. He had not looked at it in a year. It had been given for the loft on the night a house went quiet in a way that had nothing to do with sleep, and he had kept it since as he kept the seven years: exactly, privately, without ever bringing either thing out where a thumb could be laid on it. A warmth that had been put into his hand and could be entered nowhere. A keeping that was not a payment, and would not become one by being hidden better.

He took the stone out of the pot. It was smaller than he remembered and colder than he had expected, though a whole year had gone by without his once thinking the checking worth the trouble. He held it in his two hands a moment, turning it so the light from the opening found the smooth place on one face. He set it on the sill where the bar of light reached it, where a man coming up the ladder would have seen it lying. He stood over it and looked at it a long while, with the wheel going below him and the afternoon going off the yard.

Then he put it back in the pot, and set the pot's foot down on the stone floor where it had stood, and went down the ladder. Not because it was forgiven. Not because the debt had moved by so much as the width of a hair. He had not reconciled the one with the other and had not tried, which was not forgiving, and the debt stood exactly as it had stood at the dark, and the stone lay where he had put it, still cold. There was no word spoken over any of it, no figure, no payer, no slate.

Below, the boy heard a small dry scrape of a clay pot's foot set down on stone, from the direction of the shed-loft, and then nothing. He did not see the stone. He did not know what had been moved, or that anything had been moved at all, or who had moved it; he could not have named the room or put a hand on its ladder. He had been given a sound with a direction, and he filed it on a place of his own, where a sound from a place he could not enter goes. He did not join it to the fraction held at the iron on the morning of Day one hundred and sixty-one, or to the beat that had stayed under the shelf on Day one hundred and sixty-six, or to the half-check the broom had made on the flagstones on Day one hundred and seventy-one. Three changes stood on three places, a wall, a room and a yard that touched neither, and he had spent every day since keeping a finger between them. A fourth likeness standing up beside them would call itself a cause, and a cause would want a payer under it, and a rule is an account that has found a payer. The scrape was not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping and not a payment. He let it stand alone and went on with his looking out of his two inches of world.

\scenebreak

When Tarn came down there was nothing about him to show what he had done, which was the whole of the discipline: a man can go up a ladder and come down it and leave no entry behind him. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth again, as it had on other evenings, and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure in it and no name. He had carried it so far so many times that the stopping had become a habit of the mouth, the way a man's tongue goes to a missing tooth. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom.

No tenth stopping stood up before the seventh year was earned. The nine stayed nine objects on the tray, intervals and not payments, with no payer named to any of them, and the Seed sixteen breath stayed unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. The loan stayed uncalled. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; something must give at the end of it, and the end was not here.

At the loaf shelf Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row that stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six. She gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge of the board. The charcoal tally lay where it had lain since the night she held a finger beneath it for one counted breath, unread, uncounted, uncopied, and the beat she had held there stayed where she had left it, an afternoon and a woman's back turned being a different thing from that night.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, and the two passed on the stair of the evening without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-three times kept the height of the handle and the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that gave no account of what the keeping cost.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer; around \place[the yard well]{the well}, where the trough stood bare and dark and its water was called weather; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed fifteen and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step, no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey beside it called the thaw lay unswept, and neither had an instruction.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing. A dusk was not an opening and a bed was not an opening; the one opening was behind the household, performed before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, and the hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She went to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross} and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and eighty-three days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, among them the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on the wrong wind, and none was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. Nine stoppings stood as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, and a tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln.

Three held changes stood on three separate places — the fraction in a wall, the beat beneath a shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on flagstones in a yard that touched neither — not a pair, not a doctrine, not a rule, for no payer stood to any of them. The loft-sill sound lay on a fourth place of its own and was not laid across them. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third. The told bell kept its place beside the heard, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself; the told figure stood beside the paid. The shelf's two gaps, the crust's gap, the pan's gone round place and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that was not a key and a list that was not a hand, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

Cold came to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. One hundred and eighty-three stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made into a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold, with the stone back in its pot in the loft, still cold, unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiving.
