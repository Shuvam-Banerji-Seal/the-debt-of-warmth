The afternoon came on without a line drawn across it, the light going from the colour of wet ash to the colour of a banked coal and then to nothing a man could put a name to, and the house went on as houses go when a pot has been set down in its place and the fire kept. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where the morning had left him, at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, held where the heat came sideways and stopped at his cheek as at the end of a line written out for it. The crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool. The empty place beside it held the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put and still a finding. The thread at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. He had learned to take the field in its own order and to leave the order alone. Three hundred and ninety-eight days stood behind him exact, and the afternoon added nothing to them and took nothing away. An afternoon is an afternoon and not a door.

Because the old order was the only instrument he had been left, the surgeon took the body again as the afternoon went on: the crust gone harder in the dry heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the fold in the empty place still a finding; the thread stiff at his mouth and unwiped; cold stopped at cheek and temple and the bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, and that pink returning is the surface lying over a cold core. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the quarrel went on being the work the way a wound goes on being a wound under a dressing that does not close it. He judged neither register, which is a kind of triage and not a peace.

Under \place{the boards} the four near nails and the four on the next board kept their places, and the thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not kept its place between them. He did not lay the knot across \lore{the carried fire}'s missing pull under the floor, though both were four and a gap and both wanted a fifth nobody supplied. Two gaps agreeing are two gaps and not one account; agreeing them wants a payer put under both, and none stood. Under the floor the fire breathed four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, the way a man answers a roll-call with four names and leaves the space for a fifth he will not fill. Its giver had been without a name for three hundred and ninety-eight days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it, and nobody asked who had first kept it. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had gone unspoken under this roof the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one keeping, for a fifth pull and an unspoken name are two gaps on two places with a finger between.

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the neighbours settled into the ordinary grammar of the tend as a room settles after a guest has gone. A woman going past with a pail set it down where she always set it and said to nobody that the batch had come out light; another answered from a door that the pot would have to answer for it; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. No figure passed from the one to the other, and the space at the end of the loaf row stayed a space. \person{Sella Vail} carried the basket down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, gave the instruction to the wool, the river wind, the pot and the fire and never to the basket, and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. She kept the wide step as a keeping and not a sign. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} kept its finer grey hairline beside it, called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. A neighbour with a rake looked at the ridge and looked away, and nothing was said of it, and the ridge stayed where it lay.

He read the yard by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the pail set on stone, the rake dragged once and left, a latch that did not open again, a loaf shifted along a board. Out of those he built no account and put no figure. The order of reaching — hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both — stood where it had been filed on a place of its own, and he did not make a tenth hand of any neighbour in the yard. A voice in a yard is a voice in a yard and not a room entered; a pause between voices is not a person. He had once been able to reduce a shoulder in the dark and could not lift his own hand to his own chin, and the two facts stood side by side without becoming a lesson.

At the going of the light Sella came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. “It's the thaw,” she said to the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the small red eye under the ash showed and shut and showed again. “The house will keep it,” she said to the pot, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured, the way a woman stands over a wound she has dressed and will not look at twice. Her finger lay along the top edge of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and left the hairline of the original break showing dark between the old-brass staple and the new binding. “Leave the crack showing,” she said to the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” To the fire she said, “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing for anybody who cares to look.

She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and no figure went over it and no handful was lifted, and then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the hand's intention and the hand's arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung against the cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. She did not put her hand to it. A shelf was not an opening, a hearth was not an opening, a going of the light was not an opening; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due on a Fourthday. She carried the basket up the nineteen steps and set it under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, righted the fold at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. The crust's gap with its corner standing a hair off the wool; the shelf's two gaps; the gone pan's round ring; \place{the field's empty place}; \lore{the keeping at the fold} found where put and still a finding; the told bell's gap in its square beside the heard bell; the silence after the name from \place{Weighing} on \lore{the gap tray} beside the clean voice's missing name; the empty name in the story of \place{the Long Night} and the place \place{the Remembering} did not fill, each with a finger between; the neighbour's called-gone grey beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner; \person{Tarn}'s ear-sentence stopped at the very word that would call the loan at the very end of a term already ended; \person{Jorra Vail}'s promise under \place{the Vail wheel-shed} neither given nor withdrawn; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside the seventh notch; the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, and the degree Tarn's head had gone toward it and back to the broom without a word. The clerk was a shape brought up the yard by report and seen by nobody under the roof, not a sighting from the basket, not a room entered, not a witness standing; he called nothing and wrote no name, and no interior belonged to him. Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended, and no slate lay open within reach of the basket and no figure had been given for the boy.

He had learned the trick of taking a field as found and leaving it as found, and it bought him nothing, which was the exactness of it. The gaps went on gathering toward a hand not his and not yet come, a hand that would one day lay a district's private keepings together and call the joining an account. He did not look for that hand and did not give it a name, for a name here would want a payer standing and none stood; what gathered gathered without him and went on gathering. A surgeon waits for a wound to declare itself. He had waited years on a house that would not declare anything, and the waiting had taught him the difference between attention and intervention: one is free, the other wants a hand that can reach.

Counting was an old-life habit and not a gift. He had counted the nails in the floorboards because there was nothing else to count, four on the near board, four on the next, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and he had gone on counting in a country that had no use for what he counted. The habit was no power here and had never been one. It gave him no key to a box up the road, no slate with his own name, no hand to lift a crust two inches; it gave him only the exactness of the field and the cost of it, which was nothing. A man may count his own breaths and learn nothing from the count but the counting, and go on counting because it is the only instrument left him.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace.
