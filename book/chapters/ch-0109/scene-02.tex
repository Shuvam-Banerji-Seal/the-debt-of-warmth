In the afternoon \person{Sella Vail} carried him down the nineteen steps from under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof into \place{the Clay Yard}, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. The basket went level as a pot that must not be slopped and out of the draught, and the whole of her attention was on the keeping of that level, so that at every turn of the landing she corrected the tilt before the tilt had time to become a habit in the wicker. \person[Corin]{The boy} felt the small correction before his body felt the stair. It was the correction a hand makes under a tray, exact and unremarked; the infant under the wool wanted it to go another way, toward the crust two inches off and the frozen thread at his lip, and got no such correction at all. Nineteen steps, each at the half-pace, and nobody counted them out loud, and the afternoon went down with the basket.

She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At \place{the Vail corner} she went around the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither, because a line left where it is is still a line kept and a thaw goes when it goes. The dry cold had thickened it; the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} came up off it where it had not been swept in two years, and she did not turn her head toward the smell, having settled long ago what the corner was and what it was not. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody, and she went around the space above them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. At \place{the rack end} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the pan had gone out of the house and the ring stayed, which is how a vessel is kept after it is gone. Out on \place{the worked strips} she went around the field's empty place, and around \place{the trough}, and around the silt-clouded plate of ice on \place{the flat stone by the well} that stood a little out of the path of feet. Widest of all she went around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, so wide that her elbow cut the corner of the yard and the basket tilted the other way to balance it, and she righted it without looking down.

He took the wide step apart the way he took a field apart, by what was there and by what was not. Seven places gone around with a look and no figure; the widest gone around by nothing measurable, and once and not twice. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it, and none stood. The yard kept its places by walking around them; the keeping cost a step each time and nobody entered the steps, and he did not enter them either. The clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it unswept, thickened in the month's dry cold, and the broken slates stayed entered by nobody, and the round ring at the rack end kept its emptiness without a word written into it.

The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin drawn edge to edge across the trough, and the plate of ice on the flat stone a little out of the path of feet. They were three, and not a fourth covering laid over the three, and not a sixth low thing set beside the five held objects. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Over it from bank to bank lay \lore{the winter skin} that the cold had put on and that the cold would take off, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this afternoon and not made again, not spent on the day's work or the going of the light. A line left where it is is still a line kept; a sign is a thing that points to a payer, and no payer stood under that ground. He kept the covering on its own place beside the note and put a finger between the two, so that no row came out even by his doing.

At \place{the yard well} \person{Ilva} drew with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Stone cold giving its small dry rasp, the water dark and moving without a sound. Clay stood pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft; the people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. To the woman waiting her turn who asked what \place{Stone}'s firings had taken, her hand went toward her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, to the cord or the knot-string, and no figure came off the wood. She answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

Then she put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice; she had not looked into the basket and had no notion she had been read. The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart — hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap — and filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth. He filed no tenth, made no reader of her keeping, joined her to none and made no figure of the slate's nearness. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice that had come over the boards: no ownerless word had come on this day, and the \place{Ash} 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each where put and did not repeat and bought no lesson.

The walk-ons brought directions and never figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, said, “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price,” and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. To a woman with a basket of \place{the kindling} who asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and to two men mending a strip of fence who said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, \person{Tarn} said, “Yes, house,” to each and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument found a foot.

At the kindling the account stood in its old exactness: six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh at the lip beyond it beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. Tarn passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes over a tool put back where it belongs, told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” and cut nothing, counted nothing and spent nothing. The Ash 14 held breath and the \place{Bone} 26 thumb's resting were found where they had been put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair — not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not the calling of the loan, with no payer standing.

The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back; his chest had been tight since the dark, and the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch of \place{the flagstones} without the half-check. He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled; he did not look along the road. Two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called, with nobody standing to enter it. Garl's name had gone unspoken under this roof three hundred and seventy-eight days and the carried fire's fifth had gone unpaid the same number, and the two counts stood side by side and did not become one.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold. Dusty forearms and clean ones he kept on one tray and parted as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.”

To a pot going past on a bench he said, “You'll do,” with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, knowing the \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withholding the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted — one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word covering both mercies. The finger came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time; that holding was gathered once and stays where nobody entered it. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, gathering interest against the burned slate at five and the three Ash directions.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of the loaf shelf. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and did not tuck a second fold into the empty place. Her hand did only what the setting needed and went away; the little-door burn closed on air; the thread was left frozen and unwiped; she said nothing of where they had been. The boy filed the keeping found where put and not made again: not a habit, for a habit wants a second placing; not a way, for a way wants a third and both want a payer where none stands; not a sign, a hearing, a mending, a gift, a payment or a rule.

Under the roof the hearth grammar was renewed and the wider file checked without standing up. At \place{the hearth} she told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, said the house would keep it where another woman would have said I, counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening with neither gap figured, and kept her finger along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread. On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool with no figure lifted and no handful, and the cord of her \lore{widow's mark} hung untouched, for a going of the light and a hearth are not openings and no eighth was made due. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and seventy-eight days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and the flat knock of \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} outside them; the sixth was the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. The heard bell of \lore{the Long Night} stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into the three counts or made a seventh hearing. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, and nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. Ten arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of \place{Frost}, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash's first, Ash's second, Ash's third and Stone's first — the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine out of ten; their ten true subtractions rode locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down and the boy having no key. Four rooms kept the same gap in their count held apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account. Nothing was lifted out of its place to make a row come out even, and Stone 20's eleventh arithmetic stood one morning ahead and was not brought into this day.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel when a case is done and before the next is called, and the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-two: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

The wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the crust gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The hand lay flat against the fold on answer three hundred and thirty-two, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-eight exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-eight days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire's fifth unpaid the same number and Garl's name unspoken the same number. Stone kept its nineteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held both its names, and Stone 20's reading stood one morning ahead and was not brought in. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.
