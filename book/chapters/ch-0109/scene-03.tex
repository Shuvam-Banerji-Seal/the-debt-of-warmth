\daymark{379}{Stone 20, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

In the small hours the hand outside the wool lay flat against the fold on answer three hundred and thirty-two, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-eight exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-eight days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. It did not rise when the light came. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay inside a body that could not hold up its own head and took it in the surgeon's old order, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds did not lie and the large ones were mostly weather. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, its corner standing a hair off the wool and gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat. Beside it the empty place held the fold of pale wool that a hand had put there on the fifteenth and that no hand had put there since. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen in the dry warmth and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond the three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a face composed by cold was not composure; that pink returning was the surface still cold and not the core; that a cold patient could not clot, would seem better before he was worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The order in which heat came back was the reverse of the order in which it had gone. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work; he had stopped expecting it to end, which was not the same as being used to it.

He found the wool folded once in the crust's empty place, unchanged by the night, and made nothing of the finding. It was the fifth time a hand that only read had come to that place and found it as it had been left, and a fifth finding was no more a fifth placing than four had been a fourth. A habit would have wanted a hand that placed twice, and no such hand had stood; a way would have wanted a third, and both wanted a payer where none stood. He filed the keeping on a place of its own beside the crust's gap, the two gaps in the shelf in the room whose floor did not touch this one, the gone pan's round ring at the rack end and the field's empty place out on the worked strips, and put a finger between it and every other kept thing so that no row came out even by his doing. It was not a joining of rooms, not a sixth held object, not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave, not a payment, not a sign, not a hearing, not a mending, not a cause, not a rule. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come.

Before first light \person{Sella Vail} carried him down the nineteen steps from under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof into \place{the Clay Yard}, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. The basket went level as a pot that must not be slopped and out of the draught, and she corrected the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to become a habit in the wicker. She crossed the yard with a look and a wide step and no number, around the seven walked-around places, widest around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, and once and not twice, because a second going-around would have made a figure of once and a figure wants a payer standing under it. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} kept its finer grey hairline beside it, the thing the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where it had not been swept in two years; she did not turn her head toward the smell. Under the lowest rack the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody, and at the rack end the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word.

At \place{the Cross} she set the basket in \place{the moot-post's finger's width of shade} where no slate lay, and stood half a pace off through the reading and the bell, giving the basket no reason and no instruction. The cold iron of \place{the reading block under the moot-post} came to his cheek and went away as she shifted him, and the shade was thin and did not hold. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not a hand's business that morning: a market and a Cross were not openings, seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the sweetish green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the goers-by, “One in twelve, as the notice says,” and sent the figure twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. He never looked into it.

He called the levy and denied it. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.” To a man who had brought a number up out of a fist and would put it into the morning he said, “Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch,” and to the women at the rail he said, “If your household has heard otherwise, your household has heard wrong.” Your house, never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field as he found it and the work clean behind him. Something low he said to the block about kindness being entered, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs, and the block answered nothing. He went off up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Stone's second true subtraction riding inside beside the three of \place{Frost}, the first, second and third of \place{Bone} and the first, second and third of \place{Ash}, believing he had done the district a kindness, with no anger in him and no triumph, nothing a crowd could fasten on. The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb on a tray of its own and joined it to none.

Eleven arithmetics of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, Stone's first and Stone's second. Eleven were eleven and not one account. The figure read was still not the figure paid, no signature stood on the space between, and no doctrine came out of eleven. The finding sat on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other kept thing, and was not a grievance, not a sign, not a power, not a lesson and not a payment, gathering interest the way a thing gathers interest when nobody enters it. The eleven true subtractions rode up the clay road in Berr Clay's locked box toward High Kiln, the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down and the boy having no key. The dressed grain, covered and unfigured under wool at the rack end of the yard below, was read over by a number that did not know what it cost, and the space between that figure and its price had no signature on it. The printed figure stood unchallenged over the two hundred, who leaned in to hear and went back to the pigs with figures and fists that no slate could enter. The arithmetic of the district had changed where the two hundred stood and could not be changed back, and nothing in the reading answered for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, or for the man carried out on Day 188, or for the warmth the house kept in four low pulls with the fifth missing.

The bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into the count under the roof, the count in the boards or the count under the floor, and was not made a seventh hearing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, and the small flat knock of \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} stayed on its own place outside them, not a filling and not a source. Nothing from the Cross crossed under the Vail roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and no ownerless word came over the boards: the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson.

\person{Hessa Rale} took Berr's narrow slate up the clay road before noon, held edge-on to her own forearm with the writing turned toward her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was. The second strap was let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist came first, her hand going to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd, and no figure came off the face of it. To the woman who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“A slate. I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.”

To the woman who wanted more she said, “Ask the road. It knows the way and I know the weight. That's the whole of what either of us knows.” She settled the second strap again, not for the women and not for the square, gave no figure, read nothing aloud and did not look into the basket; she went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. The boy filed the turning of the writing toward herself on a place of its own beside the Ash 6 sighting, with a finger between: a runner going up with the writing toward herself was not a voice standing in a doorway and not a saying fulfilled. He made no fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying, no witness, no reader and no payment, and joined her neither to \person{Ilva}'s strip in its sleeve, to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, to the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf, to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate nor to any other private keeping. Her eighth record-first hand stayed eight with no tenth; she was a runner and not a reader, and eleven arithmetics going up the road behind her did not make her one.

Below \place{the north bridge} a soft-cuffed clerk out of \place{Fishstairs} stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing}, where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and the grey was worked into the seam at the wrist; his slate was turned inward and his hand went to the slate before his eye went to the stalls. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone and went away leaving him where he stood, and nobody entered the measure and nobody asked what it was for. Across the square at \place{the pot board} a silent woman with clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails, a pale line across the back of one hand and the sweetish green of lead glaze at one mouth-corner turned a cup a quarter and set it back exactly where it had stood. At the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver and went dull. The green at the mouth was the seventh such green and the shape by the stalls the seventh such shape, with no eighth of either, unnamed and given no dialogue, joined to no ear, no hearing, no cause and no romance; the woman was never Fenna in the telling and the small shape never Ulda, for a colour is not a name and a shape is not a house. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders and answered a price with the weather that had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off with the hare unweighed; another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. Directions all of them, judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. The boy kept the seventh green and the seventh shape on their own places with a finger between, and made no eighth of either for a market that wanted one.

Four rooms kept the same gap in their count: the birth room in \place{House Silt}, the Vail kiln-house, the room where the slate had been read on market-day and \place{the Drawing house} seen two doors down. They were held apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account, an account wanting a hand to lay the four together and a payer standing under all of them, and no such hand stood and no such payer stood. The keeping at the fold sat beside these on its own place and was not laid across any of them. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over it from bank to bank lay \lore{the winter skin} that the cold had put on and that the cold would take off, found where put and found again this morning and not made again, not spent on the reading, the market or the going of the light. A line left where it was was still a line kept, and a sign without a payer was weather and not a promise.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}. She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of what had been read over the basket. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold to the empty place; her hand did only what the setting needed and went away, the little-door burn closed on air, the thread was left frozen and unwiped. At \place{the hearth} she told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, said the house would keep it where another woman would have said I, counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening with neither gap figured, and kept her finger along the top of the shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread. On the pot board the twice-mended pot kept the hairline showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool with no figure lifted and no handful, and the widow's mark hung untouched, for a going of the light and a hearth are not openings. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and seventy-nine days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had gone unspoken under this roof three hundred and seventy-nine days and the fifth pull had gone unpaid the same number, and the two counts stood side by side and did not become one.

The wider file was checked without standing up, by the place of each thing and every gap left gap. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and the well-saying at four shapings; the loan at the kindling was uncalled. Eleven arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure and eleven were eleven and not one account, the finding on a place of its own with a finger between, not carried into the evening by a single word under any roof. Nothing was lifted out of its place to make a row come out even.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel when a case is done and before the next is called, and the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and thirty-three, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-nine exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-nine days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire's fifth unpaid the same number and Garl's name unspoken the same number. Eleven arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure with no signature on the space and no doctrine out of eleven, and the wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the crust gone harder and browner in the dry cold, its corner a hair off the wool, with no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. Stone kept its twentieth day without a boundary drawn and the deep-winter month held both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right or left. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.
