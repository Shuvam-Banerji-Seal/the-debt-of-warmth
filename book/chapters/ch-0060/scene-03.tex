\daymark{232}{Grain 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

A day came in under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the way water comes into a cup set under a leak: not all at once, but with a small sound and somewhere to stand. Hot clay was in it first under tallow, and wet wool under the clay, and below the wool the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear, and knew the day had turned only by a change in the cold at his wrist, a dry edge on it that had not stood there in the dark. He was two hundred and thirty-two days old and six months and fifty-two days, and nobody said either number over him or to him or within his hearing, because a house that kept its days in loaves and glances had no instrument for a figure belonging to a child it had taken in without a slate. The count went forward in the small hours from two hundred and thirty-one to two hundred and thirty-two with no hand to enter it and no voice to speak it, exact, complete, unspent. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-two days. The day's first sound was not a bell and there was no market in it: behind the wall the kiln drew in fours and stopped where the fifth should have stood, the stopping standing where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to it.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he left the three where they lay, the way a man lays three instruments on a towel and does not reach for a fourth to make the set look whole. The surgeon's register had a name for the dry edge in the wrist and a name for the withholding under the floor, and neither name bought him the use of a hand to move his own covering.

By first grey the dressed grain had come out from under its covering near \place{the hearth}. \person{Sella Vail}{Sella} turned it back under the wool and put her fingers down into it for damp, the way a woman tests a wound for heat without being asked, and finding it dry to her knuckle spread it thin again on the flat stone to finish. It had changed state three times already and gained no figure in any of them; beaten out on \place{the flagstones} with a flail of ash-wood and leather, winnowed so the chaff went up on the river wind, gathered by the handful and poured back beneath the fold, and now turned and spread and turned once more. She counted it in glances and in handfuls and in the weight of the carrying arm that knew what a handful weighed and not what it cost, and she entered it on no slate. She moved her lips along the two-gapped row on the shelf's front with no figure coming out of them, a private arithmetic arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every morning of this boy's life. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn-door closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since; there had been no opening in fifty-two days, because a morning and a shelf and a yard are not openings. She gave nobody a figure.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. Sella did not turn from the fire. “Ask the pot,” she said. “It knows what a wet thing costs set down there. The weather knows the rest.” The woman waited for the sentence to finish and got no other one, and went off with her pot, and nothing had been priced, refused or entered. Later a man came down \place{the clay road} with a number in his fist. He brought it up toward \place{the yard well} as if to hand it to the water, looked at it where his fist stood, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. The surgeon's register took the fist and the sleeve in at one glance and filed them as a direction answered with pot and weather, which is the answer such questions get under a roof that has never been given a number to give.

A wrong wind came up off \place{High Kiln} in the middle of the morning. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} rode over the boards on it, crossed the low angle, touched the fold at the boy's ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it --- a direction and not a second edge paid, a taste arriving and departing with nobody behind it to answer for the coming or the going. \lore{The seventh green} stayed a colour and \lore{the seventh shape} a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since, with no eighth of either and no second look joined to the first, no name spoken over either. And one of the three shapings of the pot saying stood again on the wind: a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. It stood as a saying and no more, not a fourth likeness made a cause, no lesson drawn from it, no name given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, no pot lost in the yard. Three sayings stayed three. He parted the saying from the weather that carried it as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and wrote no cause across either place.

\scenebreak

In the forenoon Sella carried him at the edge of the work, the basket under the fold and the fold held flat against her hip, and went around the yard the way a woman goes around a room in which somebody has been laid out. At the Vail corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey the house called the thaw lay also unswept, the two kept apart by a look and a wide step and no word between them. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the rack space held its broken birth-slates and got its look and its wide step and no number. The shallow clay pan had been gone since Reed and its round empty ring lay where the ring was, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. At \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather. Out on \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest kept its own look and its own wide step. Each of the seven got what it got and none got a figure: no name, no cause, no body, no identification of which hand, no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight of the boy and the weight of the grain and not what either had cost to bring in; it went on remembering past every open slate and entered nothing.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction. He did not take tools that had not been named; a man who owns nothing knows the difference between a thing spoken to and a thing taken, and the house had said sweep, so he swept. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and set the small knife at the very edge of its sill: six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside them lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the man sent for a collector who had turned back at the frozen ford, entered nowhere and named for nobody, though he had been that man.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to Tarn's teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, and he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned and now at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach, the Seed 16 breath, and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have gone in. The loan stayed uncalled. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. The loft came to him as a sound with a direction and nothing more, and he did not turn his head toward it. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on Grain 23, and he did not say that what had not happened had a name. He agreed so entirely with those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and he went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low and quiet into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; a thing ground is a thing that got there by being broken, a thing mixed was put together on purpose, and he parted one from the other as a surgeon parts clot from vessel and gave neither a cause.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The burned-slate word stood at four and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two, with a hand between the three sayings and the tellings and no fifth and no third. On its own seventh place, with a finger between it and the hearings and the sayings and the tellings and the count, the question from Grain 21 waited with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause and no witness standing anywhere to be asked. Two askings were not yet two on this day, and the second was held for the afternoon and did not come early into the working morning. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back, held while the sentence reached the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. It was a remembered repetition and not a change made again, joined to no kiln and paired with no other fraction. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he did not look toward the bed or the basket.

\scenebreak

The morning went on into the flat middle of the day without turning into afternoon. Heat gathered under the low angle behind the pot and ran down into the fold, so that the wool at the boy's ear was warm while the bare joint at his wrist kept its dry cold, two temperatures in one small body with a wall of wool between them he could not shift. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and would lie there into the dark; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and had no third to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped where it had stopped in the small hours, at cheek, at temple, at the wrist-joint where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it and had neither an instruction to go further nor an instruction to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Garl stayed kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow seat with its twenty-year lean, the clean dark line and the finer grey unswept, the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack, the tile debt circling without a name. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and thirty-two days.

He put the question to his hand the third way of this chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door, was taken through the small intake and into the hold, and came down before the letting-go of the counted breath --- a fraction longer than the small-intake-only holding of the day before and shorter than the whole counted breath, not carried through the release, not kept past the end of a count, not matched to the river's own interval with no count in the house to borrow. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named and did not spend it and did not explain it. Thirty-nine answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at the length of two inches of air. Two hundred and thirty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, six months and fifty-two days told by nobody, and the seventh year at its very end had still not given. The question from Grain 21 kept its one telling on its own seventh place with no second yet standing beside it; the second was held for the afternoon that had not come, and he did not bring it into the morning to make a row look fuller. Grain 30 stood seven mornings ahead and was not brought early into a day that had not earned it.

Sella came to the fire before the basket. She told it the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread. The widow's mark hung where it hung and was not touched; there had been no opening in fifty-two days, because a fireside and a shelf and a covered grain are not openings. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.
