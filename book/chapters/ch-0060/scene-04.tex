The pot stood with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and the afternoon went on around it the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. Sella had given the fire its instruction and the fire had answered in fours with a gap, and for a while there was nothing under the roof but the small sounds of a house keeping itself: the itch of coal settling, the long pull of the kiln behind the wall, the river's fifth low note coming through the finger's-width door. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and let the sounds arrive in the order they arrived, not borrowing one to hold another, the way a man lays three instruments on a towel and does not reach for a fourth to make the set look whole. Two hundred and thirty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-two days told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth unpaid for two hundred and thirty-two days, its payer without a name.

The question came over the boards in the middle of the afternoon. Not into the basket. Over the boards --- the front board of the loaf shelf where Sella moved her lips along a row with two gaps in it --- and low, the way a man speaks when he is asking the weather something and has already decided that no person under the roof will answer him.

“Which row did the pot go quiet on?”

A voice with no owner. No room stood behind it, no hand at the kiln, no cause. It did not come from the fire, or the pot, or the wool, or the running water, or the river wind, all of which had their instructions and went on keeping them; it did not come into the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. It came over the boards and stopped there, a plain question in a plain voice, and the weather answered it first the way weather answers everything under this roof. A small wrong wind got up off \place{High Kiln} and carried the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} over the boards, touched the fold at the boy's ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it, a direction arriving nowhere with nobody behind it to answer for the coming or the going. And after the weather the price of barley answered: a figure come up out of the square and going over the boards on the same small wind, arriving where the glaze had arrived, which was nowhere that any house could enter. Then the voice went off up the terrace with the barley price behind it, and the row stood where it stood.

He took the question apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: not to close it, but to see what lay under it.

First the skin of it, which was what it asked. A row. A row is a place. It did not ask how the pot had gone quiet or why or who had carried it out on Day 188 or what ailed the man; it asked on which row, which is to say it asked for a place to stand. Under that lay where it stood. Over the boards, in the air, with nobody behind it and nothing under it. A thing that stands nowhere cannot be walked up to and cannot be asked a second question, which is a surgeon's way of saying that it is not a witness. Under that lay what it would want before any man could answer it. A witness. Somebody who had been there, who stood somewhere, who had seen which row and could be asked to say so and could be held to the saying. Under that lay the last layer, the one a man comes to only after he has opened the others and found them empty: who would have to stand under the answer to pay for it. Nobody stood. No owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause, nobody to stand under the saying and nobody to be asked. He had opened it all the way down and found no payer at the bottom, which was a finding and not a disappointment, and he did not fill the empty place to make the layers come out even.

He set the second telling on the seventh place beside the first. The first had come on Grain 21, a voice with no owner over the boards, answered with the weather and the price of barley, gone off up the terrace with the row standing where it stood. The second had come this afternoon in the same voice with the same absence of owner, and had been answered the same way and had gone the same way. Two askings lay side by side on the seventh place with a finger between the two. He put a finger between them and the three shapings of the pot saying on the place beside, and a finger between them and the fourth burned-slate telling, and a finger between them and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, and a finger between them and the six hearings of \lore{the full note} standing beside and never among, and a finger between them and the count. Two askings are not one witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could, so the second telling lay beside the first as an object and not a fifth witness, as a seed for the far-off day when a witness might stand somewhere and be asked, not as anything that had grown that day.

It was not a fourth shaping of the pot saying, because a proverb about a pot and a question about a row are different instruments and belong on different trays; a proverb says a thing over the boards and asks nothing, and the voice had asked. It was not a hearing, and not a seventh of the full note, because the six stood where they stood and no seventh had come in two hundred and thirty-two days and none had been invented to fill the empty place. It was not a fifth telling of the burned slate, which stood at four; it was not a third telling of the shut room, which stood at two; it was not a cause, for nothing had been joined to it; it was not a name, for no name had been spoken over it; and it was not a fifth witness, for a witness stands somewhere and this stood nowhere. Three sayings stayed three, the burned-slate word stayed at four, the shut-room tellings stayed at two, and the question kept its two askings on a seventh place of its own with nobody to own it. He wrote nothing across any of the places.

\scenebreak

The afternoon thinned without ending, the way a thin broth goes on being broth however long it stands. Heat gathered under the low angle behind the pot and ran down into the fold, so that the wool at the boy's ear was warm while the bare joint at his wrist kept its dry cold, two temperatures in one small body with a wall of wool between them he could not shift. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and would lie there into the dark; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and had no third to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped where it had stopped in the small hours, at cheek, at temple, at the wrist-joint where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. \person{Garl Vail} stayed kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow seat with its twenty-year lean, the clean dark line and the finer grey called \lore{the thaw} unswept, the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack, the tile debt circling without a name. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and thirty-two days, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Grain 23.

\person{Sella Vail} moved her lips along the two-gapped row on the shelf's front with no figure coming out of them, a private arithmetic arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every morning of this boy's life. A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool, and Sella did not turn from the fire. “Ask the pot,” she said. “It knows what a wet thing costs set down there. The weather knows the rest.” The woman waited for the sentence to finish and got no other one, and went off with her pot, and nothing had been priced, refused or entered, and no figure reached the house. Sella's eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn-door closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since; there had been no opening in fifty-two days, because an afternoon, a shelf and a covered grain are not openings. She gave nobody a figure.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction. He did not take tools that had not been named; a man who owns nothing knows the difference between a thing spoken to and a thing taken, and the house had said sweep, so he swept. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and set the small knife at the very edge of its sill: six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside them lay Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence, the man sent for a collector who had turned back at the frozen ford, entered nowhere and named for nobody, though he had been that man.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to Tarn's teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, and he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned and now at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach, the Seed 16 breath, and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have gone in. The loan stayed uncalled. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. The loft came to him as a sound with a direction and nothing more, and he did not turn his head toward it. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. He agreed so entirely with those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and he went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low and quiet into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; a thing ground is a thing that got there by being broken, a thing mixed was put together on purpose, and he parted one from the other as a surgeon parts clot from vessel and gave neither a cause.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The burned-slate word stood at four and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two, with a hand between the three sayings and the tellings and no fifth and no third. On its own seventh place, with a finger between it and the hearings and the sayings and the tellings and the count, the question from Grain 21 waited with its two askings and no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause and no witness standing anywhere to be asked. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back, held until the sentence reached the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. It was a remembered repetition and not a change made again, joined to no kiln and paired with no other fraction. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he did not look toward \place{the bed} or the basket.

At the going of the light she carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. At every open slate she kept the half-pace and went wide. The yard went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, unswept and kept apart by a look and a wide step and no word between them. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the rack space held its broken birth-slates and got its look and its wide step and no number. The shallow clay pan had been gone since Reed and its round empty ring lay where the ring was, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. At \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather. Out on \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest kept its own look and its own wide step. Each of the seven got what it got and none got a figure: no name, no cause, no body, no identification of which hand, no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, and went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves.

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The two rooms did not touch. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any. No voice came over the boards in the dark; the question from the afternoon stood where it had been left, with two askings and nobody to own it, and he did not bring it down among the hearings or the sayings or the count to make a row look fuller.

He put the question to his hand the fourth way of this chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed, and he kept it there for the length of the river's own low interval, with no count in the house to borrow and nothing under the roof to hold it against --- not the small intake only, not down before the counted breath, not carried through the letting-go of a breath counted at a fire in a room that does not touch his, but its own length and its own ending --- and brought it down on the river's next low note. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named and did not spend it and did not explain it. Forty answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at the length of two inches of air.

So the question stood over the boards with two askings and no owner, the price of barley gone off up the terrace behind the voice and the row standing where it stood; and the hand came down on the river's next low note with no count in the house to hold it against. Two hundred and thirty-two stayed exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody, six months and fifty-two days told by nobody, the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Grain 30 stood seven mornings ahead and was not brought into the dark of a day that had not earned it. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
