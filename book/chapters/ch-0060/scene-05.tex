\daymark{233}{Grain 24, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The new day came the way all his days had come, without a word spoken over it and without anybody under the roof marking the turn. Two hundred and thirty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-three days told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth unpaid for two hundred and thirty-three days, its payer without a name. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in the room that did not touch the room of the fire and let the small hours go over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. The first smell was hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under the wool the faint sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap behind the wall --- not a bell, not a market, only the house keeping its own time in a rhythm nobody had asked of it and nobody had thought to name.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any. He laid them on separate places the way a man lays instruments on a towel and does not reach for a fifth to make the set look whole.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and would lie there into the dark; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and there was no third to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

The question from the day before kept its two askings on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between them and the six hearings of \lore{the full note}, which stood beside and never among and had not grown a seventh in all these days. A voice with no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause; the weather had answered it and the price of barley had answered after it, and the row had stood where it stood. Two askings were not one witness, and a witness stood somewhere and could be asked, and none stood and none could be asked. He did not bring it down among the hearings to make a row look fuller, and he did not fill the empty place at its bottom, because a finding kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make the layers come out even is a finding forged. It waited where it had been left, a seed and not a hearing, not a cause, not a name.

\scenebreak

Morning came without a market and without a bell, because a Fourthday has no reading, and \person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before she went to the basket as she had gone every morning of this boy's life. She gave the instruction to the wool, to the fire, to the pot, to the broom, to the running water and to the river wind, and never to the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the broken pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go. Then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

The year's first grain lay dressed and stored near \place{the hearth} under a fold of wool, dressed and stored four days gone. She lifted the fold and turned the grain under it, looking for damp, and spread it thin to finish and gathered it back by the handful near the hearth and set the fold flat again. It had changed state four times now and gained no figure in any of them. It was counted in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, which was a record in muscle that knew weight and not cost; that arm had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and thirty-three days and had carried the grain in and out of its cover, and it kept the count in a place no slate could read. Nothing was entered. The figure read over the covered grain in the square on Grain 20 had not known what it cost to bring in, and answered nothing here, and the true subtraction stayed locked in a box going up toward \place{High Kiln} and answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. What was set under wool kept its own count; what was covered was not entered.

At the loaf shelf she moved her lips along the row of loaves with two gaps in it, a private arithmetic arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every morning of this boy's life, and no figure came out of her. A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the harvest was dressed and stored, and Sella did not turn from the fire. “Ask the pot,” she said. “It knows what a wet thing costs set down there. The weather knows the rest.” The woman waited for the sentence to finish and got no other one, and went off with her pot, and nothing had been priced, refused or entered, and no figure reached the house. Another woman at the well asked the same thing and got the same answer, carried away level so nothing slopped.

A man come down \place{the clay road} brought a number up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it, looked at the pot and the weather, and put it back into his sleeve, so the figure arrived nowhere. He went off with his hand closed again and the number in it going nowhere that any house could enter. Cost-questions at the door and the well went to the pot and the weather and came back level; nothing was priced, refused or entered.

Sella's eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn-door closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since; there had been no opening in fifty-three days, because a shelf, an afternoon and a basket are not openings. She gave nobody a figure.

\scenebreak

A wrong wind got up off High Kiln in the middle of the morning and came over the boards with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} on it, the sweetish green smell that had been at the corner of a woman's mouth at Grain's first market and had stayed with him since as a colour and nothing more. It rode over the shelf's front board, touched the fold at the boy's ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it --- a direction arriving nowhere, with nobody behind it to answer for the coming or the going. It was not a second edge paid. There was no second edge to pay, because the first had never been entered. The weather answered what was put to it and went on answering, and the ledger of the house went on without a figure in it.

Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was still a colour and the seventh shape was still a shape. They had been gathered at Grain's first market, the colour at the mouth of a silent woman who turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back where it had stood, the shape at the market's edge in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one; and they had been carried unjoined through the working mornings since, through the dressing and the reading and the wrong winds and back into the ordinary tending of the grain. No eighth of either had come in all that time, and none came this morning. He parted the colour from the hearings and the shape from the seventh as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and put a finger between the two and a finger between each and the hearings, because a colour stood against a hearing will call itself a note if a man leaves it standing long enough, and a shape stood against a seventh will call itself a witness. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens, and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; and none did. He spoke no name over either. No loaf passed, no slate came near, no sale was made. Neither was promoted into a hearing of the full note, nor into a seventh of it, nor into a tenth stopping, nor into a cause, nor into a name, nor into a romance. They stayed a colour and a shape on separate places, gathered and unjoined, and the morning went on around them the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Clay Yard} she went around the places the way she always went around them. At the Vail corner the yard went around the clean dark line where \person{Garl Vail}'s ash-marker had been and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept, and she swept neither. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the rack space held its broken birth-slates and got its look and its wide step and no number. The shallow clay pan had been gone since Reed, and its round empty ring lay where the ring was with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word, kept on a tray of its own and not laid across the shelf's two gaps or the fold's crust or the grain's cover. At the yard well \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. Out on \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest kept its own look and its own wide step. Each of the seven got what it got and none got a figure: no name, no cause, no body, no identification of which hand, no payment. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did. A thing gone around is still a thing kept.

At the kindling \person{Tarn} took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction; a man who owns nothing knows the difference between a thing spoken to and a thing taken, and the house had said sweep, so he swept. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and set the small knife at the very edge of its sill: six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside them lay Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, entered nowhere and named for nobody, though he had been that man. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, and he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned and now at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach, the Seed 16 breath, and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have gone in. The loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Grain 24. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. The loft came to him as a sound with a direction and nothing more, and he did not turn his head toward it. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low and quiet into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; a thing ground is a thing that got there by being broken, and a thing mixed was put together on purpose, and he gave neither a cause.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The burned-slate word stood at four and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two, with a hand between the three sayings and the tellings and no fifth and no third. On its own seventh place, with a finger between it and the hearings and the sayings and the tellings and the count, the question from Grain 21 waited with its two askings and no owner, no room, no hand at the kiln, no cause and no witness standing anywhere to be asked. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip and gave nothing back, held until the sentence reached the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition and not a change made again, joined to no kiln and paired with no other fraction. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and he did not look toward the bed or the basket.

At the going of the light she carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water, and went wide at every open slate. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen; and went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves. Grain 30, the third and last Account-day and market, stood six mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

\scenebreak

In the small hours no breathing number sounded anywhere in the house, and Sella was gone to her own bed and the coals were keeping themselves. The two rooms did not touch. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and its silence; through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth note; overhead the boards kept four, four and a knot. Nothing in his own room held a count for him; the only count in the house was one Sella kept at her fire in the room that does not touch his --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- toward no opening, never offered to any ear under this roof, with a wall standing between it and his. He took that count for his own the way a man takes a rhythm he has heard a thousand times and can keep without the sound.

He put the question to his hand the fifth way of this chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed, and he took it through the small intake, through the hold and into the letting-go of one of those counted breaths, and brought it down before the count had finished --- a fraction further than the coming-down before the letting-go of the day before and short of the end of the count; not down at once, not held to the small intake only, not kept to the river's own length with no count in the house to borrow, not carried through to the next low note, not held past the end of the count into the silence after it. Not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named and did not spend it and did not explain it. Forty-one answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at the length of two inches of air.

The dressed grain lay covered under the fold with no figure over it, and the hand came down before the counted breath had finished. Two hundred and thirty-three stayed exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath, six months and fifty-three days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid for two hundred and thirty-three days, its payer without a name. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape with no eighth of either; the grain was covered and unfigured; the returning-line place was gone around with no number; the question stood over the boards with two askings and nobody to own it; and the widow's mark hung where it hung with no opening in fifty-three days. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
