\daymark{145}{Reed 26, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

Before first grey the dark lay in the hollows of \place{the Clay Yard} like water held in a dented pan, and the men came up through it with their collars high and their voices low, three or four of them, giving no face to the yard and taking no name from it. They went in toward \place{the Vail wheel-shed} and through the door of \place[the back room]{the room off the shed}, which stood a finger's width open and did not shut, and the cold came out of that finger's width in a thread and went nowhere the house could enter. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} past at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. She turned no head toward the door. She gave the house no name for the men and did not say the room was cold, because a room a house does not enter is a room a house does not describe, and what is not described cannot be entered anywhere else either. Woodsmoke went before her and the river wind came up to meet it, and the two passed through one another about her shoulders without either giving way. Keep it off his ears, she told the wool; it had come down with them and would go back up with them, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. One hundred and forty-five days old. Four months and twenty-five days. Neither number was spoken under any roof that morning, and if a voice in the yard had said one aloud nobody there would have known what it counted.

He lay with his cheek to the fold and the small wet sound coming and going as his head wandered and was not set right. From that height the world was a row of hems and the low reach of a door, and a man's collar held high was the same as a man's face turned away: a shape with no owner, which is the safest shape to give a slate. He had known men who held their faces like that in a room with better light, and knew the shape here without needing the word for it in \lore{Grathic}. The low voices went on behind the finger's width of door and came to nothing in the yard, and the yard went on without them the way a floor goes on under a bucket somebody will wipe on the morrow. The small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water over water that came through the open door and came down against the fold; nothing within two paces could be reached, not the door, not the thread of cold, not the hem of a coat going in. The arm that carried him was a record in muscle that told exactly what had been carried and never what it cost: level, half-paced, going wide around every slate. He read it once and set it down. One hundred and forty-five he kept where he kept it, exact and complete and entered nowhere, carried past the door as a woman carries a pot filled to the brim and will not have it slopped.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's width of door ahead of them with the morning's dust still on him, and the dust was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. It was fine grey slate dust ground small as meal, worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, the creases of the wrists full of it and the little finger's bend holding a pale line of it where nothing else reached. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray as two states of the same morning, side by side and not across, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, because a question put to that dust would have had to be put to the room it came out of, and the room was a place a house did not enter. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing, and no hand that morning went in among them. His eyes did not finish sentences; they went to the door, to the wheel, to the fire and came back without reporting, and the arranged face he turned toward the bench told the boy as little as a chart turned down tells a man standing at its foot. He had learned that a record kept in a material no slate will spell is still a record, and that the care of its keeping tells more than the reading would; but the boy could not take the telling to any door, and kept it where it stood.

Jorra went to the wheel before he went to anyone and spoke to it first. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel took it the way a wheel takes anything and went on turning. He set a pot on the bench with the flat of his hand and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. The boy heard the note in the pot before it left the wheel. It was a full note, whole and clear under the knuckle of the world, the sound a vessel makes when nothing in it has given way; he had known that sound in a room across a birth-room wall and under this very roof through a wall of clay, and had never once been able to put a hand on the vessel that made it. Jorra knew it too. The boy knew that he knew it, knew it three turns before the pot left the wheel, and kept the knowing where questions are allowed to last for years. “He's not Vail” stayed where it had been sealed on the hour it was spoken, ungiven and not withdrawn, and the boy, who was not Vail and had never been entered as one, went on being carried under the roof without a name.

A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} stood at the edge of the shed with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs and said the pot was singing before its time, and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. Jorra did not answer him. He did not say the note was full, or that he had heard it before it left the wheel, or that the man's ear and his own ear had heard the same thing and read it differently. He let the knowing stay where it was, advanced and not paid, and the boy filed it beside the three near-full answers on their own tray --- a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again --- and not among the hearings. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person; no seventh had come and he had not made one to fill the empty place. A knowing withheld is not a hearing given, and a man who keeps an answer by him is not a man who has paid it. He set the withheld knowing on its place and did not let it stand up beside the six. A clerk would have called the knowing an entry and the entry a debt; here it was a thing in a potter's head that no slate would take, and it went on gathering nothing.

\scenebreak

Sella had not finished telling Jorra the fire was low. “The house keeps it low overnight. Feed it before ---” The bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the sentence, going in ahead of his hand the way it always went, and the iron took the cold out of the fingertip and kept it, giving a little back as the finger warmed. He moved the tongue of iron a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: Sella's hand finished its half-circle with the broken pot, Tarn's broom found its line, the wheel went on turning, and the low voices in the room off the shed went lower. Nobody ordered the straightening and nobody observed it. Obedience that arrives ahead of the instruction is obedience and a correction at once, and both went past the basket without a word. The hand had gone to the iron before the sentence had got to the fire, before anybody's eyes had got to the basket: the same order he had watched in a yard shoulder turning ahead of a yard gaze, in a cutter feeling the weight settle before he looked at the girl who had asked, in a clerk's hand going to a slate before his eyes went to the stall. He set it on its place beside the other reachings and did not make a row of them and call it a rule.

From the low angle the iron was the only thing in the shed worth reading twice. It was one piece of iron with two bites. One bite closed across a pot and held the whole vessel in the fire; the other, set the wrong way or set too far, could take the hand off a man and leave the stump to learn what the fire had been doing all along. He had known that iron in a life where it was called by another name and set across a forearm to stop the Warm going up it, and there too it had been mercy and mutilation in the same bite, a clamp and a tourniquet with no seam between them. There was no word in Grathic for the two mercies at once; the language had a word for holding and a word for taking and kept them apart the way a shelf keeps two gaps apart. He filed the iron as one object joined to no kiln, worth nothing and complete, with the two bites on one tray and no third bite invented to make the pair into a rule. Beside it the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed together with no third: breath through the nose and breath in the throat, both saying it was not the war's hand, each with its date and each kept on a place of its own, and the room whose door stood a finger's width open giving up nothing to join them. A clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine. He had learned that in a room with no use for the sentence, and carried it here without moving his lips.

\scenebreak

Out in the yard Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody, standing with the bristles across her two hands and the grey of the ground showing between them. Go around, she told the bristles; it cost enough to come in and it will cost more to take out. Then, and only then, she let \person{Tarn} take the broom out of her hands, and he took it up as a man takes up a tool already named, saying “Yes, house” to \lore{the thaw} early and to it late in the same breath, agreeing so entirely that an argument had nowhere to put its foot, and going on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. He said \emph{I owe} where another man would have said \emph{I am}, and owned nothing he could not keep in a clay pot with no mark on it.

His seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood, one tend from its end and still held. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and never cut, six where the house could check and seven where it could not, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife where he kept the seventh year, and neither was entered. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the breath from Seed 16 was still unspent, a small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in; he drew the breath and did not spend it, the way a man holds the door of a room he has decided not to enter. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, and he let the light come off it without looking at it, as a man lets a thing be seen without owning the seeing.

His stroke went out over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check. The stones kept the place where the stroke had once stopped, half a beat behind a standing finger, and went on keeping it while the broom went over and past, the way a floor keeps the mark of a spilled thing after the thing is wiped. He did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. He had been sent down it once in \lore{the Long Cold}, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, neither entered. The road lay where it lay and no collector came down it, and he did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name. The ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held it, and the sentence that would call the loan had never once been said; it lay under the roof all the same, unsafe and unsaid, and the man who owned the ear went on sweeping and the man who owned the debt went on with his wheel.

Neither man asked about the ear and neither man asked about the room. Jorra's bent finger stood in the cold damper and Tarn's knife lay at the edge of its sill and the two silences went on under one roof without a word passing between them, as they had passed for a hundred and forty-five days. The dust went into the iron and the debt went over the stones, and nothing in the shed joined the two. The kiln-master went off down the yard with the pot's note still unread by him, and the men in the room kept their low voices and their finger's width of open, and the reed month went on going out without a boundary drawn at its going, the third Account-day standing six days off. One hundred and forty-five lay where the day had left it, exact and complete and entered nowhere. The bent finger stood in the cold iron; the knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house could check and the seventh where it could not; the broom went on over the flagstones without the check, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end and still held.
