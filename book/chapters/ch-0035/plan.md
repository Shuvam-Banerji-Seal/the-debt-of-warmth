# Chapter 35 — beat sheet: *Eight at the Teeth*

**Days covered.** Days 144–146, Reed 25–27, Y.H. 813, Fifthday to Seventhday. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived in full, at least eight hundred words to the day. Day 143 is finished and not revisited; Day 147, Reed 28, belongs to Chapter 36. The boy is one hundred and forty-four, then forty-five, then forty-six days old — four months and twenty-four, twenty-five and twenty-six days — and nobody under the roof says so. The reed month goes out; at the chapter's close the third Account-day (Reed 30, Day 149) is six days off and the last market of Reed (Reed 30) five days off.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof on Day 145 Tarn brings the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt that holds it **a eighth time** — after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20 and 23 — all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there, with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray beside the seven as an **eighth interval and not an eighth payment**, an object among objects and not a rule, not a hearing and not an eighth hearing, refusing to let eight likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine — for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer — with no payer named to any of the eight. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given. A second gathering, not a resolution: the district's private records — the charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate — stand side by side and not across, gathering toward a far-off hand that will lay them together and call the joining an account, with no payer named to any.

**What is withheld everywhere.** No calling of the loan, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no cutting or counting of the seventh year, no collector, no payer named to any stopping, no eighth hearing of the full note, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no sixth green, no knot counted, no word forty spoken, no name or number from Dessa, no sale, no opening of tin or box, no body, cause or figure for the missing hand, no reading of either private tally, no joining of the two empty places, no third gap, no fourth likeness made into a cause, no silt-leach, cause or cure, no Fenna named in narration. Berr Clay and the green-mouthed woman are held for the last market of Reed and are offstage here. T097 stays advanced and unpaid; T098, T099 and T100 stay carried and unpaid. The old life stays offstage: no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from the boy's eyes, no Court, collector, Waiting Room, back-room interior, Ulda on stage, Marra in Sill, watcher on stage or ending. **Eight stoppings must not stand up and call themselves a doctrine.**

## Scene 1 — *The Last Week's Going Out*

- **Day and time.** Day 144, Reed 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813, morning going into the going of the light.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard and the reed beds below Low Kiln; the edge of the worked strips east of the yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; reed-cutters going downriver and coming back with the last bundles; a cutter who judges an armful by the arm that carried it; a girl with a creel; a man who asks what the hands will be paid; yard voices. Walk-ons only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Open on the morning of Day 144 with the drop capital. The reed month goes out without a boundary having been drawn at its entering; the last market is five days off and the third Account-day six. The boy is one hundred and forty-four days old and four months and twenty-four days and nobody under any roof says so. Sella carries him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket — *Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind* (T058/T079).
  2. The last bundles go downriver and come back in armfuls judged by the arm that carried them and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter tells the girl with a creel to lift her bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it, *and that's the whole of what it knows*; the arm knows weight and not cost, and a man who asks what the hands will be paid is answered with the weather and the river, never a figure (T071/T072, T077).
  3. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look, a wide step and no number. Nobody gives a name, cause, body, figure or payment; the yard treats the place as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried, not paid).
  4. The reed-cutter's grey cord goes past along the boards unread, straightened and turned once where no slate lies. It is not laid beside the charcoal under the shelf, the empty place in the line, Mira's two cords or Orra's knot-string (T099 carried, not paid).
  5. The boy reads the carrying arm once more as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost: exact, complete and worth nothing. The surgeon's register notes the order of reaching — the hand to the record before the hand to the person — without giving him standing to enter it anywhere; a cost asked in the form of a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry (T061/T082, T077).
  6. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down against the fold; he cannot reach the bundle, the pot or any of the places gone around. One hundred and forty-four goes on without anybody's thumb upon it (T071/T072).
- **Emotional turn.** From the ordinary last work of the reed month to the exactness of a record that cannot be spent: competence here is helplessness, and the day's arithmetic stays in arms and weather where no Court thumb will read it.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet wool and woodsmoke going before Sella; grey reed bundles pale where the frost bit the tips; dry mud worked into wrists; river wind coming up off the sill; the small wet sound of the wandering head; a trough by the well with its skin of ice thin as a skim of fat.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (144 kept exact and unspent), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T099 (carried), T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T098 gathers another going-around with no name, body, cause or figure; T099 goes past unread another day; T077's cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The last bundles stand grey in the ruts with nobody counting them; the cutter goes back downriver with the bundle counted only in his arm; the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down with one hundred and forty-four exact and unspent.

## Scene 2 — *The Finger Beneath the Board*

- **Day and time.** Day 144 continued, afternoon into the going of the light.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail; the boy in the basket. No named walk-on; the neighbouring questions of earlier days are not repeated here.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket low near the bench leg at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof, gives the instruction to the wool, the pot, the broom and the fire and never to the basket, and tells the fire it was the thaw (T058/T079).
  2. Above, the shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word — in a room the fold does not touch. The saying holds that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account wants a name, and no finger in the district has laid these together (T060/T086).
  3. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze; her little-door burn closes on the wood's lip as she reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies, without looking and without a word, holds it out and back, brings it away and does not go back to the shelf (T100 carried, unread).
  4. From the low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, press a thumb where her thumb had been, lay the underside across the two gaps above or across the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or make a cause of the habit — such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two (T100, T060/T086).
  5. She tells the fire the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale in the dying light, sets the pot rim out and handle toward the reaching hand — the handle standing two inches beyond the hand that would have reached for it and would not close on it — and does not look toward the bed. The fire gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty-four days (T088, T058/T079).
  6. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she does not touch it; a shelf and an afternoon are not openings (T079).
- **Emotional turn.** A private record is handled in front of him and stays unread, and the keeping of it tells more by its hiding than the writing would; the boy refuses to be the hand that joins either the charcoal to the gaps above or the two rooms to each other.
- **Sensory anchors.** The dry warm smell of the shelf crossed for one breath by the sharper smell of charcoal from under the board; the cold iron of the bench bracket at his cheek; the grey streak showing pale off the coals; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the clay jar cold under its glaze; one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (carried, unread), T060/T086, T058/T079, T077, T079, T088.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T100 gathers toward the far-off day the district's private records are laid together and called an account; T088's payer goes unnamed one day further. Nothing read, counted, copied, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The shelf keeps its two gaps above and its charcoal marks below, the finger has come away without a word, and the two rooms of the house do not touch.

## Scene 3 — *Six Where the House Can Check*

- **Day and time.** Day 145, Reed 26, Sixthday, Y.H. 813, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard and the flagstones.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; Jorra Vail; Tarn; three or four unnamed men who speak low in the back room; a High Kiln kiln-master. Walk-ons except Jorra, Tarn and Sella.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey three or four unnamed men with collars high come low-voiced into the room off the shed. Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, turns no head toward the door, gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031).
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under the lowest rack are entered by nobody (T035/T031, T009/T013/T092).
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who said it was singing before its time (T062). The boy files the withheld knowing beside the three near-full answers, not among the hearings (T061/T082).
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence. The boy reads the iron again as one bite that holds a vessel whole and one bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut counting room stay together beside it with no third (T062, T065/T095).
  5. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed. His seventh year stands nearer its end than ever, one tend from its end and still held, the small knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the unforged collector sentence beside it, and the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach (T036/T076).
  6. His stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and he does not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector; the stones keep the place where the stroke once stopped. Neither man asks about the ear or the room and the two silences stay unjoined (T036/T076).
- **Emotional turn.** The household's arithmetic is performed in dust, iron and a knife kept at the edge of a sill; the equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to, and the ear worth more than the debt is still unsaid and still unsafe.
- **Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal worked into the creases past the bent finger; the cold iron of the damper under the finger; clay dust and tallow in the wheel-shed; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckle creases in the colour of the clean dark line; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there is.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031, T062, T065/T095, T036/T076, T009/T013/T092, T058/T079, T061/T082, T071/T072 (145 kept exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; the damper's double bite, the two shut-room tellings and the withheld full note gather interest on their own trays; the eighth stopping is owed at the going of the light and has not yet come. Nothing named, entered, joined or paid.
- **Exact last image.** The bent finger stands in the cold damper, the knife lies at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check, and the broom goes on over the flagstones without the check.

## Scene 4 — *Side by Side and Not Across*

- **Day and time.** Day 145 continued, midday into afternoon.
- **Place.** Downriver at the Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone and the lower boards; then below the sill at the fish-trade shade and the out-of-line landing under the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the reed-cutter at the Weir; Orra Weir; Mira Silt; a weir woman who cannot read a cord; a woman who asks whether the run will be thin; a buyer who names a figure; the woman who wraps the child's foot and a neighbour with a pail; the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (seen, silent). Walk-ons except Sella, Orra and Mira; the reed-cutter and the clerk are given no names and no sheets.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle and the short length of grey wool or reed-fibre cord gone grey with the oil of a working thumb. He sets the bundle on the flat blessing stone, straightens the cord, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat with the flat of his hand; to the woman who asks what it holds he says *You can ask the cord* and *No more can the Court*. No figure, knot, name or joining (T099 carried, not paid).
  2. Orra stands at the lower boards with her board on its side and her hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered and salt white at her wrists, the nasal half-song of the Fen in her voice where she is tired. She pulls one knot down with her thumb and sets it among the others with a small flat knock, and to the woman who asks what the levy will take she gives a date — *The ninth of Ilmen. The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them* — and to *That isn't the levy*, *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* The forty-first double turn, tail tucked, left over right with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in, stays legible with its name unspoken (T024).
  3. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer, *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river*, flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; the second cord twisted twice beneath it is never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child's knot untouched, her board beside Orra's and not touching (T059/T025/T024).
  4. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard and a neighbour with a pail. The surgeon's reading of the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform works perfectly and buys nothing; no silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it (T093/T094/T096 carried).
  5. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm with the writing inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; nobody steps around him and nobody has taken him off. He is not Marra, not the grey-fingered Drawing collector, not the foundling (T090/T080 carried).
  6. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket. Three private records stand within a dozen paces of the basket and none can be entered, side by side and not across; the boy keeps each on a place of its own (T058/T079, T099, T024/T059/T025).
- **Emotional turn.** The district's arithmetic is everywhere and enterable nowhere; three people keep three accounts in materials no Court thumb will spell, and the boy's exact reading of a cold foot is the one thing in reach that is worth nothing.
- **Sensory anchors.** Pale slime dried grey on Mira's sleeves and silt pale in her neck's hollow; salt white at Orra's wrists and her hands like old rope; the short grey cord oiled by a working thumb; river water going over the sill; the three turns of wool around a dark foot; the soft cuff grey from washing.
- **Threads advanced.** T099, T024/T059/T025, T093/T094/T096, T090/T080, T058/T079, T071/T072 (145 exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; the cord, the knot-string and the two twists gather toward the far-off hand that will lay them together; the cold foot gathers uncured toward the silt-leach four arcs away. Nothing counted, named, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** Three cords lie on three boards with no knot counted; the word forty stays unspoken and the child's foot stays dark in three turns of wool.

## Scene 6 — *Six Pages Toward a Floorboard*

- **Day and time.** Day 146, Reed 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813, midday; afterward along the Weir boards in the afternoon.

  2. - **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** No new thread; T089's two private records gather toward the far-off hand that will read them, and T007's sign and date toward the day the branch is spoken; T023 is not paid again. Nothing opened, read, named or paid.
- **Exact last image.** Six pages lie turned toward a floorboard with both tins shut, and the reed-cutter's cord goes back inside his coat with its knots unread.

## Scene 7 — *One Hundred and Forty-Six*

- **Day and time.** Day 146 continued, the going of the light into the small hours.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle.
- **Present.** Sella Vail; the boy on his bed. He is alone at the final line; Sella has gone to her own bed. No named walk-ons.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around a pan set out to dry and the trough by the well with its skin of ice, goes wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it goes away (T058/T079).

  6. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and forty-six days old and four months and twenty-six days, told by nobody, and if somebody under that roof said the number nobody would know what it counted. He wants the crust two inches from his ear and the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither and cannot ask for either. ## How the final moment pulls into the next chapter

The chapter closes in the small hours of Day 146 with the count exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, the eighth stopping held on the interval tray as an object and not a payment, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end; every private record — the charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate — is still kept by a hand that cannot read it, and the two empty places and the missing hand's place stay separate with no third gap and no cause made of a likeness. Day 147, Reed 28, Eighthday, belongs to Chapter 36, *The last market of Reed*: the reed month enters its last three days, the last market and third Account-day (Reed 30, Day 149) stand two and three days off, and **a sixth green at a silent woman's mouth and a sixth silent glimpse of the watcher at the market's edge are owed there** — filed as a colour and not a name and never Fenna in narration, the foundling unnamed and given no dialogue, no loaf passed, no slate seen and no sale. Forward, not closed.
