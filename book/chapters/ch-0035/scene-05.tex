The light went off the water before \person{Sella Vail} turned up the clay road. It went in broken plates, as it had lain on the river at midday, and then the plates ran together into one dull sheet, and then the sheet was gone, and the road came up under her feet with the last of the day lying in its ruts. She carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke behind her and the river wind coming round her shoulders to meet it, the two passing through one another as they had passed all day. Keep it off his ears, she told the wool; it's the river wind and it'll go where the water goes. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. One hundred and forty-five days old. Four months and twenty-five days. Neither number went up the road with them; there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would have held either long enough for a woman passing on the clay to set it down.

The arm that carried him was a record in muscle that told what it had carried and never what it cost. It told the half-pace, the level hold, the going wide around a pan set out to dry and the trough by the well with its skin of ice thin as a skim of fat, and the wider still going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay. He read it once and put it down. It was exact, complete and worth nothing, and the hand that reached for the record always went before the hand that reached for the person, an order he had learned in a room where he had been allowed to touch neither. His own hand outside the fold went up an inch toward the note of water over water that came up off the \place{Ilm} behind them and came down against the cloth. Nothing within two paces could be reached, not the pot, not the shelf, not the two inches of cold iron at the bench bracket that his cheek knew better than his hand did.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the light was a thing that had been put away. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned kept its place in the corner and the new finer grey beside it kept its place, and neither had been swept; the door off the shed kept its finger's width of open and gave up nothing. Sella set the basket low near the bench leg, at the angle at which the underside of a board becomes a roof to a man lying down, and gave the instruction to the wool, the pot, the broom and the fire and never to the basket. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk is not an opening and no opening had come in a hundred and forty-five days.

The household went on around the basket the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies. \person{Tarn} came in from the yard with the broom under his arm, and took it up only after it had been given its instruction, which was a formality between him and the house that neither of them ever skipped. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He set it back there with the small flat knock a thing makes when it is laid down on the wood that knows it, and the knock went into the room and stayed there. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check; the seventh stood where it could not, one tend from its end and still held. Beside the knife lay the unforged sentence about a collector, kept where he kept the seventh year and entered in neither. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, sour in the small wounds of the pits, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. When he had nothing to do with his hands he put them on the broom, which was a way of knowing where they were.

From the basket the knife was a thin bright line lying on a darker line of wood, and the boy knew it by the sound it had made and by the order of the hand that had set it down. Tarn was frightened; the boy read it in the quickness, in the way a man who owns nothing checks three times that he has put a thing back. When he was frightened he became helpful, doing three things at once and quietly putting the fourth right, and tonight the fourth thing was the knife, which had been put right twice already without anybody asking. The household straightened around the silence before the silence had fully arrived, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. Nobody said what \place[the back room]{the room off the shed} was cold from, and nobody said what a man might be worth under this roof; two things kept under one roof keep their equality without a word passing between them, which is nearer to an account than this house ever came in speech.

\emph{Six counted,} he told the kindling. \emph{The next I don't count.}

He put one stick on the stack and took up another, and the sentence came the way it had come on Reed 5 and Reed 8 and Reed 11 and Reed 14 and Reed 17 and Reed 20 and Reed 23, in the same order and with the same weight at the end of it, an eighth time. He spoke to the wood because the wood was what he spoke to when a thing had to be said and a person would have made of it a question. “An ear under this roof that can hear,” he said to the kindling, “is worth more than the debt that holds it. And if a man said whose ear and set a figure to it there would be a slate at the watch with a name on it and a number under the name, and a collector would come down the road and take the measure of this house by what it had kept, and---”

The word that would have called the loan did not come. No figure came with it and no name stood under it, no slate at the watch, no man at the door, only the loan that could hear going on being worth more than the debt that held it and not called. The stick lay where he put it. The wood took the sentence as wood takes everything, without answer and without keeping, and the room took it the way a room takes a man's breathing in the dark. His chest was tight. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the small interval showed where it should have gone in, the same interval that had shown in his chest every morning since Seed 16, showing tonight at the hearth where the fire could not see it and the boy could. It was the shape he had said it in for years, the statement of fact made in the tone that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to, and nobody laughed and nobody was meant to. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom.

If the word had come, there would have been a figure for the ear and a figure for the debt and a slate at the watch to hold the two side by side, and the house would have been measured by what it had kept through six years of counting and one year held back. The boy knew the shape such a slate would take although he could not read a word of it: a clean margin, a name, a number under the name, and a hand that had gone into the room off the shed before first grey and come out with slate dust ground small as meal on its forearms. He did not make that hand the hand that would write the figure. He kept the shape of the slate on one place and the dust on another and the stopped sentence on a third, and refused to let the three stand up together and call themselves a cause.

The stroke went out over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch the stones kept from the day the half-check had come and gone, and it went on without the half-check, as it had gone on every day since. He did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. He had been sent down it once, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and he kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, neither entered. The road lay where it lay and no collector came down it, and he did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with the dust of the day still worked into both forearms past the bent little finger, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in the voice that had no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and did not look toward the basket. He did not ask about the ear. Tarn did not ask about the room. The two silences stayed under the one roof without a finger laid between them, the way two tools laid on a bench stay two tools until a hand takes one up and puts it across the other. Sella, within hearing of all of it, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing, and went on counting whatever it was she counted toward no opening, her hands never still when they had nothing to hold.

In the basket the boy turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins: field, exposure, instrument, complication, cost, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, with no seventh anywhere and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. The older intervals lay where they had always lain, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from the market and the unchecking stroke come up beside the half-beat with no cause written across them. The told bell stood beside the heard bell. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side with no third.

And now an eighth stopping lay on the interval tray beside the seven. It was the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it, brought all the way this night to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent. Eight objects lay on the tray and he held them apart, because a man who lets eight likenesses stand up together will find they have stood up and called themselves a doctrine by morning, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer had been named to any of the eight. The stopping was an interval and not a payment, an object among objects and not a rule, a thing that had been stopped and not a thing that had been settled. It was not a hearing and not an eighth hearing; it went on a tray of its own and was not admitted to the six, which stayed six beside and never among. A clamp laid on a towel is a clamp and is not asked to mean more than the bite it makes, and eight clamps laid side by side do not turn into a doctrine because there are eight of them. Here there were no clamps and no field, only eight likenesses that wanted to be one and a small hand outside the wool that could not close two inches on the fold. He held them apart by hand because he had no other hand to hold them with, and the labour was invisible and bought nobody anything.

The cost of the keeping was that he could never spend any of it. A rule would have been something: a thing a man could hand to a clerk, or set on a shelf where another hand might take it up and use it. These eight he could neither hand over nor use, and the only work left to him was to keep a finger between them so they did not become one. He had no finger long enough to reach the crust two inches from his ear and no voice to say the number of his days to anybody who would know what a number was; he had this instead, the invisible labour of holding likenesses apart on a tray nobody could see. It was exact and complete and worth nothing, as the carrying arm was exact and complete and worth nothing, and he went on doing it because there was nothing else in the room that his hands could do.

The loan stayed uncalled. The breath from Seed 16 stayed unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach. The seventh year stood one tend from its end and had not given, and the unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife where the house could not check it. \lore{The carried fire} under blind ash and the back of the broken pot kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty-five days. In the next room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps and the charcoal under the board waited where no thumb of his would read it. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb; under the floor the fire kept four and a gap. Three counts in three places, and none became one. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down against the fold. Eight objects lay on the interval tray with no payer named to any, the word that would call the loan stayed unsaid under the roof that wanted it said, and the knife lay back on its sill where the house could check six.
