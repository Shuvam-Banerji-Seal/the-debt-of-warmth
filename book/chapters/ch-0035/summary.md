## Chronicle
The reed month went out without a boundary drawn. On Day 144 Sella Vail carried the boy at her half-pace from every open slate, instruction to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. Bundles were judged by the carrying arm; a cutter told a girl it knew what went into a bundle, and a man who asked what the hands would be paid was answered with the weather and the river. The yard went around the dark line, the thaw and the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. One hundred and forty-four stood exact and unspent.

Under the Vail roof she set the basket low, told the fire it was the thaw, and reached a finger beneath the loaf shelf where the charcoal tally lay, without looking. He saw the hand go under and come back and read nothing; such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look. The shelf kept two gaps and the fire four pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been.

On Day 145 men went low into the finger's-width-open back room, and Jorra Vail came through with grey slate dust on both forearms, spoke to the wheel first, withheld a pot's full note from a High Kiln kiln-master and put his finger into the cold damper --- one iron with two bites, one holding a vessel whole, one taking a hand off a man. Tarn took the broom only when instructed; his seventh year stood one tend from its end. Downriver Orra Weir gave a date where a figure was asked, and a child's foot below the sill stayed dark in three turns of wool.

That night Tarn told the kindling Six counted. The next I don't count, and brought the ear-sentence an eighth time to the word that would call the loan, after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20 and 23, and stopped with no figure and no name, drew his Seed 16 breath and did not spend it. The boy filed the eighth stopping beside the seven as an interval and not a payment, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer had been named to any.

On Day 146 Alda Reed laid her birth-slates before turning to the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower and covered the sign with Dessa's date: Stone seven. The account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out. Her Warm and breathing was exact and not true over a dead woman; six pages lay turned toward a floorboard and both tins stayed shut. In the small hours Sella set him on his bed, left the thread frozen and went to her own bed. His file held six hearings and no seventh and eight stoppings. One hundred and forty-six stood exact and unspent as his hand went up an inch toward the water and came down.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** The chapter stops in the small hours of Day 146, Reed 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813, under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. The boy lies alone on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, fold at his ear and under his chin, the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, his small hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door and coming down against the fold. \person{Sella Vail} has gone to her own bed in the other room; the two rooms do not touch. The last image is one hundred and forty-six exact and unspent in the dark, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end, the charcoal waiting under the shelf for a hand that is not his and a day that has not come. Mid-action? No: a held, open moment, not closed.
- **Day and time at end.** Day 146, Reed 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813, the small hours; one hundred and forty-six days old, four months and twenty-six days, told by nobody. Days 144 (Reed 25, Fifthday), 145 (Reed 26, Sixthday) and 146 were each lived in full under their own `\daymark`, with no day skipped or poured into another. Day 147, Reed 28, Eighthday, belongs to Chapter 36.
- **Where every on-stage character is now.** \person[Corin]{The boy}: on his bed, awake, silent, alone, wanting the crust two inches from his ear and the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what a number was, and getting neither. Sella Vail: in her own bed under the same roof, having carried him up at the half-pace, gone around the pan, the iced trough and the space under the rack, set him down and not looked at his face, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed, told the fire the thaw, said *The house will keep it*, counted under her breath toward no opening, pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, left the widow's mark untouched and gone to bed without explaining the count. \person{Tarn}: last on stage on Day 145 night at the hearth and kindling with the broom, the knife set back where the house can check six, the eighth stopping brought to the threshold and withheld; not on stage on Day 146, asleep under the roof by the small hours; wants the loan uncalled, the seventh year held, the ear unsaid and no collector on the road. \person{Jorra Vail}: last on stage on Day 145 night coming through from the shed with the day's dust on both forearms, telling a pot *You'll do* and not looking toward the basket; wants the wheel in the house, the boy outside the name, the hollow seat, and the promise of the foundling's name still ungiven. \person{Alda Reed}: last seen at midday on Day 146 three doors down in \place{House Silt}, birth-slates on the bench, six pages turned toward a floorboard, the little tin shut in her bodice, the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed shut; wants the child warm and the account in order and cannot correct the mercy-lie. \person{Mira Silt} and \person{Orra Weir}: last seen on Day 146 afternoon at the Weir, Mira's thumb under her apron to the grey cord with the second twist beneath it, Orra's forty-first double turn legible and unnamed on its board; both want their counts kept where no clerk can spell them. The unnamed reed-cutter went up the boards with his cord inside his coat unread. The soft-cuffed clerk was still set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. \person{Berr Clay}, the green-mouthed woman, the foundling/Ulda, the man from Slate Row, \person[Marra]{Collector Marra} and \person[Rusk of the Weir] were offstage; \person{Dessa Silt} is dead and unnamed to the boy; \person{Garl Vail} is absent and the shape of the house.
- **Items gained, lost, used; money; powers.** Nothing gained, nothing spent, no money at any point. The one brown-edged crust in the fold remains stiff and uneaten two inches from his ear, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The loaf shelf keeps two unnamed gaps; the charcoal under its board was handled by Sella's finger on Day 144 (and is only waited for in the night inventories of 145 and 146), still unread and uncounted. The reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's two cords, Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin were seen/carried and none opened, read, copied or joined. No powers used, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy; counting remains an old-life habit, not a gift, and the surgeon's register only colours the noticing.
- **Facts revealed, lies told, promises, debts.** The chapter's one meaningful change is that Tarn brought the ear-sentence an **eighth** time (Reed 26, Day 145) to the word that would call the loan and stopped. Alda's lie *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down* was spoken again, exact and not true, and has curdled into a fact standing over a dead woman no living person can correct. The date *Stone seven. The account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out* was laid over the birth-flower again. Jorra withheld the full note of the pot from the High Kiln kiln-master. No promise made or withdrawn: *he's not Vail* stays sealed, the foundling's name promise ungiven. No debt paid: the loan uncalled, the seventh year one tend from its end and still held, the Seed 16 breath unspent.

- **Foreshadowing planted / gathered, nothing paid.** The eight stoppings gather toward the day something must give at the end of the seventh year; the district's private records --- charcoal, reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, the clerk's inward slate --- gather toward a far-off hand that will lay them together and call the joining an account, with no payer named to any. T098, T099, T100, T097 and T023/T089 remain carried or advanced and unpaid. T050/T051 stays a fifth green that is a colour joined to no ear. No new thread opened.
- **What Chapter 36 must not forget.** Opens on Day 147, Reed 28, Eighthday, with the boy one hundred and forty-seven days old and nobody saying so, the reed month going into its last three days, the last market and third Account-day (Reed 30, Day 149) ahead. A sixth green at a silent woman's mouth and a sixth silent glimpse of the watcher at the market's edge are owed there --- filed as a colour and a shape, never Fenna in narration, the foundling unnamed and given no dialogue. No loaf passed, no slate seen near the basket, no sale, no collector, no calling of the loan, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no cutting or counting of the seventh year, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting beyond the one owed, no opening of tin or box, no name or number from Dessa, no silt-leach/cause/cure, no third gap, no fourth likeness made into a cause. Keep the eight stoppings as objects and not a doctrine; keep Marra out of Sill and Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy. The old life stays offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break, no departure from the boy's eyes.
