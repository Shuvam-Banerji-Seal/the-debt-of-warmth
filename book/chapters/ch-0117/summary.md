## Chronicle

Three days came under the Vail roof — Ilmen 8, 9 and 10 — and each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: crust two inches off with its corner hair-lifted, the fold found where put and still a finding, the thread stiff in the dry heat and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and wrist, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true; the second louder; neither won.

Sella went to the hearth before the basket each day, told the coals it was the thaw, said *The house will keep it*, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, gave both proverbs to pot and fire, turned the dressed grain under wool, set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, and kept the charcoal unread and the widow's mark untouched. Tarn kept six where the house could check, the seventh at the lip, told the wood, *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and passed his hand over blade and sill and away.

Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, spoke to the wheel before any person, withheld the full note, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence. On the ninth a wrong wind off High Kiln carried the sweet edge of glaze over the boards and out again, no hand or name under it, and the boy filed it as weather.

On the tenth, the year's first Account-day, Sella set the basket in the moot-post's shade and stood half a pace off. Berr Clay came off the clay road, red cord folded, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, passed a finger over the figure through his sleeve and read low, *One in twelve, as the notice says.* He sent it twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never I, said low to the block that kindness was entered, and went off with the box locked, believing he had done a kindness. The district's arithmetic began again and could not be undone; nobody addressed the boy it would carry.

He took the reading apart as a surgeon takes a field: first of the year, not a thirteenth, one reading not an account, no payer in the reset, the figure read not the figure paid, twelve old findings not laid across it, for paper is not a payer. Hessa went up the road before noon carrying what she would not read; the bell went four, four and a gap. Six answers were filed, 379 through 384; the count stood at four hundred and four exact, told by nobody, the fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken. In the small hours the hand came down flat on the fold, and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down and the fire kept.
