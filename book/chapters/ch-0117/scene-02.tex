The afternoon came on by small gains nobody entered, the way a room fills from a window nobody has opened: not in a stride, but by the widening of one thin edge along the floor. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the warmth held the way a pot holds water, with care, and with one crack in it that nobody mentioned. The crack was not in the pot. It was in the room, in the house, in the order of things that had been found as found and gone on being found; it held what was put into it the way a twice-mended vessel holds water, showing where the hand had been rather than pretending no hand had ever touched it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen, and the dry kiln heat had gone into the wool with woodsmoke and tallow and, under that, the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} lifting out of \place{the Vail corner} where no broom had gone in two years. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply what was wanting below it. The room kept its arithmetic badly, in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody called a number; the boy kept it exactly, and neither keeping was entered, and the afternoon went about its ordinary work without being asked to mean anything.

In the afternoon \person{Sella Vail} carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and the yard took him in at the basket's low angle and gave him its small sounds first. He took the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on stone and the stone answered with the flat word it had given every day of his life under this roof. A latch lifted. Somewhere along the wall a loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. Two voices passed the price of barley from one end of the wall to the other, and the price arrived at the other end still a price and never a number, and the space at the end of the loaf row stayed a space. A neighbour with a rake raised it at the ridge at the Vail corner and set it aside again with nothing said, and the ridge lay where it lay. He built no account out of these. A voice in a yard is a voice in a yard, and a pause between voices is not a person. He had once stood at a bay door and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and the ear that had heard the bad air going out of a chest could still separate a pail on stone from a rake on a ridge, and the separation was of the same order and as useless here, for there was no hand two paces long to put any of it to work.

Sella went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. He gave it no name, no cause and no figure, for a figure would want a payer standing and none stood. The clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey hairline beside it, called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; and out on \place{the worked strips} \place{the field's empty place} lay where it had always lain, and a neighbour's called-gone grey lay on a neighbour's step, two states on two places with a finger between. The yard would one day burn its grey and call it gone; the ridge would stay where it lay; neither saying touched the other, and he made no fourth likeness a cause. Not one of the seven stood up and called itself a rule, and the eighth was not made due on an afternoon.

The light went at the going of the light, which is to say without consultation, thinning off the wall and leaving the dust standing in the warm places. Sella came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, as she had come at the going of every dark since the year turned and before that, and said to the coals what she said. “It's the thaw,” she said, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the small red eye under the ash showed and shut and showed again. “The house will keep it,” she said to the pot, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with her hands gone still, taking a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, with neither gap figured, the way a woman stands over a wound she has dressed and will not look at twice. Her finger lay along the top edge of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved on the two gaps in the loaf row and gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The sweet edge of it had not come on the wind this afternoon. The jar stood where jars stand, and she gave it no more than the glance.

On the pot board the twice-mended pot waited with the hairline of the original break showing dark between the older brass staple and the tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, putting her thumb there before the weight came on the handle, and left the hairline showing. “Leave the crack showing,” she said to the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” To the fire she said, “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and repair stayed apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing for anybody who cares to look. She turned the dressed grain under wool at \place{the rack end} with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and no figure went over it and no handful was lifted, and the arithmetic that would one day be read over that covered grain would not know what it cost and the space between the figure and the price would have no signature. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the hand's intention and the hand's arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at that half-pace from every open slate, and set him under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold. \lore{The three coverings} stayed weather entered nowhere, and the river's skin lay where the cold had put it, found where put and not spent on an afternoon. At her throat \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay oiled by ten thousand working thumbs, and an afternoon and a going of the light were not openings; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due under a roof that had done nothing but keep a fire. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped away and a hand on the pot board's handle, and got none of the three, the wanting and the having-not going on being the work.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella had told \place{the flagstones} they wanted sweeping, which is a thing a woman says to stone when she has a man within earshot and does not want to give him an order he could carry to a slate; he said “Yes, house,” to the question and to the thing behind it, agreeing so thoroughly that the question had nowhere left to put a foot, and swept the same shallow bright patch he had swept in the morning because it was the patch the house could check. Then he went back to the wood. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. The coming away was the whole of the checking. He told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” and cut nothing, counted nothing toward the house's keeping and spent nothing. He did not turn his head toward \place{the out-of-line landing} where the soft-cuffed clerk was set below \place{the north bridge} with his slate turned inward for a measure nobody called; the degree his head had once gone toward it and back to the broom stayed a shape found where put and not made a second time, a habit, a way or a rule. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to the word that would call the loan and stopped there as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, the \emph{Seed 16} breath drawn and left unspent at the top of the reach and the loan uncalled at the very end of a term already ended. The fear that he would be entered again for vagrancy stayed his without a figure, and so did the want he did not voice, a slate with his own name on it and no mark against it. The warm loft stone in \place{the Debt Loft} stayed where it lay above him, unreconciled by his not trying, which is not forgiving.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} kept the edge of the shed and the household straightened around his silence without a word from anybody. He had come through \place{the finger's-width door} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel; he kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time; the knowing went on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them, and the shed kept its silence. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name made to men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, going on gathering interest against \lore{the burned-slate word} at five and the four old directions with a finger between every pair. One piece of iron had had its two bites at the cold \lore{damper} earlier in the day, the holding and the taking, and no \place{Grathic} word covered both mercies; he had not left the extra holding of \emph{Ash 26} a second time, and nothing under the shed was made a lesson of it.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. It was rough from the wash and smelled of tallow and the low smoke that gets into cloth when a house has no chimney worth the name, and it lay under the resting hand with the weight of a thing put down for the next hand to find. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. The crust's gap stood two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool. The shelf kept its two gaps, and the gone pan's round ring lay on the board where a pan had stood and a pan no longer stood, a round place and not a hole. \place{the field's empty place} out on the worked strips lay where it lay. \lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put in the empty place and was still a finding; however many hands found it, it remained a keeping found and not made, for a second placing would make a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer standing where none stood. The told bell's gap lay in its square beside the bell he had heard from \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap, its gap not folded into the fire's four and a gap under the floor, into the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the twelve behind one figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. On \lore{the gap tray} the silence after the name spoken over \place{the Remembering} sat beside a clean voice's missing name and the empty name the neighbours had left in their story, three emptied names with a finger between every pair, none filled, none made a witness or a reader. The neighbour's called-gone grey lay beside the left-in-place ridge at the Vail corner, two states on two places with a finger between, and neither was laid across the other to make a row come out even. Tarn's ear-sentence lay where it had stopped at the calling word in a term already ended; Jorra's promise lay neither given nor withdrawn; the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate lay at the out-of-line landing where it had been reported and not witnessed; Tarn's degree toward it lay a shape found where put and not repeated; and the face of the man from \place{Slate Row} at the yard's edge lay on a place of its own beside the older record-first hands, a shape from a low angle with no name and no interior, no loaf passed, no slate shown, no word that carried, filed and not counted among the nine. Every one lay where it lay, advanced and not paid, and none stood up and called itself a rule.

Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings stood with no tenth; six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s small flat knock outside on its own place, and neither the flat knock nor a story's gap nor a told bell was made a seventh hearing. Counting was an old-life habit and not a gift: it gave him no key, no slate with his own name, no hand to lift a crust two inches, only the exactness of the field and the cost of it, which was nothing. A man may count his own breaths and learn nothing from the count but the counting, and go on counting because it is the only instrument left him. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth was unpaid four hundred and two days and its payer unnamed four hundred and two days, and Garl's name was unspoken the same number, the two counts standing side by side without becoming one. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, the sound coming from under the covering and not from the covering, and he gave it no source, for a source is an account that has found a payer and none stood beyond the door. Twelve arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure and the year's first arithmetic waited one morning beyond the close, a reset and not a signing, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. He did not lay his twelve old findings across a figure not yet read to make a row come out even; a row made even by a hand that has no key is made even on paper, and paper is not a payer.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note under the winter skin. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the river's note's own finishing under the covering, let it go as the note ended --- neither early nor stayed --- and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer three hundred and eighty came out and the hand came down. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the child's grey fingertips, the mother's cup, the covered grain, \lore{the handful}, \lore{the thumb's resting}, \lore{the held breath}, \lore{the two bites of iron}, the river's skin, the widow's mark, \lore{the fourth direction}, the clerk's inward slate, the face at the yard's edge nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, three hundred and seventy-five through three hundred and eighty, and not one of them was a doctrine. The count came to four hundred and two exact, six months and two hundred and twenty-two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate lay open within reach of the basket, no figure was given for the boy, and no door was drawn across the tend. The crust's corner stood a hair off the wool and the thread was frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept --- forward, with the next morning still unbrought.
