\daymark{219}{Grain 10, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay down against the fold and stayed down where it had been put, the way a chisel lies where a man has set it on a bench and turned away from it. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \person[Corin]{the boy} did not go back into the dark to find a meaning in what had been given there the night before. A holding was a holding; it lay where it lay, and to pick it up and turn it over would only wear the edge. He had taken one rhythm through another and brought the two together on the river's next low note, and now the hand was down and the two rhythms were apart again and neither had been asked to answer for the other. Nothing had grown. Nothing had reached. There was no second hand under the first to make the keeping into a sign, and the making of a sign would have wanted a payer standing behind it, and no payer stood. He was two hundred and nineteen days old and six months and thirty-nine days, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so, and there was not a slate in the district with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number went on under the not-expecting of it the way a coal goes on under ash; the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing.

The day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season's name instead of a cause. The kiln drew in fours beyond the wall, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short; then the gap. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered, and this was neither; it was a fire keeping itself the way a man keeps his own breath without knowing that he counts it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by a decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and nineteen days with no payer named to it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold of grey wool, and the empty place beside it was still unnamed and no third had come to stand there and make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of a written order. It went no farther and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it, and he wanted the crust, and both were true and the child's wanting was the louder of the two, and neither was a lesson written upon the other.

The room of the fire and the room of the bed did not touch, and no breathing number in either had ever been offered to the other. There was no count anywhere in the house at this hour, nothing to hold the hand against but the river's own low interval drawing out and slackening behind the finger's-width door. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone, following the bone through the swelling until the joint gave and the man's arm came down quiet at his side; he could not now lift a crust two inches to his mouth. The two facts stood on separate trays and neither was a lesson about the other, and the surgeon's register took the measure of both and found them exact, complete and worth nothing.

\scenebreak

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline. He found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and nineteen days and none would be invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule --- a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, the pot that sang early last month not heard from since, and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. Beside them, on a place that did not touch them, stood the fourth telling of \lore{the burned-slate word}, come over the boards from \place{Slate Row} on the breath of barley price and weather, with a hand between it and \person{Jorra Vail}'s dust and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war. Four tellings of a burning were not four witnesses to one fire, and a word about a slate was not a word about a pot. A direction with a date was not a slate opened, and no slate had burned under any roof and no new fire had been lit at the kiln mouth, for a fire would have cost something and the telling cost nothing, and he kept the two apart.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself at either; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, the third reading gone up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside and \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate gone up unread with the writing to her sleeve, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; the Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161, when the hand waited at the iron for a sentence to finish; the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; the half-check made on \place{the flagstones} on Tallow 22. The small dry scrape from the loft stood on a fourth, the pale line at the glaze-room hand's gum on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with all its answers, each filed on a place of its own and none standing up to call itself a longer reach, a key, a power or a rule. He laid nothing across them and named no payer to any of them. The pan's gone round ring stayed on a tray of its own beside the loaf shelf's two gaps and the fold's one crust and the field's empty place, and he did not lay a finger on any of them. No third gap came into the yard, no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, and nothing was filled in to make a row come out even. Nothing in the file had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket. The instruction went out to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and told the wind to keep it off his ears because it was the river wind. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps and she gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat stood where it stood and asked nothing and was entered nowhere. At a morning and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in thirty-nine days, because a morning and a shelf and a basket are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and Dessa's cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story, which is the way a house keeps a man it cannot collect. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder; the seat kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to that side; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw lay unswept, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. He had not been collected, and the keeping of him was maintenance and nothing more, and nobody under this roof had asked what the debt was for. His name had not been spoken here for two hundred and nineteen days, and no lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called.

Asked in the morning, the hand went up an inch toward the note and came down at once against the fold --- a miss with no failure hidden under it, an answer refused set on a tray of its own, filed on the sixth place with no payer named and nothing standing up out of it as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power or a rule. He could not close the two inches to the pot handle she had set out, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand, and both were true, and neither was a sign.

\scenebreak

Word went up \place{the clay road} before first grey: stalls going toward \place{the Cross}, a neighbour ahead with boards on a barrow, \place{the reading block} being made ready under \place{the moot-post} for Grain's first Account-day. The market came first as boards on a road and only afterward as a reading. Over the boards went the price of barley and the weather, two sayings travelling together without either paying for the other, and the boy filed them as a direction with a date and not as a summons to himself. A neighbour at the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the stalls would want come the tenth and was told to ask the pot, the house had said it, and to ask the weather too, and went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. The boy took the question and its answer as a direction and a direction only; no number crossed the yard and no slate was opened under any roof. Two hundred people would lean to a block and straighten and go back to their pots before the morning was out, and a figure read aloud over the top of a basket would still not be the figure paid. The printed one in twelve stood unchallenged; Berr's box was locked up the road with the true subtraction inside; Hessa's slate had gone up unread with the writing to her sleeve; the soft-cuffed clerk at the out-of-line landing was still set for a measure nobody called. All of it stayed offstage in the boy's file and none of it reached back to the basket.

Sella carried him up at the half-pace, and she kept the half-pace from every open slate. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind her with the taste of water over water; the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and nineteen days. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark trough, the space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. The seventh green was owed as a colour at a silent woman's mouth and the seventh sighting of the foundling as a shape at the market's edge, and neither had come early, and no loaf, slate or sale had crossed the yard before the first market.

Her half-pace stopped short of the reading block. She set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay and stood a half pace off, and gave no instruction to the basket. The cold iron of the post waited at his cheek; the stalls were going up across the square and the block stood ready with nobody yet called to it, and the boy had not been carried to the block. The market was arriving at a body that had not been carried to it. He lay with his hand down against the fold and one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place still unnamed and no third coming, and the thread of drool frozen and unwiped at his mouth. Two hundred and nineteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody, and the count went forward into the first market of Grain with nothing settled and nothing ended.
