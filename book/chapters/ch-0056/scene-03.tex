\daymark{220}{Grain 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

The small hours gave the day to the child before they gave it to anybody else under the roof, as they had given him every day of his life, and he lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and let the dark go without asking it to stay. It came to him first as a smell and only afterward as a gap in sound: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Then the kiln drew in fours with a gap, not a bell, and the space where the fifth pull should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and twenty days. He was two hundred and twenty days old in \place{Low Kiln} and six months and forty days, and nobody who could have known what either number counted said a word about it, and there was no slate in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} with a place to enter either. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. At \place{the Cross} \place{the reading block} stood where it had been set, a plank on two trestles wet with the morning and with nobody leaning to it under \place{the moot-post}, and the boy kept it where it was --- a thing with a date and a going, filed as a direction and not entered as a day.

Grain's first arithmetic had gone into the air and could not be got back into the mouth that said it; that was the whole of what the morning had to say about the market, and the boy let it say it without answering. Under this roof no figure had been entered and no slate opened. The printed \emph{one in twelve} stood unchallenged where it had always stood, and the figure read over a basket was still not the figure paid; \person{Berr Clay}'s box had gone up \place{the clay road} with the true subtraction locked inside it, and \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate had gone up unread toward \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody had called. All three lay offstage in the boy's file and none opened or reached back, and he laid nothing across them and named no payer to any of them. A district may change its arithmetic and go on not knowing what it has changed; a house may keep its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces and never put a number to the difference. The changed figure lay outside the roof like weather, and weather is a thing a child in a basket can be carried under without once being asked to read it.

The seventh green and the seventh shape were a day old now and had gathered nothing in the gathering. The green was a colour at the mouth of a silent woman at \place{the pot board}, clay in the small crescents under the nails and the sweetish \lore{lead glaze} in the crease beside the lip; the shape was a shape at the market's edge, a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull, a head that turned toward a set-down pot a half-beat late and stopped in the middle of the turn. He had filed the one as a colour and the other as a shape on places that do not touch, with a finger between each and the hearings and no name spoken over either. No eighth of either had come to stand beside them, no second look had been joined to the first, and neither had been promoted into a hearing of \lore{the full note}, a seventh of that note, a tenth stopping, a cause, a name or a romance. Six greens behind a seventh green made a row of greens; six shapes behind a seventh shape made a row of shapes; a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it, and no payer stood under either row. The woman had no name in the narration and the shape had none, and the sale to a clerk of \place{the Waiting Room} stayed years ahead of the basket and was not brought early into a morning that had no market in it.

The file under the fold had not grown in the night. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth out of a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go, and no seventh had come to stand where the empty place had been kept empty; nine stoppings stood as objects with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, which had come and had still not given; \lore{the burned-slate word} stayed on its own place beside the wheel-keeper's dust and the two shut-room tellings with a hand between the three. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. He laid nothing across any of it and named no payer.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold of grey wool and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third had come to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of a written order; it went no further and it did not go away, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it and he wanted the crust, and both were true and the child's wanting was the louder of the two. The surgeon's register took the reading at two paces, found it exact, complete and worth nothing, and asked the room what it was for; the room gave back the slant of the heat and the sour little breath of the corner, and that was the whole answer, enough to live on and not enough to spend.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, the way she came to everything that wanted keeping: the back first, the face withheld, the hand going where it had gone ten thousand times before. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. “The house,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and told the fire it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and the fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and she did not look toward \place{the bed} to see whether the boy had heard the gap.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word. She gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room.

“The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. At a morning she did not touch the \lore{widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty days, because a shelf, a hearth and a morning are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. Garl's name was not spoken with the sentence, and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on Grain 11. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth as it had come on other mornings and went all the way to the word that would call the loan before it stopped, with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, and counted nine behind it with no tenth standing before the seventh year at its very end, which had come and had still not given. The breath from Seed 16 drew at the top of each reach and was not spent, a small interval where the cost should have gone in, and the loan went on being uncalled. The warm stone Sella gave him in the winter Garl died lay in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold long since, and he could no more reconcile it with the debt than he could the house's kindness with the account he kept of it; he had stopped trying, which is not forgiving. He did not ask about the room and nobody asked about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the shut counting room stood at two with no third, and the burned-slate word stood where it had stood, four times told and still a direction arrived nowhere from \place{Slate Row}, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, nothing a clerk could chart. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings about a pot with no lesson made and no pot lost, and a fourth likeness did not stand up among them and call itself a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer. He gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand, and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again; the boy read it as one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, an object worth nothing joined to no kiln.

\scenebreak

At the door a neighbour with a wet pot asked what the month's pots would want now the kilns were at their heat, and Sella glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and sent the question to the pot and the weather; the woman went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. At \place{the yard well} a rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked the same question in a different mouth and got the same answer, and \place{the trough} stood bare and dark beside her with its water called weather. A man come up off \place{the clay road} kept a number shut in his fist and would not let it out into the air; he brought it as far as the well and put it back into his sleeve, so that it arrived nowhere a second time, and Sella gave the weather and the river in place of a figure. The carrying arm that had brought the boy up and down this yard for two hundred and twenty days was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost.

She went around \place{the Clay Yard} the way she went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned kept its place, and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw had not been swept; a line left where it is is still a line kept. Under \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard entered them into nothing, and she went wide around the space, and around the round empty ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word, and around the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. No third gap came in the returning line and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause of any two absences, and no finger of his drew the line between them. The yard went around what it had lost the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the boy kept the wide steps as directions and gave them no figure.

\scenebreak

By late morning the ordinary had closed over the house the way water closes over a stone set where a stone lies, and the question came to the hand as it came each morning. Sella's breath went small intake, hold, letting go in the room of the fire, a count she had never offered to any ear under this roof, and there was nothing else under the roof to hold the hand against but that and the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door --- the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed for the small intake, and came down before the counted breath had finished, down with the hold still ahead of it. It was an honest miss filed on its sixth place and laid across nothing, neither a longer reach nor a hearing nor a seventh nor a stopping nor a payment nor one of the three held changes nor a key nor a power nor a rule, and no payer was named to it. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle Sella had set out, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true.

Two hundred and twenty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn or Sill or Grath; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty days; at the Cross the reading block stood a day behind with no crowd under it, and the boy lay two inches short of the pot handle with the thread frozen at his mouth and the crust keeping its empty place unnamed. The morning was ordinary again, and the inch was still a physical first worth nothing, and the count went forward with nothing settled and nothing ended.
