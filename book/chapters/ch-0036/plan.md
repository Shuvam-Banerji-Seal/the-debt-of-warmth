# Chapter 36 — *Nine at the Teeth* — beat sheet

**Arc:** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — “a warm stone, already going cold”.
**Days:** 147–149 (Reed 28–30, Y.H. 813; Eighthday, Ninthday, Tenthday). Reed 30 is the third Account-day of Reed and the last market of Reed. Day 150 is Smoke 1 and belongs to Chapter 37.
**Mode:** normal — three lived days, each opened by its own `\daymark` and lived in full, no day skipped or poured into another. Eight scenes; every day carries two or three scenes.
**POV:** Daniel Marchetti (Corin), close third-person past, one hundred and forty-seven to one hundred and forty-nine days old (four months and twenty-seven to twenty-nine days), told by nobody. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from his eyes. The surgeon's register colours the noticing; it never becomes a speech.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof on Day 148 (Reed 29) Tarn brings the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan a **ninth** time and stops there with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray as a ninth interval and not a ninth payment, nine objects and not a rule, and refuses to let nine likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer has been named to any of the nine. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given.

**A second gathering, not a resolution.** At the last market of Reed on Day 149 a **sixth** green comes to a silent woman's mouth at the pot board and a **sixth** silent glimpse of the foundling comes at the market's edge. Both are filed as a colour and a shape on places of their own, never joined, never Fenna in narration, the foundling unnamed and given no dialogue, no loaf passed, no slate seen and no sale. The sixth green and the sixth glimpse gather interest; they are not paid.

**Hard withholds for the whole chapter.** No calling of the loan, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no cutting, counting or giving of the seventh year, no collector on the road. No seventh hearing of the full note; the six hearings stay six. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand (Marra's pause); Marra is not in Sill. No name, number or recognition from Dessa; she stays dead and uncollected. No knot counted (reed-cutter, Orra, Mira), no charcoal stroke read or joined, no tin or box opened, no silt-leach/cause/cure named for the cold foot, no third crust, no third gap, no fourth likeness made into a cause, no joining of shelf gaps, fold gap and field's empty place, no body or cause for the missing hand, no payment, no payer, no sale, no ending. Alda stays offstage; Dessa stays dead; Ulda is glimpsed but is never named or given a word. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the foundling's name promise stays ungiven.

## Scene 1 — The Last Bundles Come Out

**Day and time.** Day 147, Reed 28, Eighthday, morning going into midday. First dated heading of the chapter and of Day 147; the drop capital belongs here.

**Place.** The Clay Yard below Low Kiln and the reed beds along the going-downriver road; the edge of the worked strips.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the boy in the basket; reed-cutters going downriver and coming back; the girl with a creel; the man come up from the pans who asks what the hands will be paid; the reed-cutter with the grey cord passing along the boards.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open forward from the previous chapter without recap: the reed month has three days left, the last market and third Account-day stand two and three days off, and the boy is one hundred and forty-seven days old and four months and twenty-seven days with nobody under any roof saying so. Put the number on the page as his private count, exact and unspent.
2. Sella takes him down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate; the instruction goes to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket; woodsmoke goes before her up the road and the river wind comes up to meet it, the two passing in the dark the way two people pass on a stair without speaking (T058/T079).
3. The last bundles come out of the beds in armfuls judged by the arm that carried them. A cutter tells the girl with a creel to lift her bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it, “and that's the whole of what it knows.” A man come up from the pans asks what the hands will be paid and is answered with the weather and the river, never a figure (T077, T071/T072).
4. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look and a wide step and no number (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried).
5. The reed-cutter's grey cord goes past along the boards, straightened and turned where no slate lies, one knot tied low with teeth and thumb, the end bitten off, the cord put back inside his coat with the flat of the hand. No figure, no name, no knot counted (T099 carried).
6. The boy reads the carrying arm once more as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost — exact, complete and worth nothing — and notes the order of reaching: the hand goes to the record before the hand goes to the person. He has no standing to enter either anywhere (T061/T082).

**Emotional turn.** From the month's going-out to the boy's isolation inside it: the last bundles are a public ending that nobody dates, while his one hundred and forty-seven is a private count with no hand to lay it down. The day's work is honest and complete and worth nothing to the person who reads it best.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey reeds green at the broken ends and pale at the frost-bitten tips; river cold; woodsmoke crossing river wind; wool, tallow,Ash-dry mud; a boot print that keeps its shape and gives it back slowly; the thumb-oiled cord gone grey; the half-pace measured from open slates.

**Exact last image.** The reed-cutter's coat closes over the uncounted cord as he goes up the boards, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 2 — A Finger Beneath the Board

**Day and time.** Day 147, midday into the afternoon. Second entry of Day 147.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle and at the hearth.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail; the boy set low near the bench leg; the recurring wet-pot neighbour come up from the strips.

**Beats, in order.**

2. The shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps above in a room the fold does not touch — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word (T060/T086).
3. The wet-pot neighbour asks what the row will want before the last market. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and sends her to the pot: “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.” The woman carries the unanswered question away level so nothing slopped (T077).
4. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, Sella reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies, without looking and without a word, brings it away and does not go back to the shelf. The boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say; such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two (T100 carried, unread).

6. At the hearth she tells the fire the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says “The house will keep it,” counts something under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale off the coals, and sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand (T088, T058/T079).

**Emotional turn.** Care that looks like not seeing: the woman who feeds every hungry child keeps a record where nobody thinks to look and will not say what it is. The boy's reading of the gesture lands exactly and buys him nothing — the adult mind recognises a hidden account, the infant body cannot lift the board an inch.

**Sensory anchors.** The underside of a board becoming a roof; two bare gaps in a row of loaves; the cold clay jar under its glaze; charcoal faded nearly into the grain; the little-door burn half-closing on air; tallow and woodsmoke; four low pulls of fire and the silence where the fifth should be; a pot handle two inches beyond a small hand.

## Scene 3 — One Hundred and Forty-Seven

**Day and time.** Day 147, the going of the light into the small hours. Third entry of Day 147; the day's closing inventory.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf from the basket's low angle.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying him up and going to her own bed; the boy alone in the small hours.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace: around a pan set out to dry, around the trough by the well with its skin of ice thin as a skim of fat, wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. He wants the crust two inches from his ear and the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither and cannot ask.

## Scene 4 — Six Where the House Can Check

**Day and time.** Day 148, Reed 29, Ninthday, before first grey through midday. First dated heading of Day 148.

**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, the finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the flagstones, the Clay Yard.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail; the boy carried past at the half-pace; three or four unnamed men with collars high (not followed, not named); Jorra Vail; a High Kiln kiln-master with Ash on his cuffs; Tarn with the broom.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before first grey the men go low-voiced into the room off the shed whose door keeps its finger's width of open. Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, gives the house no name for them, turns no head toward the door and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031).
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, and the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack are entered by nobody (T035/T031, T009/T013/T092).
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady” — tells a pot “You'll do,” knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from the High Kiln kiln-master who says it is singing before its time and that a pot which sings early sings itself out early. The knowing goes to the near-full tray and not to the hearings (T062, T061/T082).
4. The boy reads one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed joined to no kiln (T062).

7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check and he does not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. No collector comes. Neither man asks about the ear or the room, and the two silences stay unjoined (T036/T076).

**Emotional turn.** The equality under the roof holds at the place it has moved to: the wheel-keeper's finger leads at the damper, the debt's stroke goes on without the check, and nothing is said between them. Danger is present in two forms — the men in the room and the collector's road — and both are met by going on with the work.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust ground small as meal under the nails, distinct from bone-coloured slip; cold iron taking the warmth out of a fingertip; the wheel's clay smell and tallow; Ash on a kiln-master's cuffs; the worn thumb-mark with six in it and a seventh that cannot be checked; the pale clip-ring at a left wrist holding what light there is; the shallow bright patch on the flagstones that keeps the place where a stroke once stopped.

## Scene 5 — Ask the Cord

**Day and time.** Day 148, midday into the afternoon. Second entry of Day 148.

**Place.** Downriver at the Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards, the fish-trade shade below the sill; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail and the boy at the edge of the work; the reed-cutter with his cord; a weir woman who cannot read the cord; Orra Weir; a woman who asks what the levy will take; Mira Silt; a buyer who names a figure; the woman who wraps the child's foot and the neighbour with a pail; the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (still set, not spoken to).

**Beats, in order.**
1. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with the last bundles and the grey cord oiled by a working thumb. He sets his bundle on the flat stone, straightens the cord, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat with the flat of his hand. To the woman who says “I can't read a cord” he says “You can ask the cord” and “No more can the Court” — without anger or smile. No figure, knot, name or joining (T099 carried).
2. Orra Weir stands at the lower boards with her board on its side, hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered, salt white at her wrists, the grey streak first through the black hair, the nasal half-song of the Fen in her voice where she is tired. She pulls one knot down with her thumb and, where a figure is asked, gives the ninth of Ilmen, the collector's boat round the bend on the flood with six oars and one man at the stern who never touched them; to “That isn't the levy” she says “It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.” Her thumb goes to the cord again at the end; the boy cannot tell whether that is a lie or the habit of counting and makes no cause of it. The forty-first double turn stays legible, tail tucked, left over right, with the small weight of a thumb, and the word forty is unspoken (T024/T059/T025).
3. Mira Silt works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer: “Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.” She flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away (T059/T025).
4. When the buyer names a figure, her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; a shifting creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child's knot untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's and does not touch it (T059/T025/T024).
5. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wound it and weather to the neighbour with a pail. The boy reads the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform; it works as perfectly as the day he learned it and buys him nothing. No silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it (T093/T094/T096 carried).
6. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm with the writing inward, his hand to the slate before his eyes go to the stalls. Nobody steps around him and nobody has taken him off (T090/T080 carried).
7. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket. Five private records stand within a dozen paces of the basket — cord, knot-string, second twist, inward slate, the dark foot — side by side and not across, and none can be entered (T058/T079).

**Emotional turn.** The women keep what no Court thumb can spell, and each answers a question about a figure with something that is not a figure. The boy is in the middle of five records he can read the shape of and cannot enter; the surgeon's perfect reading of a cold foot is the most useless thing in the shade.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey thumb-oiled cord; teeth tying a knot low; silt dried pale in the hollow of a neck; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; salt white at wrists and pale eel scars uncovered; the Fen half-song; three turns of wool around a dark foot; a cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday; running water over the boards.

**Exact last image.** The market goes on over the soft-cuffed clerk as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, his hand to the slate before his eyes find the stalls, while the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 6 — Nine at the Teeth

**Day and time.** Day 148, the going of the light into the small hours. Third entry of Day 148; the chapter's hinge.

**Place.** The hearth and kindling under the Vail kiln-house roof.

3. He draws the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says “Yes, house” to the fire and goes on with the broom over the flagstones without the half-check. The small interval at the top of each reach shows where the breath should have gone in (T036/T076).

5. The boy files the ninth stopping on the interval tray beside the eight as a NINTH INTERVAL AND NOT A NINTH PAYMENT — an object among objects and not a rule, not a hearing and not a ninth hearing — with no payer named to any of the nine. He refuses to let nine likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer (T036/T076, T061/T082).
6. He holds the nine apart by hand as he held the eight, because a held word is a debt and a debt that can hear is a debt that can be called. The loan stays uncalled, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the seventh year one tend from its end and still not given, the unforged collector sentence beside the knife (T036/T076).

**Emotional turn.** The man who owns nothing brings the most valuable thing in the house to the threshold of being named and stops. The boy, who heard it all and cannot speak or move, does the only work available: he keeps the nine likenesses apart so they cannot become an account with a payer at its foot. The cost of the keeping is that he is the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

**Sensory anchors.** The knife set back where it can be checked; banked coals under the back of a broken pot; the held breath and the small interval at the top of a reach; Ash worked into knuckle creases; the pale clip-ring catching what light there is; a ninth object going into its own hollow on the tray; four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should be.

**Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (ninth stopping withheld, loan uncalled), T061/T082 (ninth likeness held apart, nine not suffered to stand up as a doctrine), T058/T079, T088, T062, T065/T095, T071/T072 (148 exact).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. Nine likenesses held apart gather toward the day a payer will be named and something must give at the end of the seventh year. The ninth stopping is not a tenth and not a payment; a tenth must not be spent before the seventh year itself has been earned.

**Exact last image.** The ninth stopping lies on the tray among eight others with no payer named to any, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold, one hundred and forty-eight exact and unspent in the dark.

## Scene 7 — The Last Market of Reed

**Beats, in order.**

2. Berr Clay comes up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat and the green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened to his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and reads “one in twelve, as the notice says” low into the goers-by over the last bundles. He calls the levy and denies it with “your house,” never “I”; says “A number goes into a slate, not a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch”; pays two marks short for eels which the stall-keeper puts in her palm without counting where anyone can hear; and goes off up the clay road with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he has done a kindness (T015/T041/T047/T077).
3. The figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and the figure that goes into the slate does not know the hand that did not come back out of the strips (T098 carried). The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the heard bell is kept beside the told bell of the Long Night (T078).
4. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. Nobody steps around him and nobody has taken him off; the market is sold over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies (T090/T080).
5. Across the square at the pot board a silent woman with clay under her nails, a pale line across the back of one hand and the sweetish lead-glaze green in the crease beside her lip turns a cup a quarter without lifting it and sets it back exactly where it stood, says nothing and goes off with the light. The boy files it as the **sixth** green at a mouth and the seventh shape of the colour counting Berr's glaze — a colour and not a name, joined to no ear, never Fenna in the narration (T050/T051 advanced, not paid).
6. At the market's edge the foundling is glimpsed a **sixth** time, unnamed and silent and given no dialogue: cast-off clothes worn like a uniform, silt dried pale in the creases of his wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, a green sliver of lead glaze on a string at his throat. When a pot is set down his head goes toward it a half-beat after the boy's, after the knuckle and after the note, then stops in the middle of the turn; he rubs the sliver twice with his thumb and opens his mouth on the tail of a kiln-hand's sentence and lets nothing out that any clerk could take down. No loaf passed, no slate seen near the basket, no sale — the betrayal kept planted, not paid (T033/T034).
7. The green and the half-turn are filed on places of their own beside the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its gap and the field's empty place, with no joining, no fourth likeness made a cause, no third gap and no name. Six greens must not stand up and call themselves a face; six half-turns must not stand up and call themselves a sale (T050/T051, T033/T034, T060/T086, T097, T098, T061/T082).
8. The count stands at one hundred and forty-nine, exact and unspent, told by nobody, as the last market sells over the clerk and the square goes around every open slate (T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** The market performs the district's whole arithmetic in one square — a figure read that is not the figure paid, a cord nobody can read, a clerk set for a measure nobody called, a green that is only a colour, a half-turn that is only a shape — and the boy is the only person who sees that all of it is the same grammar. He cannot enter any of it. The two owed sightings arrive and are held, not spent.

**Sensory anchors.** The moot-post's thin shade and cold iron at a cheek; red wool cord folded under a coat; lead-glaze green at a mouth corner; a twice-wiped thumb on a clean apron; eight pigs' worth of noise over the reading block; the bell longer than a church bell; eels, slime and short marks; clay under nails and a pale line across the back of a hand; a missing front tooth and a green sliver on a string; river silt dried pale in a child's wrist creases.

**Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077 (Berr at the Account-day reading), T078 (heard bell beside told), T090/T080 (clerk set), T050/T051 (sixth green), T033/T034 (sixth glimpse, no sale), T058/T079, T071/T072 (149 exact), T098 (carried), T061/T082 (likenesses held apart), T060/T086, T097.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The sixth green gathers toward the far day an ear will be spoken with that colour and a name will join it to a person. The sixth half-turn gathers toward the day the sale lands as the physical tell of a betrayal; no loaf, slate or word may cross yet. Berr's true subtraction stays in the locked box; the figure read does not know the missing hand.

**Exact last image.** The foundling's head stops in the middle of its turn toward the set-down pot, the green sliver going dull as he turns back, while the market sells over the soft-cuffed clerk and nobody makes anything of any of it.

## Scene 8 — One Hundred and Forty-Nine

**Day and time.** Day 149, the going of the light into the small hours. Fourth entry of Day 149; the chapter's closing inventory and the turn into Smoke.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf from the basket's low angle.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying him up and going to her own bed; the boy alone in the small hours.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, around a pan set out to dry and the trough with its skin of ice, wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She sets him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it goes away. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says “The house will keep it,” counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale off the coals, sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and neither looks toward the bed nor touches the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening. She goes to her own bed without explaining the count; the two rooms do not touch (T058/T079).
2. In the small hours he turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; the older intervals with the half-beat, the unchecking stroke and his held breath; **nine** stoppings of the near-saying — Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 — objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end and still held; the damper's double bite filed joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the told bell beside the heard bell (T061/T082, T036/T076, T062, T065/T095, T078).
3. In the other room the shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and the charcoal under the board stays unread; one stiff brown-edged crust lies in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; the word forty stays unspoken with the readable double turn; the sixth green is a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, never Fenna and never named in narration; the reed-cutter's cord, Mira's second twist and Orra's knot-string stay unread; Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin stay shut three doors down; the soft-cuffed clerk stays set for an uncalled measure; the cold foot stays the cold to one woman and weather to the yard (T100, T060/T086, T097, T098, T099, T024/T059/T025, T050/T051, T033/T034, T023/T089, T090/T080, T093/T096).
4. Three counts in three places do not become one: the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the carried fire's four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty-nine days; the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb (T088, T061/T082).
5. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and forty-nine days old and four months and twenty-nine days, told by nobody; if somebody under that roof said the number nobody would know what it counted. He wants the crust two inches from his ear and the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither and cannot ask.
6. His hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. The reed month goes out without a boundary drawn; the count crosses into Smoke unspent with nine held words, a sixth colour, a sixth shape and the loan uncalled. Nothing is joined, paid, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

**Emotional turn.** Everything the market displayed in one square has come home under one roof and been put on its own tray, still separate, still worthless, still kept. The boy is one day older in a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, and the month ends because it ends, not because anybody drew a boundary. The forward motion is the count going on into a new month with nothing settled.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and river wind passing on the stair; the iced trough; the frozen thread at the mouth; the half-beat longer than the setting; banked coals under a broken pot; nine objects in nine hollows; the fifth green as a colour, the foundling as a shape; the river's note through the finger's-width door; four pulls and a silent fifth under the floor; four nails, four and a knot; cold in its known order.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (149 exact, unspent), T061/T082, T036/T076 (nine stoppings as objects, not a doctrine), T088, T058/T079, T060/T086, T097, T098, T099, T100, T024/T059/T025, T050/T051, T033/T034, T023/T089, T090/T080, T093/T096, T009/T013/T092, T062, T065/T095, T078.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The nine stoppings, the pressed seventh year, the sixth green and the sixth half-turn all gather interest into the firing month. The charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knots, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, Berr's locked box and the clerk's inward slate stand side by side for the far-off hand that will lay them together and call the joining an account.

**Exact last image.** The note goes on over water through the gap in the door, the fire misses its fifth under the floor, and the charcoal waits under the shelf for a hand that is not his and a day that has not come; one hundred and forty-nine stands exact and unspent in the dark as his hand comes down against the fold.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 37

The chapter ends mid-keeping, not closed. The last market of Reed has stood with every withholding in one square — the figure read that was not the figure paid, the cord no Court thumb can read, the clerk set for a measure nobody called, the sixth green still a colour, the foundling's sixth half-turn still a shape with no loaf, no slate and no sale — and the boy has carried all of it home under the Vail roof and laid each piece on its own tray. Nine stoppings lie on the interval tray with no payer named to any; the seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given; the loan that can hear is still uncalled.

Day 150 is Smoke 1 and belongs to Chapter 37. The reed month is over and the firing month begins: the district goes into the heat of the kilns and the year's last working season, and the count crosses into it exact and unspent. Chapter 37 should let the firing month gather the interest rather than pay it — the ninth stopping, the charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knots, Mira's two cords, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, the missing hand's place, the cold foot, the sixth green, the foundling's half-turn and the clerk set for an uncalled measure all go on being kept by hands that cannot read them. Bring the seventh year nearer its end without calling the loan; do not let a tenth stopping stand up before the seventh year has been earned, do not name the green, sell the foundling's secret, open a tin or box, read a private tally or join any of the absences. Forward, not closed.
