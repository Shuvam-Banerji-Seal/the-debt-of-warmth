\daymark{148}{Reed 29, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

One hundred and forty-eight days old. Four months and twenty-eight days. Neither number was spoken under any roof in \place{Low Kiln} that morning, and there was no vessel in the district that would have held either one still long enough for a woman passing with a pot to set it down. The reed month had two days left and would not say so; the last market and the third Account-day stood together tomorrow beyond the bend of the road, one day and the same day, and this house kept its days the way it kept its fire --- in loaves pushed along a board, in glances at a clay jar, in the space where a thing had stood and had not been put back.

Before first grey the men came up \place{the clay road} with their collars high and their voices low, three or four of them, going toward \place{the Vail wheel-shed} and not toward the kiln. \person{Sella Vail} took \person[Corin]{the boy} past them at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and gave the house no name for the men, and turned no head toward the door they went through, and did not say that the room off the shed was cold. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears. It had come all the way down with them in the dark and would go all the way back up before the light was gone; she had said it once, and a wind takes an instruction and carries it away and there is an end of it. Woodsmoke went before her and the river wind came up to meet it, the two crossing between the houses the way two people pass on a stair without speaking, each with a hand on the wall and neither giving the other a figure for the passing.

The door of \place{the back room} kept its finger's width of open. It had kept it through every morning of his life that had a morning, and this one men had gone through it low-voiced and stood where he could not see them. From the low angle he read the gap as a wound left unclosed on purpose --- not a thing anybody would enter, and not a thing anybody could forget was there. A wound kept open a finger's width is a wound that is being kept, and a kept thing wants an account sooner or later, and there was nobody under this roof who would lay a finger to that gap and call it one. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side in his file, one from a neighbour at a well and one from a man with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs at \place{the Cross}, and this morning had brought no third. Men had gone into a room that stood a finger's width open, and no third telling had come out of it. He left the two where they lay and did not make the opening into a voice.

The yard was still the colour of wet rope when \person{Jorra Vail} came through. Slip dried to the colour of old bone stood on his forearms on other mornings; this morning fine grey slate dust was ground small as meal into both of them, past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, and there was no bone-coloured slip anywhere on him to say the wheel had had him first. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, the two states of one morning set side by side and neither laid across the other, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. Beyond the ruts the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} kept the broken birth-slates where they lay, and the yard went around it with a look and a wide step and entered them into nothing. The boy had learned early that two states of one pair of forearms will stand up and call themselves a cause if nobody keeps a hand between them, and he kept his hand between these, dusty and clean on the same tray and neither laid across the other.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anybody, as he had spoken to it a hundred mornings and would speak a hundred more. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” The clay smelled of damp earth and tallow, and the tallow had been worked in until the wood shone where a man's weight had leaned. A pot came up under his hands and he looked at it the way a man looks at an instrument he has already trusted, and said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} in it before it left the wheel --- knew it three turns ahead, whole and true, the way a man knows the note of a thing he has made with his own hands and would not have to strike it to be sure --- and he did not say so.

A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} stood at the edge of the shed with Ash worked into the creases of his cuffs. He looked at the pot on the wheel and said it was singing before its time, and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early, and he said it in the tone of a man who has said it to apprentices and expects the weight of it to be accepted. Jorra said nothing. He went on turning, and the knowing stayed where it had been put, and the kiln-master went away with the answer he had come for without having been given one.

The boy laid that knowing on the tray with the three near-full answers that had wanted a following stroke and had not got one: a cup across a birth room, a pot under this shed's wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. It was a knowing held back, and a knowing held back is not a hearing given. He did not put it among the six. The six stayed six, five from four sources and the small flat knock out of a person filed beside them and never counted with them, and no seventh was invented to make the row come out even. An empty place kept honestly is a record of a kind; a man who fills one to make a row come out even is a man with a reason, and he had learned two lives ago to be afraid of the shape of a reason in a man with clean hands and a good sentence ready. The kiln-master had a good sentence. Jorra had the note and kept it. Neither had given him anything he could enter.

\scenebreak

Sella had not finished telling the fire that it was low.

“The fire's low, Jorra. It wants ---”

His bent little finger was already in the cold iron of \lore{the damper}. He had gone in ahead of his own hand and ahead of the end of her sentence, the way he always went in, and the finger that had been bent by a splinter off the wheel when he was eleven moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing where he wanted it. The iron took the warmth out of the fingertip; the boy could see it go, the way one sees colour leave a hand that has lain too long on a cold stone. Sella shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot, and the household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. Nobody said the room was cold. Nobody said the fire had been low, or had ceased to be. The pot received the sentence that had been meant for a person and stood the straighter for it.

The boy read the iron as he had read it on other mornings and would read it again: one piece with two bites. The one bite held a vessel whole, closed the draught, kept the heat where a house needed it through a night that would otherwise take the warmth out of every room. The other bite could take the hand off a man, could close on a wrist and hold it while the Warm backed up and the flesh cooled and came away. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath; the district had a word for keeping and a word for cutting and had not put them in the same iron, because a people who put them in the same iron would have to say what they thought of the man who turned the tongue. He filed it joined to no kiln. A clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine. Set where it is needed and left where it is set, it is a piece of iron with two bites, and the second bite is not an argument for the first.

The two tellings of the counting room stood beside the iron with no third, and the morning gave no third. A door a finger's width open is not a telling. Men with their collars high had gone through it and come no nearer the basket than the sound of their boots, and the boy had not been given a name, a room entered or a sentence carried out, and he did not make one from the shape of the gap. An inventory is not a key, and a list is not a hand. He had a hundred and forty-eight days of other people's keeping laid out in places of their own, and could carry none of it to any table.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. It stood where a broom stands, and the instruction went to it before it went to him: go around, leave the clean dark line where it lay, take \place{the flagstones} and not the road unless the road was named. He took it up as a man takes up a tool already named, and said, “Yes, house,” to no one in particular, and went to sweep.

He had been going at it since before the dark, and his chest had been tight since the dark, and the breath that should have gone in at the top of each reach was still unspent, leaving its small interval where the top of the reach should have been. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the line nobody swept, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His seventh year stood at its very edge. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh stood where the house could not, and that was the whole of the account as he kept it: six where the house can check, one where it cannot. The small knife lay at the very lip of its sill where it could be seen to be back, and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it and had never been spoken, and the breath from Seed 16 lay under both and had never been spent. He said nothing of any of it. A man who owns nothing keeps the one thing he owns out of the house's reach, and the house, which counts everything else, has never once asked after it.

The boy read the thumb-mark the way he had once read a chart turned down on a table: six pressings of a thumb worn almost flat into the wood, and a seventh place that was not a pressing and could not be checked by anybody who came later with a slate. An account that can be read by the man who holds it and by nobody else is the kind of account that keeps a man awake; he knew that as he knew the order in which cold arrives, and he could no more have entered it in a house ledger than he could have lifted his own head and said the number of his days. The ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held it, which made the ear unsafe, and the sentence that would call the loan that can hear had stood at the threshold of the word for six years and had never gone over. Tarn had brought it to the word eight times and stopped there, on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23 and 26, and had drawn the breath he did not spend and gone back to the broom, and the ninth stopping was owed to the night and had not yet come. The seventh year stood one tend from its end and had not given. The loan stood uncalled.

The road that a house sends a man down when it wants a collector went away from the yard between the houses, and Tarn's stroke went over the ground that led to it and over it and past it, again and again, without once turning his head along it. No collector came. He did not go for one, and did not say that the omission had a name, and did not look along the road to see whether anybody had come. A road swept over often enough is a road nobody has taken, and a man who keeps it swept keeps it the way the house keeps the Ash in the corner, by going around what is really there. He had been sent once in \lore{the Long Cold} and had turned back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and neither Sella nor Jorra had ever asked whether it had been done, and the sentence stood with the seventh year and was entered in neither.

\scenebreak

The broom went on over the flagstones. The shallow bright patch lay where a stroke had once stopped, a little lighter than the stone around it because a man had stood still there longer than a stroke required, and the stones had kept the place as they keep a print in mud that has since dried. Tarn's stroke went over it and past it without even the half-check, without the pause behind the wheel-keeper's finger, without the turn of the head that would have made the road a road. The place where it had stopped was kept as a thing nobody would enter.

Jorra came through from the shed with the dust still on him and did not look toward the basket or \place{the bed}, and Tarn did not look toward the door. Neither man asked about the ear and neither man asked about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined under one roof. The wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold damper and the debt's stroke went on over the stones, and nothing passed between them, and the household went on at the place it had moved to. Danger stood in the room with the finger's width of open and danger stood at the end of the road, and both were met the same way: by going on with the work.

The boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and forty-eight stood exact and unspent, the loan uncalled, the seventh year one tend from its end and still held, the ninth stopping owed to the night and not yet come. The stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped, and nobody entered it.
