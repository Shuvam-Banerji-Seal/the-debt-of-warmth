## Chronicle

The reed month had three days left and nobody was keeping them. Sella Vail carried the boy at the half-pace, instruction to the wool and never the basket. Bundles came out judged by the arm that carried them; a cutter told a girl an arm knows weight and not cost, and a man asking what the hands would be paid was answered with the weather and the river. One hundred and forty-seven stood exact and unspent.

The yard went around the clean dark line, the thaw, the broken birth-slates and the empty place where a hand went in and did not come out. A reed-cutter's cord went past, tied low with teeth and hidden in his coat, its knots unread. Under the Vail roof Sella sent a woman asking the row's price to the pot, then reached a finger beneath the board where the charcoal lay, without looking. Such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look.

On Reed 29 men went low into the back room, unnamed. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust on both forearms, spoke to the wheel first, withheld a pot's full note from a High Kiln kiln-master and put his bent finger into the cold damper --- one iron with two bites, one holding a vessel whole, one taking a hand off a man.

Downriver Orra gave a date for a figure, Mira answered a thin run with Ask the river, a child's foot stayed dark and the clerk stayed set for an uncalled measure. That night Tarn told the kindling Six counted. The next I don't count and brought the ear-sentence a ninth time, after Reed 5 through 26, to the word that would call the loan, and stopped with no figure or name, his breath unspent. The boy filed the ninth stopping as an interval, not a payment, and kept nine likenesses from standing up as a doctrine with no payer named to any.

On Reed 30 the last market stood with every withholding together and none read. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve, called the levy and denied it with your house, paid two marks short for eels, and went off with the true subtraction locked in his box. A silent woman at the pot board turned a cup a quarter and set it back, a sixth green, a colour joined to no ear. At the market's edge the foundling's head turned half a beat late and stopped in the middle, a sixth shape with no loaf, no slate and no sale.

Under the roof Sella set him on his bed, put the fold right without looking at his face and went to her own bed. His file held six hearings and no seventh and nine stoppings with no payer named. One hundred and forty-nine stood exact and unspent as his hand went up and came down. The month went out without a boundary drawn.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** The chapter stops in the small hours of Day 149, Reed 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. The boy lies alone on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, fold at his ear and under his chin, the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, his small hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door and coming down against the fold. \person{Sella Vail} has gone to her own bed; the two rooms do not touch. The last image is the count crossing into Smoke unspent, the ninth stopping on the tray, a sixth colour and a sixth shape on places of their own, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end. Mid-action? No: a held, open moment, not closed.
- **Day and time at end.** Day 149, Reed 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, the small hours; the third Account-day and the last market of Reed, and the boy one hundred and forty-nine days old and four months and twenty-nine days, told by nobody. Days 147 (Reed 28, Eighthday), 148 (Reed 29, Ninthday) and 149 were each lived in full under their own `\daymark`, with no day skipped or poured into another. Day 150 is Smoke 1 and belongs to Chapter 37.
- **Where every on-stage character is now.** \person[Corin]{The boy}: on his bed, awake, silent, alone, wanting the crust two inches from his ear and the number of his days spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and getting neither. Sella Vail: in her own bed under the same roof, having carried him up the clay road at the half-pace from the market's edge, around the pan, the iced trough and the space under the rack, set him on the bed without looking at his face, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting, told the fire the thaw, said *The house will keep it*, counted under her breath toward no opening, set the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, left the widow's mark untouched and gone to bed without explaining the count. \person{Tarn}: last on stage on Day 148 night at the hearth and kindling, with the knife back at the edge of its sill where the house can check six, the ear-sentence brought a **ninth** time to the word that would call the loan and withheld, the Seed 16 breath undrawn; asleep under the roof by the small hours. \person{Jorra Vail}: last on stage on Day 148 night coming through from the shed with the day's grey slate dust still on both forearms, speaking to a pot and not looking toward the bed; he did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room. \person{Berr Clay}: last seen on Day 149 going up the clay road from the last market with the red cord of his office folded under his coat, a narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the true subtraction inside; he believes he has done the district a kindness. \person{Mira Silt} and \person{Orra Weir}: last seen on Day 148 at the Weir, Mira's thumb under her apron to the grey cord with the second twist beneath it, Orra's forty-first double turn still legible with the word forty unspoken; neither board touches the other. The reed-cutter: last seen on Day 149 passing the basket at the Cross with the grey cord closed inside his coat, the knots unread. The soft-cuffed clerk: still set on Day 149 at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls, nobody having stepped around him or taken him off. The green-mouthed woman: last seen on Day 149 at the pot board, silent, turning a cup a quarter and setting it back exactly where it stood, gone off with the light. The foundling: last glimpsed on Day 149 at the market's edge, unnamed, silent, given no dialogue, his head stopped in the middle of its turn toward a set-down pot and the green sliver going dull as he turned back. \person{Alda Reed}: not on stage this chapter, three doors down in \place{House Silt} with her sign still covered by the date and both tins shut; \person{Dessa Silt} stays dead and uncollected.
- **Items gained, lost, used; money; powers.** Nothing gained, nothing used, no money at any point, no purchase and no sale. The one brown-edged crust in the fold remains stiff and uneaten two inches from his ear, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The loaf shelf keeps two unnamed gaps; Sella's finger went beneath its front edge on Day 147 without looking or a word, and the charcoal remains unread. The reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's two cords, the clerk's inward slate, Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin were seen or carried and none opened, read, copied or joined. Berr paid two marks short for two pair of eels into a palm that did not count them aloud; the figure read at the block was not the figure paid. No powers used, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy; counting remains an old-life habit, not a gift, and the surgeon's register only colours the noticing.
- **Facts revealed, lies told, promises, debts.** The chapter's one meaningful change is that Tarn brought the ear-sentence a **ninth** time (Reed 29, Day 148) to the word that would call the loan and stopped, with no figure and no name. A sixth green came to a silent woman's mouth at the pot board and a sixth silent glimpse of the foundling came at the market's edge; both were filed as a colour and a shape on places of their own and neither was spent. Berr called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never \emph{I}, and his true subtraction stayed in the locked box. Sella's cost-question was answered with a direction, and the wet-pot neighbour carried it away level. No promise made or withdrawn: *he's not Vail* stays sealed, the foundling's name promise ungiven. No debt paid: the loan uncalled, the seventh year one tend from its end and still held, the Seed 16 breath unspent.

- **Foreshadowing planted / gathered, nothing paid.** The nine stoppings gather toward the day something must give at the end of the seventh year; a tenth must not stand up before the seventh year has been earned. The sixth green gathers toward a far day when the colour will be spoken with a name and joined to a person; the sixth half-turn gathers toward the day the sale lands as the physical tell of a betrayal --- no loaf, slate or word may cross yet. The district's private records --- charcoal under the shelf, reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knots, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, Berr's locked box and the clerk's inward slate --- stand side by side for the far-off hand that will lay them together and call the joining an account. T098, T099, T100, T097 and T023/T089 remain carried or advanced and unpaid. No new thread opened.
- **What Chapter 37 must not forget.** Opens on Day 150, Smoke 1, Y.H. 813, with the boy one hundred and fifty days old and nobody saying so, the firing month begun and the kilns going into the year's last working season. Let the month gather the interest rather than pay it. Do not call the loan, spend the Seed 16 breath, cut, count or give the seventh year, stand a tenth stopping up, name the green, sell the foundling's secret, open a tin or box, read a private tally, or join any of the absences. Keep Marra out of Sill, Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy, Alda's mercy-lie uncorrected, and *he's not Vail* sealed. The old life stays offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break, no departure from the boy's eyes.
