## Chronicle

Three days ran out the year under the Vail roof, and the boy kept a count no roof could read. Stone twenty-eight, twenty-nine and thirty came without a boundary drawn. Each morning the surgeon took the body in its old order: barley crust two inches from the ear; the empty place holding the fold; the thread frozen and unwiped, for nobody was ever told about the thread; cold stopping at cheek, temple and bare wrist-joint; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed slowly from outside, never rubbed. Under the wool the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both true, the second louder, neither winning.

On the twenty-eighth the fold was found where put, a finding and not a placing. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and set the pot rim two inches beyond his reach. Tarn kept six at the kindling and the seventh at the lip, told the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and brought his ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped. Jorra Vail parted dusty from clean forearms as clot from vessel and put his bent finger into the cold damper. Answers 349 and 350; the count stood at 387.

On the twenty-ninth the day went thin through the finger's-width door. Ilva at the well answered the woman who asked what Stone's firings had taken: *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account* — her hand to the sleeve before her eyes to the asker, the notched strip unread in the fold. The loan stayed uncalled and Jorra's promise was neither given nor withdrawn. Answers 351 and 352; the count stood at 388.

On the thirtieth the year's third and last Account-day and market came before first light. Sella set the basket in the moot-post's shade. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, read *One in twelve, as the notice says* and sent the figure twice over the basket; he called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never I, and went off up the road with the box locked and Stone's last true subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Twelve arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure: twelve were twelve and not one account, the figure read not the figure paid, no signature, no doctrine out of twelve. Hessa carried the narrow slate unread; the bell went four, four and a gap. Answers 353 and 354; the count stood at 389 exact, six months and two hundred and nine days told by nobody. Nothing under the roof stood up and called itself an account. The surgeon's satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found cost nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** The chapter closes in the small hours at the very end of Day 389, Stone 30, Tenthday, the last dark of the year. The hand outside the wool has come up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, not reaching, taken a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Answer **354** filed: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across none of the kept things. Present: Corin/Dan alone on the bed under the roof with the crust's corner standing a hair off the wool and the thread frozen and unwiped; Sella Vail somewhere under the roof after the year's last hearth grammar; Garl Vail in objects only. Not mid-action; the hand has come down and does not stay. The Five stand beyond the last of Stone and are not brought into the dark.

- **Day and time at the end.** Day 389 = Stone 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, small hours at the close of the year. The chapter covered Days 387 (Stone 28, Eighthday), 388 (Stone 29, Ninthday) and 389 (Stone 30, Tenthday), each lived in two full scenes with its own dated `\daymark` in scenes 1, 3 and 5. - **Where everyone is at the close.** Sella Vail — under the roof, having carried him up the nineteen steps at the going of the light, set him on the bed, put the fold at his ear and under his chin with no look at his face and no second fold, renewed the hearth grammar for the last time in the year and left the twice-mended pot, dressed grain and unread charcoal as found; she said nothing of what had been read over the wicker. Tarn — last seen on Stone 29 afternoon at the kindling: six in the worn thumb-mark, seventh at the lip, knife at the sill edge, hand up over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach; chest tight since the dark, Ash in his knuckle creases, pale clip-ring at the left wrist, stroke over the bright flagstones without the half-check; the loan uncalled at the very end of his seventh year, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the fear of being entered again for vagrancy kept without a figure. Jorra Vail — last seen at the wheel-shed edge and damper on Stone 29 afternoon with meal-fine grey dust white in a fist-crease, three or more unnamed men gone low into the back room before first grey, dusty and clean forearms parted as clot from vessel, the full note known three turns ahead and withheld, bent finger into the cold iron ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence and out without a second holding; *he's not Vail* sealed, the promise of the boy's name neither given nor withdrawn. Garl Vail — absent 389 days, kept in objects only: salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg, hollow wheel-seat, the clean dark line at the Vail corner with its finer grey called the thaw thickened in the dry cold with the sour tang of Ash, the twice-mended pot's hairline, the unforged collector sentence at the kindling; his name unspoken. Ilva — on stage once, at the yard well on Stone 29 morning, drawing with the pail against her knee, hand to her sleeve before her eyes to the asker, the notched birch strip unread; her ninth record-first hand stays nine with a finger between it and Hessa Rale's eighth, no tenth, no reader, expressly not the speaker of any voice, no fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying. Berr Clay — went off up the clay road toward High Kiln after the reading on Stone 30 with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Stone's last true subtraction riding inside with the eleven before it; believes he has done a kindness, with no anger and no triumph, and goes home to have that belief remembered against him; the key went up in a coat pocket and does not come back down and the boy has no key. Hessa Rale — went up the clay road toward Greybank before noon on Stone 30 with Berr's narrow slate edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve; her eighth record-first hand stays eight, no tenth, not made a reader by the year's last figure. Also on Stone 30: the soft-cuffed clerk at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge, a silent woman with the sweetish green of lead glaze at the pot board, and a small shape in cast-offs at the market's edge — sightings only, no eighth of either, never Fenna or Ulda. No Drawing collector, green-coated woman or named officer; no ownerless voice over the boards on any of the three days, and the Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17, Ash 23 and Stone 23 directions stand where put and do not repeat.

- **Items, money, powers.** Nothing gained, lost, bought or sold; no money at any point and no slate opened within reach of the basket on any of the three days. Powers: none usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp; counting is an old-life habit, not a gift, and all 354 answers are objects worth nothing at two inches of air. Twelve arithmetics stand behind one unsigned figure and their twelve true subtractions ride locked in Berr Clay's box up the clay road; the boy has no key. Ilva's strip stays in her sleeve, Hessa's slate stays unread up the road, the clerk's inward slate stays at the landing, the dressed grain stays covered and unfigured and the pot rim stays two inches beyond his reach.

- **The chapter's one meaningful change (advanced, NOT paid).** On Stone 30 the **twelfth arithmetic** went up behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, the first, second and third of Ash, and Stone's first, second and last. One in twelve was read twice over the basket and sent twice, once for the moot and once for the late households. Twelve are twelve and not one account; the figure read is still not the figure paid; no signature stands on the space between and no doctrine comes out of twelve. An account wants a hand to lay twelve likenesses together and a payer standing under all twelve, and no such hand stood and no such payer stood. Berr called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never I, taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on; he believes he has done a kindness. The boy filed the order of Berr's reaching on a tray of its own beside the other record-first hands and made no tenth, no payer and no doctrine of it. The finding sits on a place of its own and is not laid across the keeping at the fold, the four rooms' shared gap, the four directions or any of his own gaps. (T015/T041/T047 advanced, not paid.)
