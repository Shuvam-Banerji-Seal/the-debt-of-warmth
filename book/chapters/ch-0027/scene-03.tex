\daymark{121}{Reed 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the \place{Clay Yard} was a shape felt sooner than seen, the racks a row of darker uprights against a sky the colour of wet ash, and the men came up it with their collars high and their heads bent toward one another as though they carried between them something that would break if the wind got under it. They went low-voiced into \place[the back room]{the room off the wheel-shed}, which stood a finger's width open and did not shut. \person{Sella Vail} set \person[Corin]{the boy} at the edge of the light in \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, the basket level on the boards and his cheek turned to the cold of them, and stood a half-pace from every open slate. She gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to anybody. Keep it off his ears; it's the river wind. The instruction went into the wool and into the wind and nowhere near the child in the basket. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist; the wool had ridden back from the bare joint in the grey and the cold found that place and went under the fold at his ear, and the thread at his mouth had frozen where nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His head wandered and made the small wet sound and came back. He was one hundred and twenty-one days old, and nobody in the shed or under the roof had said so, or would have known what it counted if they had.

\person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for the men. He did not say the room was cold. He came through the finger's-width door with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; the two states of one pair of forearms went down on one tray in the boy's keeping, dusty and clean, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay where they had lain since the flood beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, and the yard went around the space above them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered them into nothing. Nothing in \place{Low Kiln} had entered them into anything. A place gone around with a look was still a place entered by nobody, and the going-around had a name in the yard, and the name was care.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” It was the same sentence the boy had heard from the same mouth through the whole length of the planting month, given to the wheel as a man gives a beast its handful and not to the people standing by, and nothing in it asked who had been in the room or what had been ground into the creases past the little finger. A pot came up on the wheel and he looked at its wall a moment longer than the wall required and said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{the full note} that was in that pot before it ever left the wheel; he had known such notes in pots of this house since he was eleven, and he would not say so in front of a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it. \person{Garl Vail}'s proverb had gone to a pot with no crack long ago and cost the more now that there was nobody to say it in his own voice, and the boy, who had spent two lives learning the exact weight of a note before a thing breaks, kept the knowing where questions could last for years.

A kiln-master come up from High Kiln went along the row of pots with his hands behind his back, looking at the rims and not at the boy, and said nothing that any slate could carry. Jorra said nothing either. The note was in the Vail pot before it left the wheel and he had known it there and would go on knowing it, but a thing said in front of a kiln-master becomes a thing the kiln-master has heard, and a thing heard is a thing entered in some book the boy would never see; the house had taught him that much without ever saying it. The kiln-master went off up the yard with his hands still behind him and the glaze smell on his coat, and the wheel took up its low note again as though no man had stood over it. The boy set the unsaid knowing on a place of its own and did not lay it beside the dust on the forearms, though the two things had come in on the same morning.

\scenebreak

“The fire's low, Jorra. Feed it before—”

Sella had not finished. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, the tip of it taking the cold out of the iron and giving a little back in the other direction, and he moved the tongue of the thing a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, the bucket settling, the broom settling, her hands settling into the shape of the next thing they would hold, and no order went out and no word came back. He had not answered her and had not looked at her, which was how he obeyed her instantly.

The boy read the iron once more as he had read it on Seed 16 and on Seed 28 and on a dozen mornings between: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, two doings of one piece of iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} that held both mercies in one sound. It was the same iron set at two depths, and a man who knew the feel of teeth could tell which bite he was closing before he closed it; here there was no choosing, because the iron stood where it stood and the warmth went by degrees and the payer of it was named by nobody. He set the reading on a place of its own with the other objects he kept where no slate could read it, exact, worth nothing, joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their own tray and did not come across to it. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause, and there are more ways of making a cause than there are hands to make them.

His own hand outside the wool could not have closed on the damper's tongue if the whole house had told him to. It could go up an inch and come down, and that was the whole of its reach; the iron lay two paces off with the cold standing on it like water on a stone, and he could no more have set a finger on it than he could have told the men in the room their names. He had been a man who chose his instrument before a field was opened and knew by the weight in his palm what the clamp would do; now he lay at the edge of the light and watched a boy of sixteen put a bent finger into the cold ahead of a woman's sentence, and the watching was the only instrument left to him, exact and worth nothing. A small sound came out of his mouth and went back into it, and nobody in the shed had been instructed about that sound either.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after Sella had given it its instruction. Go around, it cost enough to come in, it will cost more to take out; the instruction went into the broom and the flagstones and never into the man, and he took up the tool as a man takes up a thing already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and went around the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, and around the space beneath the lowest rack, and out toward the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. His seventh year was pressed past the end of the planting and still held. The small bench knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and never named, the one entry in this house the house could not check; the unforged collector sentence lay beside it where he had kept it since the frozen ford, and neither was entered. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the breath from Seed 16 was still unspent, a small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in, a breath stopped against iron and never let through. To the kindling, later, the account stayed as it had stayed through the planting month: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said “Yes, house” to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and kept wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to the light while he said it, and the \lore{Ash} in the creases of his knuckles showed the colour of the clean dark line no scrubbing lifted, and the ring gave back what little light there was. He had been sent for a collector once in the Long Cold and come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and that sentence lay where he kept the seventh year and was not entered; no collector had come down the road this morning and no one had gone down it for him. He put the small knife back where it could be checked, though nobody had asked, and went on sweeping toward the road with his chest held the way a man holds a door he means to keep shut.

\scenebreak

His stroke went on over the flagstones without the half-check. Behind Jorra's standing finger the bristles passed the place where the stroke had stopped on Seed 22 and did not stop there now, and the stones kept that place all the same, a shallow bright patch in the pattern of the paving that no foot would enter and no broom would call by its name. On Seed 25 the change had been held and not repeated; on Seed 28 it had been held again; on this morning of Reed it went on as though the half-beat had never been made, and the two things lay on one tray in the boy's keeping as two objects and not a rule. He set the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat and laid no cause across them. Neither man named the other's silence. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the loan that could hear was not called; a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the sentence that would call it had lain under this roof for six years without being said. An ear worth more than a debt is an unsafe thing to own, and the boy, who had spent two lives learning what an uncalled account costs, kept the two silences apart on the tray where they had lain since the night he came, with no finger laid across the space between them.

Under the roof that private file kept its objects where he had laid them the night before: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, three intervals, the told bell beside the heard bell in two places that did not touch, the word forty unspoken with its readable double turn beside it, the cold foot, the two tellings of the shut room joined to no kiln, the hand that had gone into the strip and had not come back held on a place of its own. To these he added the morning's two objects --- the dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and the stroke that went on without the check --- and set each on its own place, and laid no finger across the space between them, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause and a cause wants a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one object he had spent two lives declining to become.

Jorra did not look toward the bed and did not look into the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed where it had been spoken on the hour the boy came under the roof, sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The wheel went on under his hands, the pot took its note and kept it, the dust stayed ground small as meal in the creases past his bent finger, and the men in the room off the shed kept their names to themselves because the house had been given none to carry. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the household went on straightening around the silence without an order. Cold came at cheek and temple and wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted; the thread at his mouth stayed frozen and unwiped, and his small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water over water and came down. One hundred and twenty-one stood where one hundred and twenty had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody who would have known what a number of days counted when they said it, and the count went on into the rest of the day with no hand joining the two silences in the room.
