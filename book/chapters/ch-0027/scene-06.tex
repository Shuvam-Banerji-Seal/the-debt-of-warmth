The house settled into the small hours the way a vessel settles after it has been filled: not by going still, but by giving up its small noises one at a time. The pot had been set where it could be found in the dark. The coals lay under blind ash with the back of the broken pot resting against the hearth where \person{Sella Vail} had left it, and the little-door burn had closed on the handle and then on nothing, and she had gone to her own bed without once looking toward his. Above him the boards kept their old arithmetic: four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a fifth that was not a nail at all but a knot the width of a thumb. He had counted those boards before he had known how to count his own days, and the count had never once changed. Four, four, and a knot. It was the only fifth thing under this roof that anybody would have admitted was part of the floor, and it held nothing, drove nothing, kept nothing from coming apart. He had known men in the other life hang a coat on a nail that was not a nail and call it a nail because the wall needed one. Here nobody called the knot anything. It was simply there, and the boards held.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool had ridden back from the bare joint and the wind had found the place it had come for, going under the fold at his ear. The crust lay stiff two inches from that ear, brown-edged, past smelling of anything, with the empty place beside it still without a name. No third crust had come into the fold and no third would come tonight. The thread at his mouth had frozen before the light went and nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had frozen, a small white line drawn tight against the corner of his mouth, exact and kept by nobody. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far and came back. He could not lift it. He could not turn it. He could not wipe the thread or put the wool back over the wrist where the cold was finding the bare place. He was a man who had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and he could not keep a fold of wool on his own arm. The joke had stopped being funny around day forty and had not become kinder since.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the note of water over water, the river going downriver beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir}, a sound he had first heard under this roof when he was six days old and had kept on a place of its own ever since, never a hearing, never a vessel's note, never counted among the six. Through the floor under the blind ash came the other sound, the carried fire taking its coal and giving back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had been unnamed for a hundred and twenty-two days. The two sounds sat in two separate places and did not meet: the water over water beyond the finger's-width gap, the fire missing its fifth breath under the floor. Three counts lay in the room and none of them was one count. The river's note was one. The fire's four, four and a gap was another. The boards' four, four and a knot was a third. A man with a slate might have laid the three together and called the matching fours a rule, and the rule would have been worth exactly what the knot was worth. He kept them apart. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause, and a cause in this district always arrived with a clean hand at the foot of it.

Somewhere in the room \person{Sella Vail} breathed in the way of a woman who has spent the day carrying and has not yet put the carrying down, a breath held a little at the top, let go, held again. He heard it without turning toward it. She had moved her lips along the loaf row at the going of the light without a figure and had not touched the mark, because a shelf is not an opening and a dusk is not one. The mark hung where it hung, thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she had not touched it for a bed or a basket or a count of days. If she counted now it was under her breath, in the dark, toward no opening that any man could enter. He had stopped trying to hear what she counted. It was not his to take up, and the taking-up of another person's private number was the oldest trade in this district and the one he had spent his whole new life declining to learn.

The loaf shelf stood in the next room, through a wall and a door that was not this door. It kept two gaps and would keep them until morning: the old space at the row's end that a neighbour had named as a thing the planting brought in, and the new palm's breadth that had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word. Two neighbours had laid the old gap and the empty place at his ear under one appetite in a single breath and gone off without seeing they had joined two rooms that do not touch. He had set their sayings on places of their own and made neither saying his own. The shelf did not reach through the wall. The fold did not reach into the next room. One stiff crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, and the shelf's two gaps lay where they lay, and no finger of his had ever been long enough to lay a line between them, and if it had been he would not have laid it. A line drawn through three absences is a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent two lives refusing to become.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel, in the order he had laid them the night before and the night before that, and found every object where he had left it. There was no new object to add today beyond the one already added at the going of the light: the length of cord gone past him on the plank walk at \place{the Weir}, carried and not counted, its knots unread, its tally kept in a material no Court thumb would read. It lay on its own place beside the other second records and touched none of them. Beyond that the file was as it had been, and the fact that it was unchanged was the one thing about it he could not file.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among. Five had come from four sources: a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore{the Hearth Drawing}. The sixth was the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, from a person, set beside the five and never counted with them. No seventh had come today. No whole vessel's note had come past the door, the bell at \place{the Cross} was a told bell and not a hearing, the wheel and the pots under the roof were notes he had learned to leave where they sounded, and he had not invented one to fill the place where a seventh would have stood. Six stayed six. Beside the hearings lay the told bell in one place and the heard bell in another, two objects in two places that did not touch, and the word forty on a third place of its own, unspoken for a hundred and twenty-two days, its readable double turn kept without a name laid on the turn. He had never said the word in this world and had no intention of saying it to a room full of sleeping people who would have taken it for a figure and entered it.

Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. Three intervals lay on another tray, Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25, and beside them the half-beat of \person{Tarn}'s check and the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check, two objects on one tray and not a rule. The held breath from Seed 16 sat at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in and had not gone in, unspent and carried now into a new month, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled. The unforged sentence stood beside it, the one that would call the loan if any man under this roof said it, and nobody had said it. Six counted, the next not counted, the seventh year pressed past the end of the planting and still held at the very edge of the sill. Something must give at the end of that seventh year and nothing had given yet, and the waiting was itself an object on its own place, exact and worth nothing.

The cold foot below the sill stayed where it had been kept: the cold to one woman who would not go toward it, weather to a yard that had gone around it with a glance and a step and no word. He had laid the two tellings of the shut room on a tray of their own and joined neither to the damper's double bite, though the bite was the truest thing in the house and the least spendable: the iron that held a vessel whole and the iron that could take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. \person{Jorra Vail} had put his bent finger into that cold iron ahead of an unfinished sentence and the household had straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The boy had read the iron once and filed it as an object joined to no kiln. Two men had carried the story of a counting room shut in a war into this yard and a third had only mentioned it, each with a date in it and none of the weight, and he had set the tellings beside the iron without laying one across the other. A cause made from a likeness is the cheapest thing a man can make, and here it would have cost somebody an arm.

The hand that had not come back out of the worked strip held a place of its own. No name, no cause, no body, no figure for what the month had cost. The yard had gone around the empty place with a look and a wide step since the seed came up, and a slate would be found and a figure made if a hand came up in a month or a year, and nothing had come up. He laid nothing across it. Not the cord. Not the shelf's two gaps. Not the crust two inches from his ear. Two absences laid together make an account and an account wants a payer, and the payer under this roof was a woman breathing in the dark with a half-beat held at the top of each breath, who had carried a boy up from the rack end at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}.

There were other shapes on their own places, and he passed over them in the dark the way a surgeon passes a hand over a row of instruments without lifting any of them. The grey wool cord at \person{Mira Silt}'s waist with the second twist beneath it, neither knot counted, the cord never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door. Her board lying beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and not touching it. Six pages in a little tin turned writing-down toward a floorboard in a house he could not enter, written in a second hand no Court physician had ever seen. The round pewter tin above a bed nobody used, with a pale fold at its seam and a thumb's bright rub on the lid, shut for more mornings than anybody in the district kept count of. The foundling's head going half a beat late toward a pot set down at the market's edge, the green sliver rubbed twice with a thumb, the mouth opening on the tail of a sentence and nothing coming out that any clerk could take down. The soft-cuffed clerk at the out-of-line landing with his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called. \person{Berr Clay}'s thumb wiped twice and his finger passed over a figure that was not the figure paid, and the man taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. Five record-first hands on five trays and a sixth shape kept off every tray. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand had come today, and no knot had been counted, and the word stayed unspoken. He did not lay the cord across the half-beat or the half-beat across the thumb. Each shape kept its own place because he had put it there, and because the putting was the only work in this world that nobody could tax.

Nobody under this roof said he was a hundred and twenty-two days old. Nobody had said it at a hundred and twenty-one, or at a hundred and nineteen, or on the day he had come under the roof and a woman had carried him up the yard in a basket and told the fire what to do. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would have made the sentence mean what it would have meant in his mouth, and if there had been, nobody here would have known to say it over a basket. He had stopped expecting to be told. That was not the same as not wanting it. The wanting had gone quiet, the way a wound goes quiet when the nerve in it dies, and he had learned to tell the difference between the two the way he had once learned to tell a quiet abdomen from a relieved one, and the knowledge had not helped him. He was the only adult in this room and him with the hands of a child, and the count went on because there was nobody else to keep it.

He lay with the crust two inches from his ear and the thread frozen at his mouth and the wool riding back from the wrist, and he held the number where he had held the others, exact and unspent and spoken by nobody. A hundred and twenty-two stood where a hundred and twenty-one had stood in the morning. It had cost what all the others had cost and had been paid by nobody who would have known what a count of days was when they said it. The household kept its account badly in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, moving her lips along a shelf so the days stood as a shape no collector could take up, going around an empty place in a strip with a look and a wide step, letting the fire answer four low pulls and leave the fifth out. He kept his account the other way, one day laid edge to edge against the next in a file nobody could read and nobody had asked for. The more exact it grew the more it isolated him, and he had no hand with which to lay it down and no voice with which to offer it, and if he had had both he would not have offered it, because an account laid on a table in this district is an account that wants a payer.

The note of water over water came through the finger's-width gap and went on coming. The fire missed its fifth breath under the floor and went on missing it. The boards overhead kept four, four, and a knot. The three counts sat in three places and did not become one, and the payer of the missing breath remained unnamed through a hundred and twenty-two days of naming everything else badly, and the river went downriver toward the reed work that had gone down and come back without a figure. Out beyond the gap the bundles stood grey in the ruts with nobody counting them, and downriver the cord lay inside a man's coat with its knots unread, and in the next room the shelf kept its two gaps, and three doors down a tin stayed shut above a bed nobody used, and upriver a woman who watched got no answer from a basket. All of it held. None of it was joined.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down. It had gone up an inch toward the worked strip and come down, and toward the loaf shelf and come down, and toward a pot two paces off and come down, and now toward the river beyond the gap, and it came down. The hand was his and small and could close on nothing at two inches. In the other life it had been the hand of a man who could find a vessel in the dark by the note it gave under a knuckle, and it had closed on clamps and on a phone that held a name he would not look at, and had come back from the minivan with wool where a lit rectangle should have been. That hand was gone. This one went up an inch and came down, and the count went on with it.

One hundred and twenty-two stood where one hundred and twenty-one had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody who would have known what it counted when they said it. Six hearings stayed six beside and never among, and no seventh came, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came, and no knot was counted, and no third crust came into the fold, and the word stayed unspoken with its readable double turn kept without a name. The river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap. The fire missed its fifth breath under the floor. The two sounds sat in two separate places and the two counts did not meet. Nothing was joined, named, counted, paid or ended, and the count went on.
