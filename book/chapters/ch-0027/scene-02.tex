Back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the going of the light came down through the oiled paper in thin grey squares, and the house took \person[Corin]{the boy} in the way a cup takes water poured against its inside: carefully, and with a little left over that nobody entered. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down where no slate lay and, before she gave any instruction to anybody, gave it to the things. The bucket: stand and settle, you have come a long way up out of the dark and you will be finer in a moment. The broom: go around, it cost enough to come in and it will cost more to take out. The pot: don't set that near the door, you will pay for it in the thaw. The fire: feed it before anybody feeds themselves, because Garl's proverb had gone to a pot with no crack long ago and cost the more now that there was nobody to say it in his own voice. The instruction went into the bucket and the broom and the pot and the fire and nowhere near the basket, and the basket kept its level carry with the fold put right at his ear and never a word spent on the child inside it. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the boy, who had spent two lives learning how a person takes himself out of his own sentence, heard the taking happen and did not ask for the answer that never came.

She went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, the step widening by the width of a hand and coming back again, the basket steady through it all. Between the door and the bench she kept her half-pace from every open slate, the little-door burn closing on the handle and then on nothing. The river wind came under the eaves off \place{the Ilm} with the thin mineral edge the water leaves on everything it touches, and went under the fold at his ear and found the place it had come for. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool had ridden back from the bare joint in the going light and the cold found that place too, and went below the fold to the place where it always went. His head wandered and made the small wet sound and came back, and nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the sound. The thread at his mouth had frozen where it was and nobody had wiped it, because nobody had been instructed about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He was a man who could have told the temperature of a body by the back of a hand laid on its brow, and he could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him. Both things were true. The second was the one that was loud.

\place{the loaf shelf} stood in the next room, and the house kept its days there badly: loaves pushed along a board by their edges, a glance at the clay jar on the wall, a space at the row's end nobody called a figure, and a newer space the width of a palm nobody called anything at all. Sella stood before it and moved her lips along the row without a figure, the way a woman reads loaves by their edges and not by their number, and the two gaps kept their places in the row while her lips passed over them. The old gap at the row's end had come in with the planting, and had been named again in the yard that morning as a thing the planting brought in, and the new palm's breadth had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word, and the two of them stood side by side and were not one absence. The room the shelf stood in did not touch the room where the boy lay. In that other room one stiff brown-edged crust lay in the fold two inches from his ear with its own empty place beside it unnamed, and no third crust had come into it in a hundred and twenty days, and no third gap stood on the shelf. He had learned early what three likenesses in a row will do: laid one on another they grow a cause, and a cause wants a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one object he had spent two lives declining to become. Here the two rooms did not touch, and he kept them not touching, and the keeping cost him the only exactness in the district and bought him nothing anybody would take.

Her glance went once to the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and went back to the loaves, and rested there no longer than the glance required. Her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a shelf is not an opening and a loaf row is not a door and no opening had come under this roof in all his days. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned under the corner lay where it lay, and the new finer grey hairline beside it lay where it lay, and neither was swept. The house called both \lore{the thaw}, and the thaw would go when it went. The broom stood in the corner where it had stood through the planting month, and the instruction had gone to it twice, and the lines kept their places; going around was care, the yard said, and the yard had gone around these things every day of the month and had not once called what it did by its right name. No figure went with any of it. A neighbour come up from the strips that morning had asked what the reeds would cost now the hands were out of them, and Sella had sent her to the pot with \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it} --- the house, never \emph{I}, taking itself out of the sentence and leaving the question standing clean in it, and the answer had gone off up the yard in the woman's hands held level so nothing slopped. The boy set the sent-away question on a place of its own, and did not lay it beside the two gaps on the shelf or the gap in the field or the empty place at his ear, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause, and there are more ways of making a cause than there are hands to make them.

At the going of the light she carried him to his bed under the roof and not to \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him down the way a woman sets down a pot she has carried a long way and cannot bring herself to let go of. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted, and here they counted the breaths at his ear and entered the count nowhere. The hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- rested, and went, and did not look toward the face it had covered. That half-beat was the whole of what the house would allow itself, and it went out with the hand. He had read such half-beats before in chambers with better light, in the pause of a hand before a chart was turned, in the extra breath a nurse spent at a shoulder that had already been dressed, and had never once found a word that would carry what it meant into a slate. Here it arrived as a hand that would not let go quite soon enough, and he took it for what it was and set it nowhere, because a thing entered becomes a thing entered, and a thing entered can be called.

She went to the fire. “It was the thaw,” she told it, the way one tells a pot what it has been through and then leaves it to stand. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, lifting the blind ash and letting the air into the banked heat beneath and laying the ash back again, and the fire took the coal in its own time and gave back nothing a man could have taken up as a figure. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the keeping went into the fire and into the ash and nowhere into the room where the boy lay. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening, and the count went the way all her counts went, along the shelf and back to the same place twice, keeping the shape of the days as a shape no collector could take up as a number. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on the pot's rim and then on nothing as she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back from her temple with the same wrist; the grey streak there showed pale in the dying light off the coals, and she had said it came in the winter Garl died and would not discuss it further, and Garl's name had not been spoken under this roof in all his days. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out, handle toward the hand that would reach for it, and did not look toward the bed. The carried fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty days. Every warmth in this house had a payer somewhere, and no slate in the district carried the name.

The boy turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel, and found every object where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person; no seventh had come today and he had not invented one to stand in the empty place where a seventh would have stood. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on their own tray --- a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again --- and three intervals on another, with the half-beat of a checked stroke and the stroke that went on without it lying beside them as two objects and not a rule. The told bell lay beside the heard bell in two places that did not touch. The word forty lay unspoken with the readable double turn of a cord twisted twice beneath another beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and he had not said the word and no thumb had counted the knot. Four low things kept four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth that was not a hearing and had never been counted among the four. The two tellings of the shut counting room lay joined to no kiln, and the double bite of \lore{the damper} lay filed as an object spent on nothing, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man in one piece of iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. The held breath in a man's chest lay where it had lain, unspent, with the collector sentence nobody forged beside it and the loan that could hear still uncalled. The hand that had gone into the strip and had not come back out lay on a place of its own, entered in no slate, with no name, no cause, no body and no figure set at the foot of what the month had cost.

To these he added the day's two objects. The reed work had been named as a direction and a going-downriver and never as a figure, the bundles spoken of as armfuls when there were any to see and as glances when there were none, and a woman had been sent to the pot with a question and had carried the unanswered thing away held level in her hands. He set the direction on a place of its own and the sent-away question on another, and laid no finger across either, and laid no finger across the space between them. Two likenesses can be held apart by a hand that knows what a third would do. Three make a cause, and a cause says what happened when nobody has said it, and the hand that says it is never the hand that paid. He had been the hand that said it once in a better-lit room and had let a note lie turned down under the signature while he went on to the next body, and the shape of that had crossed with him into a country that had no word for it, and had stayed. Here he kept his places separate and did not make the joining, because the only exact account in the district was the one nobody could read, and the more exact it grew the more it isolated him, and isolation was a price he had learned to pay without flinching and without liking.

The door stood a finger's width open where it had stood through the planting month and through the flood and through the lean week, and the river's note of water over water came through the gap from beyond the yard, low and continuous and belonging to no vessel that would ever be tapped. Through the floor under the blind ash the carried fire gave its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and the two sounds came from two separate places and were kept in two separate places, and the three counts did not become one: the river's note, the fire's four, and the gap where a fifth pull should have been. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. He had counted them every night of his hundred and twenty days and would have counted them tomorrow, and nobody under the roof knew a number had moved. One hundred and twenty stood where one hundred and nineteen had stood in the small hours, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody who would have known what a number of days counted when they said it. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on quietly below the not-expecting the way the fire went on below the ash.

Cold came at cheek and temple and wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from the joint so the cold found the bare place and went under the fold at his ear. The dry brown-edged crust lay two inches away with its empty place beside it, and the thread at his mouth had frozen again in the going light, and his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound and came back. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. The river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor, and the two counts did not meet, and nothing was joined, named, paid or ended.
