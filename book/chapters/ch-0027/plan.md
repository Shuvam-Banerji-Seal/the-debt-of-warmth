## Scene 1 — A Direction, Not a Figure

**Day and time.** Day 120, Reed 1, Firstday, at the going of the light.

**Place.** The yard at Low Kiln and the edge of the worked strips east of it.

**Who is present.** Corin, carried; Sella Vail; unnamed field and yard workers; a woman come up from the strips with a question about the reeds. Berr Clay, Jorra, Tarn, Alda, Mira, Orra and Ulda are offstage. Dessa stays dead and unnamed to the boy.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{120}{Reed 1, Firstday, Y.H. 813}` at the opening. The planting month is behind; the seed is up and the hands are out of the strips. The yard turns toward the reed beds and the Weir. The first reed-cutting work is spoken of as a direction and a going-downriver and never as a figure, the way the planting was spoken of before it had a number, bundles to be counted in glances and armfuls when there are any.
2. The yard goes around its two kept lines --- the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw --- and around the space beneath the lowest rack, wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Nobody sweeps the lines and nobody enters the space.
3. The missing hand's place in the worked strip is still gone around with a look, a wide step and no number. T098 carried, not paid: no name, no cause, no body, no figure for what the month cost. Do not solve it, count it or give it a shape.
4. A woman come up from the strips asks what the reeds will cost now that the hands are out of them. Sella glances once at the clay jar and sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, the answer she gave in Seed, carried over into Reed (T058/T079).
5. The boy, one hundred and twenty days old and four months, carried at the half-pace from every open slate, reads the going-around of the field's empty place as a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody has opened --- exact, complete, worth nothing --- and sets it on a place of its own. He lays no likeness across the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the empty place at his ear; a fourth likeness would grow a cause. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch and comes down.

**Emotional turn.** The month turns without a boundary being marked: the work changes name from planting to reeds, but the empty place goes around exactly as it did before. The district's glance-counting and the boy's exactness meet again and neither buys anything.

**Sensory anchors.** Turned brown ground with seed up; river wind off the Ilm going downriver; the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it; the space under the lowest rack; a pot set down and left; wet wool; the small wet sound of the wandering head.

**Threads advanced.** T098 (carried), T071/T072 (120 exact and unspent), T009/T013/T092, T058/T079, T060/T086.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T098 gathers interest toward the later body or figure; nothing paid. No name, no cause, no body, no third gap, no cause made from a likeness, no ending.

**Exact last image.** The yard turning downriver toward the reed beds while the empty place in the worked strip is still gone around with a look and no number, Sella's level basket held at the half-pace from every open slate and the boy's hand going up an inch and down.

## Scene 2 — The Pot Has Said It

**Day and time.** Day 120, Reed 1, continued: evening into the going of the light.

**Place.** The Vail kiln-house, the loaf shelf in the next room, then the boy's bed under the roof.

**Who is present.** Corin; Sella Vail. No neighbour comes to the shelf today; all others offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella gives the bucket, broom, pot and fire their instructions before she gives them to anybody and never the basket, says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, goes wide around the space beneath the lowest rack as though it were a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and stands a half-pace from every open slate.
2. She moves her lips along the loaf row without a figure. The shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end, named again as a thing the planting brought in, and the new palm's breadth that came in the night of Seed 6 --- in a room that does not touch the fold's one stiff brown-edged crust and its unnamed empty place. No third gap.
3. She does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf is not an opening; she glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and gives nobody a figure. The clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and called the thaw.
4. At the going of the light she sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof has been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rests a half-beat longer than the setting needs before it goes away.
5. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, and pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale in the dying light. She sets the pot where it can be found in the dark and does not look toward the bed.
6. The boy turns out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, three intervals, the told bell beside the heard bell, the word forty unspoken, the hand that did not come back held on a place of its own. He adds the day's two objects --- the reed work named as a direction and the woman sent to the pot --- on places of their own and lays no finger across either. The carried fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty days.
7. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's note of water over water comes through the gap and the kiln's four, four and a gap comes through the floor under blind ash, the two sounds in two separate places and the three counts not become one. Above him the boards keep four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb.

**Emotional turn.** Tenderness arrives as the fold put right without a look at the face and the hand that will not let go quite soon enough; the household's arithmetic absorbs the new month without ever entering a figure, and the boy's separate places are the only exact account in the district.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke, tallow, clay dust, wet wool; the dry brown-edged crust two inches from his ear; the back of a broken pot turning coals under blind ash; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the grey streak pale in the dying light; the river's note at the gap and the missing fifth breath under the floor.

**Threads advanced.** T058/T079, T060/T086, T061/T082, T071/T072 (120), T009/T013/T092.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The shelf's two gaps and the fold's gap gather interest toward the far-off finger that will lay them together and call the joining an account; not his hand, not today. Nothing paid: no joining, no figure, no seventh, no ending.

**Exact last image.** His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down; one hundred and twenty stands where one hundred and nineteen stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, while the river goes over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire misses its fifth breath under the floor.

## Scene 3 — The Bite and the Seventh Year

**Day and time.** Day 121, Reed 2, Secondday, before first grey through midday.

**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried and set at the edge of the light); Jorra Vail; Tarn; Sella Vail; unnamed men who come before first grey (not followed, not named). Alda, Mira, Berr, Orra and Ulda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{121}{Reed 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}`. Before first grey men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and speak low in the room off the wheel-shed. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold; the boy does not enter it and no name is taken down.
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger --- not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel --- and keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around and entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
4. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The boy reads the iron once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object worth nothing joined to no kiln.
5. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction. His seventh year is pressed past the end of the planting and still held, the small bench knife at the very edge with six years in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone, the unforged collector sentence beside it and the breath from Seed 16 still unspent with a small interval at the top of each reach. To the kindling the account stays *Six counted. The next I don't count*; he wants the late thaw because late puts off the paying.
6. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check behind Jorra's standing finger, the stones still keeping the place where it stopped; neither man names the other's silence and the loan that can hear is uncalled. The boy sets the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat on the interval tray and lays no cause across them.
7. Jorra does not look toward the bed or into the basket; *he's not Vail* stays sealed and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn. Close on the two silences in one room with no hand joining them.

**Emotional turn.** Obedience arriving before the instruction that calls it is both obedience and a correction nobody will name; the damper's double bite is the truest thing in the room and the least spendable, and the equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal ground into the forearms; damp clay and tallow; the wheel's low note; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; broom bristles on flagstones; the small interval at the top of a reach where a breath should go in; the pale clip-ring holding what light there is.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, grey dust past the bent finger, broken slates under the rack, sealed sentence, promise ungiven), T062 (damper filed as an object joined to no kiln), T036/T076 (seventh year held past the planting, breath unspent, unchecking stroke held, loan uncalled), T061/T082, T071/T072 (121).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T036 toward the near-saying that stops short of calling the loan and the day something must give at the seventh year's end; T062 toward the later day the danger under the roof is named. Nothing paid: no name for the men, no calling of the loan, no fourth telling, no ending.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's bent finger standing in the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence while Tarn's bristles go on over the flagstones without the check, the stones still keeping the place where the stroke stopped, and neither man naming the other's silence.

## Scene 4 — The Half-Beat After the Knock

**Day and time.** Day 121, Reed 2, continued: midday and going of the light.

**Place.** The market stalls beyond the Cross; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried); the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing; a foundling glimpsed at the market's edge; unnamed market voices; a kiln-hand setting a pot down. Sella, Jorra and Tarn offstage or at the shed. Ulda unnamed and given no dialogue; Marra not in Sill; Dessa dead and stays dead.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing with his cuff grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. The market sells its reeds and eels over the top of him as though a man with a slate were a thing a landing always had; the steps come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that everybody walks over carefully without making a fall of it (T090/T080).
2. At the market's edge a foundling is glimpsed once, unnamed and silent, in cast-off clothes worn like a uniform with a man's shoulders and breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of his wrists and a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string at his throat that catches the light when he turns and goes dull when he turns back. He is not named in the narration and is given no dialogue; speak of him only as a foundling or a child.
3. When a kiln-hand sets a pot down, the child's head goes toward it a half-beat after the boy's, after the knuckle and after the note, then stops in the middle of the turn. He rubs the sliver twice with his thumb; his mouth opens on the tail of the kiln-hand's sentence and nothing comes out that any clerk could take down.
4. No loaf passes and no slate is seen. The boy files the delay as a thing that comes after the knock and is not a hearing, beside his own turning and the watcher's late turn, joined to neither, and does not call it a sale (T033/T034). The half-beat is held for the day it lands as the physical tell of a betrayal.

**Emotional turn.** A shape the district takes for a fixture of the landing turns late and says nothing; the boy keeps a delay on a tray of its own and refuses to make a cause out of a likeness, while the clerk with a slate sits ahead of a death the market sells over.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey cuff washed and grey again; a slate held to a forearm; silt pale in the creases of wrists; a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string turning light and dull; a pot set down; the half-beat after the note; the out-of-line step walked over carefully.

**Threads advanced.** T090/T080 (clerk still set for an uncalled measure, filed apart), T083 (landing walked over without a fall), T033/T034 (fifth glimpse, half-beat after the knock, no loaf, no slate), T071/T072 (121).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T033/T034 advances toward the sale and the betrayal that will land as the half-beat; nothing paid, no name for the child, no sale. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came; none of the walk-ons is Marra, Ulda named, Fenna or the watcher.

**Exact last image.** The foundling's head stopping in the middle of the turn a half-beat after the pot is set down, the green sliver going dull as he turns back, while the clerk at the landing holds his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called.

## Scene 5 — The Cord at the Weir

**Day and time.** Day 122, Reed 3, Thirdday, morning downriver at the Weir, going of the light under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The reed beds and the Weir downriver; the outer boards of the fish-trade shade below the north bridge; the boy's bed under the Vail kiln-house roof at the close.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried at the edge of the work where no slate lies); Sella Vail; Mira Silt; unnamed reed-cutters. Rusk spoken of only as the man who walks the Weir at dusk, not fully introduced. Orra may be glimpsed at the Weir beside her board and still watched, unnamed in the narration and not asked; she gives no answer from this bed. Dessa dead and stays dead, her tin seen and not opened; Marra not in Sill.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{122}{Reed 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}`. The reed-cutters work their first bundles into the yard, counted in glances and armfuls and never in a figure; nobody gives a number for what the reed work will cost.
2. At the Weir the boy, carried at the edge of the work where no slate lies, glimpses a reed-cutter's tally kept in a length of cord in a material no Court thumb will read. **Plant T099 here:** a third private record in the district, held for the far-off day it is laid beside Mira's grey cord and the second twist beneath it, Orra Weir's knot-string lying beside Mira's board and not touching it, Alda Reed's six hidden pages in the little tin turned down toward a floorboard, and Dessa Silt's pewter tin above the abandoned bed (T059/T025/T024, T023/T089). Plant only: no figure read, no knot counted, no name, no joining to the missing hand in the strip.
3. The cord is carried and not counted. It lies on a place of its own beside the other second records and does not touch them; the word forty stays unspoken, its readable double turn kept with no name laid on it. Orra is watched and not asked. Rusk is only the man who walks the Weir at dusk.
4. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it; she does not look into the basket, and her not-looking is a keeping set on a place of its own. No crust passes and no knot is counted.
5. At the going of the light under the roof the boy finds the new cord held beside the older objects, all still unread and unpaid, and closes on the count going on into Day 123.

**Emotional turn.** A third way of keeping a count appears in a material no Court thumb can read, and the district's second accounts begin, without anyone knowing it, to gather toward the day they will be laid together; the boy sees the shape and can spend none of it.

**Sensory anchors.** Cut reeds and their dry rustle; armfuls laid down; a length of cord carried and not counted; river silt dried pale at a wrist; eel slime in grey strokes on sleeves; salt at the Weir; a board lying beside a knot-string and not touching it; tallow and wet wool under the roof.

**Threads advanced.** T099 (planted), T059/T025/T024, T023/T089, T061/T082, T071/T072 (122), T098 (carried, not paid).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T099 planted for the convergence of the private records after Alda is gone; nothing paid. No figure read, no knot counted, no name, no joining to T098, no opening of tin or box, no word forty, no ending.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 28

The last image is the same reaching that closed Chapter 26, now three days further on and three days into Reed: the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the river's note and comes down with the count at one hundred and twenty-two and nobody under the roof knowing a number has moved. Chapter 28 opens on Day 123, Reed 4, Fourthday, with the boy one hundred and twenty-three days old, four months and three days, and the reed work a direction east and downriver of the Weir. Forward motion is carried in the objects planted and held here, not tied off: the reed-cutter's cord at the Weir is carried and not counted (T099 planted, unpaid); the missing hand's place in the worked strip is still gone around with a look and no figure (T098 carried); the loaf shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and the fold one crust and its gap in a room that does not touch them; six hearings stay six beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word forty unspoken, the loan uncalled and Tarn's seventh year still held and still not spent. Nothing was joined, paid, named or ended here, so Chapter 28 opens on the same held things one day further on, with the reed work going into the beds and something that must give at the end of the seventh year still not given.
