The afternoon came on in its own time, and the market went over the reading the way water goes over a stone --- not stopping for it, not going under, only showing the shape of the thing it passed and passing on. \place{The Cross} kept its two hundred finding-places; the goers-by leaned, straightened, leaned again. \place{The reading block under the moot-post} stood with its finger's width of shade on the north side, and the basket stayed where it had been set, at the edge where the reading came sideways and nobody looked down into it. \person[Corin]{The boy} took the square by its small sounds first, as he had taken it in the morning: rope under a pig's belly, the rasp of a slate laid flat on a board, the small flat knock of an iron rim on stone, a cooper's mallet checking a hoop that had been a hoop before and would be after. The small sounds did not lie. The large ones were mostly weather, and the afternoon's arithmetic was mostly weather too, going up off the wet boards in a thin steam and coming down as talk.

Nobody looked into the basket across the square. It was set where the reading came sideways and not into a face, and the two hundred went about their finding-places with the ordinary courtesy of a market, which is to leave a set-down thing alone. The boy knew the courtesy for what it was --- not kindness and not neglect, only the habit of a yard that had learned to step around what it did not want to own. He had stepped around such things himself in another life, in rooms where a chart lay face down and everybody went on with their counting.

Eight pigs went over the wet boards and no figure went with them. A drover with a stick across his shoulders drove them onto the boards and did not count them aloud; when a boy at the rail asked him how many there were he said, \emph{As many as went on}, and the boy at the rail went back to the rail with the answer he had been given and not with a figure. A pig put its head down and the boards answered hollow, and the drover's stick came down on the board and not on the pig, and the pig went on. Everything at the market was handled that way --- the thing and the space around it, with the stick falling where it would do the most good and the least harm. The assessor wrote what he wrote on a slate and the pigs went on, and the two hundred leaned toward nothing in particular once the block had done its work. The number that had gone twice over the basket in the morning was now in every mouth along the rail and in nobody's hand; it was a thing said and not a thing carried, and saying it cost nothing. Figures were brought to a heap of turnips and left standing side by side without being made to agree: a figure for the heap, a figure for the sack under it, a figure somebody remembered from a market three months back, each standing in its own place with a finger between, and no hand joining them. A man brought a number up out of a shut fist toward a slate at \place{the watch} and arrived nowhere with it --- fist to mouth to shoulder, the number looked at, the fist shut, the hand back in the sleeve. The slate at the watch kept its own figure and the man went off with his, and the two did not become one account. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost came back with a note and not a price; the note was for somebody else's hand and the price was where it had been, and the boy filed the direction and not the arrival.

\emph{Ask the pot}, a woman at the turnips said to the one who had been sent. \emph{It'll tell you sooner than that paper.}

The one who had been sent turned the note over as though a price might be written on the other side of it, and there was nothing on the other side. “It's a note,” she said.

“Aye. It's a note.”

That was the whole of the argument and it ended where yard arguments end, at the heap with the figures standing side by side. Nobody owned the afternoon and nobody made the heap agree with the sack or the sack with the remembered market. The covered grain at home did not know what it cost, and the arithmetic that would one day be read over it would not know either, and the ink-neat space between the figure and its price had no signature in it and carried no hand.

The boy took the half-beat at the setting apart as a surgeon takes a field apart --- by the place of each part, fist and eye and mouth and the interval in the gap, and the gap he gave no intention. An intention wants a payer standing under it, and none stood. A pot was set down on a board and a hand went toward it and stopped two inches off, arriving at the air and not at the clay; the hand had meant the handle and did not take it, and the space between the meaning and the stopping was the same space he had measured on every morning of his life under this roof. He knew the distance by the feel of it in a body that could not close it. He had once laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted and watched a resident's hand go to the third one and stop, and the space between the reaching and the taking had been the whole of that man's uncertainty; he had known it then the way he knew it now, by the shape of the pause. That was another room and another hand. It paid nothing here.

The surgeon read the hand as it stopped --- read the arrest, the small withdrawal, the set of the fingers that would go away as though they had never meant to come --- and the infant under the same skin wanted that hand on \place{the pot board}'s handle, wanted the weight of it and the warm iron under a palm, wanted the lift that would bring a cup within two inches of a mouth. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a verdict, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the one that had stopped in the air and gone away.

Across the square at the pot board the woman with clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails took up a cup, turned it a quarter without lifting it from the board and set it back where it stood; the sweetish green at one corner of her mouth stayed a colour joined to no ear and no name. At the market's edge the small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver at its throat and went dull, and turned its head a half-beat after a pot's knock and note and stopped in the middle of the turn. The boy's own head went toward the sound before he decided to turn it, and he filed that before the filing of the shape, both on places of their own. Neither was given a loaf, a slate, a sale or a name. The green and the shape stayed sightings on separate trays, with no eighth of either and no joining: a colour is not a name and a shape is not a house, and the two sightings of Ilmen 30 went on gathering interest on their separate places and were not spent on an afternoon that had earned its tenth.

The going of the light came on without a boundary drawn. It came the way cold comes down a chimney --- not in a fall but in a settling, laying itself first on the iron and lying longest where nobody had stirred it. \person{Sella Vail} came to him and carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate, up \place{the nineteen steps} and under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}. She said nothing to the basket of what had been read over it, and nothing of the figures left standing at the turnip heap, and nothing of the green and the shape, and nothing of the fourth subtraction gone up the road in a locked box. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; she did not tell the basket what the day had been and did not tell it to be still. A basket carried home is a pot carried home and set down, and no woman instructs the pot about the road. Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbers --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. The boy watched the counting from under the shelf's low angle and did not make a figure of it. A woman who counts spoons is not entering an account; she is keeping her hands occupied so that they will not do something else, and that is a thing a surgeon knows the shape of without being told. He had seen residents keep their hands busy in a room where somebody was dying, and it had meant nothing and everything, and he had never written it down.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the whole grammar came back at both ends of the day as though it had never been interrupted, which is the way a house has of being tender. Sella went to \place{the hearth} before the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and did not tell the basket, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the dry scrape of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

\emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it lay and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A morning, a shelf, a pot, a yard, a market, a reading, a basket, a bed and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and an Account-day did not make an eighth come due. The house took up its grammar as though nothing had turned, and the boy lay at the low angle of the shelf and watched it take it up.

She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. \lore{The keeping at the fold} lay in its empty place, found where put, still a finding and not a placing, with no third crust, no name, no gift and no payer. The crust stood horn-hard two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth was stiff in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

\person{Garl Vail} was in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door; the hollow wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody was allowed to fill; the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash}; the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline showing between staple and wire; the unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh notch at \place{the kindling} with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. His name had gone unspoken four hundred and thirty-four days and \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth had gone unpaid the same four hundred and thirty-four days, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Neither the debt nor the fifth pull was called, paid, forgiven or made to answer the other, and no lesson was made of any of it.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing and kept every gap a gap. Eight pigs, a heap of turnips with three figures standing on it, a fist that went toward the watch and came back, a note that was not a price, a cup turned a quarter and set down, a small shape that turned its head too late, a hand that stopped two inches off a handle: these he set out in the order they had come, and the order was the finding, not the sum. He did not add them. A surgeon who adds findings before the last one is in has already made up his answer, and the answer is usually the one he wanted before he cut.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s small flat knock outside on its own place; neither the knock nor the bell's told gap was made a seventh hearing. The heard bell stood beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three --- the heard bell's four and a gap, the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, the carried fire's four low pulls under the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The offered figure and its return, the well-asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, the reported second slate of the sixth, the hair-thin thaw of the seventh, the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions --- each stayed on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, and none was stood up as a rule, a witness, a tenth hand, a sixth shaping, a doctrine or a payer. A doctrine is an account that has found a payer; none stood.

Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure. Four were four and not one account. The first was not made a thirteenth by the three that followed; the second supplied no signature; the third no doctrine; the fourth no payer. The twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 he did not lay across the four to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand with no key is made even on paper and paper is not a payer. The figure read was still not the figure paid. \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s kindness said low to the block and his \emph{your house}, never I, stood on their own trays, believed and not owed; the Kindling collector's reason, the clean voice's missing name and the Long Night story's empty name went on gathering toward the far-off accounting and were not paid. \person{Tarn}'s valuable ear stood more exposed in an ended term with the loan uncalled; \person{Jorra Vail}'s promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn against the burned slate at five. The surgeon's unsent call to \person{Nina} stayed where it had been put, in a pocket that holds nothing, and was not paid again.

Answer four hundred and forty-four was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment. Six honest answers stood there, four hundred and thirty-nine through four hundred and forty-four, two to each of the three days, and not one was a doctrine. Four hundred and thirty-four exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-four days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire's fifth went unpaid and Garl's name went unspoken the same number, the man and the fire side by side and not one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. He stood no number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its low note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it and the tang thinner; the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room.

He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under \place{the boards} the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into an eleventh day not yet lived and not brought into this one.
