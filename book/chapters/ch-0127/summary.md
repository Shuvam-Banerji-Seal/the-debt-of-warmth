## Chronicle

The eighth and ninth of Silt were given over to the small honest work of a Vail household, and a chronicler of that yard would have set down nothing but pails and rope and grain. Under the loaf shelf the boy lay in the basket at the low angle of the heat, taking the field in the surgeon's old order: the crust gone into old horn two inches from his ear, the keeping at the fold found where put, the thread stiff at his mouth and unwiped. Cold had stopped at cheek and wrist as at the end of a written line. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle; the surgeon knew the wants useless and the infant wanted them all the same, and the quarrel went on without a verdict. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket at both ends of each day, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot's back, kept the charcoal unread beneath her finger, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first with the hairline showing, and set the rim two inches beyond his hand. Tarn kept six pieces where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, and his sentence about an ear worth more than the debt stopped at the word that would call the loan. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel and withheld the full note from any master who would sing a pot before its time. No market came, no bell, no collector, and nothing from the sixth's report or the seventh's thin thaw crossed under the roof.

On the tenth Sella set the basket in the moot-post's shade where no slate lay and stood half a pace off, so the reading came sideways. Berr Clay came up off the clay road with his office cord folded under his coat, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, read *one in twelve* twice over the basket, called the levy and denied it with *your house*, never *I*, said something low to the block about kindness, and went off with the fourth subtraction locked in his box and the key gone up in a coat pocket. Four readings of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure: four were four and not one account, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Hessa Rale went up before noon with the slate edge-on to her arm and the writing turned to her sleeve, and did not read it. The market went over the reading the way water goes over a stone. At the close Sella carried him home and righted the fold, and the hand came up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake, held it on the note's finishing and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool. There was no next hand in the room.
