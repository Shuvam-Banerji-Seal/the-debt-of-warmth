\daymark{434}{Silt 10, Y.H. 814, Tenthday}

The small hours of the tenth of \place{Silt} came down under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} the way cold comes down a chimney --- not in a fall but in a settling, laying itself first on the iron and lying longest where nobody has stirred it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand outside the wool flat where the going of the light had left it, on answer four hundred and forty-two, with four hundred and thirty-three days behind him exact and six months and two hundred and fifty-three days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. There was no next hand in the room. The tenth had not been brought into the ninth. It came on in its own dark with its own work waiting on it, the way a case waits on a surgeon before the light is good enough to trust.

He took the body in the old order, as a surgeon takes a field off a towel: the crust first, brown-edged and gone into old horn, standing two inches from the ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it holding \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put and still a finding, with no second fold, no third crust and no name; the thread at the lip stiff in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Three things found as they had been left and none the better for the finding. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with no order to go farther and no order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and thirty-three days; through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, ending on its own finishing. Three counts in three places, and he did not lay them together, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

He knew a face composed by cold is not composure; that pink returning is the surface warm over a core still cold; that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried and never rubbed. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. The knowledge was exact and belonged to a man with a name in another country; the body it was spent on could not lift its own head. Under the exactness the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on \place{the pot board}'s handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel went on without a verdict, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood.

Nothing from the sixth or the seventh crossed under the roof in the dark. The reported second slate stayed a shape in a mouth on a place of its own, and the hair-thin grey called \lore{the thaw} stayed where the broom had never gone, called weather, not a sign and not a mending. The neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf stayed a reaching on its own place, not joined to the arithmetic the morning would bring. An Account-day was a date on a slate, not a hand under any of them; he did not make it answer a reaching, a report or a grey.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she had at both ends of every day since he came under the roof. She told the coals it was the thaw and did not tell the basket, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped with the dry sound of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it lay and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A morning, a shelf, a pot, a basket and an Account-day are not openings; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and a tenth day did not make an eighth come due.

Before first grey she carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. She did not tell the basket what the day was and did not tell it to be still. Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the dark still held \place{the seven walked-around places}, and she went around them with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place} where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come out. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign; a sign would want somebody standing under it to say what it meant, and nobody stood.

At \place{the Cross} the market had begun to assemble in the cold before the top of the morning: boards going up, a rope of pigs somewhere behind the square, the first of the two hundred finding places to lean. \place{The reading block under the moot-post} stood with its finger's width of thin shade on the north side, and Sella set the basket there, where no slate lay, the cold iron of \place{the moot-post} at his cheek, and stood half a pace off. The boy was set where the reading came to him sideways and not into his face; the basket was never looked into. She gave no instruction and no reason to the wicker. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and nobody under any roof instructs a basket about what goes over it. From the low angle the square came by its small sounds first --- the rope under a pig's belly, the rasp of a slate laid flat on a board, the small flat knock of an iron rim on stone, a cooper's mallet checking a hoop. The small sounds did not lie and the large ones were mostly weather.

\scenebreak

Eight pigs went over the wet boards and no figure was called aloud for any of them. The assessor wrote what he wrote on a slate and the pigs went on, and the two hundred leaned toward the block. A neighbour passed the coming number from mouth to mouth along the rail without making a figure of it, and the figure was not yet in anybody's mouth.

By the time the top of the crowd had begun to lean, \person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} to the block with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks that a number is still where he put it.

Then he read low into the goers-by, \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says}, and sent the figure twice over the basket --- once for the moot and once for the households that came late --- never looking into it. It was the first reading in Silt and the fourth of the year. He called the levy and denied it. \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me}; \emph{Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch}; \emph{If your household has heard otherwise, your household has heard wrong} --- \emph{your house}, never I --- taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the work clean and the hand that did it nowhere in it. To a man who had brought a number up out of a shut fist he did not give the number back; the man was sent to a slate at \place{the watch}, and the number went from the fist to a mouth to a shoulder going up the road and arrived nowhere.

Something low he said to the block about kindness being entered, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs, and the block answered nothing. There was no anger in it and no triumph, and no payer stood under it for the boy to file; the sentence went into the stone and stayed there as a stain stays in a board.

The boy took the order of the reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart --- the thumb wiped before the slate was touched, the finger passed over the figure through the sleeve before the eyes went to the goers-by, the sentence said with the speaker taken out of it before the dressing went on --- and filed it on a tray of its own beside the other record-first hands, with a finger between it and the face of the man from \place{Slate Row} filed and not counted and another between it and the runner's eighth that would come up the road before noon. No tenth stood up in the empty place. He made no doctrine of the twice-wiped thumb and no payer of the man who had wiped it; he kept the order as an order, the way a surgeon keeps a hand that was in a field and went away again.

Berr went off up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and its writing turned to his sleeve, the box locked and Silt's fourth true subtraction riding inside, believing he had done a kindness. The key went up in a coat pocket that never comes back down, and there was no key under any roof. The boy watched the going as he had watched the coming, by the order of the parts, and filed it where it belonged and not elsewhere.

Four readings of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure. Four were four and not one account. The first was not made a thirteenth by the three that followed; the second supplied no signature; the third no doctrine; the fourth no payer. The twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 he did not lay across the four to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand with no key is made even on paper and paper is not a payer. The figure read was still not the figure paid. The arithmetic of this morning went up the road in the locked box beside the three before it, and the boy set no mark on the space where a signature would stand. He did not bring the reading down onto the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, or onto the reported second slate of the sixth, or onto the hair-thin thaw of the seventh; an account that answers a reaching wants the hand that reached standing under it, and none stood. All three stayed where they lay with a finger between every pair.

\scenebreak

The bell went four, four and a gap through the market, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, its gap not folded into the carried fire's four and a gap under the floor, into the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the four readings behind one unsigned figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s small flat knock outside on its own place, and neither the knock nor a told gap was made a seventh hearing.

\person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was, the second strap let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud unbrushed on the shortened skirt. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist came first, her hand to the slate before her eyes to the crowd. To a woman at the rail who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}; and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}. She gave no figure, read nothing aloud and did not look into the basket, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. Four figures going up the road did not make her a reader; her eighth record-first hand stayed eight with no tenth, joined to none --- not to the twice-wiped thumb up the road, not to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, not to the inward slate at the landing. The reported second slate of Silt 6 stayed a shape in a mouth on a place of its own; it was not on stage, was not hers to read and was given no figure by four figures or by any road.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing}, where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called, his cuff soft from washing and folded back twice and grey from washing, his slate turned inward and his hand to the slate before his eyes to the stalls. He was a shape brought up by word and not a sighting from the basket, reported and not witnessed, and nobody under any roof had seen his face. Across the square at the pot board a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the sweetish green of taste at one corner of her mouth staying a colour joined to no ear; at the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver and went dull, a shape with no name and no house. Neither was given a loaf, a slate or a sale. The green and the shape stayed sightings on separate trays, with no eighth of either, and he called the colour green and the shape a shape and nothing more. A colour is not a name and a shape is not a house.

\person{Tarn} and \person{Jorra Vail} were offstage at \place{the kindling} and \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, their keepings found where put and not repeated on a morning that had earned only its tenth. Tarn's six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside the unforged collector sentence; the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and no hand went over blade and sill a second time, and the sentence about an ear under this roof worth more than the debt that held it had stopped at the calling word and stayed stopped, the loan uncalled in a term already ended and the ear exposed rather than safe. Jorra kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, his bent finger taking the cold out of \place{the damper} ahead of an unfinished sentence and coming away without a second holding; the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions. The boy kept the two quiet men on places of their own under the one roof, maintenance and not a compact, and made no cause, custom or joining of them.

\person{Garl Vail} was in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door; the hollow wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody was allowed to fill; the twice-mended pot on the board with the hairline showing between the older brass staple and the tighter wire, both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person; the unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh notch at the kindling with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. His name had gone unspoken four hundred and thirty-four days and the carried fire's fifth had gone unpaid the same four hundred and thirty-four days, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Neither the debt nor the fifth pull was called, paid, forgiven or made to answer the other, and no lesson was made of any of it.

When the morning's reading was done Sella carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate, up the nineteen steps, never into \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him on the bed under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put, still a finding with no third crust, no name, no gift and no payer; the crust stood horn-hard two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, and the two did not become one count, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note under the winter skin --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let it go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat on the fold on answer four hundred and forty-three, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. The answer was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, laid across neither the four readings, the figure sent twice over the basket, the man's number that arrived nowhere, the kindness said low to the block, the bell's gap, the runner's eighth going up unread, the clerk's figure reported from below the bridge, the green and the shape on their separate trays, the neighbour's near-reaching, the reported second slate, the hair-thin thaw nor any other kept thing. No sixth hearing, tenth hand, eighth opening, sixth shaping, thirteenth arithmetic, signature, payer or reader stood on a day that had earned its tenth.

Four hundred and thirty-four exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-four days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The fourth subtraction rode up the clay road in Berr's locked box with the key gone up in a coat pocket that does not come back down, and the figure read over the basket was still not the figure paid. The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side and neither bought the other; under the roof the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place, forward into a market afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.
