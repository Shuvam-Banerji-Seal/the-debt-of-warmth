\daymark{432}{Silt 8, Y.H. 814, Eighthday}

\dropcap{T}{he small hours} of the eighth day came down under the Vail roof the way silt comes down through still water --- not in a fall but in a settling, gathering first at the skirting and lying longest where nothing stirred it, finding the thin places along the boards before it found the middle of the room. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and thirty-one exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. This was an eighth morning, and an eighth is only an eighth. Nothing had crossed into it from the days behind --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The yard had said what the yard said and the weather had been weather, and both had gone where they went without leaving an instruction under this roof. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its low note of \lore{water over water} under its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer, and nobody in the room gave the softness a name.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it six nights ago and would not try now. He could not lift it. He could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

He had once put a crust within reach of a man whose hand had stopped obeying him, thinking it a kindness, and watched the fingers come up an inch and fail; the crust had gone hard through the night and nobody in that room had thought to take it away. There had been a chart there and a thermometer and a man who would have known what a number counted. Here there was a woman who counted spoons. The two facts stood where they stood and neither bought the other, and the surgeon's register went on taking the body in its order and wrote down nothing that a Court could read.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping; all three were true and the wanting went on all the same. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on in the dark --- the old man's exactness with its instrument laid down, the small animal body with its whole unspeakable impatience, and no verdict between them, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He could not speak a word of \lore{Grathic} and had no way to ask for any of the three; the wanting was therefore complete and therefore useless, and that was the whole of the work.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as a table straightens when a weight is lifted off it. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and did not tell the basket, and said it in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on an Eighthday that had earned only its eighth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. \person{Garl Vail}'s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. The pot stood where she set it, and the warmth in it went the way warmth goes in a cold room --- out, slowly, by the whole of its surface, and faster where the metal met the air.

\scenebreak

She carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at the turn of the landing before the wicker settled, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. In \place{the Clay Yard}, Low Kiln, Sill, Grath, she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and went back to the small honest work. She went once around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back out. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The step was a keeping and not a sign. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf's stopping and not laid across the ridge at \place{the Vail corner}. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated; level is not honest and a level is not a promise.

The small sounds came first, and he took them first because they did not lie. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock that had no note in it and wanted none. The rope at \place{the yard well} settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it, going quiet when the weight was taken; the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist and eye and mouth and the half-beat in the gap. The fist was the part that arrived; the eye was the part that checked; the mouth was the part that said or held its peace. Between them stood the half-beat, a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist and put it back into a sleeve, the number arriving nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The figure read over the covered grain did not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between figure and price carried no signature. Walk-ons came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Eighthday grammar, and none looked into the wicker or gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights; a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. There was no market, no \place{Cross}, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its eighth.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, and the stack kept its own arithmetic of four, four and a space, with the space not a fifth and not filled. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}; the debt circled against a firing of tiles and was worse for being uncalled. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. His chest had been tight since the dark. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away the whole of the checking.

\emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he said to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not made a habit, a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; a second would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood. \emph{Yes, house}, he said to the fire, and did not turn his head toward the landing. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was; he took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and the sixth piece stood where the house could check it.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. There was no Grathic word for the two bites of one piece of iron and he gave the house neither. He did not look at Sella while she spoke and moved before she had finished, which in that house was how a son answered a mother and a keeper answered a fire. The two quiet men kept their places under one roof a fifth time, their silences maintenance and not a compact; the boy made no rule and no joining of what he saw. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, gathering against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions without being called or paid.

\scenebreak

When the morning's work was done Sella came to the hearth first and carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. She set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, horn-hard in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. The pot stood where she had set it, its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with the half-beat unmeasured and no look toward the bed. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

Four hundred and thirty-two exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-two days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; his name had not crossed a threshold under this roof in four hundred and thirty-two days and no account had gone up the road to call his debt. \person{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood, her strip in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth; the offered figure and its return kept their place beside her hand and the fifth shaping with a finger between every pair, not offered again, not made a custom and not joined to anything. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road} with her eighth hand eighth, and the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure stayed a report brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. \person{Berr Clay} was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed. No sixth shaping, seventh hearing, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic or eighth opening stood on a day that had earned only its eighth, and no slate had been opened within reach of the basket. He stood no number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure, for paper is not a payer, and a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth.

The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place, and the hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and thirty-nine --- forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.
