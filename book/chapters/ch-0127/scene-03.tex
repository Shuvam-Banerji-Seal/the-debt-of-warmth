\daymark{433}{Silt 9, Y.H. 814, Ninthday}

The hand came down flat on the fold on answer four hundred and forty and stayed there as a man leaves a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and there was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and thirty-two exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-two days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth had stood, and through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its low over lower under the winter skin; the two measures went on without agreeing, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

A Ninthday came on without a boundary drawn. There was no stroke of a bell to part it from the dark, no hand at a slate, only the same light lying at another angle on the same boards, finding the same knot in the near plank and the same four nails and the thumb-width space where a fifth nail had not been driven. The small hours went on, and first grey came up the yard the way cold comes up from a cellar --- not in a rush but by degrees, laying itself along the skirting, then \place{the pot board}, then the rim of the basket. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, awake because he had been awake and would stay so until somebody lifted him. The room smelled of banked coals, woodsmoke and wet wool, and under them the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} at the unswept corner --- the smell of a room holding its heat the way a cup holds water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still.

He took the body in the old order because the old order was the only honesty left to him. One brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, its corner a hair off the wool. He had watched it harden the way a man watches a dressing go stiff at the edge, and he knew it would not nourish him and would cut the mouth if it were pushed. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put again in the grey --- a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift and no payer. A hand had put it there on the fifteenth and had not come back to place it anywhere else, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places.

The thread at his mouth had stiffened further in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it. A face composed by cold is not composure. A cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, slow and from the outside and never rubbed, and that he could not ask for it; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won and neither settled anything, and the quarrel went on in the grey without a verdict, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood.

He had stood once in a warm room where they told themselves the man on the table was warm because the room was warm, and the man's fingers had gone the colour of old rope under the lamp, and nobody had thought to look past the surface that had come back pink. The room had insisted. The fingers had said nothing. He had learned to read a body by what the room refused to say, and here under the loaf shelf the house kept its own heat in the same way, insisting by its very order that a warm house meant a warm child, and keeping four pulls under the floor with the fifth unsupplied.

At \place{the pot board} the barley lay wall to wall without a number. \person{Sella Vail} took up a fist of it in her hand and weighed it there, judging it by the hand that carried it, and set it back where it had lain and said no figure. A fist judged by the hand that carries it is a finding; a figure put to it would want a payer standing under the figure, and none stood. He read the house by what it would not say rather than by what it would not survive: it would not say the number of the barley, would not say the name of the man whose coat kept its crease behind the door, would not say why the corner had gone unswept two winters, and went on warm and orderly all the same. That was the whole of the grammar, and it was enough to keep a child alive and not enough to tell him what any of it cost.

\scenebreak

Sella came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as a table straightens when a weight is lifted off it. There was no announcement. A woman who had once said \emph{The house will keep it} where another would have said \emph{I} had long since given up announcing anything to a basket that could not answer; she put her hand to the fire first and let the rest of the room wait its turn. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and she did not tell the basket. “You'll want more than that come the drying,” she said to the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. The back of the pot scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that had done this work before and expected to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps; a gap measured is a gap with a payer put under it, and none stood.

Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. No hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered; a Ninthday morning was not a day for reading what a woman had put under a shelf years ago and had never once counted aloud. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand --- two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold --- closed on air and went back to the pot. The glaze on the jar was cold enough to hold a finger against; it gave back no warmth and asked for none.

“\emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled},” she said to the pot. “\emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one},” she said to the fire, and never to a person. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and the hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing so that the next woman who takes it up can read that it was carried. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under the wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A morning, a hearth, a shelf, a pot and a going down of the steps are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due because the day had a Ninthday name. She took the basket up as she took up the pot, without a word to it, and carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket, and the basket was not told what hour it was or told to be still. It was set out of the draught in \place{the Clay Yard} where the heat came to it sideways and nobody would have chosen that place if they had known what a basket was.

Sella went once around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice. Widest was the step around \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back out. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The step was a keeping and not a sign; a sign would want somebody standing under it to say what it meant, and nobody stood. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf's stopping and not laid across the ridge. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated, and level is not honest and a level is not a promise.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, the hairline called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. Beside it the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between, a thing left in place not a thing forgiven. Both were found where put and called weather. Sella did not look twice and did not sweep; the boy did not go around them again and did not make the finding a custom, a fourth covering, a sixth shaping, a sign or a payer. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the covering; the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing. The softness was found where put and called weather, and nobody owned the day.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones} \person{Tarn} kept six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. His chest had been tight since the dark and he had not once taken his hand from a tool to rub it. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill; his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. The coming away was the whole of the checking. In the tone of a man stating the weather he told the wood, \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, once, and left it there. It was found where put and was not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not a hearing and not a rule; a second silence would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on over the flagstones without a word about the day or the debt.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, at the very end of a term already ended. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent. The loan was uncalled, and a term ending was not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom; it left a man more exposed than it had found him, his valuable ear a thing that could hear and had never been called, the fear of being entered again for vagrancy still his without a figure, and the want he did not voice still there for a slate with his own name and no mark against it. He did not turn his head toward the landing. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was, and the boy, watching from the basket, made no rule and no joining of the two quiet men under the one roof.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door and took \place{the Vail wheel-shed} edge with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold; the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said to it, and to a pot he said “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her unfinished sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. There was no Grathic word covering the two bites of one piece of iron and he gave the house neither. The promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions going on gathering without being called or paid; \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed. His eyes went to the door, to the fire, to the wheel and came back without reporting, and the household straightened around his silence as it straightened around hers.

When the morning's work was done Sella came to the hearth first and carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. She set him on his bed under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf, never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, horn-hard in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped. The pot stood where she had set it, its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with the half-beat unmeasured and no look toward the bed. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, the boards kept four and four and a knot, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin; three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

\person{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood, her strip in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth; \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up the clay road with her eighth hand eighth; the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure stayed a report brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. \person{Berr Clay} was gone up the road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed. Nothing from the eighth had come under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and Silt 10 waited one morning off as a date and was not brought. No sixth hearing, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eighth opening, sixth shaping or thirteenth arithmetic stood on a Ninthday that had earned only its ninth, and no slate had been opened within reach of the basket.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under its winter skin --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let it go as the note ended and came straight down and lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and forty-one, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place, forward into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning. Four hundred and thirty-three exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-three days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, answer four hundred and forty-one laid on the sixth place with the count at four hundred and thirty-three and no payer named under either.
