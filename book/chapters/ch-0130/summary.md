## Chronicle

Three days went under the Vail roof, and the house took them as it takes all days: in loaves and glances. On Silt the seventeenth Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, not the basket, kept the charcoal beneath the shelf unread, lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first, and set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. The widow's mark hung where it hung; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule. Tarn counted six at the kindling and held the seventh at the lip, his hand over blade and sill and away. Jorra came in with slate dust white in a fist-crease, spoke to the wheel before any person, and put his finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished word. The boy lay under the loaf shelf and took each thing apart by the place of it, adding nothing together.

On the eighteenth a man waiting his turn at the yard well opened a shut fist — not toward the water but toward the clay road going up to High Kiln — looked at the light, shut the fist and put it back into his sleeve. The number arrived nowhere. He gave no name, opened no slate within reach of the wicker, passed no money and stood in no relation to the boy, and said nothing of it to the basket. He filed the direction by the place of each part on a place of its own beside the fist-to-nowhere arrivals and the figure Ilva had once offered and taken back, a finger between every pair: not a second offer, not a custom, not a payment, not a reading, and not the calling of a loan. Ilva drew at the well with her strip of notched birch in her sleeve, and no figure came off the wood.

On the nineteenth the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey a hair less distinct along a second stretch where no broom had gone in two years, the sour tang of Ash thickened in the dry cold; through the finger's-width door the river air was softer at one edge, where the low note ran nearer under the winter skin. He found both where put, did not look twice and called them weather. Sella told the fire the same thing and told the basket nothing. The three wants — crust, thread, a hand on the pot — quarrelled without a verdict, both true, the second louder. Six honest answers were filed, two to each day, each worth nothing at two inches of air; the count went to four hundred and forty-three exact, told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire's fifth went unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number, and the river went on with its low note under its winter skin.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** the small hours of Silt 19 close the chapter with the boy on his bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf under the Vail roof, the hand outside the wool coming up an inch toward the river's note (the inch not a reach), taking a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, holding the interval whole on the note's finishing, letting it go and coming straight down to lie flat on the fold on **answer 462** as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There is no next hand in the room. He does not wipe the thread and does not lift the crust. Under the boards the four low pulls keep their silence where the fifth should have stood; the two greys at the corner keep their finger's width and the soft edge at the door keeps its own place.

- **Day and time at the end:** Day 443, Silt 19, Y.H. 814, Ninthday, the small hours. Count **443 exact**, six months and two hundred and sixty-three days, told by nobody. Six honest answers **457–462** stand on the sixth place, two to each of the three days, not one a doctrine; 462 answers stand there in all. The carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same four hundred and forty-three days.

- **Where everyone is:** Sella Vail under the roof after the last hearth grammar of Silt 19, having carried him down to the Vail corner and back at the half-pace; Tarn offstage at the kindling and flagstones, his keepings found where put; Jorra offstage at the wheel-shed edge and damper, his keeping found where put; Garl Vail in objects only; Ilva offstage with her notched birch strip in her sleeve and her fear unpaid. No market, Cross, bell, Account-day, called collector or runner on any of the three days.

- **Body and condition:** four hundred and forty-three days old, carried everywhere, unranked, a cracked low-capacity vessel; horn-hard crust two inches from the ear with its corner hair-lifted, keeping found where put in the empty place, thread stiff in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and bare wrist-joint, the Afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody here will perform. The three wants quarrel with the register without a verdict: both true, the second louder, neither winning. No wound, no gain, no rank change, no power used, no money.

- **Items, money, powers:** nothing gained, lost or bought; no purchase, sale, bond, loaf passed with a figure, or slate opened within reach of the wicker on any day. No usable Warm, no clamp, no experiment, no Grathic; counting is an old-life habit and not a gift. Four readings of the year still stand behind one unsigned figure, the true subtractions locked in Berr Clay's box up the clay road, the key gone up in a coat pocket.

Jorra's promise to the men who burn slates stays neither given nor withdrawn against the burned slate at five. The keeping at the fold stays a finding, not a placing.
