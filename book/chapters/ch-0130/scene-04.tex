The afternoon of the eighteenth went on at the small work of an ordinary Eighthday, and \person[Corin]{the boy} was set down in \place{the Clay Yard} at \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, where the coming and going of feet would not touch the wicker and out of the straight draught, within the reach of \person{Sella Vail}'s eye between her pail and her broom. \place{The yard well} stood at its own place with its stone lip worn to the colour of old bone on the side where the pail had been lifted ten thousand times and left the other side in the shade, and \place{the flat stone by the well} kept the flat knock of a pail set down and the small rasp of a rope settling into its knot, cold-stiffened in the dry air. He took those sounds before the larger ones, because a small sound does not lie about what made it and a large one is mostly weather. The basket was not told the hour. It was not told to be still. A basket set down is a pot set down, and no woman explains the afternoon to a pot.

A man stood waiting his turn at the well with his back to the basket's low angle, a coat the colour of wet ash hanging off him and \lore{Ash} grey worked into the crease of the sleeve nearest the wicker. He was a shape with a turn coming and no errand the boy could see; boots, a sleeve, a hand. The hand was shut. It had been shut since the eye found it, and the knuckles had the look of a thing holding something and not yet decided whether to open or keep. Nobody went to him. Nobody asked him what he wanted. A woman passed with a pail and went around him as the yard goes around a stone it has decided not to move, and a man called something about the weather, and the weather did not answer.

The man opened the fist.

Not toward the water. The knuckles went the other way, up and out along the line of \place{the clay road} as it climbed toward \place{High Kiln}, and the thin light of the afternoon went over the opened fingers and showed nothing lying in the palm. He looked at the light as though the light had been asked a question and might answer it. It did not. The fist shut. The hand went back into the sleeve, and the number that had faced the road arrived nowhere.

Fist open. Eye to light. Fist shut. Back into the sleeve. Nothing passed.

The boy lay in the wicker and took the direction apart by the place of each part, as he took everything apart that he could not ask about: fist toward the road, eye to the light, fist shut and back into the sleeve, and the half-beat in the gap where the looking had been. There was no intention in the gap. An intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood, so the gap stayed a gap. He knew the order of reaching the way another man knows the feel of a tool in the dark --- hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both --- and he had watched that order in a midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure, in a clerk with hand to slate before eyes to a stall. Here the mouth had not opened at all. Hand, eye, hand. A finding, not a placing.

No slate was opened within reach of the wicker. No figure was given. No money passed. The man gave no name and entered no room and stood in no relation to the boy, and the boy was not entered by him. He was a shape at a well with a fist in it, and the fist had faced a road and come back.

A pace along the well \person[Ilva]{Ilva} drew with the pail against her knee, the rope giving its small rasp, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer rack dust worked into the seam of one thumb she did not scrub out. Her hand went flat over the fold in her sleeve before her eyes went anywhere, and the short strip of notched birch lay where she had put it, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted, not shown; no figure came off the wood. She was not the speaker of any word that came up at this well. The direction had come near her and had not made her a reader, and the count stayed in wood and the fear stayed unpaid. She went on with the pail.

\scenebreak

The boy put the direction on a place of its own.

He had a place for the fist that came up out of a shut hand toward the water or the light and went back without a number, and a place beside it for the figure Ilva had once offered toward the strip and taken back into the sleeve, and he laid this new direction beside those two with a finger between every pair. It was not a second offer. It was not a custom, for a custom wants a hand to repeat it and none had. It was not a payment, not a reader made, not a sale, not a sixth shaping, not the calling of a loan in a term already ended. He did not join it to the well-asking that had been answered with the weather, or to the figure \person{Berr Clay}'s clerk had entered on an inward slate before he looked up --- that figure had come up the yard as a report from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} and had never been a sighting --- or to the neighbour's near-reaching at the loaf shelf, or to the reported second slate of Silt 6, or to the two sightings on their separate trays at the market. Five kept things and a direction, six places with a finger between every pair, and no account standing among them.

The direction gathered interest the way a thing left where it lies gathers Ash, slowly and without a hand to own it, toward the far-off hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together and call the joining an account: the charcoal beneath the shelf, the knot-string, the six pages, the tins, the inward slates, the strip of notched birch. That hand was not his and had not come. It bought him nothing and bought the man at the well nothing. Costs went away as weather in this yard, and the weather kept no ledger.

His register supplied the shape and took the name off it, read by where the fist had stopped and not by what anybody might have called the stopping. A likeness is not a cause. A number counted is a number with air around it, and air buys no warmth. He had once stood at the head of a table and named instruments in the order they would be wanted and named them again as they came away, and here there was no table and no case and no next hand to take the tool from his own. He had finished everything on his plate in another country and could not lift a crust two inches in this one, and the two facts stood on separate places and had never been entered against each other.

The small body wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the handle of the pot. The wanting went on under the filing the way water goes on under its skin, and he did not settle it, because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the one that could not close two inches of air.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella came to the wicker and carried him up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace, taking the tilt out of the turn at the landing before the wicker had settled, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot and the fire and never to the basket. She said nothing to the basket of the man at the well or of the direction he had put into the road and taken back. A woman does not explain the afternoon to a pot, and she did not explain it to the basket.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the household straightened around the order of it as a table straightens when a hand lifts a weight off one corner. She went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, and did not tell the basket, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the dry scrape of iron over stone going into the boy's ear ahead of the warmth and leaving the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps; a figured gap wants a payer standing in it and none stood. Her finger went along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread, no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count and wanted no answer. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; an Eighthday going of the light is not an opening and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its eighteenth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; the pot was mended and was not made whole.

\person[Garl Vail]{Garl} was in the room the way he had been in it for four hundred and forty-two days: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat in the shed that nobody was allowed to fill, the hairline standing between staple and wire, the unforged collector sentence at the kindling with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. His laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she lifted the dressed grain for damp and turned it piece by piece, each piece with a touch a little lighter than the one before, lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and laid it back where it had lain under wool with no figure on it and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

At the low angle of the loaf shelf the one stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, and the empty place beside it held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping was found where it had been put.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three; nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth, and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. \person{Tarn} and \person{Jorra Vail} were offstage at the kindling and the wheel-shed with their keepings found where put, and nothing from the afternoon's direction had come under the shed or to the kindling as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap and did not add them, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. The man at the well stood on his own place: fist toward the road, eye to the light, fist shut and back into the sleeve, the half-beat in the gap, and nothing under any of it but weather. The direction stood beside the fist-to-nowhere arrivals and the offered figure and its return, and beside the second asking, the face from \place{Slate Row}, the reported second slate, the clerk's entered figure, the neighbour's near-reaching and the two sightings on separate trays, every pair with a finger between, none a reader, a witness, a tenth hand, a custom, a payment or a payer. The withheld sentence of Silt 15 stood where it had been put and was not said again on a day that had earned only its eighteenth; the hair-thin thaw of Silt 16 and the softer air through the door stood on their own trays and were not called to answer the direction. A likeness is not a cause, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven.

\person{Hessa Rale}'s reported second slate stood a shape in a mouth and not a sighting, and her record-first hand stayed eighth and joined to none; the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing stayed a report brought up the yard and never a sighting. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road --- four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid --- and none was made to answer any kept thing under this roof, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. Silt 20 (Day 444) stood two mornings off as a date and was not brought.

He kept four hundred and forty-two exact, six months and two hundred and sixty-two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. \lore{The carried fire} kept four low pulls under \place{the boards} and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and forty-two days; Garl's name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and forty-two, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. Answer four hundred and sixty was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine, laid beside the others with a finger between every pair; the seventh place stayed empty.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, and the note came from under the covering and ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold on answer four hundred and sixty as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. The number that had faced the clay road had gone back into its sleeve and arrived nowhere, and under the boards the four low pulls kept their silence where the fifth should have stood and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into the nineteenth not lived and not brought.
