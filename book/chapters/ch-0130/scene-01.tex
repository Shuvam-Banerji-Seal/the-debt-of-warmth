\daymark{441}{Silt 17, Y.H. 814, Seventhday}

\dropcap{T}{he small hours} of the seventeenth day came down under the Vail roof the way \lore{Ash} comes down in a room where nobody sweeps --- not in a fall but in a settling, finer along the skirting than in the middle of the floor, finding the crack in the boards before it found the warmth. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where the going of the light had left it, on answer four hundred and fifty-six, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and forty exact, six months and two hundred and sixty days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and forty days; \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and forty, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up \place{the clay road}, four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid, with no signature set on the space; all four rode locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box behind the twelve of Y.H. 813, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. None of it had crossed under the roof in the night as sign or lesson, mending or cause, payment or payer. The things kept but not spent lay each on its own place with a finger between every pair, and a morning that had earned only its seventeenth did not tell them again.

He took the body in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it weeks ago and would not try now, and he could not lift it, and could not have lifted it when it was soft. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put on the mornings and goings of the light behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose, and could not ask for it; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

The infant's three wants quarrelled with the surgeon's register and neither won the room. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, wanted the thread wiped from his lip, wanted a hand on the handle of the pot that would bring a cup within reach. The first was old horn and would nourish nobody, the second a small dry nothing, the third a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. He had finished everything on his plate in another country and could not reach this one. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a settlement, because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the small one outside the wool that could not close two inches of air. He had once stood at the head of a table and laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted, and had known the difference between a finding and a want without having to think which was which. That was another room and another pair of hands. It paid nothing here. The wanting was not a weakness in the register and the register was not a denial of the wanting; they were two entries on the same page, and neither had been signed.

Nothing in the room had been given an instruction about the three things, so nothing in the room moved toward them. The dark went on over the crust and the thread and the cold wrist-joint, and the boy went on with the two entries on the one page, the register and the want, neither signed, while the heat came to him sideways out of the wall and asked nothing for the coming. He counted the nails in the boards without deciding to, four on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places were three and would stay three, for agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and none stood.

\scenebreak

First grey came up the clay road and into the yard without asking, and \person{Sella Vail} came to the basket with the dark still in her sleeves. She carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, taking the tilt out of the turn at the landing before the wicker had settled, and the instruction went to the wool, the wind off \place{the river}, the pot and the fire and never to the basket. The basket was not told what the day was, or what the hour was, or told to be still. A basket carried down is a pot carried down and set where the heat comes to it, and no woman explains the hour to a pot. \place{The Clay Yard} lay wet ash and old brick dust under the thin light, with barley wall to wall and no number on it, and she went around what she went around with a look and a wide step and no count; a seventeenth morning of Silt was a seventeenth morning of Silt and not a reading, and nothing from the road had come down into it to make a boundary or a lesson. Through \place{the finger's-width door} the low note of \lore{water over water} ran under \lore{the winter skin}, a low thing over a lower thing, ending on its own finishing. She set him where the coming and going of feet would not touch the wicker, out of the straight draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and went back to the small honest work of an ordinary Seventhday.

Under the roof she went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as a table straightens when a hand lifts a weight off one corner. \emph{It's the thaw}, she told the coals, and did not tell the basket. She said it in the voice another woman would have used to a child who could not hear her, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the dry scrape of iron over stone going into the boy's ear ahead of the warmth and leaving the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps; a figured gap wants a payer standing in it and none stood. Her finger went along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count and wanted no answer. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where the instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a Seventhday morning is not an opening, and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its seventeenth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; the pot was mended and was not made whole, and the crack was left where a woman could see it had been handled. Garl's laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she drew the wool over the dressed grain with the flat of her palm, turning a corner of it for damp with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, setting each piece where it had lain and giving it no figure and no handful, though a fist of barley weighed in the hand knows what it weighs. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

The morning went on at the work of a morning. Sella's hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbers, and came back to the hairline at the wire. The barley lay wall to wall without a number. The boy took the not-seeing of the yard as findings and not as slights; a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. No slate was opened within reach of the wicker, no figure was entered and no money passed, and nobody gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones} with the morning's quiet already worked into him. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name under the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}, which went on circling against a firing of tiles that would never come and was worse for being uncalled. The stack kept its own arithmetic of four, four and a space, and the space was not a fifth and was not filled. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach; the coming away was the whole of the checking. The counting sentence lay in the file where it had been put, found and not made a habit, a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; a second would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stands. The other sentence, about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it, had come all the way to the word that would call the loan and had stopped there, a remembered stopping not made a tenth. The term was already ended, the loan uncalled, his valuable ear exposure and not safety; the fear of being entered again for vagrancy was still his without a figure, and so was the want he did not voice for a slate with his own name and no mark against it. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on over the flagstones without turning his head toward the landing. A man who owns nothing knows where every small thing is; a man who has counted six years knows what a seventh is worth when nobody comes to call it. The boy took the stopping apart by the place of each part --- what was there, what was not, and no payer standing under either --- and filed the silence on a place of its own beside Garl's unforged sentence and the older stopped sentence. It was an object and not a doctrine; a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood. He did not put a tomorrow into it.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} edge \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her unfinished sentence. One bite of the iron held and one took; there was no word in the house covering both mercies. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. The household straightened around the waiting without a word from anybody. The boy lay under the shelf and kept \emph{he's not Vail} sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years; the promise to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn against \lore{the burned slate} standing at five, and the two quiet men under one roof kept their places on trays of their own, their silences maintenance and not a compact. Nothing had come under the shed as sign, mending, cause, payment or payer, and the boy made no joining of what he saw.

The boy put each thing back on its own place and added none of them together, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. A likeness was not a cause. A hand going flat over a sleeve was not a reader. A number counted was a number with air around it, and air bought no warmth. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three; nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth, and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The things kept but not spent stood each where they had been put, with a finger between every pair, none a reader, a witness, a tenth hand, a custom, a payment or a payer, and none made to answer another on a morning that had earned only its seventeenth. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure and did not answer any of them, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. The hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together and call the joining an account is far off and is not his.

He kept four hundred and forty-one exact, six months and two hundred and sixty-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire's fifth went unpaid and Garl's name went unspoken the same four hundred and forty-one, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Answer four hundred and fifty-seven was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine. The hand outside the wool took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold on answer four hundred and fifty-seven as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under the boards the four low pulls kept their silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into the afternoon of the seventeenth not yet lived and not brought into this one.
