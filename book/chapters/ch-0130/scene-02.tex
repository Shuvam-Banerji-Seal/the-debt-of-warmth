The afternoon came down into the \place{Clay Yard} the way heat comes into a pot set near the hearth --- not all at once, and not by anybody's leave, but by the slow insistings of a day that had already earned its morning and would earn its evening before it was done. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where \person{Sella Vail} had set him, out of the straight draught and inside the reach of her eye between pail and broom, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under a sky the colour of wet slate with the dry kiln smell still folded in its folds. The basket was not told the hour. A basket carried down is a pot carried down, and no woman explains the afternoon to a pot.

He took the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. That was the rule of the file, and the file was the only instrument he had: begin with what a thing does, not with what it means, for a meaning wants a hand standing under it and there were no hands in this yard that would answer to him. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock --- wood on stone, hollow, with no second sound behind it. A rope settled into its knot at \place{the yard well} with the cold-stiffened rasp that a rope makes when the damp in it has gone to iron and the hemp has not yet learned to give. Barley stood wall to wall without a number on it. A loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure.

The fist that set the pail down had three parts: knuckles first, then the flat of the palm, then the fingers opening away, and no fourth. The eye that watched the rope did not follow the knot after the rasp ended. The mouth that went with the loaf said a word about the weather and not about the loaf, and the word went off across the yard and bought nothing. Between the fist and the knock there was a half-beat, and between the rasp and the settling of the rope another. He gave each gap its own place and put no intention into any of them, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and in this yard nobody stood. A man who adds findings before the last finding is in has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut; he had learned that in a room with a white light and had carried it here without ever being able to use it on a person. Here it was enough to take the thing apart and leave it apart.

The large sounds he left where they were, because the large ones were mostly weather. The wind off \place{the river} went over the yard and under the door and was called the weather; the kiln breathed behind the wall and was called the kiln; the river kept its note under its skin and was not counted at all. Weather is the name a house gives a thing it does not intend to enter. He had no quarrel with the name. He filed the sounds by the place of each and went around the yard once, as the yard goes around itself, with a look and a wide step and no number.

Once and not twice. \place{The seven walked-around places} stood where they had stood, and he went around them the way a man moves a tray of instruments --- touching none, knowing each by the place it keeps. Widest at \place{the returning-line place} out on \place{the worked strips}, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back; no name under it, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space, no payer. A wide step is a keeping and not a sign, and he kept it and made nothing of it. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water held the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and level water in a vessel nobody had calibrated was not honest and not a promise. At \place{the Vail corner} the two unswept lines kept a finger between them: the clean dark line, and the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. He looked once. He did not look twice. He called them weather, and they were not made to stand up as a sign, a fourth covering, a payment or a payer, and no hand went into the grey to place it or to lift it out.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather. A man waiting his turn opened a shut fist not toward the water but toward the light, looked at what had not been shown, shut the fist and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. Another fist went up toward the water and came down again with nothing under it. He took each apart by the place of each part --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap --- and filed the direction on a place of its own beside the other fist-to-nowhere arrivals, with a finger between every pair. It was not an offer, not a custom, not a payment, not a reader, not a sale, not a sixth shaping, and not the calling of any loan. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost came back with a note folded twice against her palm and not a price, and the note was not entered and not shown. The arithmetic read over the covered grain at home did not know what the grain cost, and the ink-neat space between the figure and the price carried no signature, and paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. No slate was opened within reach of the wicker. No figure was entered. No money passed.

\person{Ilva} was offstage with her short strip of notched birch in its fold in her sleeve, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between; the strip was not taken out, read, copied, counted or shown, and no figure came off the wood. Her hand stayed ninth with no tenth and her fear stayed unpaid, and the nearness of any fist in the yard did not make her a reader or a speaker of any word that came up at this well. Up \place{the clay road} the single narrow slate went unread and \person{Berr Clay}'s box stayed locked behind the twelve of Y.H. 813 and the four of this year, the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. From \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{north bridge} a figure entered on a slate had been brought up the yard as a report and never as a sighting, and the boy kept it on a place of its own, a report and not a witness, not a tenth hand and not a rule.

The going of the light came into the yard without a knock, the way the end of a shift comes to a man who has been counting under his breath: not as a boundary but as the last of the work going quiet. Sella Vail came to the basket with the day still in her sleeves and carried him up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, taking the tilt out of the turn at the landing before the wicker had settled. The instruction went to the wool, the wind off the river, the pot and the fire and never to the basket. The basket was not told that the day had been the seventeenth, or what it had held, or that it was over. She said nothing to it of the fist that had gone toward the light and come back into a sleeve, nothing of the note that was not a price, nothing of the corner with its two lines called weather. A woman who says a thing aloud makes a thing the Court can enter, and this house had learned to keep its mouth on the pot.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it the way a table straightens when a hand lifts a weight off one corner. \emph{It's the thaw}, she told the coals, and did not tell the basket. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot --- the dry scrape of iron over stone going into the boy's ear ahead of the warmth --- and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps; a figured gap wants a payer in it and none stood. Her finger went along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count. One glance went to the cold clay jar standing under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour mineral tang of the corner nobody had swept, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\person[Garl Vail]{Garl} was in the room the way he had been in it for four hundred and forty-one days: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg, the hollow wheel-seat nobody was allowed to fill, the twice-mended pot with the hairline standing between the older brass staple and the tighter wire, and the unforged sentence at the kindling with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford. His laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use. Neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending, and his name had not crossed a threshold under this roof in four hundred and forty-one days, while \lore{the carried fire} went unpaid its fifth the same number, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one.

She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A going of the light is not an opening; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its seventeenth. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, leaving the hairline showing. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; the pot was mended and was not made whole.

At \place{the rack end} she drew the wool over the dressed grain with the flat of her palm, turning a corner for damp with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, setting each piece where it had lain and giving it no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. He knew that two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of the loaf shelf, never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and without a second fold. Beside the horn-hard crust the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put, still a finding and not a placing, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint as at the end of a line written out for it; \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody here would perform. He knew a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and could not ask for it.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, ending on its own finishing. The carried fire kept four low pulls under \place{the boards} and the silence where the fifth should have stood; the boards overhead kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. She went to her own bed without explaining the count, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He had once laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted and named them again as they came away; the tray here was a rough towel and the instruments were the places of a day, and he laid them out in the order in which they had presented themselves, which was the only honest order. He went over the seventeenth by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap, and he did not add them, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up his answer.

The pail's flat knock stood on its own place. The rope's cold-stiffened rasp stood on its own beside it, with a finger between. Barley wall to wall without a number stood on a third place, and the loaf stopped a finger's width on a fourth. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place; the heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth, and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The word withheld on the fifteenth stood on its own place beside the older stopped sentence and the unforged one at the kindling; three sentences were three and not one doctrine, and the loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended. At the kindling and the wheel-shed the two keepings stood found where put, and the two silences under one roof were two silences and not a compact.

The offered figure and its return stood on their own place; Ilva's count stayed in wood with her hand ninth and her fear unpaid; the second asking of Silt 12, the face seen at the yard's edge on the thirteenth, the reported second slate of Silt 6, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, the hair-thin thaw of Silt 7 and this seventeenth day's two unswept lines called weather stood each where they had been put, with a finger between every pair. The figure entered at the landing and brought up the yard as a report stood as a report and never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. The fifth shaping of the well-saying stood between the eighth hand and the road saying; the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row} stood three and were not one account; \lore{the burned slate} stood at five and the promise under it was neither given nor withdrawn; the four ownerless directions stood four and were not one witness. None of them was a reader, a witness, a tenth hand, a custom, a payment or a payer, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together and call the joining an account was far off and was not his.

Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road in Berr Clay's box behind the twelve of Y.H. 813: four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the twelve of the old year not laid across the four to make a row come out even, the figure read still not the figure paid, with no signature set on the space. None of the four was made to answer any kept thing under this roof, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. The unsent call to \person{Nina} stood where it had been put and was not paid again. Silt 20 stood three mornings off as a date and was not brought.

He kept four hundred and forty-one exact, six months and two hundred and sixty-one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire's fifth went unpaid and Garl's name went unspoken the same four hundred and forty-one, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Answer four hundred and fifty-eight was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine. The infant's three wants were still in the room and still unanswered --- the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle --- both true, the second louder, neither buying the other, and the quarrel went on without a verdict because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the small one outside the wool that could not close two inches of air.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its low note of water over water under its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer under the covering and came from under the covering and not from the covering, ending on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold on answer four hundred and fifty-eight as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under the boards the four low pulls kept their silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into the eighteenth not yet lived and not brought into this one.
