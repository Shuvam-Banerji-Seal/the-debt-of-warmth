\daymark{252}{Smoke 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay where it had been laid, against the fold at his ear, and \person[Corin]{the boy} did not move it. The seventy-eighth answer had gone down on the sixth place with no payer named and stayed there, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He had set the hand down as a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of a shift, where the next hand will find it without looking, and there was no ceremony in the setting and no meaning in it beyond the setting. It was not a sign and it was not a gift; it was a hand laid down against the wool. He had once held a clamp in that place and known from the resistance under his fingers when a vessel would take and when it would tear, and the knowing had cost him nothing at the time and everything afterwards. Now the hand was small and soft-nailed and lay outside the fold on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}. The surgeon took the inventory of the two hands and entered neither anywhere. The file lay open on the towel, every edge where a hand would look for it first, and he did not close it, because a file left open is not an invitation and a file closed is not an ending. There was no towel, no instruments and no case; there was only the order of the laying, sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt, and the boy did not ask the order to mean anything more than the order meant. The child's body was warm at the neck and cold at the wrist and did not know it was being read. That was the whole of the difficulty, and it was older than this room.

Two hundred and fifty-one had come down out of the close, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and seventy-one days told by nobody. He did not hold the number as a man holds a thing he expects to be given back. He held it the way an instrument is held in its case, sharp by sharp, and now the case opened on a new day and the number went forward into two hundred and fifty-two, six months and seventy-two days, and nobody under any roof said so. The house kept its days badly, in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a clay jar, in a space at the row's end that nobody called a number, and he kept this one exactly, edge to edge, against all of them. The wanting of a number went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under ash. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on being a small animal thing in a body that could not lift its own head. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and fifty-two days, and its payer had no name. It was the small hours and the dark had not yet thinned; first grey was a thing the day would earn and had not earned yet.

The smells came in their old order, the near before the far: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of \lore{Ash} in the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The oiled paper at the window hung slack in its frame and let the cold come through in a slow sheet that found the wall and went no further, the way water finds a bank and stops where the bank is. There was a little sound in the wool when he breathed, small and animal and entirely his own. The first sound from outside himself was not a bell. It was the kiln drawing in fours under the floor, pull, pull, pull, pull, and the silence where the fifth should have stood, a silence with the shape of a held breath in it and no chest under it to hold. Smoke 13 was that morning's work and no market had been brought into the small hours; there was no Cross and no bell. The bell that would go on the tenth of the next tend at \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap, was not heard here and was not borrowed. Smoke 20 (Day 259), the second Account-day, stood seven mornings beyond the close, a date and not a day, and was not brought early into a stretch of dark that had not earned its heading. A day is a small vessel, and a day not yet lived will not be poured into one that is.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing going over a lower thing and never counted among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh had come and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell and was not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh. The told figure stayed beside the paid figure, and Smoke's first arithmetic had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box and answered nothing here. He kept the trays apart without congratulating himself on it. A discipline is a thing a hand uses; a belief is a thing a man stands under, and he had only the hand, and it could go up an inch and no further.

The inventory arrived in its old order. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come to lie down and make the row come out even, for a third crust entering a fold where two had stood would be the beginning of an account, and this house gave without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the heat and was unwiped still, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face of a man going cold, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew the grimace severe cold wears and that the warming, if it ever came, must come from outside and slowly, and that the cold patient would seem to improve before he worsened. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true, and the small hours made the second no quieter. The surgeon took the facts in the order they arrived and entered none of them, not even in the place where he kept the count.

\scenebreak

Before first grey the house stirred the way a pot stirs when a hand goes to it in the dark, without a word and without a decision. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the fire the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, so the one grey streak showed pale off the coals and the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. The fire answered four low pulls and kept its fifth where it had always kept it, and she fed it as if it had given her no reason to count what it lacked. Her hand went to the coals and the pot and the work, and not to \lore{the widow's mark}, which hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; on this morning, as on the mornings behind it, she did not touch it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket or a bed. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household, and she did not look at it again and made no lesson of it and named no payer under it. A thing that does not touch is still a thing kept, and the two rooms stayed apart the way a surgeon keeps a field clean, without congratulating herself on that either. When she spoke it was to the fire, the pot, the wool and the weather, and never to the basket; the boy had learned long ago to hear the instruction go past him the way a man hears water go over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects through the morning, which is a keeping and not a story. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a broader man had leaned for twenty years, and the hollow wheel-seat kept its hollow on that side and would not be filled by any weight of a son's. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.} The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with; it circled the house without a name on it and was worse for being uncalled, as an untreated wound is worse for being left open in a warm room. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and fifty-two days, and the keeping of him was maintenance, the way a pot is kept clean between firings, with no lesson made of it and no payer named. No collector came up or went down the clay road on this morning, and the debt stood in the ruts with no figure on it. Smoke's first arithmetic, gone up the road in a locked box two mornings before, answered nothing under this roof and had not moved the house to meet it; the house had not gone out to the reading and the reading had not come back under the roof. The boy kept the coat and the seat and the proverb and the uncalled debt where they stood, four objects and no fifth, and did not lay them across the crust or the knot or the river.

At \place{the drying racks} the dressed grain came out from under its wool for damp. She put her fingers into it to the knuckle, felt the cold that had settled in it overnight, spread it thin on the flat stone to finish, turned it with the back of a hand, gathered it by the handful and set the fold flat again. It had changed state and gained no figure. The arm that carried it knew weight and not cost, and the count of it lived in glances and handfuls and in what the arm remembered of yesterday's weight, entered on no slate and laid across none of the absences. The figure spoken over it at the first Account-day had gone up the road without knowing what it cost, and the space between that figure and the grain's price had no signature; she did not put one there and the boy did not put one there for her. A figure read over a covered thing knows the cover and not the thing under it, and a thing not priced is not therefore free but a thing whose payer has not been named. He watched the handfuls go out and come back and the fold go flat, and the work was exact and unfigured and of no use to him, and that was enough.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kiln mouth} the dry heat came up in a small breathing that was not the fire's count, and she set a pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting that was neither a look toward the bed nor a look away from it. Two inches of air was the whole distance and the hand could not close it; the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted, a mark kept where nobody thinks to look above a row kept badly on top. She moved her lips along the row at the loaf shelf with its two gaps and gave nobody a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. A mark nobody reads is still a mark kept, and a mark read wants a figure put under it, and a figure wants a payer. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall and her hand shut half over nothing and came away; she did not touch the jar and did not say what it held. Two inches, a mark above the edge, a hand shut on nothing: three keepings in one room that did not touch one another, and the boy filed them where they were without making an account of the three.

Then he put the first question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in the asking and no answer he expected; he had learned to ask the way a man tests a tool before he trusts it, with the question small enough that a no would cost him nothing. Outside the wool the hand went down at once on the river's next low note and lay back against the fold. It was not a longer reach, a miss entered as a finding, a hearing of the full note, a seventh of it, a tenth stopping, a payment, one of the held changes, a key, a power or a rule. It was answer seventy-nine, filed on the sixth place beside seventy-eight with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He had moved the hand one inch toward a sound and it had come down on the sound; that was the whole of it, and he would not ask the whole of it to stand for more. The morning went on around him at the hearth and the racks and the kiln mouth, and the hand had answered one thing in a new day and asked for nothing in return. He laid it down where the next hand would find it without looking, and the count went forward into the rest of Smoke 13, a day that had earned its heading and would be lived to its end.
