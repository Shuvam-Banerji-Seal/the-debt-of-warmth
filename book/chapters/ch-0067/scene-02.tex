Afternoon thinned without ending. The light came down off \place{the terrace} in a thin sheet and lay on \place{the flagstones} and stayed there, and the yard held what it had and gave nothing new. It was the hour when the day has spent its heat and has not yet spent its light, when a pot set down keeps the warmth it was given and asks for no more. The warmth went behind the pot, under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, into the fold at his ear, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the basket under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} and did not move. The inventory came as it always came, in the order the facts arrived and not in the order he would have chosen. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third had come to lie down and make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry warmth and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the severe face cold wears and that a cold patient cannot clot, and that the grimace of it looks like a smile to people who have never watched a man go, and that the warming must come from outside and slowly and would seem to worsen before it mended. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, and the afternoon made no quieter work of it than the small hours had. The surgeon took the facts down in their old order and entered none of them, not even in the place where he kept the count.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects through the afternoon, which is a keeping and not a story. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a broader man had leaned for twenty years, and the hollow wheel-seat kept its hollow on that side and would not be filled by the weight of a son. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.} The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with; it circled the house without a name on it and was worse for being uncalled, as an untreated wound is worse for being left open in a warm room. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and fifty-two days, and the keeping of him was maintenance, the way a pot is kept clean between firings, with no lesson made of it and no payer named. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on this afternoon, and the debt stood in the ruts with no figure on it. Smoke's first arithmetic, gone up the road in a locked box two mornings before, answered nothing under this roof and had not moved the house to meet it. Four objects and no fifth; the boy kept them where they stood and did not lay them across the crust or the knot overhead or the river's note through the door.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket about the yard with her little-door burn shut on the handle, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and gave every instruction to the broom, the bucket, the wool and the pot before she gave one to a person, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would say \emph{I}, and the yard straightened around the saying as a pot straightens on a wheel. Under the low angle of \place{the drying racks} she went wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gave the space a look and no number. The broken slates were entered by nobody. At the Vail corner she went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, with a look and a wide step and no sweeping, telling nothing about either. Farther on she went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say what had once sat there, and the ring kept its place on its own tray. At \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark with its skin of ice gone, the water in it called weather, and she went around it as she went around the thaw, with a look and no number; a thing not priced is not therefore free, but a thing whose payer has not been named. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place kept its shape, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was gone around with a look as wide as any of the others and no figure spoken over it. Seven in a row were still seven; they were not an account until a payer stood under them, and none stood. The boy was carried past each one at the half-pace and saw the look and the wide step and heard no number, and he kept the seven where they were without counting them into his own count and without congratulating himself on keeping them there.

At the well \person[Ilva] drew water with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hand stayed in her sleeve. The short strip of birch bark notched along one edge stayed where it was, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, not read, not asked about, and not joined to the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood or to any other private keeping. A neighbour come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the firings of Smoke had cost, and Ilva's eyes went to the asker and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.} She gave no figure and no name to the thing she kept and went on with her water, and nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. The boy filed no tenth hand-shape. The ninth stayed on its own place beside the eighth with a finger between it and every other private keeping, and the fact that a count kept in wood was still a count was left where it lay, to be found or not found on some far-off day that had not earned its heading. Then a man come down the clay road with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere. It was not a missing figure; a sleeve is no slate and a figure kept is not a figure lost. A cost-question went to the pot and the weather with nothing priced, and the weather answered first and the price of barley after, and no row was named and no witness stood.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named, and said \emph{Yes, house} to the wood and to the fire and to the weather in the flat tone of a man stating what the day was. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He told the kindling \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and looked at it afterward with the flat of his hand, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. Then the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stood there, and he did not say that word. It was the ninth stopping, remembered and not a new one, an interval on its own tray and not a payment; the Seed 16 breath was drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, with its small interval showing where it should have gone in. Nine stoppings stood, nine objects and not a rule, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, which had still not given, and the loan stayed uncalled. Above the shed the settling of \place{the Debt Loft} came to the boy as a direction and nothing more, and the warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and cold long since, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon. Two ears kept their silence under one roof and neither man put a word between them; the loan that could hear was worth more than the debt and unsafe, and it was not called.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and had got no name from him and would get none now; he did not say the room was cold. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, while the broken ones under the lowest rack were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} And to the pot on the wheel, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either: \emph{You'll do.} He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. The pot sayings stood as sayings — that one sings itself out early, that it hasn't been heard from since, that every row goes quiet come the thaw — and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no pot was lost. The burned-slate word stood at four tellings out of Slate Row, a direction arrived nowhere and still not a third telling of the room shut in the war; the two shut-room tellings stood at two. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} come up from High Kiln on a wrong wind went over the boards and out again on the wind that brought it, a direction arrived nowhere and not a second edge paid. When Sella's sentence went low and unfinished at the hearth, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing — a remembered repetition, one piece of iron with two bites in it, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Through the finger's width of the back room the low voices kept their open and did not resolve into words, and the boy heard a direction only.

The afternoon spent its light and went on into the small hours without a boundary drawn in the narration, and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-two days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing going over a lower thing and never counted among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh had come and none had been invented to fill the empty place. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell and was not folded into the three. The told figure stayed beside the paid figure and Smoke's first arithmetic, gone up the road in a locked box, answered nothing here. The widow's mark hung where it hung at a rack, a well, a road, a basket, an afternoon and a dusk, because the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour; the one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again and had no lesson made of it and no payer named under it. Smoke 20 (Day 259), the second Account-day, stood seven mornings beyond the close and was not brought into a day that had earned its heading.

They set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. One brown-edged crust lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed and no third. Then he put the second question of the chapter to his hand. There was no ceremony in it and no answer he expected; he had learned to ask the way a man tests a tool before he trusts it, with the question small enough that a no would cost him nothing. In the room that does not touch his, Sella came to the end of one of the counted breaths she had been giving the fire — small intake, hold, letting-go — and the boy borrowed nothing from it but the moment before the letting-go, before the count was given up. Outside the wool the hand went down at once, before the letting-go came, on the river's next low note, and lay back against the fold. It was not a longer reach, a miss entered as a finding, a hearing of the full note, a seventh of it, a tenth stopping, a payment, one of the held changes, a key, a power or a rule. It was answer eighty, filed on the sixth place beside seventy-nine with no payer named, an object among objects, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He had moved the hand one inch toward a sound and it had come down on the sound before a borrowed count was spent, and he would not ask the whole of it to stand for more. The count went forward into the rest of Smoke 13, a day that had earned its heading and would be lived to its end.
