The afternoon came down the way a banked fire comes down --- drawn off the top first and kept at the bottom. The light left the upper wall, lay flat along the floorboards, then went under the shelf into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}. Heat that had stood all morning at \place{the kiln mouth} backed behind the pot, travelled the boards and settled where he lay, dry, patient and wrong. A man may be warm across the back of his hand and still be losing from the middle of him, and the loss does not show on the skin. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn; Smoke 16 (Day 255) stood one morning ahead with no heading earned and was not brought into an evening that had still to be lived.

The inventory came in the order the facts arrived. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, hard in the dry warmth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come to make the row come out even, for a third in that fold would be the beginning of an account and this house gave without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen hours since and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if a line had been written out for it to that length and no farther; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the severe face cold wears, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew the warming, if it came, must come from outside and slowly. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true, and the afternoon made the second no quieter. The surgeon took the facts in their order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of them, not even where he kept the count.

Two hundred and fifty-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and seventy-four days stood where six months and seventy-three had stood the day before, and no voice under any roof had put a number to either. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} drew four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and fifty-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing and never counted among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell, not folded into the three and not made a seventh; the told figure stayed beside the paid figure, and Smoke's first arithmetic, gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box, answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or the hand that went in on Seed 15.

The room that held him and the room of the fire shared a wall and did not touch. Heat came through as a fact and not as a message, sound as a direction and not as an instruction; the little silence after a count that had ended elsewhere was an interval with edges and not a summons. He kept the two rooms apart the way a surgeon keeps a field clean, without congratulating himself on it. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at a throat that was not his, touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk on Smoke 13, 14 or 15. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household, not looked at again, with no lesson made and no payer named. The next real opening lay far off beyond Smoke's last reading, in the first firing of Frost, a date and not a day, and was not reached for.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects, which is a keeping and not a story. \place{the salt-stiff coat} hung on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a broader man had leaned for twenty years, and \place{the hollow wheel-seat} in the shed kept its hollow on that side and would not be filled. The ridge that had been his ash-marker was a clean dark line now, with the new finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, and neither had been swept; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.} The debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with; it circled the house without a name and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and fifty-four days, and the keeping of him was maintenance, with no lesson made of it. No collector had come up the clay road on Smoke 13, 14 or 15, and none had gone down it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the fire the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the one grey streak showed pale off the coals and the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. The fire answered four low pulls and kept its fifth, and she fed it as if it had given her no reason to count what it lacked. When she spoke it was to the fire, the pot, the wool and the weather, never to the basket; the instruction went past him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. Her hand did not go to the mark, because a shelf, an afternoon and a dusk are not openings.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction. The instruction went to the broom and not to him: \emph{There. Go over it.} Tarn said \emph{Yes, house} to the wood and the broom and whatever else in the yard was willing to be told, in the flat tone a man uses to state the weather. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the kindling, so the sentence sounded like a fact about the light and not like a man keeping a thing back. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and looked at it after setting it, the way a man looks at a tool he has put where another will find it. Beside it lay Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence about the man who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to Tarn's teeth. The boy knew its shape the way he knew an instrument laid out on a towel: it went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure under it and no name on the man who would have paid. An ear that could hear was worth more than the debt that held it, which was why the sentence was unsafe and never finished; a loan that can hear can be called, and no voice under this roof had ever called it. It was the ninth stopping, remembered and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray, nine objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year, which had come to its very end and had still not given. Nine likenesses are nine and must not stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any. At the top of his reach Tarn drew the breath that should have gone in on Seed 16 and did not spend it; the loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the road or went down it that day. His chest had been tight since the dark.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. In an unmarked clay pot on a shelf of \place{the Debt Loft} lay the warm stone Sella Vail had given him in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold long since, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past \place{the shallow bright patch} without the half-check, the way a man sweeps over a place where he once stopped and does not stop again; the stones kept the place and gave it to nobody. It went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, and Tarn did not once turn along it. Above the shed the loft settled with a small sound and a direction, and the boy took the direction and nothing more; a place a man sleeps is not the man, and a pot on a shelf is not the warmth that went out of the stone.

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he told it, in the flat voice that did not finish sentences and did not need to. To a pot on the bench he said \emph{You'll do}, with no welcome and no unkindness, the way a man passes a tool to another and does not look up. The boy knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and Jorra knew it too and withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. It was a knowing and not a power: a man can know what a pot will say and still be unable to make it say anything else.

The burned-slate word stood at four, told from \place{Slate Row} and arriving as a direction over the boards, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three, though men had gone low before the light and spoken too low to carry. Three shapings of the pot saying stayed three, with no fourth likeness made a cause, for a likeness made a cause wants a payer and none stood to any. The high thin note a pot had given before its time two mornings ago lay on its own place and did not make a fourth; the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} on a wrong wind off \place{High Kiln} had come over the boards and gone out on the wind that brought it, a direction arrived nowhere and not a second edge paid. No name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and the men belonged to \lore{the Broken Slates} without being named by him. Three or more had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them; the room kept its finger's width of open and its low voices that did not resolve into words. He did not say the room was cold, and the boy did not say it either.

When Sella's sentence went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. One piece of iron with two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The household straightened around the silence as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody said what had been done with the iron. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed where it had been sealed on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The two silences under the roof stayed side by side and were not joined: the ear that kept a sound and the room that kept its low voices, each with its own direction and no payer under either.
