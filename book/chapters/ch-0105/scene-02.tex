The morning gave way to the afternoon without a boundary drawn, the way a banked coal gives way from one degree of heat to the next and is the same coal throughout. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof at \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the light came round to the low side of the wall and stood there a while, and \person{Sella Vail} lifted \person[Corin]{the boy} down in his basket and took the way out at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the pace of a woman carrying a pot filled that must not be slopped. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, and to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the wicker. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” Under the fold his hand lay back against the crust as a tool lies where the next hand will find it, and the crust's corner stood a hair off the wool with its empty place unnamed and no third. The kiln stood cooling behind its wall from the firing two mornings gone and bought no word on this afternoon; nobody had made a lesson of it and nobody made one now.

It was the day the month he had been born in had come round again, the deep-winter month, and it went by as an ordinary tending day goes. No loaf was set aside, no figure entered, no hand laid on anything to mark it, no word said over the basket that would have told a person a number had finished. The older women in the yard named the month by the weather and the slatemen named it by the count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for having them. He kept three hundred and sixty-six exactly, stood edge to edge in the only count that was exact, and did not stand it up as a line for anybody's foot to cross. The house kept its afternoon in pails, in glances, in the space a weight left when it was lifted, and could not say that a year had turned. There was nobody under any roof who would have known what a year counted if they had been told, and he had long ago stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same thing as not wanting it. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth and the thread at his mouth wiped, and the want went on under the exactness without winning and without being put down.

He took the yard as a field is taken before work, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. There was the stiff dry rasp of a rope at the well, the flat knock of a pail set against stone, a low word given to a pot, the scrape of a broom going the wrong way over grit, and over all of them the river wind that gave the large sounds their shape and took them away again. The half-pace shortened a hair at an open slate. The look did not settle where it fell. The step widened without haste. He had once read a room by the small sounds before he read the injuries, and here he read a household that had no idea it was being read, and took his small clean satisfaction in a field found as it was and left as it was, a satisfaction that cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places in \place{the Clay Yard}, with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} lay where the ridge had turned, with the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both and thickened in the month's dry cold. The space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} held its broken birth-slates and was entered by nobody. The shallow clay pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} had skinned over from edge to edge, and the silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} a little out of the path of feet. The field's empty place lay out on \place{the worked strips}, and \place[the worked strips]{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, was gone around widest of all, wider than the rest by the width of the step that would have put a foot where that foot had been. Seven places gone around with a look and a wide step, and no eighth made of them, and nothing entered that would want a payer standing. She did not count them and did not look twice. The boy kept them seven and made no number of his keeping either, for a thing joined to a number wants a hand to join it and none had done it.

Three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair: the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin on the trough, the plate of ice that lifted its clouded face a little out of the path of feet. Each sat on a place of its own, and a man who laid them together and called them a rule would have wanted a payer under all three and none stood. The river's note came low through \place{the finger's-width door} under its winter skin, found where it had been put and not made again, a covering put on by the cold and taken off by the cold and entered nowhere. It sat on its own place beside \lore{water over water} and was not a fourth covering, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing, not a sign, not a lesson, not a cause and not a payment. It was not spent on this day that nobody named, and it was not spent on \lore{the Hearth Drawing} three mornings off, for a thing joined to a date wants a hand to join it and none had laid one. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. The empty fingers went on a place of its own beside the one object of \place{Bone} 22, a keeping and not a second handful, and the two did not join. A second handful made to match the first would have been a finding forged, and there was no payer under either to make the finding true.

The afternoon's hunts and repairs went by as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and stood a moment with the weight settling into the small of his back, the way a load finds its own place on a man who has carried it often enough to stop thinking about it. To a woman who asked what the carrying had cost he said, “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price.” He moved the hare up a hand's breadth and went off toward \place{the clay road} with it unweighed, and nobody opened a slate and no figure came. The boy took the settling of the hare into the carrier's back as a thing the body knows before the mouth does and entered it nowhere. A man with a cracked jar in a rope sling asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late. \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, and he agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The \place{Ash} 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson, and no ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} to make a sixth telling of the burned slate, a third telling of the shut room or a sixth asking of the row.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt, and the man's name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and sixty-six days. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off, and he passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs and goes away from it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and cut nothing and counted nothing and spent nothing.

He checked the thumb's resting of \place{Bone} 26 and the held breath of \place{Ash} 14 at the sill and found each where it had been put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair. The held breath was not a sixth held object and the thumb's resting was not a seventh of anything and neither was a tenth stopping, and no payer stood under either. He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as a surgeon draws a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on: a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach and the loan standing uncalled. He did not look along the road. His chest had been tight since the dark, the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, the returned half-check of Bone 8 staying where it lay on the stone and not made again. The equality under the roof held at the place it had moved to, two ears and two silences and a loan that could hear and had never been called, and the boy entered neither side of it on a slate.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay beneath the lowest rack and were entered by nobody.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. The boy kept the knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them, and no seventh hearing stood up in the empty place because a master from outside might have wanted one. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go, and the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for or filled.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \place{Grathic} word for both mercies. He took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of \place{Ash} 26 a second time. The holding stayed where nobody would enter it, gathered once and found where put and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer and none stood. \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, gathering interest against the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two and the row at five askings with no witness. No sixth telling, third telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping or fourth direction was supplied to make any of the rows come out even. The boy filed the dust, the two bites and the unspent breath on separate places and wrote no cause across any of them, and nothing went under the roof as a sign of the day or a figure for the year that had finished.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the yard with the whole of her attention on its level, as a potter lifts a thing that is still soft, and went back around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, widest around the returning-line place. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the wicker. She said nothing of where they had been, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker.

Under the Vail roof she came to \place{the hearth} before the basket, fire before basket, as she had come at both ends of every day for three years. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure, and the two gaps stayed where they were, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air; the cord of \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because an afternoon and a going of the light are not openings. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, the sixth held behind at \place{Ash} 2 without a name, the seventh stood behind the household from \place{Stone} 5 without a lesson anybody named, and no eighth was made due and the empty place where one might have stood was not dug for or filled.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. Both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with no figure lifted and no handful and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver standing and none stood.

Then she set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as at the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew what he knew and wanted what he wanted, and the knowing did not win. A cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire, and a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the careful rewarming he would have chosen and could not ask for. The surgeon had once reduced shoulders in the dark and could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle; both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

In the small hours the file came out on \place{the towel} as instruments come out on a towel, and the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing under the covering that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer three hundred and eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the nine arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the seventh opening, the pot-crack resting, the river's winter skin, the setting-down-wrong, the ninth hand, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. The burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, the row at five askings with no witness; six hearings kept the seventh place empty; the told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space; nine hands kept no tenth and nine stoppings kept no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the promise under the wheel-shed was neither given nor withdrawn and the loan stood uncalled. The name of the man whose salt-stiff coat kept a broader shape on its peg had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. Six months and one hundred and eighty-six days were told by nobody.

The bed lay at the low angle of the loaf shelf with the crust two inches from his ear and its empty place unnamed, and the hand lay back flat against the fold on answer three hundred and eight, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap. Out in the Clay Yard the returning-line place was still the widest of the seven, although no day under any roof had been set apart for a first year finished, and the month he had been born in held on without a boundary drawn. Stone 7 had gone by in loaves and glances and weather, and three hundred and sixty-six stood exact where nobody could see it. Forward, not closed.
