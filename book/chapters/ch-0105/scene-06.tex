The light went thin and high over \place{the Clay Yard} and \person{Sella Vail} carried the basket down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, neither hurrying nor stopping, with the whole of her attention on the level of the wicker and none of it on the year that had gone by under the wool. Instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” \person[Corin]{The boy} lay with his face toward the fold and heard the grammar of the house in the step: the half-pace said a slate might be open anywhere and a slate was an account and an account had a hand waiting at it; the level of the basket said a pot filled must not be slopped. Nothing in the step said a number had finished. He had long ago stopped asking the step to say it, which was not the same as not listening for it.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. First the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}; both lay unswept and thickened in the dry cold, and a line left where it was was still a line kept and not a lesson. Then the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody. Then the round dark ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word. Then \place{the yard well} with the rope stiff in the cold, \place{the trough} skinned from edge to edge, and \place{the flat stone by the well} with its plate of ice clouded with silt set a little out of the path of feet. Last, out on \place{the worked strips}, the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out. Her step was widest there, wider than at any of the others, wider by nothing that could be measured and by no look that lasted longer than any other look. She did not go around it twice to make a figure of going around it once. The boy kept the wide step on a place of its own and did not enter it as a sign; a sign is a thing that points to a payer and no payer stood under that ground.

The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere. The white frost had gone off the well's north lip while the trough kept its skin and the silt-clouded plate stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet, and she put a finger of attention between every pair and made no fourth likeness a cause; a fourth likeness would have wanted a doctrine over it and none stood. Over \place{the Ilm}, from bank to bank since the afternoon of the month's first day, lay a winter skin put on by the cold and taken off by the cold, found where it had been put and not made again, sitting on its own place beside \lore{water over water} — a low note over a lower note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming — not a fourth covering, not a sixth low thing, not a hearing and not spent on a day nobody named or on the Drawing still three mornings off. At \place{the drying racks} the dressed grain lay turned under wool. Her fingers went in and came out without closing, no figure lifted off the heap and no handful; the empty fingers stood beside the one handful let fall at \place{the rack end} on \place{Bone} 22 as a keeping on a place of its own and not a second handful. She had not made it again in all the days between and she did not make it now, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer and none stood.

The yard's doing went on as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, said, “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price,” and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, to each of whom \person{Tarn} said, “Yes, house,” and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying. He agreed so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot; he did not take a side and he did not contradict either, and the yard went on around its places while the light went down the same degree it went down every day of the deep-winter month.

At the going of the light she carried him up, up the nineteen steps and in under the roof at \place{the Vail kiln-house} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She righted the fold at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. Her hand rested the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and the half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver standing and none stood. The thread of drool at his mouth kept its frozen place and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She said nothing of where they had been, and that was not a refusal to tell him, for nobody under any roof tells a basket what a keeping is worth and she was not the woman who would say a thing to please the speaker. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past the warming nobody in this house would perform.

At \place{the kindling} the account stood in the old exactness. Six pieces lay in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it where the house could not, and beside the seventh lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. Tarn passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and the hand went away when the checking was done and no breath stayed at the top of the reach. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather.

He checked the held breath he had left at the sill on \place{Ash} 14 and the thumb's resting on the uncut seventh of \place{Bone} 26, and found each where it had been put and made neither again. They sat each on a place of its own beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting, with a finger between every pair — not a sixth held object, not a seventh of anything, not a tenth stopping, not the calling of the loan, not a habit, a way, a sign, a hearing, a mending, a cause, a payment or a rule, with no payer standing under either. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there. It was the ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of the reach and the loan stood uncalled. The sentence said that an ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held the house going, and stopped with no figure and no name after it, and the equality under the roof held at the place it had moved to with nobody to enter it and nobody to call it. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the shallow bright patch of \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed where it had lain and was not made a fifth of anything.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he told it, and told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. Then he took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of \place{Ash} 26 a second time; it stayed gathered once on a place of its own with no payer under it, for a second would be a habit and a third a way. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, gathering interest on its own place under the shed.

No voice came over the boards on this afternoon. The Ash 5 joining of the burned slate to the shut room, the Ash 17 direction from \place{Slate Row} and the Ash 23 direction one shape further stood each where it had been put, with a finger between every pair, and did not repeat and bought no lesson. The boy took the dust, the two bites of iron and the unspent breath apart by what was there and by what was not and filed them on separate places, and wrote no cause across any of them.

At the going of the light the grammar renewed in full, and the boy lay at the edge of it and took it in by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. She came to \place{the hearth} before the basket, fire before basket, and told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. Beneath the woodsmoke came the faint sour mineral tang of \place{Ash} along the skirting board, the ridge unswept and thickened in the dry cold, and she had not swept it in two winters and was not sweeping it now. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. A man who had filled the row to make it come out even would have wanted a payer under it and none stood.

One glance went to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The cord of \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung and was not touched, because an afternoon, a going of the light and a bed are not openings. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, the sixth stood behind at Ash 2 without a name and the seventh stood behind the household from \place{Stone} 5 without a lesson anybody named, and no eighth was made due and the empty place where one might have stood was not dug for or filled. A whole year could finish under this roof without a hand going to the mark, and the not-going was not a slight and not a mercy and not an answer to a question nobody had put.

At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. Both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease kept Garl's broader shape on its peg behind the door and nobody took it down; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody filled it; the clean dark line and its finer grey at the corner kept their places unswept; the unforged collector sentence lay at the kindling and the debt against a firing of tiles stood uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and sixty-eight days, and the carried fire's fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful. The pot rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift.

Two dates stood where dates stand and were not brought into a day that had not earned them. Stone 10, the month's first Account-day and market, stood one morning beyond the close with its reading and its bell and its figure going twice over a basket; the \lore{Hearth Drawing} of Stone 12 stood three mornings further with its cold iron tube and a rekindled child worse on the third day and a reason not yet given. Both were named only inside the boy's exact keeping and neither was made due by a year finished. The month carried both its names the way a pot carries two handles, the older women naming it by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and no hand under any roof set either name right or left on this day. He had stopped expecting the question, which was not the same as not wanting it.

In the small hours the surgeon took a small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found. Nothing in the room had been moved for him and nothing had been taken away; the fold was at his ear, the crust was two inches off with its corner standing a hair off the wool, the thread was frozen at his mouth, and the cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as at the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The satisfaction cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. A man could keep a field clean and go home and still be the only adult in the room with the hands of an infant. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

The room held its small sounds. The dry rasp of a coal settling under ash came first and went away. The small tick of cooling clay ran along the loaf shelf and stopped and ran again. Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note low under its winter skin, muffled as a voice is muffled by a shut door, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Over that the roof kept its own silence, which was not the absence of sound but the sound of a house holding itself level. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and sixty-eight days. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood; the bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. Three hundred and sixty-eight stood exact, six months and one hundred and eighty-eight days told by nobody, a whole year finished and named by no roof, and nothing under any roof altered by one mark on account of it.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The hand did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the ice, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.
