The afternoon came into \place{the Clay Yard} the colour of wet ash, light lying flat across the racks so that the pots stood bellies full of a short day and gave nothing back. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, to the river wind and to the fire to be banked against their coming back, never to the basket. She had no need to tell a pot where it was going. A carried thing is told by the hand that carries it. Under the fold his ear took the yard by its small sounds first, as a field is taken before the light: rope dragging stiff, the dry small rasp of it, a pot set down a shade too near the draught and shifted, the scrape of a broom going around something instead of over it, and under all of them the river, low and going about its own work. The large sounds were mostly weather. The small ones did not lie.

She went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} lay where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, with the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay in their own dark and were entered by nobody. The shallow pan's gone ring held its round outline on the stone with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word inside it. \place{the trough} kept its skin, thick enough now to have held a poured spoon. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and not come out was gone around as it had been gone around every day of the dark month, with a look and a wide step and nothing said. Seven places, entered none. A thing entered wants a payer standing, and none stood.

Three coverings the cold had laid on three different objects stayed weather entered nowhere, and between every pair a finger, because likeness without a payer is only a likeness and not a cause. The gone white frost on the well's north lip had not come back; the skin on the trough broke under a pail and closed behind it; the silt-clouded plate of ice stood lifted a little out of the path of feet on \place{the flat stone by the well}. He filed the three where they lay and made no fourth of them. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool and Sella turned it once with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing. No figure came off it. No handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of Bone 22, a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three would be a way, and both want a payer.

A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and late. \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to each of them, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Hunts and repairs were directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. No runner came on any road and no collector went up or down the clay road; there was no market, no Cross and no bell, and no voice came over \place{the boards}.

\person{Ilva} was at \place{the yard well} drawing with the pail against her knee. The rope was stiff in the Ash cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up; clay stood dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. People noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. A slate lay open a pace away on the stone lip with the writing turned toward whoever had left it, and nobody was reading it aloud.

A woman waiting her turn, with a pot on her hip and her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Ash's firings had taken.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed. The short strip of notched birch bark lay pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away, or to the charcoal under \place{the loaf shelf}, or to the cord, or to any saying that had arrived nowhere. No figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word, and then she answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart --- hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention --- and filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand in the district, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth and no tenth. He made no reader of her own keeping and joined her to none. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice; there was no voice at this well, no fifth shaping came of the saying, and the directions of the fifth, the seventeenth and the twenty-third stood where they had been put and did not repeat. Her count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The edges of the finding came near to other edges and the finger went between.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and to a pot on the bench he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any kiln-master who would call it early.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low, Jorra, and the damper ---”

His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence. It took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \place{Grathic} word for both mercies.

Then, after the tongue had been moved and the sentence finished and the household straightened --- after the work was done, not before --- the bent finger stayed in the cold iron a breath longer than the work required. It took the cold out of the fingertip and kept it there. He made no song of it. He did not name it. He did not look toward the basket. He gave no name to the men in the back room and did not say the room was cold. The extra interval had no figure, no witness and no payer standing under it; the hand went away from the iron as a hand goes away from a tool put back where it belongs, and the yard went on about its work.

The boy took that interval apart by what was there and by what was not: a finger, a piece of iron, a tongue moved a hair's breadth, a sentence finished, a household straightened, and then a breath that the work had not asked for. He filed it on a place of its own with a finger between it and the five held objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath and his own gaps. It was a private keeping gathered once and not spent. It was not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not an eighth of anything, not a habit, not a way, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a mending, not a cause, not a payment and not a rule, and no payer stood under it. It gathered toward the far-off hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together, and that hand was not his and was still far off.

He did not join the new interval to Jorra's dust, to the damper's double bite, to the uncalled debt, to the covered grain, to the charcoal, to Ilva's strip or to the Ash 23 direction. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, while the claim went on gathering interest against it. Nothing under this roof was made a lesson of the extra breath, and nobody under this roof noticed that a breath had been held at all.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it, beside \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. He passed his hand over blade and sill as over a tool put back where it belongs, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching what little light there was and giving it back, and the hand went away when the checking was done. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the knuckle creases in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it.

He checked the Bone 26 thumb's resting and found it where it had been put and not made again. He checked the Ash 14 held breath at the sill --- hand to the sill, over the knife as the house checks, away when the checking was done, no breath held at the top of the reach --- and found it where it had been put and not made again. Neither was a sixth held object, a seventh, a tenth stopping, the calling of the loan or the seventh year given, and no payer stood under either. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled. He did not look along the road. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check; the returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 beat and the Frost 5 beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule, and neither the handful, the thumb's resting, the pot-crack resting, the new holding in the iron nor the held breath at the sill was made a fifth. Two ears under one roof kept their equality, and the new keeping under the same roof was not laid across the loan, the knife or the flagstones.

In the last of the afternoon the whole file was turned out as instruments on \place{the towel} and checked and left as it lay. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. The heard bell stood four, four and a gap in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between; eight arithmetics of the year were eight and not one account, both of the dark month's subtractions locked and unread up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, and the finding kept on a place of its own with no payer and no doctrine standing up out of it. Nine record-first hands with no tenth; nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled; five held objects with the pot-crack resting beside them and the hand-at-the-sill breath beside that, none a sixth and none a seventh. The row stood at five askings, the burned slate at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings; the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid. And the holding of the twenty-sixth lay on its own place beside the five objects, the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath and the boy's gaps, with a finger between every pair, gathered once and not spent.

No voice came over the boards to make a sixth telling, a third shut-room telling, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a tenth hand or a tenth stopping, and none was supplied to make a row come out even. Nothing from the fifth, the seventeenth or the twenty-third had crossed this afternoon as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the yard to \place{the Vail kiln-house} at the half-pace, wide around the seven places, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed, and left the thread where it lay, frozen in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath} had ever been given an instruction about the thread. She went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted. Repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed.

The hand outside the wool did not close on the rim. It could not have reached it by two inches, and the two inches were not a punishment and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched --- a going of the light and a bed are not openings, six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of \place{Ash} without a name, and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. Under the roof the surgeon's register took the body in its old order: the crust gone stiffer and browner in the dry heat, two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool and its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread frozen and unwiped; cold at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rides back, as at the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{The Afterdrop} waiting past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; and underneath the knowing the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait. The river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. It was the note's own finishing that ended the interval, and that was the whole of the honesty in it.

He filed it as answer two hundred and eighty-six: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the pot-crack resting, the new holding in the iron, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and eighty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The day's one real event was a fraction of a held breath in a piece of cold iron that nobody would ever enter, and he had registered it exactly and refused to turn it into a habit, a gift, a sign or a rule. Nothing on the twenty-sixth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. Three hundred and fifty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-five days were told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Ash kept its twenty-sixth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. \place[Ash 30]{Ash 30} stood four mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-six; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped; at the wheel-shed the bent finger had come away from the cold iron and the extra interval stayed where nobody would enter it, a keeping gathered once and kept on a place of its own. Through the finger's-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Forward, not closed.
