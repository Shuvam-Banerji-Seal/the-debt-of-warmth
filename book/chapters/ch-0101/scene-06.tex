The afternoon came on behind a sky the colour of wet ash, the light thin and the cold close at its heels, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down from \place{the Vail kiln-house} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind and the fire to be banked against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool; “it's the river wind,” she told the wind; and to the coals, “Bank yourselves. We'll be back before you've cooled.” To the thing in the wicker she gave nothing at all, the way a woman gives nothing to a pot she has already set where it will take no harm. He went down facing her shoulder, with the crust at two inches and the empty place beside it unnamed, and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back taking the cold first as it always did.

In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the seven places with a look and a step and no number, the way a woman walks around a pot left on the floor in the dark. At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay unswept, and the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw} lay unswept too, though the gone white frost on the north lip of \place{the yard well} had not come back. Under \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had gone around them for years and entered them into nothing. The shallow clay pan's gone round ring held no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word, only the pale scurf of old mud where its belly had sat. On \place{the trough} the skin had thickened until it would have held a poured spoon, and broke under a passing pail and closed behind it without a sound. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field kept its empty place the way a mouth keeps a missing tooth. On \place{the flat stone by the well} the silt-clouded plate of ice stood lifted a little out of the path of feet, its clouded face turned up to a sky that would not clear it. And at the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, the yard went widest of all, with a look that stayed the length of a step and then went off the place as a man takes his eyes off a woman who has been crying. Seven places, entered none. A thing entered wants a payer standing under it, and none stood.

He took the carrying down as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the half-pace of the feet, the rasp of rope against the stiff well-rod, the dry tick of cooling clay, the low river under all of it. The composed face above him was not composure; he knew the difference between a face arranged for a room and a face at rest, and had once made a career of the difference. In the yard the small sounds kept their exact places and nothing had changed hands since the day before. Nothing from \place{Ash} 22, 23 or 24 had crossed under this roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment; the word that had gone over \place{the boards} on the twenty-third stood nowhere to be asked and had bought no instruction here. He did not expect an answer and he did not stop wanting one. That was the work, and it went on whether or not anybody called it work.

Three coverings the cold had laid on three objects stayed weather and were entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair because likeness without a payer is only a likeness and not a cause: the gone white frost on the well's north lip, the skin on the trough that broke and closed, the plate of ice that lifted its clouded face a little out of the path. No fourth likeness was made among them. The dressed grain lay covered under wool where she had turned it once with a touch lighter than a woman uses on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object of \place{Bone} 22 as a keeping and not a second handful, for two would be a habit and three a way, and both want a payer standing and none stood.

The yard's hunts and repairs came and went as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders at the well and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, told her to ask the weather, which had carried the snow and could carry the price; he went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. Another with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was told to ask the pot; the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and late. To each of them \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house,” agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot anywhere, and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. There was no \person{Ilva} at the well this afternoon, no \person{Berr Clay} and no \person{Hessa Rale} on any road, no runner, no collector up or down the clay road, no market, no Cross, no bell, and no ownerless voice over the boards. The joining of the fifth, the direction of the seventeenth and the direction of the twenty-third stood where they had been put and did not repeat, and bought no lesson under this roof.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, and beside the seventh \person[Garl Vail]'s unforged collector sentence lay with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a man's hand could be checked on it and where a hand could also slip it off. He passed his hand over the blade and over the sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching what little light there was and giving it back, and the hand went away when the checking was done. “Six counted,” he told the wood. “The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark; grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it.

He checked the resting of the thumb on Bone 26 and found it where it had been put and did not make it again. He checked the held breath he had left at the knife-sill on Ash 14 and found that where it had been put too, and did not make that again either: the hand to the sill, over the knife as the house checked, away when the checking was done, and no breath held at the top of the reach. Neither was a sixth held object, neither a seventh, neither a tenth stopping, neither the calling of the loan, and there was no payer standing under either. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as it had stopped nine times before, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled. He did not look along the road. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, and the returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed where it lay on the flagstone's own place with the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 beat and the \place[Frost]{Frost 5} beat, four held changes on four places and not a rule. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot, unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiveness. Two ears had kept the same silence under one roof for years and the equality had moved five times and none of the changes had stood up and called itself a rule. Under that same roof the new holding of the day before lay where nobody would enter it, and he did not lay it across the loan, the knife, the flagstones or any of the other four.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into the back room before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, while the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and he kept the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies.

Then he took his finger out of the iron. He did not leave it standing a breath longer than the work required. The extra interval he had left there on the twenty-sixth was where he had put it and he left it there, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and a habit and a way both want a payer standing under them and none stood. He made no song of it, named it to nobody, did not look toward the basket and did not check whether anybody under the roof had noticed. He gave no name to the men in the back room and did not say the room was cold. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn while the claim went on gathering interest against it. He said nothing at all about the iron. The household straightened around the absence of a second holding the way it had straightened around the first, without an order from anybody, and the boy took the straightening apart by what was there and by what was not: a finger in, a tongue moved, a finger out, and nothing held at the end of it.

The whole file came out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments come out on a towel, and was checked and left as it lay. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. The heard bell stood four, four and a gap in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into any count and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between. Eight arithmetics of the year --- the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, and the first two of Ash --- stood behind one unsigned figure, one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, eight readings eight and not one account; both of the dark month's subtractions rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr Clay's box, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing up out of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, Ilva's ninth on a place of its own with a finger between it and Hessa Rale's eighth. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled. The row stood at five askings with no witness, the burned slate at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, the shut-room tellings at two, the well-saying at four shapings, and the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, advanced and not paid. Beside the five held objects, the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and his own gaps, with a finger between every pair, lay the holding of Ash 26: gathered once, not spent, not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not an eighth of anything, not a habit, not a way, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a mending, not a cause, not a payment and not a rule, with no payer standing under it. The far-off hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his. No voice had come over the boards on the twenty-seventh to make a sixth telling, a third shut-room telling, a sixth asking, a fifth shaping, a tenth hand or a tenth stopping, and none had been supplied to make a row come out even.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace, going wide around the seven places with a look and a step and no number, and set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay, frozen in the dry kiln heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath} had ever been given an instruction about the thread. At \place{the hearth} the fire came before the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She said both proverbs to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched --- a going of the light and a bed are not openings; six openings in six months are six and not a rule, the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash without a name and not spent, and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went \lore{water over water} under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. He filed it as answer two hundred and eighty-eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the new holding in the iron, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and eighty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond the reaching fingers. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-seventh. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Three hundred and fifty-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-six days were told by nobody; the \lore{carried fire}'s fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the twenty-seventh had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. Ash kept its twenty-seventh day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood three mornings ahead and was named only as a date, never as a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. No ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh opening made due, no sixth hearing, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping invented to make a row come out even. Three counts in three places --- the carried fire at four and a gap under the floor, the boards four, four and a knot overhead, the river low over lower under the door --- did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood; the bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into any of them. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution here had ever thought to tax.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and eighty-eight; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; and the extra interval stayed where nobody would enter it, a keeping gathered once and not made again. Through the finger's-width door the river went water over water, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, while the boards overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Ash 30 stood three mornings ahead and was named only as a date. Forward, not closed.
