\daymark{338}{Ash 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with his hands folded under the wool and took the dark as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. There was the dry tick of clay going on cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river low over lower under the finger's-width door. Three hundred and thirty-eight stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and fifty-eight days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-eight days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Nothing from the seventh or the eighth of \place{Ash} crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The joining said over the boards, the fourth shaping at the well and the turned writing of the runner stood each on a place of its own with a finger between, and had not come into the night to stand up as an answer; none was spent or promoted while he lay there with nothing to spend it with.

The inventory came in its old order, for order is the one thing a field will give a man who cannot move in it. One stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery through the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a thing nobody has been told to wipe is a thing that stays where it froze. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with no order to go farther and no order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping for a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face cold puts on a man, the composed face that is not composure, the ease that is no ease; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, which is the reverse and comes sooner than anybody likes. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle brought within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through every morning of his life under this roof: the surgeon taking the inventory exactly and entering it nowhere, the infant underneath taking none of it and wanting. He had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth; the joke cost him nothing and was not funny.

First light came grey and ordinary, with no market, no Cross and no bell waiting on it. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she had gone before every basket since the boy came under the roof, and gave the instruction to the coals and not to a person. “It's \lore{the thaw},” she told them, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the pot's edge drawing the ash into a small dark line which she did not smooth. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; the little-door burn on the back of her left hand opened and shut on nothing as the hand worked. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. The fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she did not supply the pull or look toward the floor to see whether anything stood there to supply it. One grey streak at her temple showed pale in the first of the light and she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and the burn closed on the hair and then on air. Nothing in the room turned toward \place{the bed}. The boy heard the handle lift and the small sound of the wire taking the weight before the light showed him the hand that had lifted it, and took both sounds as findings: the small sounds first. He took the hearing as it came, four and a gap, a count in one place that would not rise into another, because agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to either.

Her finger lay on the front edge of \place{the loaf shelf} as she passed, so that the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a number. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beside the shelf; her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air, and went on to the next thing. From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she said to the pot; and to the fire, “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” The sayings went to a pot and a fire and never to a person, and had improved in the keeping in the three years since a man who mended pots and would not let anyone mend him had last said them, and were very likely not as good as he had said them. The hairline of the original break stood dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and she left it showing, because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. At \place{the rack end} of the day's work the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth; she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the day before and turned it once and gave no figure, and her fingers went in and came out and did not close, so that no handful was lifted. The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The boy took the half-beat apart as he took everything apart, by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand's intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched. A morning and a hearth are not openings, and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the fired kiln behind the wall stood cold after Ash 2 and nobody went out to look at it or spoke of what it had done. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and the sixth was held behind the household without a name and was not brought out into the morning to stand up as a sign. Nothing had been opened, joined or paid in the night. She went about the room with her hands never still and never speaking, and gave the instruction to the wool, the pot, the fire and the river wind and never to the basket. When she carried a cup the little-door burn closed on the handle and went on closing on nothing after the cup was set down, and the day went on being kept in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces and never in a figure. No new bare place came into the room and no third gap stood up beside the two on the shelf and no fourth likeness was made into a cause.

The house kept the absent man without a story. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's crease at the left shoulder on its peg; the wheel-seat out in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and she let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with a finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to his own name. The unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at the kindling with nobody's name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without being called, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and thirty-eight days and no lesson had been made of any of it.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall at the rack end, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh at the kindling and the sixth opening at \place{the kiln mouth} sat each on its own place with a finger between every pair; none was made again on the ninth of Ash, none was a habit, a way, a fifth held change, a hearing, a gift or a rule, and no payer stood under any. \person{Tarn}'s thumb on the seventh notch was checked and left where it had been laid; \person{Ilva}'s ninth hand stayed in her sleeve a pace from the open slate and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth stayed on its own place, and no tenth hand was filed anywhere. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and the loan uncalled; five askings of the ownerless question stood with no sixth and no row named. The burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing, and the joining said over the boards on the fifth of Ash stood beside it on a place of its own, a first joining said by nobody standing, not a sixth telling, not a third telling of the shut room, not a hearing, not a cause, not a confession and not a rule. The well-saying stood at four shapings with no fifth and no witness, and the fourth shaping stood on its own place beside it; the turning of the runner's writing toward herself on the sixth stood on a place of its own beside that, a sighting and not a payment, and saying was not joined to sighting. The three movements and the five held objects lay on separate places with a finger between them and between them and the strip in the woman's sleeve, \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate and the charcoal beneath the shelf; neither made the runner a reader nor the boy at the wheel a payer, and none was spoken of under the roof.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places. None rose into another, because agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; an empty place kept empty was a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh hearing. The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between; one in twelve was printed, read and unchallenged, and had not been paid by anybody who stood where the boy could see.

Six arithmetics of the year rode locked up \place{the clay road} in Berr Clay's box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space, six readings six and not one account. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it. It was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool and read over by a number that did not know its cost, the five held objects, the handful, the thumb's resting or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. No doctrine stood up out of any of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Ash 10 (Day 339), the dark month's first Account-day and market, stood one morning ahead and was named only as a date, not a promise, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; Ash's first arithmetic belonged to its own morning. The room went on about its ordinary work around him. A board knocked somewhere and was not mended that day; a woman's step went past the door and went on; the light came up through the finger's-width opening and laid a thin grey bar on the floor that he could not see from where he lay. He took the ordinariness as the only honest record there was. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. No comfort was given and none was taken. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found, and the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the growing light.

On the morning before the first reading of the dark month the house was exactly as it had been, and the only thing that had changed in three hundred and thirty-eight days was the keeping of it: another answer, another object worth nothing at two inches of air. He could not have told a soul what he noticed and would not have been believed if he had; he took that as the ordinary condition of his days and not as a grievance, for a grievance is an account with a payer standing under it and none stood.

In the first full of the morning, with the river's note low and going lower under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and fifty-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the joining over the boards, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, Ilva's wood nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and fifty-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Three hundred and thirty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the ninth of Ash had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; the fired kiln stood cold behind the wall; and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door. Ash 10 stood one morning ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Forward, not closed.
