The afternoon of Frost 26 went out of the \place{Clay Yard} the way the last heat goes out of a baker's peel left leaning against a cold hearth: not all at once, and with no word spoken over it, but by degrees from the handle inward. \person[Corin]{The boy} came down to the edge of the work in the middle of that going. He did not know the afternoon had turned until the warmth that had stood in the fold at his ear began to thin, and he felt the thinning before he felt the cold, as a man feels the pressure ease off a wound before he feels the wound itself. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold, its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and fifteen days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

There were two men at the edge of the work and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. A hundred and sixty-five honest answers stood behind him on the sixth place, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor of the house he had left, \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-five days with its payer unnamed; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the wind, telling it to keep the river wind off his ears and never telling the child who could not have answered. She set the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lay, the way a pot is set where it will not be knocked, and went a little away from it. The yard had the look of a room somebody had swept around a sleeping animal: a wide border left where the feet went, and the animal itself undisturbed in the middle of it. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had once watched a surgeon's hand leave a field --- for what it did not touch.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line lay where the ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw} lay unswept beside it, and she swept neither, giving the broom its word before anybody: \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in; it'll cost more to take out.} Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken \lore{birth-slates} kept their place in the dust, and the yard went around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no hand picked up a piece and no slate was entered. The round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept its shape on the stone with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; a pan was gone from it years ago, and the ring had outlasted every account that might have said what became of it. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place lay where the hands had gone in on Seed 15, and in the returning line another place lay where a hand had gone in and had not come out. The boy carried the missing hand as an object on a place of its own, not named, not paid, not joined to the crust in the fold or to the man carried out on Day 188. Seven were seven and not an account; an account wants a payer standing under it, and nobody stood under any of the seven.

At the rack end she lifted one corner of the wool from the dressed grain to feel for damp and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. The arithmetic standing three mornings off would read over a thing whose price it did not know, and the space between the figure and what the grain had cost would stay unsigned; he had learned to know that space the way another man knows a face across a room, and he did not greet it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the yard's failing light, and she did not look toward the basket.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the white \lore{frost-bloom} stayed on the north lip where it had stayed since Frost 8, past noon and past the going of the light and onto days that had no business keeping it; the rope gave its dry rasp as a woman drew, and the water in \place{the trough} was skinned edge to edge with a skin that held a boot's print and would not let it go. On \place{the flat stone by the well}, set where no foot went, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt, lifted out of the trough on Frost 9 and told where to stand. The yard called all of it weather, and weather was entered nowhere; the boy kept the white lip, the trough's skin and the plate on three places of their own with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness and no cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood.

\person{Ilva} drew with the pail against her knee. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out; her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft. She was of the yard in the way the rack and the trough were of the yard, and she went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all. The district would not have turned its head twice for her. A woman with a pot came up from the strips and stood near, turning the pot in her hands as women turn a pot when they are about to put a question to it rather than to a person. The boy, set at the edge of the work where no slate lay, took the sight of the hands at a distance and did not make of them a witness, a reader or a party to anything.

The neighbour with the pot asked what the firings of the month had taken now the month was nearly out. It was a question with a figure behind it, the kind that goes onto a slate if somebody lets it; the woman put it level into the air and did not press for it, because a woman who has carried a pot up from the strips knows how an answer is given in this yard. A slate lay open on the stone lip of the well where somebody had set it down, the writing turned toward its owner and away from the yard, and nobody read it aloud. The boy lay where the slate did not reach and took the nearness of it as a thing in itself: the open slate lay near the woman who kept a count, and the nearness was not a joining and not a figure and not a cause, and he set it on a place of its own as an absence and not as a bridge.

\scenebreak

Her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. It did not go in; the flat of it came down on the place where the strip lay, as a woman lays a hand flat on a thing she has no mind to take up. The short strip of birch bark with its notches stayed where it was --- not taken out, not read, not asked about, not copied, not counted and not joined to the open slate on the stone lip a pace away --- and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the woman with the pot after the hand had settled, and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the eyes and the word, the half-beat a woman gives when she has decided which of two questions she means to answer. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account,} she said, and gave no figure and no name to the thing she kept. She put the flat of her hand down once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned to look at her twice.

The boy lay at the edge of the work with the file open on \place{the towel} under the fold, and he took the order apart the way he had once taken a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. Hand to the record first --- toward the sleeve where the strip lay; eyes to the asker second; and the mouth after both, with the half-beat clean between the looking and the word. He had seen the order in a midwife laying slates before a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before a crowd, in a keeper's finger beneath a board, in a runner's strap and wrist, in a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, in \person{Berr Clay} wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure, in the soft-cuffed clerk entering a buyer's number before he looked up, in an assessor of \place{Sill} writing eight pigs down before anyone could ask which eight, in \person{Hessa Rale} settling her strap and turning her wrist before her eyes took in the crowd, and in a woman at the pot board turning a cup a quarter and setting it back. One of those hands had been Hessa's, filed as the eighth. This one, taken from the yard's firing work and not from any house or Court, he filed as the ninth on a place of its own beside the eighth, with a finger between every pair, and he filed no tenth. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. Nine were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them; a rule wants somebody to answer to, and nobody stood at the well who would have answered for the notches.

He kept the separations as he kept the finger between. The strip was not joined to Hessa Rale's edge-on slate going up \place{the clay road} toward \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve, nor to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, nor to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate at the out-of-line landing, nor to \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages turned toward a floorboard, nor to \person{Mira Silt}'s two cords, nor to \person[Orra Weir]{Orra}'s knots, nor to the reed-cutter's wool-grey cord, nor to the little tin the woman above the abandoned bed had kept shut. The Frost 14 saying stood on a place of its own with its first, second and third shapings, a finger between every pair, beside Hessa's eighth hand with a finger between that and the nearest shaping; not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a payment and not a joining, because a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of the three stood anywhere to be asked. It was not joined to the strip in the sleeve a pace away. Ilva was not a reader of private keepings, not a party to any account, not Hessa, not the soft-cuffed clerk, not the woman who had spoken the Frost 14 saying, not Marra and not an officer, and she did not know she had been read. The boy had taken the order of a reaching and nothing else; he had not read the stick, asked her about it or taken a figure off it, and he made no tenth hand out of a second sighting of the same keeping.

\scenebreak

Sella Vail heard the exchange where she stood with the broom and went on with the house. She did not bring it under the roof as a lesson, a figure, a mending, a cause or a payment, and if she put it in the private roll nobody is shown she put it there without a number, the way she had put the voice over the boards on Frost 23, and she gave the house no news of it. The yard went around its seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the stayed frost on the well lip, the trough skinned edge to edge and the plate of ice clouded with silt on its flat stone remained weather entered nowhere, no fourth likeness made a cause and no payer named to any.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it unswept. The proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person --- feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and ninety-five days, and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it; the keeping of a man by \lore{not-doings} stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

The light went out of the yard without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked up the clay road in Berr's box toward \place{High Kiln}, the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; no lesson was made of either, and Frost 30 stood three mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. The thread at his mouth lay frozen where it had lain since morning and was left there, because a going of the light is not an opening and nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of \lore{lead glaze} clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it; she had touched it once before the kiln on Frost 2 and touched it at no shelf, well, rack, basket, yard, dusk or going of the light on Frost 26, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale in the going light, and set \place{the pot board}'s rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with the handle turned toward it. There was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and the boy was not given the means to close two inches of air. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The instructions went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket; the basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the next room. The boy took the order of the reaching at the well and set it on the ninth hand's own place as an object, the way a surgeon lays an instrument on a tray where the next hand will find it: hand to record, eyes to asker, mouth after both, the half-beat clean between the looking and the word. He did not promote the order into a reading, a joining, a tenth hand, a hearing, a payment or a rule, and he did not take a figure off the wood. The nearness of the open slate on the stone lip he kept on a place of its own as an absence, with a finger between it and the nine hands, for the slate lay near and was not opened toward him and held no figure that he could read; an absence kept whole is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The hundred and sixty-sixth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and not a virtue. He had once spent a life finding the one error that lets a set of numbers agree; here the finding was to leave them as they stood.

Two hundred and ninety-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, Sill and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and fifteen days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. In the file a pace off at the well the strip stayed in the sleeve under the flat of her hand while she went back to the pail, and under the roof the boy's hand came down on the river's low note of water over water, with the ninth hand left nine.
