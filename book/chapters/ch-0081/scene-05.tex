\daymark{296}{Frost 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

Frost 27 came on its own morning and brought nothing in with it. It was an ordinary Seventhday with no market, no reading at the Cross and no bell, and it came the way cold comes into a house whose fire has been fed and banked and left to its own devices: quietly, by degrees, taking the room one board at a time until the thing that held the heat and the thing that held the cold were the same temperature and neither could tell the other apart. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his two hundred and ninety-sixth day under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and nobody in that house told him so. Six months and one hundred and sixteen days were told by nobody; the count had gone from two hundred and ninety-five to two hundred and ninety-six the way a man sets one board beside another and does not measure the seam, and the small dry knock the second board gives when it settles is not a figure anybody has entered. A hundred and sixty-six honest answers stood behind him, each an object put away where the next hand would find it without looking, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-six days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The house smelled as it had smelled at the going of the light: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under those the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}. At the finger's-width door the river went low over lower, and the kiln answered in fours with the silence where a fifth should have stood, and it was not a bell. Nothing that had stood a pace off at the well the day before came in with the morning as a lesson, a mending, a witness, a cause or a payment; a day that has not earned a thing does not get to spend it.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and sixteen days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. The knowing bought him nothing at two inches of air. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. He had signed a chart once without reading the note turned down in the margin and carried that shape into a country where notes are turned face-down toward floorboards; here he read every note he was not given and entered none of them. The dry heat had drawn the water out of the thread and left it stiff as a wire drawn through tallow, and he could not lift a hand to wipe it, and no instruction had ever been given to anybody to wipe it, and the two facts stood side by side without either becoming a complaint. A man who has spent his life putting things right in rooms where somebody always called for him finds it a strange discipline to lie under a shelf and call nothing, and he had been learning it since his first morning under this roof, and it had not got easier.

\scenebreak

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety-six days; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10 and again at the market on Frost 20, stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; no bell had come on this morning and he would not lend an ear to one that had not earned it.

The told figure stood beside the paid and the space between them had no signature. Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; two readings were two sayings and not one account, and neither had closed the hand that went in on Seed 15 or the man carried out on Day 188. The dressed grain lay covered under wool away from \place{the hearth} at \place{the rack end}, turned with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure, so that any number travelling over it would not know what it cost. The Frost 14 saying stood on a place of its own with its first, second and third shapings, a finger between every pair, beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand with a finger between that and the nearest shaping; not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining and not paid, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of the three stood anywhere to be asked. Beside those, on a place of its own, \person{Ilva}'s ninth hand stayed nine with the notched birch strip unread in a sleeve that was not under this roof today, and he filed no tenth; he did not bring the sleeve under the roof to make a story of it, and the count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. Out beyond the finger's-width door the stayed frost on \place{the yard well}'s north lip, the water in \place{the trough} skinned edge to edge, and the plate of ice clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well} were three coverings laid over three depths that the yard called weather and entered nowhere; he kept them on three places with a finger between and made no fourth likeness and no cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood. Neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words --- the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost 30 stood three mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket, as she had gone every morning of her life in this house, and gave the instruction to the fire and never to the basket. She told it the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The small hours had laid a film of grey over everything, and she moved through it with hands that already knew what they had to do: the wool told to keep the river wind off his ears, the pot told its word, the broom told to go around, the running water told its word, and never a word spoken to the child in the fold who could not have answered. When she came to the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it was unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. At the rack end she lifted one corner of the wool over the dressed grain to feel for damp and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with the handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed.

The boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had learned to watch a surgeon's hand leave a field --- for what it did not touch --- and filed the not-touching on its own place, maintenance and not a message. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. He wanted the thread wiped and the crust lifted and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and he got none of the three and could not ask for any of them. There was no bitterness in the not-asking either, which was the second small betrayal of the morning, and he did not give it a place to stand.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and when a woman went by the door wanting the thaw early and another wanting it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of the clay road and had stood there since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping, the ninth, and he did not make a tenth of it before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of \place{the Debt Loft}, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. The boy kept the seventh where Tarn had set it and entered nothing, for a stopping kept whole is not a payment and a loan uncalled is not a loan forgiven.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door at the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the boy took it as a direction and a sound. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no \lore{Grathic} word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set the iron on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. The burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of a name was neither given nor withdrawn.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person --- feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-six days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked in Berr Clay's box up the road toward High Kiln, and the keeping of a man by \lore{not-doings} stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

\scenebreak

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the next room, and the morning offered him the old temptation: two intervals on \place{the towel}, a held one and an empty one, with a finger's width of nothing between them, and the offer to lay them together and call the joining a total. He had once spent a life finding the one error that lets a set of numbers agree, and the hunger to make these two agree was the oldest hunger in the room, older than the crust and louder than the cold for the space of a breath. He took the empty interval from the file and set it beside the held interval with a finger between, and he put the finger down and took it up again, and both intervals stayed where they had been. He borrowed no house count, supplied no fifth to the kiln, crossed no silence that belonged to another's breathing and made nothing of the nearness but a keeping. The empty interval stayed empty and the held interval stayed held; neither was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and the finger that had parted them went away leaving the gap as it had found it. A keeping is not a spending. The hundred and sixty-seventh answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and the clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. Relief and restraint had the same shape this morning and he did not tell them apart.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and ninety-six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixteen days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. He lay with the crust two inches off and its empty place unnamed. At the edge of his reach the pot rim stood where she had set it, two inches beyond the hand with the handle turned toward it, and Sella went away from the pot board without a look toward the bed.
