The afternoon thinned in the \place{Clay Yard} the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals and left on a cold stone. It did not end; it drew back. The warmth that had stood in the fold at his ear since first light went out of the wool by degrees, and \person[Corin]{the boy} felt the going of it before he felt the cold, the way a man feels the pressure ease before he feels the wound that was under it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. The afternoon went on doing what afternoons do under that roof, and he lay at the edge of it with the file open on \place{the towel} and every gap left gap.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. He had spent a life putting right what could be put right in rooms where somebody always called for him, and here the discipline was to lie under a shelf and call nothing, and it had not got easier in two hundred and ninety-four days. Below the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-four days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place kept empty; he did not invent a seventh to make the row come out even, for a finding filled to please a total is a finding forged. All of it was as it had been at first light, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Nothing in the yard's long afternoon would make it worth more, and he did not expect it to.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the half-pace was not haste and not fear but the distance a woman keeps from a thing that can be entered by anybody with a slate in his hand. She gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the wind, and said \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} to the wool and not to the child who could not have answered. The yard had the look of a room somebody had swept around a sleeping animal: a wide border left where the feet went, and the animal itself undisturbed in the middle of it. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had once watched a surgeon's hand leave a field --- for what it did not touch.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line lay where the ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it unswept, and she swept neither. She gave the broom its instruction before anybody --- \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in; it'll cost more to take out} --- and the bristles went around the two lines and left them as they lay. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken \lore{birth-slates} kept their place in the dust, and the yard went around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no hand picked up a piece and no slate was entered. On the stone at \place{the rack end} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; a pan was gone from it years ago and the ring had outlasted every account that might have said what became of it. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place lay where the hands had gone in on Seed 15, and in the returning line another place lay where a hand had gone in and had not come out. The boy carried the missing hand the way he carried the crust in the fold and the man carried out on Day 188: as an object on its own place, not named, not paid, not joined to anything that stood near it. Seven were seven and not an account; an account wants a payer standing under it, and nobody stood under any of the seven.

At the rack end she lifted one corner of the wool from the dressed grain to feel for damp, and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. The arithmetic standing five mornings off would read over a thing whose price it did not know, and the space between the figure and what the grain had cost would stay unsigned. He had learned to recognize that space the way another man recognizes a familiar face across a room, and he did not greet it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the yard's failing light, and she did not look toward the basket.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the white \lore{frost-bloom} stayed on the north lip where it had stayed since Frost 8, past noon and past the going of the light and onto days that had no business keeping it; the rope gave its dry rasp as a woman drew, and the water in \place{the trough} was skinned edge to edge with a skin that held a boot's print and did not let it go. On \place{the flat stone by the well}, set where no foot went, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt, lifted out of the trough on Frost 9 and told where to stand. The yard called all of it weather, and weather was entered nowhere. The boy kept the white lip, the trough's skin and the plate on three places of their own with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness between them and no cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood. Three coverings laid over three depths were three coverings; a fourth likeness would have been an account with no payer, which is the kind of account this house has learned to leave where it lies. Pale clay dried in the crescents of a woman's nails as she passed with her bucket, and the finer grey rack dust worked into the seam of a thumb she did not scrub out; the boy took the sight of hands at a distance and did not make of them a witness, a reader or a party to anything.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He said \emph{Yes, house} to a woman who wanted the thaw early and \emph{Yes, house} to a man who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of \place{the clay road} and had stood there since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of \place{the Debt Loft}, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no \lore{Grathic} word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set it on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over \place{the boards} and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name was neither given nor withdrawn.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person --- feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-four days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the road toward \place{High Kiln}, and the keeping of a man by \lore{not-doings} stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

\scenebreak

The light went out of the yard without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost 25 went the way Frost 1 had come, without a lesson left on the step. Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. The thread at his mouth lay frozen where it had lain since morning and was left there; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a going of the light is not an opening. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of \lore{lead glaze} clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next opening stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale in the going light, and set \place{the pot board}'s rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with the handle turned toward it. There was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and the boy was not given the means to close two inches of air. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The instructions went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. The basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

The boy wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and he got none of the three and could not ask for any. There was no bitterness in the not-asking either, which was the second small betrayal, and he did not give it a place to stand. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and he could not lift a crust two inches to his mouth; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was the first. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting and neither had authority over the other, and the two of them lay under the roof together in the dark with a two hundred and ninety-fourth day behind them and nobody in \place{Sill}, \place{Low Kiln} or \place{Grath} to tell it.

\scenebreak

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the next room. An intake came from where a woman was counting under her breath, and a second intake came a little after it, closer and shorter, and the boy took the two at their tops and set them side by side with a finger between. The nearer one ended early. He kept the early ending whole as an absence, and did not carry the first intake across to fill it or make a bridge of the space between the two, for a space filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged and a payer invented to stand under it. The letting-go in the next room went on belonging to the woman who let it go and was not crossed or borrowed; it was her breath and her count and not his. The hundred and sixty-fourth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and the clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he gave it no name. Where he had once spent a life finding the one error that lets a set of numbers agree, the finding here was to leave them as they stood.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and ninety-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fourteen days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with the held interval and the empty interval parted by a finger and nothing that had moved, the seven places gone around with a look and no number, the stayed frost white on the well lip beyond the finger's-width door. He lay with the crust two inches off and its empty place unnamed, and the river's low note went quiet under the door.
