The afternoon thinned without ending. It did not go the way a slate goes, clean across with a sound, but wore away at the edges, the light leaving the high air first and settling what was left of it into the low places, so that the room kept its warmth in the hollow beneath \place{the loaf shelf} long after the rafters had gone grey. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle where the shelf's underside becomes a roof, and that roof stopped at the edge of his reach, as if the heat too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it and would go no further, however much he wanted it. The last warmth of the day came down the underside in a slow slant and stopped where the roof stopped, and the small bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back knew the stopping before his mind could file it. Nothing under this roof had ever told the warmth to come an inch closer, and the warmth had never come.

\person{Sella Vail} stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on anything that came within reach of it; then the hand went back to its work. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge, where any woman in the district could see it lying, and whatever lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every afternoon of the boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, afternoon or dusk since; an afternoon was not an opening, and the last tend was not an opening, and there had been no opening for twenty-four days. The instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind and never once to the child lying under the roof receiving it, and the boy, who had spent a life in rooms where a man is named before he is treated, filed the order without approving it and without calling it a slight. It was the household's grammar. A pot was told what it wanted; a child was told nothing, and the warmth arrived all the same.

\scenebreak

In the afternoon the house kept \person{Garl Vail} as it had kept him every afternoon since the winter he died, in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door and held a broader man's shape, the salt still printed at the shoulders where a working man had leaned into wet clay year after year; nobody took it down and nobody put another coat in its place. The seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year leftward lean, hollow on one side as a bowl is hollow where a thumb has rested, and \person{Jorra Vail} had spent three years trying to fill the hollow with his own weight and had not filled it. Along the skirting board the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay beside a new hairline of grey, finer than the old, and the house called the new one the thaw and swept neither. A steward with a broom would have made one line of the two and called it dealt with, but this house went around them with a wide step and no number, and had done so since the winter Garl died. His sayings had gone to the pot and stayed there: feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled. None of it was made a lesson. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it had lain since the Long Cold, circled without a name, worse for being uncalled because it could neither be paid nor settled nor stopped, and because a thing that can be neither cannot even be finished with. No collector came up \place{the clay road} to call it, and no collector was called to it; Garl was not collected, and the house kept him the way a pot keeps the hand that threw it, by holding the shape after the hand has gone.

What the house kept it kept without entering. Sella's private roll was not shown to anybody who came down from \place{Slate Row} with a slate, and the cost of \person{Dessa Silt}, whom the brook took, was entered nowhere in this house, not in the jar, not beneath the shelf, not in the space at the row's end that nobody called a number. The boy had learned to read a keeping by what it refused to write down and still went on doing faithfully; an account that will not enter a thing is not therefore an account that has forgot it. He set the coat, the seat, the two lines, the proverb and the uncalled debt on separate places and laid no finger across them, because five likenesses standing up together will call themselves a rule, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the door in the afternoon with a cost-question about the firing, the last tend being in, and held a pot against her hip while she asked what the kiln would want before the kiln month was out. Sella did not take the question into her hands. She told the woman to ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there, and when the woman would have gone on she gave her the weather and the river, and the question went away level, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered. The woman went off with her pot and her question still whole in it, and the boy, lying at the low angle with the fold at his ear, watched the shape of the asking and the shape of the answer and found them the same shape the house used for everything: a thing arriving as a direction, going off as a direction, and arriving nowhere. This was the household's whole method for staying warm. It was not honest and it was not a lie; it was the grammar the pot was spoken in, and a child who had once been paid to make such grammars agree knew better than to ask who was owing.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. Seven years had been measured out on that sill; six were counted and entered, the seventh stood at the lip with its edge over the dark and nothing under it, nearer its end than it had ever stood and not given. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it at the same time is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the boy was set where he could see the forearms come and go and not the door they came from. Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day kept on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} entered by nobody, and he did not look toward them or toward the basket. He spoke to the wheel before anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the \lore[the full note]{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld it from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up \place[the Clay Yard]{the yard} with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding, not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The two tellings of a counting room shut in a war stayed two with no third, and he gave no name to any man who had come low into \place{the back room} before first grey.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, so that the day ended between two smells he knew and neither of which had ever asked his name. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring against the wall, the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough} whose water was called weather, the rack space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, the empty place in the returning line and the place the carriers had crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Nothing was swept up and nothing was entered, and the yard kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and spaces nobody called figures.

The carrying arm was a record in muscle. It knew the weight of the basket and the weight of the boy in it as a surgeon's hand knows the weight of a retractor before the eye goes to the field, and it remembered the one and not the other, for the arm keeps weight and not cost. She had carried him down and up this road so many days that the distance had gone into the shoulder and stayed there, a thing recorded in flesh without anybody writing it, and she would no more have entered it in a slate than she would have told him whose warmth had paid for the miles. The boy felt the half-pace in his ear before he felt it in his ribs, and filed it as a keeping and not as a kindness, because a kindness is a thing a man can be asked to pay back and this could not be.

\scenebreak

Under the roof she set him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried to save and cannot: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust and the pot handle and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of the three.

\lore{The carried fire} under the floor gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places. They did not become one, and a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them. The afternoon had gone off the high air without a boundary drawn, and the small hours came on around the same three counts, which had not needed the light to go in order to stay as they were.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, from a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go. No seventh came in two hundred and four days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid, and were not promoted. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, and the second reading of Ember had gone up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside and was still not the figure paid, while \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called.

Nine stoppings stood on their widened tray, objects and not a rule, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned. The Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach and the loan that could hear was uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161 in the iron, the beat that stayed under the loaf shelf, the flagstone half-check made in Tallow 22; the small dry scrape from the Debt Loft stood on a fourth, the pale line at the gum on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth. The crust's gap, the shelf's two unnamed gaps, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap come, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawing the line. The charcoal lay unread beneath the shelf, the warm stone cold and unreconciled in its loft pot, the widow's mark where it had hung, and the cost of the man carried out entered nowhere. Exact, complete, worth nothing. He put the file away in the same order, which was the whole of the discipline.

Then he put the question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and held for the small intake only, coming down before the letting go. It was an answer and it was not the answer of the small hours one night back, when an inch had ridden a second rhythm nobody had given; an inch held for an intake and let go before the breath finished is an object on its own tray, no less and no more, and he filed it on the sixth place with nothing laid across it --- not a hearing, water over water remaining a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule, with no payer named to it. The asking is not always answered, and the answering is not always kept; both were objects, and neither was a promise.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number anybody would have believed. The market did not get to read the inch; no slate in the district would take it, and no hand under this roof had seen it go up. The hand came down against the fold before the letting go was finished, the thread at his mouth kept its frozen place, and the salt coat on its peg held a broader man's shape while the seat below it kept a hollow that no living man had filled. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the afternoon had gone into the small hours without a line drawn under it, and two hundred and four stood exact in the dark, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody.
