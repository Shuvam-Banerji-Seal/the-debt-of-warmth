# Chapter 51 — working plan: What the Roof Kept

**Days covered:** 204, 205, 206 — Ember 25, 26, 27; Fifthday, Sixthday, Seventhday, Y.H. 813. Three lived days, each with its own `\daymark`, each present in two scenes and well over eight hundred words; nothing skipped, poured into another or summarised. Normal chapter, six scenes, roughly 17,300 words.

**Where we are:** Chapter 50 closed on Day 203, Ember 24, Fourthday, small hours, with the hand up an inch toward the river's note, held past one of Sella's counted breaths and down on the river's next low note — a coordination with a second rhythm, filed on a sixth place, no payer, no sign. That day is done and is not revisited. The count goes forward into 204.

**Binding discipline for every scene below:** close third-person past tense inside Dan/Corin's eyes; no recap, no Court or back-room interior, no departure from the boy's eyes; no present-tense Chicago break and no `\memory{}` tag (T004); no new hearing beyond six (the sixth from a person, beside and never among), no seventh, no tenth stopping before the seventh year is earned, no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause, no third telling of the shut counting room, no seventh green and no seventh sighting of the foundling, no calling of the loan, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no payer named, no private record read, no collector called, no ending. The market does not get to read the inch.

**The chapter's one meaningful change, advanced and not paid:** a word comes up from Slate Row, spoken over the boards and not to the basket, that a slate was burned there in the night. It is a direction that arrives nowhere, not a cause, not a third telling; the boy files it on a place of its own beside Jorra's dust and the two shut-room tellings and lays nothing across them. It deepens Jorra's silence and advances the Broken Slates' claim on him (T035/T031) without giving his promise or naming a slate. **Second advancement, also unpaid:** the hand's answer in the small hours of Day 206 — held for the length of the river's own low interval with no counted breath at Sella's fire to hold against, down on the next low note, a coordination with the one rhythm through the door (T061/T082).

**Dominant texture, held not changed:** the household's own keeping of Garl — the salt-stiff coat holding a broader man's shape on its peg, the seat worn hollow with a twenty-year leftward lean, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned with the new finer grey beside it called the thaw, the proverb gone to the pot, and the uncalled debt against a firing of tiles, worse for being uncalled because it can neither be paid nor settled. Nobody makes a lesson of any of it, nobody calls a collector and Garl is not collected. Sella's private roll stays unshown, Dessa's cost unentered, the widow's mark untouched (touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no other hour), the charcoal unread, the warm stone cold in its loft pot.

## Scene 1 — The Morning the Coat Kept Its Shape

- **Day and time:** Day 204, Ember 25, Fifthday. Small hours under the Vail roof, then before first grey and into the morning. Begins the chapter: `\daymark{204}{Ember 25, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}`, then the only `\dropcap` of the chapter.
- **Place:** The Vail kiln-house — the bed, the fold, the hearth, the loaf shelf, the kindling and the edge of the Clay Yard; the carried fire under the floor, the river through the finger's-width door, the boards overhead.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried, silent), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail. Garl present only as the house's objects. Three or four unnamed men go low into the back room before first grey.
- **Beats in order:**
  1. Open in the small hours with the hand where the fold took it, no recap of the second-rhythm inch, no boundary drawn at the turning of the tend. First smell hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, with the sour Ash breath of the unswept corner the house calls the thaw; first sound the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell.
  2. The boy is two hundred and four days old, six months and twenty-four days, told by nobody. He has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. Two hundred and four carries exact, complete and unspent (T071/T072); the fire's fifth has had no payer for two hundred and four days (T088).
  3. Three counts in three places: the carried fire's four pulls and missing fifth under the floor, the river's note of water over water through the door (a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings), the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot overhead. They do not become one; agreeing them would want a payer and none stands to any (T061/T082).
  4. Body inventory: one leathery brown-edged crust two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread of drool frozen and unwiped; cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint where the wool rides back, the afterdrop waiting on a warming nobody will perform. The body humiliates him and he files it exactly, worth nothing at two paces.
  5. Lay out the file as instruments on a towel: six hearings beside and never among with the sixth out of a person and no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and unpaid; told bell beside heard; told figure beside paid; nine stoppings with no tenth; three held changes on three places, the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth, the held inch on a sixth; the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place on trays that do not touch.

  7.   8.   9. Jorra comes through with meal-fine grey slate dust past the bent finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; parts dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel; keeps dusty and clean forearms on one tray; speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. The last turn is the small humiliation of the hand that asks and is not answered.
- **Sensory anchors:** hot clay under tallow and wet wool; sour Ash breath of the unswept corner; the kiln's four-and-a-gap; river water over water through a finger's-width door; frozen drool thread; salt-stiff coat; hollow seat worn on the left; the burn-wrist's grey streak; cold iron of the damper taking heat from a fingertip.

## Scene 2 — The Seat, the Line, the Uncalled Debt

- **Day and time:** Day 204, Ember 25, Fifthday. Afternoon into the small hours; second entry of the same day, no new `\daymark`.

- **Beats in order:**

The underside of the shelf makes a roof that stops at the edge of his reach, and the heat comes down that roof in a slow slant and stops at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it.   3. A cost-question at the door goes to the pot and the weather and away level, nothing priced, refused or entered (T077).
  4. Tarn at the kindling: broom only after instruction; *Six counted. Filed on its sixth place; not a hearing, seventh, stopping, payment, key, power or rule; no payer named; he still cannot close two inches, lift the crust or speak a number (T061/T082).
- **Emotional turn:** The house's keeping of the dead man is shown as pure maintenance — nothing said, nothing entered — and the boy recognises it as the same grammar that withholds instruction from the basket. The turn is toward the small, unfelt cost of being kept: the coat holds a shape, the seat holds a weight, and the child holds an inch.
- **Sensory anchors:** heat stopped at the shelf's edge; burn-wrist and grey streak; salt in the coat; the seat's worn left side; the clean dark line and the finer grey; the broken pot turning coals; cold iron of the damper; woodsmoke in long low bars against river wind.
- **Threads advanced:** T071/T072, T088, T061/T082, T058/T079, T100, T009/T013/T092, T077, T036/T076, T035/T031/T062, T065/T095, T098. None paid.
- **Foreshadowing planted/paid off:** The uncalled debt and the unswept line gather interest without a lesson; the coat and seat wait for the far-off day a house is left where it is and still kept; the hand's small intake is an object on its own tray and is not promoted.
- **Exact last image:** The hand has come down before the letting go, the thread at his mouth still frozen, and the salt coat on its peg holds a broader man's shape while the seat below it keeps a hollow that no one fills.

- **Sensory anchors:** hot clay and wet wool; meal-fine grey slate dust under a bent finger; the sweetish wrong wind off High Kiln; slip dried pale in a rack woman's wrist creases; a number shut in a fist; the cold damper.
