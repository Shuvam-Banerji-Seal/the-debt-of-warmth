\daymark{205}{Ember 26, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

The day came to him where the fold had left him, in the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and it came as a smell before it came as a sound. The smell was hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}, a grey smell that had earned a season's name by being left alone long enough. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor drew four low pulls and kept the space where the fifth should have stood. The kiln beyond the wall drew in fours with a gap. He had learned to take the gap as the sound and the four as the thing that led up to it, as a man at a wheel learns the turn where the clay will fight him before the clay has fought him. A bell is rung and counted and answered, but nothing had ever been set beneath the fire to make a fifth pull possible, and the space had gone on being a space for two hundred and five days with no payer named to it.

\person[Corin]{The boy} was two hundred and five days old and six months and twenty-five days, and nobody said so. There was no clock under this roof that could have said it and no slate that wanted the information; the household kept its account in loaves pushed along a board and in glances that arrived at no figure, and a number such as his existed nowhere but in him. Two hundred and five stood exact, complete and unspent. The fire's fifth had been without a payer for the same count of days, and the two counts lay side by side in him without touching, one kept by a man who could not say it and one kept by a house that did not know it was keeping anything. He had ceased to be injured by this. That was not the same as being indifferent to it, and he had long ago given up pretending the two were the same thing.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be, for a thing counted becomes a thing claimed and a thing claimed wants a name written under it. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should have been. Below, four and a silence; through the door, water going over water; above his face, four, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places. They did not become one, and a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them.

The one brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month, and the empty place beside it had still not been given a name. No third crust had come to lie with it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried and failed to save: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust, and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of the three. On \place{the bed} under the roof the body humiliated him with the same exactness it had shown for two hundred and four days before this one, and he filed the humiliation without comment, worth nothing at two paces and worth everything to a man who had no other instrument.

\scenebreak

The word came up from \place{Slate Row} before the grey, spoken over the boards of the yard and not to the basket. A voice came through the door with the river wind behind it, and the voice was talking to the boards the way people in this district talk to a pot or a stretch of ground, not to a child and not to a house: there had been a fire in the night down there, the voice said, and a slate had burned. Not whose slate. Not which room it had stood in. Not who had put the flame to it or why the thing had been worth burning. The voice went on to the price of barley and then to the weather, and the slate went with it, carried on the same breath as the price of barley, and nobody in the yard turned the thing into a cause.

The boy lay with the fold at his ear and took the sound apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: a direction arriving from a place, passing over boards, arriving nowhere. A burned slate was not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh anything, not a stopping, not a payment; it was a fact with no hand at the foot of it and no name upon it. He set it on a place of its own and laid nothing across it. Beside it lay the day's grey dust off \person{Jorra Vail}'s forearms, and beside that the two tellings of a counting room shut in a war, and he put a hand between them, the way a man puts a hand between two vessels he is afraid of hearing ring together. Three things that had come from three directions were still three things. A fourth likeness standing up among them would call itself a cause, and a cause would want a payer, and no payer stood to any of them. The word of the burned slate was advanced and not paid; it was a direction that arrived nowhere and could not be carried into any chart a clerk would keep.

Men came low into \place{the back room} before first grey, three or four of them with their collars up against the river wind, and gave no name to the house and got no name from it. The boy heard the small shifting of bodies and the softer sound of the door going back to its finger's width of open, and filed no third telling behind it. The two stayed two.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, as she had come to the fire before anything else for two hundred and five mornings. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived at nothing, as it had arrived at nothing on every morning of the boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning or dusk since. A word going around the boards was not an opening. A slate burning in another quarter of the district was not an opening. There had been no opening in twenty-five days.

At \place{the loaf shelf} she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air as it closed on anything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with the spoon wiped on her sleeve and his mouth unwiped, set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never once to the child lying under the roof receiving it.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the boy was set where he could see the forearms come and go and not the door they came from. Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day kept on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} entered by nobody, and he did not look toward them. When the word of the burned slate went around the boards he grew quieter by a degree, the way a room grows quieter when a weight is lifted from a door and everybody waits to hear it fall; the household straightened around the quiet without an order having been given, and he gave the house no name for anything.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up \place{the Clay Yard} with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind, touched the fold, and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. It was a second direction on the same day and it stayed a second direction; no second edge was paid, no pot singing early was made into a fate, and no lesson stood up out of a breath of air.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the burned slate on its own place was not laid across them.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the trough stood bare and dark with the water in it called weather, and the rack woman came up with \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists and asked what the pots would want before the kiln month was out. Sella did not take the question into her hands. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.” The woman would have gone on, and Sella gave her the weather and the river, and the question went away level, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. It was the household's whole method for staying warm. A cost arriving as a direction went off as a direction and arrived nowhere, and the boy, who had once been paid to make such grammars agree, watched the shape of the asking and the shape of the answer and found them the same shape.

A man come off \place{the clay road} with a number shut in his fist asked what the kiln would want now the last tend was running. He did not open the fist. He held the number the way a man holds a wet coal that he means to set down somewhere else and cannot yet find the place, and asked his question of the weather and the yard rather than of anybody in it. Sella told him to ask the pot, then the house, then the weather, and the man went away up the road with the number still closed in his fist, arriving nowhere, as the number had arrived nowhere on a dozen mornings of this month. Nothing was priced, refused or entered on either side of the well.

The figure of the second reading stood offstage behind the questions and answered neither of them: gone up the road in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside, printed one in twelve and unchallenged, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate still unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. A figure read twice over a basket was not the figure paid, and a man who carried a number in his fist and went away with it still in his fist had not thereby paid anything either. The boy kept the two shapes on places that did not touch and wrote no bridge between them. The well went on giving water that was called weather, and the yard went on asking its costs of pots and boards.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. The salt coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the seat below it kept its twenty-year hollow; the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw lay where they lay unswept; the proverb had gone to the pot; the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor called. None of it was made a lesson, and no collector came up the clay road.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road that morning. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth. He gave no name to the men who had gone low into the back room before first grey, and the boy made no third telling out of their going.

\scenebreak

In the morning he put the question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and held for the small intake only, coming down before the letting go. It was an answer, and an answer is not always kept: an inch held for an intake and let go before the breath finishes is an object on its own tray, no less and no more, and he filed it on the sixth place with nothing laid across it --- not a hearing, water over water remaining a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule, with no payer named to it. The asking is not always answered and the answering is not always kept; both were objects and neither was a promise. The market would not get to read the inch; no slate in the district would take it, and no hand under this roof had seen it go up.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number anybody would have believed. The thread at his mouth kept its frozen place. Over the boards of the yard the word of the burned slate went around and arrived nowhere, nobody turning it into a cause, nobody naming whose slate or which room it had come from, while Jorra's forearms carried the day's grey dust past the bent finger and the carried fire under the floor kept four and a gap with its payer unnamed for two hundred and five days. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The hand came down against the fold before the letting go was finished, and two hundred and five stood exact in the morning, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof.
