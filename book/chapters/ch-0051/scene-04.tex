The afternoon under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof thinned without ever arriving at an end, the way a pot cools: the high air gave its heat up first, and the middle air went after it, and the last of the warmth settled into the low places where it had been told it was permitted to stay --- beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot on its bank of ash, and in the fold of wool at his ear where one leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from the skin. Nothing in this house had given the heat an instruction to quit those places, so it stayed, as a thing stays where it is put when nobody thinks to move it. It was a household that kept what it had by leaving it alone, and the leaving alone was the whole of the keeping.

\person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the low angle where the underside of the shelf came down and stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that little roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as though it too had been given an order and had come to the end of it. The crust had gone leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month; it kept the shape of the barley it had once been the way a spent coal keeps the shape of the wood that made it, and the empty place beside it had still not been given a name. No third crust had come to lie with it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody beneath any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the limit of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the sequence the way a man knows the order of a thing he has tried and failed to save: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust, and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by someone who would have known what a number counted, and got none of the three, and had got none of them for two hundred and five days.

Below the floor \lore{the carried fire} drew four low pulls and kept the space where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for all of those two hundred and five days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be, for a thing counted becomes a thing claimed, and a thing claimed wants a name written under it. Above, the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have been. Below, four and a silence; through the door, water going over water; overhead, four, four and a knot. Three counts in three places, and they did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under each of them, and no payer stood to any of them. He had stopped trying to be injured by this, which was not the same thing as not wanting it.

\scenebreak

The word of the burned slate went around the yard once more in the afternoon, and went around it the way the river wind goes around a stack of pots --- touching everything, belonging to nothing, leaving no hand upon it. It came up off the boards and passed the rack and came back again, carried on the same breath as the price of barley and the state of the weather, and nobody in the yard turned it into a cause. Nobody said whose slate had burned. Nobody said which room it had stood in, or who had put a flame to it, or why the thing had been worth the burning. \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with that day's meal-fine grey dust ground past the bent little finger and under the nails, and when the word reached the boards he grew quieter by the same degree he had grown quiet in the morning, the way a room grows quieter when a weight is lifted from a door and everyone waits to hear it fall. The household straightened around the quiet without anybody giving an order for it. He gave the house no name for anything.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, gone around by feet and brooms and entered by nobody; the yard had long ago learned to step wide of them the way it stepped wide of a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. No third telling of the counting room shut in the war came up out of the afternoon to join the two that stood already in the boy's file; the two stayed two, as they had stayed through three mornings of unnamed men going low into the room off the shed. The boy kept the burned-slate word on a place of its own beside the dust off Jorra's forearms and beside those two tellings, and he put a hand between the three the way a man puts a hand between two vessels he is afraid of hearing ring together. Three things that had come from three directions were still three things at the end of the afternoon. A fourth likeness standing up among them would have called itself a cause, and a cause would have wanted a payer, and no payer stood to any of them. The word was advanced and not paid. It had arrived from a place, had passed over boards, and had arrived nowhere, and it could not be carried into any chart a clerk would keep.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket, as she had come to the fire before everything else for two hundred and five days. She told it that the grey in the corner was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived at nothing, as it had arrived at nothing on every day of the boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, afternoon or dusk since. A word going around the boards was not an opening. A slate burning in another quarter of the district was not an opening. There had been no opening in twenty-six days.

At the shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on anything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board --- the charcoal tally planted on the first Account-day of Ember, unread, uncounted, uncopied --- stayed where it lay. She had put a finger beneath that board on one day long ago and had kept it from beneath it on every day since, and the keeping was as exact as the touching had been.

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, holding his head with two fingers behind the ear, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not his mouth, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never once to the child lying beneath the roof receiving it. “The house will keep it,” she said to the fire where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and went on with her work, and the house went on keeping it.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it --- the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. The salt coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the seat below it kept its twenty-year hollow; the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw lay where they lay unswept; the proverb had gone to the pot; the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor called. None of it was made a lesson, and no collector came up \place{the clay road}.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said “Yes, house” to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. A thing held nine times was still owed, and the silence beneath the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth. He gave no name to the men who had gone low into the room off the shed before first grey, and the boy made no third telling out of their going, and no collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the boy was set where he could see the forearms come and go and not the door they came from. Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day held on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He had grown quieter since the word from \place{Slate Row} had gone around the boards, and the quiet went before him into the shed like a cold air preceding a door.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up \place{the Clay Yard} with his saying, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that wrong wind, touched the fold, and went out on the same wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. It was a direction going around the yard like the other one; no second edge was paid, no pot singing early was made into a fate, and no lesson stood up out of a breath of air.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave a little back in reverse of the core. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161, the held beat beneath the shelf on Day 166 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the burned slate on its own place was not laid across them.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was now gone without a word, around \place{the trough}, bare and dark, with the water in it called weather, around the rack space with its broken birth-slates, around the empty place in the returning line and the place where the carriers had crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight and not the cost; it had carried him up and down this road for two hundred and five days and had entered none of them anywhere. She set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, and left the thread frozen where it was. She went to her own bed, and the fire under the floor kept four and a gap with its payer unnamed.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth out of a person and no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid; nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. The burned-slate word lay on its own place beside the dust and the two tellings with a hand between them, a direction that had arrived nowhere and stayed a direction. Two hundred and five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof.

Then he put the question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and stayed up through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch his, and came down with the breath against the fold. It was the old coordination remembered, not a change made again --- the hand riding a counted rhythm in the room beside his, as it had ridden it before, and letting go when the letting go came. He filed it on the sixth place with nothing laid across it: not a longer reach, not a stronger hand, not a hearing (water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings), not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule, with no payer named to it. The market would not get to read the inch; no slate in the district would take it, and no hand beneath this roof had seen it go up.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number anybody would have believed. The thread at his mouth kept its frozen place. The widow's mark hung where it hung, untouched since before the kiln on Ember 1, because small hours under a roof are not an opening. The hand had come down with the counted breath, and the fold took it without a word. Over the boards of the yard the word of the burned slate was still going around and arriving nowhere, nobody turning it into a cause, nobody naming whose slate or which room it had come from, while beneath the roof the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it stayed where they had been left and were not swept. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. Two hundred and five stood exact in the dark, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody, and the river went on over water without noticing that an inch had gone up toward its note and come down on a breath counted at another fire.
