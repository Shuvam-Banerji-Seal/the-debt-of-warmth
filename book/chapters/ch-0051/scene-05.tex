\daymark{206}{Ember 27, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

The seventh morning came down into the dark the way the others had come, without a boundary drawn under the six behind it, and the dark under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} had the shape of a pot kept under ash: the warmth in the low places, the cold at the rim, and no hand anywhere to say which of the two had been given an instruction. The district went on calling Ember the kiln month and Tallow the firing month, and nobody corrected either name. A name left where it has stood is a thing kept, like the grey in the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}, which had earned a season's title by being left alone long enough. \person[Corin]{The boy} came to two hundred and six days and six months and twenty-six days in that dark, and nobody beneath the roof said either number, or knew there was a number that could be reached. The household kept its account in loaves pushed along a board, in glances that came to rest on no figure, and in the old space at the row's end that was named only when somebody wanted it named. Two hundred and six stood exact in the wool, complete and unspent, with nothing taken from it and nothing done to it, and the wanting to be told had gone so quiet that he could no longer hear it asking, though it was there all the same, the way a tool put aside at dusk lies where it was set and does not ask to be reached for.

The smell came first, as it always did: hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the corner, the grey smell of a thing that had been given a season's name instead of a cause. The sound came after the smell, and the sound was not a bell. Beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours with a gap, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the space where the fifth should have stood, not by any decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it that could make a fifth pull possible. A bell is rung and counted and answered; nothing had ever been set here to answer. The space had gone on being a space for two hundred and six days, and no payer stood to it. He had learned to take the gap as the sound and the four as the thing that led up to it, as a man at a wheel learns the turn where the clay will fight before the clay has fought him.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be, for a thing counted becomes a thing claimed, and a thing claimed wants a name written under it. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have been. Under the floor, four and a silence; through the door, water going over water; above his face, four, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places. They did not become one, and a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under each of them, and no payer stood to any of them. This was not a finding. It was the order in which he laid the things down and refused to stack them, and it had kept him warm in a manner of speaking for two hundred and six days.

\scenebreak

The household kept \person{Garl Vail} at its closest yet to the end of the tend, and kept him in objects, as it had kept him every morning of the boy's life. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg, with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had leaned for twenty years to reach the wheel. The seat below it held the hollow of that same lean, worn on the left side, and no one had sat in it to fill it, and no one had moved the coat to make the seat look less like a man. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay along the skirting with the newer, finer hairline of grey beside it, and both had been gone around with a look and a wide step and neither had been swept. The proverb had gone to the pot. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the room the way the broom went around the line, circled and never named, worse for being uncalled because it could be neither paid nor settled nor stopped, and because a called debt at least has the decency to arrive with a figure a man can refuse. None of it had been made a lesson. No collector had been called up \place{the clay road}, and the loan that could hear was not named under this roof.

He lay in the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} came down and stopped at the edge of his reach, and the little roof stopped the heat at the same place, as though the heat too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone stiff in the dry heat of the kiln month, and the empty place beside it had still not been given a name. No third crust had come to lie with it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the limit of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the sequence as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried and failed to save: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust, and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of the three, and had got none of them for two hundred and six days.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket, as she had come to the fire before everything else for two hundred and six days. She told it that the grey in the corner was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived at nothing, as it had arrived at nothing on every day of the boy's life. Then she said, “The house will keep it,” to the fire, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and went on with her work, and the house went on keeping it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning or dusk since. A morning was not an opening. A shelf was not an opening. There had been no opening in twenty-six days.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on anything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board --- the charcoal tally planted on the first Account-day of Ember, unread, uncounted, uncopied --- stayed where it lay. She had put a finger beneath that board on one day long ago, and had kept it from beneath it on every day since, and the keeping was as exact as the touching had been.

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, holding his head with two fingers behind the ear, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not his mouth, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand. At the setting her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting required, and then went away. She did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never once to the child lying beneath the roof receiving it.

A neighbour came to the door with a cost-question about the firing, a woman with dry scurf on her hem and a pot held against her hip, wanting to know what the last of the kiln month would want before the account was read. Sella did not turn her head toward the basket. “Ask the pot,” she said. “The house has said it.” The woman said the weather had something to answer for too, and Sella gave her the weather, and the question went away level the way a full pot goes across a threshold, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. The boy kept the unanswered cost on the tray where the other unanswered costs lay, and did not lay it across any of them. Three questions alike standing up would have made a rule, and a rule is an account that has found a payer.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind, touched the fold, and went out on the same wind. He answered it as a direction and entered it nowhere. A man passing down the yard with his collar up said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early, and the saying went past on the same wind and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost and no second edge was paid. The word from \place{Slate Row} about the burned slate was still going around the boards, touching everything and belonging to nothing, and nobody had yet said whose slate it had been or which room it had stood in or why it had been worth the burning. It had arrived from a place, had passed over boards, and had arrived nowhere. He kept it on its own place beside Jorra's dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, and laid nothing across them.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He had grown quieter since the word had gone around the boards, and the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He parted dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day held on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or four unnamed men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold, and the boy made no third telling out of their going.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from the man on the wrong wind, who went off up the yard with his saying about a pot singing early and no knowledge in his pocket. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161, the held beat beneath the shelf on Day 166 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it --- the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said “Yes, house” to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. A thing held nine times was still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth. No collector came up or went down the clay road that morning.

\scenebreak

Asked in the morning, the hand went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down before the counted breath had finished. It was a short answer and an early one, an inch let go before the letting go arrived, and there was no failure in it. He filed it on the sixth place with nothing laid across it: not a longer reach, not a stronger hand, not a hearing (water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings), not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule, with no payer named to it. The market would not get to read the inch; no slate in the district would take it, and no hand beneath this roof had seen it go up.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number anybody would have believed. The thread at his mouth kept its frozen place. The salt coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean; the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it stayed where they had been left and were not swept, and the debt against the tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and nobody called a collector. The widow's mark hung where it hung, untouched since before the kiln on Ember 1, because a morning and a shelf are not openings. The fold took the hand without a word. Two hundred and six stood exact in the wool, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, while the river went on over water without noticing that an inch had gone up toward its note and come down again, and the kiln drew in fours with the gap where the fifth pull should have stood.
