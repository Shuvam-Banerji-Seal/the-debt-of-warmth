The afternoon went on without coming to anything, the way the last heat of the kiln month went on without going out. It left the high air first and settled in the low places, under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where one leathery brown-edged crust lay stiff two inches from the ear of \person[Corin]{the boy} and kept the empty place beside it without a name. No third crust came to lie with it. The underside of the shelf made a small roof over him and stopped where his hand stopped, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same edge, as if it too had been told a thing and had come to the end of the telling. He had lain under that roof for two hundred and six days and had never once asked it to be a different roof.

The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the limit of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried and failed to save: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust, and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. The afternoon gave him none of the three and did not consider the matter.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, and the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. The household straightened around the quiet of her as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire that \lore{the thaw} was come, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every afternoon of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no hearth, shelf, well, rack, basket, yard, afternoon or dusk since. A dusk was not an opening and the last tend was not an opening; there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had said what the one opening had mended or what it had broken.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them. The instruction went to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and the running water and the river wind, and never once to the child lying beneath the roof receiving it.

\scenebreak

The household kept \person{Garl Vail} to the end in objects, as it had kept him at the beginning and in all the days between, and kept him without a story. The salt-stiff coat on its peg held a broader man's shape, with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had leaned twenty years to reach the wheel. The seat below it held the hollow of that lean, worn on the left side, and nobody had sat in it to fill it, and nobody had moved the coat to make the seat look less like a man who had once sat there. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay along the skirting with the newer, finer hairline of grey beside it, called the thaw, and both had been gone around with a look and a wide step and neither had been swept. The proverb had gone to the pot. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the room the way the broom went around the line, circled and never named, worse for being uncalled, because it could be neither paid nor settled nor stopped, and because a called debt at least arrives with a figure a man can refuse. None of it had been made a lesson. No collector had been called up \place{the clay road}. The loan that could hear was not named under this roof, and the dead man's name had not been spoken in two hundred and six days.

There was a kind of keeping that was only maintenance, and a house could do it for years without once confusing it with love or with grief, and the boy had learned to tell the difference by watching what the house did when it was left alone. A coat hung where a coat had hung. A seat kept its hollow. A line stayed on the floor. He had spent two hundred and six days refusing to lay these three along each other and call them one thing, because three likenesses standing in a row will stand up and call themselves a rule if a man leaves them long enough to want an answer, and a rule is an account that has found a payer. No payer stood to any of these. That was the whole of it, and it was worth exactly what it was worth.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it in the same breath is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the word waited on the far side of his teeth; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth. No collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon.

\scenebreak

At \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day held on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He had grown quieter since the word had gone around the boards from \place{Slate Row}, and the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew \lore{the full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from the man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man went off up the yard with his saying and his ignorance and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on that same wrong wind, touched the fold, and went out on the wind that brought it, answered as a direction and entered nowhere. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other places beside the fraction of Day 161, the beat held beneath the shelf on Day 166 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

The word of the burned slate was still going around the boards of the yard, touching everything and belonging to nothing, and nobody had said whose slate it had been or which room it had stood in or why it had been worth the burning. It had come from a place, had passed over boards, and had arrived nowhere. He kept it on a place of its own beside Jorra's dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three and nothing laid across them, and did not make a cause of it or a third telling of the room that had been shut in the war. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, as she had given it on every going of the light since the boy came under the roof. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing without taking each other's colour. The arm that had carried him two hundred and six mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark trough whose water was called weather, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. The yard swept around its absences the way a hand goes around a wound it does not mean to touch, and entered none of them.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without looking at his face and left the thread frozen. \lore{The carried fire} answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, and its payer had had no name for two hundred and six days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of water over water. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have been. Three counts in three places. They did not become one, and the two rooms did not touch; a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; no seventh had come in two hundred and six days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, and the second reading had gone up the road in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside and was still not the figure paid, while \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. Three held changes stood on three places, the fraction of Day 161, the beat under the board on Day 166 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171; the loft scrape stood on a fourth and the pale line of the carried man on a fifth. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap, no fourth likeness, no finger of his drawn across them. The carried man's cost was entered nowhere. The charcoal lay unread beneath the shelf, the warm stone cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in the loft, the sixth green a colour and the foundling a shape, the burned slate a direction and not a cause. The held inch lay on a sixth place beside the others with all its answers, the misses and the short holdings and the long ones, and nothing was laid across it. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the whole of it was exact, complete and worth nothing.

Then he put the question to his hand.

It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It had gone up an inch on other days and come down at once, or come down before the counted breath had finished, or held for the length of the small intake only, or stayed through the whole of a breath counted at Sella's fire in the room that does not touch his and come down with the breath; and once it had kept the height past the end of her count and come down on the river's next low note. These were the answers the body had given and he had filed each on its sixth place and laid nothing across them. Now there was no count at that fire to hold against. She had gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and the room that did not touch his had no breathing number going in it. There was only the rhythm that came through the door, water over water, with its own low interval and no hand at it and no number on it and nobody counting it.

The hand stayed up for the length of that interval. It rose to the inch and held while the low note drew out and slackened, and it came down on the next low note when the river gave it, not when a breath in another room told it to and not when he willed it, because there was no other count in the house to will it against. It was a coordination with the one rhythm through the door. It was not a longer reach, not a stronger hand, not a hearing, for water over water remained a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named to it and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He entered it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true; the inch was worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. The market would not get to read it and no slate in the district would take it. The answer had come wearing a rhythm nobody gave him and nobody counted, and that was the whole of its value and the whole of its smallness. The river did not stop for it. It went on over water without noticing that an inch had ridden one of its low notes for the length of one interval.

The hand came down against the fold and the fold took it without a word. Two hundred and six stood exact in the dark, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and six days; the mark had been touched once, before the kiln on Ember 1, and at no other hour; the stone lay cold in its loft pot; the charcoal lay unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and nothing was now. The coat kept its shape on the peg and the seat kept its hollow and the clean dark line and the finer grey kept their places unswept, and the count went forward.
