\daymark{165}{Tallow 16, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

\person[Corin]{The boy} came awake to the kiln counting in its sleep. Four low pulls behind the wall, a gap where the fifth should have stood, and then four again, the way a man breathes in his rest and leaves one breath out of the round without ever knowing he has left it out. He had lain one hundred and sixty-five days under that gap. Nobody under any roof had said the number to him, and nobody said it now. The grey came up between the boards and the heat came up through the floor, and neither of them carried a figure. He counted the day himself, as he had counted the others, because there was nothing else in the dark of the wool that would stay still long enough to be counted.

The basket sat where the underside of a board leaned down and became a roof. Wool at his ear, wool under his chin, the thread at his mouth frozen overnight because no instruction existed in this house about the thread. Hot clay lay under tallow in the air, and the mineral tang of \lore{Ash} ran along the skirting board where no broom had been told to go. This was \place{Low Kiln}, under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof}, and the morning reached him in two streams that did not meet: heat through the floor from the banked coals, water over water through the finger's-width door. Neither came within the two paces of his hand. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river and came down on the fold.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him out into the yard with the basket on her hip and the little-door burn shut on its weight. She gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the fire, and to the fire before the pot, and to the pot before the broom, and never once gave it to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and kept the half-pace from every open slate the way a woman keeps clear of a cooking pot with a child at her skirt. \lore{The widow's mark} hung at her throat on its cord and she did not touch it, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the firing month had made itself at home. The women went around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, and around the rack space with its broken birth-slates under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went with a look and a wide step and no number. The trough at the well stood bare and dark under its little roof and the water in it was called weather; the clay jar stood cold under its glaze, glanced at once and not touched. Nothing in the yard had been lost today and nothing in it had been entered. The danger was present in the dust ground small as meal that a man carried on his forearms, in the sweetish lead edge that came down off \place{High Kiln} whenever the wind turned that way, in the kiln's breath in fours with its gap. It lay among these as heat lies under banked ash, gathering toward an edge that could be paid later. It was not paid now. No man in the yard had said a word that any slate could take.

He read the firing as he read a theatre he could not enter: heat kept and counted behind a screen of brick, a gate with two bites, a man standing at the threshold with a towel in his hands and not going in. \lore{The damper} was one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. He had stood in such rooms while somebody else held the field, and the knowing there was worth exactly what it cost to keep quiet. Here it was worth a place from which nothing could be entered and no hand of his could reach the gate. He let the two bites lie where they lay, one iron joined to no kiln, and went on counting what was given to be counted.

\person{Tarn} stood with the broom in his hand until the broom had been given its instruction, and only then took it up.

“Go around. It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” He said \emph{Yes, house} to the neighbour who wanted the thaw early and \emph{Yes, house} to the one who wanted it late, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot between them, and went on wanting the late one all the same, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb where the house could check them. The seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not.

His stroke went over the shallow bright patch without even the half-check. It went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood and no tenth stood up to join them, and the loan that could hear was not called and had not been called in all the days of the boy's life. Tarn did not ask about the room, and \person{Jorra Vail} did not ask about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof, as they had stayed every morning for three years. \person{Garl Vail}'s coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the hollow wheel-seat kept its leftward lean, and between the two the house went about its work.

\scenebreak

At midday a kiln-hand came down off the kiln mouth with the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} on his breath and crouched by the kindling under the roof to bank the coals. He said the thing sideways, as a man says a thing to a wall in the presence of a child who might repeat it, not to the basket and not to anybody. A man in \place{Mud End} had been taken quiet in his sleep. Them that found him had called it merciful.

The word lay a moment on the coals and went down with them. Nobody asked which man, or what had taken him, or what a quiet death had to do with a firing. The kiln-hand raked the ash over the glow with the back of his hand and went off the way he had come, and the yard went on with its work. The boy read the shape of it without entering it: the extremities going before the core, the skin doing what the vessels under it do when the Warm draws back, the core holding on after the rest has given out. He had known a room in which such a body was covered before the instrument came down, and a hand lifted out of a field before the dressing went on. The knowing was exact and worth nothing at two paces. He made no lesson of the lamb and put no figure to the quietness, because a lesson and a figure both want a payer, and no payer stood to either. \place{Mud End} kept its dead man the way the yard kept its empty place, and neither was laid across anything else.

At the going of the light Tarn banked the coals under the back of a broken pot and set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where the house could see that it had been put back. Six counted where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife and was not entered. The wood stood in its four and the space where the fifth should have been, the shape a debt keeps when it will not say its own number.

“Six counted,” Tarn told the kindling. “The next I don't count.”

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came all the way to his teeth. It had come that far so many times that the boy knew the shape of it before the lips moved: the small intake at the top of the reach, the stop, the breath drawn that should have gone in and did not go in. The word that would call the loan stood behind the teeth with no figure on it and no name, and Tarn drew the breath and did not spend it, and turned the coals with the back of the pot and said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire. The breath from Seed 16 stayed where it had stood for a hundred and sixty-five days, with its small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in. No collector came up the road. No tenth stopping stood up out of the nine. The seventh year pressed nearer its end than it had ever pressed and had still not given, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled. The boy set the stopping on the interval tray as an object among objects and not a rule, an interval and not a payment, with no payer named to any of the nine and no doctrine allowed to stand up out of nine likenesses. The fraction that had stood at the damper for four mornings stayed where it had been put, held and unspoken and not repeated; a thing held is a thing kept where it was put. He laid nothing across it. The stopped sentence and the waiting hand and the ninth stopping went each into its own hollow of the towel, and nothing rested on anything else, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them.

His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash sat in the knuckle creases in the colour of the clean dark line and the clip-ring at his wrist gave back the little light there was. Up in the loft above the shed, in a clay pot with no mark on it, lay the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold long since. He kept it and could no more reconcile it with the debt than he could the house's kindness with the account he kept of that kindness, and had stopped trying, which is not forgiving. The stone was not a reconciliation and he had never asked it to be one. It lay where it lay and he kept where he kept, and the two did not touch.

Sella, within hearing of the sill, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. Jorra came through from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's dust still ground small as meal in the creases of his forearms and did not ask about the ear. The back room off the shed kept its finger's width of open and gave out nothing. Jorra did not ask about the room either. Tarn did not ask about the ear. The two keepings went on under the one roof without anybody laying one across the other, and the boy, who could enter neither, held them on separate places as he had held every other thing this house kept from its own slates.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-five days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and he would not let any one of them rise up and become the others, because a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer stood to any of these.

One hundred and sixty-five stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool. The reading stood somewhere ahead of it and nobody gave him its date, and nobody gave him the number of his days either, which was the same number and further out of reach than the crust two inches from his ear. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The small knife stood at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house could check and the seventh where it could not. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The fire missed its fifth under the floor. The count went on where nobody could hear it.
