\daymark{168}{Tallow 19, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

The note of water over water went on under the small hours and into the morning the way a debt goes on under a sleeping house, kept by nobody and missed by nobody who could have named it; and with the light the district's arithmetic came up out of its hiding places and stood in the yard together without agreeing. A number came up out of a fist at the well and went back into a sleeve. A row of loaves gave up its count to a woman's lips and to nobody's mouth. A grey cord at a weir a league downriver kept its knots where no Court thumb would spell them. Under this roof a fire answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been, and through the finger's-width door a river kept a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings. All of it was exact. None of it was entered. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the basket with his cheek to the wool and let the figures stand where they stood, because a likeness in a row will call itself an account if no hand holds it apart, and an account, once it stands, wants a name and a payer, and no payer stood to any of them.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom before she gave it to anybody, and never gave it to the basket, which was a vessel filled and carried and had no instruction of its own to answer. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and kept her half-pace from every open slate, and there was no slate open in the yard, and she kept it all the same. The day before a reading is a day like any other in a district that keeps its figures where nobody can enter them; there was a weight in the yard that nobody had put down and nobody would pick up, and the work went on around it as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies.

In \place{the Clay Yard} the women went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the new finer hairline of grey beside it that was called \lore{the thaw}, and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 in \place{the worked strips} and had not come out. They went around it with a look and a wide step and no number, the way a woman carries a pail of hot water past a child who might knock it, and entered none of them. The broom had its instruction and went where it was told, and where the yard went around, the broom went around. Hot clay came down off \place{High Kiln} on a wind that had the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} in it, and the boy read the firing as he had read it every morning of this month: a theatre he could not enter, heat kept and counted behind a screen of brick, a gate with two bites, and a cost paid by somebody whose name was not spoken under any roof. He could enter none of it. The heat was two paces off and the screen was brick and the theatre belonged to people who had been born into it and did not know they were standing at a gate.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} a woman set a row of loaves along the board and moved her lips over them, and the row stood for what it stood for and she gave nobody a figure. A man come up from the clay road below brought a number up out of his fist toward the well, looked at it as a man looks at a thing he has taken out of a pocket to see whether it is still what he put there, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. The bucket came up with a slap against the staves and \place{the trough} stood bare and dark under its little roof, and the water in it was called weather, and the clay jar stood cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, glanced at once and not touched. A neighbour with a wet pot asked what a firing would cost set down there, and Sella answered without turning, “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” and the woman carried the question away level in a full pail so that nothing slopped, and the boy kept the shape of the answer on the tray where he kept the other answers that had had their questions taken out of them.

A kiln-hand raking the mouth of a kiln spoke sideways to nobody in particular, the way a person speaks to a wall in the presence of a child who may repeat it, of a man in \place{Mud End} taken quiet in his sleep, and said them that found him called it merciful. The rake went on. Nobody asked which man, nobody said what had taken him, nobody put a figure to the quietness or made a lesson of the lamb. The boy did not enter a cause across the saying. He had once stood at the edge of a room in which a body lay under a sheet and a chart was exact and the body was not saved, and the two had not been made to agree; here there was no chart at all, only a rake and a word spoken sideways and a silence afterwards that went on with the work.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Cross} the square stood quiet, \place{the reading block} under \place{the moot-post} with nobody leaning over it and \place{the pot board} keeping the small round of dust where a cup had once stood. The figures of the district were all in that square though nobody had brought them: the fist at the well, the lips over the loaves, the knots on the weir cord, the fire's missing fifth under a roof a quarter league away, the river's note coming up through the boards under his own basket. They stood together and did not agree, and the boy kept them from agreeing as a man keeps instruments on a towel from rolling together, each in its own hollow. When the slate went up it would carry one figure; what stood around it here would go on standing around it, and neither would know the other. Nobody said when the slate would go up. A woman crossing the square with a pot said only that there would be a slate at the watch if a house wanted its figure again, and went on with her pot, and the sentence went into the air and not into any house.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and said “Yes, house” to an early thaw and a late while going on wanting the late in the private part of his head because a late thaw puts off the paying. His seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had still not given. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not; the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, and the breath from Seed 16 was unspent, standing as a small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in and had not. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, and went on over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it; no collector came. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came as far as his teeth and no further, and the word that would have called the loan was not spoken. Nine stoppings stood where they stood, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, with no payer named to any of them and no tenth stood up out of the nine before the seventh year was earned. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark. The boy kept all of it as he kept the rest: exact, complete, worth nothing, and not joined to the counting in the square.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip, keeping dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray. He said to the wheel before he said anything to a person, “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” and to a pot that would not settle, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either; and when Sella's instruction came low he put his bent finger into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her sentence arrived at the pot. He did not look toward the basket. He did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room off the shed, and the two silences stayed unjoined under one roof, as two records going inward are two records going inward and not one cause. The knowing of the pot's note stayed on the tray of near-full answers, advanced and not paid and not among the six hearings of \lore{the full note}, and no pot was lost in the yard.

The boy set the fraction from the morning of Tallow 12 at the damper and the held beat beneath \place{the loaf shelf} on two places of their own and would not let them make a pair. A hand had waited there for a sentence to finish; a hand had waited here under a board for one counted breath and come away without looking. Two waiting hands are two waiting hands, and a likeness between them would call itself a rule if he let it stand, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to either of them. He had learned the grammar of this district from people who would not have called it grammar: a thing held where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would; a chart closed over its finding leaves the neat margin showing; a clamp is a clamp and not a doctrine, set where it is needed and not asked to mean more. So he held the two fractions apart as a surgeon holds two instruments apart on a towel, and went on filing.

\scenebreak

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under its front edge for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own, named from the hollow it left the way a missing instrument is named from the space it leaves on a towel. The field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out stayed where the yard had gone around it. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling was a shape with no sale, and both stayed on places of their own. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and was not standing now, wide around the bare dark trough at the well, and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. A turn made around a thing nobody would name was a turn and nothing more; he had learned to go around such places with her and to keep the shape of each on a tray of its own.

Under the Vail roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a pot is settled on a level board. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because no instruction existed under this house about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and never told anybody. It was the only thing in the room that admitted anything.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung at her throat on its cord and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-eight days, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The file was exact and worthless, as it had been exact and worthless for a hundred and sixty-eight days; an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand. Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with the wheel-keeper's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine and the seventh year had not given. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. The fraction from Tallow 12 stood where it had been put, and the held beat beneath the loaf shelf stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it, two fractions not made a pair into a doctrine. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. One hundred and sixty-eight stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and sixty-eight days, the day that would bring the slate was not named among the counts, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
