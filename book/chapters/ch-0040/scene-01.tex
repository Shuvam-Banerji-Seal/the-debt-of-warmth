\daymark{163}{Tallow 14, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

\dropcap{T}{he first thing} \person[Corin]{the boy} knew of that morning was a smell: hot clay under tallow, the yard giving up its water into the dry cold. Under the smell came a sound that was not a bell. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, and there was a gap where the fifth breath should have been, the way a man's sentence can have a hole in it that everybody hears and nobody fills. He lay on \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in \place{Low Kiln}, one hundred and sixty-three days old and five months and thirteen days, and nobody under any roof said so.

The fold was at his ear and under his chin, wool gone damp where his breath had settled and frozen again in the dry heat. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery since the planting, with an empty place beside it that had no name and had never needed one. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and no instruction existed for that sound either.

Through the finger's-width door came the note of water going over water, a fifth low thing belonging to no count he could enter. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down against the fold. It could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him. It could not lift to his own chin. The surgeon who had once reduced shoulders in the dark now lay under a fold of wool and counted the nails overhead, four on the near board, four on the next, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb.

He had stopped expecting to be told what any of it counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. One hundred and sixty-three stood exact in the dark, complete and unspent, and he set it against the house's arithmetic in loaves and glances and spaces at the row's end without letting the two agree.

\person{Sella Vail} had been at \place{the hearth} before the grey. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot where she had turned it in the night. She told it it was \lore{the thaw} and fed it, and it answered four low pulls with a silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had had no name under any roof for a hundred and sixty-three days and she did not supply one now. She turned the coals with the back of the pot and said \emph{Feed the fire before you feed yourself}, which was a proverb of a dead man's and an instruction to the pot, not to a person. The little-door burn across the back of her left hand shut on the pot's weight and opened on nothing when she took her hand away; the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals.

Every instruction that morning went to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears}, she told the wool. \emph{It's the river wind}. To the fire she said \emph{There, then. It wanted feeding}, as if the wanting were a thing a fire had and a woman might notice the way one notices a child has stopped coughing. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and she kept her half-pace from every open slate in the yard, going around them as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing and she did not touch it, because a morning and a shelf are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

The kiln had been brought up in the night for the third working week. At \place{the kiln mouth} the clay stood in the hot dry smell of the month, dust ground small as meal under the nails of whoever had worked it, and the breath went in fours with its gap behind the wall. That breath, and not the fire's missing fifth, was the danger the house kept through all of Tallow; Sella went about it with her back turned, as if a thing not looked at could not be entered. On a wrong wind there came a sweetish edge off \place{High Kiln} that the yard called weather, and she told the fire it was the thaw and would go when it goes, and gave the wind no figure and no name.

\scenebreak

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the women went around the places the yard kept without naming. The broom went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner a year and more ago, and around the new hairline of grey beside it, finer than the old, which the house called the thaw and would go when it goes. It went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. They went around all of it with a look and a wide step and no number, the way a person goes around a grave in a yard they must cross anyway, and swept none of it. A morning is not an opening, and a yard that goes around a thing has not entered it.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the \place{trough} stood bare and dark, its skin of ice gone off in the lean week, and a woman with her sleeves wet to the elbow called the water weather and went off with her pail carried level so that nothing slopped. A woman come up from the racks with slate dust dried grey in the creases of her wrists asked what the month's firing would want set down there now the kilns had been brought up. \emph{Ask the weather}, Sella said. \emph{Ask the river. The house has said it}. She gave the instruction to the broom and not to the woman and went on at the half-pace, carrying the unanswered question away level in her hand so that nothing slopped. The woman went off with a direction where a figure should have stood, and the boy filed the shape of it beside the other answers that had had their questions taken out, keeping it from standing up with them into a cause, because a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart. He was carried past at two paces and wanted the trough and the weather and got neither. Under the roof the clay jar stood cold on the shelf by the wall, and Sella's eye went to it once as she passed and no further; she did not touch it, and no figure came off it into the morning. A jar looked at was a jar looked at. A thing kept cold under a glaze was not the same as a thing kept warm in a fold, and he set the two on separate places and drew no line between them.

\person{Tarn} stood with the broom waiting in his hand until she had given it its instruction. She gave it to the broom before she gave it to him: \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out}. \emph{Yes, house}, he said, and took it up as a man takes up a tool that has already been named.

He was twenty-two and bent at the shoulders from six years in the slip pit, which made him look older than his term. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb where the house could check them; the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and the small knife lay at the very edge with a dead man's unforged sentence beside it. His chest had been tight since the dark. The breath that should have gone in at the top of each reach was still unspent, a small interval standing where it should have been, and he did not spend it now.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped and joined to no cause. The bright patch lay where the stroke had stopped one morning weeks ago, and the stones kept it as they kept the other places, without a figure and without a payer, and Tarn went over it without looking down. It went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and no collector came. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any of them, and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled. A tenth had not stood up out of the nine before the seventh year was earned.

The fraction from the morning of Day 161 stood where it had been left: a hand held back from the cold iron until a sentence had finished, held and unspoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. Nobody repeated it. Nobody named a payer to it. Nobody looked toward the basket. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note, and the fire kept its gap under the floor, and the two things stayed in two places, as they had stayed every morning of his life.

\scenebreak

He read the firing the way he had once read a room he was not allowed into: a theatre in which heat was kept and counted behind a screen, and a man stood at the gate with a towel in his hands and did not go in. The gate had two bites. \lore{The damper} was one piece of iron with the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, mercy and mutilation in the same narrow jaw, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both. He had stood at such gates and known the heat behind the screen by its count and not by touching it, and he had known the shape of an answer that had had its question taken out of it. A field covered with a cloth before the instrument came down, a hand lifted out of a field before the dressing went on: the same motion performed on a person who could not object, and here performed on the morning while he lay in the fold and was the person who could not object. Here he could not enter, could not speak the warning, could not lift a hand to the iron. The cost of the heat was paid by a man whose name was not spoken under any roof, and the boy had lain a hundred and sixty-three days under this one and had never once heard it said.

The chart in such rooms was exact and the body on the table was not always saved, and nobody made the two agree. Here the question was taken out of everything before it reached the basket. The fire's fifth was a silence with no payer; the river's note was a fifth low thing never numbered among \lore{the full note}; the boards overhead kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and he would not let any one of them stand up and become the others, because a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer stood to any of these.

The yard's heat went on around the clean dark line and the thaw and the rack space and the empty place in the returning line, and the house's arithmetic went on in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody called a number. He lay in the fold at the low angle where the underside of a board becomes a roof and took in the morning through the two places it came to him: hot clay under tallow at the hearth, water over water through the finger's-width door. Neither came close enough to reach.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and sixty-three stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool. The fire missed its fifth under the floor, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-three days; the river kept its fifth over water; the boards kept their knot. The kiln went on breathing in fours with its gap behind the wall, and the morning's work went on around the places nobody entered, and the count went on where nobody could hear it.
