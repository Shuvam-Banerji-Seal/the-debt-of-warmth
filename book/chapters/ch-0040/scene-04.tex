\daymark{166}{Tallow 17, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

The afternoon came into the kiln-house along the low angle where the underside of a board leaned down and made a roof over the basket, so that \person[Corin]{the boy} saw his world in two strips: the grain of \place{the loaf shelf} above him and the dark beneath its front edge, where a finger had gone before and would go again. He was one hundred and sixty-six days old and five months and sixteen days, and nobody under the roof put a figure to it. The house kept its days the way a woman keeps a pot she cannot scour --- in loaves pushed along the board, in a glance at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall, in a space at the row's end that nobody called a number. He took the three things in and set one hundred and sixty-six against them, exact and unspent, in the only currency he had ever been given and had never once been told the name of.

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf with a wet pot against her hip and set it down where no loaf lay. The pot sweated a dark ring onto the bare wood and the ring was not wiped, because the board had been given no instruction about rings. She moved her lips along the row as she always did, counting what the board would not say aloud, and her eye went once to the clay jar and came away. The little-door burn shut on the pot's weight and opened on nothing when she took her hand off it. The shelf kept two gaps and gave neither a name: the old space at the row's end, and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust, no word from anybody. A neighbour come up from the strips with water on her sleeves asked what a firing cost set down there now the kilns had been brought up for the last working season.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” She gave the answer to the pot and not to the woman, and lifted the basket at the half-pace, carrying the unanswered question away level so that nothing slopped. The neighbour went off with a direction where a figure should have stood and made no more of it than a pot carried out of a doorway.

He had known a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin showing, and he knew the shape of it now: a cost asked as a direction, answered with a direction, the question taken off its owner and carried level out of the room without ever being set down. It was clean work. It was worth nothing to the woman with wet sleeves and nothing to the boy who could not have held the pot if it had been put into his hands. He filed it beside the other answers that had had their questions removed and kept it from standing up among them into a cause, because three likenesses in a row will call themselves a doctrine if no hand holds them apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer stood to any of these. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, and the sound lay where it lay.

Then she did the thing he had watched for on three evenings before and had never once seen vary. She set the basket low near the bench leg, turned her back to it, and stood over the row with her lips moving. Her hand went out and the finger went BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies, the short marks in their clusters pressed hard or faded nearly into the grain, a keeping put where nobody thought to look above a row kept badly on top. On Tallow 2 and Tallow 8 and Tallow 11 the finger had gone under and come away in the one motion, as a hand goes into a pocket for a thing it knows is there and brings out nothing to show. This time it did not come away. It stayed beneath the board for the length of one of the breaths she counted before she brought it away, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She did not look. She did not read, did not copy, gave no word to the wool or the pot or the fire, and did not go back to the shelf when the light went.

From the low angle he saw the held beat and nothing else: the knuckle still, the finger gone under the lip of the board, the breath counted out in the dark above him, and then the hand gone back to the pot. He had no name for what it meant and he did not invent one. He put it on a place of its own as a change held and unspoken, not a reading of the charcoal, not a payment, with no payer named to it. A surgeon knows a hand that stays a fraction longer than the setting needs, and knows that the knowledge is worth exactly what it costs to keep quiet; he had held such knowledge in rooms where the field was covered and the finding was not spoken over the table. The reason still stood where it had stood on three evenings: a hand that goes under without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason is left standing between the two. He kept the held beat from going across to the fraction that had waited at the damper for a sentence to finish, and from the older intervals at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, and from the nine stoppings. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and there was no payer under that roof for any of them. The two things stayed on two places, each where it had been put, and no likeness stood up between them to call itself a doctrine.

To him the difference between the three evenings and this one was the size of a fraction: a finger passing under and a finger staying under. He had held pressure on a vessel a beat past the point where it took, and knew that the extra beat is the whole distance between a closed field and a field left open; a hand that stays tells you something the hand that withdraws cannot, and tells it to nobody else in the room. Here the field was a board and the finding lay under it, and the only witness was a basket that could not have lifted the board an inch. He kept the fraction as a fraction, small enough that no man under the roof could have been made to name it.

The shelf kept its two gaps and no third came. The old space at the row's end stayed where it had always stayed; the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 stayed beside it, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no word to say what had gone. In the other room one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third put there. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own, named from the hollow it left as a missing instrument is named from the space on a towel, and was not laid across the shelf's two gaps or the crust's empty place or the field's empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The sixth green stayed a colour joined to no ear and the foundling stayed a shape with no sale; neither was brought into the room. A saying of the loaf-shelves held that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together, and that an account then wants a name; no finger under any roof in Low Kiln had laid these together. A fourth likeness would have made a cause and a cause would want a payer, and no finger of his drew the line.

She turned from the shelf and did not go back to it. The marks stayed under the front edge where they had been put, some pressed hard, some faded nearly into the grain, and waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. One hundred and sixty-six stood exact and unspent against the loaf row and the glance at the cold jar and the space nobody numbered. He lay in the wool and kept the held beat where he had put it, separate as one clamp set down beside another that is not the same bite.

\scenebreak

The light went off the boards in the order it always went, the high strip first and the grain under the shelf last, and the house went on with its work as though nothing had been counted anywhere. At the going of it she lifted him down from the shelf's corner and carried him to \place{the bed} under the roof, not to \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a pot is settled on a level board. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because no instruction existed under this house about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and never told anybody. It was the only thing in the room that evening that admitted anything.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, the way a woman counts the breaths of a sleeping child and tells nobody what she counts. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung at her throat on its cord and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-six days, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The file was exact and worthless, as it had been exact and worthless in the country he carried nowhere; an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person --- with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with the wheel-keeper's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine and the seventh year had not given. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. The fraction from the morning of Tallow 12 stood where it had been put, held and unspoken, and this evening's held beat beneath the shelf stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it --- two fractions not made a pair into a doctrine. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. One hundred and sixty-six stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The payer under the floor had had no name under any roof for a hundred and sixty-six days, and the finger's held beat stayed where it had been put, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come.
