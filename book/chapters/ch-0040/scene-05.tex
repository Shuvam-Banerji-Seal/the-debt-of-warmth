\daymark{167}{Tallow 18, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

The reeds had gone out of the day and lived only in the arm that carried him, the way a burden goes on living in a muscle after the load is set down: the going downriver and the coming back, the two turns of a wrist that had made a season's cutting into a road the body kept without being asked. \person[Corin]{The boy} had no bundle to see and no stalks to count, only the small stubborn memory of weight under his own weight, an arm that remembered what it had held and not what it had cost. A woman come up from the beds asked what the last of the cutting would want set down there, and \person{Sella Vail} gave her the river and the weather, and the question went off with her in a full pail carried level so that nothing slopped. He had once closed a chart over a finding and left the neat margin showing; he knew the shape of an answer that had had its question taken out of it, and he met one every morning under this roof. No entry stood anywhere. A cost asked as a direction was answered with a direction, and the arm went on keeping the weight.

She carried him down \place{the clay road} with the instruction already given to the wool and to the running water and never to the basket: \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.} \place{The Weir} lay one league upriver of \place{Sill}, and the road to it had the wrong familiarity of a room entered in the dark, as if he had come back to a place that had been quietly rearranged since he last saw it. There was \place{the plank walk} on its posts, \place[the blessing stone]{the flat blessing stone} where the hearth-maids blessed the traps, the lower boards where the eels were worked, and a landing set a hand's breadth out of line below \place{the north bridge}. \place{The Ilm} went under \place[the Singing Sill]{the sill} with the note of water going over water that had come through the finger's-width door every night of his life, a fifth low thing belonging to no count he could enter. She set the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lay, gave the wool its instruction and the running water its own, and kept her half-pace from every open thing in the district. A basket set down was a pot set down, and a pot set down was left where it stood.

The reed-cutter came along the boards with the season finished and no bundle left to set down. On the flat stone he straightened the short length of wool-grey cord gone dark with the oil of a working thumb, turned it once so the knots stood, tied one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bit the end off, and put it back inside his coat with the flat of the hand, neither folded nor shown. A weir woman who could not read it asked what it held.

“You can ask the cord,” he said.

“And who'll answer me?” she said.

“No more can the Court,” he said, and went up the boards to lift a trap, with no anger in it and no smile, his hand to the cord before it went to the trap and before it went to the woman. No knot was counted. No figure came out of the cord into the morning. The boy kept the order of the reaching on a place of its own beside the other hands that did it, not laid across any of them, because a likeness in a row will call itself a doctrine if no hand holds it apart. Nothing in the cord was joined to the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and nothing in it was joined to the cord at another woman's waist.

\person{Orra Weir} stood at the lower boards with her board on its side and a grey cord at her waist, turning it with the wrist that had turned it ten thousand times. Her hands were like old rope, the pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, salt dried white at her wrists, and the grey streak went first through the black hair when she moved her head. The nasal half-song of \place[the Fenmarch]{the Fen} came into her voice where she was tired, a flat low thing that carried the words sideways. She pulled one knot down with her thumb and set it among the others with a small flat knock — the knock of a thing laid where it belonged, which was not the small flat knock of a vessel cracked all the way through, and the boy kept the two sounds on places that did not touch.

A woman with the river in her hem asked what the levy would take now the hands were out of the reeds.

“The ninth of Ilmen,” Orra said. “The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them.”

“That isn't the levy,” the woman said.

“It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried,” Orra said, and went back to the knots.

Her thumb went to the cord again as the sentence ended. Whether that was a lie or only the habit of counting the boy did not decide, because a cause laid across the two would want a payer and no payer stood on the boards. The last knot kept its double turn legible, tail tucked, tied left over right with the small extra weight of a thumb pressed in; the word that would have named it stayed where it had stayed since the year of the flower. A date laid where a figure had been asked was an answer with the question taken out of it, a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin left showing; the man at the stern who never touched the oars stood on a place of his own, apart from the midwife's reaching hand and the thane's twice-wiped thumb, and no cause was written across the three. The cord went back upriver to \place{the Eel House}, where no clerk could spell it.

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels below the boards with slime dried grey in long strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept on her face the way another woman keeps a door on the latch. A weir woman asked whether the run would be thin now the reeds were out of the water.

“Thin? Who's to say thin?” Mira said. “It comes when it comes. Ask the river.”

She flicked an eel-bone against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, moved two from hand to hand and put one away when the deciding was done. A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure for two pair.

“Two marks the pair and two short,” Mira said. “You've said it twice and I've counted it twice. There's nothing in the sentence that wants an answer.”

Her thumb went under her apron to the grey cord and stayed there longer than the figure required. Shifting a creel showed the second twist twice beneath it, never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door, neither knot counted by any thumb but her own and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lay beside Orra's and did not touch it. She did not look into the basket. A keeping and a pricing were two things done with the same face turned the same way, and the boy had no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold them apart, and had given up looking for one. A keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of its hiding than the writing would; he had learned that from a chart turned down on a table, and here there was no chart at all, only a thumb under an apron and a face held steady over eels.

\scenebreak

Below the sill, in \place{the fish-trade shade}, a woman wound a child's foot in three turns of wool and went on winding after the foot had gone dark inside them, the way a woman will go on winding when stopping would be an admission. A neighbour with a pail called it weather. The woman said nothing. The boy, carried past at two paces, read the extremity going before the core and knew the order of what would come: the vessels in that foot had gone cold and a cold vessel does not clot, and a foot in that state is not rubbed and is not set to the fire, because either brings the afterdrop on, and the afterdrop takes what the warming could not give back. He knew it as he knew the note of a pot under the knuckle, and the knowing bought nothing at two paces, in a body that could not reach the foot or speak the warning or lift the wool. It was the same knowing he had once used on a body laid out under lights, and here it was worth exactly the two paces of air between the basket and the child. No silt-leach was named, no cause and no cure, and nobody under the shade asked a man who could not answer. The afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform.

At \place{the out-of-line landing} below the bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody had called, his cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his slate turned inward to his forearm with the writing where no goer-by could read it. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, and he entered the figure before he looked at the man who had given it. Nobody had taken him off since the month turned, and the market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. A girl with a creel took the landing sideways, caught the rail with her free hand and went on selling without once looking at her feet; nobody made a fall of it and nobody helped her, and the stone lay where it lay. A measure set down before anybody called it was a measure set down ahead of something, and ahead of something was a direction, and the boy kept the shape and made no rule of it, and kept this order of reaching apart from the runner's and the reed-cutter's — three hands putting a record where no Court thumb would look, a shape and not a cause.

The note of water over water came up from under the sill through the whole morning, a fifth low thing in the day as it was a fifth low thing in the dark, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down against the fold. Every record on the boards was legible and none was enterable: a cord, a knot-string, a second twist, an inward slate, the charcoal waiting under a shelf a league away. He could see every one and open none, and the count he kept went on where nobody could hear it.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and was not standing now, wide around the bare dark \place{trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, and wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. A turn made around a thing nobody would name was a turn and nothing more; he had learned to go around such places with her and to keep the shape of each on a tray of its own. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence, named from the hollow it left the way a missing instrument is named from the space it leaves on a towel, and it was not laid across \place{the loaf shelf}'s two gaps or the crust's empty place or the field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. No fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a pot is settled on a level board. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because no instruction existed under this house about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away — the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and never told anybody. It was the only thing in the room that evening that admitted anything.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, the way a woman counts the breaths of a sleeping child and tells nobody what she counts. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung at her throat on its cord and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-seven days, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The file was exact and worthless, as it had been exact and worthless in the country he carried nowhere; an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among — five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person — with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with the wheel-keeper's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine and the seventh year had not given. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. The fraction from the morning of Tallow 12 stood where it had been put, held and unspoken, and the held beat beneath the loaf shelf stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it — two fractions not made a pair into a doctrine. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of a hand two rooms away and a new hairline of grey had gathered in the corner beside the old dark line, a thing ground small as meal that had got there by being broken, kept on its own tray from a thing mixed on purpose; no likeness was made a cause. One hundred and sixty-seven stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The payer under the floor had had no name under any roof for a hundred and sixty-seven days, and the held beat beneath the shelf stayed where it had been put, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come.
