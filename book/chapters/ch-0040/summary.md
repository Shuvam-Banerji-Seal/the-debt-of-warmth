## Chronicle

The firing month entered its third week with the kilns brought up. The boy was one hundred and sixty-three days old on the first morning and one hundred and sixty-nine on the last, and nobody said so. The kiln breathed in fours with a gap; the fire under the floor missed its fifth and its payer had no name under any roof. Sella Vail gave her instruction to wool, fire, pot and broom, never to the basket, kept her half-pace and left the widow's mark where it hung, for a morning, a shelf and a dusk are not openings.

Before first grey men went low into the back room and got no name. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust ground small as meal on both forearms, told a pot it would do and withheld its note from a kiln-master who said a pot that sang early would sing itself out early; no pot was lost. When Sella's voice went low his bent finger went again to the cold damper and waited for her sentence to finish, and nobody spoke of the waiting.

Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed and told the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count.* A kiln-hand spoke sideways of a man in Mud End taken quiet in his sleep, and nobody made a lesson of it. In the afternoon Sella held a finger beneath the loaf shelf for one counted breath where the charcoal tally lies, then did not look, read, or go back. The boy filed the held beat on a place of its own and kept it from standing up with the other fractions into a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer.

Downriver at the Weir the reed-cutter tied one knot with his teeth and hid his cord, saying *You can ask the cord* and *No more can the Court*. Orra Weir gave a date where a figure was asked, and Mira Silt answered a buyer's figure with *Two marks the pair and two short*. A child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool, and the reading of the cold bought nothing at two paces.

On the second Account-day Berr Clay came to the block, wiped his thumb twice, read *one in twelve, as the notice says*, and said the levy was not called by him. The figure stood unchallenged; the true subtraction went back up the clay road in the locked box, and the figure read was not the figure paid. Hessa Rale went up the road with his narrow slate edge-on and said *I don't read it. I carry it.* No figure came off it.

In the small hours his file was exact and worthless: six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings with no payer. One hundred and sixty-nine stood exact, complete and unspent. The fire missed its fifth; the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come.
