\daymark{169}{Tallow 20, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

The second Account-day and market-day of Tallow came up over \place{the Cross} the way the first had come, grey and close, with the river wind under the eaves and hot clay coming down on the same breath from \place{High Kiln}; and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade with his cheek against cold iron while the crowd leaned over \place{the reading block} above him. He was one hundred and sixty-nine days old and five months and nineteen days, and nobody under any roof said so, because there was no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number. Eight pigs had gone over the board before the light was properly in the boards and the potters were setting bowls out on \place{the pot board} with dust in the hollows of the rims, and the square went on with its market the way a body goes on with its work while a surgeon stands at the edge of the field, near enough to hear and not near enough to put a hand in. He could no more enter the square than he could enter the firing under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof}. The cold iron at his cheek was the whole of his share of it.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short out of \place{the clay road} a little before the top of the morning. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay there was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate that would come out of his coat and once for the figure that would be on it, the way a man draws a tool across a clean edge before he sets it to work, and then held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing turned to his sleeve where no goer-by could take a figure off it.

He passed a finger over the figure. His eyes rested on it a breath longer than the writing required, the small check of a man assuring himself that the number was still the number he had set there, and he read it low into the crowd. “One in twelve, as the notice says.” The crowd leaned in because he never raised his voice, and the leaning gave the sentence more weight than the sentence could carry. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.”

A man come up from the lower road brought a number out of his fist and held it toward the block, the way a man holds up a thing he has taken from a pocket and does not know where to set down. “What will the firing cost now the kilns are hot?”

“A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle,” Berr said. “If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” \emph{Your house}. Never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and went back to the pigs by the time the bell went.

At the rail a Sill assessor with a slate on his knee wrote the pigs' figures down before anybody could ask which eight were meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail and then to his mouth, and the boy filed that order on a place of its own beside Berr's twice-wiped thumb without making the two into one hand. A buyer brought a number up out of his coat toward the weighing board, looked at it the way a man looks at a thing he has taken out to see whether it is still what he put there, and put it back into his sleeve, so the figure arrived nowhere and the pigs went on over the boards.

The printed figure stood unchallenged. Nobody challenged it. The true subtraction went back up the clay road in the locked box with Berr inside his own good reason, and the figure that had gone into the air was still not the figure that would be paid. The boy kept the two figures in two places and did not make them agree, as he kept a chart's neat margin apart from the finding it concealed, and as he had kept the told bell beside the heard bell for a hundred and sixty-nine days without once laying one across the other. It was not that he thought one figure true and the other false. It was that a figure read aloud in a square and a figure locked in a box up the road were two records made by two hands for two purposes, and a man who lays two records together and calls them one has already chosen which hand he will believe; he had seen men do it with a chart and a body under a sheet, and the chart had been exact and the body had not been saved, and nothing in the room had made the two agree.

The bell went four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The boy counted the strokes the way he counted the boards under him, four, four, and the silence where the fifth should have been, and held the heard bell beside the told bell on a place of its own; he did not lay the bell's missing fifth across the kiln's missing pull under the Vail floor or across the knot the width of a thumb overhead. Three absences in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} came through the square going up \place{the clay road} with Berr Clay's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm, the writing turned toward her sleeve. Her hand went to it before her eyes went to the crowd: the small flat correction of the strap, the turn of the wrist bringing the edge round, the settling of the second shorter strap let out for the long road. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. The thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was.

A woman near the pigs, who had watched the slate go by the way a woman watches a loaded pot go past a child, asked what she carried.

“I don't read it. I carry it,” Hessa said. “There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.”

“Then what's on it?”

“Cross to \place{Greybank}. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.” She said it without rudeness and without apology, answered the question that had been put and not the one behind it, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. She was a runner and not a reader, and the boy filed the order of her reaching as the eighth such hand in the district on a place of its own, not joined to \person{Marra}'s pause between strokes, not to the neat dark hand that sets a name down before a face, not to the clerk at the Court's table, not to the soft-cuffed clerk set below the bridge, not to the soft hands at \place{Slate Row}, not to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, not to the finger that went beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, not to the reed-cutter's cord or \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string or the charcoal waiting under the board. Two records going inward are two records going inward. A shape is not a cause, and a cause would want a payer, and no payer stood to any of them.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk sat still set for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, and his slate lay turned inward to his forearm with the writing where nobody else could read it, his hand to the slate before his eyes to the stalls every time, entering the figure before he looked at the man who had given it. Nobody had taken him off since the month turned, and there was no instruction in the market about taking him off. The market sold its pots and pigs and eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, the buyers stepping around his slate the way a person steps around a bucket set down in a doorway, and a girl with a creel took \place{the out-of-line landing} sideways, caught the rail with her free hand and went on selling without once looking at her feet, and nobody made a fall of it because nobody had been told to. He was not Marra, not the man with grey fingertips at the Drawing, not the soft-handed man who handed a figure to a slate at the Slate Row fringe. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of something, and a direction is an answer to a question nobody asked aloud; the boy kept the shape on its own tray and made no rule of it, and kept Hessa's order of reaching on another tray beside it, the two not touching.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. She set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay and stood a half-pace from every open slate, and gave the wool its instruction and the river wind its instruction and never gave the basket one, and carried him up saying nothing of what had been read over him. She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and was not standing now, wide around \place{the trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, and wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and gave no explanation of any of the turns. A figure read over a basket in a square was a thing that belonged to the square, and the house under the roof went on keeping its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, and the two did not touch.

Under the Vail roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a pot is settled on a level board. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because no instruction existed under this house about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and never told anybody. It was the only thing in the room that admitted anything.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung at her throat on its cord and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixty-nine days, and the two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The file was exact and worthless, as it had been exact and worthless for a hundred and sixty-nine days; an inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with the wheel-keeper's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; a tenth had not stood up out of the nine and the seventh year had not given. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one mouthful. The fraction from Tallow 12 stood where it had been put, and the held beat beneath the loaf shelf stood on a place of its own beside it and not upon it, two fractions not made a pair into a doctrine. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third. The told bell stood beside the heard bell.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under its front edge for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a tray of its own, named from the hollow it left the way a missing instrument is named from the space it leaves on a towel. The field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out stayed where the yard had gone around it. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling was a shape with no sale, and both stayed on places of their own. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. He had stopped expecting to be told what the number was, which was not the same as not wanting it. One hundred and sixty-nine stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and sixty-nine days, and the charcoal waits under the shelf for a hand that is not his and a day that has not come.
