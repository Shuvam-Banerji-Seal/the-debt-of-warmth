\daymark{324}{Bone 25, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} had come down against the fold and lay there as a tool lies on a bench after use, not because the work was done but because the next hand would find it without looking. There was no sound in the putting-down, and the room went on with its own quiet, and \person[Corin]{the boy} let the quiet be. Three hundred and twenty-three exact stood behind him and three hundred and twenty-four stood in front, and neither was spoken, and six months and one hundred and forty-four days were told by nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and twenty-four days; the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg, and the sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and the name of the man the coat kept had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. Nothing from Bone 20's reading came in as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment, and the reading was not read again. Bone went on without a boundary drawn. The older women named the month by the weather that had not come and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it.

He took the body as a wound is taken, in its old order, because the order was the only thing in the room that had not changed. One stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool as though a hand had lifted it and set it down again badly; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in the small hours to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the ease that cold puts on a face was not ease but a composition, and that a patient who looks settled at the end of a line may be worse before he is better: a cold vessel cannot clot, and must be warmed from the outside, and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew it as exactly as he had once known where the third rib would tear the lung, and the knowing bought him nothing at two inches of air. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won; the argument between them was the work, and it went on in the dark without a verdict and without a witness.

He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and could not lift his own chin; he had packed cavities with both hands and could not close two inches on the basket's handle. He lay in a basket and was carried like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and the discussion of him went on over his head in the third person while he understood every word and could make none of them. Before the light he read the room by its small sounds, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones generally do: the boards giving back their heat by degrees, the fourth pull under the floor not followed by a fifth, the river through the finger's-width door keeping its low note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. He let the sounds arrive in their own order and did not arrange them, because arranging them wanted a hand to put under them and none stood.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order before any day the boy could count, and she kept it the way another woman might keep a habit of crossing herself. The coals lay grey under their blind ash with a little heat at the heart of them, and she told them it was \lore{the thaw} in the flat tone she used for the pot and the broom and the wool, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so the ash fell where she meant it to fall. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said to the fire, where another woman would have said I, and the house kept it, and the coals answered four low pulls from under the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted something under her breath --- a small intake, a hold, and a letting-go --- toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure. The grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn.

At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger lay on top of the front edge, resting a little longer than the checking needed, so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps in it, and she figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on its shelf by the wall, and shut her hand half over nothing, the burn on the back of her left hand --- two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside puckered and pale --- closing on air the way a little door closes on a room nobody goes into. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening; five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the sixth stood at Ash 2, in the first firing of \place{Ash}, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the pot, and \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the fire, and never to a person. The sayings were \person{Garl Vail}'s and had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as he had said them. The crack showed. Repair was one thing and a whole vessel another, and the house had long ago decided which of the two it would keep.

He read the pot the way he had once read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen: the first staple set honestly, the second wire drawn tighter than it needed and left longer, the hairline of the original break showing dark between them. A vessel that has been mended carries its break and is the truer for showing it, which was the whole of the saying and was not a lesson anybody had paid for. The man who had first said it had gone into the Long Cold and had not come out, and the sayings had gone on being said to a pot and a fire and had not mended him. Nobody under the roof had made a doctrine of this either, and the boy did not.

Out at \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the last time and gave no figure to it, and nothing about it that morning lifted a hand or let anything fall. The handful let fall back through her fingers on Bone 22 sat on a place of its own and was not made again; the empty fingers of the mornings since went beside it as a keeping and not as a second, for two would have been a habit wanting a payer and three a way wanting one, and none stood. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat before the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}, and went on to the next thing as though the half-beat had not been spent.

\scenebreak

Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed for three hundred and twenty-four days; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door the river kept water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, and was not numbered among the hearings at all. Three counts in three places. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them, so the three stayed where they were, side by side and not joined. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that came out of vessels; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between. Five arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure: one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, five readings five and not one account, their true subtractions riding locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, unread under this roof and not brought back as an answer to anything. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine and not a rule. It was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the dressed grain under wool, or across the four held changes on their four places, or across the handful, or across the two small objects of the hand, or across his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause; the finger went between. Bone 30 (Day 329), the month's third and last reading, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. The staying of Bone 15 and the early letting-go of Bone 17 sat each on its own place with a finger between them and between them and everything else there, neither made again, neither a habit, a way or a fifth held change, both wanting a payer who did not stand. The four held changes kept their four places and were not a rule; the handful kept its one place and was not made a fifth. At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where it could be checked and where a man's hand could also slip it off, six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond where it could not, and the boy put the place on its own tray and asked nothing of it, making no sign and no figure of the wood. He turned the file over in his mind the way he had once turned instruments out on a towel at the end of a list: an honest answer was a thing done and seen and entered where nobody could read it, a restraint and not a power, an object and not a gift, with no payer and wanting none. Two hundred and twenty-two honest answers stood on the sixth place when the light came, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

\scenebreak

First light came into the room without a market, \place{the Cross} or a bell, an ordinary Fifthday going on about its own work the way water goes over water. No slate was opened within reach of the basket and no figure came near his hand. He had stopped expecting to be told what any number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and he kept the count because attention was the one thing no institution could tax.

At the going of the water, when the river's note had come down to its lowest under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath held for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He had merely put his small hand into the order of things for the length of one note and taken it out again, and the order went on whether he did it or not. He filed it as answer two hundred and twenty-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the five arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and twenty-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and the surgeon took a small clean satisfaction in a field left as it was found, in an answer entered where nobody could see it and in nothing disturbed twice.

The infant underneath took none of it. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, a hand that would close on the pot handle; the satisfaction bought him nothing at two inches of air and no comfort was given and none taken. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument went on without a verdict. Three hundred and twenty-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-four days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the coat kept was not spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the twenty-fifth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, and the hand lay back against the fold while the river's low note came under the finger's-width door and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
