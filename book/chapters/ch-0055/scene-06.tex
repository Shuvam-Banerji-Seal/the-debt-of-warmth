The afternoon thinned without ending, the way a pot's heat thins from the rim while the belly of it holds, and the heat in the room went off the high air first and settled into the low places --- under the shelf, behind the pot, into the fold at his ear where one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from him with the empty place beside it still unnamed and no third coming to stand there and make the row come out even. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and the underside of the shelf became a roof over him that stopped where his hand stopped, which was two inches short of everything he wanted. The surgeon's register took the temperature of the slanting light and found it a direction and not a warmth; the child in him wanted the crust, and the man in him measured the wanting and found it unchanged from the morning. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of a written order. It went no farther and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf before she came to the fire, and stood with her back to the basket. She moved her lips along the loaf row and the row stood with two unnamed gaps and she gave nobody a figure; the row was a shape she kept the way she kept the fire, and the two gaps in it stood where they had stood since the night of Seed 6, and neither was named. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was a thing that stood where it stood and asked nothing and was not entered. At an afternoon and a dusk she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in thirty-eight days, because an afternoon and a shelf and a dusk are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story, which is the way a house keeps a man it cannot collect. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder; the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to that side; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw lay unswept, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. He had not been collected, and the keeping of him was maintenance and nothing more, and nobody under this roof had ever asked what the debt was for. His name had not been spoken here for two hundred and eighteen days, and no lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of each reach, a small interval showing where it should have gone in; his chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, a remembered stopping and not a new one. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, and the loan was not called; if the thing was paid at all it was paid as an interval and not as a calling. Nine stoppings stood where they had always stood, Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects on a tray and not a rule, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, which had still not given. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on Grain 9. The warm stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and he did not go up to it, and did not reconcile it with the debt by trying, which was not forgiving it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray with air under it and a want in it, and made no lesson of the sayings about the pot. Three shapings stood on their own tray and stayed three sayings about a pot: it had sung before its time and would sing itself out early, it had not been heard from since, and there was a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. No hand laid them together and no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, and he gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, who went on breathing up the clay road with the cost entered nowhere. \lore{The burned-slate word} had come a fourth time over the boards from \place{Slate Row}, a direction and not a visit, and he grew quieter whenever it went around, the household straightening around the quiet without an order the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. The word was a direction arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood where they stood with no third, and he did not join them to the dust or the pot or the men who came low into the back room before first grey. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until she finished \emph{feeding} and gave the end to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing there, a remembered repetition and not a change made again. The cold iron took the cold out of his fingertip. He read it as one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, an object joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn; the claim on him gathered interest in the rooms he went into and he did not pay it and did not refuse it, which in this district is a way of keeping a thing alive.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}; the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and eighteen days. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough}, the space under the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. The road went up toward \place{the Cross} and the boards on it were toward the tenth, and she did not turn her head along it. The reading block would be used on the morrow, but the market had not come and no slate had been opened under any roof: \person{Berr Clay}'s box stayed locked up the road with the true subtraction inside, \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. The seventh green was owed as a colour and the seventh sighting of the foundling as a shape, and neither had come early, and no loaf, slate or sale had crossed \place{the Clay Yard} before the first market. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen.

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline, and the order was the whole of it now that there was no tray to lay them on. He found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, from a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and eighteen days and none would be invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid, a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind; and now the three shapings of the pot saying stood with them --- sung before its time, not heard from since, gone quiet on every row --- three sayings about a pot and no fourth likeness standing up to call itself a cause, no pot lost and no name given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself at either; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, the third reading gone up the clay road in Berr Clay's locked box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, Hessa Rale's slate gone up unread with the writing to her sleeve, the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled, the damper one iron with two bites. Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161 when the hand waited at the iron for a sentence to finish; the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; the flagstone half-check made on Tallow 22, a place in a yard as the others are places in a wall and a room that does not touch either. A fourth place held the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone in the loft; a fifth held the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand, read at the well and on the carried body, exact and worth nothing at two paces; a sixth held the inch with all its answers, each filed on a place of its own and none standing up to call itself a longer reach, a key, a power or a rule. Beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, the burned-slate word stood a fourth direction arrived nowhere, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment and nothing a clerk could chart. No third gap came into the yard, no fourth likeness was made a cause and nothing was filled in to make a row come out even; the pan's gone round ring stayed on its own tray beside the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one crust and the field's empty place, and he did not lay a finger on any of them.

He put the question to his hand. Sella had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she kept toward no opening and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed; he took it through the small intake and the hold and the letting go of one of those breaths, and kept it through the little silence after her count had ended, and brought it down on the river's next low note. The two rooms did not touch, and neither did the two rhythms; he had kept one of them before this and he kept both now, remembered, not a change made again. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and could not now lift a crust two inches; the two facts stood on separate trays and neither was a lesson about the other. He filed the coordination on its sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a miss, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle Sella had set out, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand, and both were true, and neither was a sign.

Two hundred and eighteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, carried by the only person who would have known what the figure counted and set down in no slate; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighteen days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot, and none of the three counts became the others, because agreeing them would want a payer and no payer stood to any of them. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Grain 10 was held three mornings off --- the first Account-day and first market of the month and the first reading of Grain --- with the seventh green owed at a silent woman's mouth as a colour and the seventh sighting of the foundling at the market's edge as a shape, places that did not touch, and no loaf, slate or sale coming early to the basket. The stalls were going up and the reading block was being made ready, and the boy had not been carried to the Cross. The hand came down on the river's next low note after the house's count had ended, the thread frozen and unwiped at his mouth and one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear with its empty place still unnamed and no third. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof; the count went forward exact and unspent into Day 219, Grain 10, Tenthday, and nothing was settled and nothing was ended.
