\daymark{217}{Grain 8, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

The small hours gave the day to the child before they gave it to anybody else under the roof. He lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and did not move, and the hand that had come down on the letting go of a counted breath in the night lay against the fold where it had been put down, and he did not turn it over to ask what it meant. \person[Corin]{the boy} was two hundred and seventeen days old in \place{Low Kiln} and six months and thirty-seven days, and nobody who could have known what that counted said a word about it. A holding is a holding. Set down like a tool, it asks nothing of the hand that set it and promises nothing to the hand that may or may not take it up again; and the surgeon's register, which had read the small intake and the hold and the letting go as honestly as it had once read a chart, refused this morning to read them as a reaching.

The day came first as smell and only afterward as a gap in sound. Hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}; then the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and seventeen days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings, and overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. Grain's second working week had no market in it and no Account-day; the tenth stood three mornings off, a date going toward the Cross with no slate opened under any roof and no loaf, no sale and no reading brought early into a working day.

One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to stand there and make a row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; it went no further and it did not go away, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading at two paces, found it exact, complete and worth nothing, and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back the slant of the heat and the sour little breath of the corner, and that was the whole answer, enough to live on and not enough to spend.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, the way she came to everything that wanted keeping: the back first, the face withheld, the hand going where it had gone ten thousand times before. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. “The house,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and told the fire it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and the fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and she did not look toward \place{the bed} to see whether the boy had heard the gap.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word. She gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had rested a hand or a load for twenty years; nobody straightened the crease and nobody put a hand in it. The seat in the wheel-shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left, and a man coming into the shed took the shape of that hollow before he sat down in it. Where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner there was a clean dark line, and beside it the new, finer hairline of grey the house called the thaw, and neither had been swept; a saying of his that had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it ran --- \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, the way a wound that nobody dresses is worse than one that is dressed badly. No lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called. The keeping was maintenance, not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventeen days, and the household went around the shape of him the way it went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, with a look and a wide step and no number.

“The house will keep it,” Sella said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. At a morning and at a midday she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in thirty-seven days, because a shelf, a hearth and a morning are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

The question came to the hand as it came each morning, and the hand went up an inch toward the note and came down before the counted breath had finished --- down on the small intake, down with the hold still ahead of it. It was an honest miss filed on its sixth place and laid across nothing, neither a longer reach nor a hearing nor a seventh nor a stopping nor a payment nor one of the three held changes nor a key nor a power nor a rule, and no payer was named to it. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. The body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. Garl's name was not spoken with the sentence, and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on Grain 8. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark. He did not ask about the room and nobody asked about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

\scenebreak

It was into this morning, with \place{the Clay Yard} already warm and the boards already talking, that a kiln-hand come up from \place{High Kiln} with a barrow stopped over the boards and let a saying go. He had come on a wrong wind. There was \lore{lead glaze} at the corner of his mouth and the sweet edge of it riding the air ahead of him, and he had not come to the basket and did not look into it; he spoke over the boards the way a man speaks to the boards, with the price of barley waiting behind the sentence and the weather behind that. What he said was that there was a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. He took the saying for a general one, as men take the weather for a general thing, and went off up the yard with the barrow and the sweet edge going with him, and no pot was lost.

The boy read the man at two paces the way he read every body that came within reach of his eyes: the sweet at the mouth-corner, the hand on the barrow, the way the sentence went to the boards and not to the basket, the barley and the weather stacked behind it like instruments laid out in order. Exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, a body with no hand at the foot of its saying and no owner under it. Then he took the saying apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, laid open along its edges and refusing from the first cut to let it close on itself and call itself whole. He did not ask which pot, and he did not ask what ailed anybody, because a question put to a saying is a question that wants a payer standing behind it, and no payer stood behind any shaping of it.

Three sayings now stood on the near-full tray, and he set them out one beside the other with a finger between them and no finger laid across them. The old one, that a pot which sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, had gone around the yards for weeks and cost no pot. The next, that the pot which sang early last month has not been heard from since, had come up on a wrong wind two mornings back and turned by a hair from the general proverb into the report of one pot's silence. The third, that there is a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, had just come up on another wrong wind from High Kiln and bent back again toward the general. Three likenesses standing on one tray, and the surgeon's register knew what a row of likenesses will do to a man who wants an answer out of them. He would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any shaping; the pot that sang early was not the pot that had not been heard from, and neither was the pot that had gone quiet on every row, and a silence reported was not a second hearing of the same pot. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make three likenesses come out even is a finding forged. So the three stayed where they were set, sayings about a pot and nothing more, advanced on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, with no cause stood up, no owner, no room, no figure and no sixth hearing promoted to a seventh.

The sweet edge of lead glaze came up off High Kiln on the wrong wind, touched the fold and went out on the wind that brought it. He took it as a direction with a place and a going, not as a second edge paid and not as a second green at a mouth, and no seventh of anything came to stand where the others had not stood. The wrong wind went out of the yard with the sweetness behind it and left nothing in the air that a clerk could take down.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the shut counting room stood at two with no third, and \lore{the burned-slate word} stood where it had stood, a direction arrived nowhere from \place{Slate Row}, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, nothing a clerk could chart.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back from the kiln-hand who had come up on the wrong wind and gone off with his saying. The knowing stayed on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. He let the saying stand as a saying. He gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188 and made no lesson of the green on the wind, and no pot was lost in the yard. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand, and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The boy read it as one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, an object worth nothing joined to no kiln.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} a rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the month's pots would want now the kilns were at their heat. \place{the trough} stood bare and dark beside her with its skin of ice gone and its water called weather, and Sella sent the question to the pot and the weather, and the woman went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. A man come up off the clay road kept a number shut in his fist and would not let it out into the air; he brought it as far as the well and put it back into his sleeve, so that it arrived nowhere a second time, and Sella gave the weather and the river in place of a figure. The printed \emph{one in twelve} stood unchallenged over the boards, and the true subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the road in \place{Grath}, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188; \person[Hessa Rale]{Hessa}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank}, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called. All three stayed offstage in the boy's file and none opened or reached back. The district's arithmetic went on being done in loaves and glances and the weather, never in a figure, and the carrying arm that had brought him up the yard remembered weight and not cost.

\scenebreak

By midday the three shapings of the saying stood on the near-full tray with no hand laying them together. The boy set them out as he had set out the hearings and the stoppings and the held changes, each on its own place and the empty places left empty, and he laid nothing across them and named no payer to any of them. A report of one pot's silence had bent back into a proverb about all pots and gathered interest on a tray nobody under any roof could read, and it had not become a lesson and no pot had been lost. Two hundred and seventeen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or Grath, carried by the only person who would have known what the number counted and set down in no slate; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and seventeen days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a knot. Three counts in three places, and none became one.

The wrong wind went out of the yard with the sweet edge behind it and nothing paid.
