The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat left the high air first, going up off the boards and the peg and the rim of the pot, and settled downward as if it had somewhere better to be, till it lay longest where the day had least room to move it: under the shelf, behind the pot, in the fold at the boy's ear. No warmth this was that could be taken up and carried. It was the day's leavings, gathered in low places, and it kept him where he lay the way a shallow dish keeps a spoon. \person[Corin]{the boy} was two hundred and sixteen days old under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and the number stood where it had stood in the small hours, exact, complete and worth nothing, spoken by nobody who would have known what it counted.

At the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} the underside of the board became a roof. It was a roof with an edge: it stopped where his hand stopped, and beyond that edge the air was the air of the room and not the air under the board, and no amount of lying still made the one place into the other. He had learned the shape of that edge with the patience of an instrument laid out on a towel. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to stand there and make a row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry air and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back the slant of heat under the board and the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, and that was the whole answer, enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. \place{the salt-stiff coat} held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had rested a hand or a load for twenty years; nobody straightened the crease and nobody put a hand in it. The seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left, and a man coming into the shed took the shape of that hollow before he sat down in it. Where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner there was a clean dark line, and beside it the new, finer hairline of grey the house called the thaw, and neither had been swept. There was a saying of his that had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it: \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}. A saying about honest repairs, said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out and the line left clean. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, the way a wound that nobody dresses is worse than one that is dressed badly.

No lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called. The keeping of him was maintenance, not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and sixteen days, and the household went around the shape of him the way it went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, with a look and a wide step and no number.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf with her back to the basket, the way she came to everything that wanted keeping: the back first, the face withheld, the hand going where it had gone ten thousand times before. She moved her lips along the loaf row and the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word, and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and sixteen days. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

At an afternoon and at a dusk she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. It hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. An afternoon was not an opening and a dusk was not an opening, and there had been no opening in thirty-six days. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and the space at the row's end, and the number two hundred and sixteen was not among them; it stood in the boy's file as an object on its own tray and would not be entered by any hand under this roof.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed. The instruction went to the broom and not to him, the way every instruction in the house went to the tool before it went to the man, and he took the tool up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. Garl's name was not spoken with the sentence, and Tarn gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth as it had come before. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure under it and no name on it. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and went on with the broom. It was paid, if it was paid at all, as an interval and not as a calling: a breath held at the threshold, a word stopped short of the air, nothing entered. Nine stoppings stood where nine had stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year, which now stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. The warm stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone on Day 183 stood on a fourth place beside the three held changes, not a hearing, not a seventh, not made into a cause.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on Grain 7. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark. He did not ask about the room and \person{Jorra Vail} did not ask about the ear, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

\scenebreak

At the edge of the Vail wheel-shed Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the shut counting room stood at two with no third, and the burned-slate word stood where it had stood, a direction arrived nowhere from \place{Slate Row}, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, nothing a clerk could chart.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from the men who had come up on wrong winds, whose sayings about a pot that sang before its time and was then not heard from had stayed sayings about a pot, with no hand at the foot of them, no owner, no room, no cause and no figure. No lesson was made and no pot was lost.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand, and held until the sentence reached the pot, until \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} was finished with \emph{feeding} at the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, filed where the old fraction lay and not paired with any other. The boy read it as he had read it before: one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, an object worth nothing joined to no kiln. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. She went wide around the places the yard kept without naming: the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough} at \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed. Each had a look and a wide step and no number. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, exact, complete and worth nothing, keeping the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, and telling nothing a clerk could take down. She set him on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen and unwiped. Grain 10 held three mornings off with the seventh green still a colour and the foundling still a shape, and no loaf, no slate and no sale coming early into a working day.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and stayed for the small intake only, and came down before the letting go. It was an honest fraction on its sixth place, shorter than the whole breath and shorter than the river's own low interval, neither a miss nor a holding made twice. He filed it where the others lay and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle Sella had set out for him, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. The body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true.

Two hundred and sixteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, carried by the only person who would have known what the number counted and by no slate in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and sixteen days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places, and none became one. The hand came down before the letting go of a breath nobody under the roof had counted, the thread frozen at his mouth and the one stiff crust two inches from his ear. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
