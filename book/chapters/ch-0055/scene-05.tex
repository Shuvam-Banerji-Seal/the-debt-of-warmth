\daymark{218}{Grain 9, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

The hand came down on the river's next low note and lay against the fold where it had been put, and the small hours handed the day to the child before they handed it to anyone else under the roof. It was a holding set down like a tool is set down, no more a reaching than the spoon laid on the board when the gruel is done, and the surgeon's register, which had read the draw and the slack and the small pause as honestly as it had once read a chart, refused this morning to read the quiet hand as a promise of anything. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, two hundred and eighteen days old in the count nobody kept and six months and thirty-eight days in the count nobody had been asked to keep, and nobody who could have known what either figure counted said a word about it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighteen days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never numbered among the hearings; overhead the boards held four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have gone. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

The day arrived first as smell and only afterwards as a gap in sound. Hot clay came under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Then the kiln drew in fours with a place missing, not a bell. He lay and let the two come together the way they had come together every morning of his life, the warmth and the absence of a warmth, and the child in him wanted while the man in him took the temperature of the wanting and found it unchanged. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with the empty place beside it still without a name and no third coming to stand there and make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of a written order. It went no farther and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

The surgeon's register took the reading at two paces, found it exact, complete and worth nothing, and put the question of what it was for to a room that answered only with the slant of the heat and the sour little breath out of the corner. There was no towel and there were no instruments, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline, and he had long since learned to turn the file out without a case. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth out of a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh had come in two hundred and eighteen days and none would be invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid, and the three sayings about the pot stood where they had been set, sayings and nothing more, with no hand laying them together and no fourth likeness standing up to call itself a cause. He did not go over the whole file this morning. A man who has read a chart for twelve years does not reread it at every bedside; he reads the body in front of him, which was cold at the wrist and had one crust two inches out of reach, and he asks the question the chart cannot answer, which is who pays for the warming.

\scenebreak

The word came over the boards of \place{the Clay Yard} once more before the morning had properly opened, and it came from \place{Slate Row} as a direction and not a visit. A voice went along the boards where the neighbours stood with their baskets, spoken over the boards and not to any basket, on the same breath as the price of barley and the weather, and it said that a slate had burned in the night, with nobody saying whose slate, which room, what hand at the flame or why. It was the fourth telling of the burned slate. \lore{The burned-slate word} had come over these boards on three mornings before, a direction each time, arriving nowhere, and now it had come a fourth, and still the tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two with no third. The boy lay under the roof and took the fourth telling apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, laying it open along its edges and refusing from the first cut to let it close on itself and call itself whole. A voice from a direction was not a witness. A word going around a yard was not a hand at a flame, and four tellings of a burning were not four witnesses to one fire, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and this voice stood a quarter league away and went on to the price of barley.

He set the telling on its own place, advanced and not paid, and he set it where he had set the others: beside Jorra's meal-fine dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three and no finger laid across. It was a direction arrived nowhere, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, and nothing a clerk could chart, for a clerk charts what a payer stands to and no payer stood to a voice on the wind. He had learned in a better-lit room what four likenesses standing in a row will do to a man who wants an answer; he had watched a chart made to agree with itself and knew that the neatness of a row is no evidence that anything in it is true. So he left the four tellings on four places and let them stand there saying nothing to one another. No slate burned under any roof this morning and no new fire was lit at \place{the kiln mouth}; a new fire at the mouth would have cost something and the telling cost nothing, which was the whole of the distinction and the whole of the reason he kept the two apart. The three sayings about the pot stayed three sayings about a pot on their own tray, and he did not lay the burned slate across them either, because a word about a slate and a word about a pot are two words, and a man who makes one out of them is a man building an account out of nothing but his own wanting.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray with air under it and a want in it, and he made no lesson of the green on the wind and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, who went on breathing up \place{the clay road} with the cost entered nowhere.

Whenever the burned-slate word went around he grew quieter, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. Nobody told anybody to straighten. Sella gave the fire its instruction and Tarn gave the broom its, and the two went on with their work as if the quiet were a piece of weather they had all agreed to work in. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn; the claim on him gathered interest in the rooms he went into, and he did not pay it and did not refuse it, which in this district is a way of keeping a thing alive. He had said once that a slate burns in four minutes and timed it, and he did not say it now. The two tellings of the room shut in the war stood where they stood and he did not join them to the word over the boards, and the back room kept its finger's width of open and the men who had gone low into it before first grey got no name from him and no description of the cold.

A neighbour came to the door with a cost-question about the stalls going up at \place{the Cross} for the tenth, and \person{Sella Vail} sent it to the pot and the weather, and it went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. At \place{the yard well} a rack woman with slip dried pale in her wrists asked what the month's pots would want, and got the pot and the weather for an answer and went away with her question still whole in her hand. Nothing was entered in any slate under any roof. Word went up the clay road of boards being carried toward the Cross and of \place{the reading block} that would be used on the morrow, but the market had not come and no slate had been opened; \person{Berr Clay}'s box stayed locked up the road with the true subtraction inside, \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place[the north bridge]{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. Grain 10 was held three mornings off, a direction with a date and no slate opened yet; the seventh green was owed as a colour and the seventh sighting of the foundling as a shape, and neither had come early, and no loaf, slate or sale had crossed the yard before the first market.

\scenebreak

Sella came to the fire before she came to anything else that wanted keeping, and gave every instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. “The house,” she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and a moment later “The house will keep it,” to the coals. With her back to the basket she moved her lips along the loaf row and the row stood with two unnamed gaps and she gave nobody a figure; her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. She carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough}, the space beneath the lowest rack and the field's empty place, each with a look and a wide step and no number. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. At a morning and a noon she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in thirty-eight days, because a morning, a shelf and a noon are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

When her voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until she finished \emph{feeding} and gave the end to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing there. The cold iron took the cold out of his fingertip. The boy read the thing as one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, an object worth nothing joined to no kiln and entered in no slate, and he filed it where the old fraction lay and not as a change made again. \person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat holding a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow and its lean, the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned and the new finer hairline beside it called the thaw, and neither swept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighteen days and no lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of each reach, a small interval showing where it should have gone in; his chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, and the loan was not called; if the thing was paid at all it was paid as an interval and not as a calling. He filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, and nine stoppings stood where they had always stood, Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects on a tray and not a rule, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, which had still not given. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Grain 9. The warm stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and he did not go up to it, and did not reconcile it with the debt by trying, which was not forgiving it.

In the morning, with the house going about its keeping and no hand under any roof offered for the asking, the boy put the question to his own. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note and came down at once against the fold, down before the note had drawn out, and there was no failure hidden under the miss waiting to be found. Conditions had sometimes answered; this morning they did not, and an answer refused is an object on its own tray and no less an object for being refused. He filed the miss on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. Two hundred and eighteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, carried by the only person who would have known what the number counted and set down in no slate; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a knot, and none of the three became the others.

In the shed Jorra's quiet went before him the way a cold air goes before a door, and the household straightened around it without an order and asked nothing about the room or the ear. The boy's hand lay back down against the fold with the day still ahead of it and the count at two hundred and eighteen, the thread frozen at his mouth and unwiped, one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear with its empty place still unnamed and no third. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
