The afternoon did not end. It thinned the way broth thins, from the surface down, and the light that came through the oiled paper in the window lay on the floorboards in a flat grey sheet with no warmth in it. Heat went out of the high air first and settled into the low places --- under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay, where one leathery brown-edged crust kept its two inches from his ear and the empty place beside it kept its namelessness and no third came to stand there and make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and it stopped there as if it had reached the end of a written order. It went no farther and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading at two paces, found it exact, complete and worth nothing, and put the question of what it was for to a room that answered only with the slant of the heat and the sour little breath out of the unswept corner.

Under the shelf the underside of a board became a roof, and the roof stopped where his hand stopped. It was a small mercy of carpentry, an angle that held warmth the way a cupped hand holds water, and he had lain beneath it long enough to know its edges by the changing pressure of the air against his cheek. He did not call it shelter. A roof that ends at a hand is only a board, and a man who asks more of it than that is a man looking for a payer under a bench. He lay still and let the warmth come to him on its own terms, which were slow. The surgeon in him read the afternoon as a vital sign, the heat falling away from the extremities and gathering at the core, and the child in him wanted the crust that lay two inches beyond the reach of either hand, and the two readings stood in the same body without either one giving way. That was the whole of the argument, and it had been going on now for two hundred and seventeen days without a verdict.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf with her back to the basket, the way she came to everything that wanted keeping: the back first, the face withheld, the hand going where it had gone ten thousand times before. She moved her lips along the loaf row and the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word; she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and no man coming in could have told the two apart by the way the board was worn.

She went to the fire before she went anywhere else that wanted an afternoon's keeping, and told it it was \lore{the thaw}. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and the fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and she did not look toward \place{the bed} to see whether the boy had heard the gap. “The house,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and a moment later “The house will keep it,” to the coals, and stood with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. At an afternoon and a dusk she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in thirty-seven days, because an afternoon, a shelf and a dusk are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had rested a hand or a load for twenty years; nobody straightened the crease and nobody put a hand in it. The seat in the wheel-shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left, and a man coming into the shed took the shape of that hollow before he sat down in it. Where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner there was a clean dark line, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey the house called the thaw, and neither had been swept; a saying of his that had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it ran --- leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, the way a wound that nobody dresses is worse than one that is dressed badly. No lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called. His keeping was maintenance and not a story; a line left where it is is still a line kept. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventeen days, and the household went around the shape of him the way it went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, with a look and a wide step and no number.

The three sayings about the pot stayed where they had been set that morning, sayings and nothing more, and no hand in the household laid them together. The old one, that a pot which sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, had gone around the yards for weeks and cost no pot. The second, that the pot which sang early last month has not been heard from since, had come up on a wrong wind and turned by a hair from a general proverb into the report of one pot's silence. The third, that there is a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, had come up on another wrong wind and bent back again toward the general. Three likenesses standing on one tray, advanced on the near-full place beside the hearings and never among them, with a finger between them and no finger laid across. The boy took them apart the way he had taken them apart in the morning, laid open along their edges and refusing from the first cut to let them close on themselves and call themselves whole. He would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any shaping. A silence reported was not a second hearing of the same pot, and three shapings of one saying were not three witnesses to one death. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a row of likenesses filled in to make a case out of them is a finding forged. No lesson was made of them and no pot was lost in the yard, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, who went on breathing up \place{the clay road} with the cost entered nowhere and nobody under any roof called to answer for him.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. He gave no name to the man who had turned back, though he had been that man. He said Yes, house to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His exact account was the one thing in the house that nobody disputed and nobody could check, and he kept it the way a man keeps a wound clean, daily, without comment, with a hand that knew precisely how much pressure the thing would bear.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth once more in the going of the light, and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the loan was not called. If the thing was paid at all it was paid as an interval and not as a calling: a breath held at a threshold, a word stopped short of the air, nothing entered in any slate. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Grain 8. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark. Nine stoppings stood where they had always stood, Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects on a tray and not a rule, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, which had still not given; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of each reach, a small interval showing where it should have gone in. The warm stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and he did not go up to it, and did not reconcile it with the debt by trying, which was not forgiving it.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, its door left a finger's width open, and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the shut counting room stood at two with no third, and \lore{the burned-slate word} stood where it had stood, a direction arrived nowhere from \place{Slate Row}, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, nothing a clerk could chart. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back and let the saying stand as a saying. He gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188 and made no lesson of the green on the wind, and no pot was lost in the yard.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand, and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The cold iron took the cold out of his fingertip; the boy read the thing as one iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, an object worth nothing joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. Jorra grew quieter whenever the burned-slate word went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He did not look toward the bed and he did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough}, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number; the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, and went to her own bed before the small hours. Two hundred and seventeen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, carried by the only person who would have known what the number counted and set down in no slate; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and seventeen days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a knot. Three counts in three places, and none became one, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

In the small hours the house was quiet and there was no breathing number anywhere in it, nothing to hold a hand against but the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. In the room that did not touch his, Sella's breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she kept toward no opening and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The boy put the question to his hand as he had put it six times before and the hand went up an inch toward the note and stayed. He took it through the draw and the slack and the small pause of the river's own low interval and brought it down on the next low note, down with the water rather than before it or after it --- the one-rhythm coordination remembered, not a change made again. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The asking was answered and the answer was a keeping, and the keeping was a remembering. His body refused him and answered him in the same inch, and he let both stand; he still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down.

Two hundred and seventeen went forward exact, complete and unspent into the next day with nobody to speak it, and the fire's fifth stayed unpaid two hundred and seventeen days and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. The hand lay down against the fold where it had been put down, the thread frozen at his mouth and unwiped, one stiff crust two inches from his ear with its empty place still unnamed and no third, and the day left everything where it had found it.
