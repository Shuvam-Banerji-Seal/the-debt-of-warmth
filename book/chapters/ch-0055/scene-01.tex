\daymark{216}{Grain 7, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay down against the fold and stayed down where it had been put, the way a tool lies where a man has set it on a bench and turns away. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \person[Corin]{the boy} did not go back into the dark to find a meaning in what had been given there in the night. A holding was a holding. It lay where it lay, and picking it up to turn it over would only wear the edge, and he would not make a sign of it, because the making of a sign would have wanted a payer standing behind it and no payer stood. He was two hundred and sixteen days old and six months and thirty-six days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number went on under the not-expecting of it the way a coal goes on under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. He had stopped expecting to be told. That was not the same as not wanting it, and the distinction was a small hard object he kept and did not set down.

The day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound, the order days had kept since he could remember coming to them at all. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season's name instead of a cause. Beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short, and then the gap came. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered, and this was a drawing-in that stopped where it stopped and asked nobody to cross himself. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by a decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and sixteen days with no payer named to it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be while nobody put a hand to it; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places: the fire's four and its silence, the river's drawing and slackening, the boards' four and four and the knot. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so they lay in their three places and kept their own company.

The cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. It went no further and it did not go away. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back hot clay and wet wool and the sour corner, and that was the whole answer, enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to stand there. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry air and was unwiped. Nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

\scenebreak

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in two hundred and sixteen days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. That last had grown by a hair since the firing month. The old proverb said a pot that sang before its time generally sang itself out early; a kiln-hand on a wrong wind had said the pot which sang early last month had not been heard from since. Two sayings about a pot stood there, both sayings and nothing more, with no hand at the foot of either, no owner, no room, no cause and no figure, and no lesson made of them. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, the third reading gone up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, \person[Hessa Rale]{Hessa}'s slate gone up unread toward \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, with no tenth stood up before the seventh year, which now stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. \lore{The damper} was one iron with two bites, the dust kept apart from the \lore{slip}, and the two shut-room tellings kept at two with no third.

Three held changes stood on three places. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left, held and never spoken, no payer named to it and no slate entered. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. The flagstone half-check made on Tallow 22 stood on a third place beside the other two, a place in a yard as the others were places in a wall and in a room that does not touch either. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named. A fourth place: the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone from the loft sill on Day 183, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not made into a cause. A fifth: the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand, read at the well on Day 187 and on the carried body on Day 188, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, joined to no sweet wind and no dust and no pot that sang early. And beside them all, with a hand between, the burned-slate word on its own place beside the wheel-keeper's dust and the two shut-room tellings --- a direction arrived nowhere, told three times over the boards from \place{Slate Row}, not a cause, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, nothing a clerk could chart.

He did not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. There was no third gap in the fold and no fourth likeness made a cause, and nothing was filled to make a row come out even. Two hundred and sixteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid for two hundred and sixteen days; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

\scenebreak

Word came up \place{the clay road} before first grey that stalls were being set at \place{the Cross} against Grain 10. A neighbour went up with boards on a barrow, the wood knocking its own knock at every rut, and went on up out of the yard without looking back at any window. The boy took it as a direction with a date, the way he took the burned-slate word as a direction from Slate Row: the market had not come, no Account-day stood in these three days, and no slate had been opened under any roof. Grain 10 was three mornings off and held there. The district's arithmetic went on being done in loaves and glances and arms and the weather, never in a figure, and the boards going up meant nothing that a clerk could take down until the slate came out. He had learned to leave a direction standing as a direction and not walk it into a cause.

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket, because that was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing, and she kept it the way a widow keeps a wound clean: daily, without comment, with a hand that knew exactly how much pressure the thing would bear. She stood with her back to the basket at \place{the hearth} and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She kept her half-pace from every open slate, and she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. A morning is not an opening and a neighbour going up with boards is not an opening, and there had been no opening in thirty-six days. Her private roll stayed unshown, and Dessa's cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

A neighbour at the threshold asked what the month's firing would want, with cost in the question and the weather on the same breath. Sella told her to ask the pot what a wet thing cost set down there, and gave the weather the rest of it, and the woman went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. \person{Garl Vail} was kept to the seventh morning without a story, the way the house kept most of what it would not set down in words. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder, and the seat in the wheel-shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left that nobody filled. Where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner there was a clean dark line, and beside it the new, finer hairline of grey the house called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb had gone to a pot with no crack in it --- \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- a saying about honest repairs said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor named, circled without a word and worse for being uncalled. No lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and sixteen days, and the keeping of him was maintenance, not a story. A line left where it is is still a line kept.

In \place{the Clay Yard} the going-out went around the places the yard kept without naming. There was a look and a wide step at the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it, at the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, at the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood, at the bare dark \place[the trough]{trough} at \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, at the field's empty place and at the place the carriers crossed. Each had its look and its wide step and no number. The \place{the drying racks} stood to the east. The boy took the absences one at a time as he had been taught by a better-lit room to take a row of similar wounds, and no finger of his drew a line across them and no third gap came to make the fold come out even.

\scenebreak

The morning hand went up an inch toward the note and came down at once against the fold, down before the note had drawn out. It was a miss, and there was no failure hidden under it waiting to be found. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle Sella had set out for him, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. The body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true.

The week opened without a boundary being drawn. Beyond the yard the boards went up \place{the clay road} toward the Cross for the tenth, and the oldest man in the room was the least use in it and the only one keeping the count. Two hundred and sixteen went forward exact and unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, carried by the only person who would have known what the number counted and by no slate in Sarn, Sill or Grath. The carried fire's fifth stayed unpaid; the seventh year stayed at its very end and ungiven; Grain 10 held three mornings off with the seventh green still a colour and the foundling still a shape, and no loaf, no slate and no sale coming early into a working day. The hand lay down against the fold with the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped and the one stiff crust two inches from his ear, and the pot handle stayed two inches beyond the reaching hand. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
