# Chapter 13 — The Approach to Silt (beat sheet)

**Days:** 55–60 · Ilmen 26–30, then Silt 1, Y.H. 813. Sixthday to Firstday. **Mode:** passage — six lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and its own dated entry, each lived on the page for at least 800 words. Day 54 is finished and is not revisited; Day 61 belongs to Chapter 14. **Arc:** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — "a warm stone, already going cold." **Length:** six scenes, ~14,100 words total.

**Governing rules for every scene.** Point of view stays inside Corin/Dan; close third-person past tense. No present-tense Chicago break and no `\memory{}` tag this chapter — the old life stays offstage, though the surgeon's register colours the noticing. No recap, no exposition speech, no modern simile in Sarn. The body humiliates him in every scene: drool frozen and unwiped, head going where it wants with the small wet sound, cold at cheek–temple–wrist, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward warmth and coming down; he cannot roll, stand, speak, read Grathic, reach a cup or lift a hand to his own chin. Every cost has a payer; every figure goes past him unspendable. The trays never touch: six hearings of the full note stay six (five from four sources, one from Dessa), beside and never among; two nearly-full answers stay advanced and not paid; three intervals, five unjoined pauses, the told bell beside the heard bell, the word *forty*, the knot's shape and the weight of a thumb, Mira's two cords and Orra's knot-string on boards that do not touch, the early note beside the bells and the kiln's gap — all kept apart. No seventh hearing, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no naming of nearly, no experiment, no clamp, no rank change, no heresy, no recognition, no name or number from Dessa, no Marra, no one in three, no word forty spoken again, no quest, no ending.

**Roster on stage.** Corin; Sella Vail; Tarn; Jorra Vail; Alda Reed (Day 56); Mira Silt and Berr Clay (Day 59); Garl Vail and Dessa Silt kept by objects throughout. A man come down from Greybank speaks once at the well on Day 55 — walk-on, no sheet; the Fishstairs clerk is glimpsed and does not speak (Day 59). Orra and Fenna stay offstage; Dessa stays dead.

## Scene 1 — The Closed Counting Room

**Day & time.** Day 55, Ilmen 26, Y.H. 813, Sixthday; first light at the yard well, the bucket going round, into the carrying-home at dusk.
**Place.** The yard well in Low Kiln, then the Clay Yard and the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (fifty-five days old), Sella Vail, Tarn, the man come downriver from Greybank, neighbours at the well.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open at the well with the bucket going over the stone lip; frost-bloom gone by noon and the skin on the water spoken of to the bucket, not to the boy. Sella sets the basket in the well's shade as a pot is set, out of the draught, and gives every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket (T058, T060).
2. A man come downriver from Greybank, road-mud on his boots and the salt-and-hot-clay smell of the east road on him, waits his turn at the bucket and tells the yard what happened in the last war: a counting room was shut, the damper closed, and the men inside died of the breath — not of the war's hand, of their own heat going out with no way to feed it. He says it as a thing that happened eighteen leagues east, a curiosity with a date in it.
3. The yard receives it as history. A woman says the war did for them; a man says a room shut up is a room nobody's counted; somebody says the frost will take the pits early. Nobody in the yard connects the saying to any kiln in Low Kiln, and Sella does not connect it; she tells the fire it is the thaw and tells the fire to be fed (T085).
4. Tarn agrees with the neighbour who wants the thaw early and the one who wants it late, and goes on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. His knife is back on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back; Ash is worked into the knuckles no scrubbing lifts (T036). He sweeps the road to the Fishstairs over and over without turning along it, and no collector comes down it.
5. The boy, in the shade with the fold at his chin, hears the phrase *the breath* and *the damper closed* without Grathic words for what they are in his own house. He files the Greybank saying on a tray of its own, beside the told bell of the Long Night, the heard bell at the Cross and the kiln's own breath that misses one in every four — four low things kept in four places, and he writes no cause across any of them (T065 planted, T078, T071/T072).
6. Six hearings stay six; no seventh comes. The nearly-full answers stay on their own tray. The old-life register notes that a room can be sealed and the bill paid by the bodies inside, and he cannot lift a hand to the bucket or say a word of it (T061/T082).
7. Dusk: Sella lifts the basket level and carries him up the Clay Yard at a half-pace from every open slate; she sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, and says nothing about the man or his counting room.

**Emotional turn.** The yard's ordinary weather-talk takes in a stranger's story of men suffocating in a sealed room and turns it into a dated curiosity; the boy alone hears it as a description of the danger under his own roof. The turn is from inert history to a private, unusable recognition — a danger spoken aloud by somebody who has no idea what he has just described, and no one to receive it.

**Sensory anchors.** Cold rope and wet stone lip; the skin on the bucket; woodsmoke and wet wool; the east-road salt and hot clay on the Greybank man; the iron sound of a damper closed; breath going out of a room; drool freezing unwiped; cold arriving cheek, temple, wrist.

**Threads advanced.** T065 (planted — closed counting room during the last war, men dead of the breath with the damper closed, toward ~650), T078 (low sounds kept in separate places), T071/T072 (exact count), T058 and T060 (instruction to fire/wool/bucket; warmth entered nowhere), T085 (thaw as weather), T036 (seventh year held, late thaw wanted), T061/T082 (six hearings stay six, no joining).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Plants T065: the kiln's breath, named in this house as the thing that takes a man in his sleep, is described by a stranger as a room shut during a war and a damper closed, and the boy keeps the two sayings apart. Plants the later heretical shape that a vessel may be left where it is and the world does not end — here only as a room nobody thinks to count. Pays nothing. Joins nothing.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the Vail roof with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it; behind him the carried fire keeps under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down.

## Scene 2 — The Tin Above the Bed

**Day & time.** Day 56, Ilmen 27, Y.H. 813, Seventhday; mid-morning in House Silt three doors down, then back under the Vail roof by dusk.
**Place.** The front room of House Silt (the birth room, the room where Dessa lay) and the Vail kiln-house.
**Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Alda Reed; Dessa kept by the room and the tin.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries the basket three doors down and sets it just inside a door standing a finger's width open, at the edge of the light and away from anywhere a slate lies open; she holds it level with the little-door burn shut on the wicker and opens no slate, and turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row (T058, T084).
2. From the low angle of the basket the boy sees the round pewter tin above the empty bed: a pale fold at its seam and a thumb's bright rub on the lid. It still holds its scrap of birth-cloth; nobody visits a bank and nobody opens it. He notices the object the way he notices a vessel's seam — a wall, a closure, a place where a hand has been — and knows neither what it holds nor that the fold was warmed at another fire (T089, T074, T081).
3. Alda Reed comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye under the soap, the birth-slates under her arm. She lays the slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold flat at his shoulder a breath too long, and touches the little tin inside her own bodice once, without opening it. Two tins in one room, both shut, kept on separate trays; he cannot ask either (T023, T089).
4. She makes the two-finger sign at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing she does it, and covers the sign with the same unasked-for date she gave Dessa — Stone seven, the account up on the eighth — and goes on with a cooper's seventh in Mud End and a potter's third child in Stonemouth after there is no question left to answer (T007).
5. To Sella's *he lies quiet* she says *warm and breathing*, exact and not true. The lie told for mercy is eleven days old now and has curdled through to a death nobody entered that no living person can correct; she does not say the woman's name or the third day (T008, T073).
6. She writes no sixth note this chapter — the hidden record stays at five, turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name — and goes out with her tin shut. Sella stands a half-pace off and does not ask. The wall between the two women has no door in it, and Sella's private roll stays unshown (T023, T058).
7. Sella carries him back up the Clay Yard, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and puts the fold right without looking at his face. The six hearings stay six and no seventh comes.

**Emotional turn.** A room entered as an errand becomes a room full of unopened records: the mother's kept fold above the bed and the midwife's kept tin inside her bodice. The boy reads both as vessels with seams he cannot open or ask about; the kindness that cannot be answered becomes an object that cannot be opened. The turn is from noticing a thing to carrying an absence of knowledge about it — and from a woman who writes everything down to one who this day writes nothing at all, because the sixth page belongs to another week.

**Sensory anchors.** Cold cloth and river silt; soap gone sour under lye; pewter gone dull with a thumb's bright rub on the lid; the pale fold at the seam; birth-cloth; woodsmoke in the wool; the soft scrape of a slate laid writing-down; a door kept a finger's width open; the small wet sound of a head gone too far.

**Threads advanced.** T089 (tin above the bed, unopened), T074 (the mother's warmth kept at home), T023 (Alda's tin and five notes, no sixth), T007 (unaware sign covered with a date), T008 and T073 (the mercy-lie curdling; name and number unsaid), T081 (House Silt as a likeness of the rooms), T058 and T084 (half-pace, no bank errand, not-lookings kept apart), T061/T082 (six hearings stay six).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Carries the convergence of private records — tin, slate, cord, crust, knot-string — toward the day they are read together by no assessor (~1400). Carries the unopened pewter tin toward the later recognition that the mother's warmth was kept in a material no Court would read; carries the sign and the date toward the second notes and the rubbing of Y.H. 651 (~700). Pays nothing, opens nothing.

**Exact last image.** From the low angle of the basket in House Silt, the pewter tin keeping its place above the empty bed, the pale fold at its seam and the thumb's bright rub on the lid, while the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the shelf and comes down.

## Scene 3 — The Number Kept Badly

**Day & time.** Day 57, Ilmen 28, Y.H. 813, Eighthday; morning through afternoon and into the dusk bank under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the Vail wheel-shed and the kiln-house.
**Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl kept by his objects.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The household's stores are thinner and nobody says so. Sella moves a loaf along the shelf and comes back to the same place twice; there is a space at the end where a loaf should stand. She goes around the days to Silt in loaves and glances — three, two, whatever the moot gives out — and never gives the figure (T071/T072, T077, T057).
2. The boy keeps the number exactly: three days from Ilmen 28 to Silt 1, and he counts them as the house cannot, in the order a surgeon counts what is left before a hand goes in. Knowing it buys him nothing he can lift a hand to.
3. Jorra comes in from the wheel-shed with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger. He tells a pot it will do, tells the wheel it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady, and knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel but would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it (T035).
4. He goes to the damper ahead of Sella's instruction with the bent finger leading, so that the end of her sentence goes to the pot. He does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed or the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed (T035).
5. Tarn's small bench knife is on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back — the one entry in this house the house cannot check. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifts and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds the light and gives it back. He says *Yes, house* to the early thaw and *Yes, house* to the late and keeps wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying (T036).
6. A vessel gives its note past the yard door; Tarn's head does not turn and he does not name the sound, and the boy does not call it a seventh hearing. The two ears under one roof keep their equality, and the loan that can hear stays uncalled (T061, T076).
7. Garl's salt-stiff coat holds a broader man's shape on its peg, the wheel-seat its twenty-year lean that nobody fills, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned is not swept, and one of his proverbs goes to the pot and the fire and never to the basket (T009, T013, T065). The carried fire lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. The coal the brine-gull took on Day 45 is entered in no slate and missed in no account (T088).
8. One crust lies in the fold where two had lain, stiff past smelling, and the empty place beside it is carried into the new day without a name. The boy wants the crust and cannot reach it; he wants the number spoken and nobody will speak it.

**Emotional turn.** A household decreasing its stores becomes a household refusing to count what it has, and the boy's exactness is exposed as the least useful thing in the room. The turn is toward a shared, unspoken hunger: the house keeps its days badly in loaves and glances because to keep them exactly would be to own them, and the boy keeps them exactly because it is the only work available to him.

**Sensory anchors.** Dried bone-coloured slip; the scoop and scrape of the damper; tallow and cold clay; the space at the end of the loaf shelf; Ash ground into knuckles; the pale clip-ring catching light; iron cooling under blind ash; the small flat knock of a vessel's note coming through a door.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 and T077 (exact count against the withheld number), T057 (Sella's roll unshown), T035 (dust/slip, damper ahead, *he's not Vail* sealed), T036 and T076 (seventh year held; two silences under one roof; loan uncalled), T009, T013 and T065 (coat, seat, clean dark line, proverb, kiln's breath), T088 (coal gone with the gull), T060/T086 (one crust, empty place), T061/T082 (six hearings stay six).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Carries the withheld number toward the true deduction and the printed one in twelve; carries Jorra's dust and the unnameable back room toward the broken birth-slates and the day he hands a name to people who burn slates; carries the exact count toward the Silt flood, where it will be the only record nobody can tax. Pays nothing, joins nothing.

**Exact last image.** Sella moving a loaf along the shelf and coming back to the same place, the space at the end of the row that she will not call a number, while the boy counts it exactly in the dark and his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down.

## Scene 4 — The Sweeping Already Done

**Day & time.** Day 58, Ilmen 29, Y.H. 813, Ninthday; morning in the Clay Yard and at the drying racks, afternoon under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the drying racks and the Vail kiln-house.
**Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail (briefly, at the shed); Garl and Dessa kept by objects and absence.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The yard talks of preparing a sweeping for Silt — of going around the corners with a broom, of what will be burned and called dealt with — though the sweeping it speaks of was done on Ilmen 1 and the grey was burned before light (T013, T009).
2. Sella lays one thumb flat in the clean dark line where the Vail Ash ridge turned its corner for a year and two days, then walks two doors down and lays the same thumb in the ridge there, a finger's width wider, and brings the knowledge back without saying which was which and without fetching a broom (T013).
3. Garl's salt-stiff coat holds a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door and the hollow seat keeps its twenty-year lean that nobody fills. One of his proverbs goes to the pot and the fire and never to the basket: *leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled* — said to a pot after the crack has been burned away and the corner is a clean dark line (T009, T065).
4. Tarn's broom goes over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector goes down it for the woman the brook took twenty mornings ago, and nobody finds a word for the not-calling; the clerk's slate and the unentered body stay two facts that do not touch (T009, T088, T090).
5. Jorra comes in from the room off the shed with bone-coloured slip and no grey past the bent finger, moves the damper before Sella has finished, tells a pot it will do, and does not say the back room is cold or look toward the bed. The men are not named and the broken birth-slates stay under the drying rack, gone around and entered by nobody (T035).
6. The boy sets the clean dark line, the called-gone ridge, the unentered woman, the weak board in the plank walk at the Weir that is still there and trodden by nobody, and the carried fire's missing breath each on its own place; none of them touches another (T013, T083, T082, T065). He notices that a repair which pretends the break never happened is the district's ordinary kindness, and the dry joke lives in the proverb said to a pot with no crack in it — and costs something, because the proverb has outlived its own truth.
7. The six hearings stay six; no seventh comes. The empty place in the fold is carried through another day unnamed.

**Emotional turn.** A house prepares to sweep what it has already swept. The boy sees that the keeping of a thing gone and the burning of a thing called dealt with are two ways of holding the same absence, and that the house's proverb about showing the crack is now said over a corner where the crack has been erased. The turn is from housework to a moral observation he has no standing to make and no word in Grathic to make it with.

**Sensory anchors.** Broom bristles on trodden clay; thumb pressed flat into a dark line on skirting board; salt stiff in a coat; the scoop of the damper; the iron-cold road to the Fishstairs; the dry smell of burned grey; a proverb said to a pot; fire under blind ash missing one breath in every four.

**Threads advanced.** T013 and T009 (clean dark line, called-gone ridge, coat, seat, uncalled debt and omission), T083 (weak board still there, trodden by nobody), T082 (trays kept apart, no joining), T065 and T088 (kiln's breath; coal entered nowhere), T090 (clerk still set, filed apart), T035 (Jorra's dust and the back room), T071/T072 (exact count carried).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Carries the left-in-place line and the burned ridge toward the later heresy that a house can be left where it is and still be kept (paid in the flood of Chapter 15); carries the uncalled collection toward the first refusal to collect; carries the weak board toward the year's Breaking in Silt, still untrodden and with no accident made of it. Pays nothing, sweeps nothing early.

**Exact last image.** Sella's thumb laid flat in the clean dark line under the skirting board and then withdrawn without a broom, Garl's salt coat holding a broader man's shape on its peg behind her, and the boy's hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the wall and coming down.

## Scene 5 — The Last Market

**Day & time.** Day 59, Ilmen 30, Tenthday, Account-day and the last market before Silt; before light at the Cross and the Fishstairs, into dusk under the Vail roof.
**Place.** The Cross, the Fishstairs, the road up the Clay Yard, the Vail kiln-house.
**Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), Mira Silt, the Fishstairs clerk (glimpsed, silent), market and weir voices.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him down at a half-pace from every open slate and sets the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade; she goes around an open slate as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over, and gives the instruction to the wool (T058, T084).
2. Berr Clay comes to the block with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* twice over the basket, calls the levy for Silt and takes himself out of his own sentence with *there'll be a slate at the watch* (T015, T041, T047).
3. The true subtraction goes over the top of the basket and is spoken by nobody; the number for what the thaw and the flood will cost goes from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrives nowhere. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the boy keeps the heard bell beside the told bell and neither joins the kiln's gap or the Singing Sill's early note (T077, T078, T082).
4. The soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs is glimpsed with his hand going to the slate before his eyes go to the stalls. He does not speak and is filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector and the clerk at the Court's table; no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand comes (T090, T080).
5. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been. She answers a question about the run with a question and her own answer, moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away when the deciding is done (T059, T025).
6. A figure is spoken; her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays. Shifting a basket shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, the knots on neither counted. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it. She brings no crust and does not look into the basket (T059, T025, T024, T084).
7. A barrow of grey goes out toward the Bone Orchard and nobody looks twice; the boy puts the barrow with the ridge, the clean dark line and the charred slate, and joins none of them (T013).
8. The river stirs under its skin of ice; the Singing Sill's low note comes up through the boards and is called the water running fast under the ice. The yard says it is weather and goes home warm without owning the day (T085, T083).
9. At the turn of the wind Sella carries him up the Clay Yard at a half-pace from every open slate, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, and puts the fold right without looking at his face. He is fifty-nine days old, with one crust and the empty place beside it, the count four, four and a gap, and the Ilm beginning to move beneath the ice (T065, T086, T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** The last market behaves exactly like every market before it — figures unchallenged, flesh weighed, a barrow of grey going out unread — while the river begins to move under the ice and no one enters what it might take. The turn is from the district's perfect continuity to the boy's sense of a large failure arriving by degrees, with the same word, *weather*, covering both the barrow and the water. The six hearings stay six; the count buys him nothing but the knowing.

**Sensory anchors.** Pig boards wet at the Cross; green glaze at a mouth; twice-wiped thumb on a clean slate; eel slime, silt and salt at the Fishstairs; eel-bones clicking on a thumb; grey wool cords; the barrow's dry grey dust; the low note felt in the teeth before it is placed in the ear; skin of ice with the dark current showing through.

**Threads advanced.** T015, T041, T047, T077 (printed figure unchallenged, twice-wiped thumb, speaker removed, number withheld), T078 (bell), T090 and T080 (clerk glimpsed, record-first order filed apart), T059, T025 and T024 (two cords, knots uncounted, Nine Pots unvisited, board not touching), T084 (not-lookings kept apart), T013 (barrow toward Bone Orchard), T085 and T083 (river stirring, early note, weak board), T065 and T071/T072 (count four, four and a gap), T061/T082 (six hearings, no seventh), T058 (half-pace, fold put right without a look).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Carries the barrow and the left-in-place grey toward the district's private accounts; carries the still-set clerk and the unentered woman toward a standing charge on a district that has already spent its dead (~640); carries the weak board, the stopping hairlines and the stirring Ilm into the year's flood of Chapters 14–15, where the river will put the Fishstairs back out of line. Pays nothing, joins nothing, makes no accident of the board.

**Exact last image.** Under the Vail roof at dusk, the boy on his bed with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it; behind him the carried fire keeps four, four and a gap, and through the floorboards comes the low note of the river beginning to move under the ice.

## Scene 6 — Silt One

**Day & time.** Day 60, Silt 1, Y.H. 813, Firstday; first light through dusk.
**Place.** The Clay Yard, the drying racks, the yard well and the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
**Present.** Corin (sixty days old — two months old), Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail; Garl kept by his objects.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Mud. The month is named for what the river does. The yard goes about its work with mud on its boots and says the month came early or came as it always comes; nobody gives a figure for the days that have passed (T071/T072, T085).
2. A new hairline of grey has gathered in the corner of the Vail room beside the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned. Sella will not sweep it early and says it is the thaw. The boy sets the new grey and the left-in-place line in two places that do not touch: one called weather, one called a thing left where it is and therefore still kept (T013, T009, T085).
3. Sella gives every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, says the house where another woman would say I, and touches the widow's mark before the opening of the kiln and at no other time — not at a bank, not for a new grey line, not for the carrying of a basket (T058, T079).
4. The carried fire lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the coal the brine-gull took fifteen days ago is entered in no slate and missed in no account (T065, T088).
5. Word comes up that the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the Fishstairs with his slate for a measure nobody called. The weak board in the plank walk at the Weir is still there and trodden by nobody. More hairlines stop short of the edge in the Ilm and the Singing Sill gives its note early; the boy keeps the clerk, the board, the hairlines and the early note in separate places and lets none of them touch the new grey in the corner (T090, T083, T085, T078).
6. Tarn's knife is on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back; his broom goes over the collector's road without turning, and his head does not turn at a vessel's note. Jorra comes in from the shed with slip dried to old bone, moves the damper ahead of the instruction, tells a pot it will do and does not look toward the bed (T036, T076, T035).
7. The door of the room stands a finger's width open. The boy, sixty days old and carried everywhere, lies with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it carried into Silt without a name; his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the new grey and comes down (T086, T060).
8. The six hearings of the full note stay six, five from four sources and one from Dessa, beside the five and never among them, and no seventh comes in the new month. The nearly-full answers stay advanced and not paid. Nothing has been joined, paid, explained or ended; the count goes on (T061/T082, T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** The month named for mud arrives and the house calls its new grey thaw; the boy sees a fresh signature of the same failure he has been keeping in separate places for two months, and still has no hand to point with. The chapter closes on the smallest possible movement — a hand rising an inch and falling — against the largest thing in the room, which is a river about to come up.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud on boots and hem; the dry new grey in the corner; a finger's width of open door; lead-glazed mark under a thumb; blind ash and broken pot; river wind off the racks; the low note under the floorboards; drool frozen at the mouth; one stiff crust and an empty place in the fold.

**Threads advanced.** T013 and T009 (new grey beside the clean dark line, not swept), T085 and T083 (mud, hairlines, board, stirring river), T090 (clerk still set), T058, T079 and T065 (instruction to fire and wool, widow's mark before opening only, kiln's breath), T088 (coal gone with the gull), T086 and T060 (one crust, empty place, unrefusable kindness), T036 and T076 (seventh year held, two silences), T035 (Jorra's slip and damper), T061/T082 and T071/T072 (six hearings stay six; exact count crosses into Silt unspent).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Plants the new hairline of grey in the Vail corner beside the left-in-place line, to be carried into the year's flood and the later day a house is left where it is and still kept; carries the still-set clerk, the uncalled measure and the unentered woman toward a standing charge on the district; carries the weak board, the stopping hairlines, the early note and the stirring Ilm into the breaking of Chapters 14–15. Pays nothing, joins nothing, sweeps nothing.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed, sixty days old, with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it; in the corner a new hairline of grey lies beside the old clean dark line, unswept because the house says it is the thaw; the door stands a finger's width open, and his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the new grey and comes down. The count goes on.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 14

The chapter ends with a hairline of new grey the house calls thaw and will not sweep, a board still untrodden by nobody, a clerk still set for a measure nobody called, one crust and an empty place carried into Silt without a name, and the river beginning to move under the ice — nothing joined, paid, explained or ended. Chapter 14 opens on Day 61, Silt 2, Secondday, at the drying racks: the brook runs higher behind them and the clay comes down heavier than it did in Ilmen, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the sound of water going over water and comes down. The new grey in the corner, the still-un trodden board, the stopping hairlines, the early low note, the still-set clerk and the exact count cross into the flood with him, still on separate trays; the sixth hearing does not come, the sixth note waits for Day 64, and the Great Breaking of Arc 3 stays years away. Forward, not closed.
