\daymark{60}{Silt 1, Y.H. 813}

The month came in on mud. It had a name for what the river left and none for what the river was about to take, and the yard went about its work with clay on its boots and on the hems of its women, saying the month had come early and saying it had come as it always came, which are the same sentence said by two men who do not want the same thing. \person{Corin} came into \place{the Clay Yard} in the basket under the fold, carried level as a pot filled is carried, with the wool at his ear and the cold already at his cheek. He was sixty days old. Two months. Nobody in the house gave the figure for the days that had gone before the name changed, and he kept it exactly, as he had once counted the injured in a field before he put a hand into it. The count went on and could not be spent on a crust.

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket just inside the door of \place{the Vail kiln-house}, at the edge of the light and a finger's width from the wall, and gave the instruction to the wool. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” She did not say it to the basket. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing and opened again as she settled the fold, and she said the house where another woman would have said I. There was mud on the threshold that had not been there yesterday and she stepped over it without looking at it, the way a woman steps over a place where something has been set down.

In the corner beside the clean dark line where \lore{the Ash} ridge had turned its corner for a year and two days and been burned and called dealt with, a new hairline of grey had gathered in the night. It was finer than the old line had ever been, a dry dust laid along the skirting with no width to it, and it had the look of something written by a hand that did not mean to write. Sella stood over it with a broom in her hand and did not sweep. “It's the thaw,” she said to the fire, as though the fire had put it there. “It'll go when it goes.” She set the broom against the wall and went to the pot.

A rack-woman came past with her skirts gathered and looked at the corner. “That'll want a broom before the month's out,” she said.

“It'll go when it goes,” Sella said to the pot. She did not turn around.

“It came early, this one.”

“It came when it came.” Sella put a thumb in the clean dark line and took it out again, and went on feeding the fire. The woman went off toward the racks with the argument still in her mouth and the grey lying where it lay.

The boy heard the two sentences stand side by side the way a heap of turnips stands beside a figure set out at a market: both said the month had come early, and neither gave a date. He put the saying on its own place and left the question of which was true where it was. Two months old, and the only figure in the room that was exact was the one he could not speak.

The boy lay where the basket held him and set the new grey on a place of its own. The clean dark line lay on another place, a thing left where it was and therefore still kept; the new hairline lay beside it and did not touch it. One was weather in the house's account. The other was nothing the house had said at all. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the difference and he did not make one, because he had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. The two places stayed two places.

The body went on with its small humiliations while he kept the count. A thread of drool froze at his mouth in the first cold of the month and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left; his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far; the cold came in its known order, cheek, then temple, then wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. Nothing within two paces could be reached.

Before the kiln was opened she put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat for one breath, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and let go. It was an opening and nothing else was. She did not touch the mark for the new grey, or for the carrying of a basket, or for any bank, and when a neighbour came in with a slate under her arm she went around it at the half-pace she kept from every open slate and gave the instruction to the fire. Behind her \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg and the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean that nobody filled. \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap; the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees. The coal the brine-gull took on Day 45 was fifteen days gone now and entered in no slate and missed in no account.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the stone lip was wet and the mud had got into the rope. Sella set the basket in the shade under the little roof, out of the draught, and gave the instruction to the bucket. “Easy,” she said. “There's a skin on it yet.” She did not say it to the basket. The little-door burn shut on the rope and opened on nothing as the bucket came up, and the water inside it carried a skin that the rope disturbed and left whole again. A man waiting his turn said the month had come early; a woman at the tubs said it came as it always came; neither gave the number of days that had gone before the name changed, and the argument went down with the bucket and came up with the next one. The boy lay in the well's thin shade and kept the number exactly, and the cold came at his cheek and then his temple and then his wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. He wanted the bucket more than he wanted the argument and could not reach either.

Word came up the river road with a man who had been to \place{the Weir} and did not stop in the yard: the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at \place{the Fishstairs} with his slate for a measure nobody had called, and the weak board in \place{the plank walk} was still there and trodden by nobody. The man said it the way another man says that a step is missing, and went on toward his dinner. The boy set the clerk's order of reaching on a place of its own, apart from the woman in the grey coat at the crash, apart from the grey-fingered man who gave at the Drawing and apart from the soft cuff behind the Court's table; the board went on a second place, beside the missing Fishstairs step and the hairline that stopped short of the edge, and the new grey in the Vail corner went on a third, and none of the three touched another. The clerk sat for a debt the district had not entered. The board waited for a weight nobody would put on it. The hairline stopped where the ice thinned. The yard had one word for all of it and the boy had no word at all.

Beyond \place{the drying racks} the \place{the Ilm} ran under a skin so thin that the dark current showed through it in veins, and more hairlines had run into the ice in the night and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge. \place{the Singing Sill} gave its low note early, felt in the teeth before it was placed in the ear, and a woman at the tubs said the water was running fast under the ice and then said weather, and went home warm on it. \place{the Slip Brook} ran clay-heavy behind the racks a little higher than it should. He kept the hairlines, the early note and the higher brook on places that did not touch the clerk's place or the board's place, and did not let the new grey in the corner come among them. The note came up through the boards beneath his basket and was the same low thing the boards had kept all through the year; the house called it weather and he called it nothing, because to call it anything would have been to write a cause across four separate places.

\person{Tarn} came up the yard with the small bench knife back on its sill and told the yard where it was, though the yard had not asked. Six years were counted and the seventh held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held the light and gave it back. He agreed with the man who wanted the thaw early and the woman who wanted it late and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. His broom went over the road to a collector over and over without once turning along it, and no collector came down it for the woman the brook took twenty-two mornings ago. A vessel gave its note past the yard door and his head did not turn, and the boy did not call it a seventh hearing. The two ears under one roof kept their equality, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled.

\person{Jorra Vail} came in from the room off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger. He told a pot it would do, told the wheel it did not care whose hands, only if they were steady, and went to the damper before Sella had finished telling the fire it was low, so that the end of her sentence went to the pot. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. The boy had kept the dust of some mornings and the clean slip of others on separate places since the first days and had never asked which slate was broken; he kept the clean forearms on the dust's place as a fact and nothing more.

\scenebreak

At dusk Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up the Clay Yard at a half-pace from every open slate. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. The door of the room stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the low note of the river beginning to move under the ice, and through the floor came the carried fire's four, four and a gap, and the two sounds kept their separate places as the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, the heard bell at \place{the Cross}, the early note from the Singing Sill and the gap under his own roof had kept theirs for two months. He had no Grathic word for the flat answer, none for nearly, none for a thing a house could leave lying where it lay and go home warm.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of \person{Dessa Silt}, filed beside the five and never among them; no seventh came in the new month. The two nearly-full answers stayed advanced and not paid, third sounds on a tray of their own. The word forty stayed a saying on its tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and it had not been spoken again. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended in the turning of the month; the count went on.

One crust lay in the fold where two had lain, brown-edged and stiff past smelling, and the empty place beside it was carried into Silt without a name. He was sixty days old. Two months old. He had wanted the breast in the first days and wanted the crust now and could not reach either, and the warmth he most wanted was the empty place where a hand had been. He was the only adult in every room he had ever lain in and the least use in any of them.

In the corner a new hairline of grey lay beside the old clean dark line, unswept because the house said it was the thaw. The door stood a finger's width open. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the new grey and came down. The count went on.
