## Chronicle

On Ilmen 26 a man come down from Greybank told the yard how a counting room was shut in the last war, the damper closed, and the men inside died of the breath when their heat went out with no way to feed it. The yard took it as a curiosity with a date. Sella Vail, who had lost a husband to it, heard and told the fire it was the thaw; Tarn agreed with early and late and wanted the late, which puts off paying. Only the boy, fifty-five days old in the well's shade, knew it for true, with no word to write and no hand to lift.

The next morning she carried him three doors down to House Silt and set him inside a door a finger's width open. He saw the pewter tin above the empty bed, pale fold, thumb's bright rub on the lid, birth-cloth kept three years. Alda Reed came short with soap-and-milk cold under river silt, laid her birth-slates writing-down before turning to the fold, pressed it too long, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower's red edge unknowing and covered the sign with a date. She said warm and breathing, exact and not true, touched the tin in her bodice without opening it, wrote no sixth note and left without the woman's name.

Through the days that followed the house kept its numbers badly in loaves and glances. Sella moved a loaf and came back to the same place; a space at the row's end went uncalled, while he counted three days to Silt and could spend none. The yard prepared a sweeping already done --- grey burned on Ilmen 1, called dealt with --- and Sella laid one thumb in Vail's clean dark line, then in the death-house ridge a finger's width wider, and fetched no broom. Jorra's bone-coloured slip went to the damper ahead of her instruction; Tarn swept the collector's road without turning, and no collector came for the woman in the brook.

At the last market, Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read one in twelve twice over the basket and took himself out of his own sentence; Mira Silt answered a question with her own answer, put her thumb to a grey cord with a second twisted twice beneath it, knots uncounted, and brought no crust. A barrow of grey went out unread, and the river began to move under the Ilm's skin and was called weather. Silt came in on mud. A new hairline of grey gathered in the Vail corner beside the old clean line; Sella stood over it with a broom and would not sweep. At dusk he lay sixty days old, one crust in the fold, the empty place carried into the month without a name. The clerk was still set at the Fishstairs, the board trodden by nobody, and the fire behind him kept four, four and a gap. His hand went up an inch toward the new grey and came down.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Day 60, Silt 1, at dusk, on his bed under the Vail kiln-house roof, not the Debt Loft. The room's door stands a finger's width open; through the gap comes the low note of the river beginning to move under the ice, and through the floor comes the carried fire's four, four and a gap, kept in separate places. One crust lies in the fold, brown-edged and stiff past smelling, with the empty place beside it carried into Silt without a name. In the corner a new hairline of grey lies beside the old clean dark line, unswept because the house says it is the thaw. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the new grey and comes down. The count goes on. Sella has set him there and gone; no one else is on stage at the close.
- **Day and time at the end.** Day 60, Silt 1, Y.H. 813, Firstday, dusk. Day 55 was Ilmen 26, Sixthday; Day 59 was Ilmen 30, Tenthday and Account-day, the last market before Silt. Six dated entries, six dated headings, six days lived in full. Day 61 is Silt 2, Secondday and belongs to chapter 14.
- **Where everyone is now, and what they want.** Corin/Dan: on the bed under the roof, sixty days old, two months, awake, silent, drool frozen and unwiped, wanting the crust he cannot reach and the number nobody will speak. Sella Vail: at the fire and the bed, having carried him home at a half-pace from every open slate and put the fold right without looking at his face; she has set her broom against the wall rather than sweep the new grey, and wants the house and fire kept with nothing explained. Tarn: in the yard with his broom, the small knife back on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held, Ash in his knuckles, the clip-ring at his wrist; his broom keeps going over the road to the Fishstairs without turning, and he still wants the late thaw because it puts off the paying. Jorra Vail: in and out of the room off the wheel-shed with bone-coloured slip, moving the damper ahead of instruction, never looking toward the bed; *he's not Vail* stays sealed. Garl Vail: absent, kept by the salt-stiff coat, the hollow seat, the clean dark line, his proverbs and the uncalled tile debt. Alda Reed (on stage Day 56): went out of House Silt with her tin shut inside her bodice and no sixth note written. Mira Silt and Berr Clay (on stage Day 59): Mira at the Fishstairs with both cords unread and no crust given; Berr at the Cross, red cord folded under his coat, figure called and speaker removed. The Greybank man went back down the road he came; the Fishstairs clerk is still set, silent; Orra and Marra stay offstage; Dessa stays dead, twenty-two mornings uncollected.
- **Gained, lost, used; money; powers.** Nothing gained he can use. No rank, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy, no seventh hearing. No coin, no slate, no figure he can spend. The one crust remains uneaten and unnamed; the smoky crust taken on Day 46 is still gone with no taker named. The coal the brine-gull took on Day 45 is fifteen days gone, entered in no slate and missed in no account. The pewter tin above the bed was seen again, neither opened; Alda's tin was touched again inside her bodice, not opened; no sixth note was written (it waits for Day 64). The tin's pale fold and thumb-rub are filed as evidence of a hand without a known owner.
- **Facts revealed, lies told, debts.** A stranger described, as history eighteen leagues east, a counting room shut in a war, a damper closed and men dead of the breath with no way to feed their heat; Sella, who had lost a husband to that danger, heard it and did not connect it to her own roof. The house kept its own low word for the kiln's breath. House Silt holds two shut records in two materials --- the pewter tin above the bed keeping birth-cloth three years, and the tin in Alda's bodice. Alda again said *warm and breathing*, exact and not true, covering the sign at the flower's red edge with a date and going on with dates after the question had stopped. Berr said the levy was called for Silt and not by him, and that a number goes into a slate, not into a well. The new grey in the Vail corner was called thaw and would not be swept. No promise was made and no debt paid; Garl's tile debt and the uncalled measure for the dead woman remain unentered.

- **Chapter 14 must not forget.** Day 61 opens at the drying racks with the brook running higher and the clay coming down heavier, the boy's hand going up an inch toward the sound of water going over water and coming down. Carry forward, still on separate trays: the new grey unswept, the weak board untrodden, the stopping hairlines, the early low note, the still-set clerk, the one crust and empty place, the exact count, six hearings and no seventh, two nearly-full answers advanced and not paid. The sixth hearing does not come; the sixth note waits for Day 64; the Great Breaking of Arc 3 stays years away. Forward, not closed.
