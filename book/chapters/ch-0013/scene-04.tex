\daymark{58}{Ilmen 29, Y.H. 813}

The yard went around the corners with a broom before anybody had taken one up. It was the last day but two of Ilmen and Silt stood at the door, and a house that carried grey into the mud was a house a man with a slate would mark, so the sweeping was spoken of at the tubs, at the rack ends and over the well-roof in the future tense, as a thing not yet done: this corner lifted, that one burned, the whole called dealt with before the month turned. \person{Corin} lay in the basket at the end of \place{the drying racks} with the wool at his ear and heard the house prepare a sweeping that the house had already performed. Under the words, under the dry mineral smell that came up whenever a rake moved, lay another smell, older by twenty-eight days: grey going up before light on Ilmen 1, the pail of water, the neighbour who said it was dealt with and went home. He kept the prepared sweeping on one tray and the performed one on another. The two did not touch. There was no word in the yard for the difference, and there was no hand in the yard that would have pointed it out if there had been.

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket where the racks threw their shade and where no slate lay open, and gave the instruction to the wool. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” She did not look into the basket. Then she went to the kiln-house wall and laid one thumb flat in the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner for a year and two days --- the line burned away on Ilmen 1 before light and called dealt with by somebody else, leaving the skirting board darker than the wall around it, a seam without a seam's work in it. She held the thumb there a breath, the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing, and took it away. She walked two doors down the yard and laid the same thumb in the ridge there, the death-house ridge, a finger's width wider than Vail's had been, that corner turning without a break too, that grey also burned and also called dealt with. She brought the knowledge back up the yard without saying which was which and without fetching a broom for either. From the low angle of the basket the boy watched the thumb go into the line and come out, go into the ridge and come out, and understood that she was measuring and not sweeping, keeping and not removing. A woman who left a line where it lay was a woman who still had the line; a woman who burned a ridge and called it gone had let the ridge go into the world and kept only the word for it. He set the two ways of holding a thing on separate places and wrote no cause across either.

\place{the Vail kiln-house} kept \person{Garl Vail} the way a room is kept by what has not been moved out of it. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the salt white at the shoulders where a man had leaned into a firing, and the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year hollow on the left that nobody filled. His proverb went to a pot and to the fire and never to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. It went now to a pot with no crack in it, a pot turned whole on the wheel, and the boy lay under the fold and listened to a saying about honest repairs being said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The joke had cost something when the man was alive and had cost more now that he was not, because the proverb had outlived the thing it was true about. In this house the crack that mattered had not been shown at all. It lay under a clean dark line at the skirting, and in a hollow seat, and in a woman the brook had taken twenty mornings ago, and in the space in the fold where a crust had been. He had no standing to say so and no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry it, and a surgeon's eye for the thing a man refuses to look at was the least useful object in that room.

Two ways of keeping the same absence stood in the yard and neither would own the other. A line left where it lay was a thing held in the hand; a line burned and called dealt with was a thing handed to the world and then denied. The district called the second kindness, because it let a person go home warm without owning the day, and it had a word for that and no word for the first, which was a woman standing over a corner with a broom she did not lift. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses laid in a row will do: they arrange themselves into a cause if a man lets them, and the cause is wrong in the way a clean chart is wrong when the nurse's note has been turned down. Here was a repair that pretended the break had never happened, and it was not one repair but the whole district's ordinary kindness, from the line under the skirting to the road swept without a turning to a woman twenty mornings in a ground nobody had entered her into. He set the likenesses on their tray and would not let them become a cause, because there was no slate in the house that would bear what he would have written and no hand in the house to write it.

\person{Tarn} had his broom on the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, and he took it over the same stretch of trodden clay again, and again, without once turning his head along it. The road went down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, iron-cold in the morning even under its skin of mud, the road a house sends a man down when it wants a measure taken from somebody else. Twenty mornings had gone since the clay-heavy water behind the racks took \person{Dessa Silt} and the yard lifted her out with their hands and no words; no collector had come down that road for her and Tarn had not gone to fetch one, and nobody in the district had found a word for the not-calling. The clerk's slate stood at the Fishstairs for a measure nobody had called, and a woman lay twenty mornings uncollected, and the boy set the two facts on two places and let them stay there. Tarn's knife was back on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back, \lore{the Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the line in the corner that no scrubbing lifted, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. To the neighbour who wanted the thaw early he said, “Yes, house,” and to the one who wanted it late, “Yes, house,” and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. His broom went over the collector's road once more and did not turn. The omission had no name in the district's tongue and he was not the man to give it one.

In the afternoon \person{Jorra Vail} came in from the room off the shed with bone-coloured slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone and told it what it had been told a hundred times, that it did not care whose hands were on it, only if they were steady. To a pot turning under his palm he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. His bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it, and the end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him, so she shut her mouth on it and gave it to the clay. The scoop and scrape of iron came through the room and the fire took the change without being told twice. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. The men who came into that room were not named under this roof, and the broken birth-slates lay under the drying rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed where it had been set on the hour it was spoken, sealed as tightly as a lid. He went back to the wheel. The yard straightened around his silence without an order.

The boy lay where they had set him and kept his places. The clean dark line where the Vail ridge had turned went on one place, and the ridge two doors down that had been burned and called gone went on another, and the woman twenty mornings in a ground nobody had entered her into went on a third. The weak board in \place{the plank walk} at \place{the Weir} was still there and trodden by nobody, and it went on a fourth; the carried fire under blind ash and the back of a broken pot warmed the boards by degrees and missed one breath in every four, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went on a fifth. None of them touched another. Six hearings of the full note stayed six, five from four sources and one out of a person who had been warm that morning three doors down, beside them and never among them; the two nearly-full answers stayed advanced and not paid; the told bell stayed beside the heard bell with the early note beside both; the word stayed a saying on its own tray; the three intervals and the five unjoined pauses stayed where they had been laid. To keep the places apart was the only work in the house that needed no hands, and he did it with the exactness of a man who had once stood over a field triage and sorted twenty bodies into the ones who would live, the ones who would live only if he chose them, and the ones who would die whatever he did. He had the field and the count still. He had no hands for either.

The body went on with its small humiliations while the surgeon kept the count. A thread of drool froze at his mouth in the yard cold and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left; his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far; the cold arrived in its known order, cheek, then temple, then wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. One crust lay in the fold where two had lain, brown-edged and stiff past smelling, and the empty place beside it was carried through another day without a name. He wanted the crust and could not reach it. He wanted somebody in the house to say the number of days to Silt and nobody said it; the days went around in loaves and glances and whatever the moot would give out, never in a figure. Everything that went past him went past unspendable: the prepared sweeping, the measured ridge, the proverb said to a pot with no crack, the road swept without a turn along it, the two ridges held one after the other under one thumb. Knowing any of it exactly had never once given him a hand to lift toward it. He was fifty-eight days old, carried everywhere, the only adult in every room and the least use in any of them.

At the turn of the wind Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at a half-pace from every open slate. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. Behind her the coat held a broader man's shape on its peg, the hollow seat its twenty-year lean, and the clean dark line lay under the skirting board where the ridge had turned and been burned and called dealt with, unswept because a line left where it is is still a line kept. The yard would go around the corners with a broom in the morning before the month turned, and the grey would be burned before light and called dealt with again. She withdrew her thumb from the line and took up no broom. The carried fire kept four, four and a gap. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the wall and came down.
