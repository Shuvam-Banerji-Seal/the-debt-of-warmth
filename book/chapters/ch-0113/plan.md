## The state carried in

The hand outside the wool had come up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, taken a small intake, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended and laid itself flat against the fold on answer **354** — exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. ## Scene 1 — The Dish Going Up the Yard

- **Day and time.** Day 390, Kindling, Y.H. 813. Small hours opening the first of the Five, then first grey and morning. First scene of the chapter: the chapter's only `\dropcap` and its first `\daymark{390}{Kindling, Y.H. 813}`. Ends in the small hours with answer **355**.

- **Present.** Corin/Dan; Sella Vail. Garl Vail in objects only: the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg behind the door, the hollow wheel-seat, the clean dark line at the Vail corner with its finer grey called the thaw thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash, the twice-mended pot's hairline, the unforged collector sentence at the kindling, the uncalled tile debt. Walk-ons only: the unnamed households who pass the year's fire up the Clay Yard in a clay dish from hearth to hearth before first light. No Berr, Hessa, Ilva, Tarn, Jorra, collector, officer, market, Cross or bell. No ownerless voice over the boards.
- **Beats in order.**
  1. Open on the hand laid flat against the fold on answer 354 and the count exact at 389, carried out of Chapter 112 without recap. The Five stand here; Kindling is not called an opening, a payment or a boundary, and nothing from Stone 28, 29 or 30 crosses as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.
  2. The surgeon takes the body in its old order: leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it holding the fold put there on the fifteenth, found where put and not made again; thread of drool frozen in the dry kiln heat and unwiped because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint where the wool rides back as at the end of a line written out for it; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody here will perform. He knows a basket set level out of the draught where the heat comes sideways is the rewarming he would choose and cannot ask for, that a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed.
  3. The infant's want of the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle quarrels with the clinical inventory: both true, the second louder, neither winning, no verdict looked for. That quarrel is the work.
  4. Sella comes to the hearth before the basket, tells the coals it is the thaw, turns them with the back of a broken pot, says *the house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, counts a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither gap; her finger lies along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stays unread; her lips move along a two-gapped row unfigured; one glance goes to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze with the sweetish edge coming and going on a wrong wind, her hand shut half over nothing and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air.
  5. The twice-mended pot goes up thumb-first where the wire crosses the crack, the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; both proverbs go to the pot and the fire and never to a person; repair stays apart from restoration. The dressed grain is turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use, changed in state and lifting no figure and no handful. The pot rim is set two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed; his hand goes toward it and stops where the half-beat stopped it.
  6. She carries him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, instruction to the wool, the river wind and the fire she banks against their coming back and never to the basket; goes around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The three coverings stay weather entered nowhere with a finger between every pair; the field keeps its empty place; the river keeps water over water under its winter skin, found where put and not spent on the holy day.
  7. Before first light the year's fire comes up the Clay Yard in a clay dish from hearth to hearth. Each house says it kept the flame; nobody says where it was first lit or what it cost. The coal enters under the back of a broken pot with no hand under this roof making an opening of it. Under the floor the carried fire gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and ninety days; nobody lifts the floor and nobody supplies the pull.
  8. The widow's mark is not touched: a holy day is not an opening and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule with no eighth made due. The keeping is found where put and stays a finding; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way a third, and both want a payer where none stands. Twelve arithmetics stand behind one unsigned figure with no signature and no doctrine out of twelve; the Kindling collector's coming is felt only in the order of the morning and is not brought into an hour that has not earned him.
  9. In the small hours the towel comes out as instruments come out when a case is done. Every gap is left a gap. Answer **355** is filed exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment.
- **Emotional turn.** From the hung-over silence of answer 354 and a count nobody will name, through the double consciousness at its morning inventory, to the small clean satisfaction of a field taken as found and left as found — which costs nothing, and is therefore not peace. ## Scene 2 — The Measure Is Banked

- **Day and time.** Day 390, Kindling. Morning through the going of the light and the small hours closing the day. Ends with answer **356**.
- **Place.** The room two doors down the Clay Yard where the measure is taken, seen from the edge of the light and not entered; then the nineteen steps up to the Vail kiln-house, the hearth, the pot board and the bed under the roof.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, the collector who takes the year's first measure (walk-on, no sheet, no interior), the neighbour whose measure is taken (walk-on, no interior, not named); Garl Vail in objects. No Berr, Hessa, Ilva, Tarn, Jorra, officer, market, Cross or bell. No ownerless voice.
- **Beats in order.**
  1. Sella carries the basket to the room two doors down and sets him at the edge of the light where he can see and not join, standing half a pace off and giving no instruction to the basket. The room is not entered by the narration; it stays a sound and a shape from a basket, as the Drawing house did on Stone 12.
  2. The collector comes in out of the cold with a flat leather satchel, a cuff gone soft from washing and folded back twice with the grey of the wash worked into the seam at the wrist, a cold iron tube out of a roll of wool grey at the light and darker where his fingers have it, and fingertips grey at the tips the colour of skimmed milk gone off.
  3. His hand goes to the slate before his eyes go to the bed. He says once, low, to the slate: *The measure is banked. The account stands. The return is recorded.* The hand that writes is ordinary with no pause in it — unlike the neat dark hand, whose small stop has been seen and filed and never joined.
  4. The boy takes the order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart and files the ordinary hand on a tray of its own with a finger between it and every other: the neat dark hand, Hessa Rale's eighth, Ilva's ninth, Berr Clay's twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate, the Drawing collector's ordinary hand. This man is not Marra, not the Court-table clerk, not the Fishstairs clerk and not the Drawing collector. No tenth hand stands up in the empty place.
  5. The neighbour under the tube is not named and gives no interior: a small sound in the room, then the pale round place on the arm afterward, not a wound and no blood. The boy does not make a seventh hearing of the sound and does not use it to fill the empty place where the seventh should stand; six hearings stay beside and never among with the Drawing's flat knock outside them.
  6. The reason for the taking is planted and not spoken: the man believes he keeps the world alive by taking what is already going, and that belief, not anger and not triumph, is what will one day be remembered against him. The boy does not read the reason into the room and does not join it to the twelve readings or the four directions.
  7. At the going of the light Sella carries him home up the nineteen steps, sets him on his bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and adds no second fold; she says nothing of the room, the tube, the slate, the voice or the woman. The thread stays frozen and unwiped.
  8. The hearth grammar is renewed; the twice-mended pot is lifted thumb-first with the hairline showing and both proverbs to pot and fire; the dressed grain is turned with no figure and no handful; the widow's mark is untouched because a collection and a going of the light are not openings. The keeping is found where put and stays a finding; the carried fire keeps four low pulls and a missing fifth with its payer unnamed three hundred and ninety-one days.
  9. Small hours: the file comes out on the towel with every gap left gap; answer **356** is filed, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.
- **Emotional turn.** From the order of a stranger's reaching to the plain flat sentence, to the boy's refusal to let a fourth ordinary hand stand up and call itself a tenth or a rule. The collector goes away believing he has kept the world alive; that belief is the part of him that will be remembered.
- **Sensory anchors.** Soft washed cuff with grey worked into the wrist seam; flat leather satchel; cold iron tube grey at the light and darker in the fingers; fingertips the colour of skimmed milk gone off; the small sound in the room; the pale round place, not a wound and no blood; half-pace carried up nineteen steps; wool righted without a look at the face.
- **Threads advanced, none paid.** T080/T090 (a record-first hand with no pause filed on its own tray, not a tenth, not Marra, not the Drawing collector; the taker's reason planted, not spoken); T081 (the room from the basket kept on its own place, no fifth likeness); T061/T082 (six hearings, seventh place empty, Drawing's knock outside, no new hearing from the room); T071/T072 (answer 356 closes Day 390 exact, six months and two hundred and ten days); T060/T086 and T097/T060/T086 (body inventory, keeping found where put); T058/T079/T100 (hearth grammar, charcoal unread, grain unfigured, widow's mark untouched); T077 (grain covered, no handful); T009/T013/T092; T083/T085; T015/T041/T047 carried; T088 (fire's fifth unpaid); T012 and T093/T094/T096 carried.
- **Foreshadowing planted.** The collector's belief gathers toward the far-off day it is remembered against him; the ordinary hand on its own tray gathers toward the far-off hand that will lay the district's private keepings together, and is not that hand yet. **Paid off: nothing.**

## Scene 4 — A Term That Does Not End

- **Day and time.** Day 391, Weighing. Afternoon through the going of the light and the small hours closing the day. Ends with answer **358**.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard — the returning-line place, the Vail corner, the lowest rack, the rack end, the yard well, the trough, the flat stone by the well, the worked strips, the kindling and the flagstones — then the Vail wheel-shed edge and damper, the hearth and pot board, the bed under the roof.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Tarn at the kindling, Jorra Vail at the wheel-shed edge and damper; Garl Vail in objects. Walk-ons only: a Cold Acre hare-carrier, a cracked-jar man in a rope sling, a woman with a basket of kindling, two fence-menders arguing the thaw. No Berr, Hessa, Ilva, collector, officer, market, Cross or bell; no ownerless voice.
- **Beats in order.**
  1. The afternoon comes down thin through the finger's-width door and goes without a boundary drawn; the yard goes around its seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The three coverings and the river's skin stay weather entered nowhere with a finger between every pair; the field keeps its empty place.
  2. Costs arrive as directions and go away as weather: hare to the weather which carried the snow and can carry the price, cracked jar to a pot that says nothing, *Yes, house* to the woman with kindling and to the fence-menders wanting the thaw early and late, keeping the late because it puts off the paying and agreeing so thoroughly that no argument finds a foot (T077).
  3. At the kindling: six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt; the small knife at the very edge of its sill; the hand passes over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach, the coming away the whole of the checking; *Six counted. The next I don't count*; nothing cut, counted or spent.
  4. The Ash 14 held breath and the Bone 26 thumb's resting are found where put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair, not a sixth held object and not a tenth stopping. He brings the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stops as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year now at its very end; the Seed 16 breath is unspent, the loan uncalled and there is no look along the road. A term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom; the fear of being entered again for vagrancy stays his without a figure. Chest tight since the dark, Ash worked into the knuckle creases the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at the left wrist giving back the little light, the stroke over the shallow bright patch of the flagstones without the half-check.
  5. Jorra comes through the finger's-width door with meal-fine grey slate dust white in a fist-crease, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; three or more men unnamed went low into the back room before first grey and get no name from him and no word that the room was cold; dusty forearms parted from clean as clot from vessel; wheel and pot spoken to before any person; the full note known three turns ahead and withheld from any High Kiln master who would call it early; bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, taking the cold out of the fingertip, moving the tongue a hair's breadth, and out without a second holding of Ash 26.
  6. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise under the wheel-shed is neither given nor withdrawn while the burned slate stands at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and the well-saying at four shapings. The boy keeps Jorra's knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them.
  7. At the going of the light the household grammar is renewed — fire before basket, coals told the thaw, *the house will keep it*, charcoal unread, lips on a two-gapped row, the clay jar glanced at once, twice-mended pot thumb-first with both proverbs and the hairline showing, dressed grain turned with no figure, pot rim two inches beyond the reach — and the widow's mark is untouched because a going of the light and a bed are not openings and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule.
  8. Small hours: the silence after the clean voice's name, Tarn's stopped ear-sentence and Jorra's ungiven promise are checked on three separate places with a finger between every pair; the boy does not let three likenesses stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Answer **358** is filed.
