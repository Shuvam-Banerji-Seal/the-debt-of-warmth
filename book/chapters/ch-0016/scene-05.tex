\daymark{77}{Silt 18, Y.H. 813}

On the morning of Silt 18 the mud had set into a brown skin over the ruts, holding the shape of the water the way a cracked bowl keeps the outline of a boil after it has boiled dry --- not level, not settled, but faithful to a thing that is gone. \person{Corin}, seventy-seven days old, was carried out into \place{the Clay Yard} level in the basket as a pot filled that must not be slopped, with \person{Sella Vail}'s knee turned against the wicker to keep the draught off his ears. She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Around,” she said to the broom. “It's an old house. It'll stand another winter.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, kept her half-pace from every place a slate might lie, and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a yard is not an opening and a basket is not a door.

Three days had gone into the mud and none of them had come back with a figure on them. The house kept its count in loaves moved along a shelf, in a glance at the clay jar on the wall, in a space at the row's end that nobody called a number; the yard had turned a flood into weather and the weather into the thaw that was, and the thaw that was into nothing anybody owed. He kept the three days as three slates set edge to edge, seventy-five and seventy-six and seventy-seven, with no writing on any of them and no hand to put any of them down. The surgeon who had once read a chart with the note turned down knew the difference between a thing entered and a thing left lying, and the infant at his ear was hungry, and the hunger was the louder of the two.

The sweeping went around what the water had left. It went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned for a year and two days, and around the new hairline of grey beside it, finer than the old, which the house called the thaw and left to go when it went; it went around the space under the lowest of \place{the drying racks} where whole slate should have stood, wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The broken birth-slates lay under that space and were gone around with it, entered into nothing. A yard is not an opening. A rack is not a door. The boy had learned the grammar of a ward walking around a bed for a week, and he set the two lines, the space and the slates beneath it in four places that did not touch, because a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not a hand puts one there.

Then the door off \place{Vail wheel-shed} stood a finger's width open, and \person{Jorra Vail} came through it. The room off the shed kept that finger's width of open all morning, and he did not see into it. From the height of a basket a finger's width is a slit of floor the colour of wet ash and a shadow that does not move; that was all the room gave, and that was all he kept. A house does not enter such a room in any roll it shows, and a basket is not set down in it. He kept the slit of floor on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it, because he had learned that a cause written across a room is the quickest way of never seeing the room again.

The dust had come back. It was worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, fine and grey, ground small enough to sit in the creases and stay there; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel, which dried to the colour of old bone and cracked when it was rubbed. Two men had come in since first light and spoken low in \place{the back room}, and he gave the house no name for them, and the house asked for none. The boy held the dusty forearms and the clean ones of the day before on one tray as two states of one week, and did not ask which slate had been broken, or whether it was a birth-slate. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer; he kept the two forearms as two and let them lie. Dust in the creases of a thrower's arms is a record no officer would think to take up, and a record that lasts only as long as the arms carry it, which is the kind the district keeps best.

Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel. “It cares if they're steady.” He set a pot up and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and the wheel took the weight of his hands the way a gate takes a hinge. He knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel --- knew it in the wrist and not in the ear, the way a man knows his own weight in the dark --- and would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it. The boy kept the knowing beside the bent finger and the dust, where questions can last for years. He had no \lore{Grathic} for the note and no standing to ask, and the not-asking did not make the knowing less exact. It only made it worth nothing to anybody, least of all to him.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it, so that the end of her sentence arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. She shut her mouth on the rest and gave it to the fire. “That'll do,” she said to the pot, and the fire took the instruction and went on doing what it had been doing. She did not say the back room was cold. She did not ask about the dust. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the empty air and opened again, and she turned the basket with her knee a hand's breadth out of the draught without looking down into it.

He did not look toward the bed or into the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. It lay where it lay, which in this house is a way of keeping a thing: not spoken, not entered, not carried to any door. The boy read the shape of it --- a keeping done by leaving something out --- and set it on the place where he kept Mira's not-looking and Alda's date laid over the sign and Sella's instruction to the fire, and did not make a cause of the four. Two ears under one roof heard what they heard, and two silences of the same shape kept their equality without a word between them. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. The sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same.

\scenebreak

Before the light had settled which way the day would go, \person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill and told the empty yard where it was, the way he had told it every morning of six years. “It's on the sill. You can check.” Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, and the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Something must give at the end of it. The end was not written here, and he went on holding it as a man holds a breath he has decided not to spend yet, though his chest is already tight. He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to what light there was, and the ring held it and gave it back; the clip had sat there since Y.H. 806, when he was sixteen and somebody else's account was entered against him, and the pale place was the shape of an iron thing that had been lifted off. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line in the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it, and he did not scrub at it this morning.

“Yes, house,” he said to the man who wanted the thaw early. “Yes, house,” he said to the woman who wanted it late. He agreed with both so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. The paying had come up the road and stood in the hollows of the bank and was now a brown skin over the ruts that took a boot and gave it back slowly; he still wanted it late. He said “Six counted. The next I don't count,” to the kindling, and the kindling gave nothing back, which was the proper answer, and he put the knife where it could be checked and did not announce it a second time, because the yard had already been told and a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer.

His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. He did not go down it. No collector had gone down it for the woman the brook took, and the omission had no word and had not made a doctrine of itself; it lay where the flood had left it, the way the clean dark line lay and the space under the rack lay and the seventh year lay on the sill. A pot gave its note past the door as somebody carried it by --- a whole note, clear, one of the true ones --- and Tarn's head did not turn and he did not name the sound. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and the boy did not call this a seventh, because a note that goes past a door and is not turned toward is a thing that has entered nobody's keeping. He set Tarn's not-turning beside his own not-hearing, and the two kept their equality on the tray where they had always kept it, apart from the half-beat and the warm loaf that had come into the yard two days before, apart from the three near-full answers advanced and not paid, apart from the word on its own place with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both. Nothing had joined. Nothing had been paid. The seventh year pressed at the edge of the sill and was held.

Near midday Sella set the basket down at the edge of the racks where the boards were dry and no slate lay, and “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. She had brought him wide around the open space under the lowest rack, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she had not looked into the basket once on the walk. The crust lay stiff in the fold and the empty place beside it was still unnamed; no third had come in three days of weather and there was no word in Grathic for an absence that could not be answered. He wanted the crust and wanted the breast and wanted the bucket two inches beyond the reach of his hand, and the wanting was a thing the body did without asking him. The adult who had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen who would be worked on and who would be waited on lay in a basket at the edge of a rack and could not lift a hand to any of the three. He kept the count exactly and let the house keep it badly; he kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray and let them stay two; he kept the two silences under one roof as an equality and did not call it an argument, a loan, a danger or a promise.

Under the roof the carried fire lay where it had lain all week, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The boards above kept four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet and nobody had thought to enter the gap in either. The door stood a finger's width open and the river's low muttered note came through it, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and he kept that note in its place, apart from the fire's gap and apart from the six hearings and apart from the pot's note that had gone past Tarn without turning his head. One thing in one place and another in another, for seventy-seven days, with no word in Grathic that would let him lay them side by side and call them one.

The yard went around what the water had left with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken. The new grey lay finer than the old clean dark line and was not swept, called thaw and left to go when it went. Jorra's dusty forearms went back to the damper with the bent finger leading, and Tarn's broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and the small knife lay on its sill with six years counted and the seventh held back, and the boy lay in his basket with both states of the forearms on one tray and the count at seventy-seven going on.
