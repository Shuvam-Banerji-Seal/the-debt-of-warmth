## Chronicle

The flood went out of the yard and left mud that took a boot and gave it back slowly. The sweeping went around the clean dark line, the finer grey and the space under the lowest rack. Sella Vail told the broom to go around before she told anybody and said the house where another woman would say I. The yard counted its losses in loaves, glances and a space at the row's end nobody called a number; the figure went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrived nowhere. The boy kept it exactly.

At the well a small child in cast-offs finished a kiln-hand's sentence and made the yard laugh. When a pot was set down the boy's head went toward it before the knuckle; the child's went too, a half-beat late, and he rubbed the green sliver at his neck and could not say why. A man from Slate Row, on no roll, held out a warm loaf. The child's mouth opened on a sentence and did not finish it, and the loaf went into a pocket that held no slate. The boy kept the half-beat and the loaf on separate places and wrote no cause.

At the Fishstairs the steps came back four, four and a landing nobody made a fall of; the soft-cuffed clerk sat for an uncalled measure. Mira Silt answered a question with her own answer; when a buyer named a figure her thumb went under her apron to the grey cord, showing the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted. Three doors down the tin above the bed kept its pale fold; the grey wool below held the stone's hollow. Alda Reed laid her birth-slates down before turning to the fold, crossed two fingers at the flower unknowing and covered the sign with a date given the woman thirty-eight mornings dead. Warm and breathing, exact and not true; her sixth slate lay turned toward a floorboard.

Jorra Vail came through the wheel-shed door with grey slate dust past his little finger and named neither of the two men in the back room. He spoke to the wheel first and put his bent finger to the damper ahead of Sella's sentence. Tarn's knife lay on its sill, six years counted and the seventh held; his broom went over the collector's road without turning. The new grey was not swept, called thaw.

At dusk Sella set him on his bed under the roof, put the fold right without looking at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stood a finger's width open; the river's note came through the gap and the fire's four, four and a gap through the floor, kept apart. One crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count crossed into Silt 19 unspent.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment / day and time.** Dusk of Day 77, Silt 18, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday). Sella Vail has carried the boy up the Clay Yard at a half-pace from every open slate, set him on his bed under the Vail kiln-house roof (not in the Debt Loft), put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, comes through the gap, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor, the two kept in two separate places as for seventy-seven days. The count crosses into Silt 19 unspent. Nothing joined, paid, explained or ended.

- **Where everyone is / condition at the close.** Corin/Dan, seventy-seven days old, carried everywhere, mute, cracked, unranked, below Thin, cold arriving cheek–temple–wrist with the wool riding back from his wrist; wanting the crust, the breast and the bucket two inches off and able to reach none. Sella Vail is at the fire, the broom and the bed, having given every instruction to the wool, the fire, the bucket and the broom and never to the basket, said the house, touched no widow's mark all chapter and gone wide around every open slate; she stood over the two lines in the Vail corner with a broom and did not sweep either, and did not connect the man from Slate Row with the loaf or the loaf with any payer. Tarn is under the roof and in the yard with the small knife on its sill, six years counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held, Ash in his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his wrist; he told the empty yard where the knife was, said *Six counted. The next I don't count* to the kindling, swept the collector's road without turning and did not turn his head at a whole vessel's note past the door on Day 77, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. Jorra Vail came through the wheel-shed door on Day 77 with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger (clean bone-coloured slip on Day 76), named neither the two men who came in since first light nor the back room, spoke to the wheel before anyone, told a pot it would do, knew its full note before it left the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master, put his bent finger to the damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, and did not say the back room was cold or look toward the bed or the basket. Mira Silt was at the Fishstairs on Day 76 with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been; Orra Weir was present only as her board lying beside Mira's and not touching it. Alda Reed came short three doors down on Day 76 with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt; Dessa Silt has been dead since the opening of Day 38 and is thirty-nine mornings gone and uncollected. Ulda (unnamed in the narration, about three years old in this year) was plainly glimpsed at the well on Day 75: cast-off clothes, gap where a front tooth had been, green lead-glaze sliver on a string at his neck, finishing a kiln-hand's sentence in an affectionate voice, turning his head toward a pot a half-beat late and taking the warm loaf into a pocket that held no slate. Garl Vail is absent and kept by his salt-stiff coat, hollow wheel-seat, clean dark line, proverb said to a pot with no crack and uncalled tile debt. Berr Clay, Marra, Rusk, the Greybank and Hearth figures and all Earth figures are offstage; the Fishstairs clerk was seen again, still set for the uncalled measure.
