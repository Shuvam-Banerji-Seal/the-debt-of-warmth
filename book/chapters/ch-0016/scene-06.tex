By the middle of the afternoon the mud had gone from brown to the colour of wet ash, and the broom stood against the wall with its bristles uppermost, the way a tool is set down when the work is done and is not finished. \person{Sella Vail} drew water at \place{the yard well} with her little-door burn closing first on the rope and then on nothing, and gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “That'll do,” she said to the bucket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and the boy, carried at the edge of the well in the basket, kept her half-pace from every place a slate might lie.

“There's a skin on it,” a woman said, looking into the bucket. “Like milk gone cold. You could lift it with a spoon.”

“Weather,” another said. “It'll go when it goes.”

“Ask the pot,” Sella said to the woman who wanted a figure, and lifted the basket a hand's breadth out of the draught with her knee without once looking down into it. “It'll tell you what a wet thing costs set down there.” She gave the instruction to the bucket and the wool and never to the basket, and the yard went on drawing as though it had been given an answer, which in this district is the same thing as being given one.

The number went around them as it had gone all week: from a fist shut on a figure, to a mouth that thought better of speaking, to a shoulder that carried it off down the road and arrived nowhere. The house kept its count in loaves moved along a shelf, in a glance at the clay jar on the wall, in a space at the row's end that nobody called a number; the yard had turned a flood into weather and the weather into the thaw that was, and the thaw that was into nothing anybody owed. \person{Corin}, seventy-seven days old, kept the three days as three slates set edge to edge --- seventy-five, seventy-six, seventy-seven --- with no writing on any of them and no hand to put any of them down. The surgeon who had once read a chart with the note turned down knew the difference between a thing entered and a thing left lying, and the infant at his ear was hungry, and the hunger was the louder of the two.

A man came up with a figure in his hand and let it go in the general direction of the well. “Three days' baking,” he said, meaning it as a number, and the women went on looking at the skin on the water. The boy set the three days beside the three loaves the man had not counted and let them lie where they lay. He did not enter them. He had learned in a better-lit room that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and here the figure was not read aloud at all, only carried from a fist to a shoulder and let fall.

Below the sill the child's cold foot was still the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to the yard, and the two facts went past each other again in the light of the afternoon and never met. The boy carried the wool and the dark foot on a place of his own with no name, no cause and no cure. He knew the extremity going before the core, the blood going back too fast and too cold, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; the reading worked perfectly and was worth nothing. He could have named the order of it in the tongue of the other life and had no tongue for it here, and the woman who had wrapped the foot went on calling it the cold while the woman passing with a bucket called the water weather, and neither altered by a word. He did not lay Sella's not-going beside it as a cause. It was told of her that she had once sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and he had put that on a tray of its own beside the fold put right without a look, \person[Mira Silt]{Mira}'s not-looking and \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s taking-himself-out-of-his-sentence, four not-lookings of one shape and not a cause. A likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not a hand puts one there. He had learned that in the better-lit room, and kept the four as four.

Through the afternoon he counted what he carried, the way another man might count the tools on a bench before he shuts the shed. Nothing had been added to the trays since the morning; nothing had been taken away. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood at six: five from four sources --- a cup across a room where a child was born, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \place{the Drawing house} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of the woman the brook took as the yard lifted her, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them. A vessel had given its note past the yard door in the morning and Tarn's head had not turned; a note that goes past a door and is not turned toward is a thing that has entered nobody's keeping, and the boy did not call it a seventh. No seventh had come. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand had come. The pause in the neat dark hand was still a pause with no name attached to it, lying with four others that did not join.

On a second tray lay the three intervals, Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25, kept as objects with no answer written across them. On a third lay the three near-full answers, advanced and not paid: the first out of the long-kept interval, the second out of the nearly-full pot in open light, the third out of the day the district's note had come under his teeth. They stood near-full and stood still, as near-full things do. He had spent three days trying to make them into one answer and had spent three days not doing it, because a better-lit room had taught him what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer.

The word forty lay on a place of its own, a saying and not a vessel's sound, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it --- Mira's second cord twisted twice beneath the grey one --- and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both. The word had not been spoken again. The shape had not been counted. Four low sounds lay in four places: the told bell out of the dark, the heard bell over the water, the early note out of the stone, and the kiln's missing breath. The river's low muttered note was a fifth thing and not a hearing, and he kept it where he kept it, apart from the four, apart from the six, and apart from the fire's gap that would come later through the floor. One thing in one place and another in another, for seventy-seven days, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would let him lay them side by side and call them one.

Into the file with these went a new shape that was not a hearing and not a number: a head turned toward a pot half a beat after the basket turned, a green sliver rubbed with a thumb, a warm loaf going into a pocket that held no slate. The shape of a keeping that changes hands with no slate. He entered it nowhere and wrote no cause across it, because the shape of a sale is not the sale, and a better-lit room had taught him the cost of calling a likeness a cause. It lay beside his own turning toward the pot and did not touch it.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear and the empty place beside it was still unnamed. No third had come in three days of weather, and there was no word in Grathic for an absence that could not be answered. He wanted the crust and wanted the breast and wanted the bucket two inches beyond the reach of his hand, and the wanting was a thing the body did without asking him. The adult who had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen who would be worked on and who would be waited on lay in a basket at the edge of a well and could not lift a hand to any of the three.

Sella's hand, \person{Alda Reed}'s hand and Mira's hand stayed on their tray and did not become a cause. A kindness that could not be answered was still an absence that could not be answered; the smoky crust had gone out of the fold on a day nobody would name and nobody said it was gone, and the hand that took it was entered nowhere. He kept the three hands as three hands. He had learned what a better-lit room does with three hands in a row, and he would not give the room the satisfaction of a fourth.

Near the well the argument about the skin on the water ended where it had begun, in weather, and a woman went off with her bucket as though she had been told something. \person{Tarn} went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and told the empty yard where the small knife was, the way he had told it every morning of six years. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, and the seventh was nearly gone and still held. Something must give at the end of it; the end was not written here, and he went on holding it as a man holds a breath he has decided not to spend yet, though his chest is already tight. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it, and he did not scrub at it this afternoon. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, and the two silences under one roof kept their equality without a word between them. He did not name the sound when a vessel gave its note past the door, and his head did not turn.

Under the roof the carried fire lay where it had lain all week, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The boards above kept four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet and nobody had thought to enter the gap in either. Nobody had said who had paid for the coal that kept the fire, and the payer went unnamed as the payer had gone unnamed for seventy-seven days; warmth came into the house from somewhere the boy could not see or reach, and he set the payer on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it. The fire took what it was given and gave back what it could, and the gap fell in the same place in the fourth breath, as regular as a fault in a stone, and nobody in the house had thought to count the one thing the boards had that the fire did not.

\person{Garl Vail}'s proverb about the crack went to a pot with no crack in it. The salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg, the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean, the clean dark line lay where it had lain and the new grey lay finer beside it, unswept and called thaw and left to go when it went. The debt against the firing of tiles remained neither paid nor settled nor done with, and went around another year as Sella went around Ash. The boy kept the coat, the seat, the line, the grey and the uncalled debt in five places that did not touch, and did not make a sixth of them.

Dusk came on without a bell for it. Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. She left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from his wrist so the cold found the place.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap. The boy kept the two sounds in two places as he had kept them for seventy-seven days: the river's note in its place and the fire's gap in its place, and the six hearings in theirs, and the told bell beside the heard bell and both beside the early note and the kiln's gap and the district's new note, and the half-beat and the warm loaf on a place of their own, and the cold foot with no name, and the crust stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Nothing had joined. Nothing had been paid. Nothing had been explained.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count crossed into Silt 19 unspent.
