# Chapter 16 — The Sweeping Around: beat sheet

**Mode.** Normal, three lived days: Day 75 to Day 77, Silt 16 to Silt 18, Y.H. 813, Sixthday to Eighthday. Each day opens with its own `\daymark{N}{date}` (Scenes 1, 3 and 5 carry the marks) and is lived for well over 800 words --- roughly 4,900 words a day across two scenes. No day skipped, poured into another or summarised. Six scenes, 2,400–2,500 words each, ~14,600 words total. Day 74 is finished and is not revisited; no recap of the flood at the opening beyond what the mud itself shows. POV stays inside Corin/Dan in close third-person past tense. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no Court, Waiting Room or collector interior. The old life colours the noticing (a chart closed with a neat line, a hand reaching for a slate before a person, the trade of attention for bread read as an account) without crossing into speech.

**Chapter movement.** The water is gone and nothing with it has been settled. The recovery becomes another kind of keeping-by-avoidance: a yard that sweeps around what it left, a house that leaves two lines unswept, a woman whose tin stays shut, a boy who will not speak the thin child upriver. Meaningful change: a new person is plainly in the yard for the first time --- the child who turns his head toward a pot a half-beat late and takes a loaf from a man whose name is on no roll --- so that the boy's private file gains the shape of a sale nobody enters. Threads advanced: T071/T072, T077, T033/T034, T061/T082, T076, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T031, T093, T025/T059, T024, T089/T074/T023, T007/T008/T073, T035/T057, T036, T086/T060/T091, T065/T088. Do not pay T033 (the sale is a shape, not a betrayal) and do not pay T023 again (the sixth page was written Day 64). Do not solve the crust, the stone or the coal; do not name Dessa, speak forty, count a knot, open a tin, give one in three, bring Marra onstage, write a seventh page, add a seventh hearing or a sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, make an accident of the board, end a silence or end anything.

**A note on the watcher.** The child of Scene 2 is Ulda (sheet: `characters/ulda.md`), glimpsed and not named in the narration --- a small foundling of about three in Y.H. 813, not the eleven-year-old of the sheet's later dialogue. Keep him silent except for one borrowed sentence finished for a kiln-hand; no secret is spoken and no slate is seen. The loaf changes hands, the mouth opens and closes on nothing. T033 stays open toward the far betrayal; T034 is planted as the physical tell. If he is given a second speaking appearance in Chapter 17, his sheet must be reconciled with his age before the scene is written. He is not Marra, not the Fishstairs clerk, not the soft-cuffed Court-table clerk, and not joined to any of them.

## Scene 1 — The Sweeping Around
**Day 75, Silt 16, Sixthday. Morning to midday.** Opens with `\daymark{75}{Silt 16, Y.H. 813}` and a drop capital.

**Place.** The mud of the Clay Yard; the drying racks; the yard well; the corner under the Vail kiln-house roof. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn, yard women and a man with a number (walk-ons). Jorra at the wheel-shed, heard and not on stage; Alda, Mira, Orra offstage; Ulda not yet glimpsed. Garl kept by his coat, seat, clean dark line and proverb.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open in the mud the flood left: a boot goes in to the ankle and comes out unwillingly, the mud keeping a moment of it before it lets go. The sweeping goes around the clean dark line at the Vail corner, the burned line two doors down and the space under the lowest rack; Sella gives the broom its instruction before she gives it to anybody (*Go around. It cost enough to come in.*) (T009, T013, T092, T031).
2. The house counts what it took in loaves, glances and a space at the row's end that nobody calls a number. A man brings the figure in his fist, passes it to a mouth and a shoulder and it arrives nowhere. The boy, seventy-five days old and carried at the half-pace from every open slate, keeps the count exactly against the bad arithmetic and lets the figure lie where it lies (T071/T072, T077).
3. The child's cold foot from below the sill is still called *the cold* by the woman who wrapped it and *weather* by the yard, and the two facts go past each other again in the same mud. The boy carries the wool and the dark foot on a place of their own with no name, no cause and no cure (T093).
4. New grey finer than the old clean dark line lies beside it in the corner, unswept because the house says it is the thaw and it will go when it goes; Sella stands over both with a broom and sweeps neither. Garl's proverb about the crack goes to a pot with no crack in it and his tile debt lies where it lay (T009, T013, T092).
5. Sella gives every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, says *the house*, goes wide around an open slate at the well's edge and does not touch the widow's mark, because a yard is not an opening (T058, T079).
6. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist; the wool rides back from his wrist. One crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; he wants the crust, the breast and the bucket and can reach none (T086, T060).

**Emotional turn.** Recovery is not resolution. The yard has already changed a flood into loaves and weather, and the boy's file has grown by a note, a cold foot and a heresy nobody spoke. The surgeon reads what the mud has done to a quarter of a town; the infant is hungry, and the infant is louder.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud that takes a boot and gives it back slowly; broom bristles around a finger's width of dark; grey finer than the old line; tallow, wet wool, river silt and the sour tang of Ash; the half-pace; one brown-edged crust at his ear; drool frozen and unwiped.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072, T077, T083/T085/T092, T009/T013, T031, T093, T058/T079, T086/T060. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the cold foot held past the rescue toward the silt-leach years hence; the space under the rack and the broken slates under it; the two lines left in place toward the later day a house is left where it is and still kept. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Sella's broom going around the two lines in the corner with neither swept, the boy carried past at the half-pace with one crust in his fold and the empty place beside it, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the mud and down with the count at seventy-five.

## Scene 2 — The Half-Beat
**Day 75, Silt 16, Sixthday. Afternoon through dusk.** Continues Day 75; no second `\daymark`.

**Place.** The yard well and the path at its edge; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, Ulda (first plain glimpse, unnamed in narration), a kiln-hand, a man from Slate Row whose name is on no roll, yard voices (walk-ons).

**Beats, in order.**
1. At the well a small child in cast-off clothes that fit badly, with a gap where a front tooth was and a sliver of green lead-glaze on a string at his neck, finishes a kiln-hand's sentence for him in the warm, affectionate voice and makes the yard laugh. He says what everybody is thinking in a tone that makes it harmless; he smells of other people's hearths and small ale (T033/T034 --- Ulda's idiolect, but no secret in it).
2. A woman sets a pot down on the well-lip. The boy's head goes toward it before the knuckle; the child's head goes too, a half-beat late. He catches himself, rubs the green sliver with his thumb and grins, and cannot say why he turned. The boy files the delay as a shape on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a cause --- and keeps it beside his own turning without letting the two touch (T034, T061/T082, T076, T084).
3. A man from Slate Row whose name is on no roll holds out a loaf, warm from somebody's fire. The child's mouth opens on a sentence and the sentence is not finished; the loaf goes into his hand. No secret is named, no slate is opened and no writing is seen. The boy keeps the shape of the transaction --- a keeping that changes hands with no slate --- and enters it nowhere (T033 planted, T072, T080 filed apart from the record-first hands).
4. Sella turns the basket with her knee out of the draught, goes wide around an open slate as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, gives the instruction to the bucket and the wool and never to the basket, says *the house*, and does not connect the child to the loaf or the loaf to any payer (T058, T084, T060).
5. Tarn's small knife is on its sill before the light goes, six years standing in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it (T036, T009). The two silences under the roof keep their equality; the loan that can hear stays uncalled (T076).
6. Dusk: Sella sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot; the door stands a finger's width open and the river's note comes through the gap, kept apart from the fire (T065, T088, T058, T086). The count ends at seventy-five.

**Emotional turn.** The boy sees the district's other economy: attention is a thing a person can sell for bread, and the seller cannot say why he turned his head. The old surgeon recognises a tell and files it as an object; he does not make a cause of it, because a better-lit room taught him what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer.

**Sensory anchors.** Cast-off clothes worn like a uniform; the gap in the grin; green glaze rubbed with a thumb; a pot set down on a wet stone lip; a loaf warm from an unknown fire; the half-beat of a head turning; mud in the well rope; cold at cheek, temple, wrist; tallow and wet wool under the roof.

**Threads advanced.** T033/T034, T061/T082, T076, T072, T080, T058/T084, T036, T009, T065/T088, T086, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted:** the half-beat delay held for the day it lands as a betrayal; the loaf gathered interest in an account the child cannot read; the watcher's attention one day pricing the boy's ear. **Paid:** nothing. No secret sold, no second sighting of the neat dark hand, no recognition.

**Exact last image.** The child's head turning toward the pot a breath after the basket's, the green sliver under his thumb and the loaf going into a pocket that holds no slate, while the boy is carried up the yard with the count at seventy-five and no word for what he saw.

## Scene 3 — Two Cords Side by Side
**Day 76, Silt 17, Seventhday. Morning to midday; dusk under the roof.** Opens with `\daymark{76}{Silt 17, Y.H. 813}` and a drop capital.

**Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge; the shade at the edge of the fish-trade; under the Vail roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Mira Silt, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (silent), a weir woman and an eel buyer (walk-ons). Orra offstage, present only as her board and knot-string. Ulda not repeated this day.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The steps have come back four, four and a landing where a step should have been, a hand's breadth out of line, and nobody makes a fall of it. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the landing with his slate for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to the stalls. He is not Marra and not the Court-table clerk; the boy files the order on its own tray without a sixth sighting of the neat dark hand (T083, T090, T080).
2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer --- *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* --- moves eel-bones while deciding and puts one away when the deciding is done.
3. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never taken upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted (T025, T059). Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it; the shape of the forty-first knot is legible to the boy without its name and the word forty is not spoken (T024).
4. The thin child upriver is not spoken of and the knot for that child is not touched. The boy sets the not-speaking beside Mira's not-looking and Berr's taken-self-out-of-his-sentence and keeps the three apart, with no word in Grathic for a keeping done by leaving something out (T084, T072).
5. Sella keeps the half-pace from every open slate, sets the basket in the bridge's shade where no slate lies, says *the house*, gives the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, and carries him home at dusk without a word about what was said over the eels (T058, T086).
6. Through the day the six hearings stay six, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among; no seventh comes, and the three near-full answers stay advanced and not paid (T061/T082). The count reaches seventy-six (T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** A district keeps its most important accounts in wool, eel-bones and a second cord no officer would know to read. The boy reads the shape of a private record and cannot enter it; the ache for a figure he can spend is sharpened by the nearness of three he cannot.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried pale on sleeves; silt in the hollow of the neck; the top of the left ear gone; eel-bones flicked against a thumb and one put away; the double twist under the grey cord; cold fish, salt and tallow; a soft cuff going to a slate before the eyes go to the stalls; the mud line on the steps.

**Threads advanced.** T025/T059, T024, T090/T080, T083, T058, T084, T061/T082, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** both cords toward the day they are read together as the district's private accounts; the thin child upriver toward Nine Pots; the still-set clerk toward a standing charge on a district that has already spent its dead. **Paid:** nothing. No knot counted, no word forty, no crust given, no look into the basket.

**Exact last image.** Mira's thumb under her apron on the grey cord with the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's without touching, and the boy in the bridge shade with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed.

## Scene 4 — The Tin Above the Bed
**Day 76, Silt 17, Seventhday. Afternoon through dusk.** Continues Day 76; no second `\daymark`.

**Place.** House Silt three doors down the Clay Yard; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Alda Reed, the neighbour scrubbing in the back room (walk-on). Dessa dead thirty-eight mornings and uncollected; Garl kept by his objects; Mira and Orra offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries the boy three doors down and sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every place a slate might lie; she opens no slate, turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row and keeps her half-pace from Alda's writing (T058, T081, T084).
2. From the basket's low angle the round pewter tin above the empty bed keeps its pale fold at the seam and its thumb-bright rub on the lid, unopened. Below it the fold of grey wool on the board-bed holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone had lain, and neither house nor boy says either thing is gone (T089, T074, T086).
3. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt, lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold too long, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing she does it and covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa. She says *warm and breathing* --- exact and not true --- and touches the little tin inside her bodice without opening it (T007, T008, T073). The lie has curdled through to a woman thirty-eight mornings dead and no living person can correct it.
4. Her sixth narrow slate stays turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name. The hidden record stands at six pages, all unread; **do not write a seventh page and do not pay T023 again** (T023). The tin in her bodice stays shut.
5. Two private records in two materials stand in one room and do not touch --- the pewter tin over the bed, the turned slate toward the floorboard --- read by nobody, carried to no door. Nobody says the woman's name or the number in her thumb; no recognition, no quest to a bank, no joining of the two trays (T089, T074, T023, T081).
6. Sella carries him home at the half-pace and sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap; the one crust is stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it; the count ends at seventy-six (T058, T065, T086, T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** The tenderness is maintenance done in the wrong grammar: a fold pressed too long, a date laid over a sign, a lie told for mercy that has become a fact. The boy reads two records a Court could not confiscate and has no standing to open either; the infant wants the breast and gets a fold put right by a hand that does not look at his face.

**Sensory anchors.** Soap-and-milk cold under river silt; grey wool shawl; the pale fold and thumb-bright rub in a slant of falling light; the empty hollow in the grey wool below; a chipped cup and a brown ceiling stain; a door a finger's width open; woodsmoke and lye; the wet cloth of the scrubbing neighbour.

**Threads advanced.** T089/T074/T023 (carried, not repaid), T007, T008, T073, T081, T058, T084, T086, T065, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the convergence of second records after Alda is gone; the sixth page toward the day Dan finds it; the mother's warmth kept at home in a material no assessor would enter. **Paid:** nothing. No name or number from Dessa, no opening, no recognition.

**Exact last image.** The pale fold at the pewter lid's seam above the empty bed in the falling light, the hollow in the grey fold below saying nothing, and Alda's two fingers crossed at the red edge of the flower under the date, neither record read by anybody.

## Scene 5 — The Dust Comes Back
**Day 77, Silt 18, Eighthday. Morning to midday.** Opens with `\daymark{77}{Silt 18, Y.H. 813}` and a drop capital.

**Place.** The Clay Yard; the Vail wheel-shed and the room off it (not entered); the drying racks; under the roof. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Jorra Vail, Tarn, yard voices (walk-ons). The two men in the back room are not named. Ulda not repeated.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Jorra comes through the finger's-width-open door off the wheel-shed with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Two men came in since first light and spoke low; he gives them no name (T035). The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack where the yard goes around them and enters them into nothing (T031).
2. He speaks to the wheel before he speaks to anyone --- it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady --- tells a pot it will do, and knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel without saying so before a kiln-master from High Kiln, who would call it early and make something of it (T035, T061). The boy keeps the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray as two states of one week without asking which slate was broken or whether it was a birth-slate (T031).
3. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low when his bent finger goes in ahead of his hand at the damper and moves it, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He does not say the back room is cold (T035, T058).
4. He does not look toward the bed or into the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed as tightly as a lid nobody has tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn (T057, T084).
5. Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill before light, six years counted in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb and the seventh nearly gone and still held --- something must give at the end of it, but the end is not written here. He tells the empty yard where the knife is, holds the pale clip-ring to the light and keeps Ash in his knuckles the colour of the clean dark line; his broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it (T036, T009). A vessel gives its note past the door and his head does not turn; he does not name the sound and the boy does not call it a seventh hearing (T076, T061). Two ears and two silences keep their equality under one roof and the loan that can hear stays uncalled.
6. Dusk: Sella sets the boy on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face. The carried fire misses one breath in every four; the new grey lies unswept beside the clean dark line; one crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it (T065, T092, T013, T086). The count ends at seventy-seven.

**Emotional turn.** A house reads its own omissions as weather: dust on forearms, a space under a rack, a knife with a year held back. The boy watches two men keep silences of the same shape and refuses to join them; the seventh year presses toward a giving that nobody under the roof will write down.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust past the bent little finger; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone; damp clay, tallow and the sweet edge of lead glaze on a bad wind; the bent finger leading at the damper; the worn mark on the knife handle; the pale clip-ring catching what light there is; the broom's unchecked pass over the collector's road.

**Threads advanced.** T035, T031, T057, T084, T036, T076, T009, T061/T082, T065/T088, T092/T013, T086, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the broken birth-slates and the named-but-unwritten room toward the Broken Slates and Jorra's ungiven promise; the seventh year toward its end and what must give; the ear worth more than the debt toward the day the loan is called. **Paid:** nothing. No back room entered, no men named, no slate counted, no calling of the loan, no ending.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's dusty forearms going to the damper with the bent finger leading while Tarn's broom goes over the collector's road without turning, the knife on its sill with the seventh year still held back and the boy in his basket with both states of the forearms on one tray.

## Scene 6 — What the Count Carries

**Place.** The Clay Yard and the yard well; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin, Sella, Tarn, yard voices (walk-ons). Jorra in the wheel-shed, Alda, Mira, Orra, Ulda offstage; Dessa dead and uncollected; Garl kept by his objects.

Forward, not closed.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud that lets a boot go slowly; a broom going around a space; loaves with a gap at the row's end; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; fire four, four and a gap through the floor; the river's muttered note through a finger's-width-open door; one stiff crust at his ear; a frozen thread nobody wipes.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072, T077, T093, T061/T082, T086/T060/T091, T092/T013, T065/T088, T058, T024, T034, T083/T085. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the half-beat delay held for the day the watcher's sale lands; the cold foot held past the rescue; the two lines and the empty place carried into the lean week; the count toward the planting month. **Paid:** nothing. No seventh hearing, no sixth sighting, no name for the empty place, no experiment, no rank change, no ending.

**Exact last image.** The boy on his bed under the roof with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it, the door a finger's width open with the river's muttered note through the gap and the fire's four-four-and-a-gap through the floor in separate places, his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the water and down as the count crosses into Silt 19.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 17

The boy carries Silt 18 out under the Vail roof with nothing joined, paid, explained or ended, and the count going on into Silt 19. The lean week begins in the next chapter as a seven-day passage: the yard will go around what the water left with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken, and the boy, carried at the edge of the work, will keep the count exactly as the house keeps it badly. Account-day will come on Silt 20 with Berr Clay reading one in twelve over a thinner crowd and taking himself out of his own sentence; the printed figure and the true subtraction will still lie on trays that do not touch. The soft-cuffed clerk will still be set at the Fishstairs for a measure nobody called; Mira's two cords, Orra's knot-string, Alda's sixth page and the pewter tin above the empty bed will stay unread; the cold foot will stay the cold and weather. The child with the gap in his grin may be glimpsed again in the lean week, still only a shape in the file --- a half-beat and a loaf, no betrayal paid. Tarn's seventh year presses on toward its end; Jorra's dust and the back room point at the Broken Slates; the new grey lies finer than the old clean line and is not swept. One crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Forward, not closed.
