\daymark{52}{Ilmen 23, Y.H. 813}

Morning came into \place{the Clay Yard} the way water comes into a dry pit: not all at once, but finding the low places first. The light went into the hollows between the stacked pots and under the lip of \place{the drying racks} and stood there a while before it reached the trodden clay in the middle. \person{Corin} was carried down in the basket with the fold turned right at his chin, fifty-two days old, and \person{Sella Vail} set him in the shade at the rack end out of the draught the way a woman sets a pot where the sun will not take the glaze off it. She gave the instruction to the wool. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and went back to the fire and the buckets without once looking into the basket.

A rack-woman came along the row with a pot in both hands, turned a little sideways against the weight of it, and set it down in the yard out of her own shadow so that the light fell across its belly. She did not tap it. Nobody tapped it. She straightened her back with a small sound and went off to the tubs, and the pot stood where she had put it, the glaze the colour of skimmed milk gone thin.

The pot answered.

He had known the answer before he heard it, which was the trouble with knowing anything in this body: the ear went ahead and the hand stayed where it was. It was not the flat answer of \lore{the full note} --- not the small dead knock of a vessel cracked all the way through that he had heard five times from four sources and once from a person, six hearings kept beside and never among. It was not the silence of a pot turned toward on a bench that gives nothing and leaves an interval with the outline of a sound in it. This was a note with air under it and a want in it, a sound that went almost to full and stopped there, honest about the stopping, as honest as a man who says he has not quite enough and will not pretend otherwise. Nearly. The woman at the tubs had not heard it, or had heard it and given it no name, and went on with her wringing.

He filed it on its own tray. The nearly full answer of Ilmen 6 lay there already, set down out of another woman's shadow, gone advanced and not paid since the year turned. A second answer came to lie beside the first, and the two did not join, and neither of them joined the six. They were not hearings. They were answers to an interval he had carried since Stone thirteen, through the Five and into Ilmen, and the interval had gained a second shape without buying him a single thing he could lift a hand to.

Nearly and full were still two sounds. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held the first, none for almost, and no word of his own worth spending on it that would not have sounded to these people like a man arguing with a pot. He had no hand to tap either pot and ask it to say the thing again. The ear that would have placed a flaw under a knuckle belonged to a body that could not lift an arm to its own chin, and the surgeon in him named the note exactly --- the thin place under the pitch, the want sitting in the belly of the sound --- and could do nothing with the name. Knowing the note exactly had never been less useful. It was a tool with no handle, a clamp with nothing to bite on.

The body humiliated him exactly, as it had every morning of his fifty-two. A thread of drool went cold at his mouth and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and cold arrived in its known order, cheek first, then temple, then wrist, the wool doing less than the wind wanted. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth of the basket's fold and came down. Reducing a shoulder had been ordinary work in the life before this one; keeping his own hand on a pot's rim was not. He was the only adult in this yard and the least use in it, and he kept the note he could not tap as carefully as if keeping it were a thing somebody had asked of him.

\scenebreak

The three intervals stayed where they were: Stone thirteen, Stone seventeen, Stone twenty-five, three objects on their own tray, not hearings and never to be made into hearings by a pot that answered almost. Six hearings of the full note stayed six --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving under the iron tube, and the sixth, small and flat, out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the yard's hands went under her --- beside the five and never among them, and no seventh came into the yard with the rack-woman's washing. He had stopped expecting a seventh the way a man stops expecting a letter that is never going to be written. The absence of one had its own shape now, and he kept that shape on its own place too.

Beyond the rack end \person{Tarn} went on with the broom. The bristles made their dry sound over the trodden clay, going and coming, going and coming, and when the pot gave its answer his head did not turn. He did not slow the stroke or lift his eyes or say what the pot had given. If he had heard it, and the boy knew he had heard it as surely as he knew the pale clip-ring at the man's wrist, then the hearing lay under the same silence as all the others, the second ear under the same roof keeping the same account as the first. Two ears, two silences, in open light this time with no wall between them, and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled. He would not drag the sentence out into the yard and stand it beside the pot. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and a man who owns nothing keeps the one thing he owns quiet.

The broom went over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector and did not turn along it. Ten mornings now and no collector had gone down it for the woman the brook took, and there was still no word in the district's tongue for the not-calling. Sella went around that word the way she went around a thing left where it lay. The boy kept the not-calling on its own tray with the rest, an interval with the outline of a sound that had not come, and did not give it a cause.

\scenebreak

Near the shed \person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried the colour of old bone up both forearms and the left little finger bent. He told a pot it would do, in the flat voice that gave nothing away and asked nothing back. Sella told the fire it was low, and the bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it before she had finished the sentence, so that she shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot. He did not say the room off the shed was cold. He did not look toward the basket or the bed. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly on this morning as on the hour it was spoken, three years gone.

The boy watched the bent finger from the shade at the rack end and kept what he saw where questions can last for years. Jorra knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel --- knew it the way he knew the bend of his own finger --- and would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it. Under this roof two people could hear a vessel speak and both of them kept the knowing to themselves, and the boy, who could also hear, was the least use of the three. He had no word in Grathic to ask a man why he hid his ear, and no standing to ask it if he had. He set the knowing on its own place and did not let it touch the nearly full answer lying on its tray, though both were sounds and both were kept, because a likeness was not a cause and he had learned in a room with better light what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer.

\scenebreak

Sella came for him at the turn of the wind and carried him in out of the draught. She lifted the basket level, a pot filled that must not be slopped, and set him on his bed under the \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. She gave every instruction to the fire and the wool. She never gave one to the basket. The house's arithmetic went on around him in loaves moved along a shelf and a glance at the clay jar on the wall, a space where a loaf should stand and no figure spoken, and he kept the count exactly beside her as he had kept it all his fifty-two days. One crust lay in the fold and the empty place beside it kept its shape, eaten by nobody, given to nobody, taken by nobody anyone would name. No third crust had come. He had set Sella's hand, \person{Alda Reed}'s hand and \person{Mira Silt}'s hand on three places on a tray weeks ago and would not let the tray become a cause, and the tray still did not become one.

\person{Garl Vail} was absent and kept by objects, which in this house is a way of keeping a man that no Court can enter. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door. The hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean and nobody filled it. The clean dark line where the \lore{the Ash} ridge had turned its corner was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and the proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. Behind the boy the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. \lore{the widow's mark} stayed under Sella's collar where it lay, because a midday carrying is not an opening and she touched it for no pot and for no nearly full sound.

At the dusk bank she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The clean dark line where the Ash turned was still not swept. Nobody in the yard, or under this roof, or at the Cross where the figure was printed and read over the tops of baskets, had found a word for how no collector had gone for the woman in the brook; the not-calling lay where it fell and the morning had come over it as over a stone, and the evening came over it the same way. The boy had no word for it in the district's tongue and no hand to write one, and he did not make a doctrine of the omission, because a doctrine was a thing people built when they wanted to be warm without owning a day.

\scenebreak

Under the Vail roof he lay with one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it, fifty-two days old. The nearly full answer lay on its own tray beside the three intervals and did not touch them; the six hearings stayed six, five from four sources and one from a person; the five pauses stayed unjoined; the told bell lay beside the heard bell and never on it; the word and the knot's shape and the weight of a thumb were apart; two cords and a knot-string stood on boards that did not touch; and the third sound that had come into the yard this morning was a second answer that had bought him nothing but the exact knowledge of a want in a pot he could not tap. The fire behind him missed one breath in every four. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down.
