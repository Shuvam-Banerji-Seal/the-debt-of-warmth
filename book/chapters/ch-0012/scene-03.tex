\daymark{50}{Ilmen 21, Y.H. 813}

The \place{the Clay Yard} was still the colour of wet ash when \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down at the end of the \place{the drying racks}, out of the draught that came off \place{the Slip Brook}, and turned the fold right under his chin. \person{Corin} was fifty days old and awake to the setting-down, the way a pot is awake to the hand that lifts it and knows before the hand does what is going to be done with it. The instruction went to the wool. “Keep it off his ears,” she said. “It's the river wind.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and did not look into the basket, and went to the fire before she went anywhere else, because the fire came first in that house and had for four generations.

By the time the light got up over the racks the men had begun. The boy lay where a pot is laid to dry, at the edge of the floor where nothing would be set on him and nobody would step between him and the writing they had come to do. He had once known a man go past a chart in a better-lit room with his hands in his pockets and his eyes on the door, and the care here had the same shape, though it was given to a different god and cost a different kind of attention. He kept the distance because she kept it. The cold came at cheek, then temple, then wrist, and the drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and a pot's rim is not wiped in the yard. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for going around a thing you would not step between, and he wrote no cause across it.

Then \person{Jorra Vail} came out of the room off the \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of his forearms past the bend of his little finger. It was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The slip dried pale and sat on top of the skin where a man could see it; this dust had gone into the creases and stayed there, grey as the inside of a slate, ground fine as meal and rubbed in with the sweat of a hand that had been splitting stone. He said nothing about where he had been. His eyes went to the door, to the fire, to the wheel, and came back without reporting, which was how his eyes always went.

The boy kept the dust and the clean slip of other mornings in two places on a tray and could not ask which of them meant what. The men in the room off the shed were not named by anyone, and the broken birth-slates under the racks stayed where they had been laid, gone around by everybody and entered by nobody. He had learned that a room a house does not enter is still a room, and that the space under a rack where whole slate should have stood is still a space, and that two facts laid side by side will invent a cause for a man who wants one badly enough. He did not want one badly enough. He had learned the cost of wanting in a better-lit room. He read the grey into the creases of the forearms as he had once read a stain under a nail on a hand that swore it had not been there --- exact, unsentimental, and worth nothing to anyone who could not see it.

“You'll do,” Jorra said to a pot on the bench, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He turned the wheel with his palm and did not look up. “The wheel doesn't care whose hands are on it. It cares if they're steady.”

He knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel; the boy knew that he knew it, and knew that he would not say so before a kiln-master, who would call it early and make something of it. Two things he could keep and could not spend, and the keeping of them was the whole of his work. The note was in the pot before it left the wheel and no calling it early would change it. Questions could last for years in this house. He had already seen one last three.

Sella told the fire it was low. The bent finger went in ahead of the hand at the damper and moved it before she had finished the sentence, so that she shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot instead. “That'll do,” she said to the pot. Jorra did not say the room off the shed was cold. He did not look toward the bed under the \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof or the basket at the rack's end. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as it had been on the hour it was spoken, and the foundling went on being carried under the roof without a name written on him that anybody would show.

\scenebreak

The small bench knife lay on its sill with six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. \person{Tarn} had set it there before the light and had not announced it to the yard, because the yard had been told already and a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer. He said it to the sill instead. “It's back,” he said. “You can check.”

\lore{the Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles the colour of the ridge that had turned the corner and been burned and called dealt with, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught the light as he passed the bench and gave it back, a small round of bright on a man who owned nothing and knew where every small thing in the house was kept. He moved fast, the way a man moves who has six years in the slip pit behind him and one still owing, and he stopped moving altogether when there was nothing for him to do.

A man went past the yard door carrying a vessel, and the vessel gave its note as it went. The boy heard it, and Tarn's head did not turn, and neither of them said anything about it. The loan that could hear was still uncalled and the sentence was still unsaid under the roof. There was no name for that sound in Grathic, and the boy would not have spent one if there had been. He did not call it a seventh hearing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six --- five from four sources and the sixth from a person --- beside and never among, and no seventh came in through the door with the man and his vessel. Two silences kept under one roof keep their equality without a word passing between them, which is nearer to an account than anything else this house had ever entered. A loan that can hear is worth more than the debt that holds it, which is why a house might one day learn to count what it has been keeping for nothing.

Tarn's broom went over the road down toward \place{the Fishstairs}, the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. No collector went down it for the woman the brook took twelve mornings ago. He did not go for one and did not say the thing that had not happened had a name. He agreed with the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot, and went on wanting the late one. A late thaw puts off the paying. “Yes, house,” he said, to the ground, and swept the road again.

\scenebreak

\person{Garl Vail} was absent and kept by objects, which in this house is a way of keeping a man that no Court can enter. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the salt still standing in it in pale lines where the wet had dried. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean to the left and nobody filled it. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner for a year and two days was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept; there was a proverb in the house about leaving the crack showing, and the house had gone on saying it to the pot after the crack was gone.

His sayings went to the fire and the pot and never to the basket. “Feed the fire before you feed yourself. It'll still be there in the morning. You won't.” Sella said it to the coals as she turned them, and the boy lay under it with one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it and did not take it as a sentence spoken to him. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, going around the talk the way a woman goes around Ash in a corner.

In the kiln ash the charred edge of a slate lay black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark, the writing gone off the end. The house had swept around it for a year and never picked it up. The boy kept it in its place with the clean dark line on the wall and the grey that went out in a barrow toward \place{the Bone Orchard} on market-days, three keepings of the same colour in three places, and did not join them. He had learned to leave a thing where a house had left it and to keep its shape on a tray of its own, and the writing gone off the end was a shape like any other. Behind him the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap.

\scenebreak

Sella went around the house's days the way she went around Ash or a debt, without entering them. She moved a loaf along the shelf and glanced at the clay jar she had brought up from \place{House Silt} and set by the wall, and there was a space where another loaf should have stood and no figure spoken over it. She came back to the same place twice, the way she always came back, keeping the number of days as a shape the household could live beside without ever saying it aloud. A number kept as a shape in loaves cannot be taken up by a clerk and entered, which was the whole point of keeping it that way; the boy knew the point without being able to use it. Beside her he kept the count exactly. He had kept it every day of his fifty days and it had never once bought him a hand to lift toward anything.

There was no opening that morning, so \lore{the widow's mark} stayed under her collar and was not touched. At the dusk bank she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch it then either, because a bank is not an opening. She put the fold right without looking at his face and set him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and gave the instruction to the wool and said nothing about where any warmth had come from or who had paid for the milk in the house's dish.

One crust lay in the fold where two had lain. Beside it was the empty place, carried into this day as it had been carried into the last and the one before, eaten by nobody, given to nobody, taken by nobody anyone would name; no third had come and nobody had said the one that went was gone. He had set her hand, \person{Alda Reed}'s hand and \person{Mira Silt}'s hand on three places on a tray and would not let the tray become a cause. There was no word in the district's tongue for a thing taken and not spoken of, and no word of his own worth spending on it.

Under the Vail roof he lay with the fold at his ear, fifty days old, while Garl's coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door and the clean dark line where the Ash had turned was left where it lay, unswept. The fire behind him kept four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, and the dust on Jorra's forearms and the dust in Tarn's knuckles kept in two separate places in the boy's file and did not touch. Nothing had been joined, paid or explained. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down.
