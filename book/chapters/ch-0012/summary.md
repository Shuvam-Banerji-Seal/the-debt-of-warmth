## Chronicle

Frost wrote itself back onto the well-lip in white fronds. The woman who said the ice would hold said nothing now; the man who called it honeyed kept his counsel. The oiled paper in the Vail window hung slack, and Tarn marked it without a word. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and gave every instruction to the fire and the wool, never to the basket. The boy, forty-eight days old, carried the empty place beside his one crust into the new day; the barley breath did not come on the turning wind, and he wrote no cause across its absence.

On Account-day Berr Clay, red cord under his coat and green glaze at his mouth, wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve over the basket at the Cross. The true subtraction went unspoken; the thaw number went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrived nowhere. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. The clerk still sat at the Fishstairs with a slate for a measure nobody called.

Jorra Vail came out of the room off the wheel-shed with grey slate dust ground past his bent little finger and said nothing of where he had been; a vessel gave its note past the door and his head did not turn. Alda Reed laid her slates writing-down before the fold, crossed two fingers at the flower's red edge without knowing it, covered the sign with a date, and turned a fifth note toward a floorboard in a house he could not name. Her warm and breathing was exact and not true.

A pot set down in the yard answered nearly full a second time, with air under the note and a want in it; he filed it beside the first as advanced and not paid, with no hand to tap either pot and ask it to speak again. At the Fishstairs Mira Silt moved eel-bones while deciding whether to lie and put her thumb to the grey cord when a buyer named a figure; shifting a basket showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver, the knots uncounted. Her board lay beside another woman's and did not touch it.

More hairlines ran into the Ilm's ice and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge; the Singing Sill gave its low note early and was called weather. A weir woman with salt white at her wrists said the weak board in the plank walk was still there and would not carry a man, and nobody had trodden it; the boy set it beside the missing step and the stopping hairline and let none of them touch. Nothing had been joined or paid. At dusk Sella set him on his bed, fifty-four days old, one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it, the fire missing one breath in every four. The count went on.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** The boy lies on his bed under the Vail roof, fifty-four days old, with one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it carried through all seven days. Sella Vail carried him up from the yard well at a half-pace from every open slate at the turn of the wind, set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right without looking at his face. The carried fire keeps under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four; the clean dark line where the Ash turned is unswept; Garl's coat holds a broader man's shape on its peg and his hollow seat its lean. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the warmth and comes down. Nothing has been joined, paid, explained or ended. Forward, not closed.
- **Day and time at the end.** Day 54, Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813, Fifthday, dusk under the Vail roof, after a day that opened before light at the yard well. Seven lived days, one dated entry and one `\daymark` each: Day 48 (Ilmen 19, Ninthday) at the well and under the roof; Day 49 (Ilmen 20, Tenthday, Account-day) at the Cross; Day 50 (Ilmen 21, Firstday) in the Clay Yard, wheel-shed and under the roof; Day 51 (Ilmen 22, Secondday) under the roof; Day 52 (Ilmen 23, Thirdday) at the racks and under the roof; Day 53 (Ilmen 24, Fourthday) at the Fishstairs and racks; Day 54 (Ilmen 25, Fifthday) at the well, racks and under the roof. This is the seven-day passage cap. No recap, no skipped or poured day, no present-tense break, no old-life voice, no `\memory{}` tag. Day 47 is finished and not revisited.
- **Where everyone is now.** Corin: on his bed under the roof, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, mute, drool frozen and unwiped, head going where it wants with the small wet sound, cold at cheek–temple–wrist, hand up an inch and down. Sella Vail: at the fire and the bed, half-pace from every open slate, instruction to fire, wool and bucket and never the basket, saying *the house*; she did not touch the widow's mark at a well, a reading, a carrying or a bank; her private roll stays unshown and neither crust nor its absence is named by her. Tarn: broom set away, small knife on its sill with six of seven counted, Ash in the knuckles no scrubbing lifts, pale clip-ring at his wrist; his head did not turn at a vessel's note on Days 50 and 52; his broom went over the collector's road without turning and no collector came; he agrees with early and late alike and wants the late thaw, because a late thaw puts off the paying. Jorra Vail: in and out of the wheel-shed with grey dust on Day 50, clean bone-coloured slip on the other days, bent finger ahead of his hand at the damper ahead of Sella's sentence, telling pots they will do; he did not say the back room was cold and did not look toward the bed; *he's not Vail* stays sealed. Alda Reed: came short on Day 51 with soap-and-milk, birth-slates, the tin touched once unopened, the unaware two-finger sign covered with a date, a fifth note turned toward a floorboard, exact dates after the question had stopped; offstage at the close. Berr Clay: at the Cross on Account-day, red cord under his coat, green glaze at his mouth, thumb wiped twice, read one in twelve twice, took himself out of his own sentence, box locked, off toward High Kiln; never looked into the basket. Mira Silt: at the Fishstairs on Day 53, eel slime dried pale on her sleeves, silt in her neck, head held right, eel-bones while deciding, thumb to the grey cord at a figure, second cord shown, board beside Orra's not touching; offstage at the close. Orra Weir: offstage throughout; only her board and grey knot-string are a presence, the shape of the forty-first knot legible to the boy without its name. Garl Vail: absent and kept by objects; the clean dark line unswept, coat and seat holding his places, proverbs going to fire and pot. Dessa Silt: dead since Day 38, sixteen mornings gone and uncollected, still without a name or a word for the omission. Fishstairs clerk: still set at his slate, hand to slate before eyes to the stalls, for a measure nobody called; walk-on, no dialogue, no sheet. Walk-ons: the well-neighbours including the woman who used to say the ice held and the man who called it honeyed, the man who brought a number in his fist and thought better, the rack-woman who set down the nearly-full pot, the weir woman with salt white at her wrists who spoke plainly of the board, the market crowd, pig-weighers, eel buyers, rack-women.
- **Items gained, lost or used.** Gained: the second visible signature of the thaw — the oiled paper slack in its frame — and a silence where an argument used to stand; a **second** nearly-full answer (Day 52, Ilmen 23) to the interval carried since Stone 13, beside the first of Ilmen 6, both advanced and not paid and neither a hearing; the plain sighting of the second cord twisted twice beneath Mira's grey cord, never carried to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, knots on neither counted; the plain saying that the weak board in the plank walk at the Weir is still there and trodden by nobody; more hairlines in the Ilm ice stopping a thumb's breadth short of the edge; the Singing Sill's note early; Alda's **fifth** note turned toward a floorboard (four went before it), her tin touched once and shut; the readable shape of Orra's forty-first knot held without its name. Lost/unchanged: the smoky crust Mira put in the fold on Stone 28 remains gone since Day 46, not eaten in his sight, not lifted, not named, not solved; the spent crust from Stone 15 stays stiff past smelling, unlifted and unnamed; no third crust has come; the empty place is carried through seven days without a name and the three hands stay on their tray and do not become a cause. The coal the brine-gull took on Day 45 is entered in no slate and missed in no account (nine days ago at the close). The small pale river stone stays in the fold of grey wool in House Silt, unmentioned; the bench knife is on its sill, announced to nobody; the charred slate lies in the kiln ash eaten into the shape of bark; basket and wool are Sella's, lent without a word; the clay jar is the house's, on the wall shelf; Sella's roll unshown, Berr's box locked, Orra's cord upriver where no clerk can spell it.
- **Money and figures.** No money reaches the boy. One in twelve stays printed and unchallenged and the true subtraction goes over the basket unspoken; Berr reads it twice and says a number goes into a slate, not into a well, and there'll be a slate at the watch; the number for the thaw goes from fist to mouth to shoulder and is given to nobody; a man at the well brings a number in his fist and thinks better of giving it; eels change hands four marks the pair on Day 53 (earlier chapters give six for five); the house goes around its days in loaves and glances with a space where a loaf should stand and no figure spoken, while the boy keeps the count exactly; warmth given, taken and lost is entered nowhere. The word *forty* is not spoken again and he does not know it counts children or that he is one of them.
- **Powers and cost.** No usable power, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no heresy. Cracked, low-capacity, unranked, below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best, with a flaw nobody has named. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift. Six hearings of the full note stay six — five from four sources (cup across the birth room, pot in the wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at the Cross, a man giving under the iron tube) and the sixth, small and flat, out of Dessa as the men lifted — beside them and never among them, and no seventh came in seven days. The nearly-full note, now with a second answer beside it, stays on its own tray, advanced and not paid; the many gaps keep their separate places: five unjoined pauses; three intervals (Stone 13, 17, 25); three near-misses (board, missing step, stopping hairline); four witnesses to the thaw (frost-bloom, honeyed ice, hairlines, higher brook); four low sounds (early note, told bell, heard bell, kiln's gap); the word, the knot's shape and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart; the two cords and Orra's knot-string on boards that do not touch. No Grathic word for the flat note, for nearly, for the failure in a vessel or for a thing taken and not spoken of. Naming every note exactly has bought him no hand to tap a pot or lift a crust.
- **Facts, lies, promises, debts.** Alda's *warm and breathing* remains exact and not true; the lie told for mercy, eleven days old when she covered the sign again on Day 51, has curdled all the way through to a death nobody entered and no living person can set it right; she did not say the woman's name or the third day. Nobody has said the crust is gone, who took it, or what became of it; nobody made a doctrine of the missing collector for the woman in the brook, sixteen mornings dead, and the clerk's slate and the unentered body still do not touch. Garl's debt against the firing of tiles is neither paid nor settled nor done with; the clean dark line is not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept. Tarn will not count the seventh year; Jorra's sentence about the foundling stays sealed; the rooms off the shed and under the racks stay unentered. No new promise made, nothing explained, no ending.

Pays nothing, joins nothing, closes nothing, makes no accident of the board.
- **The next chapter must not forget.** Day 55, Ilmen 26, Y.H. 813, Sixthday, opens chapter 13 at the yard well: a man come downriver from Greybank speaks of a counting room shut during the last war where men died of the breath with the damper closed, and nobody in the yard connects it to the kiln; the boy is fifty-five days old and files the saying beside the kiln's own missing breath without writing a cause. ## Relationships at the close of chapter 12

- **Nina** --- love and resentment braided; the debt that made him portable. The unread name in a lit pocket was not reached for again this chapter; the old life stayed offstage, no present-tense break and no `\memory{}` tag. Nine promises, no boat.
- **Des, Ostrowski, Vesci** --- the dead life's voices; none returned in voice. The district's grammar of weather, Berr's passive sentences and the order of reaching --- slate before child, date before wonder, record before person --- stood in for them.
- **Orra Weir** --- a watching that has outlasted seven more days of no answer; she is offstage now, her board and knot-string only a presence, the shape of the knot legible to him and the word still unspoken. Not a friend, not an enemy: a person who set a thing down and waits to see whether it will be picked up.
- **Sella Vail** --- the hand that puts the fold right and never looks at his face, that says *the house* and gives the instruction to the wool; a keeping with no figure and no explanation. One of three hands on the tray, not a cause.
- **Alda Reed** --- the hand that presses the fold too long, crosses the sign without knowing it and covers it with a date, and writes what she will not say into a fifth hidden note; another of the three hands.
- **Mira Silt** --- the hand that put the crust there and keeps two cords no officer can read; the third of the three hands and the one he has wanted the crust from longest. The hand that went to the cord when a figure was spoken now stands on the tray with the other two, kept and not entered.
