\daymark{51}{Ilmen 22, Y.H. 813}

By mid-morning the light under the \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had come up the colour of thin slip, the grey that lies on top of a bowl before the fire takes it, and \person{Corin} lay on his bed with the fold turned right at his ear. He was fifty-one days old. One crust lay in the fold, stiff past smelling, and beside it the empty place kept its shape with the exactness of a wound that has stopped bleeding and will not close. He had learned the difference between a thing that hurts and the place where a thing used to be, and the district had a word for neither. Cold came in its known order --- cheek, then temple, then wrist --- and the wool did less than the wind wanted. A pot was set down somewhere beyond the bench and gave nothing, and he let the nothing lie where it fell. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among. No seventh had come in with the morning and none came now.

He heard \person{Alda Reed} before the door was fairly open: the short step, a breath held and let go, the small clatter of slate against slate under an arm. She came short under the roof, bending a little as she came, smelling of soap and milk gone cold under river silt, with lye sharp under the soap and wet wool at the hem of her grey shawl. The clay under her scrubbed nails was the grey of the yard after rain. She had been bending over other people's rooms since before light and had not straightened up yet.

She laid the birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold.

He had seen the order of reaching in a midwife, in a neighbour at a well, in a clerk behind a Court table, in a man who priced him, in Berr passing a finger over a figure, in the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs, and in this woman three times under this roof. The record first. The person after. The hand went where the writing was before it went where the warmth was, and there was no cruelty in it, which was what made it worth watching. He had once seen a man go past a turned note on a chart with his eyes on the door and his hands in his pockets, and he had learned in a better-lit room what it cost to call a likeness a cause. He kept this reaching on its own place and wrote no cause across it.

\person{Sella Vail} was at the fire before either of them had finished arriving. “He lies quiet,” she said, and gave the sentence to the pot on the bench, as if the pot might answer for the child. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and her little-door burn closed on nothing as she turned a coal with the back of a broken pot and left the mark under her collar where it lay, because mid-morning is not an opening.

Alda crossed to the bed. She pressed the fold flat at his shoulder a breath too long --- longer than cloth requires, long enough that the warmth of her hand went through the wool and stayed after the hand was gone --- and her other hand went once inside her bodice to the little tin and came away without opening it. The tin was a second record in a small round box, and he had never seen it open and never would in this house. Her fingers came back to the fold, and the fold was where the two-finger sign happened, at the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} where the wool had ridden back from his wrist.

She made the sign before she knew she had made it. Two fingers crossed at the branching red edge, a breath, and then the hand moved on to straighten the wool as if nothing had been there. He had watched her do it on the day he was born, at the market's edge, in this room on other mornings, and she had never once looked at her own hand while she did it. The sign was not a word in \lore{Grathic} and had no name in any slate she kept. He set it on its own place.

“Stone seven,” she said. “The account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out.”

The date came over the top of the basket and covered the sign as neatly as a hand laid over a small flame. It was the date she had given the woman three doors down, and that woman was gone into the water now and nobody alive could correct what had been told her. He kept the sign and the date in two places and made no cause of them.

\scenebreak

“Does he take?” Sella asked the bench. That was how the question went in this house --- not to Alda, not about the child, but to the bench where the pot stood, as if the bench might report on it.

“Warm and breathing,” Alda said. “That's all I've set down.”

It was exact and it was not true. A full note is exact; a vessel that gives a full note can still be failing in a way nobody has tapped for. He knew the difference the way he knew his own hand was small. The words did what the date had done: laid a flat surface over a thing too large to carry, and went on lying there with nobody lifting it. She did not say the woman's name. She did not say the third day. The lie told for mercy was eleven days old and had curdled all the way through to a death nobody entered, and no living person could set it right.

“A cooper's seventh in \place{Mud End},” she said. “The potter's third child in \place{Stonemouth}. Last high water was the year the glaze froze in the pots. They take a turn when the graft settles, most of them. Then they come right.”

There had been no question left to stop her. The dates went on arriving faster than any question needed them, because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry and a woman who has carried four hundred births knows that a handle is the whole of what you are given. She said the last high water after there was no question left, and then a date for a birth in a house nobody in the room had been in, and the room went on being a room with a bench, a pot, a draught under the door and a gap in the figures that nobody entered.

He kept the order of her reaching as he kept everything in this house: the slate before the child, the date before the wonder, the sign before the date that covered it. Three objects in three places, and the places did not touch. He had no word in Grathic for any of them and no standing to ask what any of them meant.

\scenebreak

Alda took up a narrow slate from under her arm. She did not lay this one writing-down on the bench with the others. She held it against her shawl where the fold of cloth made a small dark pocket, and her hand went to work in the second hand that no Court physician had seen --- the hand that had been handed down from a maid in Y.H. 651 and added to every year since, a hand that wrote what the first hand struck out. The boy could not have read a word of it. He could read the order of the thing: the reaching for the narrow slate before the reaching for the child had been done once already this morning, and this was the second reaching, the one that went where no officer's thumb would look.

She wrote, and the slate turned face-down into the shawl, and the shawl went toward a floorboard in a house he could not name. A fifth page went under that floorboard. Four had gone before it and none had been read by anybody who could have done anything with what was on them. The tin went back inside her bodice, shut. The shut of it was a small sound, almost nothing, and it was the sound of the morning.

He had no hand to lift toward the shawl. He had no word in the district's tongue for a record kept where no Court could confiscate it, no way to ask which house held the floorboard, and no way to hand the gesture back to the woman who made it. He was the only person in the room who had seen the order of reaching, and he was the least use in it.

Sella stood a half-pace off. She did not ask what had been written. A turned slate and a half-pace stood between the two women in place of the arrangement they had made on the day the boy was taken and had kept every morning since, a door in it that neither of them opened. Alda did not offer the slate and Sella did not look at it. Each was careful with a child entered in two different accounts that had never been shown to him, and the care had the same shape as the coldness, and the boy lay under both of them with the fold at his ear.

\scenebreak

Beyond them \person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill with six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. \lore{the Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles the colour of the ridge that had turned the corner and been burned and called dealt with, and no scrubbing lifted it. He said nothing about the knife, because the yard had been told already. He agreed with the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. His broom went once over the road a man takes when a house wants a collector, and no collector went down it for the woman the brook took. He did not look toward the bed.

\person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the left little finger bent. He told a pot it would do. He told the wheel it did not care whose hands were on it, only if they were steady. Sella told the fire it was low, and the bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it before she had finished the sentence, so that she shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot. He did not say the room off the shed was cold and did not look toward the bed or the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed.

\person{Garl Vail} was absent and kept by objects, which in this house is a way of keeping a man that no Court can enter. His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door. The hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean and nobody filled it. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner for a year and two days was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and the proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to the basket: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. Behind the boy the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap.

Alda went out by the path into \place{the Clay Yard} with the birth-slates under her arm and the fifth note turned face-down under her shawl and the tin shut inside her bodice. She did not look back at the bed. She had done everything she had come to do and none of it could be undone. The boy lay where she had left him with the fold pressed too long at his shoulder and the sign covered with a date.

Earlier, before the short step and the clatter of slate, Sella had fed him barley gruel thinned with milk from the house's own dish, two fingers behind his ear, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and his mouth not wiped. She had not said who paid for the milk. The warmth had gone into him through the house's dish and had been entered in no slate in this house or any other. He had wanted the dish and could not reach it, and he had wanted the crust that was gone and could not reach that either. The empty place beside the one crust stayed as it had stayed through all the days of this month, eaten by nobody, given to nobody, taken by nobody anyone would name. He had set Sella's hand, Alda's hand and \person{Mira Silt}'s hand on three places on a tray and would not let the tray become a cause.

At the dusk bank Sella turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A bank is not an opening. She put the fold right without looking at his face and left him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. Outside, the yard went on with its buckets and its sweeping, and the district had one word for the water coming up under the ice and for the slack frame at the window and for the writing that frost had left on the stone lip, a word that let a person go home warm without owning the day. He had no word for any of it in the district's tongue and no hand to write one.

Under the Vail roof he lay with one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it, fifty-one days old, while a fifth note lay turned toward a floorboard in a house he could not name and the tin went shut down the yard inside a woman's bodice. Sella turned the coals with a broken pot and did not touch the mark. The fire behind him missed one breath in every four. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down.
