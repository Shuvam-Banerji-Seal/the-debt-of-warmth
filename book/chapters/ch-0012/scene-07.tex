\daymark{54}{Ilmen 25, Y.H. 813}

More hairlines showed in the ice of \place{the Ilm} before the light got down to \place{the yard well}, dark lines running in from the current and stopping a thumb's breadth short of the edge, so that the skin held by that much and no more. A man come down from the sill squatted and put a knuckle on the ice and took it away again, the way a man tests a pot before he puts his weight on the shelf. The hairlines went in and stopped. They did not go over. Under them the river moved in its dark veins, keeping its own time under a skin it had nearly outgrown. \person{Corin} lay in the basket in the lee of the well's little roof with the fold turned right under his chin, fifty-four days old, and saw the lines the way he had once seen a fault running under the skin of a limb: not as a sign, but as a thing with a depth and an end. He could not tell how deep. He could see where it stopped, which was a thumb's breadth from the edge, and the stopping was the part he kept.

A neighbour let the bucket down and the rope went over the lip without touching the white that had written there earlier in the month and was gone by noon of the same day; \lore{frost-bloom} came and went and left no slate the poorer. The woman who had said the ice held stood with her hands in her wash and said nothing, and the man who had called it honeyed kept his counsel, and between them a man come back from looking at the river held a number in his fist with the thumb tucked under. He set the fist on the stone lip, looked at it a breath, and thought better of the giving. A number goes into a slate, not into a well; somebody had said that at \place{the Cross} and the yard had kept the sentence without keeping the figure. The man put his fist in his sleeve and went off toward the road. The hairlines were weather by the time the bucket came up. Weather let a woman go home warm without owning the morning, and nobody in the yard owned it now.

\place{the Singing Sill} gave its low note before it was due. It came up through the boards under the well roof and through the floor of the house behind him, a note with no words and no owner, low enough that a man felt it in his teeth before he placed it in his ear. Word came down from \place{the Weir} that it had been heard upriver before the fires were lit, and a woman at the racks said it was the water running fast under the ice and nothing to make a song of. The boy set that early note in a place of its own. Beside it he kept the bell told in the dark and the bell heard in the square, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and beside those the kiln's own breath that missed one in every four. Four things that came low and arrived early or late or not at all, and he did not write a cause across any of them, because three likenesses laid in a row had once taught him in a better-lit room what a man can do with an answer he wants too much. The early note lay where he put it and did not join the bells.

\scenebreak

The number for what the thaw would cost went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder and arrived nowhere. A man brought it up from the road and passed it to a man at the racks, and the man at the racks passed it to a woman shifting slip, and the woman lifted a shoulder at him and said nothing at all. There was a shape in the morning where the figure should have stood, and the shape went around from hand to hand and was spent by nobody. A neighbour with her sleeves wet to the elbow said the pits would be worked early if the thaw came, and another said a late thaw was a longer week of a body's own fire. \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to the first and “Yes, house” to the second, and went back to his broom. He agreed so thoroughly with the early and the late that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. He owned no sentence a house could enter, and the account he kept most exactly was the one he never spoke. His small bench knife was on its sill with six years of the seven counted and the seventh held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. \lore{the Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what light there was and gave it back. He swept the road over and over without once turning along it, and the broom went and came and went. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same.

A weir woman come up from the river road had salt dried white at her wrists and in the cuffs of her sleeves, white as if she had dipped her hands in meal and forgotten to wash. She stood at the edge of the racks with her apron wet to the knee and said the thing plainly, which nobody had said plainly before. “That board's still in \place[the plank walk]{the walk},” she said. “Nobody's trodden it. It won't carry a man when the river comes up. They'll step on it and find out.” She did not say which board. She went back down toward the river with the salt white on her wrists and the cuffs of her sleeves, and the yard went on with its buckets.

The boy set the board on a tray of its own. Beside it he set the missing step at \place{the Fishstairs}, four, four and a landing where a step should have been, and beside that the hairline in the ice that ran in and stopped short of the edge. Three things with a place in them where a body's weight would go and no body's weight had gone. A board gives warning the way a joint does before it lets go: a small sag where the grain has run, a sound that changes under the foot, the way weight settles where it should not. He had once been paid to notice such things in the dark. Here he noticed them from a basket, and was paid nothing, and could not have put a foot on any of the three to test them. He kept them in three places and let none of them touch, and no man stepped on the board that morning and no accident was made of it. He had learned to hold a gap without filling it. It was the whole of the work available to him, and it bought him nothing he could lift a hand to.

\scenebreak

Behind \place{the drying racks} \place{the Slip Brook} ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should, the colour of a pot washed out into the water, and the women at the tubs said the pits had been worked early and called it the colour of the water. The boy kept four shapes in four places and did not let them touch: the white writing on the well-lip that came and went and was called \lore{frost-bloom}, the honeyed ice of a man's saying three weeks ago, these new hairlines stopping short of the edge, and this higher clay-heavy brook. Four witnesses to a thing the district had one word for, and the word let every woman go home warm. In \lore{Grathic} there was no word for the failure in a vessel and none for the thaw that was not a thaw until it had taken somebody; there was a word for weather, and weather was where a person put a day they did not want to own. He had no word of his own worth spending on it either.

\person{Sella Vail} had carried him down before the light and came across now with the little-door burn closing on the bucket handle and then on nothing, and set the basket in the well's shade out of the draught as she would set a pot that must not be slopped. She went at a half-pace from every open slate and around a slate propped at the rack end as wide as if it had been a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the bucket: “Easy. There's a skin on it.” She gave every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, and she said the house where another woman would say I. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a well is not an opening. When a neighbour asked her what the hairlines meant she told the fire it was a cold and that the fire would have to be fed before the thaw took the house. The neighbour went away warm without owning a day of it.

One crust lay in the fold where two had lain, and the empty place beside it had been carried through seven days without a name. Eaten by nobody, given to nobody, taken by nobody anyone would name; no third crust had come. The spent crust from Stone fifteen lay stiff past the smell of anything where it had always lain, unlifted and unnamed, and nobody had said either crust was gone. The boy had set Sella's hand, \person{Alda Reed}'s hand and \person{Mira Silt}'s hand on three places on a tray weeks ago and would not let the tray become a cause, because he had learned in a room with better light what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. The tray had not become one all week and did not become one now. He had wanted the crust with the whole of a child's appetite and had wanted the cause with the whole of the other appetite, and neither had been given him. He did not ask which hand had been near, which was not the same thing as not knowing that hands had been near. A woman was sixteen mornings dead in the clay-heavy rise behind the racks, uncollected, \person{Dessa Silt} with her number in her thumb and her name under her scar, and the omission had no word and had not made a doctrine of itself. The boy kept that absence on its own tray too, and did not join it to the empty place in the fold, though both were shapes with a thing missing.

The body humiliated him exactly, as it had every morning of fifty-four. A thread of drool froze at his mouth and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and cold arrived in its known order, cheek, then temple, then wrist, the wool doing less than the wind wanted. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the crust and came down. Reducing shoulders had been ordinary work in the life before this one; keeping his own hand on a bucket's rim was not, and the joke had not gone stale and had never once been funny enough to say aloud. He was the only adult at this well and the least use in it.

\scenebreak

At the turn of the wind Sella came for him and lifted the basket level, and carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at a half-pace from every open slate. Behind her Tarn's broom went over the trodden clay, going and coming, going and coming, without once turning along the road a man takes when a house wants a collector. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. She said nothing about the number that had gone from fist to mouth to shoulder, or the board in the walk, or the note that had come early, or where they had been. \person{Jorra Vail} came in from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger, and went to the damper before she had finished telling him the fire was low, the bent finger going in ahead of his hand. “You'll do,” he told a pot, and gave the end of her instruction to the clay. He did not say the back room was cold. He did not look toward the bed. \person{Garl Vail} was absent and kept by objects, which in this house is a way of keeping a man no Court can enter.

His salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on the peg behind the door and the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean and nobody filled either. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept, and one of his proverbs went to the pot and the fire and the other to the fire again, and neither went to the basket. Behind the boy the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The coal a brine-gull had taken upriver nine days ago was entered in no slate and missed in no account, and the fire had not missed it and the house had not missed it, and nobody had found a word for the not-calling of a collector for a woman gone sixteen mornings. Warmth left where it fell and the morning came; that was the whole of it, and there was no doctrine in it because nobody had made a speech.

He lay on his bed, fifty-four days old, with one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it carried through seven days. On the trays of his own the six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six, five from four sources and one from a person, beside the five and never among them, and no seventh came at the well or anywhere. The nearly full answer of Ilmen six lay on its own tray, advanced and not paid; the word said low into a basket, the shape of the forty-first knot and the weight of a thumb on wicker lay on three places apart; Mira's two cords stood on a board beside the other woman's \lore{knot-string} and the two boards did not touch. The board in the plank walk at the Weir stood beside the missing step and the hairline stopping short of the edge, and none of them touched the others. The early note lay with the told bell and the heard bell and the kiln's own gap, and those four did not join. The frost-bloom, the honeyed ice, the new hairlines and the higher brook kept four places that did not touch. Five unjoined pauses stayed unjoined. The count went on in him exactly as the house kept it badly, in loaves moved along a shelf and glances at a clay jar on the wall shelf with no figure spoken, and the figure the district had entered for the year had not entered him. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. The count went on.
