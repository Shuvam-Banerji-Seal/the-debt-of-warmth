\daymark{48}{Ilmen 19, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he frost} had written again on the stone lip of \place{the yard well} in the first thin light, white fronds standing up where \lore{the Warm} had lately gone out of the stone, a branching map laid down overnight in a hand nobody had hired and no slate would keep. It was the same shape that ran down his own ribs under the wool, the red flower the midwife had crossed herself at and called \lore{the birth-flower}; he had carried that shape for forty-eight days and had never been told what it counted. Here it stood on a well-lip in \place{Low Kiln} in white, as if a thing written on him had been written back onto the stone in the night. \person{Corin} lay in the basket with the fold turned right under his chin, and the fronds stood a finger high above the place where the bucket would come down.

A neighbour let the bucket down and the rope went over the lip without touching the fronds, the way a hand goes around a thing it has decided not to see. The woman at the tubs who had said all winter that the ice would hold stood with her wrists in the wash and said nothing at all now, and the man who had come up from the river in the warm spell and called the ice honeyed under the mud kept his counsel too and went off toward the road with his hands in his sleeves. They had had an argument once and the argument had ended in weather; now the weather had taken the argument and left nobody to carry it. \lore{frost-bloom} was the word the yard gave the writing on the stone, and the word let every woman go home warm without owning the morning.

A rack-woman came for water with a pot under her arm and stopped at the lip. “It'll go soft before its time,” she said, and looked at the fronds as if they had been written there by somebody who owed her money. The woman at the tubs did not answer. There was a place in the morning where a sentence used to stand, the one about the nights being cold and the ice holding, and neither woman filled it. The rack-woman set her pot down out of the draught, which is what a person does with a pot when there is nothing left to say over it, and let the bucket come to her. The boy registered the small silence with the exactness he had once brought to a chart: not an absence of sound but a space shaped like a sound, kept in its own place and not joined to the others.

\person{Sella Vail} came across with the little-door burn closing on the bucket handle and then on nothing, and set the basket down where the lip did not cast its shade. She looked at the white on the stone the way she looked at a thing that had been left where it lay, and gave the instruction to the fire before she gave it to the bucket. “It's the thaw,” she told the fire. To the bucket: “Easy. There's a skin on it.” She said the house where another woman would have said I, and she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a well is not an opening. The fire took the word and went on keeping what it kept, and the bucket came up with a little silt in it that everybody called the spring and nobody entered.

The window of the \place{Vail kiln-house} stood toward the yard with its oiled paper stretched in a frame of split lath, and the paper sat slack. It had lifted hard in a dry cold on the morning he came under the roof; now it hung in its frame without taking the wind, and the frame showed through it as a shape with nothing in it. \person{Tarn} saw the slack before the light did. He came past with the broom under his arm and \lore{the Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted, and his eye went a degree to the paper and back to the road, and he said nothing. A debt with a broom notices when a thing stops lifting and does not report it. The boy kept the slack frame and set it beside the honeyed ice and the white writing on the lip, three shapes the district had not entered and would call weather before the pot was cold. He had learned to read the edge of a thing without touching it, and reading was the whole of the work available to him.

The pale clip-ring at Tarn's left wrist caught what light there was and gave it back, and he put the broom to the dust under the window as if the slack paper were a thing a man sweeps around and not a thing he looks at. Six years of the seven were behind him and he held the seventh where he always held it, and there was no word in the yard for a frame that had stopped taking the wind. He did not say the paper had gone slack. He did not say the ice had been honeyed or that it would hold. He agreed with the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying. The boy kept the degree of his turning beside his own noticing and did not let the two touch. Two people under one roof had marked a thing and said nothing of it, and the house had no name for the equality.

\scenebreak

Sella carried the basket a few paces into the well's shade out of the draught and set it down as she set a pot that must not be slopped, and turned the fold right under his chin without looking at his face. The well stood at the low end of \place{the Clay Yard}, where the houses gave onto the racks and the ground went soft under the rope. One crust lay in the fold where two had lain. The spent one from Stone 15 was stiff past the smell of anything and lay where it had always lain, uneaten and unlifted and unnamed, and no third crust had come in all the days since. Beside the one crust was the empty place, and the empty place was the warmth he had most wanted and could least reach. He carried it into the new day as he had carried it across the last one, without a name and without a cause, the way a man carries a wound he has learned not to dress in words. \lore{Grathic} had no word for a thing taken and not spoken of, and he had no word of his own.

When the wind turned off the water the thin breath of barley that used to come from the smoky crust did not come. He had known the smell on the afternoon it stopped and he knew the shape of its absence now on the morning after, and the absence had the exactness of a wound. It was not the loss of heat, which is a number on a slate, but the loss of a thing, which is a place in the hand. He wrote no cause across it. He set Sella's hand, \person{Alda Reed}'s hand and \person{Mira Silt}'s hand on three places on a tray and would not let the tray become a cause, because he had learned in a room with better light what three likenesses laid in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. He did not think who had taken the crust, which was not the same thing as not knowing there were hands that might have taken it.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the crust and came down, which was the whole of the distance between the basket and the warmth he most wanted. He had once stood at a bedside and known, before anyone spoke, that a body had turned; the knowledge had come into his hands and not into his head. Here the knowledge came the same way and ended in the same place, an inch short. He did not try again. A man who has learned what an inch costs does not spend it twice before the fire is fed.

The ear held six hearings of \lore{the full note} --- five from four sources, a cup across the birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed} through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving under the iron tube at the collection, and the sixth from a person, out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted --- and the six stayed six with no seventh at the well or anywhere this morning. Beside them lay the nearly full note of Ilmen 6 on its own tray, advanced and not paid, neither a hearing nor the third thing. The word for nearly was not in the district's tongue and there was no word of his worth spending on it. He had no hand to tap either pot and ask it to say the thing again, and the ear that would have placed a flaw under a knuckle belonged to a body that could not lift an arm to its own chin.

The body humiliated him exactly, as it had every morning of his forty-eight days. A thread of drool froze at his mouth and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and cold arrived in its known order, cheek first, then temple, then wrist, the wool doing less than the wind wanted. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the bucket and came down. Reducing shoulders had been ordinary work in the life before this one; keeping his own hand on a bucket's rim was not, and the joke had not gone stale and had never once been funny enough to say aloud. He was the only adult at this well and the least use in it.

\scenebreak

Sella gave every instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. “Keep it off him,” she said to the wool; “it's the river wind.” To the bucket she said “That'll do” when it came up, and to the fire that the thaw would have to be fed before it took the house. She never once put the boy's name into a sentence that asked something of somebody. When a slate stood open propped at the end of \place{the drying racks} she went around it as wide as if it had been a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over, and she did not set the basket near it and did not say why. Behind the racks \place{the Slip Brook} ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should, and the road beyond went down toward \place{the Fishstairs} and the market, and neither the brook nor the road was entered in anything the boy could read.

A neighbour came toward the well with a slate under her arm and stopped a hand's breadth short of the basket, the way a person stops short of a pot that is doing work. “Whose house?” she asked, not of him. “The house,” Sella said, and gave the question to the fire: “Feed it before you feed yourself. It'll still be there in the morning. You won't.” The neighbour looked at the fronds, which the sun had begun to take off the lip, and went back to her tubs with the argument still unsettled. She had got no I from Sella and no name for the boy, and she went home warm without owning a day of it. He kept the order of Sella's reaching --- fire first, pot second, the instruction to the wool third and never to the basket --- on a tray of its own and did not let it become an answer.

At the turn of the wind she lifted the basket off the dust and carried him in. She gave the instruction to the wool on the way and said the house would be warmer inside, which was true of the house and was not a promise to him. Under the Vail roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. Behind the door \person{Garl Vail}'s salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the hollow seat kept its twenty-year lean and nobody filled either, and the clean dark line where the Ash had turned its corner was not swept, because a line left where it is is still a line kept. One of his proverbs went to the pot and another to the fire, and neither went to the basket.

From the bed he could see the inner edge of the window and the frame standing with its paper slack, a rectangle with a little air in it. In the birth room and the death-house two doors down and the Court's room heard through a doorway there were a bench and a pot and a draught along the floor, and here there were the same bench, the same pot, the same draught and the same gap. Six rooms with a gap in them, and he kept them in six places and had not written a cause across any one. The slack frame lay in a place of its own and did not join them.

Behind him the carried fire kept under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and taking its breath in fours. Four, four and a gap. It had kept that count through the burning of the ridge and the taking of a woman into the water and the going-out of one crust where two had lain, and it would keep it after this house was cold and another house had the keeping of it. The coal that had gone up the river on the back of a brine-gull was entered in no slate and missed in no account, and the fire had not missed it and the house had not missed it. Nobody in the district had found a word for the not-calling of a collector for the woman the brook took, and nobody had made a doctrine of it; the omission lay where it fell and the morning came, and neither was entered.

The boy lay on his bed, forty-eight days old, with one crust in the fold and an empty place beside it, the oiled paper slack in the window behind him and the fire missing one breath in every four. The frost on the lip would be gone by noon and the yard would call it weather, the woman who used to say the ice held would go on saying nothing, and the man who had called it honeyed would keep his counsel. He kept the slack frame and the white writing and the missing smell in three places that did not touch; the six hearings stayed six, the nearly-full note lay on its own tray advanced and not paid, and the three hands stayed on their tray and did not become a cause. Nothing had been joined or paid or explained. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. The count went on.
