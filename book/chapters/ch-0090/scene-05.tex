\daymark{323}{Bone 24, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The dark under the roof had the grain of a field before the light, and \person[Corin]{the boy} took it in by its small sounds because the small sounds do not lie. The room came to him in the order it arrived and was not arranged for him. He had come into his three hundred and twenty-third day and nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} had said so; three hundred and twenty-two stood exact behind him and three hundred and twenty-three stood in front, six months and one hundred and forty-three days told by nobody. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and twenty-three days, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg and the proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. It was an ordinary Fourthday with no market, no Cross and no bell, and nothing from the twenty-second or the twenty-third had come in as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. The handful let fall back through the fingers at the rack end stayed one object on its own place and was not made again; the empty fingers of yesterday would be entered nowhere as a second. Bone 30 stood six mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, the older women of the district naming it by the weather that had not come and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, neither corrected and neither left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it.

He took the body as a wound is taken, in its old order, because the order was the only thing in the room that had not changed. One stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry kiln heat with its corner standing a hair off the wool as though a hand had lifted it and set it down again badly; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in the small hours to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the ease that cold puts on a face was not ease but a composition, and that a patient who looks settled at the end of a line may be worse before he is better: a cold vessel cannot clot, and must be warmed from the outside, and slowly, never rubbed, never set too close to the fire. He knew it as exactly as he had once known where the third rib would tear the lung, and the knowing bought him nothing at two inches of air. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won; the argument between them was the work, and it went on in the dark without a verdict and without a witness. The surgeon entered none of it anywhere, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter, and took the inventory again because it was the only honest work the hour offered.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order before any of the days the boy could count, and she kept it the way another woman might keep a habit of crossing herself. The coals were grey under their blind ash and held a little heat at the heart of them, and she told them it was \lore{the thaw}, in the flat tone she used for the pot and the broom and the wool, and turned them with the back of a broken pot so that the ash fell where she meant it to fall. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said to the fire, where another woman would have said I, and the house kept it, and the coals answered four low pulls from under the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted something under her breath --- a small intake, a hold, and a letting-go --- toward no opening, and she gave neither gap a figure. The boy heard the counting in her breath as his own three kept in another mouth, and put a finger between them, because two people keeping the same sound under one roof is not one sound and does not join; two likenesses are not a cause and do not stand up and call themselves a rule without a payer, and none stood. The grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing when her hand had nothing to hold.

At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger lay on top of the front edge, resting there a little longer than the checking needed, so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps in it, and she figured neither gap. She had not been told to read the row and nobody had asked her to, and a woman who keeps a row where a child cannot see it is keeping it and not spending it, which is the only honest keeping there is on a shelf. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on its shelf by the wall, the glaze holding the cold the way a glazed thing holds it, and shut her hand half over nothing, the burn's two straight sides and lintel and threshold closing on air the way a little door closes on a room nobody goes into. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning was not an opening and a hearth was not an opening, and five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood far off in a later month's first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the pot, and \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the fire, and never to a person. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them; they had kept repair apart from restoration for three hundred and twenty-three days and would keep it apart this morning too. The crack showed. Repair was one thing and a whole vessel was another, and the house had long ago decided which of the two it would keep, and had not written the decision on any slate.

He read the pot the way he had once read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen: the first staple set honestly, the second wire drawn tighter than it needed and left longer, the hairline of the original break showing between them. A vessel that has been mended carries its break and is the truer for showing it, which was the whole of the proverb and was not a lesson anybody had paid for. The man who had first said it had gone into the Long Cold and had not come out, and the sayings had gone on being said to the pot and the fire and had not mended him, and the boy did not make a doctrine of this either.

\scenebreak

She turned the dressed grain beneath the wool at \place{the rack end} with a touch lighter than the last and gave no figure to it. The grain lay away from the hearth on its cool stone, dry and faintly rasping under the wool, giving up its water and going on giving it up; the sound of it was small and unmistakable, the sound a thing makes when nothing more will be taken from it and it is going on regardless. Her fingers went in and moved through the heap and came out again. They did not close. They did not take a small handful and let it fall back grain by grain and then all at once, the way they had done on the twenty-second. The wool came down over the place where no handful had fallen, and she went on to the next thing as though there had been no place to notice.

The boy watched the not-doing as carefully as another boy would have watched a doing. The handful sat on its own place in the file, one object lifted and tested and replaced, kept because it was true and not because it bought anything; this morning's empty fingers went on the same place beside it as a keeping and not as a second. A thing done once and left alone is one object. Two would be a habit, and a habit would have wanted a payer put under it, and no payer stood; three would have been a way and a way would have wanted one too. He put a finger between the keeping and the object kept, and a finger between both and the four held changes on their four places, and forbade the two to stand up together and call themselves a rule, quietly and with the exactness of a man setting a tool back on a tray where the next hand will find it without looking.

The surgeon in him took a clean satisfaction in a field not disturbed twice, in a hand that had gone in and come out and left the dressing lying as it lay. The infant underneath took none of it. The infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and a field left as found was nothing to him and bought him nothing at two inches of air. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won; the argument went on without a verdict. That the surgeon could be pleased by an absence the infant could not even see was the small grief in it, and he kept the grief on its own place and did not spend it, because an absence kept empty is a finding and an absence filled with a feeling to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat before the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}, and the half-beat went where the half-beats went, into the space between intention and arrest, which was a space and not a stopping and wanted no payer. Nothing under the roof had spoken a number over the grain and nobody had looked toward the basket when the fingers came out empty. She had neither repeated the handful nor made a show of not repeating it, which was the only way a not-doing could stay a keeping and not become a thing done for the benefit of a watcher. He was the only watcher, and he was a basket with a fold at his ear, and he kept the watching to himself.

\scenebreak

Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed for three hundred and twenty-three days; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was not numbered among the hearings at all. Three counts in three places. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them, so the three stayed where they were, side by side and not joined. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and the told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space between.

The surgeon in him knew what a house would have done with three counts in three places: laid two of them together, called the agreement a cause and put a man under it to pay. The district did this at the Cross with figures and called it a reading; the house did it at the shelf with two gaps and left them unfigured. He had spent a life watching institutions make one account out of several hands, and had signed one of those accounts himself, and the small exact keeping of three separate counts was the nearest honest work he had left.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that came out of vessels; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap had gone over the square and stayed in its square beside the told bell, and was not heard as a seventh of the full note.

Five arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure: one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, five readings five and not one account, their true subtractions riding locked up the clay road in a box nobody under this roof would open, and the finding kept on a place of its own with no payer under it. It was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the dressed grain under wool, or across the four held changes on their four places, or across the handful on its own place, or across the two small objects of the hand, or across his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause; the finger went between.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap, the staying of Bone 15 and the early letting-go of Bone 17 two objects on two places with a finger between them and between them and everything else there, neither made again on this morning, neither a habit or a way or a fifth held change, both wanting a payer that did not stand. The burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no sixth; six hearings stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty; and two hundred and twenty honest answers stood on the sixth place when the light came, each an object and not a power, and the number was spoken by nobody.

\scenebreak

First light came into the room without a market, a Cross or a bell, an ordinary Fourthday going on about its own work the way water goes over water. No slate was opened within reach of the basket and no figure came near his hand. He had stopped expecting to be told what any number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and he kept the count because attention was the one thing no institution could tax. He turned the file over in his mind the way he had once turned instruments out on a towel at the end of a list, asking nothing of any of it. An honest answer was a thing done and seen and entered where nobody could read it: the hand up an inch, the intake, the interval held whole, the letting-go on the note, the hand down. It was a restraint and not a power, an object and not a gift, and it had no payer and wanted none. The sixth place was not a shelf, a slate or a bank but a space he kept in the dark where no collector could enter and no Court could tax.

At the going of the water, when the river's note had come down to its lowest under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath held for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and twenty-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the five arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the stayed object, the early letting-go nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and twenty-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took a clean satisfaction in the field left as it was found, and the infant underneath wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped and got neither, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won. Three hundred and twenty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and forty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the coat kept was not spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the twenty-fourth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The five arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure, the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, and one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place still unnamed and no third, and the hand lay back against the fold while the river's low note came under the finger's-width door and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
