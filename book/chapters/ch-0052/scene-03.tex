\daymark{208}{Ember 29, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

A new day came on under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} without a boundary drawn in it, and the first thing the boy knew of it was a smell --- hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under the wool the sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where he had been set, in the room that did not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one leathery brown-edged crust two inches from it and its empty place still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He was two hundred and eight days old and six months and twenty-eight days, and nobody said so, and he had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. There was no roof under the pale sky that had a word for the number, and the house kept its days in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody called a figure. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and eight days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth should have stood. Three counts in three places. None rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them.

The surgeon's register read the order of a body taking its heat back and found it exact, and the exactness bought nothing at two paces. He wanted the crust, and he wanted the pot's handle where it would be set out later, and he wanted somebody to say the number the way another man might say the weather; the wanting was loud in the small body and quiet in the old one, and the two wants sat side by side without either paying for the other. The room held its warmth the way a cup holds water with a hairline in it: carefully, and with a place nobody would mention until the thaw.

Before first grey the word went around the boards again, spoken over them and not to the basket. A voice came up from \place{Slate Row} on the river wind and said that a slate had burned in the night; no owner was named, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason, and the voice went on to the price of barley and the weather as if the burning were a thing like any other thing the morning brings. Nobody in the yard turned it into a cause. The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound with no ball in it: a place it came from, a thing said to have happened there, a mouth that carried it, and a row of prices it went on to, each part laid where it belonged and none of them given a hand at the foot of it. He filed it again on the place of its own beside the dust off \person{Jorra Vail}'s forearms and the two tellings of the room shut in the war, with a hand between the three. It was a second telling of a direction and a second is not a third; the shut room stayed at two and got no third from a burned slate, and nothing in \place{Grath} that keeps a slate would have known where to enter it, for a cause is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood here.

\scenebreak

Jorra came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He had gone quieter since the word began to go around, and the quiet went before him into \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the way a cold air goes before a door; the household straightened around it without an order given, the way a room straightens when a man comes in with weather on his coat. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man who came up on a wrong wind out of \place{High Kiln} and said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The man took Jorra's silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying; the saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off the kiln on that same wrong wind, touched the fold and went out on the wind that had brought it, answered as a direction and entered nowhere: no second edge paid so soon after the carrying-out, no cause named in \lore{Grathic}, no collector called. The boy laid it where such things go and wrote nothing across it. It came and went the way its kind came and went, and he did not make it stand up beside the burned slate and ask the two what they wanted.

\scenebreak

Sella carried him out into \place{the Clay Yard} before the heat went off the high air, at the half-pace, with woodsmoke before her in long low bars and the river wind behind with the taste of water over water. At \place{the yard well} a rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the pots would want before the last market; she had come up from the racks with her hands still caked and set the question down level, the way a woman sets down a pot she means to carry away again. \person{Sella Vail} sent her to the pot and the weather. “Ask the pot what it wants,” she said. “Ask the weather. The house has said it.” The woman went away level with her question still in her hand, nothing slopped, priced, refused or entered, and the carried fire went on under the roof behind them, giving four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been. \place{the trough} stood bare and dark beside the well with its skin gone and its water called weather, and a cost asked of the firing and answered with the pot and the sky is a cost that never arrives at a figure; the surgeon's register filed it where such things go and wrote no cause across it.

Near the well a man come off \place{the clay road} kept his number shut in his fist. He asked what the kiln would want now the figure had gone up twice, and did not open the fist, and paid nothing. The printed one in twelve stood unchallenged where it had stood since the second reading, and the true subtraction sat in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box up the road, out of reach of every hand in the yard; a figure read aloud twice over a basket was still not the figure paid. The man shut his fist a little tighter and went off toward the gate with his number in it, and the boy, who could not have lifted that fist or counted what was held inside it, kept the shut number on a place of its own and did not bring it into the same room as the burned slate. Two directions going off in two hands are two directions going off in two hands, and a row of such hands standing long enough will call itself an account if a man lets it; he let neither stand.

The surgeon's register read the closed fist the way it reads a dressing left too long: a hand held tight enough to whiten the knuckles tells you the size of the grip and nothing at all of what is under it, and a man who will not open his hand has answered the question in the only way he means to. At two paces the yard could be sorted in ninety seconds --- the rack woman with her hands given away by the pale slip, the man with his number shut where no clerk could read it, the kiln hands going about a heat that would not touch either of them --- and the sorting bought nothing, because there was no hand in the basket to carry any of it to a slate. He had once been made to sort a crowd that size in ninety seconds and be right about most of it; here he sorted a yard he could not enter, and got nothing for it. The competence was intact and useless, and it lay in him beside the child's simple want of the crust. The knot of the fist went up the road with its owner and the pale slip went back to the racks, and the yard went on being a yard where a cost could be asked and answered and left lying where it fell.

\scenebreak

Under the roof again, with the morning going on around the basket, Sella came and stood with her back to it and gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and moved her lips along the loaf row on \place{the loaf shelf}, which stood with two unnamed gaps, and gave nobody a figure; kept her finger on the top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal beneath lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, the beat she had held under it long ago standing where it had been left; glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and shut her hand half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire the thaw was come and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath --- small intake, hold, letting go --- toward no opening that came. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning or afternoon since; a morning was not an opening and there had been no opening in twenty-eight days. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them; it wanted the pot and it wanted the crust, and the half-beat was a thing no slate would ever be asked to carry.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and the unforged sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford lay beside it. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark; \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, the word waiting on the far side of his teeth; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. No collector came up or went down the clay road. The house kept its debt and the road kept its ruts, and the two did not meet in any figure anybody had been asked to believe.

\scenebreak

In the morning, with the yard's words still going over the boards and arriving nowhere, she set him down in the fold at the shelf's low angle and gave no instruction and no reason to the basket. He put the question to his hand. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house at that hour, only the rhythm that came through the door, and the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down at once, before the small intake had finished, against the fold. The asking is not always answered and there is no failure in the miss and no triumph in the answer; an inch that comes down at once is an object on its own tray and no less than an inch that stays. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them, and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule.

The burned-slate word went over the boards behind him and the man's shut number went over the boards with it, and neither came down into the basket. Two hundred and eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; the carried fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and eight days; fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places that did not become one. The hand lay down against the fold, the thread at his mouth still frozen and unwiped, one stiff leathery crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the boy kept his own count without a figure and went on being carried.
