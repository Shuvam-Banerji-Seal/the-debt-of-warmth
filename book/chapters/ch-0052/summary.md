## Chronicle

Three days went out at the Vail kiln-house as the kiln month spent its last working week and came to its last market, unmarked. The boy in the fold was two hundred and seven days old on the first morning and two hundred and nine on the third, and no slate had a place to enter it. The fire missed its fifth; the river kept a low note never counted among the hearings; the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot.

Sella Vail gave her instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom, never to the basket; she moved her lips along a two-gapped row and gave nobody a figure, said the house would keep it where another woman would say I, set the pot rim out two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed; the widow's mark was untouched. Garl was kept in objects, not in a story --- the salt coat, the hollow seat, the unswept line, the debt without a name --- with no lesson made and no collector called.

A second word went around the boards of a slate burned in Slate Row, with no owner, room, hand at the flame or reason; the boy took it apart. Jorra Vail grew quieter, named none of the men who went into the back room, told a pot it would do, and held his bent finger in the cold damper until Sella's sentence reached the pot. Tarn brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped.

On the third day the district's arithmetic changed for the last time. Two hundred leaned to the reading block while eight pigs went over the wet boards, and the bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. Berr Clay came up short off the clay road, wiped his thumb twice, passed a finger over the figure and read One in twelve, as the notice says, low into the crowd; he said the levy was called and not by him, and went off with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness. Hessa Rale carried his slate up the road unread and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set below the bridge; a figure read aloud was still not the figure paid.

In the small hours, with Sella gone to her bed and no breathing number in the house, the boy put the question to his hand. Under the conditions that had once given a holding, the hand went up an inch toward the river's note and came down at once against the fold --- a miss, and no failure in it. He filed it on its sixth place; the file was exact, complete and worth nothing. Ember went out unmarked, and the fire went on in the room that warmed him while the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

Sella has gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves.
