\daymark{209}{Ember 30, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

The last dark of the kiln month came down under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof without a boundary drawn in it, and the boy knew it first by a smell --- hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under the wool the sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the room that did not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one leathery brown-edged crust two inches from it and its empty place unnamed and no third standing up to take the place beside it; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He was two hundred and nine days old and six months and twenty-nine days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} that would have had a place to enter it if somebody had. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and nine days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. None rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The surgeon's register went over the order of a body taking its heat back and found it exact, and the exactness bought nothing at two paces. He wanted the crust and he wanted the pot's handle and he wanted somebody to say the number the way another man might say the weather; the child's want was loud and the old one quiet, and the two sat side by side without either paying for the other. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

The dark went on toward its end without a mark drawn at the turning of the month. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, in a glance at a jar cold under its glaze, in a space at the row's end nobody called a figure; the boy kept his in a number exact enough to set edge to edge against all of them and useless enough that nobody had ever asked for it. Two hundred and nine stood complete, unspent and unspoken in the dark of the wool. He had put the expectation of being told away long ago, though the wanting of it went on living under the expectation the way a coal goes on living under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. The last market of the kiln month waited beyond the finger's-width door without being brought into the room; the carried fire missed its fifth under the floor and the river kept its fifth through the door, and the boy lay where he had been set with the crust at his ear and the empty place beside it still without a name, carried into the last day before anybody under any roof had said what the day was.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down in the morning at the half-pace, before the heat went off the high air and while \place{the clay road} still held the damp of the night in its ruts. Woodsmoke lay before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of water over water, and between the two she kept the basket level the way a woman carries a pot filled to the lip and does not look into it to see whether it has slopped. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that knew the weight and did not ask the cost; it had carried the boy down this road and up it through every Account-day of his life and had never once turned the errand into a question. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. She gave the basket no reason for the walk and no word of what would be read over it, and she had given it no word of what had been read over it at the last market, and the two silences were one silence kept in two places without a seam between them. At every open slate on the way she kept her half-pace and went wide, and no slate came near the fold.

At \place{the Cross} she set the basket down in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, with the cold iron of the post at his cheek, and stood half a pace off. It was the third and last Account-day of Ember and the last market of the kiln month, and the square had come up around \place{the reading block} while they walked. She said nothing of the day to the basket and gave it no instruction and no reason, and the instruction she gave went to the wool, which was to keep the river wind off his ears, and to the wind itself, which was to go around and not under. Her burn was shut on nothing. She did not look into the basket, and she never had.

The crowd came in the way a market comes, by stalls and by the pig rail and by the turnips heaped where men left figures side by side without making them agree, until two hundred stood leaning toward the reading block under the post. Eight pigs went over the wet boards with the mud throwing up in little fans behind them, and a man with a slate followed every pig and put a mark down for each. The bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, longer than a church bell and emptier, and the boy kept the heard bell on its own place beside the told bell out of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap in both, and laid nothing between them. He could not have lifted his chin to the bell or turned his head to the pigs; he lay in the thin shade with the iron at his cheek and took the square in by the part of it that came to him --- the shouts going over the top of him, the wet thud of boards, the low voice that would be spent on the crowd in a moment and was not yet spent. A figure would go twice over the basket before the morning was out, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and a figure read twice is still not the figure paid.

The bell had gone four, four and a gap at the opening of the market and would go again at its close, and the boy had kept both tellings of it on separate places since the Long Night, four, four and a gap in the dark told over the fire, four, four and a gap in the morning heard over the pigs. Nobody crossed himself at either. A told bell and a heard bell are two objects and a man who laid them together would want a payer for the gap, and there was no payer standing to either. The crowd leaned toward the block with its backs to the shade where the basket sat, and the post kept its finger's width of iron against his cheek, cold enough to be a thing and not a promise. He lay inside the sound of the market the way a man lies inside a fever: aware of it in the order it came, unable to put a hand to any part of it. The pig man's slate took its eight marks and went on taking them; the turnips kept their figures side by side; the thin shade kept the boy. None of these things was asked to agree with any other, and none did.

\scenebreak

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road to the reading block under the post a little before the top of the morning. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat and not worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} sat at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean, and he wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, as a man scrubs before a field he means to leave clean. He held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned to his sleeve, passed a finger over the figure, and let his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks that a number is still where he put it. Then he read “One in twelve, as the notice says” low into the top of the crowd, and the crowd leaned in because a low voice in a moot makes people lean, and the figure went twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that came late. It went over the top of him and not into him. He did not look into the basket and had never looked into it, and the boy lay in the thin shade with the iron at his cheek and heard a figure said aloud that had nothing to do with the figure paid.

“The levy is called,” he said. “It is not called by me.”

He said “your house” where another man would have said I, and gave the household the sentence and kept the speaker out of it, taking himself out of his own words as neatly as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. It was the grammar of charts and signatures, and the boy knew it the way a man knows the smell of his own trade on another man's coat: clean, reasonable, and with no hand left in it to hold what it had opened. Berr's twice-wiped thumb went on a tray of its own with the other record-first hands, beside the midwife's reach for a slate before a child, the thane's finger over a figure before the crowd, the clerk's number entered before the eyes found the man who had said it, the wheel-keeper's word to the wheel before a pot, Sella's finger on the shelf's front edge, and the runner who would come presently with a slate turned to her sleeve; eight hands going to the record before the person, and a row of eight standing long enough will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing and wants an answer. He let none of them stand. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of them.

Berr went off toward \place{High Kiln} with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The arithmetic of the district changed for the third time in the kiln month where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back afterwards; the printed figure stood unchallenged where it had stood since \lore{the Opening of the Slates}, and a figure read aloud was still not the figure paid. The bell went on with its four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself at the gap, and the boy kept the heard bell beside the told and let them lie there. The reading had answered nothing for the glaze-room hand carried out on Day 188. It had given no figure, called no collector, opened no slate, named no name and no cause in \lore{Grathic}, and had invented no offstage death for a man who had gone up the road breathing and gone on breathing. The pale line at that hand's gum stayed on its own place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and the boy laid no instruction across it.

\person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road toward \place{Greybank} before noon, and she did it the way she had done it on every Account-day of the firing and the kiln months: edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve, her hand arriving at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round, and the second strap, let out years ago for the long road, settled itself without being asked. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf which she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake; her thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was. Two women who had come up from the stalls asked what she carried. “I don't read it,” she said. “I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the one who wanted more than the question she had put she said, “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” answering the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. She was a runner and not a reader, and her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, advanced and not paid, joined to none of the other hands and not set down where a clerk could find it. The boy did not file her twice. He had learned that eight likenesses must be held apart by hand, and that an eighth made into a rule would want a payer and no payer stood to any of them.

Below \place{the north bridge} at \place{the Fishstairs}, on \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, entering a buyer's number before he looked up to find the man who had said it. Nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. The market sold its pots and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the boy, carried in his strip of shade with the cold iron at his cheek, kept the inward order of that reaching on a place of its own beside the runner's outward one: two records going inward are two records going inward, and two such records standing beside eight such hands will call themselves a keeping if a man stands them up long enough and asks them what they mean. He did not stand them up. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of something, and ahead of something is a direction; he kept the shape and made no rule of it.

The surgeon's register read the twice-told figure and the taking of the speaker out of his own sentence together, as a chart is read: a clean hand, a deduction, a signature, and nobody's warmth in the margin to say who had paid. At two paces the square could be sorted in ninety seconds --- the thane with the green at his mouth and the cord folded under his coat, the runner with the writing turned inward, the clerk at the out-of-line landing with his cuff gone grey twice over, the pig man following his eight, the women with their figures left standing among the turnips --- and the sorting bought nothing, because there was no hand in the basket to carry any of it to a slate. He had once been made to sort a crowd that size in ninety seconds and be right about most of it; here he sorted a market he could not enter and got nothing for it. The competence was intact and useless, and it lay in him beside the child's simple want of the crust. The market did not get to read the inch, and no payer was named to any of it.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} the sixth green stayed a colour in the crease beside a silent woman's lip, turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it had stood, and went off without a name and without being joined to any ear; at the market's edge the foundling stayed a shape in cast-off clothes with a green sliver going dull at his throat, a head stopped half a beat toward a pot and a thumb to the sliver, no loaf passing, no slate opened within reach of the basket and no sale made. No seventh green came to Ember's last market and no seventh sighting of the shape came with it, and the boy put neither name to either although he knew both names and had kept them in the place where he kept what he would not spend. The reading had been given and taken and gone up the road, and the district's arithmetic stood changed where two hundred had heard it and could not be changed back. The crowd leaned and straightened and went back to its pots and its pigs. The bell's gap had nobody crossing himself, the heard bell lay beside the told, and the red wool cord went up the clay road folded square under Berr's coat with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, while the basket sat in the post's thin shade and the boy two hundred and nine days old went on being carried.
