\daymark{207}{Ember 28, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

\dropcap{H}{e lay} where the fold had put \person[Corin]{the boy} down, in the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and the day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound. The smell was hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season's name instead of a cause. The kiln drew in fours beyond the wall, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short; then the gap. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by any decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible; a bell is rung and counted and answered, but the space where a fifth pull should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and seven days, and no payer had been named to it. He had learned to take the gap as the sound and the four as the thing that led up to it. The last working week ran on into its eighth morning and nobody under this roof had drawn a line under the running; a household that keeps its days in loaves and glances has no use for a boundary a slate would call the turning of a tend.

He was two hundred and seven days old and six months and twenty-seven days, and nobody under any roof said so. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same thing as not wanting it; the wanting had gone quiet enough that he could no longer hear it asking, but it was there in the dark as a hand is there under a blanket. Two hundred and seven stood exact, complete and unspent, as the days before it stood, a count kept in a body that could not lift its own head. The fire's fifth had had no payer for the whole of that count. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings because it came from water and not from a vessel; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. None rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them. The two rooms did not touch. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold, stiff and gone dark at the edges, and the empty place beside it had no name and no third came to stand in it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of its own order. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order of it as a man knows the order of a thing he has tried and failed to save: the extremities give first, the core holds on after, and the almost-smile is a grimace and not peace. He did not want the almost-smile. He wanted the crust, and the pot handle two inches beyond his hand, and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. The dark gave him none of the three and did not consider the matter. The surgeon's register read the order of a body taking heat back as exactly as it had once read a chart, and the reading bought him nothing at two paces.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and seven days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on a wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid; the second reading had gone up the road in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside, and \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood, Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, the word waiting on the far side of \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn}'s teeth; the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach; the loan that could hear was uncalled. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161, the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish; the beat that stayed under \place{the loaf shelf} on Day 166; the flagstone half-check of Day 171. The scrape from \place{the Debt Loft} stood on a fourth, heard only as a sound with a direction; the pale line of the carried glaze-room hand on a fifth; and the held inch on a sixth with all its answers. Beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, lay the burned-slate word: a direction that had come from a place, passed over boards and arrived nowhere, with no owner, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason named. He laid nothing across it. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, no third gap, no fourth likeness and no finger of his drawn across them. Nothing in the keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and nothing had been filled in to make a row come out even. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing.

\scenebreak

Before first grey \person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket. The instruction went to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and the running water and the river wind, and never once to the child lying beneath the roof receiving it. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would say I, and kept the half-pace from every open slate that she had kept on every morning of the boy's life, and did not say whose house or whose child or whose hundred. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, wiped the spoon on her sleeve and not his mouth, and left the thread where it was.

At the shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board lay unread, uncounted and uncopied, and the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire that the thaw was come, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every morning of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no hearth, shelf, well, rack, basket, yard, morning or dusk since. A morning was not an opening and there had been no opening in twenty-seven days. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

A neighbour came to the door with a cost-question about the firing, how much the kiln would want now the last working week was running out. Sella sent her to the pot and the weather. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.} The woman went away level with her question still in her hand, nothing priced, refused or entered, and the carried fire went on giving four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and seven days. A cost asked as a direction and answered with the pot is a cost that never arrives at a figure, and the surgeon's register filed it where such things go and wrote no cause across it.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back.

To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. To want a thing and to agree against it in the same breath is not a contradiction in this house; it is the whole method of it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the word waited on the far side of his teeth; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. A thing held nine times is still owed, and the silence under the roof was not peace but the shape of a debt held at the teeth. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} that morning.

At the wheel-shed \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one working day held on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He had grown quieter since the word had gone around the boards from \place{Slate Row}, and the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from the man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson; the man went off up the yard with his saying and his ignorance and no pot was lost. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and lay on neither of the other two places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, mercy and mutilation in the same mouthful, with no word in Grathic that would carry both, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

The word of the burned slate went around the boards of the yard once more, spoken over them and not to the basket, touching everything and belonging to nothing. Nobody said whose slate it had been or which room it had stood in or why it had been worth the burning; nobody named a hand at the flame or a reason. It had come from a place, had passed over boards, and had arrived nowhere. He kept it on a place of its own beside Jorra's dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three and nothing laid across them, and did not make a cause of it or a third telling of the room that had been shut in the war. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Then he put the question to his hand. Sella had gone about the fire and the yard and the coals were left to keep themselves, and there was a breathing number at the hearth in the room that did not touch his, small intake, hold, letting go. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down before the counted breath had finished. It had gone up on other mornings and answered other ways; this morning the asking was not answered and there was no failure in the miss. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them, and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a sign. It was an inch an infant body gave and refused, worth exactly what an inch is worth in a household that keeps its accounts in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe.

The morning went on around the small asking of the hand. The salt-stiff coat kept Garl Vail's broader shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the seat below it kept its twenty-year hollow and nobody sat in it to fill it, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it kept their unswept places, the proverb had gone to the pot, and the debt against a firing of tiles went around the room without a name, worse for being uncalled because it could be neither paid nor settled nor stopped. No lesson was made of any of it and no collector was called up the clay road; the dead man's name had not been spoken in two hundred and seven days. The widow's mark hung where it hung. The crust lay stiff two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at the mouth frozen and unwiped, and the river went on over water in the room he could not enter without noticing that an inch had gone up toward it. Two hundred and seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; the fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and seven days; the count went forward.
