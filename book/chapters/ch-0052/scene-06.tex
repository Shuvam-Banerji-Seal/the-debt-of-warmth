The market went out of the square the way heat leaves a room --- by degrees, and with nobody calling the hour. The two hundred leaned and straightened and went back to their pots and their pigs; the turnips kept their figures side by side without agreeing; the pig man's slate took its last marks and shut. The bell would go four, four and a gap at the close and nobody would cross himself at the gap. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade with the cold iron at his cheek while the figure that had gone twice over him went up \place{the clay road} in a locked box and was not brought back. \person{Sella Vail} lifted the basket when the light had thinned to the colour of wet wool and carried him up at the half-pace, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, saying nothing of the square or the thane's low voice or the cord folded square under a coat. The carrying arm knew the weight and did not ask the cost; it had brought him to every reading of his life and had never once turned the errand into a question.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, and the afternoon went on around him without ending. Heat went off the high air first, up through the finger's-width door and the chinks between the boards, and stayed longest in the low places: under the shelf, behind the pot, and in the fold at his ear where one stiff leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches away with its empty place unnamed and no third coming to stand beside it. The underside of the board made a roof that stopped where his hand stopped, and the warmth came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same edge, so that the space between the roof's edge and the pot's handle was the whole of his country. First smell hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under the wool the sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. First sound the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell.

He was two hundred and nine days old and six months and twenty-nine days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} with a place to enter it. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register went over the order of a body taking its heat back: exact, complete, worth nothing at two paces.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and nine days; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. None rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The two rooms did not touch: the fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. The kiln month's last working week ran out while he lay there. No voice under the roof corrected either of the district's two names for the time, and no boundary was drawn at the going out of the month; the days went on as loaves go along a shelf, pushed by no hand that would admit to the pushing.

Sella came and stood with her back to the basket. She did not look into it, and she had not looked into it on any day of his life. Her lips moved along the loaf row, which stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 --- and she gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied; the beat she had held under it on Day 166 stayed where it had been left. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire that the thaw was come, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, hearth, well, rack, road, basket, yard, market, morning, afternoon or dusk since. There had been no opening in twenty-nine days. Her private roll stayed unshown, and \person{Dessa Silt}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them. It wanted the pot and it wanted the crust and it wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted; the wants sat in him side by side, the child's loud and the old one quiet, and neither paid for the other. A cost asked as a direction and answered with the pot is a cost that never arrives at a figure; the surgeon's register filed it where such things go and wrote no cause across it.

The keeping of \person{Garl Vail} went on around him into the last day without a story, carried in objects and not in a telling. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door and held a broader man's shape with the crease at the left shoulder where a shoulder had once pressed into it; the seat below kept its twenty-year leftward lean and its hollow, and nobody sat down to fill the hollow. The clean dark line where the ridge had turned with the years lay unswept along the skirting board, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey that the house called the thaw lay unswept also, a look going around both and a wide step going around both and no broom taking up either. His proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone to the pot and stayed there, and the debt against a firing of tiles went around the room without a name, worse for being uncalled, because a thing uncalled can be neither paid nor settled nor stopped. No lesson was made of any of it. No collector came up the clay road because of it. The dead man's name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and nine days, and the household kept him the way it kept its heat: by covering the place where he had been and asking nobody to step into it.

That was the grammar of the house, and he had come to read it as a surgeon reads a dressing left exactly where it was put: nothing entered, nothing named, the edges held. Keeping and withholding were one motion performed with two hands. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both, and he had no hand to carry either, and the two facts lay on one tray in him without joining. He had once known a man who could strip a name off a chart and hand the chart back clean; here a whole district did it daily to a man who was not there to be entered, called the omission kindness, and sent the kindness up the road in a box that no key in this house would open. The competence to read it was intact and useless, and he kept it beside the child's simple want of the crust.

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and the dead man's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, the word waiting on the far side of his teeth; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and went on sweeping. No collector came up or went down the clay road at the going of the light. The warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on a sill stayed on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not laid across the three held changes and not made into a cause, for a fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause and a cause would want a payer.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He gave no name to the men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open back room before first grey, and the room kept its finger's width of open without his saying it was cold. He kept the burned-slate word on a place of its own beside the dust and the two tellings of the room shut in the war, with a hand between the three, and made no cause of it: a direction that had come from a place, gone over boards and arrived nowhere, with no owner, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason, and nothing in \place{Grath} that keeps a slate would have known where to enter it. No third telling came to stand beside the two. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man who came up on a wrong wind and said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost. Then Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- and his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot; then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and it lay on neither of the two places beside the fraction of Day 161 and the flagstone half-check of Day 171. He read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in Grathic that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, with woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called weather, the rack space under \place{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number; the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and it kept the boy the way it kept the basket's handle --- by the feel of what was in it and without once asking what it was worth. The last working week ran out under her feet without her giving it a word, and she said nothing to the basket about the square or the figure or the cord folded under a coat. Under the Vail kiln-house roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread at his mouth frozen. The two rooms did not touch. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note; under the floor the carried fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a knot. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up to become the others.

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel, no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and nine days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid. Nine stoppings stood on the widened tray as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; three held changes stood on three places with the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth with all its answers; the burned-slate word lay on its own place beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings with a hand between the three. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap stood, no fourth likeness had been made a cause, and no finger of his had been drawn across any of them. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing.

Then he put the question to his hand. Sella had gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. Under the very conditions that had given the holding on an earlier night, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down at once against the fold. It was a miss. The asking is not always answered, and there is no failure in the miss. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them, and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The river did not stop, and the fold took the hand without a word.

He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true. The one true change of the district had happened in a square he could only be carried through, and the last living motion in the room was a hand refused under the conditions that had once answered. The hand had come down at once against the fold, the thread at his mouth still frozen and unwiped, one stiff leathery crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the salt coat on its peg held a broader man's shape and the seat below it kept a hollow no one filled. Ember went out without a boundary drawn; two hundred and nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; the carried fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and nine days. The count stood ready to go forward into a day nobody under the roof had named, and the fire went on in the room that warmed him while the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
