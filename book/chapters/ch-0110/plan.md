## Scene 1 — The Seventh Finding

**Day and time.** Day 381, Stone 22, Secondday, Y.H. 813, the small hours at the opening of the day running into first light and the morning's tending. This scene carries the chapter's only `\dropcap` and its first `\daymark{381}{Stone 22, Secondday, Y.H. 813}`.

## Scene 2 — The Well and the Wide Step

**Day and time.** Day 381, Stone 22, Secondday, afternoon in the Clay Yard through the going of the light, the hearth grammar of the evening, and the small hours at the close of the day. No new dated heading.

**Place.** The nineteen steps down from the Vail roof; the Clay Yard and its seven walked-around places; the yard well; the kindling and the flagstones; the wheel-shed edge and the damper; the bed under the roof; the towel at the head of the bed.

**Present.** Corin/Dan; Sella Vail; Tarn at the kindling; Jorra Vail at the wheel-shed edge and damper; Ilva once at the yard well. Garl Vail in objects. Walk-ons only: a Cold Acre hare-carrier shifting a hare across his shoulders, a cracked-jar man in a rope sling, a woman with a basket of kindling, two fence-menders arguing the thaw. No Hessa, Berr, collector, green-mouthed woman or foundling; no market, Cross or bell; no ownerless voice.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, instruction to the wool, the river wind and the fire she will bank against their coming back, never to the basket; she corrects the tilt at every turn of the landing before it settles into the wicker, so the boy feels the small correction before the stair. He takes the order as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small things first, because the small things do not lie. The infant wants the correction to go another way, toward the crust two inches off and the frozen thread, and gets none of it.
2. The yard goes around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner with the finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, unswept and thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it where it has not been swept in two years; the space under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie entered by nobody; the rack end's round ring where the shallow pan once stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; the field's empty place out on the worked strips; the trough; the silt-clouded plate of ice on the flat stone set a little out of the path of feet; and, widest of all, the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, gone around so wide that the elbow cuts the corner and the basket tilts the other way to balance it, righted without looking down, once and not twice.
3. He takes the wide step apart by what is there and by what is not. Seven places gone around with a look and no figure; the widest gone around by nothing measurable. A second going-around would make a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The keeping costs a step each time and nobody enters the steps and he does not enter them either.
4. The three coverings stay weather entered nowhere with a finger between every pair — the gone white frost off the well's north lip, the skin drawn edge to edge across the trough, the silt-clouded plate of ice on its flat stone. The river's winter skin stays on its own place beside water over water, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this afternoon and not made again, not spent on the day's work or the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson. No hand laid the skin, no payer stands under it, no source stands to be asked: a sign points to a payer, a covering left where it is is weather and not a promise.
5. **Ilva, ninth record-first hand (T080):** at the yard well she draws with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the Stone cold giving its small dry rasp, the water dark and moving without a sound; clay stands pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust is worked into the seam of one thumb she does not scrub out. People notice the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which is the order she prefers. To the woman waiting her turn who asks what Stone's firings have taken, her hand goes toward her sleeve before her eyes go to the asker and her mouth opens after both, with the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention. The short strip of notched birch lies in the fold where it always lies, pale where a thumb has rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between; it is not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, to the cord or the knot-string, and no figure comes off the wood. She answers the question put and not the one behind it, *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, puts the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and goes back to the pail, and nobody turns twice. She has not looked into the basket and has no notion she has been read. The boy files the order of reaching as the ninth record-first hand on a place of its own with a finger between it and Hessa Rale's eighth; he files no tenth, makes no reader of her keeping, joins her to none and makes no figure of the slate's nearness. She is expressly not the speaker of any voice.
6. The day's directions arrive nowhere: the hare-carrier's carrying is answered with the weather which carried the snow and can carry the price; the cracked jar in its rope sling is sent to a pot that, being a pot, says nothing; to the woman with kindling and the two fence-menders arguing an early thaw against a late he says *Yes, house* to each and keeps the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument finds a foot. No collector comes up or down the clay road.
7. At the kindling the account stands in its old exactness: six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check, the seventh at the lip beyond it beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lies at the very edge of its sill. Tarn passes his hand over blade and sill as a man passes over a tool put back where it belongs; the hand goes up and comes away, and the coming away is the whole of the checking. He tells the wood *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and cuts nothing, counts nothing, spends nothing.
8. The Ash 14 held breath and the Bone 26 thumb's resting are found where they were put and not made again, beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair — not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist catches what little light there is and gives it back; his chest has been tight since the dark; the Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line; his stroke goes over the shallow bright patch of flagstones without the half-check.
9. He brings the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stops there, as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled. He does not look along the road. Two ears, two silences, a loan that can hear and has never been called, with nobody standing to enter it. A term ending is not a payment, a forgiveness or a freedom; a man with no house stands where the road runs and the fear that he will be entered again for vagrancy stays his, kept without a figure and without asking anybody to carry it.
10. Jorra comes through the finger's-width door off the wheel-shed with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it is not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed have gone low into the back room before first grey; he gives the house no name for them and no word that the room is cold. Dusty forearms and clean ones he keeps on one tray and parts as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lie under the lowest rack and are entered by nobody.
11. He speaks to the wheel before any person — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — and tells a pot going past on a bench *You'll do* in a voice with no welcome and no unkindness either, knowing the full note that pot will give three turns before it leaves his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withholding the knowing from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. The boy keeps that knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them; a pot's note known before its time is not a hearing and no source stands under it.
12. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, takes the cold out of the fingertip, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and is left standing so that the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted — one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word covering both mercies. The finger comes out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. Neither looks at the other; neither says what the iron was asked to carry, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. *He's not Vail* stays sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn.
13. At the going of the light Sella carries him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into the Debt Loft, sets him on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, says nothing of where they have been, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and adds no second fold to the empty place. Her hand does only what the setting needed and goes away; the thread stays frozen and unwiped; the wool is found where put at the going of the light and not made again.
14. Under the roof the hearth grammar is renewed — coals told the thaw and turned with the broken pot's back, *The house will keep it*, charcoal unread, grain covered and unfigured, pot lifted thumb-first with the hairline showing and both proverbs to pot and fire, jar glanced at once with the hand half over nothing, widow's mark untouched, fire in fours with its fifth unpaid. In the small hours the file comes out on the towel with every gap left gap; the hand takes its small intake on the river's note and lays itself down.

**Emotional turn.** The yard's arithmetic stays exact and unspendable; Ilva's order of reaching is read and not joined; Tarn's term at its end and Jorra's ungiven promise stand side by side without becoming one account. Nothing is reconciled. The boy's small satisfaction in things left as found is again not peace.

**Sensory anchors.** Stiff well rope and its small dry rasp; pail against a knee; clay pale in nail crescents and grey dust in a thumb seam; the sour tang of Ash at the Vail corner; dry cold and thin light; knife at the edge of a sill; pale clip-ring catching light; Ash in knuckle creases; meal-fine grey slate dust white in a fist-crease; cold iron of the damper; woodsmoke, wet wool, banked coals.

**Threads advanced (none paid).** T097/T060/T086 (wool found where put at the going of the light, not made again); T071/T072 (Day 381 exact, answer 338); T080 (Ilva's ninth hand, strip unread, no tenth, no reader, no voice); T009/T013/T092 and T098 (seven places, returning-line widest, once and not twice); T083/T085 (three coverings and river's skin unspent); T036/T076/T006 (six and seventh, checked keepings not made again, ninth stopping with no tenth, loan uncalled, seventh year at its end); T062/T035/T031 (dust, forearms parted as clot from vessel, damper's two bites, Ash 26 not repeated, promise ungiven); T058/T079/T100 and T077 (hearth grammar, charcoal unread, pot thumb-first, grain unfigured, no handful); T061/T082 (six hearings, flat knock outside, heard bell beside told); T088 (fire's fifth unpaid); T012 and T093/T094/T096 carried.

**Exact last image.** The hand laid flat on the fold on answer **338**, with the count standing at 381 exact, six months and two hundred and one days told by nobody, the fire's fifth unpaid and Garl's name unspoken the same number.
