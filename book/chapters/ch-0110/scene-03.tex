\daymark{382}{Stone 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid down at the close of the day before, and the count stood at three hundred and eighty-one exact. \person[Corin]{The boy} had not carried it forward by an act of choosing; a man's attention goes where his field is, and his field was the two inches of air above a barley crust and the small sounds of a house waking in its sleep. He lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, in the last full working week before the year's last reading, and this morning had a date no roof under that sky would speak aloud: \place{Stone} 23, the twenty-third day of the deep-winter month, six months and two hundred and two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore[the carried fire]{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. The eleventh arithmetic stood behind the household and had bought it no teaching; the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments lie out when a case is done and before the next is called, with every gap left gap. No voice had come over \place{the boards} in the night, and the three directions that had come in the dark month stood where they had been put without repeating; the fourth of nothing had not come, and no row was made even by his doing.

He took the body in the old order, because a field is checked in a sequence and an impression that has not first been set in order is an impression only. The crust came first: one stiff leathery barley crust with a brown edge on it, lying two inches from the ear, hardened another night in the dry kiln heat, its corner lifted a hair off the wool. Two inches had been its distance for three hundred and eighty-one days and two inches would be its distance this morning; the width was not a wish and had no giver standing behind it. Beside it the wool lay folded once in the empty place, where a hand had put it on the fifteenth and where no hand had been since, and the place held its fold and its emptiness together the way a wound holds both the edge the dressing left and the gap the dressing did not close. Then the cold, coming cheek, temple, the bare little joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopping there as if a line had been written out for it with no order to go farther and no order to go away. \lore[the Afterdrop]{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a rewarming nobody under this roof would perform. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen stiff in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

He knew what a basket set level out of the draught, where the heat came to a body sideways, would be: the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, that such a patient will seem better before he is worse, and that heat returns to the extremities before it returns to the core, which is why a surgeon warms from the outside and slowly, never rubbing, never hurrying, never setting the body too near the fire. The knowing was exact and useless at two inches of air. It had once been his trade to put a hand into a cavity and know by feel which vessel had given way. Now he could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the afterdrop went on doing its patient work in a body that had no surgeon in it.

The infant under the wool wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. The want went on under the exactness as water goes on under ice, with nowhere to go and no leave to stop. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. He had long since stopped looking for a verdict in the quarrel, which was not the same as having found one; the quarrel itself was the work, and this morning it began again on the same two notes as every morning since the first.

\scenebreak

The eighth finding came before the light, as the others had. The wool lay folded once in the crust's empty place, unchanged by the night, exactly where it had been put on the fifteenth. He found it. He did not place it. No second fold had come in the dark, no third crust stood against the two gaps to make a row come out even, no name had been spoken over the place, no gift left in it. A hand that finds is not a hand that places. Seven findings from a hand that only read had added no seventh placing, and this eighth added none either: a habit wants a hand that placed twice and none stood, a way wants a third and none stood, and both want a payer where no payer stood.

He filed it on a place of its own beside the crust's gap, the two gaps in the shelf in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the gone pan's round ring at the rack end and the field's empty place out on the worked strips, with a finger between it and every other kept thing so that no row came out even by his doing. It was not a joining of rooms, not a tenth held object, not a sign, not a hearing, not a mending, not a cause and not a rule. It was not joined to the Ash 5 joining or to the Ash 17 or Ash 23 direction, each standing on its own place, nor to any fourth that had not yet stood; it was not laid across the eleven arithmetics standing behind the household or the four rooms with the same gap held apart. It had gathered interest all night without being spent, as dust gathers in a corner nobody sweeps, and a corner is kept by nobody's hand all the same. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was still far off, was not his, and had not come. An account wants a hand to lay the places together and a payer standing under all of them, and no hand stood and no payer stood.

The small satisfaction of a field found as it had been left and left as it had been found cost him nothing. That was how he knew it was not peace. Under it, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, as she had at both ends of every day for three years. The room carried woodsmoke and wet wool, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the \lore{Ash} ridge along the skirting board, which no broom had crossed in two years and which she did not intend to cross now. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, the pot's back going down into the bed and bringing up a small breath of heat that came under her forearm and died into the room.

“The house will keep it,” she said to the coals, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment on a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps. Her hands were never still; with nothing to do they counted breaths, nails, the strokes of a handle going round. Here the work went to the fire first, for a fire that has been kept does not know whether anyone is sorry, only whether it has been fed.

Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a two-gapped row of the shelf's edge and gave neither gap a number, the way a woman might move her lips along a row of nails without counting them. She glanced once at \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air --- two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold, the skin inside puckered and pale. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} hung oiled at her throat where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and she did not touch it now: a morning and a hearth are no openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot stood with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. She lifted it thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, the thumb reaching the fault before the weight came on the handle, and left the hairline where it was.

“Leave the crack showing,” she told the pot. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.”

“If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. The proverbs had been improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had thought them when he first said them; they went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, because a thing said to a person becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Repair stayed apart from restoration in her hands. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing, and the old-brass staple and the tighter wire crossed over the old fault like two men holding a gate that had fallen once and would fall again if either let go. The boy read the crossing as he would have read a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen and kept the knowing to himself.

The dressed grain she turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, turning it once, lifting no figure and taking no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed --- the half-beat the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift. Inside the wool his hand went toward the rim and stopped where the half-beat had stopped it. The infant's want of that hand on the pot handle went on without an answer. Nothing crossed to him from the hearth; no instruction had ever gone to the basket, and none went now.

\scenebreak

Before noon Sella carried the boy down the nineteen steps and set \place{the basket} level out of the draught at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard}, where the upper stretch of \place{the clay road} showed between the walls. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. From the low angle of the wicker the road was a band of thin winter light and pale scurf, and a person going up it was seen first as a stride and then as a coat.

\person{Hessa Rale} came up that road toward \place{Greybank} with \person{Berr Clay}'s narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned toward her sleeve. The thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was --- not concealment, but the manner of carrying a slate so the face of it is not rubbed and no goer-by takes a figure off it without a hand to put there. The second strap was let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist came first, her hand arriving at the slate before her eyes arrived at the yard, and no figure came off the face of it.

“What do you carry?” a woman by the path asked, though the question she meant was another one.

“I don't read it. I carry it,” Hessa said. “There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.”

The woman wanted more. “Then who set the figure on it?”

Hessa settled the second strap again, not for the woman and not for the road, with the small flat correction of a person settling a burden that has become part of the walk. “Ask the road. It knows the way and I know the weight. That's the whole of what either of us knows.”

She read nothing aloud, gave no figure and did not look into the basket, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. Nothing in her road wore a name, a date or a figure for the boy to enter. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the women and her eyes went to the women before her mouth opened, and that order was the whole of what he had to go on.

The boy took the turning of the writing toward herself apart as a surgeon takes a field apart and filed it on a place of its own beside the Ash 6 sighting, with a finger between. A runner going up with the writing turned to her own sleeve is not a voice standing in a doorway and not a saying fulfilled. He made no fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying, no reader of her, no witness standing and no payment, and joined her neither to \person{Ilva}'s strip in its sleeve nor to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, nor to the charcoal under the loaf shelf, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate nor to any other private keeping. Her eighth stayed eight; he filed no tenth hand; eleven arithmetics going up the road behind her in a locked box whose key did not come back down did not make her a reader of what she carried. She was a hand and stayed a hand, and the figure went up the road unread and would come down at somebody else's hour.

\scenebreak

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and eighty-two days. Nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull and nobody named the payer, and the silence stood on its own place beside the pulls and was not counted as a fifth. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for. \lore[The Hearth Drawing]{The Drawing}'s small flat knock out of the neighbour on the pallet stayed on its own place outside them, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place, for a hearing wants a source standing and that room had been seen from a basket and not entered. The heard bell of \lore{the Long Night} stayed in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into any of the three counts.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the towel came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, with every gap left gap. Three hundred and thirty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place and the three hundred and thirty-ninth was to come. The five held objects stood with the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper beside them, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair; the keeping at the fold stood beside the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the gone pan's round ring and the field's empty place and was not laid across any of them. The finding that eleven are not one account sat on a place of its own too, with a finger between it and every other kept thing. No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, tenth hand, tenth stopping or twelfth arithmetic was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Nothing she carried stood up here and called itself an account.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, muffled under \lore{the winter skin} as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The skin the cold had put on and the cold would take off was found where put, not made again, not spent on the week's work or on a lesson under any roof; no hand had laid it, no payer stood under it and no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is is weather and not a promise.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-nine: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the child's grey fingertips, the mother's cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river's skin, the widow's mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-two exact, six months and two hundred and two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire's fifth unpaid the same number and Garl's name unspoken the same number. The twenty-third came on without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. The year's last reading stood beyond the week and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and \lore{the Five} stood beyond that. The hand lay flat on the fold. The runner was gone up the clay road with the writing turned to her own sleeve. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.
