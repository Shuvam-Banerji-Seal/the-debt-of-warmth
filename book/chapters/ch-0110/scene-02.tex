The afternoon came on without a boundary drawn, the light going thin over the roofs of \place{Low Kiln} in \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down from under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof at the half-pace she had kept from every open slate for three years. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. “Keep his ear covered. The wind will have it else,” she said to the wool. She said nothing to the thing that held him, because a basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and not why.

There were nineteen steps between the door and \place{the flagstones}. She corrected the tilt at every turn of the landing before it had time to settle into the wicker, so that he felt the small correction before he felt the stair, and the whole of the carry was that correction, made nineteen times and never once named. He took the descent as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small things first, because the small things do not lie: the scrape of a heel on the second step, the draught that came up at the fifth and went out again at the sixth, the particular hollow the basket made against her hip when she shifted her weight at the turn. The infant under the wool wanted the correction to go the other way, toward the crust two inches off and the thread frozen at his lip, and got none of it. Both wants were true and the second was louder and neither won. That was the quarrel, and the quarrel was the work.

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the afternoon went about its ordinary work and left its accounts where they lay. Nothing from the eleventh arithmetic standing behind the household had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and nothing crossed out here either. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the month's dry cold, carrying the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; the round ring at \place{the rack end} where the shallow clay pan had once stood, keeping no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; \place{the trough}; the silt-clouded plate of ice on \place{the flat stone by the well}, set a little out of the path of feet; and, widest of all, the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out. The going-around there was so wide that her elbow cut the corner and the basket tilted the other way to balance it, and she righted it without looking down. Once. Not twice.

He took the wide step apart by what was there and by what was not. Seven places gone around with a look and no figure. The widest gone around by nothing measurable at all, by the turn of an elbow and the counterweight of a wicker basket, which are not measures anybody keeps. A second going-around would have made a figure of once, and a figure wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The keeping cost a step each time and nobody entered the steps and he did not enter them either. The satisfaction of a field taken as found and left as found cost him nothing, and because it cost him nothing he knew it was not peace.

The three coverings stayed where the weather had put them, with a finger between every pair. The white frost was gone off the well's north lip; \lore[the winter skin]{the skin} was drawn edge to edge across the trough; the plate of ice clouded with silt lay on its flat stone out of the way of feet. Weather entered nowhere and was not made a cause, a sign, a payment or a promise. Beyond \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and over it from bank to bank lay the winter skin, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this afternoon, not made again, not spent on the week's work or on the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. No hand had laid the skin, no payer stood under it, no source stood to be asked. A sign points to a payer; a covering left where it is, is weather.

\scenebreak

\person{Ilva} was at \place{the yard well} once, drawing with the pail against her knee. The rope stood stiff in the \place{Stone} cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up, and the water went dark and moving below without a sound. Clay stood pale in the crescents of her nails; the finer grey rack dust was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub it out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. The people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred.

A woman waiting her turn, with her shawl pulled tight against the river wind, asked what Stone's firings had taken.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker, and her mouth opened after both, with the half-beat lying in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. The short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches, dark in the uncut grain between. It was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, nor to the charcoal under \place{the loaf shelf}, nor to the cord, nor to the knot-string, and no figure came off the wood.

“Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.”

She had answered the question put and not the one behind it. She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice. She had not looked into the basket and had no notion she had been read.

The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart: hand to the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, the half-beat in the gap between the looking and the word. He filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth. He filed no tenth, made no reader of her keeping, joined her to none and made no figure of the slate's nearness. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it, and the notches gathered interest toward a far-off hand that was not his and had not come.

The day's directions arrived nowhere. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders and answered a question about its price with the weather which had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed. A man with a cracked jar in a rope sling was sent to a pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. To a woman with a basket of kindling who asked what the seventh year would want, and to two fence-menders who said the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late, because a late thaw puts off the paying. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. No collector came up or went down the clay road, and no bell came on any road.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the account stood in its old exactness. Six pieces lay in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where a hand could check it and where a hand could also slip it off. Tarn passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs; the hand went up and came away, and the coming away was the whole of the checking. He held no breath at the top of the reach.

“Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and cut nothing, counted nothing, spent nothing.

The \lore{Ash} 14 held breath and the Bone 26 thumb's resting were found where they had been put and were not made again, standing beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair. They were not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not the calling of the loan. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark; the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the Vail corner; and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch of the flagstones without the half-check, the patch keeping the place where the stroke had once stopped and joined to no cause.

He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. He did not look along the road. Two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called, with nobody standing to enter it. A term ending is not a payment, not a forgiveness, not a freedom. A man with no house stands where the road runs, and the fear that he will be entered again for vagrancy stays his, kept without a figure and without asking anybody to carry it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot going past on a bench he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, knowing \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it. He withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. The boy kept that knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them: a pot's note known before its time is not a hearing and no source stands under it.

When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no \lore{Grathic} word covered both mercies. The finger came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and found where put. Neither looked at the other and neither said what the iron had been asked to carry, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}. She set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, said nothing of where they had been, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and added no second fold to the crust's empty place. Her hand did only what the setting needed and went away. The thread stayed frozen and unwiped. The wool was found where put at the going of the light and was not made again, and the keeping was not done a second time because a second keeping would make a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood.

Under the roof the grammar of the hearth was renewed as it had been at both ends of every day for three years. She came to \place{the hearth} before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I, standing a moment to count a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening and figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. She glanced once at \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and did not touch the \lore{widow's mark} hanging at her throat: a going of the light and a bed are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot was lifted thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire. Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, because a thing said to a person becomes a thing the Court can enter. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. The dressed grain she turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, lifting no figure and taking no handful, and she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and eighty-one days. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond the warm room the boy could not reach, past a rewarming nobody in this house would perform; and the infant still wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and got none of the three.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called. Every gap was left gap. The five held objects stood with the pot-crack resting and the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper beside them; the keeping at the fold stood beside the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the gone pan's round ring and the field's empty place, with a finger between it and every other kept thing. The finding that eleven arithmetics are not one account sat on a place of its own with no signature on the space and no doctrine standing up out of eleven. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the seventh place empty and not dug for, \lore[The Hearth Drawing]{The Drawing}'s flat knock outside them on its own place, the heard bell of \lore{the Long Night} in its square beside the told, four, four and a gap. Nine record-first hands kept no tenth; nine stoppings kept no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and the well-saying at four shapings. The Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 and Ash 23 directions stood where they had been put, did not repeat and bought no lesson; the fourth of nothing had not come and no row was made even by his doing. No sixth hearing, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eighth opening or twelfth arithmetic was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water under its winter skin, muffled as a voice is muffled by a shut door. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and thirty-eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eleven readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the child's grey fingertips, the mother's cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river's skin, the widow's mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. The count stood at three hundred and eighty-one exact, six months and two hundred and one days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, the carried fire's fifth unpaid the same number, the payer unnamed, and Garl's name unspoken the same number. Stone kept its twenty-second day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month holding both its names, older women by weather and slatemen by Account-days, neither set right nor left in so many words. The year's last reading stood beyond the week and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and \lore{the Five} stood beyond that. The hand lay flat on the fold. The count went on under the roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath.
