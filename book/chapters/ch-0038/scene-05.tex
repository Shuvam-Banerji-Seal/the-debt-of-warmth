\daymark{157}{Tallow 8, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

The underside of \place{the loaf shelf} became a roof at the angle where the basket sat, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay under it the way a man lies under a bench turned on its edge, looking up at the grain of a board he could not have lifted. The wicker rim rose at his cheek and became a wall; beyond it the room was all skirt-hems and the slow passage of loaves along a board. He watched them go in the order a row keeps and not as a number: the brown one, the pale one, the one with the cracked corner that had been set last so the crack would not travel, and the space at the end where a loaf had stood and was not standing. No woman in the house called that space a number, because calling it would have made it one. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in a glance at a clay jar that stood cold under its glaze against the wall, and in that space at the row's end. His count had neither rim nor handle and would not have sat among them. It stood where it stood. One hundred and fifty-seven days stood in it, five months and seven days, exact, complete and unspent, and nobody under the roof had words to take it from him if he held it out.

Morning came into the room without being announced. It came as hot clay under tallow, as wet wool, as the small mineral edge of the \lore{Ash} ridge along the skirting board that no broom had been given an instruction about. Through the wall the kiln breathed in fours with a gap, and the gap was a silence the house had long ago stopped listening for. \person{Sella Vail} came and went at the shelf with the half-pace she kept from every open slate, though there was no slate within a yard of the basket. She moved a loaf an inch and then an inch back, and her lips moved along the row as if she were reading a line of a sentence that stood in some other room of her head. Her eye went once to the clay jar, as a hand goes to an instrument before the case is opened, and went away; the jar was not touched. She did not look into the basket and she did not speak to it. Every instruction in that house went to the wool, the pot, the fire and the board before it went to any person, and never to the basket.

He had learned to read the order of reaching before he had learned to read the order of words. The hand went to the record before it went to the person: a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with his hand to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls, a cutter straightening his cord before he lifted his trap, a woman reaching beneath a board before her eyes went to the row. Five hands performing one grammar in one district, and not one of them explaining it. He set the grammar on a place of its own and made no rule of it, because a rule wants a payer and no payer had been named to any of them.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came up from the yard with a wet pot in both hands and her sleeves dark to the elbow, and set it down near the bench with the care of a woman setting down something that has cost her a carry up the clay road. What does a firing cost, set down there? she asked, and she asked it of the room rather than of Sella, the way a person asks a wall in the presence of a child who may repeat it.

Sella did not turn. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on the pot's absence and opened on nothing as she reached past it.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

The woman stood a moment with her question still in her mouth; then she took the sentence as it was given and carried it away level, the way a woman carries a full pail level so nothing slops. She went out with her sleeves dark and her pot dry at the rim. The question went with her and arrived nowhere, and the room went back to its loaves.

He had learned that a thing said twice under a shelf is not therefore a thing said to anybody. The second voice does not answer the first; it takes the question off the ground and carries it out, level, so that the floor stays dry. A cost asked of a pot is a cost put where no Court thumb will find it, and a house that has said a thing is a house that has said it where nobody can enter the saying. The woman had wanted a figure. She went away with an instruction to clay. He set the exchange on its own place beside the others and did not join it to the two tellings of the shut counting room that stood side by side somewhere behind the wall, because a joining would want a third telling and no third had come.

\scenebreak

Sella came back to the shelf and stood with her back to the basket. She moved her lips along the row, counting the loaves as another woman might count spoons or the breaths of a sleeping child, and then, without looking down and without a word, she reached a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies. The finger went under the board and came away. She did not go back to the shelf.

The boy saw the hand go under and saw it come back. He saw nothing of what the marks said. Under the front edge the charcoal clusters lay in the grain, some pressed hard enough to have left a small black shine, some faded until they were almost the colour of the wood itself, so that a finger going beneath knew them by touch and not by sight and perhaps not even by touch after so many mornings. Such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stood exactly between the two, with nothing to choose between them and no word from her to push it either way. A keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of the hiding than the writing would; he had learned that from a chart with its note turned down and a hand going past it to a signature. Here the care was in the not-looking. The hand went to the record before the eyes went to the row, and the mouth stayed shut through the whole of it.

He did not read the marks. He did not copy them. He did not enter them in the file he turned out in the small hours, because a figure is a thing that must be paid by somebody and no payer had been named to the charcoal in all the days since the first Account-day of Reed when the tally had been set beneath the board. The hand knew the board; the boy knew the order of the reaching. Between the knowing and the order a whole district stood, and he kept them on separate trays.

\scenebreak

The shelf kept its two gaps and no third came. There was the old space at the row's end, which had been there since before the boy came under the roof, and there was the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and the two stood in the row like two teeth gone from the same jaw, each keeping the shape of what had been taken out of it. But he did not lay them together. In the other room one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still without a name and with no third. The three absences were three. They did not stand up in a line and call themselves a doctrine, because he kept a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a place of its own, as a missing instrument keeps its hollow in a towel. It was not laid across the shelf's two gaps. It was not laid across the crust in the fold or the empty place at his ear. It was not laid across the field's empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, a place the yard still went around with a look and a wide step and no number. It was not laid across the sixth green, which was a colour and joined to no ear, or the foundling, who was a shape and had no sale. A fourth likeness would make a cause, and a cause would want a payer, and no payer had been named to any of them. No finger of his drew the line.

Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and a gap where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one. The adult who had once stood with a towel in his hands in a room where heat was kept behind a screen watched the numbers keep their separate places the way he had once watched a field kept clean; the child's body lay under a board and was cold and wanted the crust two inches from its ear and the warmth that never came within two paces of the reaching hand. Both were true and the second was louder. The cold had come cheek, then temple, then the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread.

\scenebreak

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} the day went the way the days went, without a boundary drawn and without a slate the wiser. At the going of the light she carried him up from the yard at the half-pace, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. She went around the place on the wall where the pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, tilting shoulder and basket so nothing knocked; at the well she went around the trough whose skin of ice had gone; at the yard she went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket.

She set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread frozen and unwiped, and rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. The half-beat was the only thing in the room that admitted anything. It went away with the hand.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving on the row that stood in the other room of her head; then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple pale off the coals, tipped the carried coal under the back of the pot and set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not look toward the bed. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk is not an opening, and a hearth is not a door, and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She went to her own bed without explaining the count. The two rooms did not touch.

The pot handle stood two inches beyond the reaching hand that would not close on it. The fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-seven days. In the dark the count stood exact, complete and unspent, and the boy's hand went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
