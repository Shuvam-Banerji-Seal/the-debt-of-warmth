\daymark{159}{Tallow 10, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

The tenth day of \place{Tallow} came up over \place{the Cross} grey and close, with the river wind coming under the eaves of the square and the smell of hot clay coming down on the same breath from \place{High Kiln}. It was the first Account-day of the firing month and the market had come with it the way a cart comes with its load, the pigs already over the weighing board before the light was properly in the boards, and the potters setting bowls out on \place{the pot board} with the dust still in the hollows of the rims. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the basket in the finger's width of shade at the foot of \place{the moot-post}, his cheek against cold iron and one crust's worth of tallow and wet wool in his nose, and the crowd leaned over \place{the reading block} above him with their backs to the sun that had not yet come. He was one hundred and fifty-nine days old, five months and nine days, and nobody in the square had any reason to say so.

\person{Sella Vail} set the basket in the shade where no slate lay and stood a half-pace off from every open slate in the square. She gave the instruction to the wool at his chin and to the river wind coming up under the eaves, and gave none of it to the basket. A woman near the pigs asked what the firing would cost now that the kilns had hands in them, and Sella sent her to the pot with \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, and carried the question away level in her own hands so that nothing slopped. The boy watched the half-pace keep its width while the crowd came and went around it, and noted, as he had noted every market of his life, that a woman could stand all morning in a square without once being asked to answer for the basket at her feet.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road}. He had the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned where the goers-by could not see it. Then he passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required, the check of a man setting down a dish he is already sure will not slop.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said low into the goers-by.

The crowd leaned in because the voice was low and there was nothing else to do with their bodies. He said the levy was called and said it was not called by him. A man with a number shut in his fist asked what the firing would cost set down there, and Berr said a number went into a slate and not a bundle, and that if the man's house wanted it again there would be a slate at the watch. \emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand goes out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field open and clean and nobody's. The figure went from the man's fist to his mouth to the shoulder of somebody going up toward High Kiln and arrived nowhere. The printed one in twelve stood where it stood and nobody challenged it. Berr went off up the clay road with the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked in his house behind him and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness.

Eight pigs went over the weighing board while the reading was still in the air, driven across by two men with poles, and a Sill assessor put the figures on a slate before anybody could ask which eight he meant. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the rail. At the square's edge people brought numbers to a heap of turnips and left them standing side by side without making them agree, which is the district's whole arithmetic done in the open air. A stall-keeper with meal ground into the creases of her forearms sold a loaf and took the mark into her palm without counting it where anyone could hear. The boy watched the hand go to the mark and then to the loaf: record first, person after, cost wherever it could be set down without a name. He had once set a figure down on a chart while a body lay open under a sheet, and the chart had been exact and the body had not been saved, and nobody had made the two agree. He did not call the market a lesson. A lesson wants a payer, and the morning was only doing what it always did.

The bell went four, four and a gap, and nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood beside the told bell from the Long Night, two lots of four with the same silence where the fifth should have been, and the boy kept them on separate places without putting a hand across either. A figure read aloud was not a figure paid; a chart can say one thing while a room has gone and done another. He had once held a towel in a room where heat was kept behind a screen and had watched a man stand at the gate and not go in, and the grammar of this square was older than the square: the name taken out of the sentence, the hand out of the field, the cost out of the account. Above him Berr's twice-wiped thumb and the finger laid on a figure went onto a place of their own among the record-first hands, not joined to the midwife's reaching hand, the clerk behind the Court's table, the woman who kept the fire, the cutter with his cord, or the runner with the slate. Nine hands in one district performing one grammar and not one of them explaining it. A rule wants a payer, and no payer had been named to any of them.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} came through the square again in the middle of the morning, taking the slate up the clay road toward \place{Greybank}. Her hand went to the narrow slate before her eyes went to the people at the rails, the small flat correction of the strap set and the wrist turned to bring the edge round, and only then did her face take in the crowd, the pigs, the pots, the basket in the shade. She held the slate edge-on to her own forearm with the writing toward her sleeve, and nobody in the square read a figure off it. The mud of the road had dried pale in scurf on the shortened skirt of her coat, which she had not brushed, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. She was a runner and not a reader of anyone's private keeping; the edge-on was the manner of carrying a slate so the writing is not rubbed, and not the hiding of a thing from herself.

“Road's long,” she said to a man who asked what reading had gone up before noon, and shifted the strap to the other shoulder. “Reading goes up. Carrying goes out. I've got the one that goes out.”

The man wanted the figure and she gave him the direction instead, and went on up the road at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. The boy filed the order of reaching as the eighth such hand-shape in the district and put it on its own place. He had learned that grammar before he had learned the order of words: the hand to the record before the hand to the person. It was not \person{Marra}'s pause between strokes at the edge of a flare light, not the neat dark hand, not the clerk at the Court's table, not the soft cuff set below the bridge for a measure nobody called, not the soft hands at the fringe of \place{Slate Row}, not Berr's twice-wiped thumb, not the finger that went beneath the loaf shelf in the other house without looking. Eight hands, one grammar, no joining. He kept a finger between them because likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer had been named to the eighth.

Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing}, the soft-cuffed clerk was still set for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday. He held his slate to his forearm with the writing turned inward, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, entering the figure before he looked at the man who had given it. Nobody stepped around him and nobody had taken him off since the month turned. The market sold its pots and pigs and eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and a girl with a creel took the landing sideways, caught the rail and went on selling without looking at her feet. No fall was made of it. The boy put the clerk's order of reaching on its own place beside the others and did not join it to the runner's; two records going inward were two records going inward, and the resemblance was a shape and not a cause.

He kept the shape of the set clerk the way he kept the shape of a clamp left standing on a vessel: not what it did, but where it stood and how long it had been standing there. Nobody had set this one down in front of him and nobody had taken it away. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of something, and ahead of something is a direction, and a direction is an answer to a question nobody in the square had asked aloud. He kept it as a shape and made no rule of it. By midday the cuff had gone grey a second time from washing and grey again from the work, the eels went on being sold over the top of him, and the boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the stalls and came down against the fold with one hundred and fifty-nine still exact and unspent.

The afternoon went over the square in the ordinary way, with bowls turned a quarter on the pot board and set back exactly where they had stood, and numbers going from fists to mouths to shoulders and arriving nowhere. Sella did not shift the basket out of the shade and did not look into it. At the going of the light she took up the half-pace and carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair as they had passed every evening of his life. She went wide around the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out through Reed and was not standing now, around the bare dark trough at \place{the yard well} and around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. She said nothing about what had been read over him. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap, and in \place{Low Kiln} the fires were being banked for the night under broken pots, four low pulls apiece and a silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-nine days.

\scenebreak

In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, from a person. There was no seventh and none was invented to fill the place. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, the knowing withheld from a kiln-master and the pot known before it left the wheel among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger and the unchecking stroke and his own held breath from a market morning. On the widened tray lay nine stoppings --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. A tenth had not stood up out of the nine; the seventh year was earned by no man under this roof and had not given, and the loan that could hear was uncalled. Beside them lay \lore{the damper} as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln, the holding and the taking with no Grathic word for both mercies. The two shut-room tellings stood side by side with no third, and the told bell stood beside the heard bell.

In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps and its unread charcoal under the front edge, a finger had gone beneath it without looking and had come away, and no mark had been read or copied or entered. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in \place{the bed} with the empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The pan's gone place kept the round shape of its absence on a place of its own and was not laid across the shelf's gaps, the crust's empty place, the field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, the sixth green as a colour joined to no ear or the foundling as a shape with no sale. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer; no finger of his drew the line. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places and none of them became one.

One hundred and fifty-nine stood exact, complete and unspent. The adult who had once stood with a towel in his hands while somebody else held the field watched the square's arithmetic go up the road inside a locked box and knew that nothing under this roof had changed by it. The child's body was cold and wanted the crust two inches from his ear and the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither and could not ask for either. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from his chin; want came up small and importunate and was not answered. The crust had gone stiff past smelling of anything and he wanted it for the warmth that was not in it, and the number he wanted was not any number but the one somebody who had known what a number was might have spoken once over the basket and gone away leaving behind. Both were true and the second was louder. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.

The fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-nine days. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
