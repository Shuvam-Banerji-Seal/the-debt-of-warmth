\daymark{154}{Tallow 5, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}

Before first grey the yard had not yet decided what sort of day it would be, and the men who came up the clay road had the look of men who had already decided for it. Three or four of them went low-voiced with their collars high against the river wind, and turned in at the door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} that kept its finger's width of open, going down into \place{the back room} the way a man goes into a room he has entered before and expects to enter again. No woman under the roof said who they were. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} past at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the little-door burn closing on the wicker handle and opening again, and she turned no head toward the door, and gave the house no name for the men, and did not say the room was cold. The instruction went to the wool and the pot and the fire before it went to anybody, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.”

He was one hundred and fifty-four days old and five months and four days, and nobody under any roof said so, and nobody under any roof would have known what to do with the saying of it if a man had put the words into their mouths. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board and in a glance at a clay jar and in a space at the row's end that no woman called a number. His count had no rim and no handle and no woman passing with a pot would have set it down. It stood where it stood. It went on.

There were two tellings of a counting room shut in the war with the men inside dead of the breath, and both had come downriver as a curiosity with a date, and the yard had taken them for history both times. A third voice had once only mentioned the room, which is not the same thing as telling it, and he had kept the difference as carefully as he kept the rest. This morning no voice told it again. The two tellings lay side by side where they had been laid and did not touch, and he did not set a third beside them to keep them company. A row of likenesses will stand up and call itself a rule if a man leaves it long enough alone, and a rule wants a payer at its foot; he had learned to keep his finger between them.

\scenebreak

Sella set the basket low at the edge of the wheel-shed work where no slate lay, and the boy lay at the angle at which the underside of a shelf becomes a roof. The hot dry smell of clay giving up its water came up off the yard and got under the nails and stayed there; under it was the fainter warmth of tallow, and under that again, on a wind that came the wrong way off \place{High Kiln}, the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} that put a false sweetness in the mouth and made a man think of fruit where there was no fruit. Dust lay ground small as meal on the sill and on the backs of hands and in the white of a knuckle when a fist opened. He did not turn his head toward the wind. A smell was a smell and not a hearing, and what a wrong wind carried was not the same thing as what a man said when he chose to say it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with the day's fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The dust lay in the creases of his wrists and in the white of a knuckle when a fist opened, finer than slip and drier, and the boy set the two on two places on one tray and did not let one stand for the other. A thing ground is a thing that got there by being broken. A thing mixed is a thing somebody put together on purpose. He had learned to tell the two before he could have said why, and the telling, exact and complete, stood in the basket and was worth nothing. Jorra asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate, and kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray without a word passing between the two states. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they lay, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no woman reached under them and no man entered them into anything.

\scenebreak

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he told the wheel, in the flat voice that went to the tool before it went to the clay and to the clay before a person. He told a pot, “You'll do.” He knew \lore{the full note} of it before it left the wheel --- knew the pitch it would give under a knuckle when it had cooled and been set down --- and he did not say so when a kiln-master come up from High Kiln on the bad wind, with \lore{Ash} the colour of the ridge on his cuffs, said the pot was singing before its time and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. Jorra said nothing. The kiln-master went off with his sentence whole and the pot went on turning, and the knowing went on the tray of answers that had come near to full and had not been paid, and not among the hearings. There were six hearings of the full note, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, and there was no seventh and none invented to fill the place where none was owed. A knowing held back is not a hearing given; he set the two on separate hollows and kept his finger between them. The room came level again in the space of a breath, with nothing added to it and nothing taken away.

He had watched the withholding the way a man watches a hand keep pressure in a cavity that will not hold pressure on its own. The pot's note was in the pot before ever a knuckle went to it, and Jorra knew the pitch the way a man knows the weight of a thing he has lifted a thousand times, in the muscle and not in a figure; but a knowing spoken before a kiln-master becomes a thing the kiln-master can enter, and the man who says it early will be told it is early and made to answer for the saying. So the knowing stayed where it was put, advanced and not paid, on the tray with the other answers that had come near to full. He did not grudge it its place. An answer that has not been spoken cannot be used against the man who kept it, and an answer that cannot be used is worth exactly what he could do with it, which was nothing.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair's breadth, and left it standing. The cold took the warmth out of the fingertip as iron takes it, without haste and without malice. The end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it: Sella's hands went on to the next pot, \person{Tarn}'s head stayed down over his work, and nothing in the room turned to look at what had been done. It was an obedience and it was a correction neither of them would name. He read the iron as one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath, and filed it as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln. A clamp is a clamp and a gate is a gate; a man who calls the one the other already wants an account, and an account wants a payer at its foot. There was no payer named to this iron and no name spoken over it under this roof.

Tarn took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, with the small knife set back at the very edge of its sill where six could be counted and the seventh could not, the unforged sentence lying beside it and the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and he did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. No collector came. He did not ask about the room and Jorra did not ask about the ear, and the two silences stood in one room unjoined, the loan that could hear still uncalled and the seventh year still held. The stroke that went on when it might have checked was another interval on the tray, an object and not a payment, the place in the stone kept as a thing nobody would enter; he laid it beside the standing finger and laid no finger across the two.

\scenebreak

At midday Sella carried him back under the roof and set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, going halfway around the place on the wall where a shallow clay pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, tilting shoulder and basket so nothing knocked, and giving no explanation of the turn to the basket. The round bare ring where the pan had been kept its shape on a place of its own. It was not laid across the shelf's two gaps or the crust's empty place or the field's empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; no finger of his drew the line, because a fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer, and none had been named to any of them.

She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along \place{the loaf shelf}, where the row kept its two unnamed gaps and the space at the end that nobody called a number, and glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge today. The charcoal tally lay where it lay under the board, unread and uncounted, and a hand that either knew the row by heart or had been told not to look stayed where it was. In the other room one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, unnamed, and no third crust came into the fold. The shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring, the crust's empty place and the field's empty place stayed each on its own tray, and he kept them there by care rather than by discovery, the way a man lays instruments out on a towel with nothing resting across anything else.

The kiln's breath came through the wall in fours with a gap, and under the floor the carried fire answered in the same figure, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-four days; through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one. He laid his file away as he had laid it out, with six hearings beside and never among, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, nine stoppings with no payer named to any and no tenth stood up, the damper's double bite joined to no kiln, the two shut-room tellings with no third, the told bell beside the heard bell. One hundred and fifty-four stood exact, complete and unspent. The adult count and the child's body argued as they had argued every day of his life, the one dry and exact and given to keeping, the other immediate and cold and impatient for the crust; both were true and the second was louder, and neither had a hand to enter anything. Want came up small and importunate for the crust two inches from his ear and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither. The round empty ring on the wall kept the shape of its absence on a tray of its own while, through the wall, the fire answered four low pulls and a silence with no payer named, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold.
