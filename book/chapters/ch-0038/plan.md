# Chapter 38 — *A Figure Held Edge-On*

**Days 153–159, Tallow 4–10, Y.H. 813, Fourthday to Tenthday.** A passage chapter at the seven-day cap: seven dated `\daymark` headings, each day lived in full for at least 800 words, none skipped, poured into another or summarised. Day 152 is finished and not revisited. Day 159, Tallow 10, is the first Account-day and market-day of the firing month and is the chapter's last day. Seven scenes, one per day, 16,900 words planned. Drop capital belongs to Scene 1. The first Account-day of Tallow is the only market in the chapter; the Cross is silent through Days 153–158.

**Arc:** Arc 1 — *The Cold Stone*, “a warm stone, already going cold.” The arc is not to be closed; its work here is to keep the count exact and the private records unread while the firing month gathers interest.

**Meaningful change of the chapter:** a new named character with a real role enters — **Hessa Rale**, a slate-runner from Slate Row who carries the narrow slate between the Cross and Greybank. Her record-first hand (hand to slate before eye to person) becomes the eighth such shape in the district, filed on a place of its own and joined to none. She is planted on Day 158 and seen again on the Account-day; no figure is read from her slate, no private record is joined, and the sheet must be written (at least 500 words, `design/templates/character.md`) **before her first line of dialogue**. Slug: `hessa-rale`; a name unlike any in the saga. Secondary movement: the first Account-day of Tallow happens with the printed figure unchallenged and the true subtraction still locked in Berr's box.

**Hard withholdings (continue from Ch 37):** no calling of the loan, no tenth stopping, no spending of the Seed 16 breath, no giving or cutting of the seventh year, no seventh hearing of the full note, no name or number from Dessa, no silt-leach/cause/cure for the cold foot, no sale or betrayal paid, no opening of tin or box, no reading of charcoal or any private tally, no joining of the pan's gone place to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust or the field's empty place; no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause. ## Scene 1 — The Second Working Morning

- **Day and time:** Day 153, Tallow 4, Fourthday, before first grey through mid-morning.
- **Place:** under the Vail kiln-house roof, the hearth, the kiln mouth, the Clay Yard and the yard well.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; yard women as walk-ons.

### Beats, in order
1. Open before first grey with the boy on his bed, fold at his ear, one stiff brown-edged crust two inches away with its empty place unnamed, thread of drool frozen and unwiped, hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and coming down against the fold. One hundred and fifty-three days old, five months and three days; nobody says so. No recap of Ch 37.
2. The first smell is hot clay under tallow; the first sound is the kiln's breath with its gap, not a bell. Sella feeds the fire under the back of a broken pot; it answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-three days.
3. The widow's mark hangs where it hung; do not let her touch it here. She gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*. Half-pace from every open slate.

5. At the trough by the well the water stands bare and dark; the skin of ice is not revived, and it is called weather to nobody in particular. The clay jar stands cold under its glaze, glanced at once and not touched.
6. Tarn takes the broom only after it is instructed: six counted where the house can check, the seventh where it cannot, no collector on the road, nine stoppings still nine. His stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check.
7. The boy files the opening as a thing done and gone past: a theatre with heat kept and counted behind a screen, a gate with two bites, a cost paid by somebody whose name is not spoken. He reaches an inch toward the water and comes down with the count at one hundred and fifty-three, exact and unspent.

## Scene 2 — Dust Ground Small as Meal

- **Day and time:** Day 154, Tallow 5, Fifthday, before first grey into the morning, with a short return under the roof at midday.
- **Place:** the Vail wheel-shed, the flagstones, the dry rack end, under the Vail roof.

### Beats, in order
1. Before first grey three or four men go low-voiced up the Clay Yard and into the finger's-width-open back room; Sella carries the basket past at the half-pace, turns no head, gives the house no name for them, does not say the room is cold. Two tellings of the shut counting room stay side by side with no third.
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel, and withholds that knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who says the pot is singing before its time and that such a pot generally sings itself out early. The knowing goes to the near-full tray, not among the hearings.
4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the cold damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence. ## Scene 3 — What the Arm Knows

- **Day and time:** Day 155, Tallow 6, Sixthday, morning into afternoon and the going of the light.
- **Place:** downriver at the Weir — plank walk on posts, flat blessing stone, lower boards, fish-trade shade below the sill; out-of-line landing below the north bridge.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Mira Silt, Orra Weir, the reed-cutter (walk-on), a weir woman who asks about the run, a buyer who names a figure, the soft-cuffed clerk (walk-on), a woman winding a child's foot (walk-on). The foundling is offstage.

### Beats, in order
1. The last reed work is finished and has become a direction, not a bundle. The going downriver and the coming back are counted in the arm that carries them; a cost asked as a direction is answered with the river and the weather and leaves no entry.
2. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies, gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket, and keeps her half-pace.
3. The reed-cutter straightens his grey cord on the flat blessing stone, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat with the flat of the hand. To a woman who asks what it holds: *You can ask the cord* and *No more can the Court*. No knot counted, no figure read.
4. Orra, hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered, salt white at her wrists, the grey streak first through the black hair, the nasal half-song of the Fen where she is tired, pulls one knot down with a small flat knock. To the woman who asks what the levy will take now the hands are out of the reeds she gives the date: the ninth of Ilmen, six oars, one man at the stern who never touched them; to *That isn't the levy* she says *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* The forty-first double turn stays legible and the word forty is unspoken. Whether her thumb's return is a lie or the habit of counting, the boy makes no cause of it.
5. Mira works the eels with slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, small smile kept like a door on the latch. She answers the thin-run question with her own question and answer, flicks eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, and when the buyer names a figure says *Two marks the pair and two short. You've said it twice and I've counted it twice. There's nothing in the sentence that wants an answer.* Her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; the second twist stays twice beneath the shifting creel, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's and not touching.
6. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool; the surgeon reads the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform, and the reading buys nothing. The woman calls it the cold, a neighbour with a pail calls it weather, and the two never meet. No silt-leach, cause or cure is named.
7. At the out-of-line landing the soft-cuffed clerk stays set for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate turned inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; a girl with a creel takes the landing sideways and nobody makes a fall of it.

### Emotional turn
The river work looks like simple physical labour, yet every person there is keeping a record no Court thumb can spell. The boy's surgical reading of the cold foot lands and is worth nothing: competence without a hand is the chapter's quiet humiliation.

### Sensory anchors
Eel slime dried grey on sleeves; silt pale in the neck's hollow; salt white at wrists; old-rope hands; the small flat knock of a knot set down; grey wool gone dark with a working thumb; river silt and cold fish; the almost-smile that is not peace on a dark foot; the out-of-line step walked over without a fall.

## Scene 4 — The Man in Mud End

- **Day and time:** Day 156, Tallow 7, Seventhday, morning through the going of the light.
- **Place:** the Clay Yard, the kiln mouth, the yard well, the drying racks, under the Vail roof.
- **Present:** Corin/Dan (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn; the rack woman who speaks of Mud End, a woman at the well, a neighbour with a pail (walk-ons).

### Beats, in order
1. A woman raking the kiln mouth speaks sideways of a man in Mud End taken in his sleep, quiet as a lamb, and them that found him called it merciful. Nobody makes a lesson of it and nobody puts a figure to him. The kiln's breath is a named danger in the yard and the district goes on with the work.
2. The boy reads it as he once read a danger behind a screen: heat kept and counted, a gate with two bites, the almost-smile that is not peace. He knows the order in which a man goes when the breath takes him wrong, and the knowing is worth nothing with the heat two paces off. No payer is named to the silence.
3. At the well the bucket comes up and a woman says the water tastes different; another calls it weather, and the argument ends where such arguments end, with nobody owning the day.
4. The yard goes around the clean dark line, the new finer grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates and the empty place in the returning line where the hand went in and did not come out — a look and a wide step and no number.
5. Tarn takes the broom only after its instruction and says *Yes, house* to the early thaw and the late, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument can find a foot, while wanting the late because it puts off the paying. His stroke goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without turning along it. No collector comes.
6. At the going of the light Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace, goes around the pan's gone place, the bare trough and wide around the rack space, sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needs before it goes away.
7. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale off the coals, sets the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, does not look toward the bed, does not touch the widow's mark because a dusk is not an opening, and goes to her own bed. The two rooms do not touch. Four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-six days.

### Emotional turn
A death spoken of sideways is folded into the ordinary work, and the boy's exact knowledge of it cannot be used. The household's kindness is maintenance performed without ever entering an account; the half-beat at the setting is the only thing in the room that admits anything.

### Sensory anchors
Woodsmoke going before and river wind coming up to meet it on the stair; dust ground small as meal; the almost-smile of a man taken in his sleep; the bare dark water; the basket's outward swing at the wide turn; the grey streak pale in dying light; the pot handle set two inches beyond the hand.

## Scene 7 — One in Twelve, As the Notice Says

- **Day and time:** Day 159, Tallow 10, Tenthday, the first Account-day and market-day of Tallow; morning at the Cross up to the reading block under the moot-post, afternoon at the stalls, going of the light on the clay road, small hours under the Vail roof.

### Beats, in order

The final moment is held open, not closed: the first counting of the firing month has gone up the clay road inside a locked box without changing one thing in the Vail house, the slate-runner's narrow slate has gone east ahead of the light, and the count has reached one hundred and fifty-nine with nobody able to say what it counts.
