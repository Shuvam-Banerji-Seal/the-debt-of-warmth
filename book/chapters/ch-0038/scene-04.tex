\daymark{156}{Tallow 7, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

At \place{the kiln mouth} in \place{the Clay Yard} a woman raked in the morning with her eyes on the ash and her face turned a little away from the basket, the way a person speaks to a wall rather than to a child who might repeat it. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the angle where the wicker rim becomes a wall and heard her before he saw her; the rake went over the clinker with the small dry rasp of a tool drawn across grit, and the kiln breathed behind her in fours with a gap, and the two sounds went together into one thing that the yard took no notice of. “They found him in his sleep,” she said sideways, to nobody. “In \place{Mud End}. Quiet as a lamb. Them that found him called it merciful.” Another woman at the slip tubs made a sound that was not an answer and went on with her hands in the grey water. Nobody asked which man, and nobody said what had taken him, and nobody put a figure to the quietness of it. The rake went on. The work went on. The danger had a name now in the yard --- the kiln's breath --- and the name was spoken as other dangers are spoken in a working place, sideways and once, and then put down with the tool that had said it.

He had been one hundred and fifty-six days old for the whole of that morning, five months and six days, and nobody in the yard would have known what to do with the number if a man had put it into their mouths. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board and in a glance at a jar that stood cold under its glaze; the yard kept them in the weight an arm remembers at the end of a carrying. His count had neither rim nor handle. It stood where it stood and went on standing.

The woman's sentence lay where she had laid it, and he took it up the way he took up everything in this district that people set down and walked away from: as an object with its own shape, to be turned over and not entered. It was a theatre he could not enter. The heat was kept behind a screen of brick and counted there in four low pulls with a silence where the fifth should have been; the gate in the brick had two bites, the one that holds a vessel whole and the one that can take a hand off at the wrist; and the hands that knew where the screen stood were the hands that could not go behind it and say what they had seen. Somewhere behind the brick a man had gone the way a man goes when the breath takes him wrong --- head first, the skin doing what the vessels under it do, the core holding on after the hands and the feet had given out, the almost-smile settling on the face, which is a grimace and is not peace. He knew the order of it as he knew the order in which cold arrives at a bare wrist, and had learned that the knowing is worth exactly nothing when the heat is two paces off and there is no hand long enough to reach it. No payer was named to the silence. Nobody made a lesson of the lamb, and the lamb went into no account.

\scenebreak

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the rope went over the stone lip and the bucket came up with a small slap of water against the staves. \place{the trough} stood bare and dark under its little roof, the skim that had lain on it like fat set aside in a dish gone now these four days and not revived, and the women drew as they had drawn in flood and in frost, with their sleeves pushed up and their talk going to the bucket. A woman with the rope in her hand tasted the water from her palm and said it was different.

“Different how?” another said, without turning.

“Different from what it was,” the first said, and let the bucket go down again.

“It's the weather,” the other said.

The first said nothing to that, and the second said nothing more, and the argument ended where such arguments end in this yard --- not in a winner, but in nobody left to carry it. Nobody owned the day. The water went into the pails and the pails went away, and the change in the taste stood where it had been put, a shape with no name and no cause, entered in no slate and paid by nobody. He had learned that a thing said twice under a well-roof is not therefore a thing said to anybody, and that the second voice does not answer the first; it only takes the question off the ground and carries it away level, so that nothing slops.

\scenebreak

The yard went around what it would not enter, as it had gone around it every morning of the firing month. There was the clean dark line in the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey called \lore{the thaw}, and neither had been swept. There was the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and the women went around it as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. And there was the empty place in the returning line of \place{the worked strips} where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out: a woman looked once at the space between two stooping backs, took a wide step and said nothing; another took the step behind her without looking at all. No number came out of the looking, and no name, no body, no cause, no figure and no payment had come out of it in all the days since. The boy held the absence on a place of its own and did not lay it across \place{the loaf shelf}'s two gaps, the empty place at his ear, or the round bare ring on the wall where a pan had stood out to dry through Reed. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer. No finger of his drew the line.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before; he held it a moment with the small tool he had put into his pocket when he thought nobody looked. To a woman who wanted the thaw early and a man who wanted it late he said, “Yes, house,” to both, agreeing so thoroughly that the argument had nowhere to put a foot, while wanting the late one in the private part of his head because a late thaw puts off the paying. His seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and was still held, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not, the small knife set back at the very edge with the unforged collector sentence beside it, the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval standing at the top of each reach. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood and no tenth stood up before the seventh year had been earned; a tenth stood up now would be a debt invented to make the nine into a bill. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it stayed where he kept it, unsaid.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every evening of his life. Halfway up she went around the place on the wall where the pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, tilting shoulder and basket so nothing knocked; at the well she went around the bare trough; at the yard she went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack, as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind before she gave it to any person, and never gave it to the basket. Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. The half-beat was the only thing in that room that admitted anything. It went away with the hand.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving on a row that stood in some other room of her head; then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple pale off the coals, tipped the carried coal under the back of the pot and set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not look toward the bed. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk is not an opening. She went to her own bed without explaining the count. The two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours one hundred and fifty-six stood exact, complete and unspent, five months and six days, and spoken by nobody under any roof. The crust two inches from his ear kept its brown edge and its empty place beside it, and no third came. The shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone place, the field's empty place and the crust's empty place stayed each on its own tray, and none was laid across another; no fourth likeness was made a cause. Want came up small and importunate for the crust and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Two counts in two places, and the fire's fourth below the floor making a third, and none of the three became one. The adult count and the child's body argued as they argued every day, the one dry and exact and given to keeping, the other immediate and cold and impatient for the warmth that never came within two paces of the reaching hand; both were true and the second was louder, and neither had a hand to enter anything. The fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-six days, and the boy's hand went up an inch toward the river's note and came down against the fold.
