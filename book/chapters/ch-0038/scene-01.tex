\daymark{153}{Tallow 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

\dropcap{B}{efore first grey} the room held the shape of the night in it: the hollow of \place{the bed}, the crease of the fold, the small warm place where a head had lain and was lying still, and the crust two inches from his ear with its brown edge gone leathery in the dry heat. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on his back under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} and knew the hour by no clock the house kept, only by the order of the cold, which had come cheek, then temple, then the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it kept its line. Beyond the finger's-width door the river went over itself with the low note of water over water that had been there every night of his life, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down against the fold. He was one hundred and fifty-three days old and five months and three days, and nobody under that roof said so, and nobody under that roof would have known what to do with the saying of it if somebody had put the words into their mouths. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, in a glance at a clay jar, in a space at the end of a row that no woman called a number. His count had no rim and no handle and no woman passing with a pot would have set it down. It stood where it stood. It went on.

He laid the night's file away the way a man puts instruments back into a case, without opening it to add anything and without taking anything out to keep the empties company. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six with the seventh place left as empty as it had been found; three near-full answers stayed on their own tray; the nine stoppings stayed objects with no payer named to any and no tenth stood up among them. He had learned that an empty hollow is a thing in its own right and not a place waiting to be filled, and that a man who sets an instrument into one because the hollow is there has already decided what the case is. The file lay down. Nothing in it had grown a hand during the night.

The first smell of the day was hot clay under tallow, coming through the finger's-width door before any sound of the yard; it went under the wool and along his cheek and was gone, leaving a dry mineral edge in the mouth. The first sound was not a bell. There would be no bell before \place{Tallow} 10 and no market before it, and \place{the Cross} kept its silence the way a slate keeps a figure that no hand has been set upon. The sound was the kiln's breath behind the wall, four low draws and a gap where the fifth should have been, regular as a sleeper breathing through a partition and no more addressed to him than that. Under the floor the carried fire answered in the same figure, and through the door the river kept its fifth low note, and he set the three on three places and did not lay one across the other. Two likenesses in a row are the beginning of a rule, and a rule wants a foot at the bottom of it with a name under the foot. He kept his finger between them.

\person{Sella Vail}'s \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it had hung since the first firing of the month, a thumb-length of \place{Low Kiln} clay under its glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and it was not touched in this room. Three days had gone since the hand went to it before \place{the kiln mouth}, held one breath and let go, and the rule had kept itself every hour since with nobody to praise it and nobody to see it broken. A shelf was not an opening. A well was not an opening. A morning under a roof with a basket set down in it was not an opening. The mark kept its place; the house kept its count in loaves and glances; the boy kept his in a number that no mouth under the roof would have known how to take from him if he had held it out.

\scenebreak

Sella came in from the yard with the basket on her hip, the little-door burn closing on the wicker handle and opening again, and she did not look into the basket. She gave the instruction to the wool and the pot and the fire and the broom before she gave it to anybody and never gave it to the basket, which was a vessel filled and carried and had no instruction of its own to answer. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To \person{Tarn} in the doorway she said the house wanted the yard swept before the sun came on \place{the flagstones} --- not \emph{I} want it, not \emph{you} sweep it --- and Tarn said “Yes, house” as though the sentence had been finished for him.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving on a row that stood in some other room of her head; then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals, tipped the carried coal under the back of the pot and set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. It had missed the fifth for a hundred and fifty-three days and no payer had been named to the silence. She did not look toward the bed, and her hand did not go to the widow's mark, because a morning was not an opening and a hearth was not a door.

Then she lifted him out of the basket without looking into it and carried him out under the roof, base first and weight settled, the way a pot is lifted that must not be slopped. He felt the turn in the shoulder that carried him and the small outward swing at the threshold and the settling back, and he kept the half-pace from every open slate because she kept it, though there had been no open slate within reach of the basket all morning. A woman with a wet pot asked what the firing would cost set down there; Sella sent her to the pot with “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” and carried the unanswered question past at the half-pace with nothing slopped from it.

\scenebreak

\place{the Clay Yard} had come up in the night into the hot dry smell of clay giving up its water, and the women went around what they would not enter as they had gone around it every morning since the kilns were brought up. There was the clean dark line in the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey that had gathered through the lean weeks and was called the thaw, and neither had been swept. There was the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and the yard went around it as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. And there was the empty place in the returning line of \place{the worked strips} where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out: a woman looked once at the space between two stooping backs, took a wide step and said nothing, and another took the step behind her without looking at all. No number came out of the looking. No name, no body, no cause, no figure and no payment had come out of it in all the days since, and the boy held the absence on a place of its own and did not lay it across \place{the loaf shelf}'s two gaps or the empty place at his ear or the round bare ring on the wall where a pan had stood out to dry through Reed. A fourth likeness would make a cause and a cause would want a payer; no finger of his drew the line.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark under its little roof, the skin of ice that had lain on it like a skim of fat set aside in a dish gone these three days and not revived, and a woman letting down the bucket called the water weather to nobody in particular. The weather had heard worse and answered nothing. On the wall the clay jar stood cold under its glaze; Sella's eye went to it once, as a hand goes to an instrument before the case is opened, and went away, and the jar was not touched. He had learned the order of reaching --- the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and had seen it performed by a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, a thane passing a finger over a figure before looking at the crowd, a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, a reed-cutter straightening his cord before lifting his trap, a woman reaching a finger beneath a board before her eyes went to the row, and Sella's glance at the jar before anything else. Not one of the hands explained the grammar it was keeping. He set the grammar on its own place and made no rule of it, because a rule wants a payer and no payer had been named to any of them.

Tarn took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before; he held it a moment with the small tool he had put into his pocket when he thought nobody looked, and said, “Yes, house. I'll sweep it. I swept it this morning. I'll sweep it again.” His seventh year stood one tend from its end and still held, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not, the knife set back at the very edge with the unforged collector sentence beside it, the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval standing at the top of each reach. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and he did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. No collector came. The nine stoppings stood where they had stood and no tenth stood up among them before the seventh year had been earned; a tenth stood up now would be a debt invented to make the nine into a bill.

The kiln's breath came through the wall again in fours with a gap, and the kiln mouth lay two paces off with its heat kept and counted behind the screen of its own brick. He read the opening as a thing done and gone past the way a door is gone past once a man is through it: a theatre in which the danger is kept behind a screen, and the hands that know where the screen stands are the hands that must not go behind it. Somewhere in the brick was a gate with two bites, the bite that holds the heat and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and the heat was counted while the cost was paid by somebody whose name was not spoken under any roof. He knew the order in which a man goes when the breath takes him wrong --- head first, the skin doing what the vessels under it do, the almost-smile that is not peace, the core holding on after the extremities had given out --- and the knowing was exact and worth nothing with the heat two paces off. Nobody made a lesson of it. The yard went on with the work.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every morning and every evening of his life. Halfway up she went around the place on the wall where a pan had stood out to dry through Reed, at the well she went around the trough now without its skin of ice, and at the yard she went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack, and gave no explanation of any of the turns to the basket. Under the roof she set him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it,” stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not look toward the bed. She did not touch the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-three days. The two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours he turned the file over once and laid it back without adding to it. One hundred and fifty-three stood where no mouth under that roof would have known how to set it down, exact, complete and unspent, five months and three days; want came up small and importunate for the crust two inches from his ear and for the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and got neither. The crust kept its empty place beside it and no third came. The round bare ring on the wall, the shelf's two gaps, the field's empty place and the crust's empty place stayed each on its own place and none was laid across another. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the fire missed its fifth; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places and none became one. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down against the fold with one hundred and fifty-three exact, complete and unspent in the dark. The month had gone one day further into itself without an entry, and the count went on.
