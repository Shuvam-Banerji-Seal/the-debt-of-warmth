## Chronicle

The firing month settled into its second working week. The boy was one hundred and fifty-three days old on the first morning and one hundred and fifty-nine on the last, and nobody said so. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap; under the floor the fire answered four low pulls with the fifth missing, and the river kept its note. Sella Vail gave her instruction to wool, fire, pot and broom, never to the basket, kept her half-pace, and left the widow's mark where it hung: shelf, well, morning and dusk are not openings.

The yard went around the clean dark line, the thaw, the broken birth-slates and the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. Men went low into the back room and got no name. Jorra Vail came through with dust small as meal on both forearms, withheld a pot's note from a kiln-master; his finger went into the cold damper ahead of her unfinished sentence. Tarn took the broom only after instruction; his seventh year pressed nearer its end and did not give, the stoppings stayed nine, and no collector came.

Downriver the last reed work became a direction counted in the carrying arm, which knows the weight and not the cost. The reed-cutter tied one knot low with his teeth; Orra Weir gave the ninth of Ilmen where a figure was asked; Mira Silt answered a thin run with Ask the river and kept her second twist twice beneath the creel. A child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool; the surgeon's knowledge of the cold bought nothing.

A woman raking the kiln mouth spoke sideways of a man in Mud End taken quiet in his sleep. At the loaf shelf Sella sent a neighbour's cost to the pot, then reached a finger beneath the front edge where the charcoal lay, without looking, and brought it away. On the eve of the Account-day Hessa Rale came through with a slate held edge-on to her forearm, hand to it before eye to the yard: I don't read it. I carry it. He filed the reaching as an eighth such hand, on a place of its own.

The first Account-day came with the market at the Cross. Berr Clay read one in twelve, as the notice says, called the levy and denied it with your house, never I, and went off with the locked box and its true subtraction. The bell went four, four and a gap. Sella carried the boy home and said nothing of what had been read over him. In the small hours his file was exact and worthless --- six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings with no payer, one crust and its empty place, two shelf gaps. One hundred and fifty-nine stood exact and unspent. The fire missed its fifth; the charcoal waited for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come.
