\daymark{105}{Seed 16, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the men came in. They came up the yard from the road side with their collars held high and their feet set down carefully on the trodden ash, and they went into the room off \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the shed} that stood a finger's width open, and did not shut it behind them. There had been a frost in the night and the boards had tightened with it; the door kept its width the way a door keeps a habit, and a thin seam of cold came through it and went along the floor toward the wheel. \person[Corin]{The boy} was carried in in the basket under the fold of wool and set at the edge of the work where he could see the door and not be part of what went through it. Voices came out of the room, low and few, the way a man speaks when he has laid a hand over a thing before he says its name. They did not carry. He had learned to hear the shape of a saying without the words of it, and he set this shape on a place of its own, where other shapes of the same kind lay and none of them had been entered.

\person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for them. When a woman at the rack end asked who had come in before light, he did not say the men and did not say the room, and he did not say the room was cold. He said nothing at all, and went on with what his hands were doing, and the yard went on around him the way cloth goes around a weight that has not moved. The boy watched the order of it: the door a finger's width open, the man's back turned to it, the forearms going to the wheel. He had seen that order in a midwife going to a slate before a child and in a clerk going to a record before a face, and here it ended in a silence that cost nobody a word and could be entered nowhere. He wrote no cause across it. Three likenesses in a row will make a man who wants an answer go home satisfied with the thing he has made, and the only hand here was small and outside the wool.

He came through the finger's-width-open door with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger. It was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Bone-coloured slip dried to the colour of an old bone and sat on the hair of the arm where a thrower had dipped; this went finer, into the creases and under the bent finger where a hand does not wash itself clean without knowing it has been at something. On other mornings the forearms had come out pale and dry and the boy had kept those clean ones and these dusty ones on one tray as two states of one man's two mornings. He asked nothing of either state. He had no tongue for the question of which slate had been broken and no hand to point with, and beyond that he had decided not to want the answer, because a question that cannot be put becomes a place, and he had places enough. The broken birth-slates lay under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody through a hundred and four mornings and this one.

\scenebreak

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said, in the flat voice he kept for tools and pots. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel gave its low note under the words and went on turning, and the shed smelled of damp clay and tallow and, carried up from \place{High Kiln} on a bad wind, the sweetish lead edge that sat at the back of the mouth and did not go away with water. The boy's head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. Nobody pulled it down; the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wrist.

A pot came up under his hands and he looked at it the way a man looks at a thing he has already judged and is only now asking it to confirm. “You'll do,” he said, with no welcome in the sentence and no unkindness either. He knew the full note of a Vail pot before it left the wheel --- knew it in the hand before the knuckle went over the rim and before any pot had been set down for anybody else to hear --- and he would not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln, who would call it early and make something of it. The boy knew this about him as he knew the bend of the finger and the dust in its creases, and kept both where questions can last for years. \lore{The full note} was a fact of the vessel and not a saying about it; a man who could hear it in his hands before the world heard it under a knuckle had either a gift or a trick of attention, and in this district either one would be made to mean something the man had not said. He kept the knowing on its own place and did not offer it to the kiln-master who was not in the room.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The house keeps it low overnight. Feed it before --- ” The bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the sentence, going in ahead of his hand the way it always went, and the iron took the cold out of his fingertip and left the fingertip warmer than it had been before it touched. He moved it. The end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He had not answered her with a word and had not looked at her. Her little-door burn shut on the empty air over the bench where her hand had been and opened again on nothing, and nobody in the shed asked what had closed it.

The boy watched the order of the reaching and kept it: her mouth on the instruction, his finger on the iron, the sentence going to the pot and the pot taking it. It was an obedience and it was a correction, and neither of them had given the other a word with which to call it. Under the same roof another ear kept a sound of its own and said nothing about that either, and the two silences lay side by side without touching, as they had lain since the boy came under this roof. He did not join them. A household that straightens around a silence is a household that has heard the silence and agreed what it means, and there was no \lore{Grathic} for that agreement either, any more than there was a word for the two faces turned the same way that meant a keeping and a pricing. He set the straightening on the place where he set such things and wrote no cause across it.

He read the damper once more while the iron cooled back to the temperature of the room. It was any iron that stops a thing from running: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man were one bite and one piece of iron, and no word in Grathic held both mercies at once. He had held such iron himself in a room he no longer stood in, closing it on a vessel that would have emptied a man in four minutes and closing it just as readily on a limb that had to come away, and the two acts had used the same pressure from the same hand and the same dry counting of what would be lost. Here the house called the iron a holding and never a taking, and had called it so for a hundred and five mornings. He filed the reading as an object on a tray, exact and complete and worth nothing, joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut counting room lay on another place, breath through the nose against breath made in the throat, saying \emph{not the war's hand}, and he did not bring them across to this iron and did not let a third telling come.

\scenebreak

The room off the shed kept its finger's width of open through the morning and no one went through it that the boy could see and no name was given to anyone who had. At midday the yard went around the space under the lowest rack with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken; the clean dark line where the old \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner and the new finer grey beside it lay unswept and were called \lore{the thaw} that would go when it went. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was touched at no hour, because a shed, a strip, a shelf and a rack are not openings and no opening had come. She glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and said nothing over it, and gave the instruction to the fire and the pot and never to the basket. The boy had watched that glance for a hundred and five days and did not know what the jar held or why it stood on the wall shelf and not on the bench with the pots, and he had stopped wanting to know in the way a man stops reaching for an instrument that is not on the tray.

At the going of the light she carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He was one hundred and five days old, three months and fifteen days, and nobody under the roof knew it and nobody under the roof would have known what to count if they had; there was no word in Grathic that would have carried the saying. The crust two inches from his ear was brown-edged and stiff and gone past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it kept its unnamed place with no third; in the next room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two gaps in a room that did not touch this one.

He checked the file the way a man turns out instruments on a towel. Six hearings of the full note stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, beside and never among and no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid; three intervals lay on another tray; the word forty was unspoken with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both; four low things kept their four places with the river's note a fifth and not a hearing; the damper's double bite lay filed as an object and the loan that can hear was still uncalled. Nothing had moved. Nothing had been paid. The door stood a finger's width open and the river's low muttered note came through from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for a hundred and five days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. He wanted the crust, and the breast, and the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and he could reach none of them and could ask for none. The bent finger had gone into the cold iron ahead of the sentence and the house had straightened around it, and the iron had taken the cold out of a fingertip and given it back warmer, which was a fact about iron and not about anything else, and he kept it as a fact. Nothing was joined. Nothing was paid. Nothing was named. The count stood at one hundred and five, unspent, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
