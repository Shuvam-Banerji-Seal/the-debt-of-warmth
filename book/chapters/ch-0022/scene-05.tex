\daymark{106}{Seed 17, Y.H. 813}

Before the market woke, the river wind came up the steps under \place{the north bridge} and put its cold into everything at the end of \place{the fish-trade shade}, into the wicker of the creels, into the slime on the boards, into the folds of the wool where the boy lay with his thread frozen at his mouth. \person{Sella Vail} carried him down \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set the basket at the very end of the shade where the shadow thinned and no slate lay within reach of a hand. She had chosen the place before she chose the shade. The instruction went out ahead of her hand, first to the wool and then to the water running in the gutter of the shade, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” And to the water: “Go where you're going.” Only after the wool and the water had been told did she set him down. \person[Corin]{The boy} had been carried like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and he understood that a basket set down was a pot set down and left, and that a shade, a strip, a shelf and a bed are not openings.

\person{Mira Silt} worked the eels at the outer boards where the shadow ended, with pale slime dried on her sleeves in the shape of her forearms and river silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept on her face the way a door is kept on the latch. She had a knife in one hand and an eel-bone in the other, and she flicked the bone against her thumb while she decided, and the boy, set where he could see the trade and not be seen into, watched the thumb and the bone and the order of them. He had once watched a man decide the same way over a body on a table, with the hand going to the chart before the hand went to the patient; here the hand went to the bone and then to a cord, and the chart was never written at all. Two women in this market kept two records that no Court thumb would ever read, and he set the keeping on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it.

A woman come down the steps with salt white at her wrists asked whether the run would be thin now the planting was in the ground. Mira answered the question and then her own answer, the way she always did, which gave her the room to choose which answer the listener got. “Thin?” she said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.” She moved two eel-bones against her thumb, one over and one back, while the deciding went on behind the small smile, and put one away when the deciding was done. The deciding and the not-saying were one movement of the same hand, and the boy kept them on one place without a word between them.

His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far; nobody turned it back, because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. He was one hundred and six days old, three months and sixteen days, and nobody under any roof here knew it, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would have carried the saying if they had. Nobody had told him. He had stopped expecting to be told.

\scenebreak

A buyer in a coat with the river in its hem named a figure for two pair of eels and set the figure down between them the way a man sets a stone down where he wants it to stay. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord at her waist and stayed there while she decided, and the boy watched the staying of it as he had watched other hands in other places --- a thane wiping his thumb twice before touching nothing, a clerk going to his slate before his eyes went to a face, a midwife reaching past a bare board before she looked at the child. He had seen that order in a room where the chart came before the patient and the signature came before the chart, and had never once found a way to make it clean. Here the hand went to the cord and not to the eels and not to the buyer; the figure stayed where the buyer had put it, and neither of them entered it anywhere.

When she shifted a creel to reach another pair, the grey cord rode up and showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it, the one that had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door. The knot for the thin child upriver was untouched on its own twist, and neither knot on either cord was counted by any thumb but her own. The boy counted neither of them either, because a reading made by a hand that cannot lift itself to its own chin is a reading and not an account. He had made a life's work of readings nobody would pay for. Here it was the whole of the morning's trade.

Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and did not touch it. Orra watched from where she watched, a little beyond the buyer's shoulder, and got no answer, and had got no answer at \place{the Weir} and at the flood's edge and at every market since. The two records lay side by side on two boards with a finger's width of wet air between them, and the boy set them where he set the three gaps in the two rooms under another roof, in places that do not touch. Three likenesses in a row will make a man who wants an answer go home satisfied with the thing he has made, and he had spent a hundred and six days keeping his finger off the shelf and off the knot-string both.

Mira did not look into the basket. It was a keeping set on a place of its own, the opposite of a pricing, and there was no word in Grathic for the difference between a woman who would not look and a man who would not look at the same thing for the same length of time. He had tried to find one for a hundred and six days and had given it up the way a man gives up an instrument that is not on the tray. The smile did not move on her face while the knife went on with the eels, and she said nothing about the figure and nothing about the cord and nothing about the child two paces from her knee.

He did not know what the grey cord counted or what the second twist held, and did not try to know. A count kept where no clerk's thumb will look is the only kind this district keeps honestly, and he had learned to read the shape of a kept thing in wool and cord the way he once read the shape of a kept thing in tin and slate and the fold of a sheet in a room that is no longer his. None of it could be spent. That was not the same as none of it being true.

\scenebreak

The steps came back as they always came, four and four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line where the fourth step should have been, and a whole market went over them carefully and without a fall, the way a man steps over a crack in a floor he has decided not to mend. A girl with a creel went over sideways, caught the rail and went on selling, and nobody made an accident of it and nobody put a figure to the danger. The boy, who could not have stepped over the landing or the crack or anything else, set the missing step beside the gone board upriver and the hairlines that stopped short of the edge, three places entered in no account, and made no accident of any of them.

At the landing the soft-cuffed clerk still sat with his cuff grey from washing and his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, as regular as the river's note under the bridge. He had known men who sat like that in rooms he no longer stood in --- men set at a desk before anybody had decided what the desk was for, patient enough to outlast the people who put them there. The cuff grey from washing was the only thing about him that said he had a body at all; the slate to the forearm was the part that would be entered. The boy set the grey cuff and the neat slate on two places and did not let them touch, because a man who washes his own cuff has a hunger or a habit, and a man who holds a slate at the edge where nobody else can see the figure has a use. He had sat here for weeks and nobody had taken him off and nobody had called the measure he was set to take. The boy filed him apart from the woman in the grey coat and from the collector at \place{the Drawing} with grey fingertips and from the man at \place{the Court's table} with soft hands and a patient face, four record-first hands on four trays and no fifth, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came on Seed 17. A measure set down before anybody calls it is set down ahead of a death, and the market went about its morning over the uncalled thing the way a market will.

At midday she carried him three doors down to \place{House Silt} and set the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light, away from every open slate. She opened no slate and turned no errand into a walk to \place{Slate Row}, and stood a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing. No word was spoken over him in that room and no word was carried out of it. Then she carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace, going around what the yard had left lying where it lay.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, and the breast, and the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and he could reach none of them and could ask for none.

He checked what he carried the way a man turns out instruments on a towel, and found it as he had left it: six hearings of \lore{the full note} beside and never among and no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; three intervals on another tray; the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn beside it; four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut counting room joined to no kiln with no third; the damper's double bite filed as an object; the loan that can hear still uncalled. To these he added, without entering them, two women keeping two records in wool and knots on boards that did not touch, and a clerk set at a landing for a measure nobody had called. Nothing had moved. Nothing had been paid. Nothing had been joined.

The door stood a finger's width open and the river's low muttered note came through from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed for a hundred and six days, the two sounds in two separate places. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row kept its two unnamed gaps, and in the fold one brown-edged crust lay stiff with its empty place unnamed and no third. The clean dark line and the new finer grey lay unswept and were called \lore{the thaw} that would go when it went.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. Mira's second cord lay twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted, her board beside Orra's knot-string and not touching it, and the soft-cuffed clerk's hand went to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls for a measure nobody called. The count stood at one hundred and six, unspent, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
