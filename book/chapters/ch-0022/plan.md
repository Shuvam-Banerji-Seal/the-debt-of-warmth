**Days.** 104–106 = Seed 15–17, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday, Sixthday, Seventhday). A **normal** chapter of three lived days, not a passage. Day 103 is done and is not revisited; Day 107 belongs to Chapter 23. Each day opens with its own dated `\daymark` in the scene that starts it, and each is lived in full for at least 800 words. No day skipped, poured into another or summarised.

- Scene 3 carries `\daymark{105}{Seed 16, Y.H. 813}`.
- Scene 5 carries `\daymark{106}{Seed 17, Y.H. 813}`.

**Arc.** Arc 1 — *The Cold Stone*, subtitle *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Point of view.** Close third-person past, inside Corin/Dan, carried everywhere. No departure to a Court, Waiting Room or collector interior. The old life stays offstage: the surgeon's register colours the noticing (a fist of seed read as the ninety-second sorting of twenty bodies, the order of reaching, the double bite of iron as a clamp and a tourniquet in one) but earns **no** present-tense Chicago break and **no** `\memory{}` tag. The body humiliates him on all three days (frozen drool, wandering head and the small wet sound, cold cheek–temple–wrist with the wool riding back, hand up an inch and down, no reach on a cup or his own chin).

**Meaningful change.** The direction the boy has carried for a month becomes worked ground with bodies in it. The hands the strips were said to want at last go in — counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure — and a man standing with the barrow nearly speaks the number and thinks better of it, so the district's withholding becomes something done in the planting itself. Still nothing is joined, named or paid: the exact count runs 104, 105, 106 unspent against a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances at the clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number.

## Scene 1 — The Hands Go In

**Day and time.** Day 104, Seed 15, Fifthday, morning into midday.

**Place.** The worked strips east of Low Kiln, reached through the Clay Yard; the boy carried at the edge of the work.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), field voices: a woman counting seed into a fist, a man with a barrow who nearly speaks the number of hands, rack-workers with brooms. Walk-ons only for the field voices.

**Opens with.** `\daymark{104}{Seed 15, Y.H. 813}`. No recap: the chapter starts inside the work, not by referring back to Chapter 21.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The worked brown ground the flood left holds a boot-print; brooms go around what the sweep left; hands go into the strips at last. The work is read in glances and handfuls: nobody puts a figure to hands, strips or seed.
2. A woman lets seed out of a fist and will not say what the fist holds; chaff works into the creases of her fingers. The boy reads the emptied fist as a chart closed with a neat line and a signature — the ninety-second sorting brought to a seed-strip — an exact clean reading worth nothing to a body that cannot lift a hand to its own chin.
3. A man with the barrow opens his mouth on the number of hands, looks at the strip and thinks better of it. The figure goes nowhere. This is the district's withholding performed as part of the planting (T077), not explained.
4. Sella gives the instruction to the bucket and the broom before she gives it to anybody and never to the basket; she keeps her half-pace from every open slate, goes around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and says *the house* where another woman would say *I*.
5. Berr Clay comes down from the north road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says*, says *a number goes into a slate, not into a strip* and *the levy is called. It is not called by me*, and goes off with the box in his house locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he has done the district a kindness.
6. The boy sets the direction he has carried for a month beside the number the yard will not give, and holds them on two places. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the worked ground and comes down.

**Emotional turn.** From the long-promised work arriving — a small, real lifting of the month — to the cold recognition that arrival changes nothing about the count. The hands are here, and the number is still nowhere, and the only exact thing in the field is a boy who cannot reach it.

**Sensory anchors.** Worked earth holding a boot-print; seed slipping through a fist; chaff in creases; broom bristles over brown skin; the barrow's wooden edge; Berr's twice-wiped thumb and glaze-green mouth; river wind off the Ilm; the half-pace kept around an open slate. Food: nothing eaten within reach; the house will pay in loaves later. Money: no coins, a figure that goes into a slate and not a strip. Faith: *as the notice says*, the levy called by nobody.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (104 exact against the yard's arithmetic); T077 (the near-spoken number of hands, figure into slate not strip); T058/T079 (instructions to bucket and broom, never the basket; half-pace); T009/T013/T092 (worked ground, mud, two lines left); T041/T047 (Berr's twice-wiped thumb, self-removal, locked box).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Advanced only: the direction-becomes-work movement the planting month has been building toward; the district's habit of withholding a figure now active in the work itself. Nothing paid. No figure, no joining of direction and number.

**Exact last image.** The woman's fist closes on the seed with chaff in its creases and the number of hands goes nowhere; the boy, carried at the edge, has his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the worked strip and coming down, the direction and the number still two things on two places.

## Scene 2 — Two Rooms That Do Not Touch

**Day and time.** Day 104, continued: afternoon at the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house; going of the light on the bed under the roof.

**Place.** The Vail kiln-house — the loaf shelf and the clay jar on the wall shelf; the Vail corner; under the roof.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, a neighbour at the loaf shelf (walk-on, T097 witness shape but not named as such in narration).

**Beats, in order.**
1. A neighbour reaches past the bare board, opens for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, closes on the loaf beyond and says in one breath that the old space at the row's end came in with the planting; her hand has gone to the board before her eyes went to the boy. She goes with her pot.
2. The boy files the saying beside the one already waiting there — T097 planted, no joining, no name, no payment — and keeps the shelf's two unnamed gaps in the next room from touching the fold's stiff crust and its own unnamed empty place.
3. Sella moves her lips along the loaf row and gives the house no figure; she glances at the clay jar on the wall shelf. She touches the widow's mark at no hour, because a shelf, a strip and a bed are not openings.
4. She stands over the clean dark line and the new finer grey with a broom in her hand and sweeps neither; the new grey is called the thaw that will go when it goes. The space under the lowest rack is gone around and entered by nobody.
5. She tells the fire it is the thaw; the fire takes the coal and gives back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and four days.
6. Dusk: she carries him up at the half-pace from every open slate, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, and leaves the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The river's note comes through the finger's-width-open door and the kiln's gap through the floor in two separate places.

**Emotional turn.** A near-joining refused: the neighbour has already laid a planting beside an appetite in one breath, and the boy's whole work is to keep his finger off the shelf. The refusal buys him nothing but keeps the two places clean — which is his exact, unspendable dignity.

**Sensory anchors.** Loaves pushed along a bare board; the palm's breadth of the new gap; the clay jar cold under its glaze; woodsmoke, wet wool and the sour tang of the unswept corner; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the fire's four low pulls and the missing fifth; river water over water through the door gap. Food: a loaf taken, a stiff crust uneaten. Weather: the thaw named after the fact. Faith: the widow's mark held while no opening comes.

**Threads advanced.** T060/T086 (one crust, two shelf gaps, rooms that do not touch); T097 (witness advanced, not paid); T071/T072 (104); T058/T079 (*the house*, instruction to the fire, never the basket); T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer); T009/T013/T092 (two unswept lines, left thing kept); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing; the two sounds in two places).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T097 advanced toward the far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call them an account; still not paid, no third gap, and the boy does not make the neighbour's joining his own. Nothing else paid.

**Exact last image.** On the bed under the roof with the count at one hundred and four unspent: the river's low muttered note from the Ilm through the finger's-width-open door and the carried fire's four, four and a gap through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred and four days; the boards overhead keeping four nails and four and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts not meeting.

## Scene 3 — The Bite That Holds and Takes

**Day and time.** Day 105, Seed 16, Sixthday, before first grey through midday; a short dusk under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (not entered); the damper; the Clay Yard; under the Vail kiln-house roof.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Jorra Vail, Sella Vail. The men in the back room are unnamed, unentered, unfollowed.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Men have come in before first grey and spoken low in the room standing a finger's width open; Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger — not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel — and the boy keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray without asking which slate was broken or whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody.
3. Jorra speaks to the wheel before anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.* He tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
4. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, the iron taking the cold out of his fingertip, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He has not answered her with a word and has not looked at her.
5. The boy reads the damper once more as any iron that stops a thing from running: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man are one bite, and no word in Grathic holds both mercies. He files the reading as an object, exact and worth nothing, joined to no kiln.
6. Dusk under the roof: the new grey stays unswept, one crust stays stiff in the fold, the file is unchanged.

**Emotional turn.** The house straightens around a silence rather than a word — an obedience that is also a correction neither will name. Under it, the boy's reading of the iron is perfect and valueless: he knows what the bite is and cannot spend the knowing or say it.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust in the creases past a bent little finger; bone-coloured slip on other mornings; damp clay, tallow, the sweetish lead edge off a bad wind; the wheel's low note; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; the damper's bite. Work: wheel, pots, damper. Bodies: the bent finger, the boy's hand outside the wool. Faith: the household's silence as a weather signal.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (unnamed men, grey dust, broken birth-slates under the rack); T062 (the damper's double bite filed, not paid); T076/T036 (two ears and two silences under one roof, loan uncalled); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing, file unchanged); T058/T079; T071/T072 (105).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T062 advanced toward the later day the house's danger is named; T035/T031 advanced toward the day the foundling's name is handed to the men who burn slates. **Nothing paid**: no name for the men, no opening of the back room, no slate examined, no seventh hearing.

**Exact last image.** The bent finger still at the cold iron of the damper, the bite that holds a vessel whole and can take the hand off a man with no Grathic word for both, while the household has straightened around the unfinished sentence and the boy, under the roof, keeps the reading filed as an object worth nothing.

## Scene 4 — The Road He Does Not Turn Along

**Day and time.** Day 105, continued: afternoon at the yard well and in the Clay Yard; going of the light under the roof.

**Place.** The yard well, the Clay Yard, the road toward the Fishstairs; under the Vail kiln-house roof.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Tarn, Sella Vail (brief), yard voices arguing the thaw early against late.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction. He says *Yes, house* to those who want the thaw early and to those who want it late, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went.
2. His chest has been tight since the dark. The small bench knife lies on its sill with six years standing in a mark his thumb has worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check; to the kindling he says *Six counted. The next I don't count.*
3. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back. He calls everyone *house* and says *I owe* where another man says *I am*.
4. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. He was sent once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done; he keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year and enters neither.
5. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence that would call it is never said under this roof. No collector comes. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it does not give here, and the held breath presses to its very edge.
6. Dusk under the roof: the private file is unchanged, one stiff crust in the fold, the new finer grey unswept, the river's note and the fire's gap in two places.

**Emotional turn.** The deference with a nail in it: he agrees with everybody and still wants the late thaw, and the one thing he owns — the exact account of the seventh year and the unforged sentence — is the one thing the house cannot check. The pressure is up but nothing gives; the keeping is the whole of it.

**Sensory anchors.** Worn thumb-mark on the knife handle; Ash in knuckle creases the colour of the clean dark line; the pale clip-ring catching the little light; broom over the rutted road; tight chest; the collector's road left unturned. Weather: the thaw argument ending where arguments end. Bodies: the boy's cold cheek–temple–wrist under the roof. Faith/money: the unforged sentence kept beside the seventh year like a tool set down in a dark room.

**Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (six counted, seventh held, the loan uncalled, two ears and two silences); T009 (no collector, the thing left where it lies still kept); T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing); T062 (carried forward, still filed); T071/T072 (105).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T036 advanced toward the day something must give at the seventh year's end and the loan can be called; the held breath and the unforged collector sentence are carried, not spent. **Nothing paid**: no seventh year counted, no collector sentence entered, no turn along the road.

**Exact last image.** The small bench knife on its sill with six years in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, his chest tight with the breath he has decided not to spend, the broom set away and the collector's road gone over without once turning along it; under the roof the two sounds come in two separate places and the count stands at one hundred and five, unspent.

## Scene 5 — The Board Beside Hers

**Day and time.** Day 106, Seed 17, Seventhday, dawn market; a short turn at midday; going of the light.

**Place.** The Fishstairs below the north bridge; three doors down in House Silt (midday, brief); the Clay Yard and the Vail roof.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Mira Silt. Orra Weir present only as her knot-string lying beside Mira's board and not touching it; the soft-cuffed clerk and the field/market voices are walk-ons. No Ulda, no Marra, no sixth sighting.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket at the end of the fish-trade shade where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the water and never to the basket.
2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little to the right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie.
3. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched.
4. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it. Orra watches from where she watches and gets no answer. Mira does not look into the basket; the not-looking is a keeping set on a place of its own.
5. The steps come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks over carefully without a fall. The soft-cuffed clerk still sits with his cuff grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls, filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector and the Court-table clerk.
6. Midday three doors down: the basket is set just inside the finger's-width-open door away from every open slate; no slate is opened and no errand is turned into a walk to Slate Row. Dusk: carried home at the half-pace, set on the bed under the roof, fold righted without a look at his face, thread left frozen.

**Emotional turn.** Two women keeping two records in materials no Court will read — knots and a cord — with the boards that do not touch standing for the boy's own rule about likenesses. Against that, the Court's ordinary presence: a clerk set for a measure nobody called, reaching for the record before the person, patient as a doorway. The boy files the order and buys nothing.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime and silt at the neck; eel-bones against a thumb; the grey cord and the second twisted twice beneath it; the out-of-line landing walked over without a fall; the clerk's washed-grey cuff and hand-to-slate; river wind, tallow smoke, salted eels. Food: eels sold, no crust given. Money: a figure named, a cord touched, nothing entered on a slate the boy can read. Faith: a market held over the unentered woman, the measure set before anyone calls it.

**Threads advanced.** T059/T025/T024 (Mira's second cord twisted twice, neither knot counted, board beside Orra's not touching, knot for the thin child untouched); T090/T080 (soft-cuffed clerk still set for the uncalled measure, hand to slate before eyes, filed apart — no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand); T083 (out-of-line landing, no fall); T071/T072 (106); T058/T079 (half-pace, instruction to wool and water, never the basket).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T059/T025/T024 and T090/T080 advanced toward the later convergence of the district's second records and toward the Court presence that arrives ahead of a death. **Nothing paid**: no knot counted, no word *forty*, no sixth sighting, no sale, no betrayal.

**Exact last image.** Mira's second cord twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted, her board lying beside Orra's knot-string and not touching it, and the soft-cuffed clerk's hand going to his slate before his eyes go to the stalls for a measure nobody called.

## Scene 6 — One Hundred and Six

## Pull into Chapter 23

The chapter ends on the hand going up an inch toward the water and down, the count at 106 unspent and no opening, no joining, no payment anywhere. That exact forward motion carries straight into Day 107, Seed 18, Eighthday: Tarn's seventh year will stand at the very edge of the sill with the knife where it can be checked and the seventh where it cannot, the held breath still pressed, the sentence that would call the loan still unsaid — the thing this chapter pressed to its edge and did not spend. The planting goes on under the same arithmetic, the two shelf gaps and the fold's gap still in rooms that do not touch, T097 still planted, the clerk still set at the Fishstairs, the cold foot still the cold. On Day 108 the watcher will be seen a fourth time with the half-beat and the green sliver and still no sale, and on Day 109 Account-day will read one in twelve over the worked strips with the bell going four, four and a gap. Nothing has been closed here, so the next chapter opens on the same held things, one day further on, with the seventh year still not given.
