The hand came down. It always came down, the small hand outside the wool returning to the place the fold had made for it, and the note of water going over water went on under the door without waiting for anybody to enter it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the dark with his thread frozen at his mouth and began the check the way a man begins any honest work, by turning out what he carried and laying it where he could see it.

There was no table here and no light over one, no field and no instrument within reach of a hand that could not lift itself to its own chin. There was only the towel of his attention and the objects laid upon it in the order he had learned to lay them, one beside another, each on its own place. He had carried the day in whole. He would set it down whole. That was the work and the whole of the work's worth, and he had long since stopped expecting it to buy anything.

\scenebreak

The midday room came up first under his hand. \person{Sella Vail} had set the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door of \place{House Silt}, at the edge of the light and away from every open slate; she had opened no slate and turned no errand into a walk to \place{Slate Row}, and stood a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing. In the dark he turned the room over and looked again at its writing.

\person{Alda Reed} came short into it with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, her nails scrubbed to the quick and clay still under them. She had her birth-slates under her arm. She laid the writing-down on the bench before she turned to the fold, in the order of a hand that reaches for the record before the person, and only then put her two fingers to the wool at his shoulder and pressed it flat a breath longer than a fold requires. The pressure was a kindness and it was not for him; it was for the room and for whatever the room would bear. He had learned to tell the difference without ever being taught the words for it.

Her hand went to the red edge of \lore{the birth-flower} at his wrist and crossed two fingers there before she knew she had done it. The sign came before the thought, as a hand comes down on a chart before the eyes find the name at the foot of it, and she covered it at once with the date she had given \person{Dessa Silt} --- the seventh of Stone, the account up on the eighth before the paper went out --- laying a date over a sign where a name should have stood, and a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of under a date nobody alive can take off. She did not look at the boy while she did it. She had not looked at the woman when she told her, either. The mercy had been spoken then as an entry and not as a story, and it had curdled all the way through to a fact in a face that no voice living could correct.

“Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down,” she said, exact and not true.

Then, because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry, she went on after the question had stopped. A cooper's seventh in \place{Mud End}. A potter's third child in \place{Stonemouth}. The last high water, and what the river had done to the step at the landing. The dates arrived faster than dates had any business arriving, and the room arranged itself around them the way a market arranges itself around a thing it will not address. In the back room a neighbour scrubbed at a board with lye water and did not look up. A low song came out of the scrubbing with its words flattened by work, and the song went under the door and along the floor and did not stop at the grey ridge running from the bench to the skirting. The boy lay in the basket with the song coming through the floor the way warmth comes through a cracked pot --- by degrees, and with a loss that nobody entered. A room could hold two private records and a third thing besides, and the third thing was the one a person carried away when the records were shut.

Her hand went once into her bodice to the little tin and came away without opening it. The sixth narrow slate stayed face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name, six pages in a second hand, all unread, no seventh written and nothing paid again. Above the abandoned board-bed the round pewter tin kept its pale fold at the seam and its thumb-bright rub on the lid, shut; below it the grey wool held only the hollow where the small pale river stone had lain, and nobody had said either was gone.

Two private records in two materials stood apart in one room and were carried to no door. He set them on two places and wrote no cause across the space between them. He could not read \lore{Grathic} in writing or in speech, could not ask which house held the floorboard, could not lift a hand toward either tin, and knew the shape of a kept thing in tin and slate and wool as well as any man alive. Knowing it had never once given him a way in. He had once held a young woman's chart with the nurse's note turned down and had gone past it to the signature at the foot, and the signature had been his own. He understood exactly what Alda had done at his wrist: she had covered the sign with something a clerk could read so that what a clerk could not read would stay where it lay. It was the correct treatment. It was also the wrong one, and there was no word in this country in which to say the difference. He kept the two readings on two places and went on turning the towel.

The number stayed in a thumb gone into the water and the name stayed under a scar, and the third day was still unsaid.

Sella said nothing of the writing, nothing of the tin, nothing of the fold pressed too long at his shoulder. She was a half-pace off with her burn shut on nothing, and when she lifted him it was with the care of a woman moving a pot filled that must not be slopped. The arrangement between the two women was a wall with a door in it that neither had ever opened, and the boy had been carried through that door a hundred and six times without once being asked what he had seen on either side. He had seen everything. He had been entered in neither account.

\scenebreak

He checked the rest of what he carried as the same man might check a tray of instruments after a long case: by touch, in order, knowing before he looked which ones were missing.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note}. Five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a wall, the district's own slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at \place{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh. A pot lifted a hand's breadth too high at the well had come up to the rim of a note and gone back down; the half-beat after the knock was a different object from the knock and lay on the interval tray where it had been put. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. Three intervals lay on another. The word forty was a saying on a tray of its own, unspoken in three days, with the readable double turn of a second cord twisted twice beneath a grey one beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker kept apart from both. Four low things kept their four places, and the river's note of water over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing. The two tellings of the shut counting room, breath through the nose against breath made in the throat, both saying not the war's hand, stayed joined to no kiln with no third. The double bite of \lore{the damper}, the iron that holds a vessel whole and can take the hand off a man with no word in Grathic for both mercies, lay filed as an object and joined to no kiln. The loan that can hear was still a loan that could be called, and the sentence that would call it had not been said under this roof.

He added, without entering them, two women keeping two records in wool and knots on boards that did not touch, a clerk set at a landing for a measure nobody had called, a soft-cuffed hand going to a slate before its eyes went to a stall. Nothing had moved. Nothing had been paid. Nothing had been joined.

The keeping cost him nothing and earned him nothing, which was its exact distinction. A man who keeps a record in a ledger is paid in the authority of the ledger; a man who keeps one in the head of a child who cannot speak is paid in the child's own silence, and the silence is not even his to spend. He had spent a hundred and six days becoming the only exact instrument in a house that kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances, and the instrument had no handle and no use and no hand to hold it.

He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. A man turns out instruments on a towel because he means to use them. He had turned out a whole district's kept things on the towel of his attention and there was no operation to perform, no field to open, no hand to carry any of it to a door. The file was exact, complete, and worth nothing. The count went on.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof Sella told the fire it was the thaw. She did not say whose thaw, and the fire was the better for being addressed first. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire took the coal and gave back four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, the way it had given them for a hundred and six days. Its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and six days. A man might have asked who paid for the coal that warmed the boards by degrees. There was no man under this roof who would have answered him, and the boy had stopped asking questions nobody in the room could hear.

She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and did not look toward the bed.

On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the end that had come in with the planting and the new palm's breadth that had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word. In the fold one brown-edged crust lay stiff with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. No collector had come for the woman in the brook and the morning had come all the same. The omission stood where it had been left, unentered and unnamed, and bread went on being broken over it the way bread is broken over any table. He did not call it a heresy. A heresy is a thing with a name and somebody at the foot of it to answer; this was only a thing not done, and the not-doing had gone on long enough to have the shape of a custom. Somewhere an account wanted a collector and nobody had sent one, and the morning had come anyway, with nobody to answer for it. Warmth could be left where it fell and the morning would come over it; the boy carried that plain fact on a place of its own and did not make a doctrine of it. A doctrine would have wanted a name, and a name would have wanted somebody to answer for the morning.

The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned its corner lay where it had always lain, and the new, finer hairline of grey lay beside it unswept and was called \lore{the thaw} that would go when it goes. The space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} had been gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the broken birth-slates beneath it were entered by nobody. No opening had come, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung at her throat where it hung, untouched at a shelf, a strip, a basket, a road, a door and a bed. The private roll stayed unshown.

The door stood a finger's width open. The river's low muttered note came through the gap from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed. The two sounds came in two separate places as they had come for a hundred and six days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the night wanted, riding back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and had not been wiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, and the breast, and the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and he could reach none of them and could ask for none.

Somewhere off the bed, in the shed where the wheel stood, the small bench knife lay on its sill with six years standing in a mark a thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The breath held behind it had not been spent. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence that would call it had never been said under this roof, and nothing had given yet.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. The count stood at one hundred and six, unspent, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
