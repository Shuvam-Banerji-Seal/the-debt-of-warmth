## Chronicle

On the fifteenth of Seed the hands the strips had been said to want went into the worked ground east of Low Kiln, seed out of fists in handfuls nobody counted and no figure spoken. A woman would not say what her fist held; a man with a barrow shaped the number of hands and shut his mouth. Berr Clay came down off the north road, read one in twelve as the notice says, said a number goes into a slate, not into a strip, and went off with his box locked. Sella Vail gave the instruction to the bucket and the broom before anybody and never to the basket, and said the house where another woman would have said I. The boy, one hundred and four days old and told by nobody, held the direction beside the number; his hand went up an inch toward the worked strip and came down.

At the loaf shelf a neighbour said the old place at the row's end had come in with the planting and went off with her pot, never seeing the empty place at his ear. Sella gave nobody a figure and told the fire it was the thaw; the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been.

On the sixteenth men came in before the grey and spoke low in the room off the wheel-shed that stood a finger's width open, and Jorra Vail gave them no name. He came out with grey slate dust past the bent little finger, spoke to the wheel first, told a pot it would do, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, so the household straightened around his silence. At the well Tarn agreed with those who wanted the thaw early and late, wanting the late because it puts off the paying; his knife held six counted years and a seventh nearly gone, and his broom went over the collector's road without turning. No collector came.

On the seventeenth Sella set the basket at the end of the fish-trade shade and gave the instruction to the wool and the water, never to the basket. Mira Silt worked the eels, answered a question with her own answer and put her thumb to the grey cord when a buyer named a figure, showing a second cord twisted twice with neither knot counted, her board beside Orra Weir's without touching. The soft-cuffed clerk still sat for a measure nobody had called. Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates before the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower and covered the sign with a dead woman's date, said Warm and breathing, exact and not true. Under the roof the boy found his file unchanged: six hearings and no seventh, the word forty unspoken, two shelf gaps and one crust in rooms that did not touch. The count stood at one hundred and six, unspent.
