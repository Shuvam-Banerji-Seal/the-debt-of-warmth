\daymark{223}{Grain 14, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand outside the wool lying down against the fold, and the lying-down was a posture and no more, the place a thing comes to rest when there is nothing further asked of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was the louder, as it had been on every day of his life, and he had long ago given up asking the two to agree.

The day's first sound was not a bell and there was no market in it. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping went on being a stopping with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. He was two hundred and twenty-three days old and six months and forty-three days, and nobody said either number over the basket or into the air above it; a house that kept its days in loaves and glances had no use for a figure belonging to a child it had taken in without a slate. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-three days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart.

Before the light was properly in the room he turned out what he knew the way a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth out of a person and filed where a thing out of a person must go; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid on a tray of their own; the told bell lay beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid. There was no hand among the instruments that could lift one of them. That finding did not change with the number of days and he did not ask it to.

\scenebreak

Before first grey \person{Sella Vail} carried him out into \place{the Clay Yard} as she had carried him out every morning of the harvest, at the half-pace, with the basket held level like a pot filled that must not be slopped and the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. \place[the worked strips]{The strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} were already going back under hands. A neighbour at \place{the yard well}, letting another draw, asked what the reaping would take now that the hands were back in the strips --- what it would want of a household, what a man would owe by the time it was in.

Sella's eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf back under the roof, though the wall was behind her and the jar out of sight; the glance was a thing she carried with her and spent at a well the way other women spend a look at a pot. Her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

She gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody and carried the unanswered question away level, so that nothing slopped. No figure came off it. Nothing was priced, refused or entered.

A man come up off \place{the clay road} with a number in his fist brought it toward the well. He had shaped the number in there with his thumb tucked over it, the way a man shapes a thing he is not sure he wants to let go, and he looked at it a moment as if to satisfy himself it was still the right one. Then he put the fist in his sleeve and went off, and the figure arrived nowhere. No slate took it, no mouth gave it, and the boy, who had watched a hundred such numbers come up out of fists and go back into sleeves, kept the shape of the fist on a place of its own and entered nothing. A number that arrives nowhere is not a number kept back; it is a number that has not been paid, and the two are different objects.

He took the question apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, because it was his habit and because there was no hand among his instruments to enter anything. What was it. Where had it come from. What did it ask. Who had paid. It was a cost asked in one direction and answered in another: asked toward a figure and answered with a pot and the weather, which were two things that had never held a figure and never would. It had come from a woman at a well who wanted to know what the year would take before it took it. It asked for a number to set against the grain. Nobody under any roof had paid a number to it, and the house had said so without saying so, which was the house's whole manner. He filed the asking and the answering on a place of their own and did not lay them across the fist's number or the covered bundle under the roof, and named no payer to either. A cost asked as a direction is not an account until somebody turns it around and finds a payer standing at the other end, and nobody in this yard was standing there.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the district's first arithmetic of Grain went on unread. The printed figure stood at one in twelve and stood unchallenged because a figure printed on a notice has no mouth to challenge with; the figure read aloud over a basket on Grain 10 was still not the figure paid, and had been read twice and was no closer for the twice; the true subtraction lay locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up the road toward \place{High Kiln} and answered nobody who had not the key; \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate had gone up toward \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve and had not been read by the woman carrying it; and the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at the \place{out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. All of it lay in the boy's file where he had put it and none of it reached back. He did not open any of it, because there was no hand to open it and because an opened figure wants a payer under it and none stood under these. A district may change its arithmetic and go on not knowing what it changed, which is a thing a surgeon knows about a chart: the neatness of the writing is not the truth of the wound.

Beside the hearings but never among them, the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, three days gathered now and gathering nothing in the gathering. The green had come to the mouth of a silent woman at \place{the pot board}, clay under her nails in the small crescents and a pale line across the back of one hand; the shape had come at a market's edge, a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull and a head that turned toward a set-down pot a half-beat late. He had filed the one as a colour and the other as a shape on places that do not touch, with a finger between each and the hearings and no name spoken over either. No eighth had come, no second look had been joined to the first, no loaf had passed, no slate had come near, no sale had been made, and neither had been promoted into a hearing of the full note, a seventh of it, a cause, a name or a romance. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had put none under either row, and he had not called either by a woman's name or a child's.

The yard went around its absences as it goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it: wide, with the eye on the pail and the step set outside the danger and no word about why. The clean dark line where Ash had turned kept its place and the new finer hairline of grey beside it, called the thaw, had not been swept. The rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} kept its broken birth-slates and entered them into nobody. The shallow clay pan was gone and its round ring stayed where the ring was, with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no chip to say how. \place{the trough} at the well stood bare and dark and its water was called weather by people who did not taste it. The field's empty place kept its place and the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, was gone around with a look and a wide step and no number --- no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. A man who stood at the yard's edge and wanted these to stand up together would have had a row of them and the row would have wanted a payer. No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause of any of them. He kept them on separate trays and no finger of his drew the line between any two, for a likeness is not an account and an account is a likeness that has found a payer, and no payer stood under any of these.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof Sella came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word --- and she gave nobody a figure, though the first bundle of the year's grain lay covered near the fire behind her. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The boy lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion, and he put it where it belonged and laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. At a morning and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-three days, because a morning, a well and a shelf are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other mornings and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled. No collector came up the clay road on Grain 14. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said, and told a pot “You’ll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. He let the three shapings of the pot saying stand as sayings about a pot with no lesson in them and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates neither given nor withdrawn.

With no breathing number anywhere in the house and Sella gone about her work with the two rooms not touching, he put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down before the counted breath had finished --- down with the hold still ahead of it, an honest fraction shorter than the whole of one of the breaths she counts at her fire. It was a miss with no failure hidden under it, filed on the sixth place beside the others with no payer named to it. It was not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle Sella had set out, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The well's answer had gone to the pot and the weather and the fist's number had gone back into the sleeve, and neither had been given or entered. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
