The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat went off the high air first and came down under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot and into the fold where one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust kept its empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to lie there and make the row come out even. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} with the thread of drool at his mouth frozen in the dry heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was the louder, and he had long since stopped asking the two to agree.

As the light went off the high air and gathered under the shelf, the room smelled of hot clay under tallow, with wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had kept it since the first, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-three days. Through the finger's-width door the river held its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays. The trays did not touch.

Then: the grain came in.

It came the way field things come into a house that keeps its days in loaves and glances --- not announced, not entered, set down. Hands came out of \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} at the going of the afternoon with the first cut grain of the year in a cloth, and \person{Sella Vail} took it from them at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} and carried it under the roof and set it near \place{the hearth} and covered it with a fold of wool, so that it lay where a man might step around it without seeing what it was. She gave nobody a figure and no slate was opened. The hands that had cut it read the weight of it in their arms and let their arms keep the weight, which is a record in muscle and knows nothing of cost; a man can carry a thing that way across a whole field and never once know what it would take to buy it back. The yard counted the grain in glances and handfuls, the way it counted loaves and the space where a loaf should have stood, and the count arrived nowhere and was entered by nobody.

He took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, because it was his habit and because there was no hand among his instruments to enter anything. What was it. Where had it come from. What did it ask. Who had paid. It was a weight that had been carried in from a field and set under a roof, and the question of what it cost had gone that morning to a pot and the weather and had come back with neither, and now the thing itself lay covered a little way from the fire and answered to none of it. It had come out of ground that hands had gone back into after a month of nobody going in. It asked for nothing of him that he could give; a covered bundle asks only to be left covered. Nobody under the roof had paid a figure to it and nobody had been asked to, and the house had said so on the morning the levy was read and had gone on saying so every day since without ever using the word. He filed the bundle on a place of its own beside the row's two unnamed gaps and the one crust in the fold, and he did not lay it across either and named no payer to it. A first grain kept is a grain kept; it is not a number kept, and a row of three kept things is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. No payer stood under these.

The afternoon went on around the covered bundle. Light came in low through the finger's-width door and lay on the floor and went off it, and the bundle kept its place and its weight and its covering, and nobody lifted the fold to look at it a second time. That was the house's way of keeping: cover the thing, go around it, give the instruction to the pot. He had learned to read that way of keeping as other men read a chart --- the thing entered nowhere is often the thing most carefully kept --- and he had learned also that a chart read at two paces is worth nothing without a hand to carry it. His hand lay down against the fold outside the wool and kept its place, and the place was a posture and no more.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word --- and she gave nobody a figure, though the first bundle lay covered behind her near the fire. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The boy lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion, and he put it where it belonged and laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. At an afternoon and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-three days, because an afternoon and a shelf are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept to the day without a story. The salt-stiff coat held his broader shape on the peg with the crease settled at the left shoulder where the hook had pressed it for two years and more; the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and hollow on that side and nobody filled it, not Jorra, though he came and went past it with dust on his forearms. Along the skirting board the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned lay where it lay, and the new finer hairline of grey beside it, called the thaw, had not been swept, and the two lines kept their two places and did not become one line, for a line left where it is is still a line kept. His proverb went to the pot and not to a person --- \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- and went to a pot with no crack in it, said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, and cost the more for it now there was nobody to say it in his own voice. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the talk as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, careful and wide and with no word for the thing gone around; it was neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled, because a debt nobody names cannot be paid and cannot be settled and cannot be finished. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and twenty-three days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of any of it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don’t count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on Grain 14. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn’t care whose hands. It cares if they’re steady,” he said, and told a pot “You’ll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. He let the three shapings of the pot saying stand as sayings about a pot with no lesson in them and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light she carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, the basket held level like a pot filled that must not be slopped and the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Woodsmoke went before them in long low bars and the river wind came behind with water over water, and the two did not mix but lay over one another the way a board lies over a board. She went wide around each absence as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, with the eye on the thing and the step set outside the danger and no word about why: the clean dark line along the skirting board, the new finer grey called the thaw, the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} bare and dark with its water called weather by people who did not taste it, the field's empty place, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number --- no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. A man who stood at the yard's edge and wanted these to stand up together would have had a row of them and the row would have wanted a payer, and no payer stood under any of them. No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause of any of them, and the first grain under the roof did not join them either, lying covered where it had been set. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost; it had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and twenty-three days and had never once entered what it carried. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen.

\scenebreak

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Sella had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and the two rooms did not touch. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof, but the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. He put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and stayed; with nothing in the house to hold it against he kept it to the river's own low interval --- draw, slack, small pause --- and brought it down on the next low note. The one-rhythm coordination was remembered, not a change made again; he had kept it before this, on another day when there had been no count anywhere in the house to keep it against, and he had not made it new here. It was an honest answer to a house with no number in it. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. It was not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle she had set out, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, and cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at his wrist with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The first grain lay covered beside the fire and the count stood exact and unspent. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
