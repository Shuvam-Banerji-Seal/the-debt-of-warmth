\daymark{224}{Grain 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The day came in as a smell before it came as light under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof: hot clay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand outside the wool lying down against the fold, the posture of a thing set on a bench when there is nothing more asked of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place unnamed, and no third came to lie there. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was the louder, and he had long ago stopped asking the two to agree.

He was two hundred and twenty-four days old and six months and forty-four days, and nobody under the roof knew a number had been reached, or that there was a kind of account in which a life might be set down at all. The count was his, a habit he had brought across the crossing and could not put down, exact and complete and worth nothing to anybody but the man who kept it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had kept it from the first, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river held its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. First sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell; no market stood under this roof and no slate lay open within two paces of the basket. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays. The trays did not touch.

Grain 10 stood three mornings behind and Grain 20 stood five mornings ahead, and neither was brought early into this ordinary working morning. The district's arithmetic had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and lay outside the roof like weather: the printed one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read over the basket still not the figure paid, the true subtraction locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up toward \place{High Kiln}, \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate gone up unread toward \place{Greybank}, the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. All of it lay in his file as a chart read at two paces, worth nothing without a hand to carry it, and none of it reached back under the roof to be opened, copied or paid. A district can change its arithmetic and go on not knowing what it changed, and the house had kept its own days in loaves and glances through every reading.

\scenebreak

The harvest's third working morning came with hands going back into \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} and the first cut grain of the year coming out of them in the going of the afternoon. \person{Sella Vail} took it at the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} and carried it under the roof and set it near \place{the hearth} and covered it with a fold of wool, so that it lay where a man might step around it without seeing what it was. She gave nobody a figure and no slate was opened. The hands that cut it read the weight of it in their arms and let their arms keep the weight, which is a record in muscle and knows nothing of cost; a man can carry a thing that way across a whole field and never once know what it would take to buy it back. The yard counted it in glances and handfuls, the way it counted loaves and the space where a loaf should have stood, and the count arrived nowhere and was entered by nobody.

And with the returning hands the yard went around the empty place in the returning line once more --- the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest --- with a look and a wide step and no number: no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. He filed it beside the disappearing hand's other goings-around and forbade it to stand up with the shelf's two unnamed gaps, the fold's one crust and its gap, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place. For a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood under any of these, and a row of kept things only calls itself an account when somebody is willing to find a payer to stand beneath it. No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause. The grain lay covered near the fire; the place in the line lay gone around; the two kept their two places and did not become one keeping, though a man standing at the yard's edge and wanting them to might have had a row of them and a payer to hunt for under it.

A wrong wind came up off High Kiln with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} on it, and a kiln-hand's voice went over the boards at the yard's edge and let one of the three shapings of the pot saying stand: a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. The voice went on to the price of barley and the weather and gave no owner, no room, no hand at the foot of it and no reason. He took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, because it was his habit and there was no hand among his instruments to enter anything. What did it name. A pot. Where had it stood. On every row, come the thaw, which is a direction and not a room. What did it ask. Nothing of him. Who had paid. Nobody under any roof had paid a figure to a saying. He left it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and unpaid, a saying about a pot with no pot lost in this yard. No fourth likeness stood up out of it to make a cause, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. A voice from a direction is not a witness, and a word about a pot is not a word about a person; the saying cost no pot and made no lesson.

\scenebreak

A neighbour at the door carried a cost-question about the middle tend, and Sella sent it to the pot and the weather with \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, and carried the answer away level with nothing slopped. At \place{the yard well} a rack woman asked what the reaping would take now that hands were back in the strips, and the bare \place{the trough} answered dark with its water called \lore{weather} by people who did not taste it; nothing was priced, refused or entered. A man off the clay road brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere.

Under the broom the yard went around its old absences as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, with the eye on the thing and the step set outside the danger and no word about why: the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned, the new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, the bare trough, the field's empty place and the returning-line place. Each had its look and its wide step and its no number. The walked-around things stood in a row, and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did.

Three working mornings had gone over the seventh green and the seventh shape and changed nothing in them. The green was still a colour at the mouth of a silent woman at \place{the pot board}, and the shape still a shape in cast-offs at the market's edge with silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists and a sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull. No eighth of either came, no second look was joined to the first, no name was spoken over either, no loaf passed, no slate came near the basket and no sale was made. Neither woman nor child was called by a name in the telling of it. Six greens behind a seventh make a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh make a row of shapes, and a row is a row until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He kept a finger between the two trays and the hearings, and the colour stayed a colour and the shape a shape.

Back under the roof Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word --- and she gave nobody a figure, though the first bundle lay covered behind her near the fire. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion. He put it where it belonged, laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. At a morning and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-four days, because a morning and a shelf are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and nothing in it was entered.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on Grain 15.

To the woman who wanted the thaw early and the man who wanted it late he said “Yes, house” to each, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them.

Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. He let the three shapings of the pot saying stand as sayings about a pot with no lesson in them and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, the promise of the boy's name to the slate-burners neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Sella had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and the two rooms did not touch. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go at her fire, a count she keeps toward no opening and has never offered to any ear under this roof, but the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. He put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, and came down at once against the fold, under conditions that had sometimes answered and this time did not. It was an honest miss and no failure was hidden under it, and he filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. It was not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle she had set out, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe.

The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, and cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at his wrist with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. The first grain lay covered beside the fire and the place in the returning line had been gone around for the third day and was still without a number. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof, and the count stood exact and complete and unspent at two hundred and twenty-four, spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}.
