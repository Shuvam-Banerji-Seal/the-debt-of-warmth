\daymark{222}{Grain 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay} came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand outside the wool lying down again against the fold, and the lying-down was the whole of what had happened in the small hours: the hand had gone up an inch, been taken through the small intake and the hold and the letting go of a breath counted in a room that did not touch this one, had kept through the little silence after that count had ended, and had come down on the river's next low note. Then it lay down against the fold as a man lays a tool on a bench when the work is finished, with no word about the work and no looking round to see whether anybody had watched it. It was a coordination and nothing more --- not a sign, not a growth, not a reaching, not a power, not a thing to be spent or explained. The room that warmed him and the room he could not enter kept their old wall between them, and the fire went on in the one and the river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping went on being a stopping with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. He was two hundred and twenty-two days old and six months and forty-two days, and nobody said either number in his hearing or over his head; a house that kept its days in loaves and glances had no use for a figure that belonged to a child it had taken in without a slate. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-two days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it: it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform; he knew the afterdrop when he saw it and he wanted the crust, and the child's wanting was the louder of the two, as it had been on every day of his life. He could not lift his chin two inches to the crust, close his hand two inches on the rim of the basket that held him, or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. He had learned to hold the humiliation and the small capacity on separate trays and let neither stand up as a lesson about the other.

\scenebreak

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and twenty-two days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule --- a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and a fourth telling, of a slate burned somewhere in a direction over the boards, stood on a place of its own with a hand between the three sayings and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three. None was promoted into the hearings, no cause stood up, no lesson was made, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. Four tellings of a burning were not four witnesses to one fire, and a word about a slate was not a word about a pot; no slate had burned under any roof and no new fire had been lit at the kiln mouth, for a fire would have cost something and the telling cost nothing.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, Grain's first reading gone twice over a basket and still not the figure paid, the true subtraction locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the road, answering nothing for the glaze-room hand, and the printed figure standing where it had always stood. Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, which had come and had still not given, and no payer named to any. The breath from Seed 16 drew at the top of each reach and was not spent, and the loan went on being uncalled.

Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction at the iron on the morning of Day 161, the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166, and the flagstone half-check on Tallow 22. A fourth place held the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone in \place{the Debt Loft}; a fifth held the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand, read at the well on Day 187 and on the carried body on Day 188, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces; a sixth held the held inch, with each answer put there one at a time and no payer named to any. Three held changes were not a pair and not a doctrine, and a fourth, fifth and sixth place beside them did not make one either, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood under any of them.

Beside the hearings but never among them, the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, gathered now beyond counting and gathered nothing in the gathering. The green had come to the mouth of a silent woman at \place{the pot board} and the shape had come at a market's edge, a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull; he had filed the one as a colour and the other as a shape on places that do not touch, with a finger between each and the hearings, and no name spoken over either. No eighth had come, no second look had been joined to the first, and neither had been promoted into a hearing, a seventh of the full note, a tenth stopping, a cause, a name or a romance. Six greens behind a seventh green made a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh shape made a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had put none under either row.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before the basket, the back of her hand first and the face withheld, as she had come to it ten thousand mornings. The instruction went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and kept the half-pace from every open slate as if the floor were laid with things that must not be touched. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Standing with her back to the basket she moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word; she gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The boy lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on; it was a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion, and he put it where it belonged and laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. At a morning and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-two days, because a shelf, a morning and a bed are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and Dessa's cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept to the morning without a story, as he had been kept to every morning since the water went. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder holding a broader man's shape, and the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left and nobody had filled either. In the corner the clean dark line where Ash had turned kept its place, and the new finer hairline of grey beside it, called the thaw, had not been swept; the two lines lay in the returning order and no broom had been given an instruction about either. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The debt against a firing of tiles went on circling without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. No lesson came out of it and no collector came up \place{the clay road} for it; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and twenty-two days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. He brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, filed it as a remembered stopping and not a new one, counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and went on wanting the late thaw because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up the clay road on Grain 13.

At the edge of the wheel-shed \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the morning's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times and arrived nowhere, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. When Sella's voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Word went over the boards that the harvest had come to \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} and that hands were going back into them. The first cut grain was counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure --- a direction with no number at its foot, a thing the yard could carry and set down and not enter. The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: what was it, where had it come from, what did it ask of anybody, and who had paid. It was grain, it had come out of the strips, it asked for hands and shelter under a roof, and nobody under any roof had paid a figure for it or for the place in the returning line that went around with a look and a wide step. He filed it on a place of its own and did not lay it across the planting's old directions or across the number the yard would never give. No name came up for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the last hands, no cause stood up for it, no accident, no body, no figure and no payment; no third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause of the field's empty place, the pan's gone round ring, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the bare trough and the fold's one crust, and no finger of his drew the line between any two of them.

A neighbour at the door with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the reaping would take now that hands were back in the strips. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” She carried the question away level, with nothing slopped. At \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark with its water called weather, and a rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked the same thing of the weather and was answered with the weather; a man come off the clay road brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere and was entered by nobody. Nothing was priced, refused or entered.

Asked in the morning, the hand went up an inch toward the note and came down at once against the fold --- a miss with no failure hidden under it, filed on the sixth place beside the others, laid across nothing, with no payer named to it. It was an honest answer to a question the house had not asked; the inch was a physical first worth nothing and he did not spend it or explain it. Grain 20, the second Account-day and market, waited beyond this working week and was not brought early into an ordinary morning; there was no bell and no slate opened under the roof.

The first reapers were going out toward the worked strips as the light came up, and the yard was already going around the empty place in the returning line with a look and a wide step and no number. The boy lay two inches short of the pot handle with the thread frozen at his mouth, one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear and its empty place still unnamed. The hand was down against the fold. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
