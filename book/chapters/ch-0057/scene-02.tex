The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat left the high air first, the way it leaves the top of a stone when the sun goes behind a roof, and settled low: under the loaf shelf, behind the pot, into the fold where one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust held its empty place beside it unnamed and no third came to make the row come out even. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle with the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, for nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Under all three of the settled places ran the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it and had neither warrant to go further nor leave to go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was the louder, as it had been on every day of his life, and he had long ago stopped asking the two to agree.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-two days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low fifth thing never counted among the hearings, and overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because to agree them a man would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart. Two hundred and twenty-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof; six months and forty-two days of it were a fact in his head and a fact with nowhere to be set down.

\place{The worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln} had been worked since the planting, and now they gave. Hands came out of the line at the going of the light with the first cut grain, and the yard went around the empty place in the returning line the way a woman carries a pail of hot water past a child's knee --- wide, with the eye on the pail and the step set outside the danger, and no word about why. One hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the last hands. The yard went around its place with a look and a wide step and no number. No name came up for it, no cause stood up, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand had been the one, no figure and no payment. The place was gone around and that was the whole of what the yard did with it, the way a pot is left where a pot stood and is not spoken of as having stood there.

A bundle of cut grain was set down near the fire under the roof and covered with a fold, and the doer of it gave nobody a figure. The carrying arm that set it down was a record in muscle: it knew the weight of the bundle the way it knew the weight of the basket it had carried up and down this yard for two hundred and twenty-two days, and it knew nothing about cost. Cost was a question asked in one direction and answered with the pot and the weather, and the pot and the weather had answered this one by giving grain and asking nothing back in figures. The bundle took its place on a tray of its own under the roof and no slate was opened for it and no quantity passed from a fist to a mouth to a slate. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and it kept this in neither; it kept it by covering it.

He took the harvest apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, because it was his habit and because there was nothing else to do with a thing that had no hand to enter it. What was it. Where had it come from. What did it ask of anybody. Who had paid. It was grain, dry husk and cut straw and the fine dust of it already working into the creases of the wrists of the hands that had carried it; it had come out of the worked strips east of Low Kiln; it asked for hands and shelter under a roof, and both had been given; and nobody under any roof had paid a figure for it or for the place in the line that went around with a look and a wide step. He filed it on a place of its own and did not lay it across the planting's old directions or across the number the yard would never give, and he named no payer to it. A field that gives back what the fist would not count is a field giving back, and nothing in the giving stood up to call itself a harvest's account.

No third gap came and no fourth likeness stood up to make a cause of the returning-line place, the gone round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood behind \place{the drying racks}, the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, \place{the trough} bare and dark with its water called weather, and the field's empty place. Five absences, if a man stood at the yard's edge and wanted them to stand up together. They stayed five. He kept them on separate trays and no finger of his drew the line between any two of them, for a likeness is not an account and an account is a likeness that has found a payer, and no payer stood under any of these.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the loaf shelf} with her back to the basket, as she had come to it ten thousand afternoons, the back of her hand first and the face withheld. She moved her lips along the loaf row and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word --- and she gave nobody a figure, though the first bundle of the year's grain lay covered under the roof behind her. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. The boy lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on; it was a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion, and he put it where it belonged and laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. At an afternoon and a dusk she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-two days, because an afternoon, a shelf and a dusk are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept to the afternoon without a story, as he had been kept to every afternoon since the water went. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder holding a broader man's shape, and the seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow and its lean to the left and nobody had filled either. In the corner the clean dark line where Ash had turned kept its place, and the new finer hairline of grey beside it, called the thaw, had not been swept; the two lines lay in the returning order and no broom had been given an instruction about either. His proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. The debt against a firing of tiles went on circling without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. No lesson came out of it and no collector came up \place{the clay road} for it; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and twenty-two days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, neither entered. He brought the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, filed it as a remembered stopping and not a new one, counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and went on wanting the late thaw because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up the clay road on Grain 13. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times and arrived nowhere, a direction out of \place{Slate Row} with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. He let the three shapings of the pot saying stand as sayings about a pot with no lesson in them and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates neither given nor withdrawn.

Sella carried him home at the going of the light as she had carried him up, at the half-pace from every open slate, with woodsmoke before her in long low bars and the river wind behind carrying the taste of water over water. The yard went around each absence as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it --- the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the gone round ring behind the drying racks, the bare dark trough, the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in and not come out --- each with a look and a wide step and no number. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost; it had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and twenty-two days and had never once been asked what any of it cost, and it went on carrying. Under the roof she set him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed, and the covered bundle of the first grain stood near the fire, counted in glances and by the carrying arm's weight and entered on no slate.

With no breathing number anywhere in the house, Sella gone to her own bed before the small hours and the two rooms not touching, he put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and stayed up for the small intake only, and came down before the letting go had finished --- an honest fraction shorter than the whole of one of the breaths she counts at her fire, filed on the sixth place beside the others with no payer named to it. It was a coordination and a fraction and nothing more: not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The harvest was housed under the roof and counted in glances and never in a figure; the place in the returning line was gone around in the yard with a look and no number. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
