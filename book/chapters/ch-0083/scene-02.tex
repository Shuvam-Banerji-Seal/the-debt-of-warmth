\person{Sella Vail} carried him down in the afternoon at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, with the fold laid over \person[Corin]{the boy}'s ears against the river wind and the instruction going to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. The light had thinned without ending; it lay on \place{the Clay Yard} like tallow poured thin and gone cold, and under it the yard went about its ordinary work of not touching what it had agreed not to touch. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on Firstday, and the district kept the face it turns to the world on a day when nothing is due: a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the Ash ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the skinned water in \place{the trough}; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out. Each got the look and the wide step, and Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody --- \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out} --- and did not sweep any of them.

On the north lip of \place{the yard well} the stayed \lore{frost-bloom} kept its white fronds long past the hour when it should have gone with the sun; the trough wore its skin from edge to edge; and the plate of ice lifted off the water stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded with the silt that had risen into it as it froze. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them. He kept them on three places of his own with a finger between every pair, and made no fourth likeness of them to call a cause, and named no payer to any. The stayed frost was a covering laid over a depth; the skinned water was the same covering laid over a different depth; the clouded plate was a piece of the depth lifted up and set where no foot would go. They were three, and a joining of any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood. He did not call the weather a kindness, or a promise, or the face of anything that had been left; it was weather, and out of the path of feet.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from \place{the hearth}, and she lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning's, lighter than the morning before, and gave it no figure. Three readings had gone over it in the district and not one of them had known what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and the grain lay where it lay under the wool, changed in state and unfigured. The surgeon's hand turned a wound to see the hidden part and turned it back; the boy's hand lay outside the wool and went nowhere. He had once known the weight of a spleen in two hands and had now no hand to lift a corner of wool, and the knowing and the not-lying stood edge to edge with a finger between them and made no doctrine.

Heat had been drawing back from him all afternoon the way heat goes out of a dish lifted off the coals and set on a cold stone: it left his cheek first, then his temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay. He took the inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; the knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches on the handle of the basket that held him. Both were true, and the second was louder, and the wanting went on under the knowing the way a coal goes on under ash.

Three hundred stood exact and complete and unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty days were told by nobody. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. The round figure was a threshold kept and not spent; it was not a milestone spoken over the basket, and no hand under any roof lifted it and called it a number. He had stopped expecting to be told what it counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. The three arithmetics of Frost, with their true subtractions, rode locked up \place{the clay road} in a box toward \place{High Kiln} with one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid; three readings were three and not one account, and the finding stayed on a place of its own with no payer under it. The nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the strip of notched wood stayed unread in a sleeve not under this roof, the ninth hand staying nine with a finger between it and the eighth; no tenth was filed, and nine must not stand up and call themselves a rule. He did not lay the kept finding across the house's \lore{not-doings} or across his own gaps, and did not bring any of it into the afternoon as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment.

At the well a man come down from \place{Cold Acre} waited his turn with a hare across his shoulders. It was laid there the way a man lays a coat when his hands are full --- the hind legs hanging along one collarbone, the head tucked in against the other, the weight settled where the shoulder could best carry it --- and the yard judged the day's carrying by the arm that had done it and not by any figure. The beast was lean and long-eared and stiff already in the dry cold, with the grey of the hill frost still caught in the fur at its throat. Nobody asked what it weighed. A man who had carried it from the ridge could have told you what it weighed in the only currency the yard used, which was the ache in the carrying arm, and that was a thing a man knew in his muscle and not in a slate.

A woman waiting with a pot at her hip looked at the hare and asked what the carrying cost. The man told her the weather. He did not say a figure and did not shape his mouth around one; he shifted the hare a hand's breadth higher on his shoulder, as a man shifts a load that has begun to tell on him, and went off toward \place{the north gate road} with it, and the woman went on with her pot. Nobody turned twice. No slate was opened, no price was spoken, no name was given to the man or to the beast, and nobody followed him to see where the hare went. The month's trade came into the yard as a direction and went out of it as a direction, judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and left no account behind it.

The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and took the exchange in its order, the way he took a wound apart by what was there and by what was not. A cost had been asked and answered with the weather; a carrying had been shown and weighed by an arm and not entered; a man had gone off toward a road and no one had made a procession of it. He did not join the hare to the missing hand, or to the covered grain at the rack end, or to the cold foot nobody here would warm, or to the silence where the fifth pull should have stood under the floor. A cost asked as a direction and answered with the weather left no entry; a hare carried across a man's shoulders was a carrying and not a figure, and the winter's hunting was a direction in the dark beyond the finger's-width door and not a destination anyone had invited him to. He had once priced twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen which of them would live, and now the only arithmetic open to him was the two inches of air between his mouth and a crust gone leathery. The hare went over the shoulder and down the road, and nothing in the basket was the richer or the poorer for it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not. The small knife lay at the very edge with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it --- the sentence about the man who was sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt --- and neither was entered. He had told the wood the same thing every evening of this month and told it again now: \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the other sentence came up to his teeth as it always did --- the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it --- and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stood there, with no figure on it and no name. He did not say the word. It was the ninth stopping, remembered, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; he drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it.

\emph{Yes, house}, he said to the ones who wanted the thaw early, and \emph{Yes, house} to the ones who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot. He went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and had not moved. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the small interval at the top of each reach showed where the breath should have gone in and had not. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on another day and nobody named it. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and said nothing about the ear, and nobody asked him about the room.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men had come before first grey and gone low into \place{the back room}, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound, a door kept a finger's width open and low voices that went into it and stayed there.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he told it, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; the knowing was advanced and not paid, and he would not say it before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He read one piece of iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, and he filed it joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness, and the three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood beside his eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair, three and not a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and nothing of that saying stood anywhere to be asked. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket or the bed, and the boy did not make a fourth likeness of any of it or lay the withheld note across the day's hare or the kept finding.

At the going of the light she went to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the grammar of \place{the Vail kiln-house}, and it held at dusk as strictly as at first light or on an Account-day: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. Sella told the coals it was the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and she did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour and a going of the light is not an opening. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next stood in Bone's first firing if the household fired, earned by Bone's own light, and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward \lore{water over water}. From the next room, whose floor does not touch his, two small intakes stood at once --- the nearer one and one further off --- and he held them apart with a finger between, neither carried into the other, neither given the other's note. The nearer ended early, before the further had finished its holding, and he kept the early ending whole as an absence rather than filling it to make the two come out even; an absence kept whole is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The hundred and seventy-sixth answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off at his ear, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing bought by it could reach two inches.

Three hundred stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the afternoon of the first of Bone: no figure came off the hare or the grain or the weather, no fourth likeness was made a cause, no tenth stopping stood up, no collector came up the road, and no fifth pull was supplied to make the row come out even. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at \place{the bed}'s head with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The plate of ice stood clouded with silt on the flat stone out of the path of feet, and the hand came down on the river's low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap.
