\daymark{301}{Bone 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

The second morning of Bone came into \place{the Vail kiln-house} the way the first had, by degrees and without announcement, taking the room one board at a time in the small hours while \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and nobody under any roof told him what had come. Three hundred and one stood exact and complete and unspent in the dark, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody. The count had gone from three hundred to three hundred and one the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the join. Nothing from the yard of the day before came in with it, and nothing stood at the door asking to be taken in as a lesson or a sign. A morning is a room swept into before the day has put anything in it, and the surgeon took it in its own order, checking the small sounds first because the small sounds were the ones that would not lie.

There was the smell before there was the light: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under them the sour mineral breath off the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}. \place{The hearth} had gone cold under its ash in the night and kept the shape of the coal that had been turned there. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered in fours with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Behind the wall the kiln took its own breath in fours and missed one, and neither of them was a bell. Three counts in three places: the fire's four low pulls and the gap, the river's water over water, and the four nails on the near board of \place{the boards} and four on the next and the thumb-width knot where a fifth nail should have stood. They were three and they did not become one, because agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. The heard bell in its square stayed beside the told, and the told figure stayed beside the paid with the space between them unsigned. He kept the counts in three places with a finger between and let them lie.

The body he was in had come awake before the room had, and he took its inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at his cheek, his temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire, and that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder, and the wanting went on under the knowing the way a coal goes on under ash.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and one days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. The house kept him in objects and not in a story: the coat on its peg behind the door, salt-stiff at the shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed with its hollow on the left that nobody filled; the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned its corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it; the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} and none had gone down it. His fire still missed its fifth under the floor, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it.

A surgeon knows the difference between a vessel opened and a vessel mended, and that the second is not the first with a nicer name put on it. An opening under a roof is a thing done and then left; the heat comes back, the household straightens around it, and the one who paid for the heat is not asked to stand forward. He had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and knew what a reconnection costs when it is done under a clamp, and he knew also that nobody in this room would call what was about to happen a reconnection at all. They would call it the first firing of Bone, and they would go on with their work. That was the whole of the event, and it was not an event, and the not-being was the thing he kept.

Heat comes back to the outside of a vessel before it comes back to the middle, and a room can look mended while the thing in it stays cold; he had watched that lie on a hundred faces in a ward where the lamps were good and the watchers were tired, and the face that seemed easier was often the face that was going. The firing would warm the pot and the boards and the air of the room by a degree or two, and the boy's cheek would be as cold when it was done as it had been before the coal went under. The afterdrop waited past the warming nobody in this house would perform, and it did not occur to anybody under the roof to perform it. A firing is not a warming of a person, and a room's heat is not the heat of the thing laid in it, and the two facts stood side by side with a finger between them and made no argument that anybody could hear.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the kiln mouth} before she came to the basket. It was the first firing of the month, earned by that morning's own light, and the house had kept itself ready for it the way a house keeps a thing ready without saying what it is ready for. She came across the fire room with her burn hand half closed, the little-door-shaped burn on the back of her left hand shutting on nothing, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light. She did not look toward \place{the bed}.

Her hand went to \lore{the widow's mark} on its cord at her throat --- a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb --- and held it one breath, and let go. The breath was the whole of it: one counted breath, no more and no longer, and then the hand came away from the cord and went to the clay lid. She lifted the lid with the back of a hand, not the fingers, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took.

It answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and one days. Nobody praised the opening. Nobody named what it mended, entered a slate or made a lesson of it; nobody raised a hand to a face or said a number over the basket. The household straightened around the sound the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and went on with what it had been doing. Garl's proverb went to the fire in the house's improved voice --- \emph{Feed the fire before you feed yourself. It'll still be there in the morning. You won't} --- and never to a person, and no one heard in it anything that had been paid.

Five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The four behind --- at one hundred and fifty, one hundred and eighty, two hundred and forty and two hundred and seventy-one --- stood where they stood and were not looked at again as signs, payments or menders. A fifth opening did not make a sixth due, and no hand under any roof counted toward the next one. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour: a shelf, a well, a rack, a basket, a yard, a morning, a hearth, an afternoon and a going of the light were none of them openings, and the mark hung where it hung through all of them. He took the order of her reaching the way he took the order of reaching at a well, by what was there and by what was not --- hand to the mark, then hand to the lid, the breath counted between and the coal going under the pot --- and filed no tenth hand over it. A woman touching a mark before a kiln is not a hand at a slate, and nothing came off the clay but the firing.

He kept the fifth opening for the far-off day a house might be left where it is and still kept, and did not bring that day nearer. It stood as an act and not a promise. It was not hoped for, not counted toward and not spent; five were five and four were four and neither number stood up and called itself a rule. The house mended what it had to mend by doing it and saying nothing, and the boy set the kept act on a place of its own without putting a name on what it mended, for a name would have wanted a payer and none stood to it.

\scenebreak

At the very moment of the opening the boy took a small intake from the next room, whose floor does not touch his. It came through the wall as a thread of sound comes through cloth --- a woman's breath at her own work, the small intake and the hold of it --- and he held it against the first low pull of the fire without carrying it into the missing fifth. He borrowed no house count and supplied no pull. The hand went down on the river's note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap.

The hundred and seventy-seventh answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was a restraint and not a payment, a key, a gift or a rule. He had held one thing whole at the very instant another thing happened and had not laid the two together, and the not-laying was the answer: an intake kept on its own note, a pull counted where it stood and the silence between the two left silent. No payer was named to either. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the coordination; the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand that could close two inches on a pot handle. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument itself was the work, and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing he had done could reach two inches.

He kept the fifth opening on a place of its own with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings} or across the dressed grain covered under wool at \place{the rack end}. A joining would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either. The opening was a kept act and not a settlement; it had mended nothing anybody had named, and the account it might have settled stood where it had stood before the coal went under the pot. He set it beside the four behind it and gave it no figure, and the finding was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air.

\scenebreak

The surgeon in him read the opening the way he had once read a vessel taken back into circulation after a clamp: a controlled reconnection, the way opened slowly, the return of heat from outside, the response of the vessel under the hand, the complication held ready for the moment it should arrive. A reconnection is paid for by somebody, and here the warmth that came back into the pot had been paid for by a man nobody in the room would name, three hundred and one days ago and every day since. Knowing that changed nothing about the distance between his mouth and the crust. He had once held pressure on a graft until the vessel took and had known exactly what the taking cost and who bore it; now he lay under a roof where the taking and the cost were both done to him by people who did not know there was a surgeon in the basket. The clamp in this house was a widow's mark touched for one breath and let go. It was mercy and mutilation in the same iron gesture, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, and no slate on which to enter the one without the other.

Once he had had a tray laid out and a room full of people who would put an instrument in his hand before he had finished asking for it, and a field he could open and close with a knowledge that had cost twelve years to buy. Now the only thing that answered him was a hand that could go up an inch and stay for a small intake, and he had learned to take what it gave without asking it to be more than it was. The opening under the roof had happened at the very instant the hand had taken its intake, and the two things stood side by side on two places with a finger between them, not a cause and not a consequence, not a sign and not a cure. He had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen which of them would live; here the whole of his practice was to hold one small sound on one small note and let it go whole, and to call nothing that happened under the roof a lesson written for his benefit.

When the fire had taken she went back to the ordinary work of the house. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said to the coals where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and kept her finger above the charcoal so the tally underneath stayed unread. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay, and gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket. The grey streak at her temple went pale off the low light and was gone back into the coal-black hair when she turned away. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

Three hundred and one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The fifth opening stood on its own place with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and neither was laid across the other, and the hand came down on the river's low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap. The clay lid was held in the back of her hand over the broken pot while the fire took in fours, and the fifth stayed a silence under the floor.
