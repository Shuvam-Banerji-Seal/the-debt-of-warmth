## Chronicle

Bone came on without a boundary drawn, as Frost had gone out, and the district wore its face for a morning when nothing is due. Under the Vail kiln-house roof the boy came into his three hundredth day; the count went to three hundred the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock, and nobody told him what it counted. He kept the round figure, a threshold, and the carried fire missed its fifth under the floor with its payer unnamed.

He took the body in its old order: a stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third to even the row; the thread frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; cold stopping at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on the pot handle.

That afternoon a hunter come down from Cold Acre waited at the well with a hare across his shoulders, the weight judged by the carrying arm. A woman asked the cost; he told her the weather and went off toward the north gate road. At the end of the day the hand held a small intake against the kiln's fourth low pull and let it go whole: the hundred and seventy-fifth answer, worth nothing at two inches of air.

On the second morning Sella Vail went to the kiln mouth before the basket. She touched the widow's mark at her throat for one breath, lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot. The fire took and answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Nobody praised the opening or made a lesson of it; five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The boy held a woman's intake against the first low pull and did not carry it into the missing fifth. In the afternoon Ilva, asked at the well what the firings had taken, said, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account. Her strip stayed in her sleeve; the boy filed no tenth hand.

On the third day the white went off the north lip of the well while the yard was looking elsewhere; the trough kept its skin and the silt-clouded plate stood out of the path of feet, and the yard called it weather. He set a held interval beside an empty interval and put a finger between; at the very end a small intake stood whole and was let go on the same note. The count closed at three hundred and two, told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid as many days, and Garl's name unspoken under the roof. The hand laid itself back against the fold, and the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap.
