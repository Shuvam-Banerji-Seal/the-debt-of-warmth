The afternoon of the second of Bone thinned without ending, the way heat goes out of a pot lifted off the coals and set upon a cold stone; it did not leave all at once but drew back by degrees, from the lip first, then from the belly, then from the foot where the stone took what was left, till the pot stood where it had stood and was only less warm than it had been. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the fold laid over his ears against the river wind, the instruction going to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on Secondday, and the district kept the face it turns to a day when nothing is due: a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. The morning's firing lay behind them and was not looked at. It had been a thing done and then left, like a pot mended and set back on its shelf, and nobody had lifted it and carried it into the afternoon to show what it had mended.

She went around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the Ash ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; the skinned water in \place{the trough}; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each got its look and its wide step. She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody --- \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out} --- and did not sweep any of them, and no figure came off any of them into the afternoon.

On the north lip of \place{the yard well} the stayed \lore{frost-bloom} kept its white fronds long past the hour when it should have gone with the sun; the trough wore its skin from edge to edge; and the plate of ice lifted off the water stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded with the silt that had risen into it as it froze. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them. He kept them on three places of his own with a finger between every pair, and made no fourth likeness of them to call a cause, and named no payer to any. The stayed frost was a covering laid over a depth; the skinned water was the same covering laid over a different depth; the clouded plate was a piece of the depth lifted up and set where no foot would go. They were three, and a joining of any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood. He did not call the weather a kindness, or a promise, or the face of anything that had been left; it was weather, and out of the path of feet.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from \place{the hearth}, and she lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning's, lighter than the day before, and gave it no figure. Three readings had gone over it in the district and not one had known what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and the grain lay where it lay under the wool, changed in state and unfigured. The surgeon's hand turns a wound to see the hidden part and turns it back; the boy's hand lay outside the wool and went nowhere. He had once known the weight of a spleen in two hands and now had no hand to lift a corner of wool, and the knowing and the not-lying stood edge to edge with a finger between them and made no doctrine. He did not lay the covered grain across the morning's opening, or across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across his own kept gaps; a joining would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to any.

Heat had been drawing back from him all afternoon the way it goes out of a dish lifted off the coals and set down on stone. It left his cheek first, then his temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to stay. He took the inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; the knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the handle of the basket that held him. Both were true and the second was louder, and the wanting went on under the knowing the way a coal goes on under ash.

Three hundred and one stood exact and complete and unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. The opening that morning had not settled any of it. No slate had been entered, no lesson made, no name given to what it mended; a real opening had warmed the pot and the boards and had left the fifth pull missing under the floor as it had found it. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the boy did not look at them as a row that wanted a sixth. The house kept Garl in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed with its twenty-year hollow nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it unswept, the proverb gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person, the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} and none had gone down it in the afternoon, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the missing pull. The surgeon knew a reconnection paid for by somebody, and here the somebody was not named; knowing it changed nothing about the two inches between his mouth and the crust.

At the well \person{Ilva} was drawing with the pail against her knee. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the drying racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out; her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft. The rope at the well had stiffened in the dry cold and gave a small dry rasp as the bucket came up, and she went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all. She was of the firing work and not of a house.

A woman waiting with a pot asked what the month's firings had taken, now the household had fired in Bone and the first opening of the month stood behind it. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of notched birch lay where it lay in the wood, not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted and not joined to the open slate standing on the stone lip a pace away; no figure came off it. Her eyes went to the asker after the hand had settled, and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account}, she said, and put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail. Nobody turned twice.

The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and took the order of reaching as he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. Hand toward the record; thumb over the notches; eyes to the asker; and only then the mouth. He had seen that order in a midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before the crowd, in a keeper's finger beneath a board; and here it came again from the yard's firing work and not from any house or Court. He filed it as the ninth such hand, on a place of its own beside the eighth with a finger between every pair, and he filed no tenth. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it, and nothing of the well went under the roof as a lesson, a figure, a mending, a cause or a payment. He did not make her a reader of her own keeping, and did not join the strip in her sleeve a pace away to the morning's opening, or to the three shapings of a well-saying with no owner standing to be asked, or to the charcoal beneath the shelf. Nine hands were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. She did not know she had been read, and went on with her water.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip of the sill where it could not. The small knife lay at the very edge with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it --- the sentence about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt --- and neither was entered. He told the wood what he told it every evening: \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the other sentence came up to his teeth as it always did, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it, and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stood there with no figure and no name; he did not say the word. It was the ninth stopping, remembered, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it.

\emph{Yes, house}, he said to those who wanted the thaw early, and \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot; he went on wanting the late one because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the small interval at the top of each reach showed where the breath should have gone in and had not. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped on another day and nobody named it. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the road and none went down it. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said nothing about the ear, and nobody asked him about the room.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men had come before first grey and gone low into \place{the back room}, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound, a door kept a finger's width open and low voices that went in and stayed. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot he said \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid; he would not say it before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln} who would have called it early and made something of it. When Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He read one piece of iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, and he filed it joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket or the bed, and the boy made no fourth likeness and laid the withheld note across nothing in the yard.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she went to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the grammar of \place{the Vail kiln-house}, and it held at dusk as strictly as at first light: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and she did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the fifth that morning had not made a sixth due. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward \lore{water over water}. From the next room, whose floor does not touch his, a small letting-go came through the wall as a thread of sound comes through cloth --- the end of a woman's count at her own work --- and under the floor the carried fire held its fourth low pull with the silence where the fifth should have stood. He set the letting-go on one place and the fourth pull on another with a finger between, and carried neither into the other. He did not take the letting-go into the missing fifth to make the row come out even, and he did not carry the fourth pull into the woman's count to give it a note it had not earned. The hundred and seventy-eighth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to either place. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact holding was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off at his ear, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing bought by it could reach two inches.

Three hundred and one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-one days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the afternoon of the second of Bone: no figure came off the well or the grain or the weather, no tenth hand stood up, no collector came up the road, and no fifth pull was supplied to make the row come out even. The morning's opening stood on its own place and had settled no account. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the bed's head with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The plate of ice stood clouded with silt on the flat stone out of the path of feet, and the hand came down on the river's low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap.
