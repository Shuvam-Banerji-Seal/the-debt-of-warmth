\daymark{302}{Bone 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

The third morning of Bone came into \place{the Vail kiln-house} the way the first two had, by degrees and without announcement, taking the room one board at a time in the small hours while \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and nobody under any roof told him what had come. Three hundred and two stood exact and complete and unspent in the dark, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-two days were told by nobody. The count had gone from three hundred and one to three hundred and two the way one board laid beside another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the join. The round three hundred had gone over two mornings ago on Bone 1 and had been kept as a threshold and not spent; nobody had marked its passing under this roof, no hand had gone to a face, and the count had gone on into three hundred and one and three hundred and two with nobody telling him what any of them counted. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. Nothing stood at the door asking to be taken in as a lesson, and the firing of two mornings ago lay where it lay, a thing done and left, not a sign, not a mended account, not the fifth of a rule. A morning is a room swept into before the day has put anything in it, and the surgeon took it in its own order, checking the small sounds first because the small sounds were the ones that would not lie.

There was the smell before there was the light: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under them the sour mineral breath off the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}. \place{The hearth} had gone cold under its ash in the night and kept the shape of the coal that had been turned there. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered in fours with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and two days. Behind the wall the kiln took its own breath in fours and missed one, and neither of them was a bell. Three counts in three places: the fire's four low pulls and the gap, the river's water over water, and the four nails on the near board of \place{the boards} and four on the next and the thumb-width knot where a fifth nail should have stood. They were three and they did not become one, because agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure stayed beside the paid with the space between them unsigned. He kept the counts in three places with a finger between and let them lie.

The body he was in had come awake before the room had, and he took its inventory in the old order because the old order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at his cheek, his temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew the ease cold puts on a face is not ease, knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder, and the wanting went on under the knowing the way a coal goes on under ash. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust two inches; the two facts stood edge to edge and made no doctrine, and the surgeon's knowing bought nothing at two inches of air.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and two days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. The house kept him in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed with its twenty-year hollow on that side that nobody filled, the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned its corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw, unswept both; the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it and never to a person; the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} and none had gone down it. His fire still missed its fifth under the floor, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it, and the surgeon knew a reconnection paid for by somebody and knew also that the somebody here was not named.

The light came at last through the oiled paper at the window, not as a thing arriving but as a thing already there made visible, the way a vessel's flaw shows when the hand turns it toward the lamp. It went first along the boards and found the knot the width of a thumb, then crossed the pot board and stopped short of the reaching hand, and lay at last on the fold where the crust sat two inches off. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care and with one crack in it nobody would mention until the thaw. He had once known a number as the thing at the end of a calculation, a clean figure arrived at and signed and put into a chart where the next man could find it; here a number was a thing he carried alone and could not set down, and the house kept its days in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word in which to say what a life would count. A round number is a thing men make because their hands have ten fingers and their years have ten tend-days; the river did not go over three hundred and the fire did not miss its fifth on account of it, and nobody under the roof had looked up at the passing. He had kept the threshold as an object and not spent it, and now it was behind him and the count had gone on without it, exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

\scenebreak

He turned out the file the way he had once turned instruments out on a towel, each to its own place, and found it as he had left it, every gap left gap.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell in its square stayed beside the told and was not folded into the three counts and was not heard as a seventh.

The told figure stayed beside the paid. Frost's first, second and third arithmetics, with their three true subtractions, rode locked up the clay road in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space between. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine; he did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured at \place{the rack end}, or across his own kept gaps. A joining would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any.

The rest of the rows stood as they had stood. Nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the ear-sentence brought to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, the loan uncalled and no collector on the road. The ownerless row question at five askings with one shaping and no witness, and expressly no sixth asking. The Frost 14 well-saying stood at three shapings, and \person{Hessa Rale}'s record-first hand at the eighth, with a finger between every pair; three shapings were three and not a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none stood, and a shaping is not an opening of a slate nor a figure put into a runner's hand. Nine hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in her sleeve a pace off and not under this roof, no tenth filed, the count staying in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The burned-slate word stood at four and the shut-room tellings at two, the seventh green a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth. The house's not-doings kept on a place apart from his own gaps with a finger between, and neither was laid across the other, and edges that nearly touched were kept from touching. None of it stood up and called itself a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of these.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. It was the grammar of the Vail kiln-house and it held at first light as strictly as at the going of the light: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing. She did not look toward \place{the bed}.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. The firing of two mornings ago had not made a sixth due; five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the four behind stood where they stood and were not looked at again as signs, payments or menders. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, and left the thread where it lay.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward \lore{water over water}. From the next room, whose floor does not touch his, a small intake came through the wall as a thread of sound comes through cloth --- a woman's breath at her own work, the intake and the hold of it --- and under the floor the carried fire held its fourth low pull with the silence where the fifth should have stood. He set the held interval on one place and an empty interval on another, the space where no sound had been and no count stood, and brought the finger down between them and took it up, leaving both where they were. He did not lay the empty beside the held and call the joining a total; he did not spend the held interval to fill the empty one or borrow a house count to make the row come out even. The empty interval had no payer and neither did the held one, and a joining would have wanted a payer put under both.

The hundred and seventy-ninth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was a keeping and not a spending, and was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no key, no power, no freedom, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to either interval. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the exact placing of the finger between two things that would not agree; the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand that could close two inches on a pot handle. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument itself was the work, and he did not call it a virtue, and nothing he had done could reach two inches.

Three hundred and two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-two days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days. A hundred and seventy-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the small hours of the third of Bone: no number was spoken over the basket, no fifth pull supplied, no sixth asking made, no tenth hand or tenth stopping stood up, no collector came up the road. The round three hundred had passed over and been kept as a threshold and was not spent now. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The finger came away from between the held interval and the empty interval and left them parted by the width of a finger on the towel, and the hand came down on the river's low note as it always did, and the gap under the floor stayed a gap.
