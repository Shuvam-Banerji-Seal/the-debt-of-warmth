\daymark{292}{Frost 23, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

Frost the twenty-third came on its own morning and asked nothing of the boy under the fold. It came as all his mornings came, by degrees and from the outside, the small hours going under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof before any light had been given to them, and \person[Corin]{the boy} came into his two hundred and ninety-second day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Two hundred and ninety-one had gone to two hundred and ninety-two the way one board laid against another gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam; six months and one hundred and twelve days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. Nothing of the days behind was brought into this one, for Frost 23 was an ordinary Thirdday with no market, no reading at \place{the Cross} and no bell, and a day like that asks to be let alone. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid two hundred and ninety-two days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. He had long since stopped expecting anybody to tell him what a number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on beneath the expectation the way a coal goes on beneath ash.

The smells came in their old order, because the house had no notion of order and so could not disturb it: hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under both the faint sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the fire answered in four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, and overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Four, four and a gap; four and the silence; water over water. The kiln answered in fours and was not a bell. No bell had been brought under the roof on this morning and none was lent an ear it had not earned. There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body; the older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and the morning did not put the two in the same sentence.

\scenebreak

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had ever promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust to his mouth; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal. Two inches of air and an infant's reach were the whole of the distance, and the distance did not care who had once held a clamp.

\scenebreak

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. The carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence; the river kept its water over water; the boards kept their four, four and the knot. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. A man who has spent his life finding the one error that makes a set of numbers agree finds it a strange trade to stand before three honest counts and refuse to make them one, but he had been at it two hundred and ninety-two days and had not grown skilful at it, only obedient to the discipline of it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The four sources kept their four shapes --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross --- and the boy did not make the two tellings of the slate into two sources, because a source is a thing that stands somewhere and can be asked and the second telling stood in the same hand as the first. He had once written a chart that made two disagreeing accounts agree by leaving one of them out, and the habit of that correction was still in him, quick and shameful and ready; he did not give it a slate. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, heard and not borrowed, not folded into the three counts and not heard as a seventh of the full note.

On \place{the towel} at the head of \place{the bed} the file lay open with every gap left gap. The Frost 14 saying stood on a place of its own with one saying and one second shaping beside it, and a finger between, not a witness, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining and not paid, filed beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair. Three had not come of two in the night and would not come of it this morning, however much the quiet under the boards might invite a third likeness to stand up and be counted. The boy did not invite it. The nine stoppings stood nine with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; the nine record-first hands stood nine with the ninth, \person{Ilva}'s strip in a sleeve a pace off, unread and joined to none; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness, and was not asked a sixth time. Nothing in the file had moved since the hand came down on it in the small hours, and nothing in this morning's quiet had earned the right to move it.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. That was the grammar of the house and it held exactly, as it had held every morning since he came under the roof: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, in the low voice she kept for tools, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. She was broad and impossible to move, with her hair coal-black and the one grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals as she pushed a strand back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn; the burn closed on nothing and the grey did not go away. She told the fire it was the thaw and the fire answered four low pulls with its gap, and she did not ask it what it had cost or who had paid for the fifth.

At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it was unread; she moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She kept a half-pace from every open slate and carried nothing slopped. When she set the pot down she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat before she went away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; she had never given an instruction to the basket at any hour of any day, and she gave none now. The boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had learned to watch a surgeon's hand leave a field --- for what it did not touch --- and filed the not-touching on its own place, maintenance and not a message.

The broom got its instruction before any person did, and so did the running water: go around what the water left, it cost enough to come in and will cost more to take out; keep the draught off the grain; let the pot stand where the pot stands. She said the words into the yard and the sink and the fire, and never once turned them toward the basket, as though the basket were a stone that had no ears and needed none. When she carried him past a slate set out to dry she kept the half-pace without appearing to measure it, and the fold went right at his ear without a look at his face. It would have been easy to read care in it and easier still to read the absence of care; he read the distance instead, which was the only thing in the room that was honestly there. A woman who says the house where another woman says I has taken the first person out of her own sentence, and a boy who lies under the sentence learns to notice where the gap is.

\scenebreak

Later, in the turn of the morning, she uncovered the dressed grain at \place{the rack end} and turned it beneath the wool with a progressively lighter touch, giving it no figure. It lay on the cool stone away from the hearth, covered against the draught, and the numbers that had been read over it twice in the last days did not know what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it and remained unsigned. Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked in \person[Berr Clay]'s box up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln}, with the printed one in twelve unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and nothing under this roof had opened, copied or entered any of it. The thane's large clean hands had wiped the thumb twice on an apron before touching the slate, and passed a finger over a narrow figure and let the eyes rest on it a breath longer than the figure required; the boy kept the twice-wiped thumb on a place of its own and did not lay it across the covered grain, because a hand that takes itself out of its own sentence is not the same as a hand that puts a number on a sack. The boy kept the told figure beside the paid figure and the twice-spoken figure as two sayings of one figure and not two accounts, and he kept the unsigned space where it was, waiting, and did not sign it himself. A figure read twice is still a figure that wants a payer under it, and he was not a payer and had no slate to enter one on. He turned the covered grain over in his mind the way she turned it under the wool, looking for the hidden part, and found only the covering and the cost nobody had written down. It was the same with the ash in the unswept corner: a covering laid over a depth that the house called weather and asked nothing of, and no fourth likeness was made between the grain under wool and the frost on the well lip and the skin on the trough, because one covering wants one cause and no cause stood to any of them.

\scenebreak

In the small hours, while the house kept its number in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's own going. It took the small intake of the carried fire's silence rising through the floor and carried it down onto the river's next low note of water over water, and it kept the two sounds in two places and did not make them agree, for agreeing them would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. The hundred and fifty-ninth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; neither sound was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

Frost the thirtieth, the month's third and last Account-day, stood six mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The hand did not reach toward it as a sign, for a sign wants somebody to read it and nobody under this roof had been given the office. Two hundred and ninety-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twelve days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The hand came down against the fold with the fire's silence and the river's low note kept in two places and a finger between, the crust two inches off with its empty place unnamed, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap, and the river going low over lower under the finger's-width door.
