## Chronicle

For three mornings in the last working week of Frost the house under the Vail roof kept its number in loaves, in glances, in the gaps between instructions. The boy came into his two hundred and ninety-first day, and his two hundred and ninety-second, and his two hundred and ninety-third, and nobody in Low Kiln told him what any of them counted. Six months and one hundred and thirteen days went over him like water over a stone, and the carried fire gave four low pulls each morning with the silence where the fifth should have stood.

He lay under the loaf shelf with one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself. The thread at his mouth froze in the dry heat and was left unwiped, for no instruction had ever been given about the thread. Cold came to cheek and temple and the bare joint of the wrist and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He knew that a face may seem eased before it is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly; he wanted the crust lifted, and could not close two inches on the pot handle. The surgeon kept the inventory; the infant kept the wanting.

Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket each morning and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, turned the coals with a broken pot, set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, and carried him down and up at the half-pace from every open slate. The yard went around seven places with a look and a wide step and no number; the stayed frost and the silt-clouded plate of ice stayed weather, entered nowhere. Tarn kept six at the kindling and a seventh at the lip; Jorra kept his forearms dusty and his silence whole; Garl was kept in a coat on a peg and a lean in a wheel-seat, his name unspoken two hundred and ninety-three days.

On the afternoon of Frost 23 a voice came over the boards and the well-roof with no owner to be asked and no figure in it, and gave the Frost 14 saying a third shaping: the slate-runner had once carried a name the district had spent, and did not say whose. Sella heard it and went on with the house. The boy filed it beside the first two with a finger between every pair. Three shapings did not stand up as a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. Six answers, the hundred and fifty-seventh through the hundred and sixty-second, were exact and worth nothing at two inches of air; the hand laid itself back against the fold.
