\daymark{306}{Bone 7, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} had been laid down against the fold at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} as a man lays a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and it lay there still in the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, the small soft wrist going no further than the wool, the one hundred and eighty-sixth answer finished and put away. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and sixth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and twenty-six days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; the count had gone from three hundred and five to three hundred and six the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam behind it, and he took the knock as he took all his counts, whole and unspent and worth nothing to a hand that could not close two inches of air. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and six days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days; nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of \place{the bed} with every gap left gap, and he turned over no page of it, because a file turned over twice is a file being asked to say something.

Bone came on without a boundary drawn, the way the last of a thing comes on in a house that does not keep its months in numbers. The older women named the months by what the weather did and the slatemen named them by how many Account-days had gone into them, and the two habits lay side by side like two hands hauling on one rope, neither set right and neither left wrong in so many words. Seventhday had no market, no reading at \place{the Cross} and no bell; the district kept the face it turns to a morning when nothing is due --- a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. Three hundred and six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and one hundred and twenty-six days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl's name unspoken the same number. He had stopped expecting to be told what any of the numbers counted. That was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

Nothing that had gone before this morning came under the roof as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. The fifth telling of the burned slate stood where it had been set down on the fifth of Bone, a voice from \place{Slate Row} over the boards with no owner standing under it and no hand named at the flame; five were five and a sixth was not due on a morning that had not earned one, and the finger stayed between the fifth and the four. The pot on \place{the pot board} carried its twice-mended handle and its crack showing, but a repair that held was not a restoration and mended nothing anybody would name; the two sayings belonged to the pot and the fire and had not been said to a person. The hand at the well that went to a sleeve before it went to an asker, with the notched strip staying in the wood where no slate could take a figure off it, was nine and stayed nine; no tenth stood up out of it and no figure came off that wood into this room. He kept these on their own places with a finger between every pair and wrote no cause across any two, and the edges nearly touched and did not touch.

Under the fold the body made its inventory in the old order, as a file is turned out on a towel before the instruments are laid back into it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone past the point where any child under any roof would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third coming in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a face that is not ease, the grimace of severe cold that passes for a smile among people who do not know better. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man so taken must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowing was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away, and he wanted both louder than he wanted the inventory taken in its order; both were true and the second was louder, and neither was entered, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him, and the pot handle set beyond his reach was two inches of the same air. The surgeon took the inventory. The infant wanted the crust. The two voices argued and neither won, and the checking was the work and not a virtue.

Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and six days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. No fire-sitter stood over the coals to be borrowed from, and no house count came to be lent. He laid them side by side with a finger between and did not make the third a key to the first two, for a key is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

\scenebreak

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the last market of Frost with not one hand in two hundred going to a face, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; no bell had come on the Seventhday and none was lent an ear it had not earned before its morning. \person{Berr Clay}'s first, second and third arithmetics, with their three true subtractions, rode locked up \place{the clay road} in his box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between had no signature on it. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's not-doings, the grain under wool or the boy's own kept gaps with the edges nearly touching and the finger going between.

Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row of askings stood at five with one shaping and no witness, and no sixth asking had been supplied on a day that had not earned one; the saying at the well stood at three shapings beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between every pair, and a fourth shaping was not to be stood up as a witness. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in a sleeve and no tenth, and nine were nine and must not call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood at the well. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four; the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two with no third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the seventh green stood a colour at a woman's mouth and the seventh shape a shape at a market's edge, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over them. Every tray was parted by the width of a finger, and nothing on any of them stood up and called itself a rule.

The first light came into the room the way tallow light comes, not as a beam but as a softening of the dark on the underside of the shelf, and \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket because it was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing, as it shut on nothing whenever her hand was empty.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. When she set the pot down on the pot board she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

The twice-mended pot stood where she had set it, with the repair standing visible and no attempt made to hide it. Two staples held the handle: one the colour of old brass, gone soft with years of use, and the second a wire drawn tighter and left longer than it needed to be, with a hairline of the original crack showing dark between them. She put her thumb where the wire crossed the crack before letting the weight come on the handle, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then lifted; the pot answered full under the handle and the crack stayed showing. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot and to the fire, and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. She said both to the pot and the fire and never to a person; the sayings had belonged to a man who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him, and they had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them. Repair is not restoration; a mended vessel carries its break, and a repair that holds mends nothing anybody will name. The boy took the measure of the two staples with his eyes and made no figure of them, and did not lay the crack across his own kept gaps for want of a payer.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where the house could check it and where it could slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond the mark where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lying beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stood there. He did not say the word. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back; his stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where the stroke had stopped. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it; the two ears under the roof kept their equality and the two silences their one shape, and nothing on this Seventhday turned the seventh year into eight.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. The shed held the sweetish edge of lead glaze in its dust, a wrong wind's direction that had once gone over the boards and out again on the wind that brought it, arrived nowhere and entered nowhere and not a second edge paid. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not have said it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. The burned slate stood at five tellings and he put a finger between the fifth and the four and supplied no sixth; when the saying went around the boards he grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not gained a third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. He joined nothing on the wheel-keeper's forearms to a cause, and Slate Row stayed a direction over the boards, and the fifth telling waited on the far-off day the promise would be called in, not a confession and not a payment.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story, and his name had not been spoken under the roof for three hundred and six days. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held his broader shape with the crease at the left shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with a new finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- and it had improved in the keeping, as all his sayings did, and was very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said it. The debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; a debt circled without a name is a debt that gathers weight from being gone around. Up in the loft the stone lay cold in its pot and the debt lay uncalled, and the two were kept apart by a man who had stopped trying to reconcile them, which is a kind of keeping and not a kind of peace. Nobody made a lesson of any of it and nobody said the name.

Three hundred and six stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-six days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of Garl Vail had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the seventh of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth telling of the burned slate, no fourth shaping of the well-saying, no eighth green or shape, no fifth pull supplied and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. Bone 10 stood three mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note, and the thing he took and laid away was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his. He laid it beside the carried fire's fourth low pull on a place of its own, whole and unjoined, and did not carry it into the silence where the fifth should have stood, or into the bell's gap left behind in the square, or into the river's low over lower. It borrowed no house count and supplied no payer. The hundred and eighty-seventh answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The surgeon in him took a clean satisfaction in one sound placed against its note, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear, and the two voices argued and neither won, and the checking was the work and not a virtue. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did, and the silence where the fifth should stand was left a silence.
