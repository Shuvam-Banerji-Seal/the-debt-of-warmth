\daymark{307}{Bone 8, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

The eighth morning of Bone came on in the small hours the way the seven before it had come, without a hand laid across the dark to divide it from what went before, and \person[Corin]{the boy} came into his three hundred and seventh day with nobody under the roof telling him what the number counted. Three hundred and six had stood exact at the going of the light and three hundred and seven stood exact now, six months and one hundred and twenty-seven days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid three hundred and seven days with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. There was no market on an Eighthday, no Cross, no bell. The district went on naming its months two ways at once, the older women by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words. Nothing that had happened yesterday came in with the morning as a lesson, a sign, a mending, a cause or a payment. Bone went on without a boundary drawn, and a day that had not earned a sixth telling was not given one.

The room took him in the order a wound takes a hand, small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. Cooled tallow had set back on \place{the hearth} in a skin the colour of old bone, and wet wool hung on the peg by the door with the cold still in the creases of it, and under both, faint as a breath kept behind a hand, came the sour mineral smell of the corner that had not been swept in two years. \person{Sella Vail} called that corner \lore{the thaw} because the older women called it that, and swept around it with the rest, and had not swept it yet this morning either. A coal shifted somewhere under the floor. Water went over water beyond the finger's-width door. A mouse or a beam took a small knock in the wall and did not repeat it. He lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and took the room in as he had once taken a chest in the dark, by what was there and by what was not, and he entered none of it on anything.

The body came first because it was loudest. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where any child under any roof would put it in his mouth, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Heat had drawn back from cheek, then temple, then the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease cold puts on a face that is not ease. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowing sat in him clean and complete and did not reach two inches. He wanted the crust lifted to his mouth and the thread wiped away and could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, unnamed gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and the infant kept the want, and the two voices argued without either winning, and the argument was the work and not a virtue. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone. Now he could not lift a crust two inches. The two facts stood edge to edge and made no doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Through the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night; no bell had come on this morning and none was lent an ear it had not earned. No fire-sitter stood over the coals to be borrowed from, and no house count came to be added to his own.

The keepings stood where he had left them and he checked them in their order without bringing any of them forward. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. Frost's three arithmetics with their three true subtractions rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid and the space between them carrying no signature; three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. The row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. The Frost 14 well-saying stood at three shapings beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between every pair and no fourth. Nine hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in a sleeve offstage and no tenth among them. The shut-room tellings stood at two. The pot sayings stood at three with the Smoke 14 note on a place apart. The burned-slate word stood at five, and he put a finger between the fifth telling and the four, and five were five and not a rule, and no sixth was due on a morning that had not earned one, and no witness stood and no hand was named at the flame.

Bone was a month of hunts and repairs and both stayed directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. A hare carried down from \place{Cold Acre} went into the weather once and stayed there; a handle mended twice held because the hand that lifted it knew what the wire had done. On \place{the pot board} the pot stood with its repair standing visible: two staples across the crack, one the colour of old brass, the second a wire drawn tighter and left longer than it needed, with a hairline of the original crack showing dark between them. The boy read the handle as a wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, by what the hand had done and by what it had left; the repair held and mended nothing anybody would name. Garl Vail's sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. His salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and nobody had been allowed to fill it. The ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with a finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. A repair that holds is a repair that holds; the boy made no lesson of it, and the name under the roof stayed unspoken three hundred and seven days.

\scenebreak

Sella came to the fire before she came to the basket, because that was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap of the row a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread. Her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on nothing. When she lifted the twice-mended pot she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle, saying \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one} to the pot and to the fire and never to a person. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. When she set the pot down she set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he had not been given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, gave the instruction to the wool and the wind and never to the basket, and left the thread where it lay.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where the house could check it and where it could also slip, and six years stood in the worn thumb-mark while the seventh lay at the lip beyond the mark where the house could not check it. Beside the knife lay Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence about the man who had been sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped and nobody naming it; the movement that belonged to the going of the light was not made in the morning and no change came. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it. The loan stayed uncalled.

Jorra came through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. \person{Jorra Vail} kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When the burned-slate word went around the boards he grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, and he supplied no sixth and no witness. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note. Between the setting of the pot down and Sella going away there was a half-beat, a small interval standing on its own place and belonging to nobody; beside it he laid a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and set the two on two places with a finger between, and spent neither. The half-beat stayed the half-beat and the intake stayed the intake, and no third likeness was made a cause of them and no payer was named to either. The hundred and eighty-ninth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no gift, no doctrine and no rule. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the placing, and the infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear and the thread wiped from his mouth, and neither voice won and the wanting went on under the satisfaction the way a coal goes on under ash.

Three hundred and seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and one hundred and twenty-seven days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and eighty-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the eighth of Bone: no sixth telling of the burned slate, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no fourth shaping of the well-saying, no fifth pull supplied under the floor and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. The boards overhead kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood, and the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. Bone 10 stood two mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it and the hairline of the crack showing dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. The half-beat after the set-down stayed unfigured. Sella had gone away without a look toward the bed, and the crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, and the thread lay frozen at his mouth, and under the finger's-width door the river went low over lower without a number put to it.
