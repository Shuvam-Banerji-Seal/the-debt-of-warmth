\daymark{308}{Bone 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

The ninth of Bone came on under the roof as the days of this month had come on, without a line drawn across it and without anybody counting it where a count could be heard. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and the hand laid back against it on the one hundred and ninetieth answer, a tool put away where the next hand would find it without looking. He came into his three hundred and eighth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Six months and one hundred and twenty-eight days stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name of \person{Garl Vail} had not been spoken under the roof the same number. Nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull, and the silence where the fifth should have stood was left a silence.

Ninthday had no market, no Cross, no bell. The older women named the months by what the weather did and the slatemen named them by the number of Account-days gone into them, and the two habits lay side by side on this morning as they had on the six behind it, neither set right and neither left wrong in so many words. Bone was the month of hunts and repairs; men came down with hares across their shoulders and went off toward \place{the north gate road} answered with the weather, and a handle was mended where it stood and judged by the carrying arm and the day's dry cold, and neither left a figure behind it. The district kept the face it turns to a morning when nothing is due --- a pot set down, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks. He had stopped expecting to be told what three hundred and eight counted. That was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

Nothing from the eighth of Bone came under the roof as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. The half-check on the flagstones was not made again on this morning, and no man stopped his stroke to make it a ninth stopping or a hearing or a rule; it stood where it had been left on the stone's own place with the three older held changes beside it, and four were four. He did not lay it across the covered grain or the house's \lore{not-doings} or his own kept gaps, and did not let four likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The burned slate stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, and a sixth was not due on a morning that had not earned one. The pot on \place{the pot board} carried its twice-mended handle and the crack showing between the staples, but a repair that held mended nothing anybody would name; the two sayings belonged to the pot and the fire and had not been said to a person. The hand that had gone to a sleeve before it went to an asker was nine and stayed nine, with the strip in the wood where no slate could take a figure off it and no tenth standing up out of it. He kept each of these on its own place, and the edges nearly touched and the finger went between.

Under the fold the body made its inventory in the old order, as a file is turned out on \place{the towel} before the instruments are laid back into it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear, gone past the point where any child under any roof would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third coming in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at the mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a face that is not ease, the grimace of severe cold that passes for a smile among people who do not know better. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man so taken must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowing was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away, and he wanted both louder than he wanted the inventory taken in its order; both were true and the second was louder, and neither was entered, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him, and the pot handle set beyond his reach was two inches of the same air. The surgeon took the inventory. The infant wanted the crust. The two voices argued and neither won, and the argument was the work and not a virtue.

Under the floor the carried fire kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and eight days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. No fire-sitter stood over the coals to be borrowed from, and no house count came to be lent. He laid them side by side with a finger between and did not make the third a key to the first two, for a key is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

\scenebreak

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the last market of Frost with not one hand in two hundred going to a face, stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; no bell had come on the Ninthday and none was lent an ear it had not earned before its morning. \person{Berr Clay}'s three arithmetics, with their three true subtractions, rode locked up \place{the clay road} in his box toward \place{High Kiln}, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between had no signature on it. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's not-doings, the grain covered under wool at \place{the rack end} or the boy's own kept gaps with the edges nearly touching and the finger going between.

Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row of askings stood at five with one shaping and no witness, and no sixth asking had been supplied on a day that had not earned one; the saying at the well stood at three shapings beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between every pair, and a fourth shaping was not to be stood up as a witness. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in a sleeve and no tenth, and nine were nine and must not call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood at the well. The burned slate stood at five; the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two with no third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the seventh green stood a colour at a woman's mouth and the seventh shape a shape at a market's edge, with no eighth of either and no name spoken over them. Every tray was parted by the width of a finger, and nothing on any of them stood up and called itself a rule.

\scenebreak

The first light came into the room the way tallow light comes, not as a beam but as a softening of the dark on the underside of the shelf, and \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket because that was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing, as it shut on nothing whenever her hand was empty.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a morning is not an opening. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. When she set the pot down on the pot board she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. She set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he was not given the means to close two inches of air. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story, and his name had not been spoken under the roof for three hundred and eight days. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held his broader shape with the crease at the left shoulder; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line with a finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- and had improved in the keeping and was very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said it; the other saying about a thing worth mending twice went with it to the pot. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. The keeping of an absence is maintenance and not a story, and nobody made a lesson of any of it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where the house could check it and where it could slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond the mark where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lying beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stood there. He did not say the word. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went out over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} and over it without the half-check; the movement of the day before stood where it had stood, remembered and not made again, and he set no finger of his across it to make four into one. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it; the two ears under the roof kept their equality and the two silences their one shape, and nothing on this Ninthday turned the seventh year into eight.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not have said it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. The burned slate stood at five tellings and he put a finger between the fifth and the four and supplied no sixth; when the saying went around the boards he grew quieter and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not gained a third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. He joined nothing on the wheel-keeper's forearms to a cause, and \place{Slate Row} stayed a direction over the boards, and the fifth telling waited on the far-off calling-in of the promise, not a confession and not a payment.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note, and the thing he took and let go was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his. He let it go whole on the same low note, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, carrying it into neither the carried fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square nor the river's low over lower. The hundred and ninety-first answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number, and the infant in him wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and neither voice won, and the argument was the work and not a virtue. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Three hundred and eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and ninety-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the ninth of Bone: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth telling of the burned slate, no fourth shaping of the well-saying, no eighth green or shape, no fifth pull supplied and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. Bone 10 stood one morning ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

The stiff leathery crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and the afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. Under the finger's-width door the river went low over lower without a number put to it, and the small hours went on without a boundary drawn.
