The afternoon of the eighth of Bone came on without a line drawn across it, the way Bone itself came on without a boundary drawn, and \person{Sella Vail} came for the basket when the sun had gone past its height and the wind had come up off the sill. She lifted with the hand that carries the burn, the little-door shape of it closing first on the wool and then on the weight, and gave the instruction to the fold as she gave it at both ends of every day --- keep it off his ears, it's the river wind --- to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. The basket was a pot taken up and carried and set down, and pots are not told why. \person[Corin]{The boy} went down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, which is the pace of a woman carrying something that must not be slopped and that must not be seen into.

He came into the afternoon still in his three hundred and seventh day, six months and one hundred and twenty-seven days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. None of it was said over the basket. Nothing that had happened in the morning came out into the yard as a lesson or a sign or a mending or a payment --- not the pot with the crack showing between the two staples, not the half-beat after the set-down, not the handle turned toward a hand that could not close two inches of air. The day went on as it had begun, ordinary, with no market on an Eighthday, no Cross, no bell. There was no sixth telling due on a day that had not earned it, and none was supplied.

In \place{the Clay Yard} the dry cold held the way a coal holds under ash, and the river kept its note of \lore{water over water} beyond the racks. He took the yard in the order it arrived, as he took a wound in the order it bled: first the smells, wet clay and cooled tallow and the sour mineral breath off a corner that had not been swept in two years, then the sounds, a pot set down flat somewhere, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water, then the places the feet were going to avoid. A child in a basket sees the ground first. There were seven of them, and she went around all seven with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned lay beside a finer grey hairline the older women called \lore{the thaw}, and both were unswept; she set her foot past them as she would set a cup past the edge of a table, and gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them, and the space under the rack was gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. On the stone lip of \place[the yard well]{the well} the shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word on it, a thing named from the shape it had left and not from anything that remained. The water in \place{the trough} wore its skin. Far out on \place{the worked strips} a field's empty place stood where a field should have stood, and in the returning line a place stood where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. She looked at each as a woman looks at a stone in a path she knows well, stepped wide, and entered none of them into anything. A thing gone around with a broom is still a thing kept.

The stayed frost had gone off the north lip of the well on Bone 3 while the yard was looking elsewhere. There was no white on the lip now, only the grey stone showing where the white had been, and the rope had lost its stiffness and gave again under the bucket. The trough kept its skin from edge to edge, and the plate of ice lifted out of it stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded with the silt that had risen into the water as it froze. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all three weather and entered none of them. He took them apart as he had been taught to take a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. The frost that had gone from the north lip had been a covering laid over a depth, thin and white, gone in an afternoon without anybody seeing it go. The skin on the trough was the same covering laid over another depth, grey where the first had been white, holding its shape while the water under it went on being water. The clouded plate was a piece of the depth itself lifted up and set where no foot went, with the silt that had been in the water held in it the way a clot holds what was moving in a vessel. He kept the three with a finger between every pair. The first was not the second and the second was not the third, and no fourth likeness was made a cause. To call them one thing would have wanted a hand under all three, and no such hand stood in the yard, and he named no payer to any of them.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from \place{the hearth}, spread on the cool stone in the shade the racks threw across it. She set the basket down where no slate lay, lifted a corner of the wool with two fingers and turned the grain beneath it with a touch lighter even than the touch she had given it the day before, and laid the wool back. The grain had been lifted for damp and turned and spread thin and gathered by the handful, changed in state a dozen times and given no figure on any of them. Three readings had gone over it up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with their true subtractions locked in, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid and the space between had no signature on it. He did not bring any of that in as a lesson. Three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not laid across the covered grain and not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings} or across his own kept gaps. Some of those edges nearly touched. The finger went between them.

Bone was a month of hunts and repairs, and both stayed directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather and never figures. A cost asked of the month was answered with the weather and a direction that left no entry, and nobody weighed what was carried or opened a slate for it. The yard went on with its work around the basket and did not turn twice.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where the house could check it and where it could also slip, and six years stood in the worn thumb-mark while the seventh lay at the lip beyond the mark where the house could not check it. Beside the knife lay Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence about the man who had been sent for a collector and turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it.

He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. He took up the broom only after it had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named, and his stroke went out over \place{the flagstones} toward the road. The stones kept a shallow bright patch where a stroke had stopped once before, and nobody entered the place or named it. On this afternoon his stroke went over the patch as it had gone over it on the day before and every day since, without the half-check.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

When the burned-slate word went around the boards he grew quieter, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The word stood at five with a finger between the fifth telling and the four, and five were five and not a rule. He supplied no sixth and no witness, and he neither paid nor refused the claim gathering against the promise he had not given; the promise to the men who burn slates waited on a far-off calling-in, not a confession and not a settlement. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed. He did not look toward the basket or \place{the bed}, and his eyes did not finish the sentence they had begun at the door.

Then the going of the light came on without anybody calling it, the way all the comings and goings of this month came on, and Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the fire. Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

And on the flagstones, half a beat behind the standing finger, Tarn's broom checked.

It was not a stop. The bristles checked on the stone, held the small interval, and went on toward the road without a word and without a turn of the head. But the place where the stroke had checked kept itself, and the shallow bright patch on the flagstones had a new shallow brightness at its edge, a thing nobody entered and nobody swept. The boy lay where no slate lay and took the half-beat apart as he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not. The half-check of Seed 22 had come back once on Day 171; now it had come back again, changed, after a hundred and thirty-six days. Neither man named the other's silence. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the road or went down it, and no tenth stopping stood up to join the nine. It was not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not a reconciliation, not a mercy and not a gift. The movement had not come on the seventh of Bone and was not to come again on the ninth; Bone 10 would carry it unchanged.

He filed the returned half-check on the flagstone's own place. Beside it lay the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 held beat beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, and the returned beat at two breaths on Frost 5. Four held changes on four places, with a finger between every pair, and he forbade them to stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in keeping four as four. The infant in him wanted the crust two inches off at his ear and the thread wiped from his mouth, and the wanting went on under the satisfaction the way a coal goes on under ash. Nothing he had filed could reach two inches of air, and he did not call the filing a virtue.

Garl Vail was kept in objects and not in a story, with his name unspoken three hundred and seven days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder held his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and nobody had been allowed to fill it. The ridge that had been his ash-marker was now a clean dark line at the Vail corner with a finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the other saying about a thing worth mending twice went with it, both improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. A repair that holds mends nothing anybody will name, and the keeping of an absence is maintenance and not a story; he made no lesson of either.

Sella went to the fire before she went to the basket, because that was the grammar of the house and the grammar held. She told the coals it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither of the row's two gaps a figure. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and the little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a working thumb, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and a going of the light is not an opening. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. She set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and he had not been given the means to close two inches of air. She carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, laid him on his bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, gave the instruction to the wool and never to the basket, and left the thread where it lay.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note. Between Jorra's finger arriving at the iron and the end of Sella's sentence reaching the pot there had been a little interval standing on its own place and belonging to nobody; he held it there and did not fill it to make a row come out even. It was not the half-check on the flagstones and it was not the fraction at the damper, and he set no finger of his across them to make four into one. The hundred and ninetieth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was promoted into no reach, no hearing, no seventh, no stopping, no payment, no held change, no gift, no doctrine and no rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the placing, and the infant in him wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and neither voice won, and the argument was the work and not a virtue.

Three hundred and seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-seven days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and ninety honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended on the eighth of Bone: no sixth telling of the burned slate, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no fourth shaping of the well-saying, no fifth pull supplied under the floor and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. \place{The boards} overhead kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood, and the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. Bone 10 stood two mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

The shallow bright patch on the flagstones kept the place where the broom had checked. The stroke had gone on toward the road without a turn of the head, and the place where it stopped was kept by nobody. Under the finger's-width door the river went low over lower without a number put to it, and the small hours went on without a boundary drawn.
