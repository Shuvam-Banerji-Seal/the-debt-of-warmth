The light went off the \place{Clay Yard} by degrees at the going of the ninth day, not out but away from the things it had been touching, the way a stone that has held the sun gives up its warmth first at the edge and last at the middle. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the fold over his ears against the river wind and the instruction going to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. She had gone to the fire before the basket in the morning and went to it again now, and the order held the way a pot is set down before it is lifted, without anybody saying why.

Bone went on without a boundary drawn. The older women of the district named the months by what the weather did and the slatemen named them by the count of Account-days that had gone into them, and the two habits lay side by side on this last light of the ninth day as they had lain on the six behind it, neither set right and neither left wrong in so many words. There was no market in the yard, no Cross, no bell; there was a pot set down with a small flat sound, a broom drawn over stone, water going over water somewhere behind the racks, and the district kept the face it turns to an afternoon when nothing is due. The month's hunts and repairs went on as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather. A hare had gone toward \place{the north gate road} answered with the weather; a handle had been mended where it stood and judged by the arm that would lift it and the dry cold of the day. Neither left a figure behind it, and no man weighing a hare or pricing a repair stood up in the yard to be asked. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what the day's work counted. That was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

She went wide around the walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, as she had gone around them in the mornings and would go around them again. The clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its place, and the finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw} lay unswept beside it; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} kept the broken birth-slates where nobody entered them; the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its bare shape with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; the water in \place{the trough} kept its skin; out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place kept its direction; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out kept the shape of a thing gone around rather than a thing named. Seven places gone around with a broom and a glance are still seven places kept, and a thing kept by going around it is kept as carefully as a thing kept under a lid. He went around each with her and gave none of them a number, and did not let seven likenesses stand up and call themselves a row.

At \place{the yard well} the three coverings stayed where they had been put. The stayed frost that had gone off the north lip while the yard looked elsewhere was gone and had left no white behind it; the trough kept its skin; and the piece of depth lifted out of it stood clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet. Three coverings in three places, all called the thaw and weather, entered nowhere. There was a finger between every pair of them and no fourth likeness made a cause, and no payer stood under any of the three. He took them as he took a wound's three edges --- saw what lay there, saw what had gone, saw what had been lifted and set where no foot went --- and wrote no cause across the space between. A covering laid over a depth, a covering lifted off a depth, and a piece of the depth set on a stone are three acts of one hand, and three acts of one hand are not one account for want of somebody to stand under them and say what they paid.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from \place{the hearth}, and she lifted a corner and turned it beneath the wool with a touch lighter than the morning's and gave it no figure. The three readings that had gone over it stayed three and not one account, riding up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box toward \place{High Kiln}, with one in twelve printed and unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid. There was no signature on the space between the figure and its price. He kept the three readings on three places and the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine; and he did not lay the finding across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the grain under wool at the rack end, the half-check kept on \place{the flagstones}, or his own kept gaps. The edges nearly touched and the finger went between. A finding with no payer is a thing that can be carried a long way without becoming a rule, and he carried it that way.

The returned half-check of the day before stayed on the flagstone's own place with the three older held changes beside it: the fraction at \lore{the damper}, the beat on \place{the loaf shelf}, the returned beat counted on Frost 5. Four were four and not a rule. He did not make a fifth likeness out of them and did not let four stand up together and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of the four. The shallow bright patch on the stone kept the place where the stroke had checked, and the stroke had gone on toward the road without a turn of the head, and neither man had named the other's silence. On this ninth day the stroke had gone over the patch without checking, and the place was kept anyway; a thing kept by not being made again is still kept. He set the four on four places and went around them with a finger, and none of them became the key to another.

The burned slate stood at five. The fifth telling had come over the boards from \place{Slate Row} on the fifth of Bone with no owner standing under it, no room behind it, no hand named at the flame and no reason given, and a voice with no throat is not a witness however often it is said. He put a finger between the fifth and the four and did not let the fifth walk into the row and make six; a sixth telling was not due on a day that had not earned one, and this day had not earned one. The word went around the boards at the going of the light and \person{Jorra Vail} grew quieter, and the household straightened around the quiet the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. No witness stood. No hand was named at the flame. The seed of the saying waited on the far-off calling-in of Jorra's promise, not a confession and not a payment, and the asking stood at five with one shaping and no witness and expressly no sixth.

The small keepings stayed small and were not brought forward. The charcoal beneath the loaf shelf stayed unread under the finger laid on top of the shelf's front edge; \person{Ilva}'s strip stayed in her sleeve offstage on this day and the day before, her ninth hand nine with no tenth and no figure coming off the wood; the three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stayed beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between every pair, and a fourth shaping was not to be stood up as a witness; the seventh green remained a colour at a woman's mouth and the seventh shape a shape at a market's edge with no eighth of either and no name spoken over them; the reed-cutter's cord lay where no Court thumb had read it; the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate stayed at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. None was a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. Nine hands were nine and must not call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood at the well. He kept them where they lay and did not carry one of them under the roof.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill where the house could check it and where it could slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond the mark where it could not. \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stood there. He did not say the word. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went out over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones and over it without the half-check; the movement of the day before stood where it had been left, remembered and not made again, and he set no finger of his across it to make four into one. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it; the two ears under the roof kept their equality and the two silences their one shape, and nothing on this Ninthday turned the seventh year into eight.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story, and his name had not been spoken under the roof for three hundred and eight days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it; the ridge that had been his ash-marker was a clean dark line with a finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, and neither had been swept. His proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- and had improved in the keeping and was very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said it; the other saying about a thing worth mending twice went with it to the pot. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. The keeping of an absence is maintenance and not a story, and nobody made a lesson of any of it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} Jorra came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey \lore{slip} dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either.

He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not have said it before a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it. The burned slate stood at five tellings and he put a finger between the fifth and the four and supplied no sixth; the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and had not gained a third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Sella's last grammar of the day held as the first had held. She went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and neither gap was entered; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour and a going of the light is not an opening; five openings in five months were five and not a rule. When she set the pot down on \place{the pot board} she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. She set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

In the small hours he turned out the file as he had once turned instruments out on a towel at the end of a list, laying each thing on its own place and asking nothing of any of them. The held interval lay where it had been laid; the empty interval lay empty; the missing fifth under the floor was missing and nobody had supplied it; the two shelf gaps stood unfigured; the ninth hand stood nine with the strip in the sleeve and no tenth; the fifth telling stood at five with the finger between it and the four; the four held changes stood four on four places. Nothing had been spent to make a row come out even. Nothing had stood up out of its place as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing moved. He had once laid out a tray for a man who never came off the table and had known the exactness of an instrument count without ever asking the count to save anybody; the old satisfaction came and went without bitterness, which is its own small grief.

At the very end of the day the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note, and the thing he took and let go was a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his. He let it go whole on the same low note, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, carrying it into neither \lore{the carried fire}'s missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square nor the river's low over lower. The hundred and ninety-second answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man in him took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away; both were true and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument was the work and not a virtue. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Three hundred and eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and twenty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. A hundred and ninety-two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made them stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nothing on the ninth of Bone was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended: no sixth asking, no tenth hand, no tenth stopping, no seventh hearing, no third telling of the shut room, no sixth telling of the burned slate, no fourth shaping of the well-saying, no eighth green or shape, no fifth pull supplied and no payer beneath the pot's mended handle. The month's first Account-day stood one morning beyond the reach of this day and had not been brought into it. The story moved because the hand kept answering and the count kept going, not because anything under the roof had settled.

The stiff leathery crust lay two inches from the ear with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. Under the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water} without a number put to it. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet, and the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
