The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat went off the high air first, drawn up and away as water goes off the outer wall of a pot before the water within has given anything up, and stayed longest in the low places --- under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with one leathery crust two inches from his ear. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. The crust had gone leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month, brown at the edge and stiff enough to keep its own shape against the wool; its empty place lay beside it without a name, and no third came to stand where the second had been. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and no instruction had been given about the thread, so it stayed where it was, a small bright line that nobody under any roof had been told to wipe. Cold had come as far as his cheek, his temple and the bare joint at his wrist where the wool had ridden back, and stopped there, as if it had reached the end of its own instruction too. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

Under the low roof the ordinary work went on around him in the order it had always kept. \person{Sella Vail} came in from the yard with her apron holding the day's dust and gave the instruction before she gave it to anybody, and it went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. The basket was a pot that had been filled and set down, and a pot set down and given no instruction is a pot left where it stands. She moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it, and then the hand went back to the row. She kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge and did not go beneath it, so the charcoal tally lay unread, uncounted and uncopied where it had lain since the day she had put a finger under the board and brought it away without a word. The beat that had stayed under that board stood where it had been left, a thing held between knowing the shelf by heart and being told not to look, and she did not look toward the bed.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening: the small intake, the hold, the letting go. The count arrived nowhere, as it had arrived nowhere on every other day of the boy's life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath and let go, and at no shelf, no hearth, no afternoon and no dusk since. An afternoon was not an opening. There had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under any roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The pot stood where it stood, and the two inches between its handle and his hand were not a distance anybody in this house had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them. He wanted the crust, and wanted the pot handle, and wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of the three, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same thing as not wanting it. The surgeon's register in him took the afternoon as a man takes a chart held at the wrong end: heat going, cold holding at the wrist, a thread frozen where nobody had been instructed to wipe it, the small hand with its inch of possible motion filed separately from all of it, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that no house could enter. Beside it lay the unforged sentence of \person{Garl Vail}, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford; it lay there as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man, and nobody under the roof had forged it in all two hundred days. \person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. A tool that had not been spoken to was a tool with no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who took up a tool before its work was named was a hand that would be entered for it.

“Yes, house,” he said, and told the kindling what it had been told so many times that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark. “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The other sentence came up as it came up every evening, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stood there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the small interval showing where it should have gone in, and went on with the broom. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent at the top of every reach; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on the day it had made one and gave it to nobody. The broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} in the afternoon, and the unforged sentence stayed beside the knife.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He had given the men no name and had not said the room was cold; the door kept its finger's width of open after them and the two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the boy did not lay them across the dust or the iron or anything else standing near them. Jorra kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay beneath \place{the lowest rack} and were entered by nobody.

“It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel before he said anything to a person. “It cares if they're steady.” To the pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind, who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, which stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} that had come up off \place{High Kiln} earlier in the day had gone out on the same wrong wind, answered as a direction and entered nowhere; a wrong wind was a direction, and that was the whole of the house's method for staying warm.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished with feeding and gave the end of the sentence to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, the same holding he had made on older days, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the flagstones and not stood up with either into a rule. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road, and the instruction went where it always went. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool and to the river wind, and never to the basket. The basket was carried as a pot is carried that has been filled and must not be slopped, at the half-pace she had kept going up in the sun and would keep coming back in the dark, and the boy inside it came down the road with the afternoon behind him and nobody under any roof asking what the figure had counted. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars, laid one above another across the falling air, and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}; the two passed without taking each other's colour, the smoke going up and the water going over, and she walked between them at her half-pace as a woman walks between two things she has been given no instruction to join. The arm that carried him had carried him two hundred mornings. It kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the small turn of the wrist at the end of a step, a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. Had somebody asked that arm what it had carried, the arm could not have told them; it could only have gone on carrying.

She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the low place of the wall, where nothing had stood for a month and no scrape, crumb, print or chip had come; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, its water called weather; around the space beneath the lowest rack of \place{the drying racks}, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. It was the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she gave the yard no name for any of the places, because a name is a handle and a handle is the beginning of a cost. No third gap came to stand beside the crust's empty place, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place; no fourth likeness stood up among them and called itself a cause; no finger of his drew the line between any two of them.

Under the Vail roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, and left the thread frozen. She told the fire it was the thaw and banked the coals with the back of a broken pot, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed; its payer had gone two hundred days without a name. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note, a fifth thing never counted among the hearings and never asked to stand up and be one; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He laid them where they lay and kept his hand between them, such hand as he had. A row of like things left standing long enough will call itself an account, and an account will want a figure, and a figure will want somebody to have paid it, and nobody under this roof had been asked to supply one.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the sources. No seventh came in two hundred days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. None had been promoted into the hearings and no pot had been lost in the yard. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket the day before and still not the figure paid. \person{Berr Clay}'s box had gone up the road locked with the true subtraction inside, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve and no figure coming off it, and the soft-cuffed clerk was still set below the bridge for a measure nobody had called. The market had read the figure and the pigs and the eels and the weather and read everything except the child under the roof, and none of its reading had entered this house.

Nine stoppings lay as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year was earned and the seventh nearer its end than ever and still not given. Three held changes stood on three places and did not stand up together and call themselves a doctrine: the fraction at the damper, the beat held under the board, the half-check on the stones, three places in a wall, a room and a yard, with no word spoken over any and no payer named. The small dry scrape from the loft lay on a fourth place, the pale line at the gum of the man carried out lay on a fifth, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces with no cause spoken over it in Grathic and no collector called, and the held inch lay on a sixth. The stone was cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in the loft; the charcoal under the shelf's front edge was unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan was uncalled; the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. The crust's empty place, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had stood up as a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line between any two of them. A row of absences left standing long enough will call itself a doctrine, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these. He kept his hand between them as he had kept it all his days, and the file came back exact, complete and worth nothing, with the figure gone into the air up the road and not one mark of it under this roof.

Then he put the question to his hand. He did not put it aloud; there was no need, and no ear in the house to hear a question if he had had the voice to make one. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, went up as it had gone up on other mornings and other small hours, and this time it did not come down before the breath finished and did not come down at once. It stayed up. It stayed through the small intake at the top of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch his, through the hold, and on into the letting go; and it came down with the breath rather than before it, against the fold. The rhythm went on without him and did not stop to notice that an inch had ridden it for the length of one breath. It was not a greater reach than any other inch and not a stronger hand. It was a coordination with a rhythm in a room he could not enter, and the rhythm belonged to the fire and to the woman counting at it, and neither had given him the count or known that he had taken it.

He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and did not lay it across them. It was not a reaching, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule; no payer was named to it and nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true. The longest holding was not a compensation for anything the body could not do; it was worth exactly what an inch was worth, which was nothing in a district that kept its accounts in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. The fold took the hand without a word.

Two hundred stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. The fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred days; the widow's mark had been touched once, before the kiln on Ember 1, and at no other hour; the stone lay cold in its loft pot, unreconciled; the charcoal under the shelf lay unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan that could hear was uncalled; the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note, under the floor the carried fire missed its fifth, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot --- three counts in three places that did not become one, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. Nothing in his keeping had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended now. The hand came down with the breath against the fold, the fold took it without a word, and the count went forward.
