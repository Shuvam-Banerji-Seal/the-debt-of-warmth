\daymark{200}{Ember 21, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

Hot clay came before the grey, under the tallow and under that the wet wool, and under all of it the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner. \person[Corin]{The boy} took the smells in their order as a man takes a pulse before a history, one thing arriving and then the next and the last thing staying longest, and \place{Low Kiln} came awake around him in that order too. It was the kiln month's last working week and the district still called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and corrected neither, as it had all year; a boundary drawn anywhere would have wanted a figure written on it, and there was no figure under this roof that anybody had been asked to write. Behind the wall the kiln drew in fours with the gap where a fifth pull should have closed, not a bell, and through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of \lore{water over water}, and above \place{the bed} the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

He was two hundred days old and six months and twenty days, and nobody under any roof said so. The number stood in the wool where the others had stood, exact, complete and unspent, and he had stopped expecting it to be spoken, which was not the same thing as not wanting it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed; its payer had gone two hundred days without a name. The one brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third, the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and been left unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and cold had come as far as his cheek, his temple and the bare joint at his wrist where the wool rode back and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the morning its instruction before she gave it to anybody. It went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket; the basket was carried as a pot is carried that has been filled and must not be slopped, and a pot that has been given no instruction is a pot that has been set down and left. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and kept the half-pace from every open slate that she had kept going up and down the road for two hundred mornings, though there was no slate under this roof and none in the yard at that hour; the half-pace had stayed when the reason for it had gone away, as a count stays in a man's hand after the thing counted has been carried off. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it, and then the hand went back to the work.

At \place{the hearth} she fed him from the house's own dish, two fingers behind his ear, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and not on his mouth. He got the taste of barley and the smaller taste of smoke and the warmth that came from neither of them in any way he could enter, and wanted both the food and the telling of where the warmth had been bought, and got the food. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said the house would keep it where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening: the small intake, the hold, the letting go. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} the water stood bare and dark in \place{the trough} under its little roof, and a woman letting down a bucket called it weather, so the bucket went down into weather and came up with weather in it. The woman had \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists, the colour of the clean dark line in the Vail corner laid on a person instead of a wall; she set the bucket down and asked what the pots would want before the kiln month was out, as if a month were a pot with a handle a body could take hold of. Sella did not turn toward the basket. “Ask the pot,” she said. “Ask the weather. The house has said it.” The rack woman took the pot and the weather away as an answer and went off level with her empty hands, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing slopped and nothing entered, and the boy felt the question go past him and arrive nowhere, and kept the shape of its travelling where such shapes go, joined to no rule.

A man come up off \place{the clay road} with road-dust dried pale in the seams of his coat asked what the kiln would want now that the figure had gone up twice. He brought the number up out of a fist the way other men bring up a tool, and held it there a moment for somebody to take, and nobody took it. “Ask the house,” Sella said. “Ask the weather.” The man put the number back into his fist and went away with it, and the fist went down the road and the number went nowhere, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. The printed one in twelve stood unchallenged a day's walk up the road; the true subtraction sat locked in a box that no hand under this roof could reach back into. A figure read twice over a basket was still not the figure paid.

The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind, that sweetish metal taste that had gone into a man's breath while two men carried him across the yard and up the road with his eyes open and his chest moving, and it went out again on the same wind. Nobody named the sickness in \lore{Grathic}. Nobody called a collector. The pale line at the man's gum lay on a place of its own, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, not joined to the wind or the dust or the pot that had sung early or any other likeness standing near it, and no second edge was paid so soon after the carrying-out. Sella gave the wind the pot and the weather as she had given them to the woman and the man, and it went where they went. A wrong wind was a direction, and a direction answered and let go was the whole of the house's method for staying warm.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its absences in the old order, as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. It went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it that was called the thaw and would go when it went; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the round empty ring on the low place of the wall where the shallow pan had stood through Reed and had gone without scrape, crumb, print or chip; around the trough bare and dark with its water called weather; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. No third gap came to stand beside the crust's empty place, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place; no fourth likeness stood up among them and called itself a cause; no finger of his drew the line between any two of them. A row of absences left standing long enough will call itself a doctrine, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

\scenebreak

Before first grey three or four men came up the yard with their collars high and went low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and neither the house nor \person{Jorra Vail} gave them a name, and nobody said the room was cold. The door kept its finger's width of open after them. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war with the men inside dead of the breath stood where they had stood, one brought upriver on a summer's day and one at a bucket-round with the mud still on the teller's hem, and no third came to join them, and the boy did not lay them across the dust or the iron or anything else standing near them.

Jorra came through from the shed with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, each lying where it lay on the tray of his attention and neither made to explain the other, and kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray without asking which slate had been broken or whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” To the pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early; the man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, which stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished with feeding and gave the end of the sentence to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, the same holding he had made on older mornings, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the board or the half-check on the flagstones and not stood up with either into a rule. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in Grathic that would carry both mercies in one mouthful; he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that no house could enter. Beside it lay the unforged sentence of \person{Garl Vail}, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford; it lay there as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man, and nobody under the roof had forged it in all two hundred days.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. A tool that had not been spoken to was a tool with no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who took up a tool before its work was named was a hand that would be entered for it. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood what it had been told so many times that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The other sentence came up as it came up every morning, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stood there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the small interval showing where it should have gone in, and went on with the broom. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent at the top of every reach; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on the day it had made one and gave it to nobody. The broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it; no collector came up or went down the clay road in the morning, and the unforged sentence stayed beside the knife.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the morning went on in its ordinary shapes, and the boy lay at the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} makes a roof that stops at the edge of his reach. Sella came and went, set the pot rim out again with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. The pot stood where it stood, and the two inches between the rim and his hand were not a distance anybody had been asked to measure.

Then he put the question to his hand. He did not put it aloud; there was no need, and no ear in the house to hear a question if he had had the voice to make one. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door, went up as it had gone up on other mornings, and held for the length of the small intake only, the top part of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that did not touch his. It came down before the letting go. It was shorter than the longest holding, the body keeping its own time, and an inch let down early was no more a failure than an inch that stayed or an inch that came down at once.

He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and did not lay it across them. It was not a reaching, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule; no payer was named to it and nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true.

The reading was a day behind him now, gone up the road in a locked box with the true subtraction inside and the printed one in twelve standing unchallenged where it had been read. The market had read the pigs and the eels and the weather and had not read the inch under the fold, and the market did not get to read it now; no slate in the district had a place for a small hand going up toward water over water and coming down before the breath was done. Two hundred stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody. The river kept its fifth low note, the carried fire missed its fifth under the floor, and the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot, three counts in three places that did not become one. The pot rim stood with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand had rested a half-beat longer at the setting and gone away, and the boy's small hand came down before the letting go against the fold, which took it without a word, while the count went on.
