The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat went off the high air first, lifted as if by a hand from the upper boards and set down lower, and it stayed longest in the low places: under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with one leathery crust two inches from his ear. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as though it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. Nothing crossed that edge. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dark and no instruction had come for it in the afternoon, and it stayed where it had been put, a small stiff line he could neither feel nor lift.

He lay under the roof of the shelf under the roof of \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the house}, two roofs, and neither reached past the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back. Cold had come that far in the small hours and had gone no farther. Between the cold and the warmth there was the width of a joint, and that was the whole of his country. A man who had once stood at the edge of a field and decided which bodies would be carried and which left where they lay now measured a kingdom by the exposure of one wrist. He did not make a grievance of it. The body's humiliations were exact and ordinary, and the surgeon's register took them down without comment: cheek, temple, wrist, the afterdrop waiting on a warming nobody in this house would perform.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row. It was not a song and not a count that anyone could have taken down. The loaf at the end stood where a loaf had stood; the old space at the row's end kept its place through the kiln month; and a palm's breadth of bare board had come in on the night of Seed 6 and had not been filled since. Two gaps stood in the row and she gave neither a figure. A figure spoken over a shelf would travel; a figure kept behind the teeth stayed where it was put, which was a thing a woman could do with a gap as with a pot --- set it down and leave it standing. Her lips went along the row and came back to the same place twice, the way her hand went to the same place twice when she counted fuel, and she gave nobody the thing she counted.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf. Her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it, the little-door burn closing on the air and opening again. The jar had been fetched from three doors down in another month and set there for a reason nobody had told her to give. Its glaze was cold and she left it so. Her finger, going to the shelf's front edge, stayed on top of the board and did not go beneath. Beneath that edge lay the charcoal tally, put there by a hand that was not hers, unread, uncounted, uncopied by anyone under this roof; and the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood where it had been left, on a place of its own, not stood up again this afternoon and not paired with anything. She straightened the board with the flat of her palm and turned from the shelf. The two gaps stayed in the other room, where a shelf's absences do not touch the one crust lying two inches from a boy's ear.

\scenebreak

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}. She said it to the fire and not to the room and not to the basket, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the pot's back going in under the ash and lifting and letting the heat come up through the gaps. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. The fire took the coal under its blind ash and answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-eight days. She did not supply one. A man who wanted the four to agree with the fifth would have had to put somebody under them, and no payer stood to the fire's gap.

Then she stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening. The small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. The count went out of her and arrived nowhere, and this was the whole of the house's method for staying warm: a cost asked as a direction, an answer given to the pot and the weather, nothing entered. From the low angle of the shelf he knew the shape of the count by the pause in her hands, the way a man learns the rhythm of another man's breathing in the next bed. It was not his breath. It was not his fire. There was a wall between the hearth and the bed, and the wall held.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; it had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no shelf, fire, basket, afternoon or dusk since, because an afternoon is not an opening. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Two inches was the whole distance: not a great gulf and not crossable. He could no more close it than lift his chin to the crust or speak the number that stood in his head. She did not look toward \place{the bed} at the setting. She never looked toward the bed at the setting, and the room that held the pot and the room that held the boy went on not touching each other, as they had touched in no hour of his life.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six years standing in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not --- the one entry in this house that no house could enter. Beside it lay the unforged sentence of Garl, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. It lay there as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man, and nobody under the roof had forged it in all the boy's days.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. A tool that had not been spoken to was a tool with no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who took up a tool before its work was named was a hand that would be entered for it; so his fingers closed on the handle only when Sella had given the broom its word. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the kindling what it had been told so often that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to.

A neighbour wanted the thaw early; another, coming off \place{the clay road} with dry scurf of mud on her hem, wanted it late. “Yes, house,” he said to each, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot between them, and went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road that afternoon. He swept the length of it as a man sweeps a step he does not mean to descend, and kept his eyes on the bristles.

His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it out; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth as he worked. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure in it and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach --- the breath that had stood unspent since Seed 16 with its small interval where the going-in should have gone --- and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood where they had been set, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments; no tenth stood up among them before the seventh year was earned, the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that could hear was not called. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch kept there from another day without the half-check, the stones holding the place where the stroke had once stopped and nobody entering it, and he did not look toward the bed or ask about the room.

In the edge of \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the shed}, where a finger's width of glass let the afternoon in thin, \person{Jorra Vail} spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel turned on. The three or four men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open back room before first grey got no name from him, and he did not say the room was cold; the two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention, the grey \lore{slip} dust ground small as meal past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip; and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath \place{the lowest rack} and were entered by nobody. To a pot coming round under his hands he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who stood a moment at the shed's edge with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner; the man went off up the yard with his silence taken for the silence of a man at work, and his saying about a pot singing before its time stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson. No pot was lost. The knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished the sentence with feeding and gave its end to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, the same holding he had made on older days, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones and not stood up with either into a rule. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed in whatever room he kept it, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind before it went anywhere near a person, and never to the basket: “Keep it off his ears,” she said, and the wind went on doing what it had been told and the basket was carried without a word. Woodsmoke lay in long low bars before her, laid one above another across the falling air, and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}. The two passed without taking each other's colour, the smoke going up and the water going over, and she walked between them at her half-pace as a woman walks between two things she has been given no instruction to join.

The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-eight mornings kept its record without a word said over it. The height of the handle, the give of the shoulder at a step's end, the small turn of the wrist that set the basket level again --- these were held in the muscle the way a surgeon's hands hold the feel of a cavity they have packed, a record of weight and not of cost. The arm knew what it had lifted and had never been told what the lifting was worth. It did not arrive anywhere. It went on carrying.

She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the wall's low place, where nothing had stood for a month and no scrape, crumb, print or chip had come; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark with its water called weather; around the space beneath the lowest rack of \place{the drying racks}, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing and the pale line at his gum. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. It was a way of walking a yard that has learned not to step where a thing is missing, the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it; and she gave the yard no name for any of the places, because a name is a handle and a handle is the beginning of a cost. Three absences left standing long enough would call themselves a cause and a cause would want a payer, and no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, left the thread frozen, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand resting a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. The widow's mark hung where it hung; a dusk was not an opening and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. She told the fire it was the thaw and banked the coals with the back of a broken pot, and \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and ninety-eight days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of water over water, and overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire, a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a boy who could not close two inches and a number nobody had spoken. The wall between them kept both.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, with no towel and no instruments and no case, but the whole order of the laying. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the sources. No seventh came and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid. Nine stoppings lay as objects and not a rule. The Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach, and the loan that could hear was uncalled.

Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at the iron, the beat under the shelf, the half-check on the stones --- and no hand of his laid them across one another. The small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on loft stone stood on a fourth place; the pale line at a gum, read at two paces and worth nothing at two paces, stood on a fifth; the held inch, asked and answered and answered differently in the afternoon, stood on a sixth. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch, with no third gap come, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawn across them. The stone lay cold in its unmarked pot in the loft, two years gone and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving; the charcoal under the shelf was unread; the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere.

The figure that would be read at \place{the Cross} on the morrow was not entered in any of it. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and the printed one in twelve stood where it had been read, unchallenged, while the true subtraction went up the road in a locked box and was not brought back under this roof. The read figure lay beside the paid and never became one; it was a thing said over a crowd, and the crowd went back to its pigs, and nothing under this house had been entered by it. He set each thing down in its own hollow and nothing rested across anything else, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. He took the file up again in the same order, exact, complete and worth nothing. The hand stayed down against the fold, and the count went on.
