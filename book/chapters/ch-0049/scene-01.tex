\daymark{198}{Ember 19, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down. It had gone up in the dark and come down in the dark, and the dark had not changed its mind about anything. He lay on \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the roof} with the fold against his cheek and one leathery brown-edged crust two inches from his ear, its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth frozen where the small hours had left it, unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The first smell of the day was hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and beneath all of it the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner, which the house called \lore{the thaw} and had called it so for a year without sweeping it; the first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap behind it, not a bell. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. \person[Corin]{The boy} had been a hundred and ninety-seven days old in the dark and was a hundred and ninety-eight days old in the morning, six months and eighteen days, and nobody under any roof said either number. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

\scenebreak

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have closed, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-eight days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing heard beside the others and never numbered among them; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The man who had once read a chart by its neat margin and known the finding was worth nothing without a hand to carry it read these three the same way and let them lie where they lay. A count that finds a payer becomes an account, and an account becomes a rule with a hand to spend it; there was no hand here and no payer, so the count went on exact, complete and unspent, and the morning came up over \place{Low Kiln} without drawing a boundary around anything. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had long since learned to want them without making a petition of the wanting.

The body he had been given kept its own poor account. It could not roll, could not lift its chin two inches to the crust, could not close the two inches to the pot handle, could not hold up its own head through a whole spoonful; it drooled and was fed by a sleeve that was not its own, and was carried, dressed and discussed in the third person by a woman who said \emph{the boy} where another woman would have said \emph{my son}. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and known which rib would tear the lung, and now the whole of his field was one bare joint at a wrist where the wool had ridden back. The old life did not break into the room; it stood behind him like a man waiting outside a door with a chart in his hand, and the chart said nothing he could use. Both things were true at once, and the younger of them was louder, being hungry.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} gave the instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, “it's the river wind,” and to the fire, “It's the thaw,” and to the pot, “Don't set that near the door. You'll pay for it in the thaw.” She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, carried the basket at a half-pace from every open slate, and gave no instruction whatever to the basket, which went out under a fold and came back under the same fold without a word spoken over it, as a pot filled is carried that must not be slopped. At \place{the hearth} she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and told the fire it was the thaw, and the fire took the coal under its blind ash and answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and ninety-eight days. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening: the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. No opening stood at the end of the count and she had not looked for one, for a woman who counts toward a thing she does not want to find will keep the count exact and let it arrive nowhere.

Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, her hand starting toward it and shutting half over nothing without touching it --- the little-door burn closing on the air and opening again --- and she left the jar where it stood, fetched from three doors down in another month for a reason nobody had told her to give. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; it had come in the winter \person{Garl Vail} died, and she would say no more than that. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat; it had been touched once before the kiln on Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and at no hour since, for a morning and a shelf are not openings and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1. Then she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She did not look toward the bed at the setting. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish, the spoon wiped on her sleeve and not on his mouth, two fingers behind the ear holding his head the way one holds a thing that will not hold itself, and said nothing about where the warmth in the dish had come from or who had paid for it. The boy watched her hand arrive at the pot before her eyes arrived at the room, and put that order on a place of its own where he kept such orders, without making a row of them and without letting the row stand up and call itself an account. She wiped the spoon on her sleeve and did not wipe his mouth, and did not wipe the thread at the corner of his lip, because the thread had not been given an instruction and neither had she, and the two omissions lay in two places that did not touch. He watched the burn close on the dish and open again, and wanted the crust, and wanted somebody to say what a day counted, and got neither, and did not turn the wanting into a petition.

\scenebreak

Before first grey had fairly gone she carried him out into \place{the Clay Yard}. Woodsmoke lay in the yard in long low bars and the river wind came behind it with the taste of water over water, and the two passed without taking each other's colour, as a clean hand passes a stained one on a stair and neither leaves a mark. At \place{the yard well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather; the yard went wide around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it, with a look and a wide step and no number. At the threshold a neighbour with dry mud-scurf on her hem stopped them and asked what the firing would want now that the second reading stood a day off.

“Ask the pot,” Sella said. “The house has said it.”

“It's the firing,” the woman said. “It'll want something.”

“Ask the weather.”

“Then somebody's carrying it,” the woman said. “That's what it comes to, with another reading a day off and nobody saying what it wants.”

“The house will keep it,” Sella said, and turned the pot a half-inch on the board with two fingers, as if turning the pot might settle the question where answering it would not.

“Our lot kept it last year.”

“Ask the pot. It knows more than I do.”

The woman stood a moment with her question still in her mouth, and set her pot down, and looked at the pot, which kept nothing and told nothing, and took the question away level, with nothing priced, refused or entered, and went back to her work without appearing to have seen the basket. The figure that had gone up the road in a locked box stayed up the road and was not brought back under this roof; the printed one in twelve stood where it had been read, unchallenged, and a figure read aloud was not the figure paid. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind and went out on the same wind, a direction answered with the pot and the weather, not a second edge paid so soon after the carrying-out; the sickness was not named in \lore{Grathic}, no collector was called, and no lesson was made of the glaze. At the edge of \place{the clay road} she turned him toward home. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-eight mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the turn of the wrist at a step's end --- a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost --- and went around the pan's gone round ring with a look and a wide step and no number, and did not ask the yard for a figure. Under the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood the dust kept a round ring with no scrape, no chip and no word, and she went around it as she went around the trough and the rack space and the place the carriers had crossed. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. The boy kept the ring on its own tray, not laid across the shelf's two gaps or the empty place at his ear, because three absences left standing long enough will call themselves a cause and a cause wants a payer. No third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line.

\scenebreak

Back under the roof the warmth had gone off the high air first and had settled in the low places: under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where he lay with the crust two inches from his ear. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. Nothing crossed that edge. The thread of drool had not been wiped in the yard and was not wiped now. He watched the slant end where his fingers ended, and thought, without arriving at anything he could have told somebody, that a house which could stop heat at an edge and stop cold at a wrist and stop a question at a pot had found the same answer three times and would not have called it an answer at all.

At the note of water over water through the finger's-width door he put the question to his own hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch and he asked it to stay. It came down before the counted breath had finished --- a shorter holding, the body keeping its own time, an inch let go early and no failure in it, for an inch held briefly is no more a reaching than an inch held long --- and the fold took it without a word. The small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go: the rhythm went on in the other room without him and did not stop to notice whether an inch of a boy's hand had stayed with it. That was the whole of it, and there was nothing to be proud of in it --- not a reach, not a stronger hand, not a sign that anything under this roof had been entered, only a brief agreement between a hand and a breath in a room he could not enter. He filed it on a place of its own beside the others and did not lay it across them: not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named and no doctrine stood up out of it. The man who had once kept pressure in a cavity with both hands until the vessel took was keeping nothing now but one inch of a small soft hand, and the keeping cost him nothing and bought him nothing. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin to the crust or speak a number; the body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true. The asking had been answered and then answered differently, and the answer was worth nothing and nobody saw it. Ember's second Account-day and market stood one day off, and the market would not get to read the inch.

One hundred and ninety-eight stood exact in the dark of the wool, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof. The carried fire had had no payer for a hundred and ninety-eight days; the charcoal under the shelf was unread by a hand that was not hers and for a day that had not come; the mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the warm stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving; the Seed 16 breath was unspent at the top of every reach and the loan that could hear was uncalled; the cost of the man carried out while the month was young was entered nowhere. The river kept its fifth low note, the fire missed its fifth pull under the floor, and the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb, three counts in three places and none rising up and becoming the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The count went forward.
