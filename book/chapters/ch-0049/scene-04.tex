\person{Sella Vail} carried him home up \place{the clay road} at the going of the light, and the instruction went where it always went. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool and to the river wind, and never to the basket. The basket was carried as a pot is carried that has been filled and must not be slopped, at the half-pace she had kept going up in the sun and would keep coming back in the dark, and \person[Corin]{the boy} inside it came down the road with the market behind him and nobody under any roof asking what the figure had counted. The road had dried pale in the week's heat. The ruts held the day's dust in their shallow troughs, and the dust rose behind her step and settled again without ever being entered, as the district's arithmetic had risen that morning and settled where it had been spoken.

Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars, laid one above another across the falling air, and the river wind came behind her with the taste of \lore{water over water}. The two passed without taking each other's colour, the smoke going up and the water going over, and she walked between them at her half-pace as a woman walks between two things she has been given no instruction to join. The arm that carried him had carried him a hundred and ninety-nine mornings. It kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder, the small turn of the wrist at the end of a step, a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost. Had somebody asked that arm what it had carried, the arm could not have told them; it could only have gone on carrying. That was the house's whole way of knowing a thing, by the shape of it in the hand and never by what it was worth.

She went wide around the pan's gone round ring on the low place of the wall, where nothing had stood for a month and no scrape, crumb, print or chip had come; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, its water called weather; around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} of \place{the drying racks}, where the broken birth-slates lay and were entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the carriers had crossed with a man breathing and the pale line at his gum. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. It was the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she gave the yard no name for any of the places, because a name is a handle and a handle is the beginning of a cost.

A neighbour come up from the racks with \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists stopped at the yard's edge with a question already half out of her. What would the firing want, now the figure had gone up twice? Sella did not turn toward the basket. “Ask the pot,” she said. “Ask the weather. The house has said it.” The woman took the pot and the weather away as an answer and went off level with her empty hands, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing slopped and nothing entered. The boy felt the question go past him and arrive nowhere, and kept the shape of its travelling where such shapes go, joined to no rule. The district's figure had gone into the air over two hundred people and could not now be got back into a fist; under this roof a cost asked as a direction came to rest on a pot and went no further. It was a method for staying warm, and it had kept this house warm a long while without once saying who paid.

\scenebreak

Under the Vail roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, into which no sound from the reading had crossed and would not have been entered if it had. She put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face, left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, her hand resting a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat. A dusk was not an opening, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1, and nobody under that roof had praised what the one opening had mended or seen what it had broken. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward nothing entered.

Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have closed. Its payer had been without a name for a hundred and ninety-nine days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its fifth low note of water over water, a fifth thing that had never been counted among the hearings and was never asked to stand up and be one; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He laid them where they lay and kept his hand between them, such hand as he had. A row of like things left standing long enough will call itself an account, and an account will want a figure, and a figure will want somebody to have paid it. The market had had its figure that morning, read twice over a basket that had not entered it, and here under the roof the same figure had not crossed the wall; the market's arithmetic and the house's arithmetic stood in two rooms and did not touch, and he did not lay one across the other to see which would balance.

\scenebreak

At \place{the loaf shelf} she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. One glance went to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on whatever came within reach of it; then the hand went back to the row. Her finger stayed on top of the front edge and did not go beneath, so the charcoal tally lay unread, uncounted, uncopied, where it had lain since the day she had put a finger under the board and brought it away without a word. The beat that stayed under that board stood where it had been left, a thing held between knowing the shelf by heart and being told not to look. The fire was told \lore{the thaw} as a direction; the mark went untouched, for a shelf and a dusk are not openings and the cord at her throat had been answered once and at no other hour. The two gaps kept their places in the row and did not call themselves a number, and the empty place at his ear lay on a tray of its own in a room that did not touch this one.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in a worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh at the lip where it could not, the one entry in this house that no house could enter. Beside it lay the unforged sentence of \person{Garl Vail}, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford. It lay there as a tool lies that has never been beaten into shape, neither a proverb for the yard nor a charge against a man, and nobody under the roof had forged it in all the boy's days. \person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. He did not take it when it was merely lying there; a tool that had not been spoken to was a tool with no work in it, and a clay-bound hand who took up a tool before its work was named was a hand that would be entered for it. Sella gave the instruction to the broom, and only then did his fingers close on the handle.

“Yes, house,” he said, and told the kindling what it had been told so many times that the words had worn smooth as the thumb-mark: “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it in the tone of a man stating the weather, a statement of fact that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The other sentence came up as it came up every evening, the one about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stood there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the small interval showing where it should have gone in, and said “Yes, house” to the fire and went on with the broom. Nine stoppings now stood as nine objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the Seed 16 breath was unspent at the top of every reach; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on the day it had made one and gave it to nobody. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. No collector came up or went down that road at the going of the light, and the unforged sentence stayed beside the knife.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip. Three or four men had gone low into that room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray of his attention and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third, and the boy did not lay them across the dust or the damper or anything else standing near them.

“It doesn't care whose hands,” Jorra said to the wheel before he said anything to a person. “It cares if they're steady.” To the pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The man took the silence for the silence of a man at work and went off up the yard with his saying, which stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost. A wrong wind was a direction and went out on the same wind, and the sweet edge that had come off \place{High Kiln} that morning had been answered the same way and entered nowhere.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held there. He held it while she finished with feeding and gave the end of the sentence to the pot; then the tongue moved a hair's breadth and the finger was left standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, the same holding he had made on older days, not a change made again, not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the flagstones and not stood up with either into a rule. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and nobody spoke of the waiting. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket, and the boy did not ask him for what he had not asked.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file again, in the same order, the way a man lays instruments on a towel with no towel and no instruments and no case, but the whole discipline of the laying intact. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among: five from four sources and the sixth out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the sources. No seventh came and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket and still not the figure paid. Berr's box had gone up the road locked with the true subtraction inside, Hessa's slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing to her sleeve and no figure coming off it, and the soft-cuffed clerk was still set below the bridge for a measure nobody had called. The market had read the figure, the pigs, the eels, the turnips and the weather, and read everything except the child under the roof; none of its reading had entered this house.

Nine stoppings lay as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year was earned and the seventh nearer its end than ever and still not given. Three held changes stood on three places and did not stand up together and call themselves a doctrine: the fraction at the damper, the beat held under the board, the half-check on the stones, three places in a wall, a room and a yard, with no word spoken over any and no payer named. The small dry scrape from the loft lay on a fourth place, the pale line at the gum of the man carried out lay on a fifth, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces with no cause spoken over it in Grathic and no collector called, and the held inch lay on a sixth. The stone was cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in the loft; the charcoal under the shelf's front edge was unread; the Seed 16 breath was unspent; the loan was uncalled; the cost of the man carried out was entered nowhere. The crust's empty place, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had stood up as a cause, and no finger of his had drawn the line between any two of them. A row of absences left standing long enough will call itself a doctrine, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these. He kept his hand between them as he had kept it all his days, and the file came back exact, complete and worth nothing, with the figure gone into the air up the road and not one mark of it under this roof.

\scenebreak

Then he put the question to his hand again. He did not put it aloud; there was no need, and no ear in the house to hear a question if he had had the voice to make one. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water that came through the finger's-width door, went up as it had gone up on other mornings and other small hours, and came down at once with no holding in it at all. The asking was not always answered. An inch let go at once was no more a failure than an inch let go early or an inch held through a whole counted breath and on into the letting go; the body kept its own time and gave what it gave, and there was no payer under any of them to make one more than another.

He filed the miss on the sixth place beside the holdings, where such things go, and did not lay it across them. It was not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. No payer was named to it and nothing was stood up out of it to call itself a sign. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The hand came down against the fold and the fold took it without a word. One hundred and ninety-nine stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody, and the market's figure was not entered anywhere under the roof. The river, the fire and the boards kept their three counts in three places, and the count in the wool went forward.
