\daymark{211}{Grain 2, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

The small hours thinned as a fire thins, by degrees and with nobody calling the hour. He lay under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} with the fold at his ear and the hand outside the wool lying where the fold had taken it, and the new day reached him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. Then the kiln drew in beyond the wall, four great mouthfuls and a short letting go, and the gap where the fifth should have stood. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered; this was neither, and nobody under the roof crossed himself at it or gave the gap a name.

Two hundred and eleven days stood exact in him, complete and unspent, and six months and thirty-one days, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} with a line for either. \person[Corin]{The boy} had stopped expecting to be told where he stood in the count, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash, and he had long since given up asking which of the two was hotter. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the fifth had now been without a payer for two hundred and eleven days, two hundred and eleven days of heat given away and no name set to the giver. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings because it came from water and not from a vessel. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He did not join them. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; the river was not asked, the fire had no name over it, and the nails had been driven by a hand long dead and entered in nothing.

The crust lay where it had been laid, leathery and brown-edged, two inches from his ear, and the empty place beside it kept its empty name and no third came to stand there. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- and the reading bought nothing at two paces. He knew the sequence of a body taking its heat back and could not alter one step of it, and the child's body went on being cold while the man's mind recorded how cold, and the two occupied him without either buying the other a thing.

\scenebreak

The firing thinned into the ordinary tending of a year begun. It did not end; nothing under this roof ended, it wore away the way a pot wears at the rim, so that by the time the light came a man would have been hard put to say when the kiln month had stopped being the kiln month and Grain had started being Grain. What was left was the round. The heat went off the high air first, off \place{the kiln mouth} and off the peg where the coat hung, and stayed longest in the low places. The yard went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner, and around the new finer hairline beside it that the house called the thaw, with a look and a wide step and no broom taken to either; it went around the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the circle the shallow clay pan had left when it went, a round ring on the ground that named a thing by the shape of its absence; it went around the bare dark \place{the trough} at the well whose water was called weather, and around the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number spoken over any of them. In \place{the Clay Yard} nobody counted the goings-around and nobody entered them; the house kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and in the space where a thing had stood, which was the only arithmetic it would own to. He had learned to take the empty places one at a time, the way a man lays out instruments on a towel and keeps each in its own place --- the crust's gap here, the shelf's two gaps there, the pan's gone ring on its own tray, the field's empty place on another --- with no finger of his drawn across them and no third gap made to come out even. The firing had given these nothing and taken nothing from them; it had only gone off the high air and left them standing in the low places where they had always stood.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} a rack woman stood with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists, the pale of it showing where a fist had opened and shut all morning, and she asked what the new month's pots would want now the firing was done. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down for the asking and gave the instruction to the running water before she gave it to the woman. “Ask the pot,” she said. “The house has said it. Ask the weather too, if the pot's not talking.” The rack woman took it as an answer and went away level with her question still standing in the air behind her, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered, the question carried away as carefully as a pot carried full. A man come up off \place{the clay road} with a number shut in his fist stood a moment with his mouth open on it and paid nothing; the number went back into the fist and the fist went back into the coat, and the man went off up the road with his arithmetic still his own. Two directions had come over the boards in the space of a morning and neither had landed. He took them apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound that has come in dirty --- the cost from the woman, the number from the man's fist --- and kept them on separate places, and neither stood up out of its place to call itself a cause.

Up the road \person[Berr Clay]'s box had stayed locked, going toward \place{High Kiln} with Ember's third true subtraction inside it, and the figure printed in twelve stood unchallenged where it had been printed, and \person[Hessa Rale]'s slate lay unread at \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to the sleeve of a coat shortened for the road. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody had called, his hand going to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls. All of it stood in the boy's file where it had been put and none of it was opened and none of it reached back; he had no hand to carry any of it to a slate and no instruction to do it, and the file lay exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces.

Under the roof Sella came to the fire before the basket, as she had come on every day of his life, and gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind and never to the basket. “The house,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She kept her half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the pan's gone ring; her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Standing with her back to the basket she moved her lips along the loaf row, which stood with two unnamed gaps, and gave nobody a figure; her finger lay on the top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. She told the fire that the thaw was come and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, because a morning shelf and a well are not openings: thirty-one days with the mark untouched. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside it lay \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been instructed, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. Grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since an older binding, held what light there was and gave it back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the small interval stood where it should have gone in, unspent since the seed-month and carried into the new tend like a tool he had been told to keep and had never used. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood at its very end now, nearer than it had ever stood, and had not given. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} in the morning. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached him only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot, two years gone, and the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on a sill stayed on a place of its own.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail] came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. The men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey were not named by him and the room was not said to be cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the burned-slate word that had come over the boards and arrived nowhere. He kept that word on a place of its own beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, and made no cause of it. The broken birth-slates under the lowest rack were gone around by the yard and entered by nobody, and he entered none of them. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up from High Kiln on a wrong wind who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost in the yard.

Then Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth} --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- and his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot; then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful: the iron was one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences kept their old shape under one roof with nobody putting a word between them.

\scenebreak

In the morning, asked, the hand went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down at once against the fold. Not on the small intake, not after a hold; down at once, before the note had drawn out. The asking is not always answered and there is no failure in the miss and no triumph in an answer. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person and no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; the told bell beside the heard, four, four and a gap, and the told figure beside the paid, one in twelve read three times and still not the figure paid. None of it had moved. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true.

The hand came down at once. The thread at his mouth was still frozen and unwiped, one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the salt coat on its peg behind the door held a broader man's shape while the seat below it kept a hollow that nobody sat down to fill. The rack woman's question and the man's shut number had gone over the boards and arrived nowhere; the fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. Two hundred and eleven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, the carried fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and eleven days, and the boy kept his own count without a figure.
