The afternoon thinned without ever coming to an end. It did not break, it wore away the way a pot wears at the rim, so that nobody under the roof could have said where the light had gone, only that it had gone and the day was thinner for it. Heat left the high air first. It went off the rafters and off the top of the wall and off the peg where the coat hung, and stayed longest in the low places: under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold where one leathery brown-edged crust lay stiff against his ear. No third came to stand beside it. The empty place beside it kept its empty name. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

The underside of the shelf made a roof over him, and the roof stopped where his hand stopped. Heat came down that roof in a slow slant all the afternoon and stopped at the same edge, the way water stops at the lip of a trough that has been filled too full and not spilled. He lay at the low angle with his hand outside the wool and watched the slope of the heat come down toward him and not quite arrive. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the order of it without being asked --- the surface warm, the core not, the reading exact --- and the reading bought nothing at two paces. He knew the sequence of a body taking its heat back and could not alter one step of it, and the child's body went on being cold while the man's mind recorded how cold, and the two occupied him without either buying the other a thing.

He had stopped expecting to be told where he was in the count, which was not the same as not wanting it. The wanting went on under the expectation the way the heat went on behind the pot. Two hundred and ten days stood exact in him, complete and unspent, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} with a line for any of them; the fire's fifth had had no payer for the whole of that count and no name had been put to the payer in all that time. The month had gone out as a fire goes out, by degrees, with nobody calling the hour, and neither of the district's two names for the time had been corrected at the turning. Grain had come on in the dark and gone on coming, and the ordinary tending of a year begun ran on around the fold without a mark at the start of it.

\scenebreak

The house kept \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} in objects and not in a story at the turning of the month. The salt-stiff coat hung on its peg behind the door and held a broader man's shape with the crease at the left shoulder where a wheel had pressed for twenty years; nobody took it down and nobody filled it. The seat below held its twenty-year leftward lean and its hollow on that side, and a man who came into the room went around the hollow without looking at it, the way a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned the corner lay along the skirting board, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey the house called \lore{the thaw} lay where it had been laid, and neither had been swept. A look went around both and a wide step went around both and no broom took up either. His saying about leaving the crack showing had gone to the pot and stayed there, said to the fire and never to a person; and the debt against a firing of tiles went around the room without a name, worse for being uncalled, because a thing uncalled can be neither paid nor settled nor stopped. No lesson was made of any of it and no collector came up \place{the clay road} because of it. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ten days. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and \person[Corin]{the boy}'s own exactness was maintenance too, and neither made a lesson of the other.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} in the afternoon and stood with her back to the basket, as she had stood on every day of his life and had never once turned to look into it. She moved her lips along the loaf row, which stood with two unnamed gaps, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied; the beat she had held under it on an older day stayed where it was left. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it, and went back to its work.

She told the fire that the thaw was come and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and at an afternoon it hung where it hung. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life, and the house kept its warmth without once saying who had paid for it. “The house will keep it,” she said to the fire, where another woman would have said I. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. She never looked toward the bed at the setting. The two inches were not a distance anybody under this roof had been asked to measure, and the hand lying outside the wool on the fold did not close them. A dusk was not an opening and a shelf was not an opening, and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1: thirty days with the mark untouched. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been instructed, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since an older binding, held what light there was and gave it back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood at its very end now, nearer than it had ever stood, and had not given. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the small interval stood where it should have gone in, unspent since the seed-month and carried into the new tend like a tool he had been told to keep and had never used. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. No collector came up or went down the clay road in the thinning afternoon. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached him only as a sound with a direction, the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot, two years gone, and the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on a sill stayed on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes and not made into a cause, for a fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause and a cause would want a payer. He kept his exact account of what he owed and did not enter the stone in it, and the two things stood side by side in him without agreeing.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail] came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. The men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey were not named by him and the room was not said to be cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the burned-slate word that had come over the boards and arrived nowhere. He kept the burned-slate word on a place of its own beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, and made no cause of it. The broken birth-slates under \place{the lowest rack} were gone around by the yard and entered by nobody, and he entered none of them. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind who said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and no pot was lost in the yard.

Then Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- and his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot; then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, and it lay on neither of the other two places beside the fraction of an older damper morning and the flagstone half-check. He read the iron as one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences kept their old shape under one roof with nobody putting a word between them.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water} in it, the fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings because it came from water and not from a vessel. She went wide around the pan's gone round ring, the bare dark \place{trough} whose water was called weather, the rack space under \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. At \place{the yard well} she set the basket down for a moment to shift her grip, and the instruction went to the wool and the running water and never to the basket, and she did not look into it. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost; it knew the basket as a hand knows a weight it has carried before, and never asked what the weight was entered for. She carried him up under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} and set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it had frozen. Nothing had been priced on the walk and nothing had been entered, and the year's first tend went on beyond the door without a boundary drawn behind it.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the carried fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and ten days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, drawing out and slackening and coming again when it came. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He did not join them. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; the river was not asked, the fire had no name over it, and the nails had been driven by a hand long dead and entered in nothing. The two rooms did not touch. \lore{The carried fire} went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. The crust kept its empty place unnamed and no third came; cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited where it had waited all afternoon.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh had come in two hundred and ten days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, Ember's third true reading gone up the clay road in a box that had been locked and was not brought back. Nine stoppings stood as objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year that now stood at its very end and had still not given. Three held changes stood on three places, with the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. Beside the dust and the two tellings of the room shut in the war, with a hand between the three, the burned-slate word lay on a place of its own --- a direction come from a place, gone over boards and arrived nowhere, with no owner, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason. The crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the shallow pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. He had no third gap, no fourth likeness made a cause and no finger of his drawn across any of them. Nothing had been filled in to make a row come out even.

Then he put the question to his hand. Sella had gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door, the low note drawing out and slackening and the next low note coming when it came. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and held for the length of the small intake only, and came down before the letting go. It did not go up to the pot handle, which sat two inches beyond it; it did not lift his chin two inches to the crust; it did not speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The asking is not always answered, and there is no failure in the miss and no triumph in an answer. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true.

The hand came down before the letting go. The thread at his mouth was still frozen and unwiped, one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the salt coat on its peg behind the door held a broader man's shape while the seat below it kept a hollow that nobody sat down to fill. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and the count stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, ready to go forward into the next day with no mark at the turning.
