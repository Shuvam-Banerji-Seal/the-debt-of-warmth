The afternoon went off the high air first and came down into the low places, as heat does in a house built of clay and shadow. It left the peg by the door and the top of the wall and settled under \place{the loaf shelf}, behind the pot, in the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with one stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear. The crust had lain there through the morning and would lie there through the night; the empty place beside it kept its empty name, and no third came to stand there, though the shelf in the other room had gaps of its own that nobody joined to it. The underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped exactly where his hand stopped, and the warmth came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same edge, so that he lay in a small country of his own with a warm ceiling and a cold wrist. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. Nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- and the reading bought nothing at two paces. He knew the sequence of a body taking its heat back and could not alter one step of it, and the child's body went on being cold while the man's mind recorded how cold, and the two occupied him without either buying the other a thing.

Two hundred and eleven days stood exact in him, complete and unspent, and six months and thirty-one days, and there was no slate in \place{Sarn} or \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} with a line for either. He had stopped expecting to be told where he stood in the count, which was not the same as not wanting it; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the fifth had now been without a payer for two hundred and eleven days, two hundred and eleven days of heat given away and no name set to the giver. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that was never counted among the hearings because it came from water and not from a vessel. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. He did not join them. A man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them; the river was not asked, the fire had no name over it, and the nails had been driven by a hand long dead and entered in nothing.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, which stood with two unnamed gaps. She gave nobody a figure. Her hand went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her little-door burn closed on air as her hand shut half over nothing without a touch, and she turned back to the row. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and what lay beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied. The charcoal had lain there since the first Account-day of Reed and had been reached beneath only by a finger that did not look and did not stay; an afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as a finger under a board, and she knew the difference as well as she knew the weight of a full pot.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire that \lore{the thaw} was come, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- and the count arrived nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, because an afternoon and a dusk are not openings: thirty-one days with the mark untouched. Her private roll stayed unshown, and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

The house kept \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} in objects and not in a story through the second day of Grain. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held a broader man's shape with the crease at the left shoulder where he had rested his hand; the seat kept its twenty-year leftward lean and the hollow on that side that nobody sat down to fill. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner lay unswept, and the new finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called the thaw lay unswept beside it, two lines in one corner that the yard went around with a look and a wide step and no broom. The proverb about leaving the crack showing had gone to the pot, where such sayings go in this house; the debt against a firing of tiles went around the house once more, circled without a name and worse for being uncalled, because it could neither be paid nor settled nor stopped. No lesson was made of any of it, no collector called, and the dead man's name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eleven days. The keeping of him was maintenance, and maintenance was not love, and neither of those things was entered anywhere.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Beside it lay Garl's unforged collector sentence, the one about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been instructed. His chest had been tight since the dark; grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since an older binding, held what light there was and gave it back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

“Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and set the knife back where it could be checked.

The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the small interval stood where it should have gone in, unspent since the seed-month and carried into the new tend like a tool he had been told to keep and had never used. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood at its very end now, nearer than it had ever stood, and had not given. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check the stones remembered and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} at the going of the light. Above the shed, in \place{the Debt Loft}, the warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked clay pot, and the small loft-sill scrape stayed on its fourth place with no word spoken over it and no payer named to it.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail] came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. The men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey were not named by him, and the room was not said to be cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the burned-slate word that had come over the boards and arrived nowhere. He kept that word on a place of its own beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, and made no cause of it. The broken birth-slates beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} were gone around by the yard and entered by nobody, and he entered none of them. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind. The man said that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early, and the saying stayed a saying about a pot and was not made true as a lesson, and cost no pot, and no pot was lost in the yard. Jorra grew quieter whenever the burned-slate word went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order; he gave the men no name and did not say the room was cold, and the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door.

Then Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- and his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached \emph{feeding} and the end was given to the pot; then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered holding and not a change made again, filed where the old fraction lay and not made twice over. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful: the iron was one piece with two bites, the holding and the taking, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences kept their old shape under one roof with nobody putting a word between them.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. She went around the circle the shallow clay pan had left when it went, a round ring on the ground that named a thing by the shape of its absence; around the bare dark \place{the trough} at the well whose water was called weather; around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; around the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and the arm went on keeping the record while the mind went on keeping its other one, and neither was laid across the other. In \place{the Clay Yard} nobody counted the goings-around and nobody entered them; the house kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and in the space where a thing had stood, which was the only arithmetic it would own to.

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it was. The fire answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for two hundred and eleven days; the river kept its note through the finger's-width door; the boards kept four nails, four and a knot. Three counts in three places. Neither the fire nor the river stopped for the ear under the roof, and the boy lay where the fold had put him with one crust at his ear and the empty place beside it still without a name.

\scenebreak

In the small hours Sella had gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house --- nothing to hold against but the rhythm that came through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening, and the next low note coming when it came. He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person and no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and the told figure beside the paid, one in twelve read three times and still not the figure paid. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray as objects and not as a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year was earned and now at its very end. Three held changes stood on three places with the loft scrape on a fourth, the pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. The burned-slate word lay on its own place beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings with a hand between the three, and the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. Nothing had been filled to make a row come out even, and nothing in the file had moved since he laid it down.

Then he put the question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and stayed for the length of the river's own low interval --- the draw, the slack, the small pause before the next note came --- and came down on the river's next low note. It was the one-rhythm coordination remembered from a night days back, when there had been no count anywhere in the house to hold it against, and it was not a change made again. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The inch kept a rhythm nobody in the house had counted and was worth nothing even to the person keeping it. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The body humiliated him and moved by an inch in the same motion, and both were true.

The hand came down on the river's next low note. The thread at his mouth was still frozen and unwiped, one stiff leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the salt coat on its peg held a broader man's shape while the seat below it kept a hollow no one sat down to fill. Two hundred and eleven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, the carried fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and eleven days, and the widow's mark hung where it hung with no opening in thirty-one days. Grain's first tend went on beyond the door without a boundary drawn under it and with neither of the district's two month-names corrected, and the boy lay under the roof with the count in him and the fire going on in the room that warmed him and the river going on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
