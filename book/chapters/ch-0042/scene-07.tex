\daymark{179}{Tallow 30, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

The grey came up over \place{the Cross} with river wind under the eaves and hot clay coming down on the same breath from \place{High Kiln}, the two meeting in the square and going each its own way without a word spent on either. It was the last market of the firing month and the third Account-day of Tallow, and nobody under any roof said so over the basket. \person[Corin]{The boy} came up \place{the clay road} under the fold in \person{Sella Vail}'s arm, one hundred and seventy-nine days old and five months and twenty-nine days, the only thing in the square that kept the number, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and there is no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number. The crust lay stiff and leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it; his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water under \place{the north bridge} and came down against the fold. It could go no further.

She carried him at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind and the running water and never to the thing in the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool, which had no ears, and the wind, which had nothing to give back. Eight pigs went over the weighing board before the light was properly in the boards, and potters set bowls out on \place{the pot board} where the dust lay in the hollows and the light went along the rims; a buyer brought a number up out of a fist toward the rail, looked at it, thought better of giving it, and put the fist back in his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere and stood in no account. The market went on over the top of it.

At \place{the moot-post} she stopped. There was a finger's width of shade on the square side of it where no slate lay --- not a hand's breadth, not a span, only a strip the width of a man's finger where the iron kept the light out --- and she set the basket into it the way a woman sets a pot where it will not rock, base first and the weight settled. Then she stepped back half a pace and stood. She gave the basket no instruction about the shade and no reason for the setting and no word of why the place under the post was other than the place a pace to the left where the crowd was already leaning over \place{the reading block}. The instruction went to the wool and the wind. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. The half-pace held. It was the half-pace she kept from every open slate in every market of his life, and this strip had no slate lying in it, which might have been the reason for it and might not, and the boy did not make the two stand up together and call themselves a cause. Three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and seventy-nine days taking them down again one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

The cold iron of the post came through the wool at his cheek. He knew its order from other Account-days: iron first, then the wool going damp where his breath settled, then the small warmth of his own skin in the place the fold did not cover. The crowd leaned over the block above him in sections, those come late pressing behind those who had stood since the pigs, and a man with a wet pot set it down badly and did not hear what the pot said, and nobody made a lesson of the sound. The danger of the month had gone up the road with the kiln-masters and stood here only as the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} on a wrong wind off High Kiln, a false sweetness laid on the tongue before the throat knew to distrust it, entered in nothing and paid by nobody in the square. He filed it where a danger goes and not among the three near-full answers standing on their own tray advanced and not paid, and not among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} either, which stood beside and never among. A thing out of season was a fault in the handling and not a fourth answer to anything.

\scenebreak

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road a little after the pigs, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth. Under the hot clay he carried the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned to his sleeve. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read, low into the top of the leaning crowd, “One in twelve, as the notice says.”

The crowd took it without a challenge, the way a crowd takes a thing it has already heard twice in a month and has no hand to carry away. “The levy is called,” he said. “It is not called by me.” To a man who brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the firing had cost now the last tend was in, he said, “A number goes into a slate, not a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” Your house, never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand goes out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field exactly as it had been and the figure where he had set it, and the boy, who had spent a life in rooms where that gesture was ordinary, kept the twice-wiped thumb and the finger over a figure on a tray of their own and did not lay them across any other keeping.

The surgeon's register read the twice-wiped thumb as a hand cleaning the field before the instrument goes in: not cruelty, not even carelessness, only the order of a man who wants the slate clean when he sets his name on it. It read the \emph{your house} as the grammar that takes the speaker out of a room and leaves the account standing in his place, and it read the printed figure held apart from the paid figure as a chart that says one thing and a room that did another, with a steady hand and a signature at the bottom to make the two agree. The reading was exact and worth nothing at two paces. He was the only adult in the square and he had the hands of a child, and the figure went into the air over the top of the basket and nobody in the square could have told him what it counted.

Berr went back toward the pigs by the bell and then off up the clay road toward High Kiln with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The printed figure stood unchallenged on the post; the figure read aloud was still not the figure paid. And the figure that went into the slate did not know the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; that absence went on being gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, carried here under the shade of a post and named to nobody --- not to the crowd, not to the basket, not to the man who read the figure above it.

The bell went four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell and with nobody crossing himself at it. It went on after a church bell would have done, as if the man ringing it had counted the same hole in the strokes that the kiln kept behind the wall and the boards kept overhead, and had gone on ringing into it rather than fill it. The heard bell stood beside the told bell of the Long Night, four, four and a gap, and the two did not become one. The river kept its note of water over water under the bridge, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; at home, under the broken pot, the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-nine days. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} of \place{Slate Row} came through before noon going up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve, her hand to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd. There was the small flat correction of the strap, the turn of the wrist bringing the edge round, the settling of the second shorter strap let out for the long road; her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. The thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was. To a woman near the pigs who asked what she carried she said, “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the woman who wanted more she said, “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” and went on with the road's pace, answering the question that had been put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology. No figure came off the slate. She was a runner and not a reader, and the order of her reaching --- hand to the record before the eye to the person --- went to a place of its own beside the other record-first hands, the eighth such hand filed in the district, advanced and not paid and joined to no other keeping. Two records going inward are two records going inward; a shape is not a cause.

Below the bridge at \place{the out-of-line landing}, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; his slate lay against his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, entering the figure before he looked at the man who had given it. Nobody stepped around him and nobody had taken him off; the market sold its pots and eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. A girl with a creel took the landing sideways, one hand out to the rail, and went on selling without looking at her feet, and nobody made a fall of it. The way down kept four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line, walked over carefully by a whole market, and the boy kept the clerk's order of reaching on its own place and did not lay it across Hessa's or Berr's or the assessor's hand at the rail, for two records going inward are two records going inward and a row of such hands will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer.

Across the square at the pot board a silent woman stood looking at pots with the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip, clay under her nails in the small crescents and a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long enough to leave its mark. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, and went off with the light. She was given no dialogue and no name; the green was a colour joined to no ear, the sixth such colour held on a place of its own and not laid across the green at Berr's mouth or any other mouth in the square. At the market's edge the foundling was not seen. He was a shape with no sale, his sixth glimpse unspent, no loaf passing, no slate opened within reach of the basket and no secret sold to any man with a waiting hand. The boy kept the colour where the colour was and the shape where the shape was, and no finger of his drew a line between them and the absence carried under the post.

Sella kept the half-pace from every open slate through the forenoon and into the afternoon, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and the running water and never to the basket. Nothing in the square was joined, named, opened, called or paid. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a market is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The box had gone up the clay road locked; the cords and the knot-string stayed where they were carried, where no Court thumb would read them; the clerk stayed set for his uncalled measure; the sixth green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape.

One hundred and seventy-nine stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the square and the least use in it. The payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and seventy-nine days. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and nobody spoke one; he had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water under the bridge and came down against the fold.

The afternoon light went along the rims of the pots on the board and entered none of them. The box went up the road locked and the bell went on going four, four and a gap over a crowd that did not cross itself, and Sella Vail stood half a pace off in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay, with the basket set down in it and no word given to the basket about why.
