\daymark{174}{Tallow 25, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}

Before first grey they came up \place{the Clay Yard} with their collars high, three or four of them, and went low through the finger's-width door of \place{the back room}. They had no names when they came and the house asked for none when they went in. The door kept its usual slit, a finger's width and no more, and the cold came out of it in a thread and went nowhere that anybody could enter. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} past it at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge; she turned no head and gave the house no name for the men and did not say the room was cold. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and to the road under her feet. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” None went to the basket. None went to the men, who under this roof were a passage of coats through a door and nothing a slate need know.

The two tellings stood where they had stood. A man come downriver from Greybank had told one at this well with a date in it, eighteen leagues east: \lore[the closed counting room]{a counting room shut in the last war} with \lore{the damper} closed, and the men inside dead of the breath rather than of the war's hand. A second man, come downriver on another morning, had told it again in the same place with the same date and the same ending, and the yard had taken both as history, the way a floor takes a spilled bucket that somebody will wipe on the morrow. The boy had kept the two tellings side by side and not touching: breath through the nose in the one, breath caught in the throat in the other, both saying not the war's hand. No third telling had come to stand with them. Sella, who had lost a husband to that danger, had told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} on both mornings and had never once described that room in the words she used for her own. He had learned early that a man can hear a thing eighteen leagues east and keep it eighteen leagues east without lying, and that a house can keep its breath in fours and a gap and call no likeness between the two.

She set him down at the edge of the light at \place{the drying racks}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, and took her hands off him without looking into the fold. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and the empty place beside it had no name and had never needed one. The thread at his mouth had frozen again in the dry cold and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further.

\scenebreak

After the men had gone in and the door had shut itself to a finger's width again, \person{Jorra Vail} came through from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened and closed. It was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Slip dried pale and flaked and could be brushed off on a thigh; this dust went in, the way a fine grit goes into the grain of a hand and stays there through a morning of washing. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray in the private file and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The two states were two states of one man's morning, and a question put to either would have made them one thing with a cause under it. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard went around them and nobody went in, and that place kept its own shape, entered by none.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anybody. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” He had said it so often that the saying had worn smooth as the seat worn hollow on the left by twenty years of another man's leaning, and the wheel went on under it without reporting whose hands were on it. To a pot on the bench he said, “You'll do,” in the flat voice he kept for pots, with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. Jorra knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands. The boy had learned to read that knowing the way he read a pulse in a neck: it stood on the near-full tray, beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. He did not give it to the air or to the yard. A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind stopped at the bench with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} riding up from the terrace ahead of him, and listened to the pot turning, and said, “That one's singing before its time. A pot that sings early generally sings itself out early.” He took Jorra's silence for agreement, as a man will take a silence when he has come up a hill to say a thing and wants to go down again having said it, and went off up the yard with his saying. No pot was lost in the yard and no lesson was made of the morning. The knowing stayed where it had been put, a thing a man knew and had no hand to hand to anybody who would have known what to do with it.

\scenebreak

Sella's voice went low at the hearth, where the day's instruction had been given to the fire and the end of it was still in her mouth. “The fire's low. It wants --- ” She did not finish. Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper and waited there for the sentence, ahead of his hand and ahead of the word. The iron took the warmth out of the fingertip and kept it, giving a little back as it warmed in the reverse of the core. He held it. This time he held it while she finished. “--- feeding,” she said, and gave the end of the instruction to the pot rather than to her son. Only then did he move the tongue of iron a hair's breadth and leave it standing. The household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: Sella's hand finished its half-circle with the broken pot, Tarn's broom found its line, the wheel went on turning. Nobody had ordered the straightening and nobody had observed it. Nobody spoke of the waiting, and no payer was named to the fraction.

The boy read the iron once more as one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He filed it where he had filed it before, an object joined to no kiln, exact and worth nothing at two paces. What had altered was not the iron but the holding: a hand kept at the cold until a sentence had been allowed to finish, the keeping remembered in the holding and not in any word. It was the same holding he had watched on other mornings, and its coming round again did not make it a change twice over or a third. A thing repeated is a thing kept. He had spent every day of his life taking likenesses down one at a time so that they would not stand up and call themselves a truth, and he took this one down now without a word, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before. He came up over \place{the flagstones} with his chest tight from the dark and grey Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, so that no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was under the roof and gave it back. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at \place{the kindling} where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the very lip of the sill where it could not, the small knife laid back at the very edge with \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it. He said nothing of any of it. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch the stones had kept since Seed 22 and went on past it without the half-check, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector and did not once turn along it. No collector came. He did not look toward the shed and Jorra did not look toward the flagstones, and neither man named the other's silence.

Three held changes stood on three places and stayed three. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left, held and never spoken, no payer named to it and no slate entered; it had been held again on later mornings and the holding was not a second change. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own and not upon the first, the reason standing between knowing a board by heart and being told not to look, and no finger of Sella's had gone beneath the board this morning. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man's broom checked half a beat behind it on the flagstones and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two, a place in a yard and not in a wall or under a board. Today the broom had gone on past the bright patch without checking, so the third place was not repeated either. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named to any; he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to the three.

\scenebreak

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the rope on the rim was wet and stiff in the cold, and the \place{trough} stood bare and dark with the water in it called weather by those who did not taste it. A woman come up from \place{the worked strips} with a pot just out of the tubs asked what the kilns would want now the last tend was in, shaping the question toward a figure the way a hand shapes a fist and then opens it. Sella glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze beside the wall and did not touch it. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.” She gave the woman the weather and the river and the pot between them, never a figure, and the question went away level so that nothing slopped and nothing was priced and nothing was refused. The yard went around the clean dark line of the old Ash and the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, and around the space beneath the rack and the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing and she did not touch it, because a well and a morning were not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and would not be invented now to stand up as a seventh. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-four days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others.

He was one hundred and seventy-four days old, and nobody under any roof said so. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The crust lay stiff two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come; he could reach neither, and there was nobody to tell him what either counted. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The river kept its fifth low thing. The fire missed its fifth under the floor. One hundred and seventy-four stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody, while the payer went on without a name.
