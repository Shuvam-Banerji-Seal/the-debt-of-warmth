\daymark{178}{Tallow 29, Y.H. 813 (Ninthday)}

Hot dry clay lay over \place{the Clay Yard} before the light came, and under the clay the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years; it was the yard giving up its water to the dry cold, and it was the ninth morning of the last tend. Under the smell came a sound that was not a bell. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, and there was a gap where the fifth breath should have been, a hole in a sentence that everybody heard and nobody filled. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in \place{Low Kiln}, one hundred and seventy-eight days old and five months and twenty-eight days, and nobody under any roof said so, because there was no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number.

The fold was at his ear and under his chin, the wool gone damp where his breath had settled and frozen again in the dry heat. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the last of the heat, with an empty place beside it that had no name, had never needed one, and would not call a third to keep it company. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and there was no instruction for that sound either. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further.

The kilns were brought to their last heat of the month, and the danger had an order and no name and could not be carried into a chart. Dust lay ground small as meal on the sill and under the racks and in the white of a knuckle when a fist opened; on a wrong wind the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up from \place{High Kiln} and lay on the tongue before the throat knew to distrust it. A wet pot set down wrong at \place{the kiln mouth} gave its high thin note before its time, and the boy filed the sound where a danger goes, not among the three near-full answers standing on their own tray advanced and not paid, and not among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} either, which stood beside and never among. A note out of season was a fault in the handling and not a fourth answer to anything. A potter crouched at the mouth set a cup down and told it it would do, and the cup stood where it was set and was not lost; no lesson was made of it, no room was changed, and nothing in the yard was entered.

At \place{the drying racks} the yard had opened its day around the things it would not enter. At the corner where the ridge lay, the clean dark line of the old \lore{Ash} was broken by a new finer hairline of grey that the district called \lore{the thaw}, and the two lay side by side with nobody's broom near either. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody went in, and in the returning line there was the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and the yard went around it with a look and a wide step and no number. A woman come up from \place{the worked strips} with dry slip dust white in the creases of her knuckles reached past the bare board with her hand before her eyes found the basket, and went off with her pot without a word spent on what she had reached for.

\person{Sella Vail} carried past at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and gave the instruction to the wool and the wind and the running water and never to the thing in the basket. Under \place{the loaf shelf}, where the board's underside became a roof over the basket, she moved her lips along her own row of loaves standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze beside the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it. The shelf's two gaps stayed on the shelf; the one crust in the fold kept its empty place under the roof; the shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring on a tray of its own; the field's empty place stayed where the yard went around it. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line between them, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing. Her finger did not go beneath the board, and the charcoal waited under the front edge for a hand that was not hers to read and a day that had not come.

\scenebreak

Before first grey three or four men had come up \place{the clay road} with their collars high and gone low-voiced into \place{the back room}, which kept its finger's width of open. She carried past at the half-pace, turned no head, gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold, and the boy did not count men who had been given no name. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third, and neither of them produced a room.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone, “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” and to a pot on the wheel he said, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and held the knowing back from a kiln-master come up from High Kiln on the wrong wind, who said a pot singing before its time generally sang itself out early. The knowing went to the near-full tray, beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid; no pot was lost in the yard and no lesson was made of the saying.

Then, as Sella's voice went low and unfinished at \place{the hearth}, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and waited. “The fire's low. It wants---” she said, and did not finish, and the finger stood in the iron until she said, “feeding,” and gave the end of the instruction to the pot. Only then did he move the tongue a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the iron. Nobody spoke of the waiting, and no payer was named to it. The boy kept the fraction on the place where it had been put, a change held and unspoken, not repeated here and not made a pair with anything under a wall or a board.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before. He came up over \place{the flagstones} with his chest tight from the dark and grey Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, so that no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was under the roof and gave it back. “Yes, house,” he said to a woman who wanted the thaw early and again to a man who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late in the private part of his head because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and did not once turn along it. No collector came.

At \place{the kindling} six were counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the very lip of the sill where it could not, the small knife back at the very edge with \person[Garl Vail]'s unforged collector sentence beside it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came up into his teeth and went no further; he drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, and the loan that could hear was not called. The Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of each reach, with its small interval standing where a breath should have gone in and had not. Nine stoppings stood where they had stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned.

The surgeon's register read the held breath as a clamp left too long on a vessel that would not bear the taking off, and the near-saying stopped at the word as a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin showing: exact, and worth nothing at two paces. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left, held and never spoken; the beat that had stayed under the loaf shelf on Day 166 stood on a place of its own; the half-check that had moved the Tallow 22 stroke on the flagstones stood on a third place beside them. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named to any; he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to the three. The bright patch was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either.

\scenebreak

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-eight days. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its gap; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Above the bed the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others; he had spent a hundred and seventy-eight days taking them down again one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up the clay road, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair of the evening without a word spent on either, as they had passed every evening of his life. The instruction went to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. The arm that had carried him a hundred and seventy-eight mornings kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost. She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, wide around \place{the trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well} whose water was called weather, and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; and there was one more turn, the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and she gave it the same wide berth and the same look that asked for nothing and found nothing. She gave none of the turns to the basket.

Under the Vail roof she set him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was not wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, had never told anybody, and would not have called kindness now if a slate had been held under it and a clean hand set to the figure.

“It was the thaw,” she told the carried fire, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and the intake came and the hold came and the letting go came and nothing opened. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so that the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a shelf and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms do not touch.

The work had become ordinary on its last day and the danger stayed gathered without an edge being paid. Nobody under the roof spoke a date over the basket or drew a boundary around the month; the kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with its gap, the fire missed its fifth under the floor, and the boy was one hundred and seventy-eight days old and five months and twenty-eight days with nobody saying so. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. One hundred and seventy-eight stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and seventy-eight days. The pot rim stood out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand while the kiln breathed in fours behind the wall and the fire missed its fifth under the floor, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
