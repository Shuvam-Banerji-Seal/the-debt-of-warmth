\daymark{175}{Tallow 26, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

Midday stood in \place{the Clay Yard} with the heat held in the clay and nothing to show for it but the dry smell and the lifting of the oiled paper at the windows of \place{the Vail kiln-house}. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and gave the instruction to the wool and the running water and the wind that came up off the river. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” None of it went to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about what goes on inside one. The crust lay stiff and leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen again and nobody had wiped it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} the rope on the rim was wet and stiff in the cold even at this hour, and the \place{trough} stood bare and dark with the water in it called weather by those who did not taste it. A neighbour come up from \place{the worked strips} with a pot just out of the tubs held it at arm's length so that the water should not slop on her coat, and asked what a firing costs set down in the Vail house. The question was shaped like a fist and opened at the end, as such questions do; the woman stood with wet sleeves folded across her belly waiting for a figure the way a person waits at a counter for a thing that has been promised.

Sella's eye went once, and only once, to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze beside the wall. Her hand started toward it, shut half over nothing, and came away. She did not touch the jar and did not say what was in it or what it had cost or what a number set down in a house might be thought to mean. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

The question went away level. Nothing slopped, nothing was priced, nothing was refused. The woman carried it off in the arm that carried the pot, and the answer went with her in the manner of an answer given as a direction: it arrived nowhere and left no entry, and was nevertheless an answer, exact and not spendable. The boy watched the going-away of it from the low angle of the basket and filed the shape beside the other answers that had had their questions taken out of them, keeping it from standing up among them into a cause, for a cost asked in one country and answered in another is still a cost, and a direction is the only change a house will make to a figure before it hands the figure back.

A second neighbour, come up with a bucket that had a skin of water on it, called the morning a cold. A third, who had been raking at the kiln mouth and had dry dust in the whites of her knuckles, said there was a wet one under it. They stood a moment, each with her own weather in her mouth, the one wanting the frost to hold and the other wanting \lore{the thaw}, and the argument ended where such arguments end, in the weather itself, with nobody owning either day and nobody able to put a figure to either. The thaw was a hairline of finer grey beside the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and the yard had gone around both for a hundred and seventy-five mornings without sweeping either or naming either as a number.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the house had given it its instruction. He did not look toward the basket and the boy did not look toward him; the two silences under this roof kept their old shape and nobody put a word between them, as nobody had for a hundred and seventy-five days. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and did not once turn along it. No collector came. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at \place{the kindling} where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, the small knife laid back at the very edge with the unforged collector sentence beside it. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He said nothing of any of it, and the boy entered nothing on his account. An interval kept is an interval and not a payment; a stopped sentence is not a sentence spoken; a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

\scenebreak

At \place{the drying racks} the women went around the clean dark line where the old Ash ridge turned and the new finer grey called the thaw, and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number. Dry slip dust lay in the shadows under the slats, fine as meal, and came up pale in the creases of a fist when a woman opened it. \lore{Lead glaze} had left its sweetish edge on a wrong wind off \place{High Kiln} and gone; the smell went and came and was never entered in anything a slate could read. A pot set down wrong would sing its high thin note before its time. The boy filed that sound, when it came, as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer, the way he had filed it on other mornings, keeping the hearing tray clean of a thing that wanted only to be feared.

He watched the turns the way another man reads a face: not by what it says but by what it goes around. A space entered by nobody is a space with a shape, and a shape is a thing a man can keep without having a word for it; he had kept this one for a hundred and seventy-five days without letting it stand up and call itself a cause. Three likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent his whole life taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

Under the Vail roof the afternoon came into the kiln-house along the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof over the basket. Sella set him down with her back to him and did not look into the fold. She moved her lips along the loaf row, counting what the house kept in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody called a number. A rack woman come up from the strips with a wet pot said two bare boards and a child's appetite were the same hunger in the end, putting the shelf's old gap and the empty place at his ear in one breath as if a man might put two fingers into one wound and call it one. Sella heard the saying and gave nobody a figure. She gave the woman the weather and the river and the pot between them, and carried the unanswered question away level so that nothing slopped. Two sayings were two sayings and not one absence.

The shelf stood with its two unnamed gaps: the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6. The one stiff brown-edged crust in the fold kept its empty place beside it and had no name and no third. The shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring on a tray of its own, and the field's empty place was where the yard had gone around it. Four absences stood in four rooms and on four trays and none lay across another. No third gap came into the fold, no fourth likeness stood up and made itself a cause, and no finger of his drew the line from one to the next, because a better-lit room had taught him what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer. He had spent a hundred and seventy-five days keeping the trays apart with nobody to help and nobody to see, and the keeping was the whole of the discipline and the discipline was older than any word in this country.

From the low angle the charcoal marks waited under the shelf's front edge in clusters pressed hard or faded nearly into the grain. Sella did not reach beneath the board this day. Her finger stayed where it was and went to the pot and the fire instead, and the beat that had stayed under the board on the morning of Day 166 stood where it had been left, held and unspoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. The boy did not read the marks, did not count them, did not copy them. They waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come, as they had waited since the first Account-day of the month and would go on waiting; an inventory that is not a key is still an inventory, and a list that is not a hand is still exact, and exactness at two paces buys a man nothing he can carry into a room with a signature at the bottom.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without a word spent on either. The carrying arm kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost; it had carried him a hundred and seventy-five mornings and would carry him on the morrow and gave nothing away about any of them. The light went off the clay in sections, first the ruts and then the hard high places, and the smell of the tallow smoke went before her along the road while the river came up behind with the wet note it had kept all day and would keep after they were under the roof.

She went wide around the shallow pan's gone place against the wall, the bare dark \place{trough} where the water was called weather, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and did not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number. She gave none of the turns to the basket and no explanation of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. A turn made around a place is a turn made around a place, and a woman who keeps a house does not owe an account of her elbows to the thing she carries in them. The boy watched the turns without reading them as directions meant for him; they were the house's arithmetic performed in the feet of a woman who would not have called it arithmetic at all, and he had learned to leave such figures where they stood because a figure once stood up wants a name and a payer, and no payer stood to any of these.

Under the Vail roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, and took her hands off him without once looking at his face. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and had never told anybody. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would say \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so that the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. A shelf and a well and a road and a bed are not openings either. The two rooms do not touch. The crust lay two inches from his ear in the room that did not touch the other one, and the charcoal waited under the board in that other room, and neither thing was carried across to make the row come out even.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented now to stand up as a seventh. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-five days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, and neither was swept at this hour and neither had an instruction. Cold arrived cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust and the breast and the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

He was one hundred and seventy-five days old, and nobody under any roof said so. The count stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool while the fire missed its fifth under the floor and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
