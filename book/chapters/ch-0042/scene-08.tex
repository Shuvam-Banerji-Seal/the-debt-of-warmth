At the going of the light she came back for it.

The shade had gone a finger up the post while she stood, and she put her hand to the handle on the side the iron had not cooled, lifted base first and level the way a woman lifts a filled pot from a board, and carried it out of the finger's width of shade without a slop and without a word. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind coming up under \place{the north bridge}. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she said to the wool, which had no ears, and to the wind, which had nothing to give back, and nothing was said to the basket about the box that had gone up \place{the clay road} locked, or the figure that had gone into the air over the top of it, or why the strip under the post had been other than the place a pace to the left where the crowd had leaned over \place{the reading block}. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot carried level has not been told anything.

The woodsmoke went before her down the clay road in long low bars out of \place{Low Kiln}; the river wind came up behind her, damp, with the taste of water over water, and the two met on the stair of the evening and passed as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and the arm that had carried him a hundred and seventy-nine mornings kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost. It was a record that arm kept, not an entry: the height of a handle, the give of a shoulder, the turn of a wrist at a step's end, nothing written and nothing owed. His head went where it wanted inside the fold and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about that sound.

At the edge of the yard she went around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, looking for no scrape and finding no word; around \place{trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, bare and dark, whose water was called weather by those who did not taste it; and wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody. There was one turn more, the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and she went around it too with a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. She did not call any of the turns by a name. The yard kept its account the way it kept everything, in loaves pushed along a shelf, in glances at a clay jar standing cold under its glaze, in a space at a row's end nobody called a figure; the number under the wool had no place among them and was not set down.

Behind her the bell spent its last strokes and stopped, four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody in the yard crossed himself at the stopping. The heard bell kept its place beside the told bell and the two did not become one. The box had gone up the road locked with the true subtraction inside, and the man who locked it believed he had done a kindness; the figure read over the basket was still not the figure that would be paid, and the figure that went into the slate did not know the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. None of this went into the basket. The carrying arm kept the weight and gave no account of the cost, and the two went down the road together, the arm and the kiln breathing behind the wall in fours with its gap, keeping time without either keeping the other's account.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat of the last tend, with the empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was not wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, had never told anybody, and would not have called it kindness if a slate had been held under it and a clean hand set to the figure.

“It was \lore{the thaw},” she told \lore{the carried fire}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and the intake came and the hold came and the letting go came and nothing opened. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so that the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms do not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file again, the way a man lays his instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The laying was the discipline and the discipline was older than any word in this country, older than the house and older than the woman asleep in the room that did not touch this one. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and he laid them out in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them, and the order of the laying was the whole of it.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{Vail wheel-shed} seen through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}. The sixth was the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, and it went where a thing out of a person must go, filed beside the five and never among them, for a sound out of a person is not a sound out of a vessel and a man who mixes the two will find a flaw where there is only a person. No seventh came in the day and none was invented to fill the empty place. An empty place kept empty is a finding; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that had answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, its note coming early and going on; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a kiln-master come up on the wrong wind --- the full note a Vail pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, held and not spoken and not spent. Each had come near enough to be entered and had not been entered, and that difference mattered more than the sound. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the stroke that went on over the bright patch without the check, and his own held breath from a market morning, each lying where it had been laid and not one risen up between the others.

On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any. A tenth had not stood up out of the nine. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled; and the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. Beside them lay \lore{the damper}, one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Three held changes stood on three places. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 --- the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish --- lay where it had been left, held and never spoken, no payer named to it and no slate entered; the repetitions had not made it a change twice over. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own, a hand that goes under without looking knowing the board by heart or having been told not to look, the reason standing between the two and never settled; no finger had gone beneath the board this day, so the beat was not repeated. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man's broom checked half a beat behind it on \place{the flagstones} and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two --- three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named. The bright patch was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either. He would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three.

The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third, and the told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself and nobody asking why the bell went longer than a church bell. Neither telling had produced a room. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent, no loaf passing near the basket and no slate opened within reach of it; a colour is a colour and a shape is a shape, and a man who lays one across the other has made a cause where there were only two things seen.

In the other room the one stiff brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third. The loaf shelf kept two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under the front edge for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. The shallow pan's gone place kept its round ring on a tray of its own and the field's empty place stayed where the yard had gone around it; neither was laid across the shelf's two gaps or the fold's crust and its gap, and the sixth green was not laid across any of them. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and had spent a hundred and seventy-nine days taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-nine days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. \lore{Ash} lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it, neither swept at this hour and neither with an instruction. Cold arrived cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody would perform.

He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. The body wanted small and immediate things, the crust and the breast and the warm joint of a hand; the register wanted the figure that would set them in their places, and neither had a mouth under this roof. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The figure read was not the figure paid; the box had gone up the clay road locked; the cords and the knot-string and the second twist stayed where they were carried, where no Court thumb would read them; the clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called; the sixth green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape. One hundred and seventy-nine stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The firing month went out without a boundary drawn and the count went forward into Ember with the fire's fifth still missing, the ninth stopping still nine with no tenth, the Seed 16 breath still held, the three changes still on their three places and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
