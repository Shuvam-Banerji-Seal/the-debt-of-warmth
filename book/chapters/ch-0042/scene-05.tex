\daymark{177}{Tallow 28, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday)}

The going of the light came down the \place{clay road} in two smells that met and passed one another on the stair of the evening and did not take each other's colour. Woodsmoke went before \person{Sella Vail} in long low bars from the chimneys of \place{Low Kiln}; the river wind came up behind her, damp, carrying the taste of water over water, and the two crossed where the ruts turned and went on as they had come. She carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge, and the instruction went to the wool and the wind and the running water and never to the thing carried in the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool, which had no ears, and the wind, which had nothing to give back. The arm that had carried him a hundred and seventy-seven mornings kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost, the way an arm keeps a record it cannot put into words and does not try.

She went wide around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, looking for no scrape and finding no word; wide around the bare dark \place{trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, whose water was called weather by those who did not taste it; and wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody. There was one more turn, the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and she gave it the same wide berth and the same look that asked for nothing and found nothing, and gave none of the turns to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot carried level is a pot that has not been told anything. She said nothing of the turns. Behind her the yard kept its days the way it kept everything, in loaves pushed along a shelf, glances at a clay jar standing cold under its glaze, and a space at the row's end that nobody called a figure; the number he carried was not one of them and had no place to be set down. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap, and the two counts went on at their own pace, the carrying arm and the kiln, keeping time together without either keeping the other's account.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat of the last tend, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was not wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, had never told anybody, and would not have called kindness now if a slate had been held under it and a clean hand set to the figure.

“It was \lore{the thaw},” she told \lore{the carried fire}, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and the intake came and the hold came and the letting go came and nothing opened. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so that the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms do not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. The order of the laying was the whole of the discipline, and the discipline was older than any word in this country, older than the house, older than the woman asleep in the room that did not touch this one. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and he laid them out anyway in the dark, with a child's hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed}, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}. The sixth was the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, and it went where a thing from a person must go, filed beside the five and never among them, because a sound out of a person is not a sound out of a vessel and a man who mixes the two will find a flaw where there is only a person. No seventh came in the day and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that had answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, its note coming early and going on; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept from a kiln-master come up on the wrong wind --- the full note a Vail pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, held back and not spoken and not spent. Each had come near enough to be entered and had not been entered, which is the whole difference between a near-full answer and a full one, and the difference mattered more than the sound. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke that went on over the bright patch on the stones, and his own held breath from a market morning, all three lying where they had been laid and not one of them risen up to stand between the others.

On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. A tenth had not stood up out of the nine. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled; and the breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. \lore{The damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Three held changes stood on three places. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 --- the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish --- lay where it had been left, held and never spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered; the repetitions of it had not made it a change twice over. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own, a hand that goes under without looking knowing the board by heart or having been told not to look, the reason standing between the two and never settled; no finger had gone beneath the board on this day, so the beat was not repeated. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man's broom checked half a beat behind it on \place{the flagstones} and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two --- three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named. The shallow bright patch on the stones was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either. He would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three.

The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third, and the told bell stood beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself and nobody asking why the bell went longer than a church bell. Neither telling had produced a room. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear, and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent, no loaf passing near the basket and no slate opened within reach of it; a colour is a colour and a shape is a shape, and a man who lays one across the other has made a cause where there were only two things seen.

No third gap came, and no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line between the shallow pan's gone ring on its own tray, the shelf's two unnamed gaps, the fold's one crust and its empty place, and the field's empty place where the yard went around. He had learned early that three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and had spent a hundred and seventy-seven days taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see. The two rooms did not touch. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others. \lore{Ash} lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line, and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept at this hour and neither with an instruction.

Cold arrived cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its instruction; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. The body wanted small and immediate things, the crust and the breast and the warm joint of a hand; the register wanted the figure that would set them in their places, and neither had a mouth in this house. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. Two paces of air and a child's body were the whole of his reach. He could have named the double bite of the iron and could not have reached it, and there was nobody under this roof to whom the naming would have meant anything.

One hundred and seventy-seven stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The payer under the floor had had no name for a hundred and seventy-seven days. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the file lay in its hollows the way it had been laid, and the charcoal waited beneath the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
