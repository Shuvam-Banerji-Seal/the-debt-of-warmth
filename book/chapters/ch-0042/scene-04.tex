\daymark{176}{Tallow 27, Y.H. 813 (Seventhday)}

The last tend stood at its heat and \place{the Clay Yard} smelled of a thing held between two hands that would not stay held. Hot clay gave up its water to the dry cold and went on giving it up, and the smell came down off the kilns in sections, the sweet dry breath first and the sour one under it, where the edge of \lore{lead glaze} came riding the wrong wind off \place{High Kiln} and went where it liked, entered in nothing. \person[Corin]{the boy} came down into the yard at the half-pace in the basket under the fold, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge; the instruction went to the wool and the wind and the running water and never to the thing carried in the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” \person{Sella Vail} told the wool, which had no ears, and the wind, which had nothing to give back. Her burn closed on the basket handle and opened again, the little door shutting on nothing when there was nothing in it to hold.

The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap. It had breathed so through the firing month and would breathe so until the month was out, and the gap was kept as carefully as the breaths that came; a man who counted would have said there was a hole in the order of it, but the yard had no word for the hole and made no lesson of it. To the boy the whole firing was a theatre he could not enter: heat kept and counted behind a screen, passed through a gate of two bites, paid for by a man whose name was not spoken in the yard. He had been given a seat at the front of it and no way to reach the stage. Two paces of air and a child's body were the whole of his reach.

The danger of the month had an order and no name. Dust lay ground small as meal on the sill and stood white in the creases of a fist when a woman opened it; a pot set down wrong would give its high thin note before its time; the sweetish edge of glaze came and went on the wrong wind; and behind the wall the kiln kept its four and its gap. None of it could be carried into a chart. An order without a name is a thing a man may keep but cannot enter, and he kept it as he kept the rest, on a place of its own. When a rack woman set a pot down too quickly and it gave its thin early sound, he filed it as a danger of the month and not as a fourth near-full answer. No pot was lost and no room was changed.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the house had given the broom its instruction. The instruction went to the broom and to \place{the flagstones} and never into the man, and he took up the tool as a man takes up a thing already named, which is a different matter from being told. He did not look toward the basket and the boy did not look toward him; the two silences under this roof kept the shape they had kept for a hundred and seventy-six days and nobody put a word between them, as nobody had on the days before.

At \place{the kindling} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. Six were counted in a thumb-mark worn nearly flat where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the lip where the house could not, held back and not spent and not yet named. Beside it lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence --- the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford --- neither the sentence entered nor the debt it carried paid. The warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold in a clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and he had long ago stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt that held him, which was not forgiving it. A man who cannot bring a kindness and an account into the same room has one place left to put the kindness, and he had put it there and shut the pot on it. He had not looked at the stone in a year and the boy had never seen it at all, and it went on being a warmth given by one hand to another and entered nowhere, like the answer that went away level from the well.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it. He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to what light there was and gave it back, as he had given it back on other mornings, holding light and returning light with no gain to either. His chest had been tight since the dark. The breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16 was still unspent, and there was a small interval at the top of each reach where it should have gone in and had not, as neat a gap as the kiln's own.

At \place[the yard well]{the well} a neighbour come up from the tubs with water on her sleeves said \lore{the thaw} would come early, and a man raking at a kiln mouth said there was a wet one under it and it would come late. Tarn said \emph{Yes, house} to the one and \emph{Yes, house} to the other, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and would stand there when the kilns were cold. The \place{trough} stood bare and dark beside him and the water in it was called weather by those who did not taste it. His stroke went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came, and he did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name.

\scenebreak

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up into his teeth and went no further. It had come to the word before and stopped there, and it came to the word now and stopped, with no figure under it and no name on it. He drew the breath he should have spent on the saying and did not spend it; the small interval stood at the top of the reach and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled. He did not stand a tenth stopping up to join the nine, because the seventh year had not yet been earned. Stand an account up before its hour and you stand up an account with a payer already written in, and no payer stood here. Something waited at the end of the term. It had waited through six years of mornings and it waited now, and he kept his hand off it as a man keeps his hand off a dressing that is not yet due to be lifted.

On Tallow 22 the wheel-keeper's bent finger had stood in the cold iron of \lore{the damper} until a sentence was allowed to finish, and the debt's stroke had checked half a beat behind it on the stones and gone on without a word or a turn of the head. A hand held back from the iron is still a hand that knows where the iron stands; a hand that waits a second time and a third is not making the same change twice over but holding it. The boy had watched the finger stand and the stroke check, and had kept both on places of their own, and would not let the three held changes stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. The fraction at the iron on the morning of Day 161, the beat that had stayed beneath \place{the loaf shelf} on Day 166, the half-check on these stones on Tallow 22 --- three changes on three places, one in a wall, one in a room, one in a yard, and this morning added nothing to them and took nothing from them. He had spent a hundred and seventy-six days taking likenesses down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see, and the day belonged to the debt with hands, in which the held seventh year and the held word gathered weight together without either being spent.

A woman come up from the tubs with a wet pot asked what the last firing would want now the tend was nearly out. The question was shaped to receive a figure and opened at the end, as such questions do; she stood with wet sleeves folded across her belly waiting the way a person waits at a counter for a thing that has been promised. Sella's eye went once, and only once, to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze beside the wall. Her hand started toward it, shut half over nothing, and came away. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.”

The question went away level in the arm that carried it. Nothing slopped, nothing was priced, nothing was refused; the answer arrived nowhere and was nevertheless exact, a direction given in place of a figure, which is the only change a house will make to a number before it hands the number back. The boy watched the going away of it from the low angle and filed the shape beside the other answers whose questions had been taken out of them, keeping it from standing up among them into a cause.

The yard went around its two unswept lines --- the clean dark line where the old ridge had turned and the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw} --- and around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number. Two lines and one absence were things a yard could go around. A figure was a thing it would have had to stop for, and the yard had been going around them for a hundred and seventy-six mornings without once stopping.

\scenebreak

Under the Vail roof the afternoon came in along the low angle where the underside of the loaf shelf becomes a roof over the basket. Sella set him down with her back to him and did not look into the fold. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was not wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. From the low angle the charcoal waited under the shelf's front edge in clusters pressed hard or faded nearly into the grain. Her finger did not go beneath the board this day, so the beat that had stayed under it since Day 166 stood where it had been left, held and unspoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. He did not read the marks or count them or copy them. They waited for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.

At \place{the hearth} she told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so that the grey streak showed pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning and a shelf were not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms do not touch.

The surgeon's register read the held breath in the clay-bound man's chest as a clamp left too long on a vessel that would not bear the taking off --- mercy and mutilation in the same bite, with no word in Grathic that would carry both. It read the sentence stopped at the word as a chart closed over its finding with the neat margin showing, the finding exact and worth nothing once it was carried into a room with a signature at the bottom. It read the whole of the day as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand. He was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it. He could have named the clamp and could not have reached it, and there was nobody in the house to whom the naming would have meant anything. Exactness at two paces buys a man nothing he can carry into the next room.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented now to stand up as a seventh. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-six days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others.

He was one hundred and seventy-six days old, and nobody under any roof said so. The count stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool while the fire missed its fifth under the floor and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. He wanted the crust and the breast and the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
