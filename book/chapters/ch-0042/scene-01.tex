\daymark{173}{Tallow 24, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay} lay under the tallow and the wet wool before the light came, and under the clay the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years; it was the yard giving up its water to the dry cold, and it was the first thing \person[Corin]{the boy} knew of the fourth day of the last tend. Under the smell came a sound that was not a bell. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours, and there was a gap where the fifth breath should have been, the way a sentence carries a hole in it that everybody hears and nobody fills. He lay on \place{the bed} under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} in \place{Low Kiln}, one hundred and seventy-three days old and five months and twenty-three days, and nobody under any roof said so, because there was no instruction under any roof about telling a basket a number.

The fold was at his ear and under his chin, wool gone damp where his breath had settled and frozen again in the dry heat. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery since the planting, with an empty place beside it that had no name, had never needed one, and would not call a third to keep it company. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the night and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and there was no instruction for that sound either. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water going over water beyond the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. It could go no further.

In the dark before first grey \person{Sella Vail} gave the fire its instruction, and the pot's, and the wool's, and the broom's, and the running water's, and never once gave an instruction to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the pot she said, “Don't set that near the door. You'll pay for it in the thaw,” though the pot had not moved and had no door to be near, and to the broom, later, “Round it. Go around.” She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and had said it so many mornings that the words had worn smooth in her mouth like a thumb-mark rubbed into a sill. At \place{the hearth} she told \lore{the carried fire} it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze beside the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it, the way a hand closes on a thing it has decided not to lift. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a morning and a shelf were not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

Under the floor the fire answered her with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, four, four and a gap, the same hole the kiln had left in its breathing behind the wall. Its payer had had no name for a hundred and seventy-three days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and would not be invented to stand up as a seventh. Above the bed the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others; he had spent a hundred and seventy-three days taking them down again one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

At first grey she carried him out under the roof and down the clay road to \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a basket clear of a slate's open edge. The instruction went to the wool and to the wind and to the road under her feet, and never to the thing in the basket, because a basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told where to stand and is not asked what it wants. She kept the half-pace from every open slate at \place{the drying racks} and \place[the yard well]{the well}. The arm that had carried him a hundred and seventy-three mornings kept the weight of him in muscle and gave no account of the cost; he kept the count of her steps without deciding to, as he kept everything that would not leave him alone.

The yard had opened its day around the things it would not enter. At the corner where the ridge lay, the clean dark line of the old \lore{Ash} was broken by a new finer hairline of grey that the district called the thaw, and the two lay side by side with nobody's broom near either. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody went in, and in the returning line there was the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and the yard went around it with a look and a wide step and no number. At the well the \place{trough} stood bare and dark, its water called weather, and the rope on the rim was wet and stiff in the cold. Where a shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up against the wall through Reed there was now only the round ring of its gone place, which the boy kept on a tray of its own and did not lay across anything.

A rack woman come up from \place{the worked strips} with dry slip dust white in the creases of her knuckles reached past the bare board with her hand before her eyes found the basket, and went off with her pot without a word spent on what she had reached for. Two neighbours at the well settled the morning between them, one calling it a cold and the other saying there was a wet one under it, and the argument ended where such arguments end, in weather, with nobody owning either day. Sella carried past them at the half-pace, turned no head, and gave the pot and the running water the rest of what needed saying.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, never before. He came up over \place{the flagstones} with his chest tight from the dark and grey Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, so that no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was under the roof and gave it back. “Yes, house,” he said to a woman who wanted the thaw early and again to a man who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late in the private part of his head because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark at \place{the kindling} where the house could check; the seventh stood at the very lip of the sill where it could not, and the small knife lay back at the very edge of its sill with \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came up into his teeth and went no further. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent at the top of each reach, with its small interval standing where a breath should have gone in and had not, and the loan that could hear was not called.

His stroke went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and went over it again, and did not once turn along it. The bristles dragged and lifted on the stone, and the grey meal of clay dust went up and settled back into the seams between the flags. No collector came. He had brought the half-check of Tallow 22 to the bright patch on the stones and let it go on without stopping, and that place stayed where it was, in a yard, and was not repeated here or made to stand for anything under a wall or a board.

\scenebreak

The month had come into its last tend and the danger had an order and no name and could not be carried into a chart. Dust lay ground small as meal on the sill and under the racks and in the white of a knuckle when a fist opened; on a wrong wind the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up from \place{High Kiln} and lay on the tongue before the throat knew to distrust it. A wet pot set down wrong gave its high thin note before its time, and the boy filed the sound where a danger goes, not among the three near-full answers standing on their own tray advanced and not paid, and not among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} either, which stood beside and never among. A note out of season was a fault in the handling and not a fourth answer to anything. No pot was lost in the yard and no lesson was made.

In the basket he kept nine stoppings where they had stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. A tenth had not stood up out of them before the seventh year was earned, and would not stand up now to make a bill of nine. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 lay where it had been left, held and never spoken, with no payer named to it and no slate entered. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own and not upon the first, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; no finger of his had gone beneath it this morning. And the third movement, come on Tallow 22 when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron and the clay-bound man's broom checked half a beat behind it on the flagstones and went on without a word or a turn of the head, stood on a third place beside the other two. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named to any; he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to the three. The shallow bright patch on the flagstones was a place in a yard; the waiting at the iron was a place in a wall; the beat under the board was a place in a room that did not touch either. He had not repeated one of them this morning and did not make one stand in for another.

He read the firing as a theatre he could not enter. Heat was kept and counted behind a screen and a gate of two bites, and the cost of it was paid somewhere by a man whose name was not spoken under any roof, so that the warmth arriving under the roof at dusk came as an instruction and never as an account. He had a surgeon's eye for the kept and the counted, and the file he held was exact and worthless: an inventory that was not a key and a list that was not a hand. He was the only adult under the roof and the least use in it, with no case to begin and no instrument he could lift. The certainty was not triumph. It lay in its own hollow with the rest and bought him nothing at two paces.

The yard went on around its two unswept lines into the middle of the morning. Tarn's broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and went over it again, and did not once turn along it; no collector came. Behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with its gap; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and seventy-three days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and seventy-three stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof, while the payer under the floor went on without a name and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
