The market thinned without ending. That was the way of it at \place{the Cross}: not a closing but a drawing-off, as heat goes out of a pot set down off the coals onto a cold stone and sits a while in the pot before it goes anywhere. The two hundred who had leaned over the reading block unleaned and went back to the pigs, and the pigs went on being eight, and the wet boards kept the shallow print of the reading where nothing had been written. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in the finger's width of thin shade under the moot-post with the cold iron at his cheek and the wool over his ears, and the market went on over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

The inventory renewed itself without his asking, in the old order, because that order had never lied to him. At home on \place{the bed} one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself with no third come in to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle standing beyond his reach at home. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and nine days old the second was louder; he took the facts in their order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of them, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-nine days; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

The bell went over the square in the turn of the afternoon. It was longer than a bell in a temple yard, and it came from somewhere above the reading block and went out over the pigs and the stalls and the fish-trade shade below the bridge: four strokes, and four, and then the place where the fifth should have stood, and the square went on with what it was doing. Not one hand in two hundred went to a face. The boy held his inch through the stroke that did not come and came down on the sound after it, supplying nothing to fill the hollow. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, a heard thing beside a told thing; it was not folded into the three counts under the roof, not laid across the fire's missing fifth or the boards' thumb-width knot, and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Four was four in the fire, four in the nails, four in the bell, and the gap stood where a fifth should have stood in each of the three places and rose up in none of them. A man who has learned the note a vessel gives when it is set down wrong knows to leave a note where it falls and not make it answer a question nobody put to it; here there was no vessel and no answer, and the square went on with its pigs. He lent the bell no more than an ear it had already earned, and let the silence after it be a silence and not a summons.

\scenebreak

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a woman stood without speaking. The sweetish green of \lore{lead glaze} sat at one corner of her mouth in the crease beside her lip; clay had dried pale under her nails in the small crescents, and a pale line lay across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, and went off with the light. The light went along the rims of the cups while she turned the one, and the shallow print her cup had made stayed where it was after she went. No name was spoken over her and no second look was joined to the first. The boy filed the green as a colour on a place of its own beside the six behind it, with a finger between it and the hearings, joined to no ear, no cause and no romance, and would not let an eighth likeness stand up as a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of it. The pale line on the back of her hand went on its own place and was not laid across another line read at a well or the line on a man carried out of a kiln; the board kept the shallow print where the cup had stood and gave nobody a figure. There was no loaf passed, no slate opened within reach of the basket, no sale and no hearing, and she was gone as quietly as she had come.

At the market's edge a small shape stood in clothes cast off by a bigger boy, worn the way a uniform is worn by a child who was never measured for one: a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been. A green sliver of lead glaze hung on a string at the throat and caught the light on the turn and went dull going back. No name was spoken over this one either, and no second look was joined to the first, and no eighth of the colour or the shape came into the square that afternoon. Somebody set a pot down among the stalls with a small flat sound, and the child's head went toward it half a beat after the knock and stopped in the middle of the turn. The boy's own head had not gone that way; it had gone toward the bell, or toward nothing, and he did not lay the two turnings together. The knock was a knock and the turn was a turn after the knock, and that was the whole of what stood. He read the small shoulders the way a man reads a fracture that has set in the wrong position: not a hurt done now but an old one carried in the set of the thing, and nobody in the square was setting it. He filed the green as a colour and the shape as a shape on places of their own with a finger between them, and forbade the eight likenesses to stand up as a rule, because a rule wants a payer and none stood to either of them. No loaf crossed to the basket, no slate was seen, no figure was entered, and the child went off with the thinning light the way a market goes off a stone.

The square kept the day's great change in it for a little while and then let it go. It had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box while two hundred people leaned over a figure that would not change what it counted, and it had left no figure in the room and no mark on the boards; the green and the shape waited toward later markets with no eighth and no name, unbrought and unbought. The boy made nothing of the waiting and did not let it stand up as a sign.

\person{Sella Vail} came for him at the going of the light. She lifted the basket without a word about what had been read over him and carried it up the clay road at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. Wet wool and woodsmoke went along with her, and the basket rode steady as a pot filled that must not be slopped, and she did not once look down into it, though a woman who has carried a filled pot across a yard knows what slopping costs; the keeping of him was in the not-looking and went unentered like everything else she kept. She said nothing of the figure or the twice-spoken sentence or the crowd that had leaned. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle toward him and a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. Every instruction at that hour went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and none of it went to the basket. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

He heard her grammar as a man hears a language he has not been taught but has learned to parse from the order of the words. Nothing of the square came home with her in it: no lesson, no figure, no mending, no name for the man in the box going up the road. The house kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood, and kept its dead in a coat on a peg and a seat with a hollow nobody filled, and kept this boy by not putting him into any of it. The keeping was maintenance and not a story, and he had learned to tell the difference the way he had learned to tell a sound vessel from a cracked one: by what answered when you tapped it.

In the small hours the file was turned out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments are turned out on a tray, each thing in its own place with room between. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and was not filled to make a row come out even. The heard bell of this afternoon lay in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, not a hearing and not a seventh, its four, four and a gap left where they had fallen and not laid across the fire's missing fifth or the boards' thumb-width knot. Three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note apart on its own place, and no fourth likeness was made a cause. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in her sleeve and no tenth to make a rule. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth of either. The Frost 14 saying stood with its second shaping beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand and a finger between every pair, not a witness, not a confession, not a joining, not a payment and not a reader made of a runner who carried and did not read.

Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second lay locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the clay road, the printed one in twelve unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, read over by a number that did not know what it cost. The stayed frost on the lip of \place{the yard well}, \place{the trough} skinned over and the plate of ice clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet were called weather and entered nowhere, and no fourth likeness between them and any twice-read figure was made a cause, because a cause wants a payer and none stood. The finding that a house keeps by its not-doings stayed on a place of its own, and his own gaps on another with a finger between --- two gaps above the shelf, one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the gone pan's round ring, the field's empty place out on the worked strips, the returning-line place where a hand went in and did not come out; no third gap made a row and no fourth likeness made a cause. Frost 30, the month's third and last Account-day, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

One hundred and fifty-three honest answers stood behind this one on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact and worth nothing at two inches of air; the held interval and the empty interval lay on their own places and neither was spent to make a number come out even.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note and stayed for a small intake. From the file it took the heard bell's gap held beside a counted breath from the room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go of one of the breaths a woman counts at her fire in the next room --- and set them side by side with a finger between, borrowing no house count, supplying no fifth to the kiln, crossing no silence that belonged to another's breathing. The hundred and fifty-fourth answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: the heard bell kept beside the told, and neither made to agree with the figure that had gone twice over the basket. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue.

Nothing of the reading came back under the Vail roof as a lesson, a figure, a mending, a cause or a payment. The two arithmetics were two and not one account, and a finding kept on a place of its own wants no payer put under it. Frost the thirtieth waited nine mornings off and no hand of his reached toward it as a sign; when the third figure came it would go over the covered grain and not know its cost, and he would keep the days exactly while the house kept them in loaves and glances. The hand came down on the hundred and fifty-fourth answer with the heard bell's gap and the counted breath side by side on their own tray, the file open on the towel with every tray parted by a finger's width, one crust in the fold with its empty place unnamed and the fire under the floor answering in fours with its gap, and the river's low note of water over water going quiet after the hand came down. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended.
