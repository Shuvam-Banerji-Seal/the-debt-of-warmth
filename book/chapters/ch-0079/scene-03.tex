\daymark{289}{Frost 20, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

\person[Corin]{The boy} came into his two hundred and eighty-ninth day the way a man comes into a room he has been standing outside in the dark: by degrees, and with no announcement, and with the same cold already in it that he had left there the night before. Nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof told him the number. Two hundred and eighty-eight had gone to two hundred and eighty-nine without a sound, the way a hand goes from one card of a pack to the next and the pack does not notice; six months and one hundred and nine days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Frost 20 had come on its own morning. Yesterday had gone around it with a look and a wide step and no number, as a woman goes around a sleeping animal, and had not brought it in; now it stood here with its market in it and its reading and two hundred people leaning in, and it asked nothing of the boy under the fold except that he be carried to where it was happening.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had ever promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on \place{the bed}, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and nine days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach, and the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and eighty-nine days; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on Frost 10, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note, but a heard bell beside a told bell. He had not heard the bell this morning and would not lend it an ear it had not yet earned, for a bell comes on its own morning the way a month comes, and a man who borrows one to make a row come out even has already put a payer under it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. She gave it its instruction in the low voice she kept for tools, told it the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}; she lifted a corner and turned it once with a progressively lighter touch, put the wool back and gave it no figure, though a figure was already travelling up \place{the clay road} toward a number that would go over it before noon and would not know what it cost. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge while her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps, and neither gap got a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a reading is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. Behind her the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing moved. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle toward him and a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note and stayed for a small intake, and came down again without filing anything, because the intake wanted a place put under it and none stood. This morning the work of the hand was only to be ready; the answer it would give had not yet happened to it. She carried him down at the half-pace from every open slate, in the basket under the fold of wool like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. At the Cross she set the basket under \place{the reading block under the moot-post}, in the finger's width of thin shade where no slate lay, with the cold iron at his cheek and the shade lying on him like a cloth laid over a loaf to keep the dust off, and stood half a pace off through the reading, giving no instruction and no reason to the basket.

\scenebreak

The square was full of people leaning in. Two hundred of them stood shoulder to shoulder over the wet boards where eight pigs had already gone and would go again, and the reading block under the moot-post stood at the centre of them like a stone set out to be leaned upon. \place{High Kiln} stood up the steep street at the north edge of the square, and the road that came down from it was the clay road, and up that road the district's arithmetic had gone twice already and had come back with a figure the crowd could lean over without its ever touching what it counted. The boy lay in the thin shade and felt the cold iron at his cheek and heard the market going on over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road a little before the top of the morning, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath, and the two hundred leaning in did not know they were smelling it. His hands were large and clean; he wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks a stitch he has already set rather than a wound he is about to open. Then he read \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says} low into the two hundred, and the sentence went over the top of the basket without ever going into it.

He sent the figure over the basket twice: once for the moot, where the two hundred stood, and a second time for the households that had come late and had not heard the first. He called the levy for Frost and denied it in the same breath, \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me} --- never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the figure to stand on its own with nobody's hand under it, and the two hundred leaned in to hear it.

A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and held it where the crowd could see it, wanting a figure put against the figure that had just gone into the air, and Berr did not take the number into his hand. \emph{The figure stands as read}, he said. \emph{If your household has heard otherwise, your household has heard wrong. There'll be a slate at the watch.} He said it to the rail and not to the man, the way a man speaks to a pot he means to have obeyed, and the man put the fist back into his sleeve with the number still in it. The notice stood pinned to the moot-post with one in twelve printed on it in a clean hand, and nobody took it down and nobody set a mark against it, because a printed figure is a thing a man can be seen reading and the reading of it is not the paying of it. The crowd went back to the pigs.

The boy knew the gesture. He had done it himself in a white room with a body open on the table and a resident waiting for the sentence, hands out of the field before the closure, the chart written in a clean hand to make what the room had done agree with what the room would say; the knowledge came without a picture and without a name, as a skill comes to a hand that has not been told it is remembering. Here the field was a square of wet boards and the dressing was a figure that would be entered against households who had not stood there to hear it, and the hand that took itself out belonged to a man who believed he had done a kindness. Frost's second true subtraction went up the clay road in the locked box beside the first, and behind it the district's arithmetic changed a second time for the month and could not be changed back. The printed figure stood unchallenged. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and nobody in the square asked who had signed it.

The reading knew nothing of what it read over. No figure in the square, no slate on the block, no reader with a thumb wiped twice, answered for the man carried out on Day 188, whose name had not been spoken over the basket; none answered for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; none knew the dressed grain covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end at home, now read over by a number that did not know what it cost. The boy kept Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second on two places with a finger between, two and not one account, and kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it. It was not a lesson. It was not a grievance he could carry up the road and set down at a door. It was not a mending, for nothing had been mended, and not a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of it.

\person{Hessa Rale} took the narrow slate up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} before noon, edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve and nothing pressing where the writing was. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round; the second strap was let out for the long road, and the dried pale scurf of mud on the skirt she had shortened for the wet stood unbrushed, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd. To the women who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}; and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}. No figure came off the slate. She did not look at the woman who had spoken at \place{the yard well} on Frost 14, and the two shapings of that saying stood where they stood, beside her eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair; they were not joined to her slate and did not make her a reader. Eight was eight and was not a rule, and the road under her kept its own count in the legs.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} for a measure nobody had called, a fifth step's worth of ground where no step stood. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, and he held the slate to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it; his hand had gone to the slate before his eyes found the man who had said the buyer's number, and the number was entered before the looking. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and he did not rise out of it, and nobody took him off.

\scenebreak

The two hundred leaned in to a number that would not change what it counted, and then went back to the pigs. That was the seduction of the square: not the shout, not the cord, not the green at a mouth-corner, but the way a figure stood up in the air and looked like a thing that had been settled, when it had only been said twice. The boy lay in the finger's width of shade with the cold iron at his cheek and felt his hand come up an inch toward the twice-spoken figure. He took the small intake of the space between the moot's reading and the late households' reading --- the first going into the two hundred who stood there, the second going into the doorways of people who had not stood there --- and set it beside his own unnamed gap with a finger between, and put no figure on either. The hundred and fifty-third answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: a told figure beside a paid figure, and neither made to agree. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off at home, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. A figure read twice is one figure spoken two times; a payer is wanted under it and none stands; the hand that had taken itself out of the sentence had left the space empty, and an empty space kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Nothing of the reading came back under the Vail roof as a lesson, a sign, a mending, a cause or a payment. No collector came up the clay road for \person{Garl Vail}'s uncalled tile debt and none went down it, though the debt stood in the ruts of the road and was worse for being uncalled; his name had not been spoken under the roof for two hundred and eighty-nine days, and the house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor. The stayed frost, the skinned trough and the plate of ice on its flat stone were called weather at home and entered nowhere, and no fourth likeness between them and the twice-read figure was made a cause, because a cause wants a payer and none stood to any of it. Frost 30, the month's third and last Account-day, stood beyond this day and was not brought into it; the figure read over the covered grain at the rack end waited for a reading that would not know its cost, and the boy made nothing of the waiting and did not let it stand up as a sign.

Berr went off up the clay road with the box locked, Hessa's slate went up unread, the soft-cuffed clerk sat on over by the landing with his cuff grey again, and the two hundred went back to the pigs. The boy's hand came down on the hundred and fifty-third answer in the moot-post's finger's width of shade, the told figure beside the paid figure with a finger between, the file open and every tray parted, and the river's low note of water over water going on under everything, a low thing over a lower thing, neither of them counted and neither of them paid.
