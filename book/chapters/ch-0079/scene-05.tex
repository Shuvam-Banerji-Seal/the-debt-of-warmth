\daymark{290}{Frost 21, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

Frost came on its own morning the way a late frost comes into a yard that has been warm all night --- not as an arrival but as a discovery, the grey lying on the well lip where no grey had lain when the coals were banked. Nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof told \person[Corin]{the boy} that the number had moved. Two hundred and eighty-nine had gone to two hundred and ninety the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals and sits a while in the pot before it goes anywhere, and the sitting was not a counting and asked nothing of the air. Six months and one hundred and ten days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. There was no market on a Firstday, no gathering at \place{the Cross}, no bell; the square had kept its arithmetic and let it go, and the house kept its number in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. Between the one keeping and the other there was nothing to carry across, and the boy carried nothing. The morning under the fold was the private one again, small, domestic, close as the inside of a cup, and to the man behind the boy's eyes that closeness was almost a relief.

The room announced itself before the light did. Hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, the faint sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw} --- these came first, in the order they always came, and the boy took them without welcome because they had never promised him anything. Under the finger's-width door \place{the Clay Yard} lay under its own thin light and the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, neither of them numbered among the hearings. The carried fire answered under the floor in fours, four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and answered in fours again, and the silence stood where it stood. It was not a bell. A bell comes on its own morning and goes off when its morning has spent it; the fire under the floor had not spent its silence in two hundred and ninety days and would not spend it on a Firstday, because there was nobody under this roof to supply the pull and no instruction written anywhere to supply it.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had ever promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on \place{the bed}, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, and that the face cold puts on is a face of ease which is not ease at all. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and ten days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. In a room of white light he had learned to read the small improvement that comes before the turn for the worse and to say nothing about it to anybody who wanted comfort, because the saying would have cost more than the silence; here the improvement had not even the courtesy to arrive, and he stood ready with the fact anyway, the way a house keeps its charcoal under a finger and does not look.

\scenebreak

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety days; at the finger's-width door the river went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any, so the three stayed where they were, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The first instinct of the man he had been in another room was to find which two things agreed and call the third the error; here the error would have been to agree at all, because agreement wanted a payer and no payer stood. He let the three be three.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on Frost 10, stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, a heard thing beside a told thing, not folded into the three counts under the roof, not laid across the fire's missing fifth or the boards' thumb-width knot and not heard as a seventh of the full note. He had not heard a bell this morning and would not lend one an ear it had not earned, for a bell comes on its own morning the way a month comes, and a man who borrows one to make a row come out even has already put a payer under it.

\scenebreak

On \place{the towel} at the head of the bed the file lay turned out as instruments are turned out for a man who will never be called to use them, each thing in its own place with room between, known by weight before the eye fell on it. The surgeon's hand went over it in the dark and found every object where it had been laid. Six hearings lay first with the empty seventh place beside them kept empty; the heard bell lay beside the told in its square; three sayings about a pot lay together with the premature Smoke 14 note apart on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the ownerless question lay with five askings and one shaping beside them, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named. The nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth in her sleeve unread and no tenth, the count kept in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either to make a rule.

He set the Frost 14 well-saying on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand with a finger between: one saying and one shaping now, no more, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. A voice with nobody standing behind it had given the saying its second shaping over the boards two mornings back, asking what a runner carries in a slate that has a name in it, and had gone on with the day the way the yard says the weather; no owner of the voice stood anywhere to be asked, no room, no hand at the writing, no name for the person, no figure spoken. It did not make Hessa a reader of what she had carried for years and it was not joined to the strip in a sleeve a pace away, nor to a thane's twice-wiped thumb, nor to a clerk's inward slate, nor to the charcoal under the shelf. Two shapings were two and must not stand up as a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them.

Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second lay locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln}, the printed \emph{one in twelve} unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the space between the figure and the price kept its unsigned edge. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, read over by a number that did not know what it cost. The stayed frost on the lip of \place{the yard well}, \place{the trough} skinned over and the plate of ice clouded with silt on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet were called weather and entered nowhere, and no fourth likeness between them and any arithmetic was made a cause, because a cause wants a payer and none stood. The finding that a house keeps by its not-doings stayed on a place of its own, and his own gaps on another with a finger between --- two gaps above the shelf, one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the gone pan's round ring, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, the returning-line place where a hand went in and did not come out; no third gap made a row and no fourth likeness made a cause.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, told the fire it was the thaw when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. In the unswept corner the grey ridge called the thaw lay finer than the clean dark line beside it, and the \lore{Ash} in it had not been lifted in two years; she had not swept it and did not intend to sweep it. On a Firstday morning the hand at her throat went to the kiln-lid and not to \lore{the widow's mark}, because a morning is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. With her back to the basket she kept her finger on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread, uncopied and unfigured, moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. At the cool stone she lifted the wool from the dressed grain and turned it once with a touch lighter than the day before, put the wool back and gave it no figure, though a figure had gone over it yesterday that did not know what it cost and the space between that figure and its price had no signature on it that any hand under this roof could supply.

To the wool she said \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} in the old proverb-form, and to the broom she gave its instruction before anybody else could, \emph{Go around}, and the broom went around. She said nothing of the reading, nothing of the figure twice spoken over a basket, nothing of the box gone up the road; a thing said aloud becomes a thing a slate can enter, and she had long ago decided that no slate should enter it. She stood the pot rim on \place{the pot board} two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle toward him and a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. At \place{the hearth} the fire answered four low pulls and left the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, then went away without looking toward the fold. Every instruction at that hour went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and none of it went to the basket.

He heard her grammar as a man hears a language he has not been taught but has learned to parse from the order of the words: fire before basket, the instruction to the tools and never to the thing carried, \emph{the house} where another woman would say \emph{I}. Nothing of the square came home in it. The house kept its dead in a salt-stiff coat on a peg and a wheel-seat with a hollow nobody filled, kept its debt against a firing of tiles neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled, kept \person{Garl Vail}'s name unspoken two hundred and ninety days, and kept this boy by not putting him into any of it. The keeping was maintenance and not a story, and he had learned to tell the difference the way he had learned to tell a sound vessel from a cracked one: by what answered when you tapped it. The relief of the ordinary morning was small and real and he did not spend it; under it the wrist went bare and cold where the wool rode back, and the crust lay two inches off, and the body went on being a body that wanted what it could not reach.

\scenebreak

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's own going. It took the small intake of the river's low note and held it past that count's letting-go and through the little silence after it, and left the silence whole and empty, not filled to make a row come out even. There had been a silence in the square yesterday between the reading and the going back to the pigs, and a silence under the floor where the fifth pull should have stood, and a silence on the shelf above the two gaps; three silences in three places, and none of them rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. He did not join them. The empty seventh place lay on its own tray with the empty interval beside it and a finger between, both kept empty, neither spent to make a number come out even; an empty space kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The hand held the little silence as a surgeon holds pressure on a vessel that has stopped bleeding and will not yet be trusted, with no more than the pressure the thing would bear and not one beat longer.

The hundred and fifty-fifth answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was an object and not a power, entered on the sixth place with a finger's width between it and the one behind it; a round run of answers did not stand up and call itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. The satisfaction was small and clean and he did not spend it; the wanting was large and damp and would not be answered by any counting. Neither the intake nor the silence after it was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. He had once called such intervals trivial because they could be measured; he no longer called them trivial, and he still could not close the two inches.

Frost the thirtieth, the month's third and last Account-day and market, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought into this one. The hand did not reach toward it as a sign and did not spend the held silence on it; when the third figure came it would go over the covered grain and not know its cost, and the boy would keep the days exactly while the house kept them in loaves and glances and an unnamed space at the row's end.

The hand came down from holding the intake through the little silence and laid itself against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The silence was left whole and empty under the finger's-width door, and the river went low over lower under it, a low thing over a lower thing, neither counted and neither paid. Two hundred and ninety stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and ten days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The crust lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed and no third come to make the row come out even, and the thread at his mouth stayed frozen where it had been laid. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended.
