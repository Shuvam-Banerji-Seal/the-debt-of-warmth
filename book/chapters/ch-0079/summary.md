## Chronicle

The nineteenth day of Frost came on under the Vail roof by degrees and from the outside. The boy lay carried and awake, two hundred and eighty-eight days old by a count nobody kept, with one crust two inches off and the empty place beside it unnamed. He took the body as it presented itself — crust, gap, thread frozen in the dry heat, cold at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true, and the second was louder.

Sella went to the fire before the basket, said *The house will keep it*, turned the coals with a broken pot, kept the charcoal unread, set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand and left the widow's mark untouched, for a morning is not an opening. The yard went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the stayed frost on the well lip and the silt-clouded plate of ice on its flat stone were called weather. In the afternoon an ownerless voice over the boards gave the Frost 14 saying a second shaping: what a runner carries in a slate that has a name in it. The boy filed it beside the first with a finger between, not making Hessa a reader and not letting two shapings stand up as a witness.

On the twentieth the district's arithmetic changed a second time. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, read *one in twelve* into the two hundred, sent the figure over the basket once for the moot and once for the late households, called the levy and denied it with *It is not called by me*, taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. The subtraction went up in the locked box and could not be changed back; the figure read was still not the figure paid. Hessa carried his slate toward Greybank unread. Two sightings at the market, a cup turned a quarter and a half-turn after a knock, were a colour and a shape with no eighth.

On the twenty-first the house kept its small grammar. The boy came into his two hundred and ninety days, told by nobody; the hand held an intake past its letting-go and through the little silence, left it whole and empty, then set the held interval beside the empty interval with a finger between and came down on the river's note. One hundred and fifty-six answers stood exact and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand laid back against the fold, 290 stood spoken by nobody, and the river went quiet. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** Day 290, Frost 21, Firstday, the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln, Sill, Grath. The boy lies on his bed under the roof (never the Debt Loft) with the fold at his ear, one crust two inches off and its empty place unnamed, the thread frozen and unwiped, the hand laid back against the fold on the **one hundred and fifty-sixth** answer. The file lies open on the towel with every gap left gap and every tray parted by a finger's width; nothing moved. Beyond the finger's-width door the frost stands white on the well lip and the plate of ice stands clouded with silt on its flat stone out of the path of feet. The river's low note goes quiet after the hand comes down. No other character is on stage at the close; the chapter ends on continuation, not a drawn line.

- **Day and time at the end:** Day 290, small hours, six months and one hundred and ten days, told by nobody. Days 288 (Frost 19, Ninthday), 289 (Frost 20, Tenthday) and 290 (Frost 21, Firstday) were each opened by their own dated `\daymark` and lived in two full scenes of well over eight hundred words (19,323 words total). The carried fire's fifth is unpaid **290 days** with its payer unnamed. Frost 19 and 21 were ordinary tending days with no market, Cross or bell; Frost 20 was the month's second Account-day and market. Frost 30 (Day 299), the month's third and last Account-day and market, stands nine mornings ahead and was not brought into an unearned day.

- **Corin/Dan:** carried everywhere, awake, silent, cannot roll, stand, speak, read Grathic, reach a cup, close two inches on the basket rim, lift a crust to his mouth or wipe the thread at his mouth. Cold stops at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint; the afterdrop waits past a warming nobody will perform. The cracked low-capacity vessel is unchanged, below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers, flaw unnamed, rate of loss unmeasured. The only movement is the hand up an inch toward a sound, an intake, a held interval, a bridge, a letting-go. He wants the crust lifted and the thread wiped and gets neither. No rank, no power, no experiment, no new cast to any spell. There remain two men under the roof and only one has a body: the older keeps the inventory, the younger keeps the wanting, and neither has authority over the other.

- **Where everyone is now, and what they want:** Sella Vail, keeper of the fire, goes to it before the basket, says *The house will keep it*, keeps the charcoal unread and the pot rim two inches out, turns the grain under wool with a lighter touch and no figure, leaves the thread frozen, never touches the widow's mark except before a kiln (last on Frost 2), carries him down and up at the half-pace from every open slate, and says nothing of what was read over him; she wants the house kept whole by its not-doings. Tarn, at the kindling, keeps six checkable and a seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, stops the ear-sentence at the word that would call the loan (a remembered **ninth** stopping, no tenth before the seventh year at its very end), chest tight, Seed 16 breath unspent, Ash in his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his wrist; he wants the paying put off though the debt stands in the ruts. Jorra Vail, at the wheel-shed edge, parts dusty forearms from clean, speaks to the wheel and a pot before any person (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*; *You'll do*), withholds the note that pot will give three turns ahead, gives the unnamed back-room men no name, and puts his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's sentence; *he's not Vail* stays sealed and the promise of the boy's name is neither given nor withdrawn. Garl Vail stays in objects — salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease, wheel-seat with its twenty-year lean and hollow nobody fills, clean dark line and the finer grey thaw unswept, tile debt worse for being uncalled — his name unspoken **290 days**, no collector up or down the clay road. Ilva stays offstage on these three days, her notched birch strip unread in her sleeve; her ninth hand stays nine. Berr Clay went off up the clay road on Frost 20 with the box locked and the second subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness, wanting the accounts kept and the figure standing as read. Hessa Rale took his slate up toward Greybank before noon on Frost 20, edge-on and unread, still not a reader and not joined to the two shapings. The soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, cuff grey again by midday, his buyer's number entered before he looked up. The green-mouthed woman at the pot board and the foundling at the market's edge were sightings on Frost 20 only — a cup turned a quarter, a pale cord-mark, a child's shoulders in cast-off clothes, a green sliver on a string, a half-turn after a knock — never named, never joined, never an eighth. Unnamed men went low into the back room before first grey on all three days, a direction and a sound only. Alda Reed, Mira Silt, Orra Weir, Dessa Silt, Rusk, Marra, the reed-cutter and the man from Slate Row with soft hands are all offstage and unchanged.

- **Items, money, powers:** Nothing gained, lost, bought, sold or bonded; no money at any point and no slate opened within reach of the basket on any of the three days. The printed one in twelve stands unchallenged and has now been read **six** times over the basket (Smoke 10, 20 and 30, Frost 10 and 20 — the twentieth twice, once for the moot and once for the late households); the figure read is still not the figure paid. Smoke's three arithmetics, Frost's first and now **Frost's second** are locked in Berr's box toward High Kiln and cannot be changed back. Coals turned with the back of a broken pot; grain turned beneath wool with no figure; pot rim set two inches out with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. No usable power was used; the six additions to the held inch are honest hand-coordinations, not abilities.

- **Facts, lies, promises, debts:** No new fact is revealed that the reader may treat as settled. Berr's second reading is a real, irreversible administrative change and a false comfort: the figure stands as read, the levy is called and denied in the same breath, and the space between figure and price has no signature. Hessa's answer stays the question put and not the one behind it: *I don't read it. I carry it.* The Frost 14 well-saying now stands at **one saying and one second shaping**, filed with a finger between it and Hessa's eighth hand — not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness, not paid, never making Hessa a reader; two shapings must not stand up as a rule.
