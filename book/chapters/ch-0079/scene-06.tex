Frost went on without a boundary drawn. It had come in the dark beyond the finger's-width door and it went into the afternoon the way a pot goes on cooling after it has been set off the coals --- not by any decision of the pot, but by the degree of the stone it stands on. Nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof set the district's two habits of naming its months right or left them wrong in so many words. The older women called a month by what the weather did in it, the slatemen called it by the number of its Account-days, and both went on being used at the well and at the Cross, and neither was corrected into the other. Two readings stood behind the household now, the first of Frost gone up on the tenth and the second gone up yesterday, and no lesson was made of either, no mending anybody could name and no payer put under them. The next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward; four openings in four months were four and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them.

The afternoon drew on without ending. The light thinned over the worked ground the way heat draws back from a fold and sits behind the pot, where a hand might still find it if a hand had been given to put there. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the pot rim standing two inches beyond him, and the file was still turned out on \place{the towel} with every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing moved. \person{Sella Vail} came in, went to the fire before the basket, and carried him down at the half-pace from every open slate, out under the door and into \place{the Clay Yard}, where the day was wearing on without drawing to a close.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that order had never lied to him and no other had ever promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and ten days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

There were two men under the roof and only one of them had a body. The older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other; the older could name the afterdrop and the younger could only be cold, and the naming did not warm him. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust to his mouth, and the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and ninety days; at the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and he let them be three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any.

\scenebreak

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number. At the Vail corner the clean dark line lay where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it, called \lore{the thaw}, lay finer still; neither had been swept, and the broom went around both as a woman goes around a sleeping animal. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had been laid, and she gave the space under the rack the wide berth she gave a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, without looking at the slates and without entering them. On the flat ground near \place{the drying racks} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word; a pan gone is known by the ring it leaves, and the ring was not a message and had no hand to send it. Beside the well \place{the trough} had skinned over from edge to edge and its water was called weather by everybody who passed it, and the weather was not asked what it cost. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place stood where it had stood since the planting, and the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, was gone around with a look and no number.

Seven were seven and not an account. She did not count them under her breath and he did not count them for her; the missing hand was carried, not named, not paid and not joined to the one crust in the fold, or to the dressed grain covered and unfigured at \place{the rack end}, or to the man carried out on Day 188. A house that keeps by going around things keeps its accounts in the feet, and a man who reads such a house learns to count the steps and to call no total. The yard's goings-around went on over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and he was the stone and not the water. He had once known the exact number of the instruments laid on a tray without deciding to, and here he kept seven places that nobody had numbered and would not number them, because a total wants a payer and the yard stood under no payer to any of them.

The stayed frost lay white on the lip of \place{the yard well} where the Warm had lately gone out, and the rope stiffened as the bucket came up and gave its small dry rasp. On \place{the flat stone by the well}, set out of the path of feet where nobody would knock it, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt where the silt had risen into it, a covering laid over a depth that the yard called weather and asked nothing of. The boy kept the white lip on a place of its own and the plate on another, and made no fourth likeness between them and the trough's skin and the stayed grey at the corner, and named no payer to any of them. A cause is a thing that wants a payer standing under it, and nobody stood. The bucket went down and came up; the rope gave its dry note; the weather went on being weather, entered nowhere. Two hundred and ninety days stood exact under the roof and the frost on the well lip had no figure on it and wanted none.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and when one woman went by him wanting the thaw early and another wanting it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to each so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of the road and had stood there since the water went.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and his chest was tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of \place{the Debt Loft}, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and asked nothing of the sky, and the boy, who had once counted the exits in a room without deciding to, kept the seventh where Tarn had set it and entered no figure against it.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door at the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid.

Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the boy took it as a direction and a sound. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the ownerless row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness, and was not asked a sixth time. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

Garl was present in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, with the crease at the left shoulder where a collector's hand had once rested or where a man had leaned; the boy had never decided which, and the not-deciding kept the coat honest. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person --- feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety days. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and none went down it; the first and second true subtractions of Frost rode locked in a box toward \place{High Kiln} with the printed figure unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the keeping of a man by \lore{not-doings} stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; she would sooner swallow the mark than touch it at a basket or a bed. At the shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally was unread, uncopied and unfigured, her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. To the wool she said \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} in the old proverb-form, and to the broom she gave its instruction before anybody could, \emph{Go around}. She stood the pot rim on \place{the pot board} two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle toward him and a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. Every instruction at that hour went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and none of it went to the basket.

He heard her grammar as a man hears a language he has not been taught but has learned to parse from the order of the words: fire before basket, the instruction to the tools and never to the thing carried, \emph{the house} where another woman would say \emph{I}. Nothing of the square came home in it, and nothing of the day's arithmetic went into the fold. The house kept its dead in a salt-stiff coat on a peg and a wheel-seat with a hollow nobody filled, kept its debt against a firing of tiles neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled, kept Garl's name unspoken two hundred and ninety days, and kept this boy by not putting him into any of it. The keeping was maintenance and not a story, and he had learned to tell the difference the way he had learned to tell a sound vessel from a cracked one: by what answered when you tapped it. At \place{the hearth} the fire answered four low pulls and left the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, then went away without looking toward the fold.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the file was turned out on the towel as instruments are turned out for a man who will never be called to use them, each thing in its own place with room between, known by weight before the eye fell on it. The surgeon's hand went over it in the dark and found every object where it had been laid. Six hearings lay first with the empty seventh place beside them kept empty; the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her lay where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the heard bell lay beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} in its square, a heard thing beside a told thing, not folded into the three counts under the roof and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Three sayings about a pot lay together with the premature Smoke 14 note apart on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the burned-slate word stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the ownerless question lay with five askings and one shaping beside them, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named.

The nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth in her sleeve unread and no tenth, the count kept in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either to make a rule. He set the Frost 14 well-saying on a place of its own beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand with a finger between: one saying and one shaping, no more, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid; it did not make her a reader of what she had carried for years, and two shapings must not stand up as a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them. Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second lay locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the clay road toward High Kiln, the printed \emph{one in twelve} unchallenged and the figure read still not the figure paid, and the space between the figure and the price kept its unsigned edge.

He kept the finding that a house keeps by its not-doings on a place of its own, and his own gaps on another with a finger between --- two gaps above the shelf, one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the gone pan's round ring, the field's empty place out on the worked strips, the returning-line place where a hand went in and did not come out --- no third gap made a row and no fourth likeness made a cause. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone, read over by numbers that did not know what it cost; the stayed frost on the well lip, the trough skinned over and the plate of ice clouded with silt on its flat stone out of the path of feet were called weather and entered nowhere. The house's keeping and his own kept absences stayed two keepings and not one account for want of a payer under either, and he did not lay the finding across Garl's absence or any other absence. It was maintenance and not a story, and he left it in its own place with the file open around it and room between.

\scenebreak

Outside the wool the hand took the held interval and the empty interval from the file, set them side by side with a finger between, borrowed no house count, supplied no fifth to \lore{the carried fire}, crossed no silence that belonged to another's breathing and came down on the river's next low note of \lore{water over water}. Neither interval was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a gift, a keeping, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a doctrine or a rule. The hundred and fifty-sixth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; a round threshold did not stand up and call itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. The satisfaction was small and clean and he did not spend it; the wanting was large and damp and would not be answered by any counting.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and ninety stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and ten days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Frost the thirtieth, the month's third and last Account-day and market, stood nine mornings ahead and was not brought into this day; he made nothing of the nearness and did not let it stand up as a sign. He lay with the crust two inches off and its empty place unnamed and no third come to make the row come out even, the hand laid back against the fold on the hundred and fifty-sixth answer, the file open on the towel with every gap left gap; beyond the finger's-width door the frost stood white on the well lip and the plate of ice clouded on its flat stone out of the path of feet, and the river's low note of water over water went quiet after the hand came down.
