## Chronicle

Sella Vail carried the boy at the half-pace from every open slate and gave the instruction to the wool and the wind, never to the basket. The yard went around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the rack space and the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, with a look and no number. Reeds came in armfuls judged by the arm that carried them. Under the loaf shelf Sella reached a finger beneath the board where the charcoal tally lay, without looking and without a word.

At the Weir a reed-cutter tied one knot with his teeth and put his grey cord back inside his coat: *You can ask the cord* --- *No more can the Court*. Orra Weir gave a date where a figure was asked; Mira Silt answered a thin run with her own answer, her thumb under her apron to a grey cord with a second twist twisted twice beneath it. At dusk Sella set the boy on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without looking at his face and left the thread frozen. The river's note came through the door, the carried fire missed its fifth breath, the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot: three counts, none become one.

Before first grey men went low into the room off the wheel-shed, a finger's width open; Sella gave them no name. Jorra Vail came through with grey slate dust past the bent finger, spoke to the wheel before anyone, told a pot *You'll do* and put that finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence. Tarn took the broom only after it was instructed, wanting the late thaw because it puts off the paying, his seventh year still held. At the hearth he brought the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt a fourth time to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, no figure, no name, drawing the breath he never spent. The boy laid a fourth interval beside three on his tray.

At the Cross a silent woman with green at one mouth-corner turned a cup a quarter, set it back and went off with the light: the fourth green at a mouth, the fifth with Berr Clay's glaze. Berr came up short, wiped his thumb twice, paid two marks short for eels and said a number goes into a slate, not a bundle. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool, the cold to one woman and weather to the yard; the reading bought nothing. Back under the roof Sella's finger went beneath the board again. In the small hours his file held six hearings and no seventh, four stoppings as objects not a rule, an uncalled loan. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down with one hundred and thirty-four exact and unspent.
