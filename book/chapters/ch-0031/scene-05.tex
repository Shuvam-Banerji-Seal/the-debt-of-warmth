The afternoon went down toward the river without being told to, the way water goes along a gutter that has already learned its course. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} off the clay road and down to \place{the Fishstairs} below \place{the north bridge} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around a board set out to dry on the bank as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, going under the fold where nobody had been given an instruction to put a hand. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and no instruction had ever been given about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put, stiff and doing nothing. Below the bridge \place{Low Kiln} thinned into wet wool and tallow smoke behind them; ahead, salt stood white on the posts where the flood had touched them and gone away, and under the noise of the stalls ran the note of \place{the Ilm}, a fifth low thing he had kept on a place of its own since he was six days old. Her arm went on keeping its exact account of what it held and saying nothing of what it cost, and he did not enter it, because the one account of all of them he had no standing to enter was the one that carried him. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth of her shoulder and came down against the fold.

At the foot of the steps, where the fifth step should have been and was not, \place{the out-of-line landing} lay a hand's breadth out of line with the rest of the stair, and the whole market went over it carefully without making a fall of it and without making a step of it either. The soft-cuffed clerk was still set there for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, the second grey earned in the working day the way a seam takes the colour of the hand that draws it through; his slate lay against his forearm with the writing turned where nobody else could see it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, the way a man passes a finger over a seam before he trusts it and looks at the cloth afterward. He had not been taken off since the month turned, or if he had been taken off somebody had put him back in the dark. A buyer named a figure at the eel boards and the clerk's hand went to the slate before his eyes went up to find the man who had said it, so the figure was entered before it was looked at and the looking came too late to change anything. A woman with a bundle of reeds called the price of them into the noise and the noise took it; a stall-keeper shifted a creel and went back to her perch. The market sold its reeds and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and did not lower its voice. A girl with a creel took the landing sideways, one hand out to the rail, and went on selling without looking at her feet. Nobody stepped around the clerk, because a man with a slate is a thing every landing has, and nobody took him off.

The boy set the washed cuff on one place and the slate held where no other man could read it on another, because a man who washes his own cuff has a hunger or a habit, and a man who holds a slate where nobody can read the figure has a use, and a use wants a payer. Which of the two this man was he could not say, and did not try to say; a thing seen at the edge of a market and still seen there was a thing seen, and he kept the not-knowing whole rather than fill it. A measure set down before anybody calls it is a measure set down ahead of something. He made a shape of that and not a rule, the way he kept the heard bell beside the told bell in two places and had never laid one across the other, the way four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb stayed three counts in three places and did not become one. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for the order of a hand that reaches for the record before it reaches for the person, and if there had been it would have been a word for a thing every clerk in the district did and no clerk would have owned to doing. The surgeon's register took the cuff's two greys and the hand's one order and entered them nowhere, exact, complete, worth nothing to anyone who could have written it down. He could not have handed the shape to a slate even if a hand had lifted him to one, and that was the whole of its usefulness, which was none.

The light went off the boards and the market thinned the way a fire thins when the wood is down to its last few pieces. Sella carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer hairline of grey beside it lay unswept and were called the thaw; \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung, because a dusk is not an opening. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, and went to her own bed.

At the hearth the coals were banked with the back of the broken pot, leaving one small place where the heat came through, and \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} had set the small knife back where it could be checked, on the sill at the very edge, with six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and never cut, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no figure in it and no name. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He measured the kindling stack with his eyes the way he measured everything that had to last, four, four, a space where the fifth should be, and took up a stick and told it what he told it every night, in the flat voice he kept for wood and tools and pots and never for the basket. “Six counted,” he said. “The next I don't count.”

The stick lay where he put it. He took up another, and the sentence went on past the counting the way a man walks on past a gate he has left open, not meaning to go far and going all the same. It came the way it had come on Reed 5, on Reed 8 and on the night of Reed 11, in the same order and with the same weight at the end of it: an ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan --- the word that would put a slate at the watch and set a collector on the road and take the measure of the house by what it had kept --- and stopped there. No figure came with it and no name stood under it. His chest was tight. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and the small interval showed where it should have gone in, the same interval that had shown in his chest every morning since Seed 16, showing tonight at the hearth where the fire could not see it and the boy could. It was the shape he had said it in for years, the statement of fact made in the tone that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to, and nobody laughed and nobody was meant to. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom.

It was the fourth time. Sella, within hearing of the word that had not been said, gave the end of her instruction to the pot --- keep it where it can be found, the house has said it --- and asked nothing. \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger, spoke to the wheel and not to the man at the hearth, told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and did not ask about the ear. Tarn did not ask about the room. The two silences stood under one roof with no hand between them, like two pots set on a bench that have both been judged and neither entered, and the household straightened around them as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, though no weight had been named and no order had been changed. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled; the seventh year stood at the very edge of the sill and had not given. Something must give at the end of a seventh year, and it had not given here, on the fourth bringing of the sentence, with the knife where it could be checked and the one entry the house could not check held where it was always held.

The boy had watched three go to the same word and come back with nothing, and he had laid them on the interval tray as objects and not as a rule; tonight he laid the fourth beside them with the same care and the same refusal. Four likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if no hand holds them apart, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer --- a thing that can be entered on a slate and handed to a clerk, a thing with a figure under a name. Four intervals lying side by side are only four intervals and belong to nobody who could spend them. He would not let four stoppings stand up and call themselves a cause. The labour of holding them apart was invisible and worth nothing anyone could enter; it was the only honest work going in the room, and no instruction had ever been given about it and no slate had ever asked for it. He turned out the tray as a man lays instruments on a towel, field, exposure, instrument, complication, cost, and here there was no field and no cost he could spend, only the objects laid edge to edge and nothing laid across anything else. The half-beat behind the standing finger lay where it lay; the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check lay beside it; his own held breath from the market lay beside that; and the four stoppings of the near-saying lay in their row, four objects and not one rule. He had refused to let two stand up and call themselves a cause; he refused it with three; he refused it now with four. A habit of the first life, this laying-out, that had survived into this one without his ever choosing it and without his being able to stop. A rule is a thing a man can hand to a clerk. These he could not have handed to anyone, and that was why he went on laying them out on a towel in the dark where nobody could see him do it.

In the small hours the house kept its three counts in three places and none of them became one. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir}, a fifth low thing never counted among \lore{the full note}; through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-three days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. He had learned to leave the three where they lay. A man who lays three counts together and calls them one account has made a cause out of a room, and there was no room under this roof for another cause. The told bell stood beside the heard bell; the word forty stayed unspoken with the readable double turn beside it and the small weight of a thumb pressed in apart; the clerk at the landing stayed set; the loan stayed uncalled; the seventh year had not given.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint so the cold found the bare place and went under the fold where nobody had been told to put a hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and thirty-three days stood exact in him and were spoken by nobody. He filed the fourth stopping on the tray where it belonged, beside three others and not across them, and went on holding the four apart in the dark where nobody could see him do it and nobody had asked him to. The broom went on over the banked fire. The loan that could hear was uncalled, and the sentence that would call it had come four times to the word and had not once said it. Nothing was spent. Nothing was paid. The hand went up an inch and came down.
