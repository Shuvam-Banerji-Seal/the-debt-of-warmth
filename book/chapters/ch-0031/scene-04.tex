\daymark{133}{Reed 14, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey \place{the Clay Yard} had the colour of a pot washed out into dirty water, and the men came up through it with their collars high. There were three or four, and they did not arrive together. One went ahead with his shoulders drawn in against the wind, two came after at a spacing that was not quite chance, and the last kept to the shadow beside the rack as though the dark were a thing he had paid for and was careful with. \person{Sella Vail} carried the boy past them at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket --- keep it off his ears, it had come down with them and would go back up with them, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. She did not turn her head toward the door of \place[the back room]{the room off the shed}. That door kept its finger's width of open, as it had kept it through a hundred and thirty-three mornings, and the low talk went into it and stayed where it went. To the house she gave no name for the men, and she did not say the room was cold.

The boy lay at the low angle where the underside of a board becomes a roof to a man on the floor, and counted what he could not spend. Three men, or four; a door a finger's width open; a woman who walked past a thing without looking at it and told him more by the not-looking than a look would have told. A room a house does not enter in any roll it shows can still be counted by the cold that comes out of it, and he had no word in \lore{Grathic} for that second counting and no voice to speak it if he had. The surgeon's register took the temperature of the morning and entered it nowhere. Men who arrange not to arrive together have arranged something; the arrangement stood on the yard with the mud going off it and was complete, exact, and worth nothing to anyone who could have written it down.

The little-door burn across the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing as she shifted him higher against her hip, and the arm that carried him went on keeping its account of what it held and saying nothing of what it cost. He had learned to read that record in other arms in the yard, the reed-cutter's, a girl's with a creel, a woman's come up from the strips with mud dry in the creases of her wrists; her arm was the one that carried him, and it was the one account of all of them he had no standing to enter. The half-pace from the open slates held all the way past \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the shed} and round toward the wheel, and the boy's small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth of her shoulder and came down against the fold.

By the time the light came properly over the sill \person{Jorra Vail} had come through from the shed, and the dust was on him before the boot scraper was. Fine grey slate dust, ground small as meal, worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and into the half-moons under the nails; it had gone into the grain of the skin and stayed there, and was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel, which dried to old bone and flaked away. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as he had kept them on a hundred mornings, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The questions stood where all his questions stood, in a mouth that could not form the Grathic for them and a hand that could not have written the answer if one had been handed to him. Across a hundred and thirty mornings the two states of the same man's arms had come and gone and no hand in the district had laid one beside the other and called them one account. The boy did not do it either, and did not make a cause of his not doing it. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

At the wheel he spoke before anyone spoke to him. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel, in the flat voice he kept for tools and pots and never for the basket. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel gave its low note under his hands as it came round, and with the note came damp clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze carried up from \place{High Kiln} on a bad wind. A pot on the bench answered nearly full, and he told it “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew its full note before it left the wheel --- knew the air under it and the small want in it, the thing a kiln-master would have called early and made something of --- and he did not say so when a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs stood in the shed mouth and looked at the row of pots and said nothing a slate could carry.

“That one's singing before its time,” the kiln-master said.

Jorra put a hand to the wheel and did not answer him. The saying went off on the wind with the sweetish glaze in it and arrived at no figure, and the boy kept the knowing where he had kept it on other mornings, as a fact about a man and not a finding of his own. Neither man looked toward the basket, and the two not-lookings stood on two different places on the yard and did not meet.

\scenebreak

Sella had not finished telling the fire it was low. “The fire's low,” she said to the fire, and the rest of it --- whatever instruction would have gone to the pot, or the wool, or the hand at \lore{the damper} --- stayed in her mouth. Before the end of it came, his bent finger went into the cold iron ahead of her sentence, the little finger leading as it had led since he was eleven, and moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave a little of it back, warmer, the way a clamp gives back nothing and yet holds a vessel whole while the heat comes up through it. The end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, though no weight had been named and no order had been changed; only a tongue of iron stood a hair's breadth differently from where it had stood a moment before. Her lips went on with the instruction and finished it at the pot. She did not look at his hand and he did not look at her face; the two not-lookings were an arrangement older than the boy, and they held as the iron held, without being entered in anything.

The boy read the iron again from the low angle. It was one piece of iron with two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole, and the bite that can take the hand off a man if the heat behind it has nowhere else to go. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies under one roof of a mouth, and if there had been he could not have spoken it, and if he had spoken it nobody in the yard would have known what he meant by mercy. It was the same iron the smiths mended east of the yard for the collectors' cold tubes, and the same bite a man closes on a vessel that is leaking faster than it can be kept; here it stood in a shed mouth at the edge of first light and held a small fire in a house that kept its warmth badly. He filed the object as an object, exact and complete and worth nothing, joined to no kiln and no doctrine. A clamp is a clamp; it is not asked to mean more than the holding it does. He had no standing to improve it and no hand to lift it, and the reading went onto the tray with the rest.

Beside it, on the same tray and not laid across it, lay the two tellings of the shut counting room, each with its date, both saying not the war's hand --- one breath through the nose, one breath in the throat, eighteen leagues east and a curiosity carried in a man's bag. The first had come at the well from a man with road-mud on his boots; the second at the same well from another with a bag; both had been taken as history with a date, and the yard had gone back to its tubs. No third telling had come in a hundred and thirty-three days, and he did not add one, though the door a finger's width open off the shed gave its low talk to the yard and took nothing back. Both had to do with the stopping of breath in a room, and that was as far as he let them go. A joining of the two was a thing waiting on a hand not his and a day that had not come, and he left it waiting where it stood.

\scenebreak

Outside at the edge of the yard \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took up the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. Go around, it cost enough to come in, it will cost more to take out; the instruction went into the broom and \place{the flagstones} and never into the man, and he took the tool up as a man takes up a thing already named. “Yes, house,” he said to the woman who wanted \lore{the thaw} early, and “Yes, house,” to the man who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and would stand there when the reeds were cut and after they were cut. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

His seventh year stood pressed past another tend and still held. The small bench knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years counted where the house could check and the seventh where it could not, the one entry in this house the house could not check, and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no figure in it and no name. He had told the sill where the knife was, unasked, as he told it every morning, and the yard had not answered, which was the answer he worked best with. His chest was tight. The breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16 was still unspent in it, and there was a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in --- an empty place in the order of a thing a man does without thinking, a breath stopped against iron and never let through. He went around the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it without sweeping either, and around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the stroke went where the house had told the broom to go.

His stroke went on over the flagstones without the half-check. There had been a morning when the broom checked for half a beat behind Jorra's standing finger and went on; there had been mornings since when the stones kept the place where it had stopped. Today there was no check, and the stones kept the shallow bright patch the way the yard kept the space under the lowest rack and the place in the strip where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out --- by passing over it and not entering it. Neither man named the other's silence. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stood in one yard with no hand between them, like two pots set on a bench that have both been judged and neither entered. The loan that could hear was uncalled. The place where the stroke had stopped was kept as this house kept its other places, by passing over it and not sweeping it into an account. The boy had watched the equality move to this place and hold there, and held it where it held, an object worth nothing and not yet a rule. Four likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a cause if no hand holds them apart, and the holding of them apart was work no one under the roof had asked of him and no slate could have entered.

A bucket of water stood by the shed wall with the morning in it, two paces off and a world away. The thread at the boy's mouth had frozen in the small hours and had stayed frozen through the morning, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. He could not close two inches on the rim of the bucket, or reach the pot with its handle turned out, or touch the cold iron standing a hair's breadth differently in the shed. One hundred and thirty-three days stood exact in him and were spoken by nobody. Tarn's stroke went on over the flagstones without the half-check, and the stones kept the shallow bright patch where it had stopped. The boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water in the bucket and came down.
