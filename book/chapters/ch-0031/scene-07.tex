Back under the roof the afternoon had come down to the low angle, where the light lay along the floor and stopped at the bench leg as though it too had been given an instruction to go around. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} in and set the basket low and a little back near the leg, at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board that a man standing sees only the top of. It was not a place she chose for him. It was a place the light chose, and she knew the light's habits better than she knew the habits of anybody under this roof. Woodsmoke went before her and wet wool went with her, and under both the faint sour mineral tang of the \lore{Ash} that had gathered in this house for two years and would not be swept. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the fire and to the pot and to the broom and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears. Feed it. Turn it. Go around what the water left; it cost enough to come in and it will cost more to take out. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing, and she did not look down into the basket.

Above him \place{the loaf shelf} held its row the way a house keeps its days badly: loaves pushed along, a glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and two spaces where loaves should have stood and no third. One was the old space at the row's end, the one that had come in with the planting and that a neighbour could name in one breath without ever seeing it as a thing that was missing; the same neighbour had called the other the price of a wet thing set down wrong, hearing two sayings and not one absence. That other was a palm's breadth of bare board that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word, as if a loaf had lifted itself off in the dark and gone. A row comes up short and a body gets used to it. A house that kept its days in loaves could carry a whole tend without ever calling a space a number, and a gap pushed along with the row was a gap the house had decided to live beside. No finger in \place{Low Kiln} had laid the two together, and a saying of the loaf-shelves held that two gaps stay two gaps until such a finger comes and an account then wants a name. The fold at his ear kept one stiff brown-edged crust and an empty place beside it that had no name, and that record lay in the other room. The two rooms did not touch. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the second face of a board and no voice to say it if he had.

Sella stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row. The dry warm smell of the shelf went up past her and the sharper smell of charcoal came up from under the board for one breath and went away. The little-door burn closed on the wood's lip and opened again. She glanced once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and then, without looking down at the board and without a word, she reached a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lay. The finger went under far enough for the knuckle to set and the thumb to come up against the grain, and stayed there while a breath went out of her and came back, and then she brought it away. She did not go back to the shelf.

From the low angle the boy saw the hand go under and come back and saw nothing of what the marks said. He did not count the strokes or press a thumb where her thumb had been or lay the underside beside the two gaps above it or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, and he made no cause of a hand that goes under a board without looking. Such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason was left where it stood, between the two, with nothing written across it. A keeping put where nobody expects to be read tells more by the care of the hiding than the writing would, and a hand that hides a thing in front of a child is either careless of the child or trusts him not to read, and the two are very different rooms and he had no standing to enter either. He had spent a hundred and thirty-four days learning the cost of drawing the line, and the labour of not drawing it here was invisible, exact, and worth nothing anyone could have entered on a slate.

The light went off the boards and the room came down to its evening shape: the pot on the bench, the draught going along the floor to the corner, and the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned with the new finer hairline of grey beside it, both unswept and both called the thaw. Sella lifted the basket without looking into it and carried him up the \place{clay road} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the woodsmoke of \place{Low Kiln} going before her and the river wind coming up to meet her. At \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} she went around the space beneath it as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the broken birth-slates lay where they lay and were entered by nobody. Under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread at his mouth frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. The one brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came.

She told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, and the handle stood two inches beyond the hand that would have reached for it and would not close on it. The \lore{widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord against her throat, because a dusk is not an opening and a shelf, a strip, a rack, a basket and a bed are not openings. She did not touch it and did not go back to the shelf, and gave no explanation of the count she kept.

In the small hours he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before he begins, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else. He had kept six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, the district's slate twice at the Cross, a man giving at the Drawing; and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh had come in a hundred and thirty-four days, and he had not invented one to fill the place where a seventh would stand if there were sevenths to stand. The told bell stood beside the heard bell and the two did not agree or disagree, being two objects in two places, four, four and a gap in each, with nobody crossing himself at either. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: the cup, the pot, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, each answered so nearly that the want under the note could be felt, and none of them filled.

The interval tray held its older intervals, Stone 13 and Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger at \lore{the damper}, the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check, and the breath a man drew at the top of a reach and did not spend. And now a fourth stopping of the near-saying lay among them: the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it, brought all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, no figure, no name. Four likenesses lay on that tray, and a man who lets four likenesses stand up together will find they have stood up and called themselves a doctrine by morning; he held them apart, which was an invisible labour and bought nothing anyone could enter. The damper's double bite was filed as an object joined to no kiln, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies and no standing to improve either. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side with no third, breath through the nose and breath in the throat, both saying not the war's hand, and the room off the shed kept its finger's width of open and gave up nothing.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold two inches from his ear with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third, and in the other room the shelf kept two gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word. The two rooms did not touch. The saying of the loaf-shelves held that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name, and no finger in the district had laid these together and no account had wanted a name. The charcoal marks under the shelf lay on a place of their own, pressed hard and faded nearly into the grain, a keeping put where nobody thought to look, and the finger that had gone beneath the board this afternoon had come away without a word and told him nothing of what the marks said. He did not lay the underside beside the gaps above it or beside the crust in the fold or beside anything else, and left the reason of the hand where it stood, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look.

The word forty stayed unspoken with the readable double turn beside it, tail tucked, tied left over right, the small weight of a thumb pressed in apart on its own place; the knot was a shape and not a figure. The cold foot below the sill stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused, unnamed. The fourth green at the silent woman's mouth stayed a colour and not a name, the fifth shape of it if the glaze at a thane's mouth was counted, on a place of its own and joined to no ear. \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages lay turned toward a floorboard three doors down in \place{House Silt} with her little tin shut in her bodice, and \person{Dessa Silt}'s round pewter tin above the abandoned bed kept its pale fold and thumb-bright lid shut; neither tin was opened and neither page was read. The soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the landing with his slate held where no other man could read it, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled and the seventh year had not given.

Every object was in its place and nothing was laid across anything else. It was the whole of his inventory and it was worth nothing. He was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it: a man who had once laid out instruments on a towel and known every one by the wear on its handle, now a child who could not close two inches of his hand on the rim of the basket two paces off, with a tray of records he could not lift to any table and no table that would have taken them. He had spent a hundred and thirty-four days learning that a likeness is not a cause and that four stoppings do not make a doctrine, and the keeping of the things apart was the only honest work in the room and the one no slate would ever enter. Exact, complete, worth nothing.

Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir} on \place{the Ilm}, a fifth low thing never counted among the full note; through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-four days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one. A man who lays three counts together and calls them one account has made a cause out of a room, and there was no room under this roof for another cause.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint so the cold found the bare place and went under the fold where nobody had been given an instruction to put a hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. One hundred and thirty-four days old, four months and fourteen days, he was told by nobody, and if somebody had said the number aloud nobody under this roof would have known what it counted, and he would not have known either what it counted, only that it was his and exact and unspent. It went on being exact in the dark while the house kept its three counts in three places. In the next room the row of loaves kept its two gaps and under the shelf the charcoal marks waited where they had been put, for a hand not his and a day that had not come.
