# Chapter 31 — beat sheet: *Four at the Teeth*

**Days covered.** Days 132–134, Reed 13–15, Y.H. 813, Thirdday to Fifthday. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived in full. Day 131 is finished and not revisited; Day 135 belongs to Chapter 32. The boy is one hundred and thirty-two, then thirty-three, then thirty-four days old — four months and twelve, thirteen and fourteen days — and nobody under the roof says so.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof Tarn brings the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt that holds it **a fourth time** all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there, with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray beside the first three as a **fourth interval and not a fourth payment**, an object among objects and not a rule, refusing to let four stoppings stand up and call themselves a doctrine. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year has still not given. A second change gathers rather than resolves: a **fourth green** at a silent woman's mouth, unnamed, silent, never Fenna in the narration — four at mouths and five shapes counting Berr's glaze, still a colour and not a name.

**What is withheld everywhere.** No calling of the loan, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no knot counted, no word forty spoken, no name or number from Dessa, no sale, no opening of tin or box, no body or cause for the missing hand, no reading of either private tally, no joining of the two empty places. T097 stays advanced and unpaid; T098, T099 and T100 stay carried or planted and unpaid. The old life stays offstage: no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from the boy's eyes, no Court, collector, Waiting Room, back-room interior, Ulda on stage, Fenna named in narration, or ending.

## Scene 1 — *What the Arm Remembers*

- **Day and time.** Day 132, Reed 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard and the reed beds below Low Kiln; the edge of the worked strips east of the yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; reed-cutters, a girl with a creel, a man who asks what the work will want, yard voices. Walk-ons only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Open on the morning of Day 132 with the reed month in its middle tend and the boy one hundred and thirty-two days old; Sella carries him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket.
  2. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look, a wide step, no number.
  3. The reed bundles come in armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter tells a girl with a creel to lift it and let her arm tell her what went into it and that this is the whole of what the arm knows.
  4. A man come up from the strips asks what the work will want before the tend is out; he is given the weather and the river, never a figure, and carries the unanswered thing away.
  5. The boy reads the carrying arm as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost — exact, complete, worth nothing — and the surgeon's register notes the order of reaching without giving him any standing to enter it.
  6. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down; the hand cannot reach the bundle, the pot or any of the places gone around.
- **Emotional turn.** From the ordinary work of the yard to the exactness of a record that cannot be spent: the boy's competence is a form of helplessness, and the day's arithmetic stays in arms and weather.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet wool and woodsmoke; green broken reed-ends and frost-pale tips; dry mud worked into wrists; river wind at the ear; the small wet sound of the wandering head.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (132 kept exact), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T060/T086.
- **Foreshadowing.** T098 gathers interest with another going-around; T077's cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The man who asked for a figure walks off downriver with the weather in his mouth, the girl's creel bumping her hip, and the boy's hand going up an inch toward the water and down.

## Scene 2 — *A Finger Under the Board*

- **Day and time.** Day 132 continued, midday into the afternoon.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, the boy in the basket; a neighbour come up from the strips with a wet pot. Walk-on only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket low and a little back near the bench leg, at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board a man standing sees only the top of; she tells the fire it was the thaw and gives the instruction to the pot and the broom and never to the basket.
  2. Above, the shelf keeps its row the way a house that keeps its days badly keeps a row: loaves pushed along, a glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word — in a room the fold does not touch.
  3. Standing with her back to the basket, moving her lips along the row, she reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge of the board where the charcoal tally lies, the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again, stays long enough for the knuckle to set and the thumb to come up against the grain, then brings the finger away without looking and without a word, and does not go back to the shelf.
  4. From the low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, press a thumb where her thumb had been, lay the underside beside the two gaps above it or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or make a cause of a hand that goes under a board without looking — such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and he leaves the reason where it stands.
  5. A neighbour come up from the strips with dry mud in her wrists and water standing in the creases asks what the row will want before the tend is out; Sella's hand goes toward the clay jar and comes away, and she sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and the woman carries the unanswered question away level.
  6. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs and is not touched, because a shelf and an afternoon are not openings; the clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and are called the thaw.
- **Emotional turn.** A second private record is handled and kept in front of him and remains unread — care that hides itself tells more by the hiding than the writing would, and the boy refuses to be the hand that joins it to anything.
- **Sensory anchors.** Dry warm smell of the shelf crossed by the sharper smell of charcoal for one breath; cold iron at his cheek where the bench bracket was bolted; the grey streak pale off the coals; the little-door burn closing on nothing.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (carried), T060/T086, T097, T058/T079, T077, T079.
- **Foreshadowing.** T100 gathers interest toward the far-off convergence of the district's private records; the finger-under-board habit stays unexplained, held for a hand not his. Nothing read, counted, copied, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The shelf keeps its two gaps above and its charcoal marks below, and the finger has come away without a word, and the two rooms of the house do not touch.

## Scene 3 — *The Cord Inside the Coat*

- **Day and time.** Day 132 continued, afternoon downriver, then the going of the light and the small hours.
- **Place.** The Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards; then the bed under the Vail roof.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the reed-cutter with the cord; Orra Weir, Mira Silt, a weir woman who asks, a buyer who names a figure. Walk-ons except Orra and Mira.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed work goes on in armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate.
  2. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle, the short length of grey wool or reed-fibre cord inside his coat gone grey with the oil of a working thumb; he sets the bundle on the flat stone, straightens the cord on his thumb, turns it once so the knots stand in the small light, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat.
  3. To the woman who asks what the cord holds he says *You can ask the cord* and, to *I can't read a cord*, *No more can the Court*; no figure read, no knot counted, no name, no joining to the missing hand or to the word forty.
  4. Orra Weir stands at the lower boards with her board on its side and a cord at her waist, pulls one knot down with her thumb and sets it among the others; her knot-string lies beside Mira's board and does not touch it, and the forty-first double turn — tail tucked, left over right, the small weight of a thumb — stays legible and unnamed, the word forty unspoken.
  5. Mira works the eels with slime dried grey on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, answers a thin run with her own answer, moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, and puts her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a buyer in a coat with the river in its hem names a figure; the second twist twisted twice stays beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child's knot untouched.
  6. At dusk Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace, goes around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it goes away; she tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, sets the pot rim out and handle toward the reaching hand and does not look toward the bed.
  7. Through the finger's-width door comes the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the six; through the floor under blind ash the carried fire takes its coal and gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-two days; overhead the boards keep four nails, four and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, none becoming one.
- **Emotional turn.** The day gathers its private records in materials no Court thumb can spell — cord, knot-string, grey wool — all visible and none enterable, and closes on the boy holding three counts apart in the dark.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey cord oiled by a working thumb; eel slime and silt at the neck; the bitten end of the cord; the carried fire's under-ash breath; the river's note through the door's finger's width.
- **Threads advanced.** T099 (carried), T024/T059/T025, T088, T071/T072, T058/T079, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing.** T099 and T024/T059/T025 gather toward the far convergence of private records; the payer of the carried fire goes unnamed one day further. Nothing counted, named, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down with one hundred and thirty-two exact, unspent and spoken by nobody.

## Scene 4 — *The Dust Ahead of the Fire*

- **Day and time.** Day 133, Reed 14, Fourthday, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard and the flagstones.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, Jorra Vail, Tarn, the boy in the basket; three or four unnamed men, a High Kiln kiln-master. Walk-ons except the three family/house characters.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey three or four men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and speak low in the room off the shed, whose door keeps its finger's width of open; Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, does not turn her head toward the door, gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger, not bone-coloured slip, keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone (*It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*), tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who would call it early and make something of it.
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and the end of her instruction arrives at the pot.
  5. The boy reads the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies, files it as an object worth nothing joined to no kiln, and keeps the two tellings of the shut counting room beside it with no third telling added.
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed, says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying; his seventh year is pressed past another tend and still held, the knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the breath from Seed 16 still unspent, the unforged collector sentence beside it.
  7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check; the broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, entered by nobody; neither man names the other's silence and the loan stays uncalled.
- **Emotional turn.** The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to: the iron read perfectly and worth nothing, the two silences kept apart, the year pressed further toward an end that has not come.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust like meal worked past the bent finger; cold iron taking the heat out of a fingertip; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone; the sweetish edge of lead glaze on the kiln-master's coat; Ash in the creases of Tarn's knuckles the colour of the clean dark line.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031/T062, T065/T095, T036/T076, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing.** T062's double bite stays an object joined to no kiln; T065/T095 carry two tellings with no third (no fourth telling owed); T036's seventh year presses on and must still give at its end. Nothing spoken, named, called or spent.
- **Exact last image.** Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check while the stones keep the shallow bright patch where it had stopped, and the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and down.

## Scene 5 — *The Fourth Stopping*

- **Day and time.** Day 133 continued, late afternoon at the landing, then the going of the light into the small hours.
- **Place.** The out-of-line landing below the north bridge; the hearth and kindling under the Vail roof.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, the boy; the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk at the landing, market voices. Walk-ons except the house characters.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm and hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells its reeds and eels over the top of him, nobody steps around him and nobody takes him off (a measure set down before anybody called it is set down ahead of something, and the boy makes a shape of it and not a rule).
  2. At the hearth the coals are banked and the small knife is set back where it can be checked; Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, with six years in the worn thumb-mark and the seventh held at the very edge.
  3. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes **the fourth time** all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there with no figure and no name.
  4. He draws the breath that should go in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom; the small interval at the top of each reach shows where the breath should have gone in.
  5. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room, and the two silences stay unjoined.
  6. The boy files the fourth stopping on the interval tray beside the first three as a **fourth interval and not a fourth payment**, an object among objects and not a rule. Four likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if nobody holds them apart; he holds them apart, an invisible labour worth nothing anyone could enter. The loan stays uncalled and the seventh year has still not given.
  7. The carried fire keeps its four low pulls and its silent fifth under blind ash, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-three days.
- **Emotional turn.** The loan comes to the word and is kept there a fourth time; the boy's whole gift is the refusal to let a held thing become a doctrine, and that refusal buys nothing and is the only honest work in the room.
- **Sensory anchors.** Banked coals under the back of a broken pot; the pale clip-ring at Tarn's left wrist holding the light; the small flat knock of the knife set on its sill; the washed cuff going grey again by midday.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (fourth stopping withheld), T061/T082, T090/T080/T083, T088, T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing.** T036 advances one interval further — a kept word is a debt and a debt that can hear can be called; something must give at the end of the seventh year and has not given here. T090's clerk stays set ahead of a death. Nothing called, spent, paid or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The breath goes in where it should not and is not spent, the broom goes on over the banked fire, and on the interval tray a fourth stopping lies beside three others as an object and not a rule.

## Scene 6 — *Four Greens and a Cold Foot*

- **Day and time.** Day 134, Reed 15, Fifthday, morning at the Cross and the market stalls, then below the sill at the fish-trade shade.
- **Place.** The Cross, the pot board and market stalls, the fish-trade shade below the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy, Mira Silt, Orra Weir, Berr Clay; the green-mouthed woman, a buyer who names a figure, the woman who wrapped the cold foot, the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing. Walk-ons except the four sheeted characters.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. A silent woman stands across the square with the sweetish lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth, clay under her nails and a pale line across the back of one hand where something was once worn tight and long enough to leave its mark; she turns a cup a quarter on the board, sets it back where it stood and goes off with the light. She is unnamed, given no dialogue and never called Fenna in the narration.
  2. This is the **fourth** green at a mouth and the **fifth** shape of the colour counting Berr Clay's glaze at his own mouth, filed beside the Silt 30, Seed 28 and Reed 7 greens on places that do not touch — four at mouths, five shapes, still a colour and not a name, no ear spoken with it and no joining to the pale line, the pots, the eel boards, the clerk or a box up the clay road.
  3. Berr comes up short with the red office cord folded under his coat and the green at one mouth-corner, wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, buys eels two marks short and says *A number goes into a slate, not a bundle*, taking himself out of his own sentence with *your house*, never *I*. His own green stays on its own tray beside the twice-wiped thumb and is not joined to the woman's.
  4. Mira answers a question about the run with her own answer (*Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river*), moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, and puts her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when the buyer names a figure; the second twist twisted twice stays beneath it, never upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted, her board beside Orra Weir's and not touching.
  5. Orra pulls one knot down with her thumb and gives a date where a figure was asked, keeping the word forty unspoken with the forty-first double turn legible and unnamed; she gets no answer from the basket and asks none.
  6. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard; the surgeon's reading of the extremity going before the core works and buys nothing, and no silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it.
  7. The soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the landing with his slate for a measure nobody called, and the market sells over him.
- **Emotional turn.** The colour gathers a fourth mouth without becoming a face or a name, and the district's withholdings — short marks, knots, weather, a wrapped foot — all stay in materials and phrases no slate can enter.
- **Sensory anchors.** Sweetish green at a mouth corner; clay under nails and a pale cord-mark on the back of a hand; silt drying pale in the hollow of Mira's neck; grey cord under an apron; three turns of wool round a dark foot.
- **Threads advanced.** T050/T051 (fourth green), T015/T041/T047, T059/T025/T024, T093/T094/T096, T090/T080.
- **Foreshadowing.** T050/T051 gathers a fourth green toward the far day an ear will be spoken with that colour; the woman is not Fenna in narration and gets no dialogue. T093/T094/T096 carry the cold foot, uncured and unnamed, toward Arc 3. Nothing named, counted, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The woman goes off with the light and the green at her mouth goes with her, the buyer's figure goes into the noise of the stalls and is gone, and the dark foot stays dark inside three turns of wool below the sill.

## Scene 7 — *One Hundred and Thirty-Four*

- **Day and time.** Day 134 continued, afternoon under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf, then the going of the light into the small hours on the bed.

  2.   4. ## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 32

The chapter closes on Day 134 with the count one hundred and thirty-four exact and unspent, the hand going up an inch toward the river's note and down, and nothing joined, paid, named, opened, called or ended. The fourth stopping lies on the interval tray beside three others and is not a doctrine; the loan is uncalled and the seventh year has still not given. The green is a colour on its own place, the fourth at a mouth and the fifth shape with Berr's, and no ear has been spoken with it. The reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's two cords, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin and the charcoal under the Vail shelf stay unread in materials no Court thumb can spell; the missing hand's place is still gone around with a look and no figure; the cold foot stays dark in three turns of wool; the clerk stays set for an uncalled measure. Chapter 32 opens on Day 135, Reed 16, Sixthday, with the boy one hundred and thirty-five days old and nobody saying so, the reed month going into its last third, the second Account-day four days off, both private tallies still unread and the near-saying held at the word. Forward, not closed.
