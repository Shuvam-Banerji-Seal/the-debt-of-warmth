\daymark{132}{Reed 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he reed month} was in the middle of its middle tend and the morning came up over \place{Low Kiln} the way a pot comes up into the light from under a bench --- a little at a time, with the shadow going first and the rim after it and the whole belly last. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} out at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going wide around the stones and the laid-down pans and anything that had been set out to dry, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. Keep it off his ears; it had come down with them and would go back up with them, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing. Woodsmoke went before her down the yard and wet wool went with her, and under both the faint sour mineral tang of the \lore{Ash} that had gathered in the corners of this house for two years and would not be swept. He was one hundred and thirty-two days old, four months and twelve days, and nobody in the yard said so and nobody under any roof in the district knew there was a number to say.

The yard received the morning the way a floor receives a bucket set down in it: without remark, keeping the shape of what had been there before. \place{the Clay Yard} had the colour of last week's mud worked into a brown skin, and the reeds stood beyond \place{the drying racks} in long grey ranks where the barley had stood a month before, broken ends green, frost-bitten tips pale as old bone. A woman at a tub lifted her head as the basket passed, saw a basket, and went back to her work without appearing to have seen it. Sella went around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned, and around the new finer hairline of grey that had gathered beside it and been called \lore{the thaw}, with a care that looked like not seeing either one. She did not sweep them. A line left where it was was still a line kept, and the house said the grey would go when it went, and the boy had learned by now that a thing the house said would go was a thing the house had decided not to carry out.

At the drying racks she went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered it no more than she had entered it on any of the hundred and thirty-one days before. Then they came to the edge of \place{the worked strips}, and there was the other place.

It was not a hole and it was not a mark. It was a space in the returning line where, on Seed 15, a hand had gone in with the seed and on Seed 27 had not come out with the rest. Sella went around it with a look and a wide step and no number, the way the yard had gone around it every morning since, and the boy felt the half-pace change by the width of a hand as she shifted the basket away from it. No name went with the step. No cause, no body, no figure, no payer. If a hand came up out of the ground in a month or a year a slate would be found and a figure made to stand at the foot of it; until then the space would go on being gone around, and the month would go on standing short. A cost with no figure was still a cost. He had spent a hundred and thirty-two days learning that this was so, and had no word in \lore{Grathic} in which to say it to anybody and no voice with which to say it if he had.

He set the going-around on the place where it already lay, beside but never among the shelf's two gaps and the crust in the fold two rooms away. There was a saying of the loaf-shelves of Low Kiln that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name, and a saying could be carried a long way on one breath if a person were careless with it. A neighbour had once laid a planting and a child's appetite under one cause and gone off with her pot without seeing that she had joined anything. A line drawn through this ground and that shelf and that fold would be a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent his whole first life declining to be and the whole of this one with no hand to be it with. He kept the three places apart without once deciding to, the way a man keeps his feet on a floor that is not level, and the keeping bought nothing and was the only work in the yard that anybody had not asked him to do.

His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Nobody had been given an instruction about that sound. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread either, so it stayed where it had been put, stiff and doing nothing. The river wind came off \place{the Ilm} and found the bare joint where the wool had ridden back, and cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The child in him was cold at the ear and wanted what was two inches off and could not be reached; the man in him read the half-pace and the wide step and the order in which the yard went around its absences. Both were true. The second was quieter and the first was louder, and neither could be handed to anybody.

\scenebreak

The reeds came in armfuls from the beds below the yard, going-downriver and coming-back, green at the broken ends and grey at the frost-pale tips, laid in the ruts and judged by the arm that carried them and by nothing set on a slate. A bundle went past on a girl's shoulder with the butt of it wet against her hip and the cut ends nodding as she walked; a man behind her carried two and swung them so the tips cleared the rack, and a third came along with one arm loaded and the other held in against his coat for the wind. Nobody counted them where a figure could be taken down. A day's cutting was a going and a coming-back, and the cost of it was in the shoulder and not in the hand that wrote.

A cutter come up from the beds set a bundle on the ground and waded into the middle of a girl with a creel who had been looking at it as a pot is looked at before it is lifted. “Lift it,” he said. She put both hands under it and got it up, and the green ends shivered. “Your arm'll tell you. It'll tell you what went into it, and that's the whole of what it knows.”

“And what it cost?” she said.

“It doesn't know cost. Ask it how heavy it is. That's a question it can answer.” He took the bundle back off her and set it where it had stood, with the cut ends toward the yard. “There's your figure. There's the whole of the account.”

He went off downriver with the question unanswered behind him, and the girl took up her creel and went after the others with it bumping her hip.

The boy watched the arm that carried the bundle. It was a working arm, corded at the forearm and pale where the reed-water ran off it, and it knew exactly what it held: how many green ends, how much weight at the butt, how far the load sat from the shoulder and where it would balance if the ground rose under it. That was a record, and it was exact, and it told the truth about what had been carried. It told nothing at all about what the carrying cost. The man with two bundles would be stiff at the shoulder by dusk and would not set that down on any slate, and the girl's creel would bruise the same place on her hip tomorrow and there was no figure in the world for it. Exact, complete, worth nothing. He had once stood at the head of a table and read a body the way these people read an arm, and the reading had told him what was wrong and had not told him who would pay for the fixing. The register worked the same here. He noted the order of reaching --- the arm to the bundle before the eye to the cutter, the hand to the record before the hand to the person --- and had no standing to enter it. A surgeon's eye in a basket that could not close two inches on its own rim. He kept the observation and let it go, as he had let a hundred others go, because an observation kept where nobody could enter it was the only kind he had ever been permitted to keep.

\scenebreak

A man come up from the strips with dry mud worked into his wrists and standing in the creases, and stood at the edge of the going-downriver with his hands loose and his mouth open on the question. “What'll the work want before the tend's out?” he said. “There's hands enough today. There'll be hands enough at the end of it. Somebody wants a figure.”

The wind took the last of it toward the beds. Sella did not turn from putting the fold straight at the boy's ear, which she did without once looking at his face. “The weather and the river,” she said. “Ask them. They'll tell you when they've had enough.”

“That isn't a figure.”

“The house has said it.” Her hands went on with the wool. “A number goes into a slate, not into a strip and not into a bundle. You want a number, go ask the river to give you one. It'll give you the weather.”

The man stood a moment with the unanswered thing in his mouth, the way a man stands with a cup he has not set down because there is nowhere on a bench to set it. Then he shut his hands and carried it away downriver, going the way the reeds went, with the weather in his mouth and no figure in it. A cost asked in the form of a direction had been answered with a direction and had left no entry. The boy had watched that done in a market and at a well and at a loaf shelf, and here it was done again in the open yard, as ordinary as sweeping and just as final. The man's wrists went grey with drying mud as he walked off. Nobody called after him. Nobody would have entered his question if they had.

The district had one word for what it would not carry and one past tense for what it would not own. The word was weather and the past tense was the thaw that was, and between them a person could go home warm at dusk without ever having owned the day that made them so. Hands went into the strips and did not come out and it was the weather; a ridge grew an inch in a corner and it would go when it went; a room shut in the last war with the damper closed and men dead of the breath was eighteen leagues east and a curiosity with a date, not the war's hand. The boy kept the man's unanswered question on a place of its own as a shape and not a rule, because a shape laid beside three other shapes will stand up and call itself a doctrine if there is nobody to hold them apart, and he had spent a hundred and thirty-two days being the person who held them apart. It was an invisible labour and it bought nothing anyone could have entered, and the yard went on with its going-downriver as if nothing at all had been decided in it.

The yard went back to its going-downriver. The girl's creel went bumping after the others toward the beds, and the reeds went over the ruts in armfuls, and the place in the returning line where the hand had not come out went on being gone around by anybody who came near it, with a look and a wide step and no number. The sun came higher and the frost-pale tips lost their pale and went green in the light. A pot somewhere under the Vail roof gave its note as it was set down and the note went where it sounded and was not made into a seventh. Six hearings stood where they had stood, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among; a pot under a roof was a pot under a roof and not an answer to a question nobody had asked it. The boy kept them where they were and went on being carried.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water coming off the Ilm, and came down. It could not reach the bundle going past on the girl's shoulder, or the pot under the roof, or the clean dark line and the finer grey in the corner, or the space beneath the lowest rack, or the empty place in the returning line that Sella had just gone around. It could not close on the creel, or the wool at his own ear, or the crust two inches off in the fold. One hundred and thirty-two days old, four months and twelve days, he was told by nobody and would not have known what the number counted if somebody had. The man who had asked for the figure walked off downriver with the weather in his mouth, the girl's creel bumped her hip and went on bumping it out of sight among the ranks of reeds, and the hand went up an inch toward the water and came down with the day exact and unspent and spoken by nobody.
