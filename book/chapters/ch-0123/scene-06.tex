The afternoon came on without an announcement, the way an afternoon comes under a roof that keeps its days in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. \person[Corin]{The boy} was carried down the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the small sounds came first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: a pail set on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set, the rope settling into its knot with the stiff dry rasp that cold puts into hemp, a loaf pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped with nobody calling the stopping a figure, barley passed from wall to wall and never made into a number. Nothing in the yard had been told what the day was, and nobody in it was counting on behalf of a basket.

He had once stood at a bay door in a country that would not have understood a yard kept in loaves, and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive; here he sorted a yard by what it would not say. The sorting bought him nothing but the order of things, and the order of things was the only instrument he had. A woman waiting her turn at the well shifted a pot from one hip to the other and said the cold had got into the rope and would not come out before the thaw, and a woman at the rail said a rope that held was a rope that held, and went back to her pail. He took the order apart by the place of each part --- the hand on the rope, the eye on the pail, the mouth after both, the half-beat lying in the gap --- and gave the words no owner beyond the mouths they came out of. He looked for an intention in the gap and found none standing; a lesser observer would have put one there and called it a reason, and a reason wants a payer where none stood. The small sounds went on. The large ones were weather.

He went around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number, once in the afternoon as he had at the morning: the stone lip of \place{the yard well}, the worn boards, the strip of hard mud, the place where a wheel had stood, the two narrow gaps between the stacked pots, and \place{the returning-line place}, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign, for a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. He did not look twice; looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there. At \place{the trough} the thin skin that had lain on the water through the turn was gone, and the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away from it, a ring on a place of its own; he did not lay that ring across the loaf's finger's-width stopping or across the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, the one the house called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; beside them the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the skin, and the tang came thinner on that edge; he found the softening where put and called it weather, and did not look twice, and did not make it a sign, a custom, a fourth covering or a payment on a day that had earned only its twenty-eighth. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform, and the afternoon gave him two inches of wool and the length of a woman's back turned at a hearth.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A number came up out of a shut fist toward the well, held where the light could see it, was looked at, and went back into a sleeve and arrived nowhere; a woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool at \place{the rack end}, and the figure read over it in its own morning did not know what it cost, and no signature stood on the space between figure and price. Paper is not a payer, and the key to \person[Berr Clay]'s box had gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. He took each arrival apart by the order of its parts --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap --- and gave the fist no figure and the sleeve no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. Two arrivals that reached nobody did not become one arrival by being laid beside each other.

The offered figure and its return were found where put on a place of its own beside \person{Ilva}'s ninth record-first hand and the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, with a finger between every pair; fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat lying in the gap, the figure that had looked at the light and gone back into the sleeve and arrived nowhere. It was not offered again, not spent, not named, not made a witness, not made a second reaching by being remembered, and not joined to the fist-to-nowhere number, the note-not-price, the asking answered with the weather, the clerk's entered figure, the second man's turned slate, the face at the yard's edge, \person{Tarn}'s degree or \person[Jorra Vail]'s ungiven promise. Ilva was offstage with her count still in wood, the strip in its fold in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth, and she was not made a reader by a reach in an empty yard.

Tarn was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, telling the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} once and not twice, found where put and not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a custom and not a rule; the loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended, and the valuable ear was exposure and not safety. Jorra was at \place{the Vail wheel-shed} and the cold \place{the damper}, parting dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, speaking to the wheel before any person and withholding the full note he knew three turns ahead from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call a pot before its time; \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn. Each kept what he kept, and none of it came into the yard as a sign or a lesson.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it, found where put and called weather; it was not a hearing, not one of \lore{the three coverings}, not a seventh of anything and not a payer, and no hand stood under it. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering; the note ended on its own finishing before any hand came down. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had gone unspoken four hundred and twenty-two days and the fifth pull had gone unpaid the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; no softening under a river's skin and no number out of a fist supplied either count with a payer or a payment.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket at the going of the light, and the household straightened around the order of it as it had at the morning. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay, and the coals gave off the dry smell of a fire banked and not forgotten. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. \emph{The house will keep it} went to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning, an afternoon, a shelf, a yard and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its twenty-eighth.

She said to the fire, in the tone of a woman who had told the same thing to the same fire for three winters, \emph{The fire doesn't know you're sorry. Feed it}, and put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, and did not look to see whether the basket had heard. Her hands went on counting when they had nothing to do --- three spoons, a nail, the small breath a child makes that nobody numbers --- and came back to the hairline at the wire as hands come back to a wound they have dressed. At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool. She turned it piece by piece with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece down where it had lain, took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

At the carrying-up she went nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket. In the yard she went around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number, widest at the returning-line place, and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. Beside the crust the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put and still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. The fire kept, and the house went on keeping with it, though a house keeps badly what it cannot count.

The small hours came without a door opening, and \place[the towel]{the towel} lay at the head of the bed as instruments lie when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. The hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid at the last answer. Four hundred and nineteen exact, and the count standing at four hundred and twenty-two, and nobody under any roof knowing what a count was. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside them on its own ground; the knock was not made a hearing and the seventh place was not supplied. The bell heard from \place{the Cross} kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and its gap was not folded into the carried fire's four and a gap below the boards, or the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or the two readings of the year behind one unsigned figure beside the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 --- three counts in three places staying three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Four rooms kept the same gap with no Court room made a fifth likeness; nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings with no tenth, and he gave no name to the gap after the ninth. He had learned that a gap left unnamed costs nothing and that the naming of it always sends a man looking for the hand that made it; he did not look.

\person[Berr Clay]'s box had gone up \place{the clay road} locked with the two readings of the year and the true subtractions inside, and the key had gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down; paper is not a payer, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. \person[Hessa Rale]{Hessa}'s narrow slate stayed unread up the road with her eighth hand eighth, and Ilva's short strip of notched birch stayed unread in a sleeve in the yard, her hand ninth with no tenth made of it. The clerk's entered figure stayed reported at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, a figure entered before the eyes went up, on a place of its own with a finger between it and Tarn's degree toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard's edge --- a report brought up the yard and never a sighting, not the calling of the loan, not a tenth hand and not a rule. The asking answered with the weather stayed beside the second man's direction down the bridge road and his narrow slate turned to the sleeve, and the fifth shaping sat on its tray between the eighth hand and the road saying, with a third finger between it and the four ownerless directions. No joining stood among them, no witness, no reader, no sale, no tenth hand and no paying. The offered figure kept its own place with a finger between it and the ninth hand and the fifth shaping, not a sale, not a reader, not a tenth hand, not a payer, not an account and not joined to the asking, the clerk's entered figure, the turned slate or the face; the keeping at the fold was found where put and still a finding, with no second fold, third crust, name, gift, payment or payer.

Answer four hundred and twenty came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the two of the new count, the printed figure, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the winter skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, 415 through 420, two to each of the three days, and not one was a doctrine. He set the file down where the next hand would find it without looking, and there was no next hand in the room.

Four hundred and twenty-two exact, six months and two hundred and forty-two days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. He did not stand it up as a threshold or a figure and borrowed no payer from the arithmetics gone up the road, for paper is not a payer; a number counted is a number with air around it, and air buys no warmth. No eighth opening, seventh hearing, sixth shaping, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic, signature, payer, witness or reader stood on any of the three days. Ilmen 30 stood two mornings off, a date and not a day earned, and he did not bring it into the small hours of a day that had earned only its twenty-eighth. The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side and neither bought the other; under them, louder, the wanting went on --- the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle --- and got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor did not touch his.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, the inch not a reach; he took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended --- neither early nor stayed --- and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. The figure went back into the sleeve and the stick kept its notch; the well gave back the weather; the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept --- forward, into a morning not yet lived and not brought into a day that had earned only its twenty-eighth.
