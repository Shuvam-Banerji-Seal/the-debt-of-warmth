The afternoon came into \place{the Clay Yard} the way an afternoon comes into a yard that has not been told what the day is: by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set, and the rope settled into its knot with the stiff dry rasp that cold puts into hemp, a grainless sound that carried no more intention than the settling of a board. At \place{the yard well} a woman waiting her turn shifted a pot from one hip to the other and said that a rope would take a finger if a body was careless, and another at the rail said cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and went on about her business. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped there, and nobody called the stopping a figure. Barley went from wall to wall and was never made into a number. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in his basket at the yard's edge where the light came sideways, and had not been told the day's name, and the basket had not been told to be still.

He took the yard as a field at rest, the way he had once taken a ward at three in the morning: not by what anybody said was wrong with it, but by what it was doing while nobody watched. The pail on its stone. The rope in its knot. The small animal sound a hinge makes when the cold has it by the throat. These did not lie because they were not trying to say anything. The large sounds were the wind off \place{the river} and the note under the winter skin and the talk that went along the bucket-round, and the wind was weather and the talk was mostly weather too. He gave the yard its small sounds first and its large sounds afterward, and kept the order, because an order is the only instrument a man has when he has no hands for the work.

Once, and not twice, he went around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number. The stone lip of the well. The worn boards. The strip of hard mud. The place where a wheel had stood. The two narrow gaps between the stacked pots. And \place{the returning-line place}, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign, for a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. He did not look at any place a second time: looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there, and on a day that had earned only its twenty-seventh there was no such hand to find. He went the round once in the afternoon as he had gone it once in the morning, and did not make two rounds of it by calling the second a checking. A checking is a custom wearing a clean coat, and a custom wants a payer where none stood.

\place[The trough]{The trough} stood bare and dark. The thin skin that had lain on it through the turn was gone, and the rim kept the line where the water had been, a lower country marked on the side of a vessel. The gone pan held its shallow round print on a place of its own, and he did not lay that ring across the loaf's stopping or across the ridge at the corner, for three things in three places are three things and not a row. At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, the one called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold, with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. The called-gone grey stood beside the left-in-place ridge with a finger between them. No agreement had been made to stop looking at the ridge; nobody had swept it and nobody owned the day on which it had been left, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. In this afternoon's air the tang came thinner off the corner than it had at morning, and the grey lay a hair less distinct along one stretch where the light fell across it. He found the softening where he had put it and did not look at it a second time. He did not make it a sign, a fourth of \lore{the three coverings}, a sixth shaping or a payment on a day that had earned only its twenty-seventh. The air was less cold at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the skin; that was weather, and weather arrives nowhere and is owed nothing. The ridge kept its place. Nothing under any roof had been paid to make it keep its place, and nothing had been paid to have it go.

Costs came to the yard as directions and went away as weather and left no entry behind them. A man come down \place{the clay road} brought a number up out of a shut fist toward the well, held it where the afternoon light could see it, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, where it arrived nowhere. He had given the number to no slate and the slate to no person; the hand had gone fist, eye, sleeve, and the half-beat had lain in the gap where a motive would have stood had there been one. The boy gave the fist no figure and the sleeve no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price; the note was an instruction to the clay and did not name what the clay wanted. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured, and the figure that had been read over it in its own morning had not known what the grain cost, and no signature stood on the space between the figure and the price. Paper is not a payer, and the key to the box of true subtractions had gone up the road in a coat pocket that never came back down.

\person{Ilva} was not at the well this afternoon; her count was still in wood and her hand was still her ninth, and the strip of notched birch lay in its fold in her sleeve where no slate had taken a figure off it. The offered figure and its return stayed where they had been put on a place of its own beside her ninth hand and the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, with a finger between every pair --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap, the figure that looked at the light and went back into the sleeve and arrived nowhere. It was not offered again on a day that had earned only its twenty-seventh, not made a custom, not a sale, not a tenth hand, not a reader made of the wood, not a witness, not a payer and not an account. Two arrivals that reached nobody do not become one arrival by being set side by side on the same afternoon. He kept the offered figure where it was and did not spend it, and kept the well's answer where it was, and the day went on without a teaching under any roof.

The clerk's entered figure stayed reported at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, a figure entered before the eyes went up, on a place of its own with a finger between it and \person{Tarn}'s degree toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard's edge --- a report brought up the yard and never a sighting, not the calling of the loan, not a tenth hand and not a rule. The first man's face stayed on its own place beside the older hands, a patient face that could wait in a doorway without shifting its weight, and nobody had asked it what it wanted. The second man's direction down the bridge road and his narrow slate turned to the sleeve stayed where they had been put, and the asking answered with the weather stayed beside them with a finger between every pair; the asking was a reaching with no hand under it, gathering interest toward a far-off calling and a far-off hand, and was not that calling, not a second sighting, not a sixth shaping and not a door. The fifth shaping sat on its tray between the eighth hand and the road saying, with a third finger between it and the four ownerless directions. The offered figure was not laid across any of these, and no hearing, witness, reader, sale, tenth hand or paying stood under any of them. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had gone unspoken four hundred and twenty-one days and his fifth pull below \place{the boards} had gone unpaid the same number, \lore{the carried fire} and the man standing side by side without becoming one; neither the figure gone into a sleeve nor the softening at the river's skin had supplied a payer to either count. The loan stayed uncalled.

Tarn was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones} when the afternoon went over them, and the household went on around him as it always went when he was doing what he was told. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away the whole of the checking. In the tone of a man stating the weather he told the wood, \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, cutting nothing, counting nothing, spending nothing. The sentence was found where put and was not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, a payment, a hearing or a rule; it did not gather a second silence on an unearned day, for a second silence would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. His chest had been tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was, his stroke over the flagstones going without the half-check. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to the word that would call the loan and went no further, a remembered stopping with no tenth, the \emph{Seed 16} breath unspent and the loan uncalled in a term already ended. A term ending is exposure and not freedom, and leaves a man more open than it found him. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction and said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire. He did not turn his head toward the landing; the degree stayed a shape found where put and was not made a second time. The fear of being entered again for vagrancy stayed his without a figure, and so did the want he did not voice for a slate with his own name and no mark against it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened --- not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person, \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time, for a pot called early is a pot whose note has been spent before it was earned and no master under that roof had ever asked who paid for the singing. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence. One bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies; he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth, and was left standing so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting. He took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn. The boy filed Tarn's withheld sentence and Jorra's withheld knowing on places of their own with a finger between, two quiet men under one roof and not a custom, joined to neither the offered figure gone back into the sleeve nor any payer.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and \place{the Drawing house}'s small flat knock outside them on its own ground; the knock was not made a hearing and the seventh place was left as it was left. The bell heard from \place{the Cross} kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and neither gap was folded into another square. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it. The boy found it where put and called it weather. It was not a hearing, not a sign, not a fourth of the three coverings, not a seventh of anything and not a payer; no hand stood under it and nobody in the yard had been asked to answer for it. The note ended on its own finishing, and he did not make a hearing of it afterward by remembering where it had ended.

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay, and the coals gave off the dry smell of a fire banked and not forgotten. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. \emph{The house will keep it} went to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. An afternoon, a yard, a shelf, a hearth and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its twenty-seventh.

She said to the fire, in the tone of a woman who has told the same thing to the same fire for three winters, \emph{The fire doesn't know you're sorry. Feed it}, and put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, and did not look to see whether the basket had heard. Her hands went on counting when they had nothing to do --- three spoons, a nail, the small breath a child makes that nobody numbers --- and came back to the hairline at the wire as hands come back to a wound they have dressed. At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool. She turned it piece by piece with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece down where it had lain, took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

At the carrying-up she went nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. In the Clay Yard she went around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number, widest at the returning-line place, and set him under the roof at \place{the Vail kiln-house} where the light came sideways and never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. \lore[the keeping at the fold]{The keeping at the fold} was found where put and stayed a finding. The thread remained stiff and unwiped. No word came up at the well a second time on this day, no collector came up or went down the road, and no sixth of anything stood under any roof.

In the small hours \place[The towel]{The towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the field by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for or filled, and the Drawing's flat knock outside them on its own ground. The heard bell kept its square beside the told, four, four and a gap, and its gap was not folded into the carried fire's four and a gap below the boards, or the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or the two readings of the year behind one unsigned figure beside the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813 --- three counts in three places staying three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Four rooms kept the same gap with no Court room a fifth likeness; nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings with no tenth; the fire's four low pulls breathed below the floor with the silence where the fifth should have stood and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. Garl's name and the unpaid fifth stood side by side without becoming one. The offered figure stayed on its own place with a finger between it and the ninth hand and the fifth shaping, not a sale, not a reader, not a tenth hand, not a payer, not an account and not joined to the asking, the clerk's entered figure, the turned slate or the face. He did not stand four hundred and twenty-one up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure, and did not borrow a payer from the arithmetics gone up the road, for paper is not a payer. Six months and two hundred and forty-one days were told by nobody who would have known what a number counted.

Answer four hundred and eighteen came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the two of the new count, the printed figure, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the winter skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Ten honest answers stood on the sixth place, 409 through 418, two to each of the five days behind them, and not one was a doctrine.

The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side and neither bought the other. Under them, louder, the wanting went on --- the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle --- and got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor did not touch his. The hand outside the wool went up the inch toward the river's note, the inch not a reach, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended --- neither early nor stayed --- and came straight down and lay flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. The offered figure was kept where put on its own place beside the ninth hand and the fifth shaping, the stick kept its notch, the well had given back the weather, and four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, the crust's corner sat a hair off the wool, and the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept --- forward, into a morning not yet lived and not brought into a day that had earned only its twenty-seventh.
