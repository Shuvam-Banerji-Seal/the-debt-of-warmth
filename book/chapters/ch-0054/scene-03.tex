\daymark{214}{Grain 5, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}

The fifth morning of Grain's first tend came up under the Vail roof not as an announcement but as a change in the air at the ear. First the smell: hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, a smell with a season's name and no cause anybody had been asked to give it. Then the sound, or rather the place where the sound failed: beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting the last go short, and then stopped. Not a bell. A bell is struck and counted and answered, and this drawing stopped where it stopped and asked nobody to cross himself. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and fourteen days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. They lay in their three places and kept their own company, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

\person[Corin]{The boy} was two hundred and fourteen days old and six months and thirty-four days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number had gone so quiet that he could no longer hear it asking, but it was there all the same, a coal under ash, and he did not rake it for the sake of seeing it glow. Grain's first working week held no market and no Account-day; the first of either waited nine mornings off on Grain 10, with the seventh green still a colour and the foundling still a shape, owed and not come early. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} he lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, where the underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped where his hand stopped, and he did not ask the roof to be longer.

One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place unnamed, and no third came to stand beside it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back hot clay under tallow, wet wool, the sour corner breath. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} a kiln-hand come up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind stood with his collar holding the cold out and the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} at the back of his throat. He had come to say a thing about a pot, and he said it to the yard and not to the basket.

“That one that was singing before its time last month,” he said. “Not heard from since.”

The boy lay two paces off and took the saying apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound. He had no knife and no towel, but the order of the laying was the whole of the discipline, and he laid it out: a direction with no hand at the foot of it, no owner, no room, no cause and no figure. He had learned to ask two questions of every injury and to ask them in order --- what has been done, and what is doing still --- and he asked them of the saying now. Something had been done to a pot; something was doing still in the silence after it; and between the two there stood no mechanism he could put a finger on and no man who would own to having set it going. The old saying had been general, a proverb about pots that sing early and generally sing themselves out early; it had gone around the yards for weeks and cost no pot. This one had a date in it and a silence. That was the change, a hair's breadth, from a rule about the behaviour of pots to the report of one pot that had stopped being reported. He turned it over and looked at the underside. Nothing lay there. A saying is a vessel with air in it; get a hand under it and it lifts easily and gives back no weight, and a man who takes that for proof of something has already forged the finding.

He set it on the tray where the near-full answers lay, beside the pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, and beside the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back. He did not promote it into the hearings. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person and no seventh; a silence was not a hearing, and a report of a silence was not a second hearing of the same pot. No seventh came on the fifth morning of Grain and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The knowing gathered interest on the tray nobody under any roof could read, and the interest was not a payment and drew no payer out of the air.

The sweet edge of lead glaze had come up off High Kiln on the wrong wind and touched the fold and gone out again on the wind that brought it. He took it as a direction and not as a second edge paid. A breath of air out of another quarter is a fact with a place and a going; it is not a cause, not a payment, not a second green at a mouth, and no lesson stood up out of it. The wrong wind went on going out of the yard and the sweetness went with it, and the back of his throat kept the taste a moment longer than the wind kept the air.

\person[Tarn Vail] passed the yard's edge with the broom not yet in his hand, waiting for the instruction, his chest tight and grey \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He did not look toward the basket and the boy did not look toward him. Two ears under one roof had kept the same silence for years, and neither of them had ever put a word between the two.

\scenebreak

\person[Jorra Vail] came through from the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} stayed entered by nobody. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the burned-slate word that had come over the boards out of \place{Slate Row} stayed a direction with no hand at the foot of it, and the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands. That knowing was the thing he kept back: a nearly-full answer with air under the note and a want in it, held on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them. The want in it was not hunger and not command; it was the small lift a vessel gives when the hand under it is about to be taken away, and he had felt it in pots for three years and had never once said what it was. He had known the note of the pot that sang early last month three turns before it left his hands, and he had withheld it then from a man come up from High Kiln on a wrong wind whose saying had stayed a saying and cost no pot. Now the saying had changed by a hair and he let it stand as a saying. He gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188 and made no lesson of the green on the wind. No pot was lost in the yard on the fifth morning of Grain.

When \person{Sella Vail}'s voice went low and unfinished over the fire his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered holding and not a change made again. The iron was one piece with two bites and there was no \lore{Grathic} word that carried both mercies in one mouthful: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He grew quieter whenever a word from another quarter went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} Sella carried him at the half-pace with her back to every open slate and the instruction given to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. \place{The trough} stood bare and dark with its water called weather. A rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the month's pots would want now the firing was done, and Sella sent her to the pot: “Ask the pot. The house has said it.” The woman went away level with nothing priced, refused or entered. A man come off \place{the clay road} kept a number shut in his fist and would not let it out, the knuckles closing a little further as the question went past him, and Sella gave him the weather. His arithmetic went from the fist toward the well and arrived nowhere, and he paid nothing while the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged and the true subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the road, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188.

The boy sorted the yard in ninety seconds with a competence intact and useless, because there was no hand in the basket to carry any of it to a slate. Two directions had come over the boards in one morning and neither had landed; the rack woman's question and the man's closed fist had gone off level and left no wake. The district's arithmetic went on in loaves, glances, arms and the space where a thing had stood, which was the only arithmetic the house would own to; the pot's silence went on gathering interest on a tray nobody under any roof could read, and nobody in the yard had asked after it.

\scenebreak

Under the roof she came to the shelf with her back to the basket, and it was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing. She moved her lips along the row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and she gave nobody a figure. No finger under this roof had laid the two gaps together, and this morning her finger did not. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “Don't set that near the door,” she told the pot, which had not moved and had no intention of moving. “You'll pay for it in the thaw.” The pot gave back nothing, which was the answer she had wanted of it. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in.

She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no morning, shelf, hearth, well, road or basket since. A morning is not an opening and a correct setting of a pot is not an opening, and there had been no opening in thirty-four days. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\scenebreak

In the morning they asked the hand. It went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door and came down before the counted breath had finished, while Sella was still at the fire counting toward nothing in the room that did not touch his. An honest miss. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down.

Two hundred and fourteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, and nobody had told him that a number had been reached, and there was not a slate in Sarn with a place to enter either it or the six months and thirty-four days he had carried. The carried fire went on in the room that warmed him with its fifth unpaid for two hundred and fourteen days; the river went on in the room he could not enter; the boards overhead kept their four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. The wrong wind went out of the yard with the sweet edge of green behind it, and the saying stood where it had been set down --- no pot lost, no lesson made, no name given, and the silence of the pot that had sung early gathering interest on a tray nobody could read.
