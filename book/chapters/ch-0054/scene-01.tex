\daymark{213}{Grain 4, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay down against the fold and stayed down. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} \person[Corin]{the boy} did not go back into the dark to find a meaning in what had been given there in the night. A holding was a holding. It lay where a tool lies where a man has set it down, and picking it up to turn it over would only wear the edge. The night had offered him a coordination and he had taken it and filed it and let it go; it was not a sign, not a growth, not a reaching toward anything, and he would not make it one, because the making of it would have wanted a payer standing behind it and no payer stood. He was two hundred and thirteen days old and six months and thirty-three days, and nobody under any roof said so, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} with a place to enter either number. The wanting of the number went on under the not-expecting of it the way a coal goes on under ash, and the two states occupied him without either buying the other a thing. He had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it, and the distinction was a small hard object he kept and did not set down.

The day came to him first as a smell and then as a gap in a sound, the way days had come to him since he could remember coming to them at all. Hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, as if a thing left long enough might earn a season's name instead of a cause. Beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting it go short, and then the gap came. Not a bell. A bell is rung and counted and answered, and this was neither; it was a drawing-in that stopped where it stopped and asked nobody to cross himself. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, not by a decision of its own but because nothing had been set beneath it to make a fifth possible, and the space where the fifth should have stood had gone on being a space for two hundred and thirteen days with no payer named to it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and never would be while nobody put a hand to it; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places: the fire's four and its silence, the river's drawing and slackening, the boards' four and four and the knot. They did not become one. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so they lay in their three places and kept their own company.

\scenebreak

The body took its own reading before the mind asked it to. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear, stiff past the point at which a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its empty name and had not been filled; no third came to stand there. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back the smell of hot clay and the gap in the drawing of the kiln. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and not enough to spend. Exact and worth nothing at two paces: that was the whole of it, and the child's body went on being cold while the man's mind recorded how cold, and neither bought the other a thing.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments and no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirteen days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on a wrong wind whose saying about a pot singing before its time had stayed a saying about a pot and cost no pot. None had been promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read three times over a basket and still not the figure paid, the third reading gone up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box with the true subtraction inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate gone up unread with the writing to her sleeve, and the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the seventh year stood at its very end now and had still not given.

Three held changes stood on three places. The fraction from the morning of Day 161 --- the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish --- lay where it was left, held and never spoken, no payer named to it and no slate entered; it had been held again through the firing month and into Grain as a remembered repetition, not a change made twice over. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166 stood on a place of its own, the reason standing between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; the finger had stayed on top of the front edge through all the days of Grain so far and had not gone beneath it. The flagstone half-check made on Tallow 22 stood on a third place beside the other two, a place in a yard as the others are places in a wall and in a room that does not touch either; it had not been repeated. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named; he would not let them stand up together and call themselves a rule. A fourth place held the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, heard from the loft on Day 183, with no word spoken over it and no payer named. A fifth held the pale line at the gum of the glaze-room hand, read at the well and on the carried body, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces, not joined to the sweet wind, the dust, the pot that sang early, the sixth green or the foundling's sliver; neither the third reading nor either of the earlier readings had answered for that man, and no figure, collector or slate knew what he cost. A sixth held the held inch, with its answers filed one beside another and nothing laid across them.

And beside the dust and the two shut-room tellings, with a hand between the three, the burned-slate word: a direction from \place{Slate Row} that had passed over boards twice and arrived nowhere, advanced and not paid, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a fourth likeness and not a third telling of the room shut in the war. Two tellings stayed two. The crust's gap here, the shelf's two gaps there, the pan's gone ring on its own tray, the field's empty place on another --- he took them one at a time and drew no finger across them. No third gap came, no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, and nothing was filled in to make a row come out even. The seventh green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape, owed to a market nine mornings off and not come early into the small hours of a working day.

\scenebreak

Morning asked the hand. There was no breath counted anywhere in the house to hold it against; the coals had been left to keep themselves and the other room was quiet. There was only the note through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down at once against the fold, down before the note had drawn out, down while the note was still going. A miss. There was no failure in it and no promise in it and no failure hidden under the miss waiting to be found. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a stronger hand, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The old man's exactness and the infant's refusal occupied the same inch and neither bought the other anything; the body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true. He could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. The market did not get to read the inch, and nobody under the roof knew there had been a question.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket. That was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing, and she kept it the way a widow keeps a wound clean: daily, without comment, with a hand that knew exactly how much pressure the thing would bear. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and if the day came when she said \emph{I} over anything in this room she did not let it come in the morning. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, which stood with two unnamed gaps and gave nobody a figure. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the \lore{Ash}-dark charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a morning and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as a finger beneath it. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air as it closed on everything that came within reach of it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. A shelf and a morning are not openings; there had been no opening in thirty-three days. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. The private roll stayed unshown, and Dessa's cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the threshold with a cost-question about the month's firing, and Sella gave the instruction to the pot and the weather and let the question go away level. Nothing was priced, nothing refused, nothing entered; the woman went off with her pot as level as she had come, carrying an answer that had arrived nowhere and could be carried home without spilling. In \place{the Clay Yard} the household went around the clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner and the new finer hairline beside it that the house called the thaw, and around the rack space beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood before it was gone, and around the bare dark \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} whose water was called weather, and around the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. A line left where it is is still a line kept; a space gone around is still a space kept. The house kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and the space where a thing had stood, and the carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} passed at the kindling with the small knife going back to its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it. He took the broom only after the broom had been instructed. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and set the knife back where it could be checked. His chest had been tight since the dark; grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since an older binding, held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the Seed 16 breath stood where it should have gone in, unspent, and nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year was earned, which now stood at its very end and had not given. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check the stones remembered and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down the clay road on the fourth day of Grain. Above the shed the warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the small loft-sill scrape stayed on its fourth place with no word spoken over it and no payer named.

\person[Jorra Vail] came through from the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He gave no name to the men who had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey, and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the burned-slate word that had come over the boards and arrived nowhere. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the full note it would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing as a thing kept on a tray of its own. He grew quieter whenever the burned-slate word went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered holding and not a change made again. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. There was no \lore{Grathic} word that carried both mercies of the iron in one mouthful; one piece, two bites, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A clamp was a clamp and was not asked to mean more.

\scenebreak

There was no market and no Account-day in this first working week of Grain. The first of the month waited on Grain 10, nine mornings off, when the seventh green and the seventh sighting were owed and must not come early; until then the green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape, with no loaf, no slate and no sale. The count went forward exact and unspent and spoken by nobody, carried in a body that could not lift its own head, and the fire's fifth had had no payer for two hundred and thirteen days and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

Sella set the pot rim out on the shelf with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand --- farther than the hand could close, near enough that the distance was a thing a man could measure and no thing a man could cross --- and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required. Her little-door burn shut on the rim and opened and shut again on nothing. She did not look toward \place{the bed}, and the thread at his mouth stayed frozen and unwiped, and the one stiff crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third. The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river went on in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.
