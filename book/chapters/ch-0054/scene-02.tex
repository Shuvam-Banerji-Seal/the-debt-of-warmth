The afternoon did not end; it thinned. It lost its body from the top down, the way a pot set at the edge of the fire gives up its heat out of the air above it first and keeps it longest in the clay at the bottom. The high air went cold and the low places held. Under \place{the loaf shelf} the warmth lay in a flat band that did not rise; behind the pot at \place{the hearth} it gathered in the crack between clay and wall; in the fold at his ear it stayed longest of all, because wool is a slow vessel and gives back only what somebody asks it for, and nobody in this house ever asked the wool anything. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place unnamed, and no third came to stand beside it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. From the basket's low angle the underside of the shelf made a roof that ran above him and stopped where his hand stopped, which was short of everything he wanted of it. He lay under a roof measured for a reach he did not have and did not ask the roof to be longer.

Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order. It went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody under this roof would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- and filed it with the others and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back hot clay under tallow, tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces, and the child's body went on being cold while the man's mind recorded how cold, and neither bought the other a thing. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged; he left the place beside the crust as it was.

\scenebreak

\person[Garl Vail]{Garl} was kept in objects and not in a story. Nothing under the roof told of him; the house carried him the way a wall carries the shape of a picture taken down, by the clean place where it had hung. \place[the salt-stiff coat]{The salt-stiff coat} on its peg behind the door held a broader man's shape with the crease at the left shoulder where a weight had sat for twenty years, and it had not been moved and did not need moving; a coat left where it hangs is a coat kept. \place[the hollow seat]{The hollow seat} beside the wheel kept its long hollow and its lean to the left, and he knew the shape of it without ever having been told whose hollow it was, the way a man knows a place at a table laid for somebody who will not come and is not told to stop setting it. At the corner \place[the clean dark line]{the clean dark line} where \lore{Ash} had turned was unswept, and the new finer hairline beside it that the house called the thaw was unswept too, and a line left where it is is still a line kept. The proverb about leaving the crack showing, because people trust a thing they can see has been handled, had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and had not come back and had not been missed. The debt against a firing of tiles went around the house in a circle without a name, and was worse for being uncalled than a debt with a collector's mark on it, because a called debt has a figure and an end and this one had neither. He went around it the way the house went around it, and did not enter it and did not settle it and did not make a lesson of it. No collector was called, no slate was opened, and the name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and thirteen days. The keeping was maintenance, and maintenance is not a story, and a house that keeps a man in objects has not therefore forgiven him anything.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the shelf with her back to the basket. It was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing, and she kept it the way a widow keeps a wound clean: daily, without comment, with a hand that knew exactly how much pressure the thing would bear. She moved her lips along the row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and she gave nobody a figure. A saying of the loaf-shelves holds that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name; no finger under this roof had laid these together, and this afternoon her finger did not. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and thirteen days. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. “Don't set that near the door,” she told the pot, which had not moved and had no intention of moving. “You'll pay for it in the thaw.” The pot gave back nothing, which was the answer she had wanted of it, and every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no afternoon and no dusk since. An afternoon is not an opening and a dusk is not an opening, and there had been no opening in thirty-three days. The private roll stayed unshown, and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered. She did not look toward \place{the bed}.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been instructed. He came at the going of the light with his chest tight and grey \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain for six years. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and put the knife back where any man could see it was back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan. There it stopped, with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the Seed 16 breath stood where it should have gone in, unspent, a small interval at the top of every reach that nobody under the roof had ever been asked to enter. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year, which now stood at its very end and had still not given. The loan that could hear was not called. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check the stones remembered, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on the fourth day of Grain. Above the shed the warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the small loft-sill scrape stayed on its fourth place with no word spoken over it and no payer named.

\scenebreak

\person[Jorra Vail] came through from the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, as a surgeon keeps a field and the instruments that must not touch it, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold. The two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two with no third, and he did not join them to the burned-slate word that had come over the boards out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere. It stayed a direction with no hand at the foot of it, no owner, no room, no cause and no figure.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and knew the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing as a thing kept on a tray of its own beside the other answers advanced and unpaid, from a man come up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind whose saying about a pot singing before its time had stayed a saying about a pot and cost no pot. He grew quieter whenever the burned-slate word went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order; the quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered holding and not a change made again. The iron was one piece with two bites and there was no \lore{Grathic} word that carried both mercies in one mouthful: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. The broken birth-slates under \place{the lowest rack} were entered by nobody.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. She went wide around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood before it was gone, and around the bare dark \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well} whose water was called weather, and around the rack space beneath \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the field's empty place and the place the carriers crossed, each with a look and a wide step and no number. A line left where it is is still a line kept; a space gone around is still a space kept. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost, and the house kept its arithmetic in loaves and glances and the space where a thing had stood, which was the only arithmetic it would own to. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he put the question to the hand again. There was no breath counted anywhere in the house to hold it against; the coals had been left to keep themselves and the other room was quiet, and there was only the note through the finger's-width door, the low note of water over water drawing out and slackening. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and stayed for the small intake only, and came down before the letting go. An honest fraction. He filed it on the sixth place beside the others and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. The body humiliated him and answered him in the same motion, and both were true. He had asked the hand and the hand had answered in its own small arithmetic, which was not the arithmetic of the house and not the arithmetic of the river and not a number anybody under any roof had been asked to believe. Grain 10 stood nine mornings off with no market and no Account-day before it; the seventh green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape, owed and not come early. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anyone would take down.

Two hundred and thirteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, and nobody had told him that a number had been reached, and there was not a slate in \place{Sarn} with a place to enter either it or the six months and thirty-three days he had carried. \lore{The carried fire} went on in the room that warmed him with its fifth unpaid for two hundred and thirteen days; the river went on in the room he could not enter; the boards overhead kept their four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because agreeing them would want a payer and no payer stood to any of them. The hand came down before the letting go of a breath nobody under the roof had counted, the thread at his mouth stayed frozen and unwiped, and the one stiff crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third. Neither sound stopped for the ear under the roof.
