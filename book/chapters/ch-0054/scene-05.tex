\daymark{215}{Grain 6, Y.H. 813 (Sixthday)}

The sixth morning of Grain's first tend came up under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the way the five before it had come, not as an announcement but as a change in the air at the ear. First the smell: hot clay under tallow, and tallow under wet wool, and under the wool the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}, a smell with a season's name and no cause anybody had been asked to give it. Then the sound, or rather the place where it failed: beyond the wall the kiln drew in fours, taking the air in great mouthfuls and letting the last go short, and stopped where it stopped, not as a bell stops, asking nobody to cross himself. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it for two hundred and fifteen days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places. They lay in their three places and kept their own company, because a man who wanted them to agree would have had to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his two hundred and fifteenth day and his six months and thirty-fifth day with nobody under any roof saying either number, and no slate in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} with a place to enter them.

He lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} where the underside made a roof that ran above him and stopped where his hand stopped, and he did not ask the roof to be longer. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place unnamed, and no third came to stand beside it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. The surgeon's register took the reading without being asked --- surface warm, core not, the order exact --- filed it with the others, and asked the room what it was for. The room gave back hot clay under tallow, wet wool, and the sour corner breath called the thaw. That was the whole answer. It was enough to live on and worth nothing at two paces.

\scenebreak

The word came over the boards again before the yard had properly woken. It came up from \place{Slate Row} as a direction and not as a visit, a voice on the river wind going over the boards of \place{the Clay Yard} and not to the basket, saying that a slate had burned in the night, and then going on, on the same breath, to the price of barley and the weather, as if a burned slate and a barley price and the state of the wind were three things a man might set out together on a counter and take away unchanged. No owner was named, no room, no hand at the flame, no reason. Nobody in the yard turned it into a cause.

The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound: the skin first, then what lay under it, then the question of what had made it, and last the question of who would pay for the answer. A wound with a track tells you where the instrument went in and what it crossed on the way; this word had a direction and no track, a place it came from and no place it arrived. It was not a hearing and not a seventh; it was not one of the nine stoppings and not a payment; it was not a likeness standing up to call itself a cause; it was nothing a clerk could chart, because a clerk wants an owner and a room and a hand, and the word had come without any of the three. This was the third telling of the burned slate. It was still not a third telling of the counting room shut in the war, of which there remained two tellings and no third, and he kept those two at two with nobody putting a word between them and himself standing between them and the slate word with a hand held out to keep the three apart. He set the word on its own place beside the meal-fine dust off \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra}'s forearms and the two shut-room tellings, and laid nothing across them. An empty place kept empty is a finding; a finding filled to make a row come out even is forged. There was no payer under any of them and he named none. The word went on standing where it had been set down, advanced and not paid, a direction that had come over boards three times and arrived nowhere, and no pot in the yard was lost to it and no lesson made of it.

\scenebreak

Jorra grew quieter whenever it went around the boards, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. It was not that he spoke less; he had never spent many words on the yard. It was that the words he did spend went to the wheel and the pots and came back from them, and the space where a man might have put a sentence about the burned slate stayed empty, and everybody under the roof went around that empty space the way they went around the Ash ridge in the corner, with a look and a wide step and no figure spoken. The quiet went before him into \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the way a cold air goes before a door, arriving at the wheel a half-beat before the man who carried it. He came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray, parting dust from slip as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under \place{the lowest rack} stayed entered by nobody. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody lifts, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He told a pot, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and kept back the note he knew it would give three turns before it left his hands, and said nothing about where the slate had burned or why.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the door with a cost-question about the mid-tend work, standing on the threshold with a pot in her hands and her mouth already open on the figure she had brought with her. \person{Sella Vail} gave her no figure. She sent her to the pot and the weather, in the order she sent everything, and the woman went away level with her question still standing in her mouth, nothing priced, nothing refused, nothing entered. At \place{the yard well} a rack woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the month's pots would cost now that the firing was done and the tend had turned, and Sella sent her to the pot as well, and to the weather, and the rack woman went off level carrying her question the way she had carried it in, without slopping a drop of it into any slate. \place[the trough]{The trough} at the well stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. Two cost-questions had come over the boards in one morning and neither had landed; the district's arithmetic went on in loaves and glances and the space where a thing had stood, which was the only arithmetic this house would own to. Nothing was bought and nothing was sold. A figure that goes away in the mouth of the person who brought it has not been paid and has not been refused, which is a distinction the house keeps more carefully than a Court would keep it, and the boy kept it on a tray of its own without writing a cause across it.

\scenebreak

Sella came to the fire before she came to the basket, because that was the order of the house and had been the order since the first firing, and she kept it as a widow keeps a wound clean: daily, without comment, with a hand that knew exactly how much pressure the thing would bear. She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the loaf row, and the row stood with two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night years back with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch, the little-door burn closing on air the way it closed on everything that came within reach of it. Her finger lay on the top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying; the charcoal under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and a woman's back turned was not the same thing as a finger beneath it. A hand that knows a board by heart and a hand that has been told not to look leave the same mark on a room, and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and withheld the fifth, and the space where the fifth should have stood went on being a space with no payer named to it. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. “Don't set that near the door,” she told the pot, which had not moved and had no intention of moving. “You'll pay for it in the thaw.” The pot gave back nothing, which was the answer she had wanted of it. Every instruction in the room went to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. She set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no morning and no midday since. A morning is not an opening and a midday is not an opening, and there had been no opening in thirty-five days. Her private roll stayed unshown, and \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa}'s cost stayed where it had been put down and never entered.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been instructed. He came at the going of the light with his chest tight and grey \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and the unforged collector sentence that belonged to \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} lay beside it where it had lain for six years. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and put the knife back where any man could see it was back. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan. There it stopped. It stopped as it had stopped before, as a remembered stopping and not a new one, with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it. If the thing was paid at all, it was paid as an interval and not as a calling: a breath taken and held at the threshold, a word stopped short of the air, nothing entered and nobody called. Nine stoppings stood as nine objects with no tenth stood up before the seventh year, which now stood at its very end and had still not given; the Seed 16 breath stood where it should have gone in, unspent, a small interval at the top of every reach; the loan that could hear was not called. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check the stones remembered, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. No collector came up or went down \place{the clay road} on the sixth day of Grain. Above the shed the warm stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the small loft-sill scrape stayed on its fourth place with no word spoken over it and no payer named.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and held until her sentence reached the word for feeding and gave the end of it to the pot, then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing there, a remembered holding and not a change made again. The cold iron took the cold out of his fingertip while he waited. The iron was one piece with two bites and there was no \lore{Grathic} word that carried both mercies in one mouthful: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man. He held the two bites apart as he held the dust from the slip, and joined neither to the burned slate nor to the two tellings of the room shut in the war.

\scenebreak

In the morning, after the yard had gone quiet around the two questions nobody answered, he put the question to his hand again under conditions that had sometimes answered. The door stood a finger's width open, the river kept its note, and the fire went on in the room that warmed him with its fifth still unpaid; there was a count in the house if a man had wanted to borrow one, and conditions under which the hand had sometimes stayed. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down at once against the fold. It did not hold for the small intake; it did not take the river's low interval or any breath counted at a fire in a room that does not touch his. A miss is a miss. There was no failure in it and no success either, and the asking was not always answered. He filed it on the sixth place beside the other answers and laid nothing across them and named no payer to it. Nothing stood up out of it to call itself a longer reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, one of the three held changes, a key, a power or a rule. He still could not close the two inches to the pot handle set out for him, lift his chin the two inches to the crust, or speak a number that anybody under any roof would take down. The body humiliated him and refused him in the same motion, and both were true.

Two hundred and fifteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under any roof, and nobody had told him that a number had been reached, and there was not a slate in Sarn, Sill or Grath with a place to enter either it or the days he had carried. The carried fire went on in the room that warmed him with its fifth unpaid for two hundred and fifteen days; the river went on in the room he could not enter; the boards overhead kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others, because agreeing them would want a payer and no payer stood to any of them. Grain 10 stood four mornings off, the first Account-day and the first market of the month, with the seventh green still a colour and the foundling still a shape, and no loaf, no slate and no sale coming early into the small hours of a working day. The asking was not always answered; an answer refused was an object on its own tray, and neither was a sign.

The fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. Jorra's quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door, and the boy's hand lay back down against the fold, the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, the one stiff crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third.
