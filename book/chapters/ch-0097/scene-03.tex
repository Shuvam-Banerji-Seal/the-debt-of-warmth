\daymark{343}{Ash 14, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and sixtieth answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails, and it had done nothing in the dark that anyone under the roof could have entered in a slate. The small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof were going the way small hours go in a dry kiln winter, by the tick of clay cooling behind a wall and the settle of a coal under its ash and the river going low over lower under the finger's-width door. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and took the room as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. Three hundred and forty-three stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and sixty-three days told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} had given four low pulls in the night and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and forty-three days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Nothing from the thirteenth of \place{Ash} crossed the dark toward him as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. A day lived is a day set down, and he did not go back into yesterday to fetch a meaning out of it.

Ash kept its fourteenth day without a boundary drawn. The older women of the district named the months by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days that had gone into them, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words under the Vail roof; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. There would be no market, no Cross and no bell on the fourteenth, and no collector up or down \place{the clay road}. The going of the light would come as an ordinary going of the light, measured in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. The ear gave a man the truth before the eye did, because the eye is always being told what to see and the ear is not; he had learned that in a room where nobody had ever thought to tell him what he was hearing.

The crust lay two inches from his ear in the dry kiln heat, gone stiffer and leathery, its corner standing a hair off the wool and the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself; no third had come in the days behind to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease that is no ease, the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile. A cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes; and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core.

He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle set within reach. Both were true, the second was louder, and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, the surgeon's register taking the small hours in its old order while the infant underneath went on being cold. It was a small humiliation and an exact one, and he played it for its exactness: a man who had reduced a thousand shoulders could not lift a barley crust two inches, could not wipe his own mouth, could not keep a fold of wool at his own chin. He had a dozen good reasons to be angry at the body he was in and chose none of them, because a grievance is an account with a payer standing under it and no payer stood. That was the whole of the discipline, and it bought him nothing at two inches of air.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the hearth before the basket, as she went to the fire before anything that breathed under the roof. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear. “The house will keep it,” she said to the fire, in the tone another woman would have used for I. She stood a moment with her hands still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. One grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, and her hands, having nothing to do for a breath, counted spoons and the breaths of a sleeping child.

Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} by the wall, and the hand half closed over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand shutting on air. The boy listened under the fold for the small flat knock a vessel cracked all the way through would give under the knuckle, and none came, because no hand tapped the jar and no note was asked of it; a cracked vessel keeps its own silence until somebody puts a hand to it, and nobody did. No fiction of wholeness was acted out here. Nothing was named and nothing was mended.

From \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, and only then let the weight come on the handle. Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” and “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. The hairline of the original break showed dark between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Repair stayed apart from restoration, because a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing; the boy had learned that over hundreds of mornings from a pot he could not lift. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay turned under wool away from the hearth; she lifted a corner with a touch lighter than the day before and turned it once and gave no figure, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, so that no handful was lifted. The empty fingers went on a place of their own beside the one object that was the handful let fall on Bone 22---a keeping, not a second handful, not a sixth held change, with no payer under it.

The pot's rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The boy took the half-beat apart by what was there and by what was not: the distance between the hand's intention and its arrest, a small exact interval that bought nothing at two inches of air and was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody had given. He wanted the rim under his fingers and got the space where it would have been, and the space was not entered.

\scenebreak

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and was not touched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth stood behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on the second of Ash and was held without a name, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark had last been touched before the first firing of the dark month and would be touched again only before an opening, and the boy had long ago stopped expecting the household to make a lesson of the intervals between.

By first light the room had its ordinary shape back: \place{the boards} fronting the shelf with four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with the file lying open on it and every gap left gap; the pot on the board with its hairline showing; the crust in the fold with its empty place beside it. Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four and its gap; overhead the boards kept their four and four and a knot; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. Agreeing any two of the three would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any.

The boy checked the whole tray and left it as it lay. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels; an empty place kept empty was a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even was a finding forged. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and was not fetched in to make a fourth count or a seventh hearing. \person{Berr Clay}'s figure stood where it stood: seven arithmetics of the year behind one unsigned figure, seven readings seven and not one account, the true subtractions gone up the clay road in a locked box toward \place{High Kiln}, the figure read still not the figure paid and no signature on the space between. Ash's first was locked away and answered nothing under this roof; Ash's second stood offstage on its own morning five days ahead, named only as a date and not brought into a day that had not earned it. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer and was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain under wool, the five held objects, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand, the sixth opening, the three movements of the fifth and sixth of Ash, the pot-crack resting or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a payment; the finger went between. No doctrine stood up out of any of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The burned-slate word stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four and no witness standing; the shut-room tellings stood at two and the row of askings at five with one shaping and no sixth and no witness; nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, the runner's eighth and the well-woman's ninth each on a place of its own; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the loan uncalled. The breath held at the knife-sill on the thirteenth was not spoken of under the roof this morning and was not made again, for the change to come would be earned by an afternoon and not by a habit of mornings. No sixth of any held series came due, and none was supplied to make a row come out even.

The boy could not spend any of it. He had the field exact, the counts in their places, the gaps kept as gaps, the finding on a place of its own with no signature and no payer, and not one of them could be lifted two inches to a mouth or traded for a hand on a pot handle. He did not treat that as a grievance, for a grievance is an account with a payer standing under it and none stood; he took it as the terms of the work, which was to keep a fact without turning it into a payment. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found, and the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary light of the fourteenth.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. It did not reach; the inch was not a reach and he had long ago stopped calling it one. It was the distance a hand may move in a field without disturbing the dressing, and he took it the way he had once taken a hand into a cavity, slowly, with the whole of the attention and the knowledge that most of what a hand does in a field is wait. The note under the finger's-width door went water over water, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, and let the intake go on the note's own finishing. It came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and sixty-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the seven arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the notched wood, the new breath at the sill nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and sixty-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one of them stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in an answer entered where nobody could see it; the infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning.

Three hundred and forty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and sixty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the fourteenth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No runner had been seen on any road and no collector come up or down the clay road; the knife lay at the edge of its sill where a hand could be checked or slip it off and no hand had stayed there this morning; and Ash 20 stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. The keeping of the number was the only honest work there had been to do, bought nothing at two inches of air, was not for sale, and went on because attention is the one thing no institution can tax. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline of the break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire; and under the finger's-width door the river kept its small sound, low over lower. Forward, not closed.
