## Chronicle

On Ash 13 the house kept an ordinary Thirdday, with no market or bell and no collector on the clay road. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the boy lay small and cold, a stiff crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed, the thread at his mouth frozen, cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and must be warmed slowly from the outside, and wanted his crust lifted and the thread wiped; both were true, the second louder, and neither won. \person{Sella Vail} went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, turned them with a broken pot and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I. She left the charcoal unread, the pot's crack showing and the grain turned under wool with no handful lifted.

In the yard she went wide around seven places and entered none. \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, said *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and answered the early and late thaw with *Yes, house*, keeping the late because it puts off the paying. \person{Jorra Vail} came off the wheel-shed with grey dust in a fist-crease, spoke to the wheel before any person, knew the note a pot would give three turns ahead and kept it where nobody could make a song of it, and put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of a sentence unfinished.

On the fourteenth, at the kindling, Tarn set the knife at the edge of its sill where it could be checked, passed his hand over it, and let the hand stay a breath longer than the check required. He cut nothing, counted nothing, called no loan and spent no breath. The boy filed it on a place of its own beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting, with a finger between every pair, and would not let it stand up as a sixth object, a habit, a sign, a cause or a payment; no payer stood under it.

On the fifteenth the yard went around again. \person{Ilva} drew at the well with the pail against her knee; to a woman asking what Ash's firings had taken she answered, *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, her hand staying at the sleeve, the strip unread. He filed her reaching as the ninth hand with no tenth. The pot came up thumb-first at each going of the light, the hairline showing. In the small hours the hand went up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake, held it and let it go on the note's own finishing; two hundred and sixty-four honest answers stand on the sixth place, each an object and not a power.
