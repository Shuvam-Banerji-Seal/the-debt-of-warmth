\daymark{358}{Ash 29, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand outside the wool lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and ninety as a man lays a tool on a bench where the next hand will find it without looking, and the room went on around it the way a room goes on around a thing that has been put away. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, in the small hour before light, and the dark had its ordinary weight and its ordinary smell: wet wool, tallow smoke, clay dust, and under them the faint sour mineral tang of the ridge along the skirting board that nobody had swept since the year turned. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and fifty-eight days; through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Above him \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood; the bell's gap stayed in its square and was not folded into them. Three hundred and fifty-seven had stood exact and three hundred and fifty-eight stood now, one more in a row nobody counted aloud, not round, not a threshold, only a day. Six months and one hundred and seventy-seven days had become one hundred and seventy-eight and nobody under the roof said so. The fire's fifth was unpaid the same number; the name of the man whose coat kept his shape on the peg behind the door was unspoken the same number.

The surgeon's register took the body in its old order before the light came, because the light would not wait and the body would. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder and browner through the long cold, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the days behind to make the row come out even. A thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sill, in Grath, had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, knew it the way he had once known it across a table in a white room where the light made everyone look equally ill: a cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core, and the core gives heat up in the reverse order to the one in which it takes it back, which comes sooner than anybody likes. He knew all of it exactly and it was worth nothing at two inches of air. The want underneath was louder and would not be argued down: lift the crust the two inches to his mouth, wipe the thread, set a hand that would close on the pot handle where the fingers could reach it. Both were true, the second louder, neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work. The body humiliated him in the exactness and not in any large way: the drool frozen, the head useless, the crust gone past the point where any child would eat it, the pot handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, a hand that could not lift itself to its own chin. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders and could not now keep a fold of wool over one ear. He could still find the joke funny without laughing, which was a way of laughing that cost less and kept the same blade in it.

In the dark before the grey \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, as she had come to it at every going of the light and every morning for three years, and turned the banked coals with the back of a broken pot. “It's the thaw,” she told them, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I. She counted a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening and figured neither gap; her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal beneath stayed unread, and she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. Fire before basket; the instruction to the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket. The boy took the ordering of it as he took the order of a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the rasp of the wire on the mended pot, the tick of cooling clay, the low river under all of it. Nothing in the room had changed hands since the day before.

The heard bell stood four, four and a gap in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not made a seventh of the full note. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, and nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the loan uncalled. The finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and no doctrine standing out of eight, and where its edges came near to other holdings the finger went between.

Beside the five held objects, with a finger between every pair, sat the pot-crack resting of Ash 9; beside that, with a finger between, the held breath at the sill of Ash 14; beside all of them, with a finger between it and each, the holding \person{Jorra Vail} had left in the cold iron on the twenty-sixth --- gathered once, found where it had been put and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way, and both want a payer standing and none stood. The staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the handful let fall at the rack end, the resting of a thumb on the uncut seventh and the sixth opening at the kiln mouth on Ash 2 sat each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair; six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held without a name and no seventh made due. The man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg behind the door had not been named under this roof for three hundred and fifty-eight days. \person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story: the coat with the crease at the left shoulder, the hollow wheel-seat in the shed that nobody was allowed to fill, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire, and the unforged collector sentence lying beside a knife at \place{the kindling} with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. In the first grey she went to the fire again and did the whole of it again beat for beat, and the boy checked the second doing against the first and found them the same. Fire before basket; the instruction to the wool, the pot and the river wind and never to the basket. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure; her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At the pot board she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, held the weight a half-beat before she let it come on the handle, and left the hairline showing. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” she told the pot. “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,” she told the fire, and neither saying was improved in the keeping and neither was wasted, and repair stayed apart from restoration, for a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing.

She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure lifted off it and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on the cord at her throat, the little-door burn half-closing on nothing; it was not touched on the twenty-ninth, because a morning and a hearth are not openings, and six openings in six months were six and not a rule, the sixth held at the kiln mouth on Ash 2 without a name and no seventh made due. It was an ordinary tending day in Ash with no market, no Cross, no bell and no collector on the clay road, and no runner came on any road; the directions of the fifth, the seventeenth and the twenty-third stood where they had been put and did not repeat. The last clean morning of the dark month came on with nothing due and nothing answered, and the keeping went on because it was the only honest work there was.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went water over water under the door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; it was the note's own finishing that ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and ninety-one: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the eight arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the holding in the iron, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and ninety-one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. Underneath it the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped and a hand that would close on the pot handle set beyond the reaching fingers. Both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the twenty-ninth. He did not spend any of it on himself or make a lesson of it. Nothing on this day had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended; three hundred and fifty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody, and the keeping of it, bought at two inches of air and not for sale, went on because attention is the one thing no institution here has thought to tax. Ash kept its twenty-ninth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Ash 30 stood one morning ahead and was named only as a date. No ninth arithmetic was supplied early, no seventh hearing, sixth held object, seventh opening, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping invented to make a row come out even.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and ninety-one; the file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; and the empty place beside the one crust kept its name to itself, with no third to make the row come out even. Forward, not closed.
