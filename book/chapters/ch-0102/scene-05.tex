\daymark{359}{Ash 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

The hand lay against the fold where it had been laid down on the two hundred and ninety-second answer, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was a small soft hand with milky nails and no strength in the wrist worth speaking of; it had once reduced a shoulder in a room that smelled of disinfectant and cold metal, and had since learned the exact weight of a barley crust at two inches. Three hundred and fifty-eight had stood exact behind it; three hundred and fifty-nine had opened in the dark and would stand exact at the going of the light, six months and one hundred and seventy-eight days becoming one hundred and seventy-nine, told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. Under the floor of \place{the Vail kiln-house} \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and fifty-nine days; on its peg behind the door the salt-stiff coat kept the broader shape of \person[Garl Vail]{a man} whose name had been unspoken the same number. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and took the dark as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: the dry tick of clay cooling behind the wall, the settle of a coal under its ash, the river going low over lower under \place{the finger's-width door}.

The thirtieth came in the dark before first light, and it came earned. There would be a market at \place{the Cross} on the thirtieth and a slate read under \place{the moot-post} and a bell, and none of those things had stood on the twenty-ninth, so none of them could be carried back into a finished day to make a lesson of them. \place{Ash} was going out with its third Account-day, and the ninth arithmetic that had stood since the first of the month as a date and nothing more now had a morning to earn; the boy did not bring it into the small hours early, any more than a surgeon cuts before the field is laid open. Eight arithmetics of the year stood behind one unsigned figure --- the three of \place{Frost}, the first, second and third of \place{Bone}, Ash's first and Ash's second --- eight and not one account; the printed figure stood one in twelve, unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between. Both of Ash's earlier subtractions rode locked and unread up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down.

The crust at his ear had gone stiffer and browner in the dry kiln heat, two inches off with its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and would stay frozen, because nobody under any roof in the district had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of his wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past the stopping, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure and the ease that is no ease, the grimace of deep cold that wears the look of a smile. A cold patient cannot clot, will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and the order in which it gives it up, the reverse, which comes sooner than anybody likes. The knowing arrived in its old order, the way instruments arrive on a towel, and was worth nothing at two inches of air. Underneath it the want was louder: the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped, a hand that would close on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, and it was the work.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, as she had come before it on every morning of the dark month. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and, under them, the faint sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge along the skirting board, which had not been swept in two years. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot, not with the poker, because a pot's back knows how much a coal will bear; the small flat sound of it went across the room and was the first honest thing said in it. “The house will keep it,” she said, in the tone of a woman setting a cup where a cup belongs, and another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting small intake, hold and letting-go toward no opening, giving neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. At \place{the pot board} she lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and left the hairline of the original break showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire, so that repair stayed apart from restoration. Then she turned the dressed grain once under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, her fingers going in and coming out without closing, no figure and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed.

She came to the basket last and lifted as a woman lifts a pot filled to the brim, weight taken before the handle. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, and “it's the river wind,” to the wind, and there was no I in either sentence and no instruction for the thing in the wicker. She carried him down out of the kiln-house before first light at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the basket level under her arm, the fire to be banked against their coming back. The half-pace was not slowness but a measurement, the balance a tray is balanced with across a room where somebody might have set down a slate and expected a figure off it. The way down was the way down: \place{the flagstones} cold enough to take the warmth out of a sole through a shoe, the pot board passed without a look, the door on its finger of open air, and the river wind coming up off the sill with the taste of iron in it.

At \place{the reading block under the moot-post} she set the basket down in \place{the moot-post's finger's width of shade} where no slate lay, the way a pot is set down out of the draught, level, with the whole of its weight on the flat of the ground. The shade was a thin thing, no wider than a finger, and it lay where the post's own edge cut the light; she chose it as she chose the cool side of a hearthstone, without thinking about choosing. His cheek took the cold iron of the post through the basket's wicker, and the iron was colder than the river wind, and the iron did not give. She put the fold right under his chin without looking into the basket, straightened the wool with the back of one knuckle, and went half a pace off and stood there through everything that followed. Half a pace was far enough that a man could read a figure over him and near enough that the house could answer for him if a hand were laid on the wicker. She said nothing to the basket at any hour of the reading, and if anybody had asked her why she would have told them to ask the pot.

\scenebreak

By the time Berr came up short off the clay road the two hundred had leaned in to the block and were leaning back again, and eight pigs had gone over the wet boards without anybody counting them aloud. The pigs went over with the small clumsy scrambling sound pigs make on boards, and a man with a pole kept their heads down, and the market went on over the noise as water goes over a stone. He came up short the way he always came up short, stopping before he reached the block so that the crowd had to turn to him, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath: a man who smells of nothing has had the sweating and the dust and the ordinary evidence of a body taken off him by somebody whose accounts are neat. He did not raise his voice. The crowd leaned in to hear him, which was why he never raised it.

His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. The boy took the order of it from two inches of air as a surgeon takes a field being prepared: the wipe, the wipe, the slate kept to the arm, the finger before the eyes, the extra breath at the end. It did not vary, and nothing in it was an accident of manner that could be taken for a weakness. A surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on and does not shake the hand afterwards where anybody can see it. Berr took himself out of his sentences in the same way.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said low into the goers-by. He sent the figure over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the late households, and his eyes passed across the top of the wicker and did not go into it. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.” To a man who had pushed a number up out of a fist and held it there, he said, “If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch” --- your house, never I, the house standing in for the man who had spoken and the man who had signed. The crowd took the figure the way a crowd takes a figure, with a sound like a held breath let go in many places at once, and went back to the pigs.

He said something more to the block, low, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs: kindness was entered, he said, or a kindness had been done and entered, the words too small to carry past the block and the post's cold iron. The block answered nothing. It was a block and had no answer in it, and he had not expected one. He went off up the clay road with the cord still folded square under his coat, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Ash's third true subtraction riding inside beside the three of Frost and the first, second and third of Bone. Nine arithmetics of the year now stood behind one printed figure. He believed he had done a kindness. There was no anger in his face and no triumph in it and nothing a crowd could have fastened on if it had wanted to, and by the time the pigs had been weighed he was a direction up the road and not a man standing anywhere to be asked.

Nine arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure: the three of Frost, the first, second and third of Bone, Ash's first, Ash's second and Ash's third. Nine were nine and not one account. The printed figure stood where it had stood all year, one in twelve, unchallenged; the figure read was still not the figure paid; no signature stood on the space between. The finding went on a place of its own with no payer standing, and was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain lying covered under wool with no figure and no handful, the five held objects, the pot-crack resting of Ash 9, the hand-at-the-sill breath of Ash 14, the holding of Ash 26 in the cold iron, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have called the touching a cause and the cause a debt and set a payer under both; the finger went between, not a decision and not a doctrine, only the placing of a small clean interval where an interval was due. He had learned that a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. No doctrine stood up out of nine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Nothing in the reading answered the hand that had gone in on \place{Seed} 15 and had not come out, or the man carried out on Day 188 with the cost entered nowhere, or the cold foot below the sill, or the carried fire's missing fifth, or the burned slate at five, or the shut-room tellings at two, or the room shut with \lore{the damper} closed. There was no answer in the box and no answer in the crowd that had gone back to the pigs. He did not spend nine readings on himself, did not make them a lesson, a grievance, a sign or a power. The district's arithmetic had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and the boy had lain under a slate while it happened and had not been asked and had not asked. The quiet exactness of the field was the whole of what he took away, and it was worth nothing at two inches of air, and that was its honesty.

The bell went through the market, four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself. The gap was where a fifth stroke would have stood and no fifth stroke came, and the bell went on being heard after the sound of it had gone, the way a cold hand goes on being cold after it has left iron. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three counts and not made a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go; the seventh place stayed empty and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\scenebreak

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the road toward \place{Greybank}, and took it at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. The small flat correction of the strap came first, the second strap let out for the long stretch, the turn of the wrist bringing the edge round, and only then did her eyes go to the crowd; her hand was at the slate before either. The dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for the road, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. The slate lay edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was. To a woman by the rail who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the one who wanted more she said, “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” and went on, leaving the rest of the conversation standing where it had been set down, without rudeness and without apology and with the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance. No figure came off the slate.

She was a runner and not a reader, and the boy did not make a reader of her because of a reading that had gone up under her; the Ash 6 turning of her writing toward herself stood on a place of its own beside the fourth shaping of the well-saying with a finger between, a sighting and not a payment, not a witness and not a prophecy. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand in the district with no tenth, on a place of its own and joined to none --- not to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, not to the soft-cuffed clerk's hand to slate before eye, not to the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, not to any keeping under any roof. Nine hands were nine hands and not a rule; a rule wants a payer and no payer had been named to any of them.

Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called, his slate turned inward and his hand to the slate before his eye to the stalls, the cuff grey from washing and the grey of it worked into the seam at the wrist. The market sold over him as water over a stone, and nobody took him off, and he did not take himself off, and a measure nobody had called stayed unentered in an inward slate that nobody read. He was a shape in the boy's file on a place of its own, and no tenth came on the thirtieth any more than it had come on the twenty-ninth.

At the market's pot board a silent woman with the seventh green at one corner of her mouth turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and a pale line across the back of one hand, the colour staying a colour and nothing coming off it. At \place{the market's edge} a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver and went dull again, the sliver staying a shape and nothing coming off it. Each was a seventh with no eighth, unnamed, not joined to Berr's green at his own mouth, not joined to each other, not joined to a loaf, a slate, a sale, a hearing, a cause or a romance, and no second look was given to either. They were never Fenna and never Ulda in the telling, for a colour is not a name and a shape is not a house. The foundling's small half-turn came after the knock and went where it went, and was a turn and not a hearing, and was not joined to the boy's own turning at the inch under the wool. The colour and the shape waited on their own places for a hand not his and not yet come.

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the thin shade with the whole of her attention on its level, as if the thing to be got right were the balance of a pot on a shelf and not the figure that had been sent twice over its top. She put the fold right under his chin with the back of one knuckle, gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and went down toward Low Kiln at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. She said nothing of where they had been and nothing of what had been read over the wicker. That was not a refusal to tell him. There was nobody under any roof who told a basket what a figure meant, and a story told to a child who could not answer back would have been a thing said to please the speaker; she was not that woman. She carried him as a pot is carried that must not be slopped, and did not look along the clay road where the box had gone and the key with it.

Under the Vail roof she came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting small intake, hold and letting-go toward no opening with neither gap figured; her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and left the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; wherever she put her fingers they went in and came out without closing, and no handful was lifted. She set him on his bed under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, let her hand rest the half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat and was not touched. A market, a Cross, a going of the light and a reading are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and six openings in six months are six and not a rule, with the sixth held behind at \place{the kiln mouth} on Ash 2 without a name and no seventh made due on a day that had not earned one. The mark answered nothing this evening for the crust at two inches, the empty place beside it, the fire's missing fifth, the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come out, or the nine arithmetics riding away in a locked box; none of those stood under it asking to be answered, and she did not ask on their behalf.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach; he had long ago stopped calling it one. Most of what a hand does in a field is wait, and he had once taken a hand into a cavity slowly, with the whole of the attention, and knew the difference between the moving and the waiting. The note went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, never numbered among the hearings, for a hearing wants a source standing and none stood. The hand took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let the intake go and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay; the note's own finishing ended the interval and not any decision of the hand, and that was the whole of the honesty in it. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer two hundred and ninety-three: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the nine arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath at the sill, the staying at the inch, the early letting-go, the sixth opening, the pot-crack resting, the Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction, the Ash 23 direction, the word that stood in no doorway, the fourth shaping, the turned writing, the strip in the sleeve nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and ninety-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and not one stood up and called itself a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it --- nine entries set down on nine places and not averaged into one account, every gap left gap, every instrument laid back where the next hand would find it without looking. It was a cold satisfaction and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not the same as peace. The infant underneath took none of it and went on wanting the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true, the second louder, neither winning, the two registers quarrelling without verdict in the ordinary small hours of the thirtieth.

Three hundred and fifty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody under the Vail roof; six months and one hundred and seventy-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the thirtieth had been opened, joined by a standing hand, named, called, paid or ended. No collector had come up or down the clay road, and there had been a market and a Cross and a bell, and they belonged to the thirtieth and not to any day that had not earned them. Ash kept its thirtieth day without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months --- the older women by the weather, the slatemen by the number of Account-days --- was set right or left wrong in so many words; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. \place{Stone} came on in the dark beyond the door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was supplied to make a row come out even.

The hand lay back against the fold on answer two hundred and ninety-three; the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap; nine arithmetics rode locked and unread up the clay road in Berr's box with the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down; the twice-mended pot stood on the pot board with the hairline showing between old-brass staple and tighter wire; one stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread frozen and unwiped; and through the finger's-width door the river went water over water while \place{the boards} overhead kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Forward, not closed.
