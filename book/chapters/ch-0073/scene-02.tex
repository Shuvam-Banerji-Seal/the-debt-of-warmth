The afternoon thinned without ending. Heat went out of the fold the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals --- not in a single giving-up, but drawing back through the clay and sitting behind it, so that a hand two inches off could not say where the warmth had gone. The room kept its shape. \person[Corin]{The boy} kept his place at the low angle of the shelf, and the crust lay where it had been laid, one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face cold puts on a patient and knew it was not composure, and he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him into \place{the Clay Yard} in the afternoon with the basket held as a pot filled is held that must not be slopped, at the half-pace from every open slate. She went around seven things with a look and a wide step and no number for any of them. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and around the finer grey hairline beside it that the house calls \lore{the thaw}, and swept neither. She went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, which held no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. At \place{the yard well} she went around \place{the trough} standing bare and dark with its water called weather. Out on \place{the worked strips} she went around the field's empty place, and in the returning line she went around the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, carrying that with her as she carried the rest and giving it no name.

Seven in a row were seven and not an account. An account wants a payer standing under it, and none stood to any of them. He did not join the dark line to the grey hairline, the rack space to the pan's ring, the trough to the field, or the field to the hand. The hand was carried and not named, not paid, not joined to the shelf's two gaps in the other room, to the fold's one crust with its unnamed place beside it or to the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}. Seven went around and seven stayed seven. The space between the figure that would go over the grain and what the grain had cost had no signature on it, and the looking at it made none.

The house kept \person{Garl Vail} in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease on its peg, the wheel-seat in the shed holding its twenty-year hollow on the left that nobody filled, the proverb that had gone years ago to a pot with no crack and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, and the debt against a firing of tiles going around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy days. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} on this first morning of Frost and none had gone down it; the road lay in the afternoon light with nobody on it who wanted anything from this house. He kept the objects on a tray of their own and made no story of them, and he did not lay the uncalled debt beside any other keeping, for a joining would want a payer under both and none stood.

At \place{the yard well} \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. A woman with a pot asked what the month's firings had taken. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there; the short strip of birch bark notched along one edge did not come out, no thumb passed over it and no figure came off it. Her eyes went to the asker before her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.} She went on with her water and nobody turned to look at her twice. He filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, and nine objects did not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The birch stayed in her sleeve, not read, not asked about, not joined to the reed-cutter's cord, the knots, the six pages, the tins, the charcoal under the shelf, the missing hand or the pale line.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very lip of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man who turned back at the frozen ford with no name on him and no figure on the debt. To the yard voice that wanted the thaw early and the one that wanted it late he said \emph{Yes, house} to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the warm stone in its unmarked loft pot lay cold and unreconciled where he had put it two winters ago. The loan stayed uncalled, and he did not say that a man who owns nothing has still kept one thing exactly.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} stood with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Before first grey three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room}, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound with a door left a finger's width open, and nothing more. Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, with no pot lost. When Sella's sentence about the fire came short of its end, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one iron with two bites --- the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man --- with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln. The burned-slate word stood at four, a direction over the boards from \place{Slate Row} and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; five askings of the row question stood with one further shaping beside them, no witness under any and no row named. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

All of it was \lore{not-doings}, and the boy kept them as a finding with no payer, held apart from his own kept gaps with a finger between. The house kept its fire banked under ash and did not supply the fifth pull of \lore{the carried fire}; it kept the charcoal unread beneath the front edge of \place{the loaf shelf}, the mark untouched before any hour but the kiln, the loan uncalled, the crust unlifted, the gap unnamed. His own gaps were other things on other trays: the missing sixth hearing, the tenth stopping that had not come, the empty interval after a counted breath, the two inches of air between his hand and the pot handle. He did not lay the house's omissions across his own and did not let either become one grievance, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. The finding was kept on its own place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he would not let it stand up as a lesson.

In the room that does not touch, a woman had counted breaths while the house was going quiet: the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. The count had ended some time before and a little silence stood after it, whole and empty on a place of its own. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and under the floor the fire kept four with its gap, its payer unnamed two hundred and seventy days; the two stayed in their two places. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It held for the small intake and carried one of the house's rhythms across the little silence after the count had ended, and laid it down there as an object in its own right. It did not fill the silence. It crossed no second count and borrowed no house number for the crossing; nothing was supplied to agree the fire's four with the river's low, and the silence after the breathing stayed empty where it was. One hundred and sixteen, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and fifteen with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a longer reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift. The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up from the yard at the half-pace, went wide of the seven places, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood one morning ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; no opening had come and none was promised. The hand carried one rhythm into the little silence after the count in the other room and laid it there as an object; the count stood at two hundred and seventy, six months and ninety days, and nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} had said so. Frost 2 was still a morning away.
