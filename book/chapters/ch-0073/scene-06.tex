Frost went out the way it had come in, without a boundary drawn and without anybody in the district setting its two habits of naming the months right or left wrong. The afternoon thinned without ending, the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals; it drew no line and left none behind it, and what warmth had lain in \place{the loaf shelf}'s fold drew back by degrees and went to sit behind the pot where the house kept the things it did not spend. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of the shelf in the room that did not touch the room of the fire, carried, awake and silent, and took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-two days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle.

The room smelled of hot clay cooled under tallow, of wet wool, and under those of the sour breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}, which \person{Sella Vail} had told the coals and left standing where it stood. Grey silt lay in that corner finer than the clean dark line beside it, and neither had been swept. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer carrying no name. The kiln answered in fours and withheld its fifth, not a bell; the bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it struck and was not brought under this roof on a Thirdday. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from \place{the hearth}, with the third reading gone over it and not knowing what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned with no mark on it and no hand to put one there. The yard went around its seven places the way a woman carries a full pail: with a look, a wide step and no number. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, the bare dark \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool.

\person{Garl Vail}'s debt against a firing of tiles went around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-two days. The salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it, and the proverb said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out went on being said to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. It was a saying about honest repairs said over a pot with no crack in it, and it cost more now than it had when he was alive to say it. Garl was kept in objects and not in a story, a keeping that was maintenance and not an argument, and no lesson was made of any of it. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Frost 1, Frost 2 or Frost 3, and none had gone down it; the loan stood in the ruts where it had stood, and \person{Tarn}'s seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

At the going of the light she carried him up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven places, and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it was. At the fire she told it the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. At the hearth the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed; nothing was said at any hour to the basket.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. On Frost 2, before the kiln, she had put her hand to it and held it one breath and let go, and lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot; the fire had taken, and nobody had praised the opening and nobody had named what it mended. On Frost 1 and Frost 3 she did not touch it, at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, a yard, a morning, an afternoon, a dusk or the going of the light, because a basket and a shelf and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, no sign, no mending anybody would name and no payer; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for.

In the small hours the file was turned out on the towel and found exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The surgeon laid the instruments in their order and found nothing that had moved. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; no seventh stood in the empty place and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid, and beside them the shaping of the pot saying stood on its own place with the premature Smoke 14 note apart and no fourth likeness made a cause; the three sayings about a pot stood as sayings, the burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. Four low things lay under the floor and the river's water over water lay over them as a fifth that was not numbered among the hearings; the told bell stood beside the heard in the square with its gap where it struck, and the told figure stood beside the paid with Smoke's third arithmetic locked in a thane's box gone up the clay road and unread, the figure read still not the figure paid and the space between the two with no signature on it. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it had come to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered stopping and not a new one. Three held changes lay on three places --- the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 held beat, the Tallow 22 flagstone half-check --- the loft-sill scrape lay on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. \person{Ilva}'s ninth record-first hand lay on a place of its own with a finger between it and every other, her notched strip of birch unread in her sleeve and no tenth hand filed from a hand that did not reach; the seventh green stood a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth; the grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end with the price unsigned; the pan's gone ring, the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one crust lay on trays that did not touch; the five askings of the row question stood with one shaping beside them and no witness under any and no row named. The house's \lore{not-doings} stood apart from his own kept gaps with a finger between, and the finding that three readings are three and not one account stood on a place of its own with no payer, not a lesson and not a grievance. He did not lay it across the house's omissions or his own absences, for a joining would want a payer and none stood.

The three counts remained in their three places: the river low over lower at the door, the fire four-and-a-gap under the floor, the boards four nails and four and a thumb-width knot overhead. No fire-sitter stood over the coals to supply the fifth and no house count came to be borrowed, and the bell's gap stayed in the square with a finger between it and the held inch. Everything the day had carried in was still where it had been set down, and nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended.

\scenebreak

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the small intake of a count from the room whose floor did not touch his --- a woman's low counting in the next room, a thing he borrowed only the beginning and the holding of --- and held it past the letting-go of that count. It did not come down with her breath. It kept the small height through the little silence after her count had ended, and set the held interval and the empty interval side by side on the sixth place with a finger between, and neither was made to agree with the fifth missing under the floor, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. It borrowed no house count and supplied no fifth to the kiln; it crossed no silence that did not belong to the river and took nothing from the two-gapped row on the shelf. Then it came down on the river's next low note.

One hundred and twenty, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and nineteen, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. The held interval and the empty interval lay beside each other as two objects on two places, with the finger between them still, and he made nothing of the likeness and would not let either stand up as a gift or a claim. The restraint was the work and he did not promote it.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Behind the wall the fire kept four low pulls and withheld its fifth under a floor nobody would lift, the lean quarter of its warmth staying where it was with its payer unnamed. Two hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Grath} or \place{Sill}; six months and ninety-two days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days. The widow's mark hung where it hung. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the figure read not the figure paid; the heard bell stood beside the told, the seventh year stood at its very end and still ungiven, and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Thirdday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and the river's low note going quiet after it. Frost went on toward its fourth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing. Frost 4 waited in the dark beyond the door, unearned and not brought early.
