The morning thinned after that, the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals --- not ending, only drawing back out of the fold and going behind the pot where the household kept its warmth. The room smelled of hot clay gone cooler under tallow, of wet wool, and under those of the sour breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}, which she had told the coals and left standing where it stood. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, carried and awake and silent, and took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third came in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Sarn} had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-one days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle, and the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; no seventh stood in the empty place and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

The opening stood behind the household and was not looked at again. Nobody went back to \place{the kiln mouth} to see what the fire had made of the coal and come away with a word for it; nobody entered a slate, struck a bell, named a payer or said that a thing had been mended. The boy did not make a doctrine of it either. He kept the not-making as a finding with no payer --- the household had opened a kiln and then refused to turn the act into a lesson, and he set that refusal on a place of its own with a finger between it and everything else. He did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}: the fire kept banked under ash, the charcoal unread beneath the front edge of the shelf, the fifth pull unsupplied under the floor, the crust unlifted, the gap unnamed, the loan uncalled, no number spoken over the basket. He did not lay it across his own kept gaps either --- the missing sixth hearing, the tenth stopping that had not come, the empty interval after a counted breath, the two inches of air between his hand and a pot handle. A joining would want a payer and none stood. The finding was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he would not let it stand up as a lesson, least of all a lesson about a fire that had been lit that morning and had gone on breathing in fours behind a wall.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth. In the afternoon \person{Sella Vail} came to it and lifted a corner of the wool with two fingers. The wool was warm from the stone's keeping, dry on the under side, and she turned the grain once beneath it for damp with a touch lighter than the one she had used the day before, as a surgeon turns a wound to see the part that lies hidden, and drew the wool back over it and set it down where it had lain. The grain was changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would be read over it in the days to come did not know what the turning cost or what the wool had kept from it; the space between the figure and the price stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there. She did not look toward the basket at the edge of the work and gave no instruction to it at any hour.

Back under the roof she carried at the half-pace from every open slate. At \place{the hearth} the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}; every instruction went to the fire and the wool and the pot and the running water, and never to the basket. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on what was not there. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the thin light. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, fed them and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, afternoon or dusk, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

A wrong wind came up off \place{High Kiln} in the afternoon and brought the sweet edge of lead glaze over the boards. It touched the fold at his ear and went out on the wind that brought it, a direction arrived nowhere. The boy kept it where such things were kept: not a second edge paid, not a fourth likeness made a cause, not joined to the colour at a silent woman's mouth years off and not joined to the man carried out of a kiln in the second firing-month. He had no \lore{Grathic} word for the sweet-sickness and no hand to place on any gum, and the not-knowing stayed where it was, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Sella did not connect the wind to anything under her own roof; if she put it in the private roll nobody is shown, she gave no sign of it, and the yard went on with its work.

The afternoon thinned without ending, the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals; it drew no boundary and left none behind it. Sella carried him up the yard at the going of the light at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline called \lore{the thaw} beside it, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, the bare dark \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on this Secondday and none went down it. \person{Garl Vail}'s debt against a firing of tiles went around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-one days. The salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the wheel-seat held its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, and the proverb said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out went on being said and not mended. Garl was kept in objects and not in a story, a keeping that was maintenance and not an argument, and no lesson was made of any of it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very lip of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not. Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it: a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. Tarn said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping there, with no figure and no name --- the ninth stopping remembered, not a tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone was cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot, and the small dry scrape of the sill lay on a place of its own. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} had the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. His bent finger went into the cold \lore{damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, and the end of it arrived at the pot; he read the iron as one piece with two bites, joined to no kiln and named in no Grathic word for both. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the five askings of the row question stood with one further shaping beside them and no witness under any and no row named. The promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

Below the sill a child who had waded after the water went down came up with a foot that did not rewarm. A woman had wrapped it in wool and called it the cold; the yard called the water weather, and the two names never met, so nothing joined them. The boy had no Grathic word for either and no hand to place on the dark foot in the wool. He read what the body did in the order it did it: the extremity going before the core, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody here would perform. He named no cause, no silt-leach and no cure, and wrote nothing across the water and the wool. The finding went on a place of its own beside the withheld number, entered nowhere, and he would not let it stand up as a lesson or a payer.

The heard bell in the square at \place{the Cross} stood beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} in two places, its four, four and a gap kept where the bell struck it and not brought under this roof. Nobody crossed himself at it; it did a temple's work with a Court's hand and nobody in \place{Sill} explained why. No market, no Cross and no bell had come to this Secondday under the Vail roof, and the telling of the bell reached the boy as a direction and a sound and nothing more, not folded into the three counts under the floor and the boards and not heard as a seventh of the full note.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It held through the small intake and the hold and the letting-go, and kept the small height through one whole interval of the river's own going --- water over water, low over lower --- and came down on the river's next low note. It borrowed no house count and crossed no silence that did not belong to the river; it supplied no fifth to the fire and took nothing from the two-gapped row on the shelf. One hundred and eighteen, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and seventeen with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift. The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Behind the wall the fire kept four low pulls and withheld its fifth under a floor nobody would lift, and the lean quarter of its warmth stayed where it was with its payer unnamed, toward the far-off accounting of \lore{the Keeping}. The count rested at two hundred and seventy-one, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-one days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end with the price unsigned, and Frost 3 waited in the dark beyond the door, unearned and not brought early.
