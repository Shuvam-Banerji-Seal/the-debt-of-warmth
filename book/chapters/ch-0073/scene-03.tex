\daymark{271}{Frost 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

The count crossed in the dark from two hundred and seventy to two hundred and seventy-one, and two hundred and seventy-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-one days were told by nobody. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, awake and silent, carried through the small hours and told by nobody what the number counted. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same thing as not wanting it, and the wanting went on beneath the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor had given its fifth unpaid two hundred and seventy-one days and its payer had no name.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-one days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; no seventh stood in the empty place and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

Frost came on in its own morning and was earned by that light. It had not been brought into a day that had not earned it; Smoke had gone out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two names for the months had been set right or left wrong. There was no market, no Cross and no bell under this roof on a Secondday, and the first firing stood in this morning and not a morning off. It was the month's first firing and it stood ahead of the household, behind the wall, waiting to be done and not yet done. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, and the arithmetic that would read over it did not know what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. Smoke's third arithmetic lay behind and unread up \place{the clay road} in a locked box, and the figure read was still not the figure paid.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at \person{Sella Vail}'s throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, hearth or bed since, because a morning and a hearth and a basket and a bed are not openings. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. It had gone so before the first firing of Tallow and of Ember and of Smoke, and it had not gone at a bank or a reading or a dusk between; three openings stood behind this one and this would be a fourth, and four were four and not a rule, for a rule wants a payer and none stood. The touch that would come in this morning's light belonged to a day that had earned it, and it had not been brought early into the small hours or made a rehearsal there.

\scenebreak

First light came up the yard and laid a thin bar along the boards and along the fold at his ear. The room smelled of hot clay under tallow and wet wool, and under those the sour breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}, which she had told the coals and left standing where it stood. He was carried and awake and silent, and the household went on around him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

Sella went to \place{the kiln mouth} before she came to the basket and before any instruction was given. There was no word to the fire first, no telling it the thaw, no turning of the coals with the back of a broken pot; those came after, as they always came after an opening, to a fire that had already taken. She had carried the coal up in the clay dish under its blind ash and she set the dish down within reach of the mouth. The burn across the back of her left hand was shaped like a little door, and it closed on nothing as she reached up. Her coal-black hair had one grey streak at the temple that her husband's winter had put there and that she would not discuss; it showed pale in the thin light off the mouth. \person{Garl Vail} was kept that morning as he was kept every morning, in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease on its peg, the wheel-seat in the shed holding its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, the proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack and said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out, the debt against a firing of tiles going around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-one days. No collector had come up the clay road in the night and none had gone down it.

Her hand went to the widow's mark at her throat. It went to the thumb-length of glazed clay and stayed there for one breath --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go, held once and not held twice --- and then it let go and came away, and did not go back. She lifted the clay lid with the back of the hand, the burn-side up and the fingers curled out of the way, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took. It took without a flourish, the way a thing takes that has been waiting for it: a small seizing at the back, a low gathering, the ash going dark at the edges and the heat coming up through the broken pot's belly. It answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and its payer had no name for two hundred and seventy-one days. The fifth did not stand up. Nobody supplied it and nobody asked who had.

Nobody praised the opening. Nobody said what it had mended. There was no slate entered, no word said over the flame, no bond changed hands and no payer named. The household straightened around the act the way a room straightens around a thing that has been done and will not be spoken of; instruction went back to the fire and the wool and the pot and the running water, and not one word went to the basket. The boy read the opening as an opening and nothing more. He let it be a kiln mouth uncovered and a fire taken, a vessel opened and closed, heat put back where heat had been going out of the house; the surgeon in him saw a thing mended as plainly as he had ever seen a wound closed, and he was not allowed, by anybody, least of all himself, to call it mended. He made no doctrine of four months' openings and did not lay the three behind this one across it to make a cause, for a cause wants a payer under it and none stood. Three openings were three openings and a fourth was a fourth; the count was kept on its own place with a finger between it and everything else.

The loan was not paid by the taking of the fire. \person{Tarn}'s seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given; the debt in the loft was neither settled nor named nor forgiven by anything that happened at the kiln mouth, and no tenth stopping stood up in the household to keep the nine company. The firing was not allowed to settle an account or become a lesson about openings. Nothing in the yard had earned a settlement and nothing in the yard was told it had one.

\scenebreak

Sella came back to \place{the hearth} and told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, fed them and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and her hand shut half over nothing. The pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. Every instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She carried at the half-pace from every open slate, set him on the bed at the low angle of the shelf and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. The two rooms did not touch, and nothing crossed between them but the half-beat at the pot's rim.

The widow's mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, morning, afternoon, dusk or going of the light, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She did not touch it at the hearth where she had just stood, and she did not touch it at the shelf where the two gaps stood. A hearth is where a fire is kept and not where one is opened; a shelf is a shelf; a basket is a basket; a morning is a morning. The rule held as exactly after the opening as before it, and it held in the boy's file as an object with no lesson laid across it.

He kept the house's \lore{not-doings} as a finding with no payer, held apart from his own kept gaps with a finger between. The house kept its fire banked under ash and did not supply the fifth pull; it kept the charcoal unread beneath the front edge of the shelf, the crust unlifted, the gap unnamed, the loan uncalled and no number spoken over the basket. His own gaps were other things on other trays: the missing sixth hearing, the tenth stopping that had not come, the empty interval after a counted breath, the two inches of air between his hand and the pot handle. He did not lay the house's omissions across his own and did not let either become one grievance, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. The keeping of the finding was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he would not let it stand up as a lesson, least of all a lesson about the opening that had just been done.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at the rack end, turned and lifted and turned again across the days and given no figure; the arithmetic that would be read over it still did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure and the price stayed unsigned. Nothing about the morning's fire had changed the grain or the number that would go over it. The seven walked-around places kept their places: the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, the space beneath \place{the drying racks}' lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape or word, the bare dark trough with its water called weather, the field's empty place, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account; the hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust. No collector came up the clay road on this Secondday and none went down it.

In the room that does not touch, a woman had counted breaths while the household went about its work: the small intake, the hold, the letting-go. He did not cross the count and did not fill the little silence after it and did not borrow its rhythm to agree with the fire's four under the floor. The kiln's four-and-a-gap and the river's low-over-lower stayed in their two places, and the empty interval stayed empty on a third with a finger between it and each.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It held for the small intake and for the hold of one of the breaths counted in the room that does not touch, and came down before the letting-go. It crossed no silence and borrowed no stroke; it supplied no fifth to the fire and took no count from the house; it was not a longer reach toward the crust and not a closing of the two inches to the pot handle. One hundred and seventeen, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and sixteen with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift. The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Behind the wall the fire kept four low pulls with the fifth still missing under a floor nobody would lift, and two hundred and seventy-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody.
