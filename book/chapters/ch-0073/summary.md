## Chronicle

Frost came on in its own light, and the boy came into it two hundred and seventy days old, carried and told by nobody what a number counted. One leathery brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear; the thread at his mouth froze; cold came to cheek and temple and the bare wrist, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He took the inventory as a surgeon takes a field and entered none of it. He wanted the crust lifted two inches. That was the louder truth.

Three counts stood in three places: four low pulls under the floor with the fifth missing, water over water at the door, four nails and four and a thumb-width knot overhead. None rose into another, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both, and none stood.

Sella went to the fire before the basket and gave no instruction to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot, said The house will keep it, kept her finger above the charcoal so the tally stayed unread, and carried him at a half-pace from every open slate, going wide around seven places with a look and no number, among them the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out.

On the second morning she went to the kiln mouth before any word. Her hand went to the widow's mark for one breath, and let go. She lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot; the fire took, four with the fifth missing. Nobody praised it. Nobody said what it mended. No slate was entered, no payer named. At a shelf and a bed the mark hung where it hung, for neither is an opening.

Each day the dressed grain was turned beneath the wool for damp and given no figure, and the arithmetic to come did not know what it cost. Tarn kept six and a seventh at the lip of the knife, brought the ear-sentence to the word that would call the loan and stopped, and the loan stood uncalled. Jorra parted dusty forearms from clean and asked no question that would join them to a cause. Ilva at the well kept her count in wood and answered, Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.

Six times the hand went up an inch toward the river's note and came down, borrowing no house count and supplying no fifth. At the close the held interval and the empty interval lay side by side with a finger between, and neither was made to agree with the missing fifth. Two hundred and seventy-two stood exact, complete and spoken by nobody. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended; the hand lay back against the fold, and the river's low note went quiet after it.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** Day 272, Frost 3, Thirdday, the small hours under the Vail kiln-house roof in Low Kiln. Corin/Dan lies on the bed at the low angle of the loaf shelf, awake, silent and carried, with the fold at his ear. The hand has gone up an inch toward the river's note, taken the small intake and hold of a woman's low counting in the room whose floor does not touch his, stayed past that count's letting-go and through the little silence after it, set the held interval and the empty interval side by side with a finger between, and come down on the river's next low note --- answer **one hundred and twenty** --- and lies back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Nobody else is in the room; the woman's counting comes from the other room as a sound with a direction, and Sella has set him down for the night.
- **Day and time at the end:** Day 272, Frost 3, Thirdday, Y.H. 813, small hours. Frost goes toward its fourth morning without a boundary drawn. No market, Cross or bell on a Thirdday and none in this chapter.
- **Three days lived:** Day 270 (Frost 1, Firstday), Day 271 (Frost 2, Secondday), Day 272 (Frost 3, Thirdday), each opened by its own `\daymark` in the first scene of its day and lived in full in two scenes. Answers **115--120**; the count carried from 269 to **272 exact**, six months and ninety-two days, told by nobody.
