\daymark{181}{Ember 2, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

Before first grey the yard had the smell of a wet thing left too near the fire: hot clay under tallow, and under that the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with the basket set low at the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, the wool at his ear and the crust two inches from it gone leathery in the dry heat. The grey came into the yard the way water comes into a shallow pan, without a boundary anybody drew. Three or four men went up \place{the clay road} with their collars high and went low through the finger's-width door of \place{the back room}, and the door kept its finger's width of open after them. They were given no name. \person{Sella Vail} carried past at the half-pace, turned no head toward the door, gave the house no name for them and did not say that the room was cold. The boy did not count men who had been given no name. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side where he kept such things --- one from a man at a well, one off the road down from \place{Greybank} --- and no third came to stand beside them and neither had produced a room. Nobody followed the men in and nobody said what the room held; it kept its finger's width of open and gave back nothing but cold.

Then \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened. It was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The surgeon's eye in him parted the two the way it parts a clot from the vessel that holds it: one was the material of the work, the other the material of a room, and the room had got into the creases of a hand and stayed there. Slate dust ground that fine goes wherever the hand goes; it sits under the nail and shows white when the fist opens, and no washing of a working man's hands in a yard bucket takes it all out. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, the way another man might keep a soiled rag and a clean rag folded on the same shelf, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard went around them, entered by nobody and counted into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone, as he had spoken to it a hundred mornings. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” He said it flat, to the wheel, and the wheel gave back the small wooden complaint that was its only answer and went on turning. A pot came up under his hands and he set the rim true with the bent finger and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. Three turns before the pot left the wheel he knew the note it would give --- knew it the way a man knows the give of a shoulder before he lays hands on it, from the wall of it, the water in it, the small unevenness at the foot --- and he kept the knowing where he kept the rest of his counsel. He did not say it aloud.

A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind stood at the shed edge with the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one corner of his mouth, the taste of it gone green on his lip, and let his eye rest on the turning pot. Jorra's hands went on. The kiln-master said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early, and said it in the tone of a man passing judgment he would not be asked to pay for; the wrong wind had brought the sweetish edge with it and took it away again when it turned, and neither the man nor his saying stayed in the yard. Jorra did not answer him and did not look at him. The knowing went to the near-full tray where the other near-full answers lay --- the pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, the rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and this third thing withheld from a man who would have made something of it --- beside the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never among them, advanced and not paid. No pot was lost in the yard. No lesson was made and no room was changed.

A finding held back is still a finding, and a chart closed over it with the neat margin showing is a chart that will be believed when it is carried into a room with a clean hand at the bottom of it. He had learned that in a country whose name he carried and did not speak, and here he had only a tray that no other eye could see. He kept the knowing on its own tray and would not promote it into a hearing, because an empty place kept empty among six is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The sixth hearing, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, lay where a thing out of a person must lie and never among the sources; no seventh had come and none had been invented to stand up as one. He spent the better part of the morning taking the likenesses down one at a time so that a row of them would not stand up and call itself a truth.

\scenebreak

At \place{the hearth} Sella's voice went low and unfinished. “The fire's low. It wants---” she said, and the end of the sentence hung in the room the way a tool hangs from a nail when the hand that would take it has stopped short. Jorra's bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand and stood there. The iron took the warmth out of the fingertip and gave nothing back; it was a piece of iron that did what iron does and asked nothing of the room. He waited while she finished “feeding,” gave the end of the instruction to the pot, and only then moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a fraction held in the holding and not in a word. Nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named to it. The household straightened around his silence as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and the fraction lay where it was left --- not paired with the beat held beneath \place{the loaf shelf} on an older day, not paired with the half-check a clay-bound broom had made on \place{the flagstones} on another. Three places were three places and not a rule with a payer standing to it.

From the basket's low angle the boy read the iron again as one piece with two bites. One bite holds a vessel whole while the fire is brought up under it; the other can take the hand off a man at the wrist and leave him to bleed into the dust. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. He knew what a surgeon knows, that the clamp is the same instrument in the same hand whether it is set to save or set to stop, and here the instrument lay joined to no kiln and entered in no slate, an object worth nothing that could take a hand off a man without anybody asking who would pay for the hand. He kept the two bites on one piece of iron and let them lie. A thing with two uses is not therefore two things, and a thing with two uses is not therefore a doctrine; the iron had no name for either bite and went on being iron.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark of \place{the kindling}'s sill where the house could put a finger and check them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, held the way a man holds a thing he has not yet made up his mind is his to spend. The small knife lay at the very edge with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, and neither knife nor sentence had been entered where the house could read them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark. The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. On the flagstones his stroke went over the shallow bright patch without the half-check. There had been a day when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron ahead of the wheel-keeper's unfinished sentence and this broom checked half a beat behind it and went on without a word or a turn of the head; that movement lay where it had been left, a place in a yard, and he did not repeat it now. The surgeon's register read three held changes on three places and would not let them stand up together and call themselves one thing: the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath the loaf shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on the flagstones in a yard that touched neither. Not a pair. Not a rule. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of the three.

At the racks the yard went around its absences as a woman carrying a pail of hot water goes around a child's place at a bench: the clean dark line where the ridge had turned, the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring on the wall where the shallow pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. A look for each and a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The two unswept lines lay side by side and neither was swept and neither had an instruction, and the yard kept its own road around them the way water keeps its road around a stone that lies where a stone lies. The absences stayed on separate trays and did not touch. No third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line: three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-one days taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

A neighbour come up from the tubs with a pot still wet asked what the newly opened firing wanted set down under this roof. Sella's eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall; her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.” She gave the question to the pot and the weather and the running water, and the neighbour carried it away level in the arm that had carried the pot, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused, the question arriving nowhere and leaving no entry. A cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction; the surgeon's register had learned that in a district whose ledgers were kept in a clean hand, and kept the shape of it without making a rule of it. The jar stood cold under its glaze and was not touched, because a question about a cost is not an opening and no opening came in this yard on this morning.

From the basket's low angle the underside of the loaf shelf became a roof over him, a board with a grain like the inside of a lid, and the charcoal waited under its front edge for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come. Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her finger did not go beneath the board. The beat that had stayed under it on an older day stayed where it was left, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look, and the marks waited unread, uncounted, uncopied. The boy did not read them, did not count them, did not copy them and did not show them to anybody, having no hand that could have lifted the least of the marks and no word in which to have shown them if he had. The roof of board above him held its two gaps and gave neither of them a name.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-one days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Cold came cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. One hundred and eighty-one stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The loan stayed uncalled, the ninth stopping stayed nine, the Seed 16 breath stayed held, the three changes stayed on their three places, \lore{the widow's mark} had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward.
