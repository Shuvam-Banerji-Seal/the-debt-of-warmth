# Chapter 43 — "Before the Kiln" (beat sheet)

**Days covered.** Day 180, Ember 1, Firstday; Day 181, Ember 2, Secondday; Day 182, Ember 3, Thirdday; Y.H. 813. A normal three-day chapter. Every day opens with its own `\daymark` and is lived in full; nothing is skipped, poured into another or summarised. Weekday labels are checked against `bible/calendar.md` (*t* = 336 + *N* − 1). There is no Account-day and no market in these three days: the first of Ember falls on Day 189 (Ember 10), which belongs to Chapter 46, so there is no Cross, no bell rung, no Berr, no Hessa, no green-mouthed woman, no foundling and no soft-cuffed clerk on stage. The district keeps calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month; neither usage is corrected in narration. The boy is one hundred and eighty days old and six months exactly on the first morning, one hundred and eighty-one and six months and one day on the second, one hundred and eighty-two and six months and two days on the third, and nobody under any roof says so.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** The first firing of Ember brings a **real opening** under the Vail roof on the morning of Day 180. Sella puts her hand to the widow's mark *before* the kiln mouth, holds it one breath, lets go, lifts the clay lid and tips the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. Nobody praises it and nobody sees it broken. The rule is held exactly as on Tallow 1 (Day 150), the fire still answers four low pulls with the fifth missing, and its payer still has no name. This is a movement of T058/T079 and T088 — advanced, not paid; no slate entered, no lesson made, no bond changed hands. Nothing else is settled in the chapter: no name, no figure, no payer, no seventh hearing, no tenth stopping, no third gap, no joining, no calling of the loan, no opening of any private record.

**Continuation from Chapter 42 (not recapped).** Day 179, Tallow 30, is behind and is not revisited. The basket came home from the last market and was set on the bed; the box went up the clay road locked with the true subtraction inside; Berr's slate went to Greybank unread; the clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing; the bell spent four, four and a gap; Hessa delivered her slate; the count stood at one hundred and seventy-nine, exact and unspent. Scenes 1–4 in this plan carry that state forward in silence.

## Scene 1 — "The Month Opens Without a Boundary"
**Day and time.** Day 180, Ember 1, Firstday: before first grey through the opening of the kiln, roughly first light to mid-morning.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof: the bed, the hearth, the loaf shelf's low angle, the kiln mouth, and the edge of the Clay Yard the basket crosses on the way out.

**Who is present.** `dan-marchetti` (Corin, carried, set down with his back to the kiln), `sella-vail`. Jorra is at the wheel in the shed, heard and glimpsed through the finger's-width door but not on stage with a line; Tarn is in the yard at his work, seen only as the going of a broom. No walk-on speaks.

**Beat sheet, in order.**
1. Open on the boy, one hundred and eighty days old and six months exactly, nobody saying so. The month turns without a boundary drawn in the narration. The first smell: hot clay under tallow and wet wool, under it the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody has swept in two years. The first sound: the kiln drawing breath in fours with a gap — not a bell. Use the drop capital (`\dropcap{O}{...}` or a suitable first word); this is the chapter's first `\daymark` (`\daymark{180}{Ember 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}`).
2. The three counts, kept in three places and not allowed to become one: the carried fire under the floor at four low pulls and a missing fifth, its payer unnamed for a hundred and eighty days; the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented as a seventh; the boards overhead at four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb (T088, T061/T082).
3. The body on the page: the stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the frozen thread of drool nobody wipes, the head that goes where it wants and makes the small wet sound, the hand outside the wool that goes up an inch toward the river's note and comes down against the fold. The adult register and the infant body quarrel; the body is louder.
4. Sella gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. *"Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind."* *The house* where another woman would say *I*. Half-pace. One glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze, hand starting and shutting half over nothing without touching it. Coal-black hair pushed back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak shows pale off the coals. Pot rim set out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand; her hand rests a half-beat longer at the setting (T058/T079).
5. She carries him out to the kiln mouth and sets the basket with its back to the opening. The yard goes around the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the pan's gone place on the wall and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look and a wide step and no number, no explanation given to the basket (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried).
6. **The meaningful change.** The first firing of Ember: her hand goes to the widow's mark at her throat *before* the kiln, holds one breath, lets go; the clay lid comes up; the carried coal goes under the back of a broken pot; the fire takes. Nobody praises it, nobody sees it broken. Render it as a procedure with a body, a tool and a price, in the surgeon's register: an opening performed on a vessel by a hand that will not name what it mends; the heat behind a screen and a gate of two bites. No payer named. The fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been (T058/T079 advanced, T088).
7. Close on his wanting the crust, the breast, the pot handle two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and getting none of them.

**Emotional turn.** From an ordinary month-turning that no one marks, through the one exact act that is an opening, back down to the unreached wanting of a body that cannot ask. The household straightens around a thing nobody will name; the reader is allowed to see the mark touched, and no character praises it.

**Sensory anchors.** Hot clay under tallow; wet wool gone damp at the ear and frozen again; sour Ash tang in the unswept corner; the kiln's breath in fours with a gap; river water over water; the small wet sound of the head going too far; the stiff leathery crust; the little-door burn; the lead-glazed clay of the widow's mark at the throat; coal tipping under a broken pot; grey streak pale off the coals.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (180 exact, six months), T088 (payer unnamed 180 days), T058/T079 (instruction, half-pace, mark before the kiln — the real opening), T009/T013/T092 and T098 (yard absences gone around), T060/T086 (crust and its gap), T061/T082 (three counts, no seventh), T077 (cost as direction, carried), T004 (old life offstage; surgeon's register colours the noticing).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Plants nothing new; advances the carried fire's unnamed fifth toward Sella's second roll (~330) and the Hearth as a wound, and the mark-before-the-kiln rule toward future openings. No thread paid, no name given to any payer.

**Exact last image.** His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold, with one hundred and eighty exact and unspent in the dark of the wool, the fire missing its fifth under the floor and the month named for what the kilns are while the district keeps its own word for it.

## Scene 2 — "Six Counted, the Seventh Held"
**Day and time.** Day 180, Firstday: afternoon into the small hours. No new dated heading (continues Day 180).

7. 8. Small hours: he lays out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel. Six hearings beside and never among — five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go; no seventh, none invented, an empty place kept empty being a finding and a filled one a forged finding (T061/T082). **Emotional turn.** The one opening is put behind the household; the equality resumes exactly, the loan held at the teeth and not called, the debt's stroke unchecking, the keeper's hand staying off the shelf. The boy's inspection is exact and buys him nothing.

**Sensory anchors.** Broom on the sill and flagstones; knife at the lip; Ash in the knuckles; pale clip-ring; worn thumb-mark; lips moving along the loaf row; charcoal under the front edge; woodsmoke and river wind meeting on the stair of the evening; broken pot turning coals; grey streak pale; pot handle two inches beyond the hand; the file laid on an imaginary towel.

## Scene 3 — "The Dust Ground Small as Meal"
**Day and time.** Day 181, Ember 2, Secondday: before first grey into the morning.

**Beat sheet, in order.**
1. New `\daymark` (`\daymark{181}{Ember 2, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}`). Three or four men go low into the finger's-width-open back room before first grey and get no name. Sella carries past at the half-pace, turns no head, gives the house no name for them and does not say the room is cold. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stand side by side with no third (T065/T095). The room is never entered by the narration.
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist opened — not bone-coloured slip. It cares if they're steady."* Tells a pot *"You'll do."* Knows its full note three turns before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master come up on the wrong wind with the sweetish edge of lead glaze at one corner of his mouth, who says a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. 4. When Sella's voice goes low and unfinished at the hearth, Jorra's bent finger goes to the cold iron of the damper and waits while her sentence reaches the pot; only then does the tongue move a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the iron. 5. The boy reads the iron again: one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.
6. Tarn takes the broom only after its instruction; his stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check. The three held changes stay three on three places (T036/T076).
7. At the racks the yard goes around the clean dark line, the new finer grey called the thaw, the rack space with its broken birth-slates, the pan's gone place and the field's empty place, with a look and a wide step and no number; T098 carried, not paid (T009/T013/T092).
8. A neighbour with a wet pot asks what the newly opened firing costs set down under this roof. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, does not touch it, and sends her to the pot: *"Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it."* The question goes away level, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused (T077).
9. From the basket's low angle the underside of the loaf shelf becomes a roof and the charcoal waits. Sella does not reach beneath the board; the boy does not read, count, copy or show the marks (T100).

**Emotional turn.** The gathered danger of the month takes physical form in dust, a wrong wind and an early note, and the household absorbs it by withholding: the note withheld, the back room unnamed, the cost sent to the pot, the fraction held at the iron. Nothing becomes an entry or a rule.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey dust under nails and in white knuckle creases; damp clay and tallow; sweetish lead glaze on the wrong wind; the wheel's rhythm; *"You'll do"*; cold iron of the damper taking the heat out of a fingertip; broom on flagstones; the shallow bright patch; wet pot and running water; charcoal under the shelf's front edge.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 and T062 (men unnamed, dust ground small as meal, withheld knowing, bent finger waiting at the damper, damper's double bite), T065/T095 (two shut-room tellings, no third), T061/T082 (near-full knowing beside and never among), T036/T076 (flagstone half-check not repeated), T077 (cost sent to pot), T009/T013/T092 and T098 (yard absences), T100 (charcoal unread), T058/T079 (half-pace, jar glanced at untouched), T071/T072 and T088 (181 exact, payer unnamed).
