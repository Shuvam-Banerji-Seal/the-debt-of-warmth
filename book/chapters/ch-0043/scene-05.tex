\daymark{182}{Ember 3, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday)}

The third day of the kiln month came in without anybody marking where the second had ended, the way water goes over water and leaves no seam for a man to put his finger in. Hot clay lay under tallow in the yard before the grey was properly in the boards, and wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. \person[Corin]{the boy} was carried out into \place{the Clay Yard} before first grey with the fold at his ear, one hundred and eighty-two days old and six months and two days, and nobody under any roof said either number and there was no word in the house that would have carried them. The thread at his mouth had frozen again in the small hours and stayed frozen; nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told nothing.

At \place{the kiln mouth} \person{Sella Vail} set him with his back to the opening, the way a vessel is set where the heat comes at it from behind and not into the face, and went about the day's tending at her half-pace. The firing of a new month is one thing and the days after it are another, and the second working day was ordinary. The kiln drew breath behind the wall in fours with a gap, four low pulls and the silence where a fifth should have been, then four again. It was not a bell. A bell spends four, four and a gap and leaves a hole in the hearing that a man may cross himself over or may not; the kiln spends four and keeps the gap in its own breathing and has never asked anybody to count it. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-two days. Through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and he spent the whole of the morning keeping them from becoming one, the way a man keeps instruments apart on a towel before he needs any of them.

The month's danger had an order and no name. It could be laid out in the order a man lays out a field --- hot clay under tallow; fine grey slate dust ground small as meal, sitting white in the crease of a knuckle when a fist opens; the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} riding up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind; and a pot set down wrong giving its high thin note before its time --- but it could not be carried into a chart, because a chart wants a name at the bottom of it and a payer under the name, and nothing in this yard had either. An order without a name is a thing a man can notice and cannot enter. In the old country a neat hand could take an order, set a figure under it and send it into a room with a signature at the foot, and the finding had been worth nothing once it got there; here there was no neat hand and no room and no signature, only a yard going around what it would not speak of.

The danger was in the dust before it was anywhere else. \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's grey worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. It had got into the creases of a hand that had opened and closed all morning and stayed there white when the fist opened again. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said flat, and the wheel gave back its small wooden complaint and went on turning. A pot came up under his hands, and he set the rim true with the bent finger and told it, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. Three turns before the pot left the wheel he knew the note it would give, from the wall of it and the water in it and the small unevenness at the foot, and kept the knowing where he kept his counsel. No pot was lost in the yard. No lesson was made and no room was changed.

A kiln-hand come up on the wrong wind stood at the shed edge with the sweet taste of glaze gone green at one corner of his mouth; the wind had brought the sweetness with it and would take it away again when it turned. He let his eye rest on the turning pot and said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early, in the tone of a man passing a judgment he would not be asked to pay for. He said nothing about a counting room. No third telling of the room shut in the war came to stand beside the two that were already there, and the two stayed where they were, likeness and difference kept, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. A saying about a pot is not a saying about a room, and a man who would make the one out of the other had not come up this wind. Jorra did not answer him and did not look at him, and the knowing went to the near-full tray with the others, beside the hearings of \lore{the full note} and never among them.

\scenebreak

At the racks the yard went around the places it keeps as other households keep their tools, by knowing where they lie and not touching them. Two lines lay unswept at the Vail corner: the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it, neither swept, neither given an instruction. The yard went around them with a broom and a glance. It went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody and counted into nothing; past the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. At \place{the yard well} the \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. A look for each and a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The absences stayed on separate trays and did not touch; no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-two days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

A neighbour come up from the tubs with a pot still wet asked what the second day's firing wanted with the kiln newly open. Sella's eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall shelf; her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. “Ask the pot what a firing wants set down there,” she said. “Ask the weather. It's the weather's month. The house has said it.” She gave the question to the pot and the weather and the running water, and the neighbour carried it away level in the arm that had carried the pot, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused. The question arrived nowhere and left no entry. A cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction; the surgeon's register had learned that in a district whose ledgers were kept in a clean hand, and kept the shape of it without making a rule of it.

Later a woman set a pot down wrong on the rack's edge. It gave its high thin note before its time, a sound with the edge off it, and went on giving nothing after the note had died. The woman did not hear it. She turned and went back to the tubs with her hands wet, and the pot stood where it had been set and was not lost. The boy turned his head toward it the way a man turns toward a sound in a room before he has decided what the sound is, and got no further, for he had no hand to set a knuckle against it and no word to tell anybody what he had heard. He filed it where a danger goes and not among the near-full answers, because a note out of season is a fault in the handling and not a fourth likeness waiting to be promoted. A potter told a pot it would do and a pot stood where it was put, and the yard went on.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, and he stood a moment with the handle in his hand the way a man stands with a tool he has been told to take up and has not yet made up his mind to own. “Six counted,” he said to the wood. “The next I don't count.”

Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark of \place{the kindling}'s sill where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, held the way a man holds a thing he has not yet decided is his to spend. \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside the small knife, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, and neither knife nor sentence had been entered where the house could read them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark. The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He set the kindling handle-out and edge-in, nothing that could roll, and went to the stones.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check. There had been a day when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and this broom checked half a beat behind it and went on without a word or a turn of the head; that movement lay where it had been left, a place in a yard, and he did not repeat it now. The surgeon's register read three held changes on three places and would not let them stand up together and call themselves one thing: the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath the loaf shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on these stones in a yard that touched neither. Not a pair. Not a doctrine. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of the three. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it, and no collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it. He said \emph{Yes, house} to a neighbour who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying. In the clay pot in the loft above the shed the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came to his teeth and went no further; he drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it, and no tenth stopping stood up before the seventh year was earned.

\scenebreak

From the basket's low angle the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} became a roof over him, a board with a grain like the inside of a lid that nobody had meant him to read. Sella stood with her back to it and moved her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze, and her hand started and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge. The charcoal tally lay under the board where it had lain since the night she put a finger beneath it and held it one counted breath without looking, two years gone and still unread, and an afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as that night. The beat stayed where it was left, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. He did not read the marks, did not count them, did not copy them and did not show them to anybody, having no hand that could have lifted the least of them and no word in which to have shown them if he had. The charcoal waited under the board for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come.

The pot under the shed wall kept its nearly-full answer where it had first been filed, air under the note and a want in it; the rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again kept its place beside it; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on the wrong wind kept a third, advanced and not paid, in a tray of their own. None was promoted into a hearing, and no fourth likeness was made of the three. Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and eighty-two days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at Sella's throat where it had hung since the first firing; it had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning, and at no shelf and no afternoon and no yard since, because an ordinary tending is not an opening.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made to stand up as a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Cold came cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces --- and he was the only adult in this yard and the least use in it. One hundred and eighty-two stood exact and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The loan stayed uncalled, the ninth stopping stayed nine, the Seed 16 breath stayed held, the three changes stayed on their three places, the two shut-room tellings stayed two, and the danger of the month kept its order and no name and went unpaid. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
