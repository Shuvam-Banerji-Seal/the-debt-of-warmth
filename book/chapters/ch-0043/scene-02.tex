The basket had been set down near \place{the kindling}, and the broom had been given its instruction before anybody gave it to \person{Tarn}. From the low angle of the basket \person[Corin]{the boy} watched the order of it the way he watched every order in this house: the eye going to the sill first, the hand going second, and the two never swapped. Tarn took the broom up as a man takes up a tool that has been named in front of him. “Six counted,” he said to the wood. “The next I don't count.”

He said it to the wood and not to a person, and the wood gave back nothing, which suited him. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark of the sill where the house could put a finger and check them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone and none would go, held the way a man holds a thing he has not yet made up his mind is his to spend. The small knife lay at the very edge with \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, and neither knife nor sentence had been entered where the house could read them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth as he reached, the way a thing comes up in a man's mouth before he has decided to say it. It came all the way to the word that would have called the loan and stood there. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. No figure came with it and no name; he stood no tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned, and the nine stoppings stayed nine objects on a tray that could not be seen, with no payer standing to any of them. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled. The Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not.

“Yes, house,” he said to an early thaw, and a moment later, “Yes, house,” to a late one, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on. He went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the month had turned and the kilns stood open and the paying stood where the water had left it, in the ruts. Wanting a thing and agreeing with its opposite were two rooms in one house, and he had lived in both all his life. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came. In the clay pot in the loft above the shed the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl Vail died lay two years gone and cold; he had not looked at it in a year and had stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt, which is not the same as forgiving it. A kindness that cannot be entered is a kindness a man cannot pay back, and he left it lying where it lay and let it stay cold.

On \place{the flagstones} his stroke went over the shallow bright patch without the half-check. There had been a day when the wheel-keeper's bent finger stood in the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence and his broom checked half a beat behind it and went on without a word or a turn of the head; that movement lay where it had been left, a place in a yard, and he did not repeat it now. The surgeon's register read three held changes on three places and would not let them stand up together and call themselves one thing: the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath \place{the loaf shelf} in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on the flagstones in a yard that touched neither. Not a pair. Not a rule. A rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of the three. The boy kept them on three places because that was where they were, and spent the better part of an afternoon taking them down one at a time so that three likenesses standing in a row would not stand up and call themselves a truth.

\person{Sella Vail} came through with the basket's weight still in her shoulder and gave the half-beat to nothing. At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to him and moved her lips along a row with two gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge where the charcoal tally lay. The beat that had stayed under the board on an older day stayed where it was left, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look; the marks waited under the edge for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come, and she gave the shelf no word and the boy no reason for standing as she stood.

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of \place{Low Kiln} and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty mornings kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost --- the height of a handle, the give of a shoulder, the turn of a wrist at a step's end, a record kept and not an entry. At the edge of the yard she went around the place on the wall where the shallow pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, looking for no scrape and finding no word; around \place{the trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, bare and dark, whose water is called weather; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie, entered by nobody; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. A look for each and a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside the clean dark line in the unswept corner and neither was swept and neither had an instruction, and the household went around both as it went around the pan's round ring and the field's empty place, which stayed on separate trays and did not touch.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and never told and not to be entered now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing and was not touched at a shelf, a road, a dusk or a bed, because a shelf, a road, a dusk and a bed are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The one opening of the day was behind the household and nobody had praised it and nobody had seen what it mended. The two rooms do not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not lift the least of them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a kiln-master come up on the wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard.

The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke over the bright patch, and his own held breath from a market morning. On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stands to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third and neither had produced a room; the told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself at the stopping; the sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent.

The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its empty place beside it, the shallow pan's gone ring and the field's empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap had come, no fourth likeness had been made a cause and no finger of his had drawn the line: three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty days taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept at this hour and neither with an instruction.

Cold had arrived cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces --- and he was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it. One hundred and eighty stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof; the loan stayed uncalled, the ninth stopping stayed nine, the Seed 16 breath stayed held, the three changes stayed on their three places, the mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward.
