The afternoon thinned without ending. It lost its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun, the heat going out of \place{the Clay Yard} by degrees and the work winding down not into stillness but into the smaller sounds a house makes once it has stopped being listened to. Hot clay still lay under tallow, and wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral breath of the \lore{Ash} corner nobody had swept in two years. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay low where they had set him, the fold at his ear and the crust two inches from it gone leathery and pale in the dry heat of the kiln month, the thread at his mouth frozen since the small hours and still frozen. Nobody had been given an instruction about the thread. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot is told its business and left to it.

\person{Sella Vail} came and stood with her back to him at \place{the loaf shelf} and moved her lips along a row standing with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall; her hand started toward it, shut half over nothing and came away without touching. The row and the jars and the gaps and the wind at the door were told what they were and what they wanted. The basket was told nothing. She took up her half-pace and kept it, the way a woman keeps a vessel level across a floor she knows by heart.

Her finger did not go beneath the front edge. The charcoal tally lay under the board where it had lain since the night she put a finger beneath it and held it one counted breath without looking, two years gone and still unread, and an afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same as that night. The beat stayed where it was left, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. He did not read the marks, did not count them, did not copy them and did not show them to anybody; he had no hand that could have lifted the least of them and no word in which to have shown them if he had. He kept the second state on a place of its own rather than let it stand in for the first, because a holding remembered is not a holding made again. The charcoal waited under the board for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} had his knife out and put it back. The small blade lay on the sill of \place{the kindling} at the very edge where a man could check it and find it where it should be, and six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, held the way a man holds a thing he has not yet decided is his to spend. \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, and neither the knife nor the sentence had been entered where the house could read them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark.

The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He set the kindling handle-out and edge-in, nothing that could roll, and drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it. The ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came to his teeth and went no further. No figure came with it, no name, no tenth stopping stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the loan stayed uncalled. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with his work, and in the clay pot in the loft the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} worked at the wheel with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger leading. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; the broken birth-slates lay beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, entered by nobody. Three or four men had gone low into the finger's-width-open room before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said flat, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. Three turns before the pot left the wheel he knew the note it would give and kept the knowing back from a kiln-master come up on a wrong wind, and no pot was lost in the yard.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and waited while her sentence reached the pot, and only then did the tongue move a hair's breadth and leave the finger standing in the iron. The fraction was held and unspoken. It was a holding remembered, not a change made twice over, and nobody spoke of the waiting and no payer was named to it, and it lay on neither of the other two places. He did not look toward the basket and did not say the room was cold.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. She carried the weight in the arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-two mornings, and the arm kept the height of the handle and the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that gave no account of the cost.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer; around \place[the yard well]{the well}, where \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather; wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. She gave each a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, and a wide step, and no number, and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She told \lore{the carried fire} it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would say \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. She did not look toward the bed.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of the month, and at no shelf and no well and no afternoon and no dusk since, because a shelf and a well and an afternoon and a dusk are not openings. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms did not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and eighty-two days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, each with its own small shape.

On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any and held apart by hand, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the nine. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Three held changes stood on three places and he would not let them stand up together and call themselves one thing: the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath the loaf shelf in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on \place{the flagstones} in a yard that touched neither. Not a pair. Not a doctrine. Not a rule. A rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third and neither had produced a room. The told bell kept its place beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself at the stopping. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent; the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called.

The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its gap, the pan's gone round ring and the empty place in the returning line lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-two days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-two days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. Ash lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold came cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The figure read was not the figure paid; the box had gone up the clay road locked with the true subtraction inside; the cords and the knot-string and the second twist stayed where no Court thumb would read them; the clerk stayed set for an uncalled measure; the sixth green stayed a colour and the foundling a shape. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces --- and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

One hundred and eighty-two stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended; the kiln month had opened without a boundary drawn and the count went forward with the fire's fifth still missing, the ninth stopping still nine, the Seed 16 breath still held, the three changes still on their three places, the mark touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, and the charcoal still waiting under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
