\daymark{180}{Ember 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay under tallow} came into the room before the grey did, and wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the corner nobody had swept in two years. It was the smell of the firing month and the smell of the kiln month at once, for the kilns had stood at their heat through Tallow and would stand at it through Ember and no hand in the district had drawn a line where one name turned into the other. The yard called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither saying was wrong and neither had been corrected; the heat went on behind the wall under both names the way a man's breath goes on while he is deciding what to call the weather. The smell settled into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay and stayed there. He was one hundred and eighty days old this morning and six months exactly, and nobody under any roof had said either number, and there was no word in this house that would have carried them.

Under the smell, under the floor, the kiln drew breath in fours with a gap. Four low pulls, then the silence where a fifth should have been, then four again. It was not a bell. A bell spent four, four and a gap and left a hole in the hearing that a man might cross himself over or might not, and the told bell kept its place beside the heard bell and the two had never become one; the kiln spent four and left the gap in its breathing and went on, and had never asked anybody to count it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing he had never counted among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} and had never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. \lore{The carried fire} under the floor answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty days.

Three counts in three places. He had spent every one of those days keeping them apart: a gap in a wall, a note in a doorway, a knot in a board, and no account standing at the bottom of them. A man who lets three counts lie together long enough will wake to find they have stood up and called themselves a truth, and a truth with a payer at the bottom of it is an account, and no payer stood to any of these. He had learned that in a better-lit room, among people who wrote things down, and had brought the habit into a house that kept its days in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces; it made him no friends and bought him nothing, and he kept it anyway, the way a man keeps a instrument clean when there is nobody to see it.

The crust lay two inches from his ear, stiff and brown-edged, gone leathery in the dry heat of the last tend, with the empty place beside it still without a name and no third. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had frozen, a small bright line nobody wiped. His head went where it wanted, and when it went too far it made the wet sound that nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about either. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew what he wanted. He wanted the crust. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, close enough to see and not to close on. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted. The wanting was small and immediate and animal, and it was louder than the man who knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, the almost-smile that cold puts on a face and that is a grimace and not peace. Both were true. The body was louder. The surgeon's register read the room as a field laid out and covered, an instrument kept behind a screen, a vessel that would not bear the taking off; the body read it as two inches of wool between a mouth and a crust. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.

\person{Sella Vail} was already at \place{the hearth} when the instruction went out, and it went out ahead of her hands. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she said to the wool, which had no ears, and to the wind, which had nothing to give back, and not to the basket. She said it to the fire and the pot and the broom and the running water before she said anything to a person, and to the basket she said nothing at all, because a basket set down is a pot set down and a pot is told nothing. “The house wants the cloth dry,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She had said \emph{the house} for three years and had never once corrected it, and the boy had never once heard her correct it, which was its own kind of answer and not one he could enter anywhere.

She moved at the half-pace, the pace that keeps a filled pot level and a carried thing clear of a slate's open edge. Her little-door burn shut on nothing as she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried it, so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; she said the streak had come in the winter Garl died and would not discuss it further, and nobody under the roof had asked her twice. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. She gave nobody a figure. At \place{the loaf shelf}, standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along a row with two gaps, she set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. Then she lifted him base first and level, the way a woman lifts a filled pot from a board, and carried him out.

Through the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the wheel was going and a forearm grey with slip to the colour of old bone came round and went again; no word came out of the shed into the yard. Somewhere a broom went over \place{the flagstones} without checking at the shallow bright patch, and the going of it was \person{Tarn} and the going of it was all the boy saw of him. The woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of \place{Low Kiln} and the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, and the two passed on the stair of the morning as they had passed every morning of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either.

At \place{the kiln mouth} she set the basket down with its back to the opening, the way a vessel is set where the heat will come at it from behind and not into a child's face. She went around the yard the way the yard goes around what it will not name: past the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay and nobody went; past the round place on the wall where the shallow pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer; and wide again around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. A look for each and a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The basket was a pot set down, and a pot is told nothing.

The first firing of Ember was an opening, and she performed it the way a hand performs an opening on a vessel it will not name. Before she went near the kiln she put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark} on its cord at her throat, the thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, held it one breath, let go. The intake came and the hold came and the letting go came, and the mark went back to hanging where it had hung, and nobody in the yard praised it and nobody saw what it mended. Then the clay lid came up. She lifted it with the back of a hand and set it where it would not rock, the way a covering is lifted off a field before the instrument comes down, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot with a movement of the wrist that had done it a hundred times and would not have called it a gift. Heat was a thing kept behind a screen in this house and let through the gate of two bites --- the holding and the taking, one piece of \lore{the damper} iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful --- and this was the one hour in the month when the screen came up. The fire took. The carried fire under the floor answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had answered every pull since the boy came under the roof, and no payer was named to the gap and no slate was entered and no lesson was made.

The household straightened around the thing that had been done. Sella turned the coals with the back of the broken pot, told the fire it wanted feeding, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening, the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, and the intake came and the hold came and the letting go came and nothing more opened. She did not touch the mark at a pot or a basket or a morning's work. The wheel went on behind the finger's-width door. The broom went on over the flagstones. Two people kept two silences under one roof and neither made anything of the other's, and the boy set the two down on separate places without drawing a line between them, because two silences standing side by side will call themselves an equality if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer.

Then she carried him in and set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread frozen, set the pot rim out again with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. The widow's mark hung where it hung and was not touched at a bed or a morning's end, for a bed is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms do not touch.

The boy lay with his back to the opening and wanted what a body wants and what a man cannot ask for. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand. He wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The register in him read the morning as an opening performed on a vessel by a hand that would not name what it mended, heat kept behind a screen, a fifth pull missing under the floor with no payer to it, an inventory exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces; he was the only adult under this roof and the least use in it. The figure read was not the figure paid; the box had gone up the road locked; the clerk stayed set at the landing for a measure nobody called. Nothing in the room had a mouth that would say what a number counted, and the one mouth that could have asked had not yet learned to make a sound anybody would answer.

Through the finger's-width door the river went on with its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never made to stand up as a seventh; under the floor the fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. One hundred and eighty stood exact and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof, while the district went on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and the kilns kept their heat through both names without a boundary drawn between them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The one opening was done and put behind the household; nothing else was joined, named, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward.
