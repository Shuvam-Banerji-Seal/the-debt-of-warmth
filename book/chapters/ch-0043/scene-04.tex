The afternoon laid itself over \place{the Clay Yard} the way woodsmoke lays itself along the ground in low bars, going under the racks and over the pots and into the corner nobody had swept, taking the colour of everything and giving nothing back. \person[Corin]{the boy} was set low at the angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} became a roof over him, the wool at his ear, the crust two inches from it gone leathery in the dry heat of the kiln month. He could see the board's grain the way a man sees the inside of a lid when the lid is off and lying beside him --- a face turned up that nobody meant him to read.

\person{Sella Vail} stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. She gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall; her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing and came away without touching. The jars and the row and the gaps were told what they were and what they wanted, and the basket was told nothing.

Her finger did not go beneath the front edge. The charcoal tally lay under the board where it had lain since the night she had put a finger beneath it and held it one counted breath without looking --- two years gone and still unread --- and a shelf, an afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same thing as that night. The beat stayed where it was left, between knowing the board by heart and being told not to look. The boy did not read the marks, did not count them, did not copy them and did not show them to anybody; he had no hand that could have lifted the least of the marks and no word in which to have shown them if he had. He kept the second state on a place of its own rather than let it stand in for the first, because a holding remembered is not a holding made again, and three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the coals had been banked and the small knife was back on its edge of the sill where a thumb of the house could find it and check it. \person{Tarn} had taken the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, and he stood a moment with the handle in his hand before he began, the way a man stands with a tool he has been told to take up and has not yet made up his mind to own.

“Six counted,” he said to the wood. “The next I don't count.”

Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark of the sill where the house could put a finger and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, held the way a man holds a thing he has not yet decided is his to spend. \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife --- the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford --- and neither the knife nor the sentence had been entered where the house could read them. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest had been tight since the dark.

The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach, with its small interval showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He set the kindling handle-out and edge-in, nothing that could roll, and as he reached he brought the sentence up.

It came the way a man brings a charge he has carried for years to the edge of his teeth: \emph{an ear under this roof is worth more than the debt that holds it}. He had brought it this far on other evenings --- to the word that would call the loan, to the figure that would stand against the debt, to the name of the man who would come up the road and take the hearing away --- and each time he had stopped with the words on the front of his tongue and gone back to the broom. He brought it now all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there. No figure came out of it. No name came out of it. The sentence sat on the front of his teeth with its small shape complete, worth everything and payable by nobody, and he did not say it.

He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it.

The surgeon's register in the basket read the small interval as it would read a clamp left too long on a vessel that would not bear the taking off: the warm held where it should have been moving, the field covered with a cloth before the instrument went down, a hand lifted out of a field before the dressing went on. He knew what a breath held past its time costs a body --- the shoulders that go up and stay up, the tightness under the sternum, the small tremor that starts in the hands and is called cold by everybody who has not held one. A man can hold a breath until the vessel gives and then blame the vessel. He had seen it done in a room with a clean floor and a chart at the foot of the bed. Here the floor was flagstones and there was no chart, and the breath was not his to spend and the debt was not his to call.

“Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom.

Nine stoppings stood on the widened tray where they had stood since Reed 29 --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. A tenth had not stood up. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that could hear was uncalled. The near-saying lay on the tray as an object and not as a payment, and the boy did not let it stand up beside the nine and call itself a tenth, because an account that has found a payer is a rule and no payer stood to this one.

From the shelf end of the room Sella's voice went low and unfinished, and she gave the end of it to the pot.

“That's dry,” she said. “Keep the handle in where the heat is. The house has said it.”

She asked nothing of the man at the kindling and nothing of the basket. Across the yard the wheel gave its small wooden complaint and went on turning, and the wheel-keeper did not come in and did not ask about the ear; the clay-bound man did not go out and ask about the room. The two silences stayed where they were, side by side under one roof and not touching, as they had stayed for three years. No word passed between the men and no word passed over the basket. The household straightened around the holding the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted and set down again in the same place.

\scenebreak

Later, when the light began to go, a neighbour said the thaw would come early and another said it would come late, and Tarn said \emph{Yes, house} to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot. He went on wanting the late one, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the month had turned and the paying stood in the ruts where the water had left it. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it; no collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it. In the clay pot in the loft above the wheel-shed the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay two years gone and cold, not looked at in a year, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. He banked the coals with the back of his hand and set the knife where it could be checked and took himself up the loft ladder without saying what he had held.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-one mornings kept the weight in muscle and gave no account of the cost --- the height of a handle, the give of a shoulder, the turn of a wrist at a step's end, a record kept and not an entry.

At the edge of the yard she went around the place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up to dry through Reed and was standing there no longer, looking for no scrape and finding no word; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, whose water is called weather; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. A look for each and a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The pan's gone place kept its round ring on a tray of its own and the field's empty place stayed where the yard went around it; neither was laid across the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust and its gap, and no third gap came and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

Under the roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and has never told anybody, and would not call kindness now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure.

She told \lore{the carried fire} it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would say \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go, the same three parts she had used at the kiln on the first morning and had not used since --- pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{the widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing; it had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning, and at no shelf and no afternoon and no dusk since, because an afternoon is not an opening. She went to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file again the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not lift the least of them.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the \place{Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \place{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and eighty-one days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a place filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a kiln-master come up on the wrong wind --- a knowing that went to its own tray and never among the hearings, because a pot that sings before its time generally sings itself out early is a saying with no payer in it and a finding is not made into a hearing by a man who wants one. The older intervals lay at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke that went on over the bright patch, and his own held breath from a market morning. Nine stoppings stood as objects and not a rule; the held breath at the top of the reach stood as an interval and not a payment; the near-saying stood on the front of a man's teeth and went no further.

\lore{the damper} lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at the iron in a wall, the beat held beneath \place{the loaf shelf} in a room that did not touch the wall, the half-check on \place{the flagstones} in a yard that touched neither --- not a pair and not a doctrine and not a rule with a payer standing to it, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of the three. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stood side by side with no third and neither had produced a room. The told bell lay beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself at the stopping. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, offstage and unspent; no loaf had passed, no slate had been seen, and no finger of his had drawn a line between the colour and the shape.

The shelf's two gaps lay on their own tray, the fold's crust and its gap on another, the pan's gone ring on a third and the field's empty place on a fourth, and none of the trays touched. No third gap had come; no fourth likeness had been made a cause; three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-one days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and eighty-one days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. \lore{Ash} lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold came cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped as if it had reached the end of its own order; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He wanted the crust two inches from his ear. He wanted the breast. He wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand and a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. The surgeon's register read the whole of it as an inventory that is not a key and a list that is not a hand --- exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces --- and he was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. One hundred and eighty-one stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. The loan stayed uncalled, the ninth stopping stayed nine, the Seed 16 breath stayed held, the three changes stayed on their three places, the mark had been touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that has not come. Nothing was joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended, and the count went forward. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.
