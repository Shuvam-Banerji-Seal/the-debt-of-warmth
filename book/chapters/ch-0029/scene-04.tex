\daymark{127}{Reed 8, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

The men came up \place{the Clay Yard} before first grey with their collars turned high against the river wind and their voices kept low enough that the words went into the wet boards and stayed there, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going wide of the low table where a slate lay flat and wide of a board set out to dry, giving the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it was the river wind, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up. There were three of them or four; from the basket he could not make a number of what was not given to be counted, and he did not try. They went in at \place[the back room]{the room off the wheel-shed} and spoke low, and the door stood a finger's width open and did not shut, and nobody under the roof went in to shut it or to ask what kept it from shutting. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold found the place it had come for and went under the fold at his ear, and the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put. He was one hundred and twenty-seven days old, four months and seven days, and nobody in \place{Low Kiln} said so. \person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for the men and did not say the room was cold.

The boy had learned what a finger's width of open meant in this house. It was not an opening; a slate left lying was an opening and a door standing so was not, and the distinction was kept with the same care the yard gave the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it, both unswept and both called \lore{the thaw}. He set the going-in of the men on a place of his own and laid no likeness across it, because three likenesses in a row will make a cause in a man who wants one, and he had learned in two lives what a cause grows from --- a hand laid on a hand, a clean line drawn at the foot of a page. His own hand could not close two inches on the wicker. The yard took its light slowly, the grey coming up off \place{the Ilm} with mud in it, and the wheel began its rhythm inside \place{the Vail wheel-shed} before anybody had spoken to it, and the boy turned his head toward the sound a half-beat before the first pot went to the bench. Damp clay and tallow came out of the shed in the little wind; the river wind went under the fold and took what the fire had given.

Sella set the basket down at the edge of the shed where no slate lay and went back to the fire, and the instruction went into the fire and the pot and the bucket and the water before it went to anybody: feed it, it's low; the pot wants turning; the bucket's by the door; the water's come round. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and she did not look into the basket, and she kept her half-pace from every open thing, going wide of the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody, and wider still of the place in \place{the worked strips} where a hand had gone in with the seed and had not come out with the rest when the last hands came up on Seed 27. A look and a wide step and no number, as there had been every morning since. She went around both as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a strip and a rack and a shelf and a well and a road and a basket are not openings and no opening had come. The thread at his mouth stayed frozen. Nobody had been given an instruction about the thread.

By the time the light got into the yard the dust was in Jorra Vail's forearms. It was fine grey slate dust ground small as meal and worked into the creases past the bend of the little finger, not the bone-coloured slip that dried on a thrower's arms and came away in pale flakes at the bench; the two states of one morning went on one tray in the boy's file, dusty and clean, and neither was entered anywhere a Court thumb could read. He came through ahead of his hands as he always came through, the bent finger leading and the dust going with it, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack, where the yard went around the space above them and entered by nobody. A slate would be found and a figure made to stand at its foot if a hand came up out of the strip in a month or a year; nothing had come up, and this morning the dust had come instead, and dust was not a figure. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray as two states of one man's mornings, and the boy kept them there beside him and wrote no cause across them.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone, as he had spoken to it every morning of his sixteen years and would speak to it on the morrow: “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” The wheel gave back its low note and took the wet clay under his hands the way a mill takes grain and says nothing about whose field it grew in. He set a pot down on the bench and told it “You'll do”, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and the boy turned toward the set-down ahead of the knuckle and heard the note come whole out of the thing before anybody tapped it. The knowing was in the thrower the way the bent finger was in him, and he kept it where questions could last for years, because a man who could hear a pot's full note before it left the wheel and said so where it could be entered would be a man with a figure at his name by noon, and Jorra had never been that man.

A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} went along the row with his hands behind his back, \lore{Ash} on his cuffs from the terrace and the sweetish edge of lead glaze coming off him in the little wind. He stopped at the pot Jorra had set down and said there was a note in that one before its time, and that a man who knew a thing like that ought to say it where it could be entered. He waited with his hands behind his back the way another man waits at a table for a figure to be handed across it, and the yard went on with its work around the two of them as a yard goes around a place it has no use for entering. Jorra did not say it. He said nothing a slate could carry. He told the wheel again that it did not care whose hands and went on to the next pot, and the knowing stayed where it was, worth nothing anyone could take and therefore kept. The boy set the knowing on a tray of its own beside the dusty forearms and joined the two with nothing, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and his hand could not close two inches on the wicker or reach the bench where the pots stood.

\scenebreak

The instruction to the fire did not get finished. “The fire's low, Jorra, it wants---” and his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the word, ahead of her even getting the instruction as far as the pot, and moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The cold took the heat out of his fingertip as it took it out every morning, giving a little back in reverse of the core, and the house straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted: Sella shut her mouth on the sentence and gave the end of it to the pot, the wheel kept its rhythm, the kiln-master went quiet with his hands behind his back, and nobody asked what the finger had known before the instruction arrived. It was an obedience that was also a correction, and neither of them would have put a word to either. The boy had seen the same shape in a midwife's hand, at a bucket, at a Court table, and under a roof where a woman gave the instruction to the fire and never to him, and he kept all of them on separate places and did not make one hand of four.

He read the iron as he had read it on other mornings and would read it on more. It was the bite that held a vessel whole and the bite that could take the hand off a man; it kept the warmth in and let it out by degrees, and the same piece of iron did both with no word in \lore{Grathic} that held the two mercies in one. The surgeon's register in him knew a clamp when it saw one and knew what a clamp leaves behind when it has held too long, and here the holding and the taking were one object with no name and no figure and no slate, and there was nobody under this roof to whom he could have said it even if his mouth had been his own. He filed it as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln, and set it beside the two tellings of the shut counting room that had come over the yard well and down the river road, each with its date in it, one with breath through the nose and one with breath made in the throat, both saying it was not the war's hand. No third telling came this morning. The men went in at the finger's-width door and came out as dust; the dust went on the tray; the tellings stayed where they were. He laid no finger across any of them.

His file was exact and worthless, and he was the only adult in this yard and him with the hands of a child. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with no seventh; the near-saying that had come up to the threshold of the word last night lay on the interval tray as an object and not a rule; the word forty stayed unspoken with the shape of its double turn kept legible and without a name; the told bell sat in its own place with the heard bell in another and the two places did not touch. He set the damper's double bite among these as one more thing that could not be entered, and went on holding them apart the only way he had ever held anything --- by knowing what each one was. A house straightens when a weight is lifted and settles when it is put back, and he had watched this one straighten twice in two mornings without anybody setting a word to either silence. The men in the room off the shed were not named. The broken birth-slates were not entered. The knowing in the thrower's hands was kept and worth nothing. Something must give at the end of the seventh year and had not given yet, and the morning went on over the flagstones with a bent finger standing in cold iron and nobody saying why.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took up the broom only after Sella had given the broom its instruction, which went to the broom and not to him, and he said \emph{Yes, house} to the neighbour who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and to the one who wanted it late and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His seventh year stood pressed past the third tend of the planting and still held, the small knife at the very edge of its sill with six years counted where the house could check and the seventh where it could not, and the breath from Seed 16 sat unspent in his chest with the small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in. The unforged collector sentence lay beside it, and neither was entered. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it; no collector came, and he did not say that the thing which had not happened had a name. His chest was tight and he agreed with both sides of the weather so entirely that an argument had nowhere to put its foot, and his stroke went where the house had told the broom to go.

His stroke went over the flagstones without the half-check it had made on Seed 22, and the stones kept the shallow bright patch where it had stopped, as they had kept it every morning since. He did not turn toward the shed and Jorra did not turn toward him, and neither man named the other's silence. The equality under the roof held at the place it had moved to: two ears, two silences, a loan that could hear and had never been called, an ear worth more than the debt that held it and therefore unsafe. Sella, within hearing of all of it, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing, and she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a flagstone and a broom and a shaft of morning light are not openings and no opening had come; a strip, a rack, a shelf, a well, a market, a road and a bed are not openings either, and she had gone around them all for a hundred and twenty-seven days without once making one of them one. Her hand, when it had nothing to hold, counted; when it had the basket to carry it counted the ground instead, and gave the count to the wool and the river wind and never to a slate.

The bent finger stood in the cold iron while the stroke went on over the flags without checking, and the shallow bright patch kept its place on the stone. The boy filed the two silences on one tray and laid no cause across them, because a cause grows from a hand laid on a hand and his hand could not close two inches on the wicker, could not reach the damper, could not lift the crust from the fold at his ear or set a finger on the slate lying flat on the low table. It was the same piece of iron, the holding and the taking, and no word in Grathic held both mercies; the two tellings of the shut room stayed beside it with no third, and the knowing in the thrower's hands stayed worth nothing and unentered, and the dust stayed ground small as meal on one forearm and not on the other. One hundred and twenty-seven stood where one hundred and twenty-six had stood the night before, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water beyond the rack and came down, and the yard went on over the flagstones with the count going into the day with him.
