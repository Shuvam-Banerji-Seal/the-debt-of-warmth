The market closed over the man with the slate and the light went off the last of the pots, and \person{Sella Vail} came back to \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade for the basket as a woman comes back for a pot she has set to cool. She put her burn-shut hand to the wicker and lifted it level, the instruction going to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind, and the river wind had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up. She went at the half-pace from every open slate, around the low table where a slate lay flat, around a board set out to dry, going wide as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The basket kept what it held. She did not look into it, and because she did not look into it \person[Corin]{the boy} had the going-home to look out.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold found the place it had come for and went under the fold at his ear, and the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and the road went on under the sound the way a mill goes on over a stone turning. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the noise behind him and came down. Nothing within two paces could be reached.

He was one hundred and twenty-six days old, four months and six days, and nobody on the road said so. The armfuls went past in the going-down and had gone past in the coming-up, judged by the arms that carried them, a record kept in muscle that told what had been carried and not what it had cost. He laid the going-home on a place of his own beside the going-down and laid no likeness across the shelf's two gaps in the room he could not see from here, or across the one stiff crust in the fold at his ear, or across the empty place beside it. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and his hand could not close two inches on the wicker, so he held them apart by knowing what each one was, which was the only instrument he had ever had and the only one he had never been able to lay down.

The yard received them as a yard receives a basket, with the going-around. She went around the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it that had been named \lore{the thaw} after the fact, both unswept since the water went, and she went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, the broken birth-slates lying where they lay, entered by nobody. Beyond the racks \place{the worked strips} held their own empty place, the place where a hand had gone in with the seed and had not come out with the rest when the last hands came up on Seed 27; the yard went around it with a look and a wide step, and there was no number anywhere in the line. It had gone around the place every day since and put no figure to the going-around, and it would go around it on the morrow and the day after and call the going care. The boy set the going-around on a place of his own. He had read it for a month as a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody had opened: exact, complete, worth nothing. In \place{Low Kiln} that evening nobody put a number to any of it.

Back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, the way a woman sets a pot down where no passing hip will find it. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left and nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the way a hand rests on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot bring itself to let go of, and then it was gone and the burn on the back of it was shut on nothing. She did not look toward the bed. She had not said whose child he was in all his days and would not have known how to say it if she had wanted to.

In the next room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its days the way this house kept most of its days, badly, and in materials no Court thumb would read. Loaves pushed along the board, a glance at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze on the wall, and spaces left where no figure stood. She moved her lips along the row without a figure, her hand going past the old empty place at the row's end and past the newer palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, and closing on a loaf beyond both. Two gaps; no third. The fold at his ear kept one stiff brown-edged crust and the empty place beside it, and that place was not the old gap and not the new one and was not laid across either by any finger of his. Two rooms that did not touch. When she had finished with the shelf she did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a shelf and a loaf and a bed and a dusk are not openings, and the mark was touched before an opening and at no other hour.

At the fire she told it what it was. “It was the thaw.” The words went down into the coals as instructions went into the bucket and the broom and the pot, and the coals answered nothing. She turned them with the back of a broken pot, the broken edge lifting and settling, and fed them, and the house kept its warmth the way a cup keeps water with a crack in it nobody mentions.

“The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}.

Then she stood with the back of the broken pot in her hand and counted something under her breath toward no opening. The lips moved; the count arrived nowhere; there was no slate and no threshold for it to be entered at. In the yard beyond the wall \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} had taken the broom only after she had given it its instruction, and had said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late and kept wanting the late because it put off the paying, and the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh where the house could not check it, and the breath from Seed 16 sat unspent in a man's chest. None of it came into the room and none of it was asked about. A house straightens around a silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and this house had straightened twice in two days without anybody putting a word to either.

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, the skin inside the door puckering as the wrist turned, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals. She had said once that it came in the winter \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} died and had not said it again. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, the way a woman sets a cup for a man who will come in late and not look where he puts his fingers. She did not look toward the bed, and went to her own bed, leaving the fire to keep what it kept and the door standing as it stood, a finger's width open.

Through the finger's-width gap came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir} and the long grey rows of the cutting, a fifth low thing kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing and never among the six. It came nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, as it had come nearer every night since the flood went out of the yard, and he had never once mistaken it for a vessel's note or given it a source it did not have. Through the floor under blind ash \lore{the carried fire} took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-six days. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one.

He turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, in the dark, with the count standing at one hundred and twenty-six where one hundred and twenty-five had stood the night before. Every object was where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot through a wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, which came from a person and was filed beside the five and never counted with them. No seventh had come. He had not made one to fill the empty place. \lore[the bell at the Cross]{The bell} that had gone over the square in the morning was a told bell, and it sat in its own place with the heard bell in another, and the two places did not touch.

Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under a wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. On another tray lay the intervals as objects and not a rule: the half-beat of a check that came behind a standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from the market, and the near-saying stopped at the word that would call the loan. The word had come up through the counting as water comes up through a crack and stood on the threshold of the teeth with no figure under it and no name on it, and had gone back where it came from; here it lay among objects, the same iron as the standing tongue of \lore{the damper}, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. It was an interval and not a payment, a near-saying and not a hearing, and not a seventh. The word forty stayed unspoken with the shape of the double turn kept legible and without a name, a shape on a place of its own in a night that had a great many figures in it and not one he could enter.

The cold foot below the sill stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed, and he did not lay it across the other absences, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause and there was no hand in this house to hold them apart but his. The shelf's two gaps kept their room and the fold's crust kept its room and no finger of his drew the line between them; the hand that had gone in with the seed held its place with no name, no cause, no body and no figure, and a slate would be found and a figure made if a hand came up in a month or a year, and nothing had come up. The reed-cutter's cord stayed inside a man's coat with its knots unread, a tally no Court thumb would spell; it lay on a place of its own beside the grey cord at a woman's waist and the second twist twisted twice beneath it, her board beside another woman's knot-string and not touching it, six pages turned writing-down toward a floorboard, a round pewter tin above a bed nobody used with its thumb-bright lid shut. No tin was opened. No box was opened. No third gap came and no fourth likeness made a cause.

Nobody under this roof said he was a hundred and twenty-six days old, four months and six days. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. The file came back exact, complete and worth nothing, and he was the only adult in the room and him with the hands of a child. The near-saying stayed where it had stopped, the seventh year stayed held, and something must give at its end and had not given yet; the loan that could hear was uncalled. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. In the other life it had closed on clamps and on a phone with a name he would not look at; here it could close on nothing at two inches, not on the crust, not on the pot set rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, not on the fold, not on a slate, not on a hand. The water went over water beyond the gap, the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor, the boards kept their knot where a fifth nail should have stood, and one hundred and twenty-six stood where one hundred and twenty-five had stood the night before, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. The dark went on keeping what it kept, and the count went on into \place{Sill}'s next morning with him.
