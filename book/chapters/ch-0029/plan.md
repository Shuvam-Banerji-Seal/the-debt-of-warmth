# Chapter 29 plan — The Word Stopped Twice

**Days 126–128 · Reed 7–9, Y.H. 813, Seventhday to Ninthday.** A normal chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived for at least eight hundred words. Day 125 is done and is not revisited; Day 129 belongs to Chapter 30. Arc 1, *The Cold Stone — a warm stone, already going cold*. Close third-person past inside Corin, one hundred and twenty-six to one hundred and twenty-eight days old, four months and six days to four months and eight days, carried everywhere. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from his eyes. No Court, Waiting Room, collector, foundling or back-room interior. The surgeon's register carries the old life.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** The near-saying under the Vail roof comes a **second** time to the threshold of the word that would call the loan and stops again (T036/T076). It is filed on the interval tray as a second interval and not a second payment. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year has still not given. The third green is glimpsed at the market's edge (T050/T051), held on a place of its own and not joined to Berr's glaze or the earlier greens.

**Held throughout, not paid, opened, joined, named or ended.** T097 (advanced and unpaid; no third gap, no fourth likeness), T098 (no name, cause, body, figure or payer for the hand that did not come back), T099 (reed-cutter's cord, no knot counted), T023/T089 (both tins shut, six pages unread, T023 not paid again), T024/T059/T025 (cords unread, word forty unspoken), T050/T051 (green not named as Fenna), T060/T086 (two shelf gaps in one room, one crust and its gap in another, no joining), T061/T082 (six hearings beside and never among, no seventh, three near-full answers advanced), T065/T095 (two shut-room tellings, no third), T090/T080 (clerk still set for an uncalled measure), T093/T094/T096 (cold foot uncured and unnamed), T088 (carried fire's fifth breath, payer unnamed for 126, 127 and 128 days). No sale, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no knitting of any two likenesses into a cause.

## Scene 1 — The First Bundles

- **Day and time.** Day 126, Reed 7, Seventhday, morning through midday. First `\daymark` of the chapter.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard at Low Kiln and the edge of the worked strips east of it; the yard well.
- **Present.** Sella Vail (carrying the boy), Tarn, the boy, unnamed cutter, sluice man, yard women. Jorra offstage until Scene 4.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Reed has a row at last: bundled stalks stand grey in the ruts where the barley stood a month ago, green at the broken ends and pale at the frost-bitten tips. Bundles are judged by the arm that carries them and never by a figure; a day's cutting is still a going-downriver and a coming-back at dusk (T071/T072, T077).
  2. A woman come up from the strips with mud worked into her wrists asks what the reeds will cost. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf at the house and sends her to the pot: *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it* (T058/T079). The woman carries the unanswered thing away held level.
  3. A man come up from the sluice with wet sleeves asks what the beds will want before the week is out; Sella gives him the weather and the river in place of a number. The boy reads a cost asked in the form of a direction and answered with a direction as an account kept in materials no Court thumb will read.
  4. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack with the broken birth-slates under it, and the place in the strip where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out with the rest on Seed 27. A look, a wide step, no number, no name, no body (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried).
  5. Sella carries the boy at the half-pace from every open slate, gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and does not touch the widow's mark at a rack, a strip or a well, because none is an opening.
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction, says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went. His seventh year is pressed past the third tend and still held: knife at the very edge of its sill, six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, chest tight, the breath from Seed 16 still unspent, the unforged collector sentence beside it (T036/T076).
  7. The boy is one hundred and twenty-six days old, four months and six days, and nobody says so. He reads the arm that judges a bundle as a record kept in muscle which tells what was carried and not what it cost; he lays the going-around on a place of its own and lays no likeness across the shelf's two gaps, the fold's stiff crust or the empty place beside it (T060/T086).
- **Emotional turn.** From the satisfaction of work that has a visible shape to the cold knowledge that the shape carries no cost anyone will enter; the district's care and its withholding are the same motion of the hand around a place it will not name.
- **Sensory anchors.** Green-cut reed ends and frost-pale tips; river silt in Boot prints; wet wool riding back from the small bare joint; clay dust on the well lip; the clay jar cold under its glaze; Tarn's Ash-coloured knuckles and the pale clip-ring catching what light there is; the drool thread frozen at the mouth and nobody instructed about it.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (126 kept exact), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T060/T086, T036/T076, T088.
- **Foreshadowing planted / advanced.** T098 gathers interest with every going-around; the seventh year is two days nearer its end and has not given; T088's payer stays unnamed for a hundred and twenty-six days. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The boy's small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water beyond the yard and comes down, with the armfuls going past him judged by the arms that carry them and one hundred and twenty-six standing where nobody has said it.

## Scene 2 — A Green at the Mouth

- **Day and time.** Day 126, midday into the going of the light. Continuation of Day 126, no second `\daymark`.
- **Place.** The stalls beyond the Cross; the moot-post's shade; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella (carrying the boy), the green-mouthed woman (unnamed, silent, no dialogue), the soft-cuffed clerk, a buyer, a girl with a creel, market voices. Berr not yet on stage this day.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket in the moot-post's shade where no slate lies, keeps a half-pace from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind, never to the basket. She does not look into it.
  2. Across the square a woman stands looking at pots with the sweetish green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth. She says nothing, turns a cup a quarter and sets it back where it stood, and goes off with the light. The boy calls the colour green and nothing else. This is the **third** glimpse of that colour, filed on a place of its own beside Berr's tasted glaze at the Cross and the greens at Silt 30 and Seed 28, with no cause written across any of them (T050/T051, advanced, not paid). She is not Fenna in the narration and gets no dialogue.
  3. At the landing the soft-cuffed clerk is still set for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing, slate to his forearm, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. The market sells its reeds and eels over the top of him; nobody steps around him and nobody asks what he is set there for (T090/T080/T083).
  4. A buyer names a figure for eels and passes on with it; a girl with a creel takes the out-of-line landing sideways, catches the rail and goes on selling. Nobody makes a fall of it (T083).
  5. The boy holds the third green where it belongs. Having learned in two lives what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, he refuses to lay a cause across the greens or across the clerk and the colour. His one hand cannot close two inches on the basket's rim; the other does not reach a slate.
- **Emotional turn.** A likeness has appeared a third time and wants to become a cause; the keeping of it apart is the whole labour, and the labour is invisible and useless to everyone but him.
- **Sensory anchors.** Sweetish lead-glaze green at a mouth corner; a cup turned a quarter on a pot board; grey-washed cuff and a clean held slate; eel slime and salt at the stalls; cold iron of the basket rim under his cheek; the bell's told strokes going four, four and a gap and kept apart from any heard bell.
- **Threads advanced.** T050/T051 (third glimpse), T090/T080/T083, T058/T079, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / advanced.** T050/T051 advances years before the ear that will one day be spoken with the green; no name, no dialogue, no trade, no box, no joining. T090/T080 carries the Court presence that arrived ahead of a death. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no sale.
- **Exact last image.** The woman goes off with the light with the green still at the corner of her mouth, and the boy holds the colour on its own place while the market closes over the man with the slate as though a man with a slate were a thing every landing had.

## Scene 4 — The Dust Ahead of the Sentence

- **Day and time.** Day 127, Reed 8, Eighthday, before first grey through midday. First `\daymark` of the day.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard.
- **Present.** Jorra Vail, Sella, Tarn, the boy, unnamed men (not followed), a kiln-master from High Kiln.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and speak low in the room off the wheel-shed. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031). The door stands a finger's width open and is not entered.
  2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of the little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel, and keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray. He asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the rack, entered by nobody.
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady*. He tells a pot *You'll do* and knows its full note before it leaves the wheel; when a kiln-master come up from High Kiln would call it early and make something of it, Jorra withholds the knowing and says nothing a slate could carry (T035/T031/T062).
  4. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing. The household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.
  5. The boy reads the iron once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object worth nothing joined to no kiln (T062). The two tellings of the shut counting room stay beside it with no third telling (T065/T095).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed and goes on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch where it stopped. Neither man names the other's silence; the equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to (T036/T076). Sella asks nothing within hearing of any of it.
- **Emotional turn.** The household reorganises itself around an unspoken instruction and an unnameable piece of iron; the boy sees that the same clamp is mercy and mutilation and that nobody in the district has a word for the double bite.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger; bone-coloured slip dried on the other forearm; damp clay and tallow; the cold of the damper taking the heat out of a fingertip; the wheel's rhythm; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031/T062, T065/T095, T036/T076, T058/T079, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / advanced.** T035/T031 gathers toward the Broken Slates and the promise of the foundling's name, ungiven and not withdrawn; T062's double bite stays joined to no kiln; T036's silence waits for the second stopping that evening. No name for the men, no third telling, no room entered.
- **Exact last image.** The bent finger stands in the cold iron while Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow bright patch, and the boy files the two silences on one tray with no cause laid across them.

## Scene 5 — The Second Stopping

- **Day and time.** Day 127, late afternoon into the going of the light and the small hours. Continuation of Day 127, closes the day.
- **Place.** The clay road and the market edge; the hearth and kindling under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Present.** Berr Clay, Sella, Tarn, the boy; Jorra gone from the shed by nightfall.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Berr comes up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands are large and clean; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm and passes a finger over a figure (T015/T041/T047/T077).
  2. He says a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says*, calls the levy and says *It is not called by me*. To a man who brings a number up out of a fist he says there will be a slate at the watch --- *your house*, never *I* --- taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. The figure arrives nowhere, the true subtraction stays in the locked box up the clay road, and the figure read does not know the hand that did not come back (T098 carried).
  3. The boy files Berr's twice-wiped thumb and finger over a figure on a tray of their own, and files Berr's green apart from the green at the market's edge on another place; no joining, no cause (T050/T051, T080).
  4. At the hearth, with the coals banked and the small knife set back where it can be checked, Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*. His chest is tight and the breath from Seed 16 is still unspent, the small interval at the top of each reach showing where it should have gone in.
  5. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes a **second** time all the way to the threshold of the word that would call the loan --- and what would follow if a man said whose ear and set a figure to it --- and stops there with no figure and no name (T036/T076). He draws the breath that should go in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom.
  6. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing. Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room; the two silences stay unjoined.
  7. The boy files the second stopping on the interval tray beside the first as a **second interval, not a second payment**, not a hearing and not a seventh. What must give at the end of the seventh year still has not given; the loan that can hear is uncalled (T061/T082).
- **Emotional turn.** The sentence reaches the word twice and is kept twice; the near-saying has become a habit of holding, and a kept thing is owed. The boy is the only person under the roof who has counted the stoppings, and he cannot spend what he has counted.
- **Sensory anchors.** Red wool cord folded under a coat; green of glaze at a mouth corner; a thumb wiped twice on an apron; a narrow slate held unopened to a forearm; banked coals and the small knife on its sill; the small interval at the top of a reach where a breath should have gone in; the kindling's four, four and an empty fifth.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (near-saying brought to the word a second time and withheld), T015/T041/T047/T077, T098 (carried), T050/T051, T080, T061/T082, T058/T079, T088.
- **Foreshadowing planted / advanced.** T036's loan gathers another interval toward its far calling; the seventh year is one day nearer its end and has not given; T098's figure still does not know the missing hand. Nothing called, named, figured or spent.
- **Exact last image.** The near-saying goes back where it came from and lies on the tray as an object among objects, the same iron as the standing tongue of the damper, with no figure under it and no name on it, while the loan that can hear stays uncalled and the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the river beyond the finger's-width gap and comes down.

## Scene 6 — The Cord Inside the Coat

- **Day and time.** Day 128, Reed 9, Ninthday, morning downriver and through midday. First `\daymark` of the day.
- **Place.** The Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards; then the fish-trade shade and the out-of-line landing at the Fishstairs below the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella (carrying the boy), the reed-cutter, Orra Weir, Mira Silt, a weir woman, a buyer, the soft-cuffed clerk, market voices.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries the boy downriver and sets him at the edge of the work where no slate lies, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  2. The reed-cutter comes along the planks with a bundle on his shoulder and the short length of wool or reed-fibre cord inside his coat gone grey with handling. He sets the bundle on the flat blessing stone, straightens the cord on his thumb, turns it once so the knots stand in the small light, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb and puts it back inside his coat. He does not show it to anyone and does not count it where anyone can hear him count (T099 carried, not paid).
  3. To a weir woman who asks how many went up yesterday he says *You can ask the cord*; to *I can't read a cord* he says *No more can the Court*, and goes to lift a trap, the question going down into the water. No figure, no knot counted, no name, no joining to the missing hand or to the word forty (T098, T099).
  4. Orra is at the lower boards with her board on its side and a cord at her waist, pulls one knot down with her thumb and gives a date where a levy question was asked: *The ninth of Ilmen. The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them.* When told it is not the levy she says it is a date and that the river takes what the Court does not; she goes back to the knots and gets no answer from the basket (T024).
  5. Orra's knot-string lies beside Mira Silt's board and does not touch it; the word forty stays unspoken with the shape of the forty-first knot legible and unnamed (T024/T059/T025).
  6. Mira works the eels with slime dried in grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been and her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away.
  7. When a buyer in a coat with the river in its hem names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey wool cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted, the thin child's knot untouched. She does not look into the basket (T059/T025/T024).
  8. At the landing the soft-cuffed clerk is still set with his cuff grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls, and nobody has taken him off (T090/T080/T083).
- **Emotional turn.** The district keeps three or four accounts in materials no Court thumb will read, and the boy can see every one of them and enter none; the keeping is care and refusal in the same gesture, like the damper's double bite.
- **Sensory anchors.** Salt dried white where floodwater touched the posts; eel slime grey on sleeves; silt pale in the hollow of a neck; a cord turned so knots stand in small light; thumb and teeth on a knot tied low; grey wool twisted twice beneath grey wool; the river's note of water over water at the edge of the shade.
- **Threads advanced.** T099, T024/T059/T025, T090/T080/T083, T058/T079, T098 (carried), T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / advanced.** T099 and T024/T059/T025 gather toward the far day the district's private records are laid together and a figure is at last given; both stay uncounted and unjoined. T098 gathers interest with no name and no body. No word forty spoken, no tin or box opened, no sixth sighting, no sale.
- **Exact last image.** The cord goes back inside the reed-cutter's coat with its knots unread, Orra's knot-string lies beside Mira's board and does not touch it, and the boy holds three unread records and one hand that cannot close two inches on the basket rim while the market sells over the man with the slate.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 30

The chapter closes in the small hours of Day 128 mid-reach: the hand goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down, and one hundred and twenty-eight stands unspent with the near-saying stopped twice and the loan uncalled. Day 129, Reed 10, Tenthday, opens Chapter 30 as the **first Account-day of Reed**: Berr Clay comes to the block with the red cord folded under his coat and the green at one mouth-corner, wipes his thumb twice, reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* over the new reed bundles, calls the levy and denies it, and takes himself out of his own sentence with *your house*, never *I*. The bell goes four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the heard bell stays beside the told bell, and the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the Fishstairs for a measure nobody called. It is on that Account-day, from the basket's low angle, that the boy first glimpses the charcoal tally on the **underside** of the Vail loaf shelf --- marks the house keeps exactly where nobody thinks to look, above a row kept badly on top --- unread, uncounted and joined to nothing (T100 planted in Chapter 30, not here). The second stopping on the tray, the third green on its own place, the reed-cutter's unread cord and the two shelf gaps all go into Account-day unchanged, still carried, still unpaid. Forward, not closed.
