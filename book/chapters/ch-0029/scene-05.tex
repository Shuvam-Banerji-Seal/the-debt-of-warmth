The afternoon went down the road ahead of them, the light thinning off \place{the Ilm} until the wet boards held it in shallow bright patches like the ones the flagstones had kept where a stroke had stopped, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up, and the basket was a pot set down and left. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold found the place it had come for and went under the fold at his ear, and the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put. He was one hundred and twenty-seven days old, four months and seven days, and nobody in \place{Low Kiln} had said so. The yard thinned behind them as the men went off toward their houses with the dust of the day on them, and the wheel in the shed lost its rhythm and was quiet, and the two lines by the rack and the space beneath it went on being gone around with a look and a wide step and no number.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} as the light got low, with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He smelled of hot clay and, under it, of nothing at all, which was the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean, the hands of a man who had once carried clay and had then stopped carrying it and kept the carrying in the way he held things. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm while he passed a finger over what was written and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. It was the check of a man setting down a dish already sure it would not slop, a small careful passing of the thumb that cost nothing and would be entered nowhere. The boy set the twice-wiped thumb on a place of its own and did not join it to the midwife reaching for a slate before a child or the neighbour reaching for a bare board before she looked at him or the clerk behind a Court table putting hand to paper before eye to face, because three likenesses in a row will make a cause in a man who wants one, and he had learned in two lives what a cause grows from: a hand laid on a hand, a clean line drawn at the foot of a page.

“A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle,” Berr said, low, into the top of the goers-by at the market's edge. He did not raise his voice, and the people nearest had to bend their backs toward him, the way people had bent at \place{the Cross} for years and called it hearing. “One in twelve, as the notice says.” He passed the finger over the figure again. “The levy is called. It is not called by me.”

A man come up from \place{the worked strips} with dry mud worked into his wrists brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the levy would take now that the hands had been out of the reeds all day. He looked at the number in his fist as though it might have changed while he carried it, thought better of giving it, and put the fist back into his sleeve. Berr watched the fist go and said there would be a slate at the watch. “If your house wants it again,” he said. Your house, never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the clean edge where the hand had been and nothing else for anybody to see. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere. The true subtraction sat in the locked box in his house up the road, and the figure that went into the slate did not know the hand that had not come back out of the strip on Seed 27, had no name for it, no cause, no body, no figure, and went on not knowing while the yard went around the place in the dark.

Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. She stood a half-pace from every open slate, and when a slate lay flat on a stall board she went around the board as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at a stall, a rack, a strip, a shelf, a well, a market, a road, a basket or a dusk, because none of them was an opening and no opening had come. She did not look into the basket. The boy had his cheek against the cold iron of the rim and the green at the man's mouth went on being green and nothing more. It stood on its own place, apart from the green glimpsed at the market's edge the day before and apart from the glaze at the Cross and the old greens at Silt 30 and Seed 28, with no cause written across any of them and no ear years away yet to speak it with. He had declined to make a hunger or a sale of a colour at every sighting, and declining cost nothing anybody could tax, which was the whole labour of it.

Berr went off up the clay road with the narrow slate under his coat and the box in his house still locked, believing he had done the district a kindness. The market went on selling its reeds and its eels over the top of the figure as though a figure read aloud were a thing every evening had, and the man with the mud in his wrists went back to the strips with his fist in his sleeve. The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb and the finger passed over a figure on a tray of their own, and the green on a place of its own, and laid no finger across either. The count went on where it had stood all day and nobody set a figure to it.

The going of the light came up the clay road behind her, and Sella carried him home at the half-pace, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, going around the place in the strip where a hand had gone in with the seed and had not come out with the rest, going around every open slate, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and to the fire before she gave it to any person. By the time they got under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra} was gone from the shed and had been gone since the men went, and nobody said where he had gone and nobody asked what had become of the dust on his forearms or of the tongue of iron he had left standing in \lore{the damper}. The room off the shed stood a finger's width open, and the dark came into it and did not shut it, and the household went on with its work around the open space the way cloth goes on holding together around a stitch nobody will look at. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed where they had been set, each with its date in it and neither with a name, and no third telling came at the going of the light.

The fire had been banked with the back of a broken pot so that one small place left where the heat came through, and the pot had been turned and set where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} had set the small knife back on the very edge of its sill where it could be checked, six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He stood at the kindling with \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The stack answered as it answered every evening: four, four, and a space where the fifth should have been. He did not fill the space and did not count it.

“Six counted,” he told the kindling. “The next I don't count.”

It was the shape he had said it in for years, the statement of fact made in the tone that invited somebody else to laugh so that he would not have to, and nobody laughed and nobody was meant to. His chest was tight. The breath from Seed 16 sat unspent in it still, and the small interval at the top of each reach showed where it should have gone in, an interval on a tray and not a payment, as the boy had learned to read such things. He put one stick on the stack and took up another, and the sentence came again as a man comes again to a door he has already shut: an ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held it, and worth more than a bed lent to a foundling and a loft lent to a debt, and if a man said whose ear and set a figure to it there would be a slate at the watch with a name on it and a number under the name, and a collector would come down the road at last and take the measure of the house by what it had kept. It came all the way to the threshold of the word that would call the loan. It stopped there. No figure came with it and no name stood under it. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled.

Tarn drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. He had agreed with the ones who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and the ones who wanted it late so entirely that an argument had nowhere to put its foot, and he went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. He said “Yes, house” to the fire and went on with the broom, which was the shape his help took when he was frightened, doing the next thing and the next and quietly putting the fourth right. The small knife lay where it could be checked. The unforged collector sentence lay where he kept the seventh year, and neither was entered, and the near-saying went back where it had come from.

Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. Jorra did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences kept their equality under one roof with nobody setting a word between them. She did not touch the widow's mark, because a kindling stack and a hearth and a shaft of evening light are not openings; a shelf, a rack, a strip, a well, a market, a road, a basket and a bed are not openings either, and she had gone around them all for a hundred and twenty-seven days without once making one of them one. When her hands had nothing to hold they counted: spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child. When she had the basket to carry they counted the ground instead, and gave the count to the wool and the river wind and never to a slate.

The boy set the second stopping on the interval tray beside the first. It was a second interval and not a second payment. It was not a hearing and not a seventh. The first had come to the threshold three nights back, on Reed 5, and had stood there with no figure and no name, and this one had come to the same threshold in the going of the light and had stood there with no figure and no name, and the two of them lay on the tray as objects among objects, the same iron as the standing tongue of the damper, with nothing under either that a Court thumb could spell. A kept thing is owed; a kept word is a debt; and a debt that can hear is a debt that can be called. He knew all three and could enter none of them. What must give at the end of the seventh year had not given, and the year stood pressed past its third tend and still held, and the hand that could have set a figure to it belonged to a man who could not close two inches on the wicker.

Sella carried him up to the bed under the roof and set him on it and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of the broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said I, stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale off the dying light, and went to her own bed without looking toward the bed where he lay. \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room kept its two gaps, the old space at the row's end and the newer palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, while the fold at his ear kept one stiff brown-edged crust and its unnamed empty place. Two rooms that did not touch and no third. She moved her lips along no row tonight and gave nobody a figure.

Through the finger's-width gap came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing and never among the six. Through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-seven days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid; the told bell sat in its place with the heard bell in another and the two places did not touch; the word forty stayed unspoken with the shape of its double turn legible and without a name; the cold foot below the sill was the cold to one woman and weather to the yard; the intervals and the two stoppings of the near-saying lay on their tray as objects and not a rule. Nothing had been paid, named, opened, called or spent. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water beyond the gap and came down. One hundred and twenty-seven stood where one hundred and twenty-six had stood the night before, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody, and the dark went on keeping what it kept while the count went into the morrow with him.
