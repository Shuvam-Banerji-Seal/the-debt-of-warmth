## Chronicle

Reeds stood in the ruts at Reed's opening, green at the broken ends and pale where frost had bitten the tips; the yard judged them by the carrying arm, never by a figure. Sella Vail carried the boy at the half-pace and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind, never to the basket. A woman who asked what the reeds cost was sent to the pot with *the house has said it*; the yard went around the clean dark line, the thaw, the space beneath the rack with its broken birth-slates, and the strip where a hand went in on Seed 15 and never came out: a look, a wide step, no number. One hundred and twenty-six days old, he read the arm as a record in muscle --- exact, worth nothing.

At the Cross a woman turned a cup a quarter on the pot board, set it back and went off with lead-glaze green at one corner of her mouth --- the third green at a mouth, no cause. Below the bridge the soft-cuffed clerk held his slate and the market sold over him; a girl with a creel took the out-of-line landing sideways.

Before first grey men came up the Clay Yard and spoke low in the room off the wheel-shed; Jorra Vail gave them no name, came through with slate dust ground into both forearms, told a pot *You'll do* and withheld its note from a High Kiln kiln-master. His bent finger went into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence. Berr Clay came up with his office cord folded, wiped his thumb twice before touching nothing, read *one in twelve*, called the levy and denied it. That evening Tarn brought the ear-and-debt sentence to the word that would call the loan a second time and stopped there, no figure and no name. The boy filed it as a second interval, not a second payment.

At the Weir a reed-cutter turned his grey cord once on his thumb, tied one low with his teeth and put it back inside his coat: *You can ask the cord* --- *No more can the Court*. Orra Weir gave a date where a figure was asked; Mira Silt answered a thin run with *Ask the river* and kept her thumb on the grey cord. Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates before the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower, covered the sign with a dead woman's date, said *Warm and breathing*, and left six pages unread.

Sella set the boy on his bed, put the fold right, left the thread frozen, told the fire it was the thaw and said *The house will keep it*. The river's note came through the gap, the fire missed its fifth breath, the boards kept a knot: three counts, none become one. One hundred and twenty-eight days old, he was told by nobody; his hand went up an inch toward the water and came down, the count unspent.
