\daymark{150}{Tallow 1, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

\dropcap{T}{he month} turned over in the dark the way a pot turns on a wheel, a quarter at a time and with no hand calling the moment of the turning; and the cold came round with it under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} and took its old order of reaching, cheek first, then temple, then the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back in the night. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, as he had lain every night since the night he was carried in, and he was one hundred and fifty days old and five months exactly, and nobody under that roof had said either number, and nobody under that roof would have known what a number of days counted if somebody had said it. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board, and in glances at a clay jar, and in a space at the row's end that no woman called a number because a woman who called it would then have to answer for it. The count he carried had no rim and no handle and no woman passing with a pot would have set it down. It stood where it stood. It went on.

The first sound of the new month was not a bell from \place{the Cross}. It was the kiln's breath coming through the wall from the yard, four low draws and a gap where the fifth should have been, regular as a sleeper's breathing through a partition and no more addressed to him than that. Under the floor the carried fire answered in the same figure, four low pulls and a silence, and he kept the two sounds on two places and did not lay the one across the other. Two likenesses in a row are the beginning of a rule, and a rule wants a foot at the bottom of it with a name under the foot; he had learned in a better-lit room how quickly a row of likenesses will stand up and acquire a payer when a man with a clean slate comes in to count them. The kiln's breath was the danger of the month and had changed its address; the fire under the floor was the old danger that had never moved. Both missed a fifth. Neither had been given a figure, and he had not supplied one.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow. It came in under the door and through the door and went to the beams ahead of the woman who carried it, so that the room smelled of the yard before the yard was in it. Under the smell, for one breath and then folded away, the clean bitter edge of charcoal from beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, where the board kept its two unnamed gaps and the marks below it waited where marks wait when nobody has been told to read them. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze in its place by the wall and had not been touched in the night. Halfway along the shelf the place where the pan had stood out to dry through Reed was a place and nothing more: no scrape, no crumb, no print, no word. He held its empty place on a tray of its own and did not lay it across the two gaps above it or across the one stiff crust in the fold two inches from his ear or across the place in the returning line the yard had gone around with a look and a wide step. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. He kept a finger between them without moving anything.

\person{Sella Vail} came in from her own bed with her hair coal-black and the one grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals, and did not look into the basket. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on nothing as she reached for the wool and shut again on the fold. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.” To the fire she said, “Feed, and that'll hold,” and to the pot whose rim stood out with its handle where a hand might find it she said, “You'll do.” She gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody, and she never gave it to the basket, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. \person{Tarn} was already at the kindling under the hearth, doing three things at once --- feeding, lining, putting the fourth thing quietly right --- and saying nothing, because a man who owns nothing keeps his place by being useful before he is spoken to. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back, and his chest was tight from something that had happened in the dark and would not be named at breakfast.

\scenebreak

She took the basket up from under the roof and carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing between the low houses the way two people pass on a stair in the dark, each with a hand on the wall and neither speaking. The half-pace was not slow and not hurried; it was a distance kept, measured from the one thing in the yard that must not be touched, as a surgeon measures a field from the instrument laid out at its edge and knows where the hand must stop. She went around the pan's empty place with the tilt of shoulder and basket that keeps a standing thing standing, and nothing knocked, and nobody said that anything was gone. At \place{the yard well} the trough had lost the skin of ice that had lain on it thin as a skim of fat set aside in a dish, and the water showed bare and dark under the little roof, and a woman letting down a bucket called it weather, and the bucket went down into weather and came up with weather in it. A thing called weather is a thing nobody has agreed to count, and a man who asks it for a figure is given a direction and sent away with the direction going downriver and no entry left behind.

The yard was already at the first firing. The kilns had been brought up in the night for the year's last working season and the air above \place{the Clay Yard} had the hot dry smell of clay giving up its water, and under it the fainter warmth of tallow from the wheel-shed, and under that again, on a wind that came the wrong way off \place{High Kiln}, the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} that put a false sweetness in the mouth. Men went about with their collars up and dust ground small as meal under their nails, going around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline beside it that the whole yard called \lore{the thaw}, going around them with a broom in one hand and a glance in the other and sweeping neither. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they lay, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and in the returning line there was a place that a woman looked at and stepped wide around and did not put a number to. He held the look and the wide step where they belonged and carried nothing across from them into the basket.

A woman come up from the lower yard with a wet pot on her arm stopped where the draught from the mouth did not reach her and said to the open door, “Cold in the mouth this morning. It'll take the coals wrong.” Sella set the basket level and told the fire to feed and hold, and told the pot not to be set wet, and told the woman nothing at all. The woman carried the question away with the pot level on her arm so that nothing slopped, and the yard went on about the firing with the danger standing where the danger stood and nobody putting a figure to it. He had learned early that a house answers a question about a cost by sending the question to a pot, and that the pot never answers, and that this is not a failure of the answer but the whole of it.

At the kiln mouth Sella set the basket down level where no slate lay, out of the draught and where the heat would come to him sideways and not full, and stood over him a moment with her hands still. Then she put her hand to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat. It was a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and the cord at her throat was glazed where a working thumb had oiled it ten thousand times, and the little-door burn half-closed on the cord and gave nothing away. She held the mark one breath and let go. She did not touch it again. She had gone around every opening for one hundred and forty-nine days because none of them had been openings, and an opening had come at last, and the rule held exactly: the hand went to the mark before the kiln mouth and at no other hour, not at a shelf, not at a well, not at a dusk, not at a basket. The hand went away and the household went on.

She opened the mouth and tipped the carried coal in under the back of a broken pot. The fire took it the way a vessel takes a thing it has been waiting for, without haste and without surprise, and gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. She did not count the pulls aloud. She did not ask anybody to hear the silence. Its payer had been unnamed for a hundred and fifty days, and the name was not spoken at the opening either, and the coal went in all the same. “That'll hold,” she told the fire, and to the pot, “Don't set that wet. You'll pay for it in the thaw,” and to the broom, “Go around,” and to the wool again, “Keep it off his ears.” She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the instruction went to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and never to the basket. She carried him past at the half-pace. The act was ordinary. She went back at once to setting pots where pots were set, and there was no weight in her hands and no look toward the mark and no sentence about what had been done.

The coal took under the back of the pot and went red at the edge first and then all through, the way a thing goes red when air is let to it, and the heat came out of the mouth on one breath that crossed his cheek and went off across the yard. He knew the order in which a man goes when the breath takes him wrong --- the head going first, the skin doing what the vessels under it do, the almost-smile that is not peace --- as certainly as he knew the order in which cold arrives, and the knowing stood in the yard and was worth nothing, because the heat was two paces off and the hand that would have pulled the wool over his ear was his own small hand and could not lift it. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear in the fold at home, and he wanted the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would have known what a number of days was, and he got the breath of the coal on his cheek and went on. She had gone back to setting pots where pots were set before the coal had finished taking.

He watched it from the low angle with the surgeon's attention that had no use in it. A theatre is a room in which heat is kept and counted and a man stands at the gate of it and does not go in; he had known rooms like that in another life, and had stood in them with a towel in his hands while somebody else held the field. Here the heat was behind a door of clay and iron and the count was in a silence nobody would enter on a slate. He knew \lore{the damper} for one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, filed on a place of its own and joined to no kiln, worth nothing, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath. \person{Jorra Vail}'s bent finger had gone into that cold iron before this morning, ahead of a sentence left unfinished, and the household had straightened around the silence as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The iron was the iron. A gate with two bites is a gate with two bites, and a man who calls it a doctrine has already found the payer he wanted. There was a cost at this opening and the cost had a name somewhere, and the name was not spoken under this roof, and the boy could not have reached the coal, the mark, the iron, or the two inches of wool at his own chin.

The kilns went on breathing in fours against the yard's going-around, and the women went on putting pots down and lifting them, and the first firing of the month stood in the yard with nobody making a feast of it and nobody crossing himself. From behind the racks came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing and never counted among the hearings; through the wall behind him the kiln missed its fifth; under the boards at home the carried fire would miss its fifth again at dusk. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one. One hundred and fifty days old and five months, told by nobody, exact and unspent in the basket at the kiln mouth while the coal took under the back of the broken pot and the fire answered four pulls and a silence with its payer unnamed. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water and came down against the fold.
