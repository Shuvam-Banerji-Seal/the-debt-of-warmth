The basket came up off \place{the flagstones} where it had stood at the edge of the work, lifted by the handle with the little-door burn closing on the wicker and opening again, and \person{Sella Vail} did not look into it. The wheel had given over its last pot and the coals had been banked under the back of a broken pot, and the going of the light had come the way the water came to the sill and went off it again, without a name put on it and without a figure. She gave the instruction to the wool and the fire before she gave it to anybody --- \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} --- and never gave it to the basket, which was a pot filled and carried and nothing that wanted an instruction of its own. Then she carried \person[Corin]{the boy} up the clay road at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, the two passing on the stair without speaking as they had passed every morning and every evening of his life. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap, and the house went on with what the house kept.

Halfway up she went around the place on the wall where a pan had stood out to dry through Reed and was not standing now, tilting shoulder and basket so nothing knocked, and she did not say that it was gone. Nobody under that roof had said it, and a thing nobody names is a thing a house keeps without an entry, kept all the more carefully for having no slate to put it on. At \place[the yard well]{the well} she went around the trough whose skin of ice had thinned and gone, the water standing bare and dark where the skim of fat had lain, and called it weather to nobody in particular; the weather had heard worse and answered nothing. At the yard she went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and did not explain the step to the boy she was carrying or to anybody else. He felt the wide turn in the shoulder that carried him, the small outward swing of the basket, the settling back; he had learned the shape of it before he had learned the shape of his own hand.

Under the roof she lifted him out of the basket without looking into it, set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she had learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and had never told anybody --- and then she did not go back to it. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals.

At the hearth she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving on a row that stood in some other room of her head, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand that would not close on it. She did not look toward the bed. Her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark}, because a dusk is not an opening; it hung where it hung at her throat, a thumb-length of \place{Low Kiln} clay under its glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and the rule held with nobody to praise it and nobody to see it broken. She went to her own bed without explaining the count, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-two days.

The two rooms do not touch.

In the small hours he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. Nothing in the room moved while he did it. The wool went on riding back from the small bare joint at his wrist, the thread at his mouth kept its frozen line, and the file turned itself out behind his eyes with the exactness of a hand that had once set out a tray and could not now lift its own chin from a fold of cloth. It was the one honest work left to him and the most useless. Everything went where it belonged, and belonging bought nothing.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot under \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} wall through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person and filed apart from the vessels --- and there was no seventh and none invented to stand in the empty place where none was owed. He had learned not to look for the seventh by staring at the empty place; a man who puts an instrument in a hollow because the hollow is there has already decided what the case is. The empty place stayed empty and the six stayed six.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: the pot under the wall, the rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and \person{Jorra Vail}'s knowing of the full note of a Vail pot, which he had kept back from a kiln-master who said it was singing before its time. Jorra's knowing went among the answers and never among the hearings. A knowing held back is not a hearing given, and the two things lay on separate hollows with the space between them kept open by a mind that could not have closed it with a finger. He did not make a fourth answer to keep the three company.

The older intervals lay where they had always lain, Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from a market morning, and the unchecking stroke that followed it. Each had happened. None had been paid. They were objects with the edges worn smooth by being taken out and put back so many times, and the taking out had never once turned one of them into a rule.

On the widened tray lay nine stoppings --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and Reed 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. He kept a finger between them in the dark, the same finger he had kept there since Reed 29, and it had never yet lifted one of them across to its neighbour. A thing held nine times is still owed. The absence of a tenth was a keeping and not a payment, and he would not stand one up to keep the nine company before the seventh year had been earned. Nine likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled, the breath from Seed 16 unspent with its small interval standing at the top of each reach, the seventh year one tend from its end and still held.

Beside them, on no tray of their own, lay \lore{the damper} as one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, joined to no kiln and worth nothing, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side with no third, and the morning had given him nothing to set between them, and he would not make a third to keep them company. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night} stood beside the heard bell out of the yard, four, four and a gap in two places that did not meet. An inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand; he had laid out the whole tray every night since he had learned to lay it out and not one object on it had ever come into his reach.

In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, and no third came to stand beside them. The charcoal under the front edge waited in its clusters, some pressed hard and some faded nearly into the grain, unread and uncounted, and the boy did not go back to it in the dark because there was no hand that could have lifted the board an inch. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear in the fold with its empty place beside it still unnamed and no third, and the pan's gone place on the wall kept the round shape of its absence on a place of its own. He set each where it stood and wrote nothing across the space between. A fourth likeness would have made a cause and a cause would have wanted a payer, and no finger of his drew the line.

The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear, and the foundling was a shape with no sale, a half-turn stopped in its arc and never given a name in the dark, no loaf, no slate, no payer. The reed-cutter's cord went inside its coat with knots unread; \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string kept its last double turn legible, tail tucked, left over right with the small weight of a thumb pressed in, and the word forty was not spoken in this room; \person{Mira Silt}'s second cord lay twisted twice beneath an apron and had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door; \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages and both tins stayed shut three doors down, and the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed kept its pale fold and its thumb-bright rub. The soft-cuffed clerk stayed set below the bridge for a measure nobody called, his slate turned inward to his forearm, and the child's foot stayed the cold to one woman who wound it in wool and weather to the neighbour with a pail. Each thing stood on its own place. There was a whole district of keeping within a dozen paces of the basket and not one of the records could be entered by a hand that could not close on a cup.

Three counts in three places did not become one. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-two days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. They lay within a hand's span of each other in the dark, three counts in three places, and none of them became one, though a man with a clean slate might have stood them up side by side and found a figure at the foot of them before the coals were cold. He had learned to know the danger of a third likeness before it was spoken. He kept the three apart by not looking at any of them too long.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint, and his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about that sound either. He knew what the cold was doing as he had once known what a chart was saying before anybody read it out, and the knowing worked as perfectly as the day he learned it and bought him nothing now. Want came up in him, small and importunate as it always was. It wanted the crust two inches from his ear, stiff and brown-edged and past smelling of anything. It wanted the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, by somebody who could have set a day against a day and said what had been reached and what it had cost. He got neither and could not ask for either, and the wanting did not go away because it was not answered. It lay down beside the other objects on the tray and took up no more room than they did.

He had been carried two days into the firing month and nobody had said how far. One hundred and fifty-two stood in the dark where it had stood on the night before and the night before that, exact, complete, unspent, five months and two days, and there was not a mouth under that roof that would have known what to do with the saying of it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold, as it had come down on every night of his life. The month had opened with a hand at the mark and a fire under a broken pot; the opening had been entered on no slate and the fifth pull had been missed in no account, and the count went on into the days ahead with nine held words, a sixth colour, a sixth shape and the loan uncalled. The note went on over water through the gap in the door. The fire missed its fifth under the floor. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
