The light went out of the yard the way water goes out of a shallow pan, by degrees and with no hand calling the moment, and \person{Sella Vail} lifted \person[Corin]{the boy} up from the edge of the work and carried him toward the roof at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. The instruction had gone to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom and never to the basket; a basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a pot that is set down is not spoken to. Woodsmoke went before her up \place{the clay road} and the river wind came up to meet it, the two passing between the low houses the way two people pass on a stair in the dark, each with a hand on the wall and neither giving way and neither speaking. It was the same passage she had made every dusk of his life and would make again, and he was one hundred and fifty days old and five months exactly and nobody walking ahead of him carried the number in any hand.

The half-pace was not slow and not hurried; it was a distance kept, measured from the one thing in the yard that must not be touched, the way a surgeon measures a field from the instrument laid out at its edge and knows where the hand must stop. She went around the pan's empty place with the tilt of shoulder and basket that keeps a standing thing standing, and nothing knocked, and nobody said that anything was gone. At \place{the yard well} the trough had lost the skin of ice that had lain on it thin as a skim of fat set aside in a dish, and the water showed bare and dark under its little roof, and the woman letting down a bucket called it weather, and the bucket went down into weather and came up with weather in it. At the yard she went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie, as wide as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and did not explain the step. The step explained itself to anybody who had lived a year in that yard, and the boy had lived a year in it in the only way he had ever lived anywhere, which was carried and set down and carried again. He went around the three absences with her and carried none of them across into the basket.

Under \place[the Vail kiln-house]{the Vail roof} she lifted him out without looking into the basket, set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and weight settled, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat of the yard and she left it frozen and unwiped, because no instruction had ever been given about the thread and a child set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. It was a longer rest than setting a bundle asks for, and it came in the same place every dusk and went away in the same way, and he had stopped, long ago, expecting it to become a touch or a word. It became neither. It went away and did not come back.

She went to the hearth. “It was the thaw,” she told the fire, which was the sentence she told it when a neighbour spoke of a counting room shut in a war and when a man came back from the ice with a number in his fist and when nothing had happened at all. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the iron edge going under and lifting and letting fall, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving along a row that was not there, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the one grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand that would not close on it, did not look toward the bed, did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at a dusk because a dusk is not an opening, and went to her own bed without explaining the count. The two rooms did not touch.

In the small hours cold came in its known order, cheek first, then temple, then the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool had ridden back. He lay on his back under the roof and turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. It was the one honest work in the room and the most useless: everything was in its place, exact, complete and worth nothing. He had spent a life reducing shoulders in the dark and could not lift his own hand to his own chin; he could lay out nine objects in nine hollows and could not have told a living person what they were. The count grew more exact and therefore more isolating, and the hand that would have offered it was the hand outside the wool that went up an inch and came down.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lay beside and never among. Five came from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \place{the Drawing} --- and the sixth was the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, from a person and not a vessel, filed beside the five and never counted into them. There was no seventh and he did not invent one to fill the empty place. The empty place was not owed a seventh; a man who invents a hearing to keep six company has already begun to want an account, and an account wants a foot at the bottom of it with a name under the foot. He kept the six where they lay. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night} stood beside the heard bell out of the yard and did not become a third sound.

Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. A rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; a pot under the wall that answered almost full with air under the note and a want in it; and \person{Jorra Vail}'s knowing of the note a Vail pot would give before it left the wheel, which he had kept from a \place{High Kiln} kiln-master who said the pot was singing before its time. Jorra's knowing sat among the answers and not among the hearings, because a knowing withheld is not a hearing given, and the boy had learned early to keep the two kinds of thing on two places. The older intervals lay where they had always lain, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the stroke that went on over \place{the flagstones} without the check, his own held breath from a market, and the unchecking stroke that came after. Each was an object. None was a cause.

On the widened tray lay nine stoppings of the near-saying --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- and he held them apart by hand. They were objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, and no payer had been named to any of them. The loan that could hear was uncalled and the seventh year stood one tend from its end and was still held. Nine likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer at the foot of it; he had known a man in a better-lit room to strip the mark off a corpse and enter it and go home clean, and he had learned what a row of neat entries will do when somebody wants a foot at the bottom. So he held them apart. No tenth stopping was suffered to stand up before the seventh year had been earned, and no tenth was owed on this night; a tenth stood up too early is a debt invented to make the other nine into a bill. The nine lay where they lay, exact, complete and unspent.

Beside these, on no tray of their own, lay the pieces that would not sit in a hollow. \lore{The damper} was one iron with two bites --- the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man --- filed joined to no kiln, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side with no third, for men had gone into a room that keeps its finger's width of open and the night had supplied no second voice to make a rule of them. The told bell stood beside the heard bell. An inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand; he could have set the whole district's withholdings on a towel in order and still not have lifted the lid of one tin.

In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and the charcoal under its front edge waited where marks wait when nobody has been told to read them. One stiff brown-edged crust lay in the fold two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. The sixth green was a colour joined to no ear, and the foundling's sixth half-turn a shape with no sale, never named in the dark and never, in any room of his head, called by the name the colour would one day want. The pan's gone place was held on a place of its own beside them all, and no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line. Two gaps in a shelf and one gap in a fold sit in rooms that do not touch, and there is a saying in \place{Low Kiln} that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name; no finger in the district had laid these together, and his was a small hand outside the wool and could not have reached the first board, let alone the second.

Three counts in three places did not become one. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. Four and a gap in three rooms, and the three gaps were three different things. He had learned in a better-lit room how fast three likenesses will stand up and find a payer when a man with a clean slate comes in to count them, and he kept a finger between them without moving anything.

Cold arrived cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and fifty days old and five months, told by nobody; if somebody under that roof said the number nobody would have known what it counted. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, and he wanted the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and he got neither and could not ask for either. The wanting was small and animal and the knowing was exact, and both were true, and the knowing had never once made the wanting quieter.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and fifty stood exact and unspent in the dark, and the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not his and a day that had not come.
