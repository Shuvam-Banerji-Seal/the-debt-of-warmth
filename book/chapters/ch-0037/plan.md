# Chapter 37 — *The First Opening* — beat sheet

**Arc:** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — “a warm stone, already going cold”.
**Days:** 150–152 (Tallow 1–3, Y.H. 813; Firstday, Secondday, Thirdday). A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each opened by its own `\daymark` and lived in full, no day skipped or poured into another. Seven scenes; Day 150 carries three, Day 151 two, Day 152 two. The next Account-day and market-day is the tenth of the month, which belongs to the following chapter.
**Mode:** normal. Every day opens with its own `\daymark{N}{date}`; the drop capital belongs to Scene 1.
**POV:** Daniel Marchetti (Corin), close third-person past, one hundred and fifty to one hundred and fifty-two days old (five months, then five months and two days), told by nobody. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from his eyes. The surgeon's register colours the noticing; it never becomes a speech.

**Calendar reconciliation (read before writing).** `plan/next.md` and `memory/state.md` call these days “Smoke 1–3” and say the firing month has begun. The binding `bible/calendar.md` does not: its mapping rule (Day 1 = Stone 7, Y.H. 812; *t* = 336 + *N* − 1) gives Day 149 = Reed 30, **Day 150 = Tallow 1, Day 151 = Tallow 2, Day 152 = Tallow 3**, all Firstday–Thirdday, Y.H. 813. Smoke is the eighth month (days 211–240). `next.md` itself instructs: *Check every label against the calendar before a scene is written.* This beat sheet and `meta.json` therefore use **Tallow 1–3**. The kiln-firing texture is kept — the kilns are brought up for the year's last working season — but the month's name is the calendar's. The following chapter's first Account-day is the tenth of Tallow (Day 159), not “Smoke 10”. Nothing else in the saga's memory is revised here.

**The chapter's one meaningful change.** A real opening comes under the Vail roof. For the first firing of the month Sella put her hand to the widow's mark *before* she opened the kiln mouth, and at no other hour, and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer still unnamed — now for a hundred and fifty days. The mark had hung untouched through a hundred and forty-nine days of shelves, strips, racks, markets, baskets and dusks because none of them was an opening; an opening has come, and the rule held exactly. The firing month has begun and the danger has changed address: the kiln's breath, hot clay, dust ground small as meal and a pot that sings before its time.

**A second texture, not a resolution.** The pan set out to dry through Reed is gone from its place with no scrape, crumb, print or word. The boy holds its empty place on a tray of its own and does not lay it across the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust and its gap or the field's empty place. No third gap comes, no fourth likeness is made a cause. The trough has lost its skin of ice; the clay jar stands cold under its glaze, glanced at once and not touched.

**Hard withholds for the whole chapter.** No tenth stopping and no calling of the loan; the nine stoppings stay nine objects with no payer named to any, and a tenth must not stand up before the seventh year has been earned. No spending of the Seed 16 breath, no cutting, counting or giving of the seventh year, no collector on the road, no seventh hearing of the full note. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand; Marra stays out of Sill. No knot counted (reed-cutter, Orra, Mira), no charcoal stroke read, no tin or box opened, no silt-leach/cause/cure named for the cold foot, no third crust, no third gap, no joining of shelf gaps, fold gap, field's empty place and the pan's gone place. No name, body or figure for the missing hand; no name from Dessa, who stays dead and uncollected; no name for the green, never Fenna in narration. The foundling is offstage all three days — the sixth glimpse has landed and must gather interest, not be repeated for effect. No loaf passed, no slate near the basket, no sale. Alda stays offstage with her mercy-lie uncorrected; *he's not Vail* stays sealed; the foundling's name promise stays ungiven. The old life stays offstage: no `\memory{}` tag, no present-tense Chicago break.

## Scene 1 — The First Opening

**Day and time.** Day 150, Tallow 1, Firstday, before first grey into the morning. First dated heading of the chapter and of Day 150; the drop capital belongs here.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the hearth, and at the kiln mouth in the Clay Yard; the yard well and the trough on the way down.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the boy in the basket; Tarn at the kindling (silent, doing three things at once); yard neighbours going about the first firing; no named visitor.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open forward from the last small hours of Reed without recap: the boy is one hundred and fifty days old and exactly five months and nobody under any roof says so. Put the number on the page as his private count, exact and unspent (T071/T072). The reed month is over; the firing month has begun.
2. First smell of the chapter is hot clay and tallow; first sound is the kiln's breath in fours with its gap, not a market bell. The pan set out to dry is gone from its place and nobody says so; the trough by the well has lost its skin of ice and is called weather; the clay jar still stands cold under its glaze (T058/T079).
3. Sella wakes the fire under the back of a broken pot. It gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; she does not count the pulls aloud and does not ask anyone to hear the silence, and its payer has been unnamed for a hundred and fifty days (T088).
4. **The change.** For the first opening of the month she puts her hand to the widow's mark before the kiln mouth, holds it one breath and lets go, tips the coal in, and does not touch the mark again. A real opening has come at last. The rule holds exactly: touched before the opening and at no other hour (T058/T079).
5. She gives the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket, and carries him past at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate (T058/T079).
6. The boy reads the opening as a surgeon reads a theatre he cannot enter: heat kept and counted, a gate with two bites, a cost paid by somebody whose name is not spoken. Jorra's bent finger will go into the cold damper ahead of her unfinished sentence later in the day; here the iron is one thing with two bites, filed joined to no kiln and worth nothing (T062).

**Emotional turn.** From the quiet kept thing to the thing finally done. The woman who has gone around every opening for five months opens the kiln and touches the mark, and immediately goes back to giving instructions to pots and wool — the act is ordinary, and the reader feels the weight of it precisely because she will not. The boy is carried through the hot month in a basket with no hand that can reach the heat or the mark or the number of his days.

**Sensory anchors.** Hot clay and tallow; the kiln's breath in fours with a missing fifth; a coal under ash going red under the back of a broken pot; thumb-length lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it under a woman's fingers for one breath; the little-door burn half-closing on the glazed cord; a trough without its skin of ice; the half-pace measured from open slates; river wind crossing woodsmoke on the stair.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (150 kept exact and unspent), T088 (payer unnamed for 150 days), T058/T079 (mark touched before the opening and at no other hour; instruction to wool, fire, pot, broom, never basket; half-pace; *the house*), T062 (damper's double bite filed joined to no kiln), T061/T082.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The first opening plants the firing month's danger — the breath that takes a man in his sleep, dust ground small as meal, a pot that sings early — for the days to come. The carried fire's unnamed payer gathers toward Sella's second roll and the far naming of the Hearth as a wound. The nine stoppings gather toward the end of the seventh year; no tenth is owed yet.

**Exact last image.** The kiln takes the coal under the back of the broken pot, the fire answers four pulls and a silence, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the river's note of water and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 2 — The Yard Goes Around

**Day and time.** Day 150, midday into the afternoon. Second entry of Day 150.

**Place.** The Clay Yard, the drying racks, the edge of the worked strips east of the yard, and the Vail wheel-shed with its finger's-width-open back room (not entered).

**Who is present.** Sella Vail with the boy at the edge of the work; Jorra Vail; three or four unnamed men with collars high (not followed, not named); a High Kiln kiln-master with Ash on his cuffs; Tarn with the broom; yard voices working the first firing.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The kilns go into the year's last working season. The kiln's breath is the month's danger: a thing that takes a man in his sleep, spoken of sideways by a yard voice as a man in Mud End once taken, not made into a lesson. Clay dust is ground small as meal under the nails, and the sweetish lead smell comes up from High Kiln on a bad wind (T009/T013/T092 texture).
2. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out --- a look, a wide step and no number (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried, not paid).
3. Before first grey three or four men went low-voiced into the room off the shed whose door keeps its finger's width of open; Sella carried past without a name for them, turned no head and did not say the room was cold. Jorra came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate (T035/T031).
4. He spoke to the wheel before anyone --- *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* --- told a pot *You'll do*, knew its full note before it left the wheel and withheld the knowing from the High Kiln kiln-master who said it was singing before its time and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. The knowing goes to the near-full tray, not among the hearings (T062, T061/T082).
5. Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing; the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The boy reads one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed joined to no kiln (T062).
6. The two tellings of the shut counting room stay side by side with no third; the morning gives no third telling though men went into a room that stands a finger's width open (T065/T095).
7. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed. His seventh year stands one tend from its end: six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, knife at the lip of its sill, unforged collector sentence beside it, breath from Seed 16 unspent. His stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check, and he does not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector. No collector comes. Neither man names the other's silence (T036/T076).

**Emotional turn.** The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to. Danger is present in two forms --- men in a room that will not be named, and the collector's road --- and both are met by going on with the work. The boy reads a kiln-master's withheld knowing as a near-full answer, exact and unspendable.

**Sensory anchors.** Hot clay dust ground small as meal, distinct from bone-coloured slip; the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a bad wind; cold iron taking the warmth out of a bent fingertip; the wheel's clay-and-tallow smell; the worn thumb-mark with six in it and a seventh that cannot be checked; the pale clip-ring catching what light there is; the shallow bright patch on the flagstones that keeps the place where a stroke once stopped.

**Threads advanced.** T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried, unpaid), T035/T031, T062, T065/T095, T036/T076 (seventh year held, loan uncalled), T058/T079, T061/T082, T071/T072 (150 exact).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The kiln's breath and the dust gather toward the firing-season danger of the chapters ahead; the two shut-room tellings and the damper's double bite gather toward ~650; the seventh year one tend from its end and the nine held words gather toward the day something must give.

**Exact last image.** Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones keep the place where it stopped as a thing nobody will enter.

## Scene 3 — One Hundred and Fifty

**Day and time.** Day 150, the going of the light into the small hours. Third entry of Day 150; the day's closing inventory.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying him up and going to her own bed; the boy alone in the small hours.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace, goes around the pan's empty place, around the trough now without its skin of ice, and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She sets him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it goes away (T058/T079).
2. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale off the coals, sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, does not touch the widow's mark at a dusk because a dusk is not an opening, and goes to her own bed without explaining the count. The two rooms do not touch (T058/T079).
3. In the small hours he turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, each thing in its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else: six hearings of the full note beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa as the men lifted, from a person; no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place. Three near-full answers on their own tray, advanced and not paid, with Jorra's withheld knowing among them and not among the hearings. The older intervals with the half-beat behind the standing finger, the stroke that went on without the check, his own held breath and the unchecking stroke (T061/T082).
4. **Nine** stoppings lie on the widened tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end and still held. He holds them apart by hand. Nine likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. He will not stand a tenth up before the seventh year has been earned (T036/T076, T061/T082).
5. Beside these, the pieces with no tray of their own: the damper as one iron with two bites joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings side by side with no third; the told bell of the Long Night beside the heard bell out of the yard. An inventory is not a key and a list is not a hand (T062, T065/T095, T078).
6. In the other room the shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and its unread charcoal; one stiff brown-edged crust lies in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; the sixth green is a colour joined to no ear and the foundling's sixth half-turn a shape with no sale, never Fenna and never named; the pan's gone place is held on a place of its own beside them all and no fourth likeness is made a cause (T100, T060/T086, T097, T050/T051, T033/T034).
7. Three counts in three places do not become one: the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the carried fire's four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty days; the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb (T088, T061/T082).
8. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and fifty days old and five months, told by nobody; if somebody under that roof said the number nobody would know what it counted. He wants the crust two inches from his ear, and he wants the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither and cannot ask.

**Emotional turn.** The night inventory is the one honest work in the room and the most useless: everything is in its place, exact, complete and worth nothing. The count grows more exact and therefore more isolating; the hand that would offer it cannot reach the crust or lift to its own chin. The dry joke lives in the surgeon laying out instruments he cannot touch.

**Sensory anchors.** The frozen thread at the mouth; the half-beat of a hand longer than the setting needed; a trough without ice; woodsmoke and river wind passing on the stair; six hearings laid beside and never among; the flat knock from a person filed apart; nine objects held by hand in nine hollows; the pot handle two inches beyond a small hand; four pulls and a silent fifth under the floor; cold in its known order.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (150 exact), T061/T082, T036/T076 (nine stoppings as objects, no tenth), T088, T058/T079, T060/T086, T097, T098, T099, T100, T024/T059/T025, T050/T051, T033/T034, T023/T089, T090/T080, T093/T096, T009/T013/T092, T062, T065/T095, T078.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The nine stoppings and the pressed seventh year gather toward the day something must give; the sixth green toward the far day an ear is spoken with that colour; the sixth half-turn toward the sale that will land as a betrayal. Every private record goes on gathering toward a far-off hand not his.

**Exact last image.** His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold, one hundred and fifty exact and unspent in the dark, while the charcoal waits under the shelf for a hand not his and a day that has not come.

## Scene 4 — A Direction, Not a Bundle

**Day and time.** Day 151, Tallow 2, Secondday, morning into afternoon. First dated heading of Day 151.

**Place.** Downriver at the Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards and the fish-trade shade below the sill; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail and the boy at the edge of the work; the reed-cutter with his grey cord; Orra Weir; Mira Silt; a weir woman who asks what the levy will take; a buyer who names a figure for two pair of eels; the woman who winds the child's foot in wool and a neighbour with a pail; the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (still set, spoken to by nobody).

**Beats, in order.**
1. The last of the reed work has become a direction rather than a bundle: a going-downriver and a coming-back, counted in the arm that carries it. A cost asked as a direction is answered with the weather and the river, leaving no entry anywhere (T071/T072, T077). Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket (T058/T079).
2. The reed-cutter sets his bundle on the flat blessing stone, straightens the grey thumb-oiled cord, turns it once so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat with the flat of his hand. To a woman who cannot read it: *You can ask the cord* and *No more can the Court*, without anger or smile. No figure, knot, name or joining (T099 carried, unpaid).
3. Orra stands with her board on its side and her hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered, salt white at her wrists, the grey streak first through the black hair, the nasal half-song of the Fen in her voice where she is tired. She pulls one knot down with her thumb and, where a figure is asked, gives the ninth of Ilmen, the collector's boat round the bend on the flood with six oars and one man at the stern who never touched them; to *That isn't the levy*, *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* Her thumb goes to the cord again at the end; whether lie or habit the boy cannot tell and makes no cause. The forty-first double turn stays legible, tail tucked, left over right with the small weight of a thumb, and the word forty is unspoken (T024/T059/T025).
4. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin she answers her own answer: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.* She flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie and puts one away. When the buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; a shifting creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the thin child's knot untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's and does not touch it (T059/T025/T024).
5. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wound it and weather to the neighbour with a pail. The boy reads the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform; the reading works as perfectly as the day he learned it and buys him nothing. No silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it (T093/T094/T096 carried).
6. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm with the writing inward, his hand to the slate before his eyes go to the stalls. Nobody steps around him and nobody has taken him off; the market sells over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies (T090/T080).
7. Five private records stand within a dozen paces of the basket --- cord, knot-string, second twist, inward slate, dark foot --- side by side and not across, and none can be entered (T058/T079, T061/T082). The foundling is offstage; no loaf passes, no slate is seen and no sale is made (T033/T034 held).

**Emotional turn.** The women keep what no Court thumb can spell, and each answers a question about a figure with something that is not a figure. The boy stands in the middle of five records he can read the shape of and cannot enter; the surgeon's perfect reading of a cold foot is the most useless thing in the shade. The held glimpse of the foundling gathers interest without being spent.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey thumb-oiled cord; teeth tying a knot low; silt dried pale in the hollow of a neck; eel-bones flicked against a thumb; salt white at wrists and pale eel scars uncovered; the Fen half-song; three turns of wool around a dark foot; a cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday; running water over the boards; the sweetish lead smell off a pot from High Kiln.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (151 exact), T077, T058/T079, T099 (carried, unpaid), T024/T059/T025, T093/T094/T096 (carried), T090/T080 (carried), T061/T082, T033/T034 (held).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. T099, Orra's knot, Mira's second twist and the clerk's inward slate gather toward the far-off convergence with the charcoal under the shelf and the field's empty place. The cold foot gathers toward the silt-leach four arcs away; no cause or cure may be named here. The foundling's held sixth glimpse gathers interest offstage.

**Exact last image.** The market goes on over the soft-cuffed clerk as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, his hand to the slate before his eyes find the stalls, while the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 5 — The Pan's Empty Place

**Day and time.** Day 151, afternoon into the going of the light. Second entry of Day 151.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle and at the hearth.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail; the boy set low near the bench leg; a neighbour with a wet pot come up from the firing work.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The pan that stood out to dry through Reed is gone from its place and nobody has said so --- no scrape, no crumb, no print, no word. The boy holds its empty place on a tray of its own (T060/T086, T097). He does not lay it across the shelf's two unnamed gaps above, the one stiff crust two inches from his ear in the other room, the field's empty place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and came back without, or the sixth colour and the sixth shape. No third gap comes, no fourth likeness is made a cause and no finger of his draws the line (T097, T060/T086, T098, T050/T051, T033/T034).
2. The clay jar still stands cold under its glaze. Sella glances at it once and does not touch it (T058/T079).
3. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, she reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies, without looking and without a word, brings it away and does not go back to the shelf. The boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say; such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stands between the two (T100 carried, unread).
4. A neighbour with a wet pot asks what a firing costs set down there. Sella glances once at the clay jar and sends her to the pot: *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.* The woman carries the unanswered question away level so nothing slopped (T077).
5. At the hearth she tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, and stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening. The fire gives its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-one days (T088, T058/T079).
6. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs at her throat and is not touched at a shelf or an afternoon, because neither is an opening. She does not look toward the bed (T058/T079). The kiln breathing behind the wall is the month's danger, present in every room without being named twice (T062 held).

**Emotional turn.** Care that looks like not seeing. The woman who feeds every hungry child keeps a record where nobody thinks to look and will not say what it is; the under-board finger stays a habit with no explanation. The boy's reading of the pan's gone place lands exactly and buys him nothing --- the adult mind recognises one more keeping, the infant body cannot lift a board an inch or hold a cup.

**Sensory anchors.** The underside of a board becoming a roof; two bare gaps in a row of loaves; a cold clay jar under its glaze; the empty ring where a drying pan stood; charcoal faded nearly into the grain; the little-door burn half-closing on air; tallow and woodsmoke; four low pulls of fire and the silence where the fifth should be; the kiln's breath behind the wall.

**Threads advanced.** T100 (carried, unread), T060/T086, T097 (held, unpaid, no cause), T077, T058/T079, T088, T071/T072 (151 exact), T061/T082, T062 (held).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. T097 gathers a further shaping toward the far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and make an account want a name; T100 gathers toward the far-off hand that will read the district's private records. The pan's gone place is a new small absence held apart, not joined and not counted. The under-board finger stays unexplained.

**Exact last image.** The pot stands rim out with its handle two inches beyond the small hand that would close on it and does not, while the charcoal waits under the shelf unread and the pan's place on the wall keeps the shape of its absence.

It cares if they're steady* --- told a pot *You'll do*, knew its full note before it left the wheel and withheld the knowing from the High Kiln kiln-master who said it was singing before its time and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. The knowing goes to the near-full tray and not among the hearings (T062, T061/T082).
5. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire was low, his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence. The boy reads one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed joined to no kiln. The two shut-room tellings stay side by side with no third (T062, T065/T095).
6. At the hearth, with the coals banked and the knife at the edge of its sill, the ear-sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it is **not** brought to the word that would call the loan this day. No tenth stopping stands up; the nine stoppings stay nine objects on the interval tray with no payer named to any. The loan stays uncalled, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the seventh year one tend from its end and not given (T036/T076, T061/T082). Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room, and the two silences stay unjoined (T035/T031, T036/T076).

**Emotional turn.** The equality under the roof holds at the place it has moved to: the wheel-keeper's finger leads at the damper, the debt's stroke goes on without the check, and the loan that can hear is not called. The day's restraint is its own kind of danger --- a thing held nine times is still owed, and the absence of a tenth is a keeping, not a payment.

**Sensory anchors.** Ash in knuckle creases the colour of the clean dark line; the pale clip-ring holding what light there is; the worn thumb-mark with six in it and the seventh held where the house cannot check; grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger; cold iron taking the warmth out of a fingertip; the shallow bright patch on the flagstones; the fired Pot's note withheld from a kiln-master; banked coals under a broken pot.

**Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (seventh year held, nine stoppings, no tenth, loan uncalled), T061/T082 (nine likenesses held apart), T035/T031, T062, T065/T095, T009/T013/T092, T058/T079, T071/T072 (152 exact).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The ninth stopping is not a tenth; a tenth must not be spent before the seventh year itself has been earned. The pressed seventh year, the nine held words, the damper's double bite and the two shut-room tellings all gather toward the day something must give and a payer be named at the foot of the account.

**Exact last image.** The stones keep the shallow bright patch where a stroke once stopped, nine objects lie in nine hollows with no payer named to any, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold.

## Scene 7 — One Hundred and Fifty-Two

**Day and time.** Day 152, the going of the light into the small hours. Fourth entry of Day 152; the chapter's closing inventory and the turn toward the first Account-day of the month.

**Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof at the bed, the hearth and the loaf shelf from the basket's low angle.

**Who is present.** Sella Vail carrying him up and going to her own bed; the boy alone in the small hours.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around the pan's empty place, the trough without its skin of ice and wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She sets him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting before it goes away. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale off the coals, sets the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, neither looks toward the bed nor touches the widow's mark because a dusk is not an opening, and goes to her own bed without explaining the count. The two rooms do not touch (T058/T079).
2. In the small hours he turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; the older intervals with the half-beat, the unchecking stroke and his held breath; **nine** stoppings of the near-saying as objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, the loan uncalled and the seventh year one tend from its end and still held; the damper's double bite filed joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the told bell beside the heard bell (T061/T082, T036/T076, T062, T065/T095, T078).
3. In the other room the shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and its unread charcoal, one stiff brown-edged crust lies in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third, the pan's gone place is held on a place of its own; the sixth green is a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, never Fenna and never named in narration; the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin stay unread and shut three doors down; the soft-cuffed clerk stays set for an uncalled measure and the cold foot stays the cold to one woman and weather to the yard (T100, T060/T086, T097, T098, T099, T024/T059/T025, T050/T051, T033/T034, T023/T089, T090/T080, T093/T096).
4. Three counts in three places do not become one: the river's note of water over water through the finger's-width door, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; the carried fire's four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-two days; the boards' four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb (T088, T061/T082).
5. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint. One hundred and fifty-two days old and five months and two days, told by nobody; if somebody under that roof said the number nobody would know what it counted. He wants the crust two inches from his ear, and he wants the number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number was, and gets neither and cannot ask.
6. His hand goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down against the fold, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. Nothing is joined, paid, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The month has opened with a hand at the mark and a fire under a broken pot; the count goes on into the days ahead with nine held words, a sixth colour, a sixth shape and the loan uncalled (T071/T072).

**Emotional turn.** Everything the firing month has displayed so far --- the opening, the fire in fours with its silent fifth, the yard going around its empty places, the direction that is not a bundle --- has come home under one roof and been put on its own tray, still separate, still worthless, still kept. The boy is two days older in a house that keeps its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces. The forward motion is the count going on into a new month with nothing settled.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and river wind passing on the stair; the trough without its skin of ice; the frozen thread at the mouth; the half-beat longer than the setting; banked coals under a broken pot; the widow's mark hanging untouched at a dusk; nine objects in nine hollows; the pan's empty ring; the fifth green and the sixth held as a colour and a shape; the river's note through the finger's-width door; four pulls and a silent fifth under the floor; four nails, four and a knot; cold in its known order.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (152 exact, unspent), T061/T082, T036/T076 (nine as objects, no tenth), T088, T058/T079, T060/T086, T097 (held, unpaid), T098, T099, T100, T024/T059/T025, T050/T051, T033/T034, T023/T089, T090/T080, T093/T096, T009/T013/T092, T062, T065/T095, T078.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. The nine stoppings, the pressed seventh year, the sixth green, the sixth half-turn and the pan's gone place all gather interest into the firing month. The district's private records --- charcoal under the shelf, reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knots, Mira's second twist, Alda's six pages and both tins, Dessa's pewter tin, Berr's locked box and the clerk's inward slate --- stand side by side for the far-off hand that will lay them together and call the joining an account.

**Exact last image.** The note goes on over water through the gap in the door, the fire misses its fifth under the floor, and the charcoal waits under the shelf for a hand that is not his and a day that has not come; one hundred and fifty-two stands exact and unspent in the dark as his hand comes down against the fold.

## How the final moment pulls into the next chapter

The chapter ends mid-keeping, not closed. The firing month has opened with a real opening and a hand at the widow's mark, and nothing that opened has been paid for or entered: the carried fire still misses its fifth pull with its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifty-two days, the kilns are working with their breath in every room, the yard still goes around its unswept lines and the empty place where a hand went in and did not come out, the pan's gone place is held apart from the other absences, and no third gap has come. Nine stoppings lie on the interval tray with no payer named to any; a tenth has not been spent before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stands one tend from its end and has not given; the loan that can hear is still uncalled. The sixth green is still a colour, the foundling's sixth half-turn still a shape with no loaf, no slate and no sale, and the clerk is still set for a measure nobody called.

Day 153 is Tallow 4 and belongs to the following chapter. The first Account-day and market-day of the month is the tenth (Day 159): Berr should come up to the reading block with his office cord folded under his coat and the green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner, wipe his thumb twice, read *one in twelve, as the notice says*, call the levy and deny it with *your house*, and go off with the true subtraction locked in his box --- the figure read still not the figure paid. Let the firing month go on gathering the interest rather than paying it. Bring the seventh year nearer its end without calling the loan; do not stand a tenth stopping up, name the green, sell the foundling's secret, open a tin or box, read a private tally or join any of the absences. Keep Marra out of Sill, Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy, Alda's mercy-lie uncorrected, *he's not Vail* sealed and the old life offstage. Forward, not closed.
