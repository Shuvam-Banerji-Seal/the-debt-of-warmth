## Chronicle

The month turned over in the dark the way a pot turns on a wheel, a quarter at a time. The boy was one hundred and fifty days old and five months exactly, and nobody under that roof said either number. The kilns had been brought up for the last working season. At the kiln mouth Sella Vail put her hand to the widow's mark at the first firing of the month, held it one breath, let go, and tipped the coal under a broken pot. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot and the broom and never to the basket, and went back to setting pots.

The yard went around what it would not enter: the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw, the broken birth-slates under the rack, and the place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. Before first grey men went low into the finger's-width-open back room and Sella carried past without a name. Jorra Vail came through with slate dust ground small as meal on both forearms, spoke to the wheel first, told a pot it would do, and withheld its note from a High Kiln kiln-master. His bent finger went into the cold damper ahead of her unfinished sentence --- one iron with two bites, joined to no kiln. Tarn swept past the shallow bright patch without the half-check; no collector came.

Downriver the last of the reed work had become a direction rather than a bundle, counted in the arm that carried it, and a woman who asked what the hands would be paid was answered with the going down and the coming back. The reed-cutter tied one knot with his teeth and put the cord inside his coat. Orra Weir gave the ninth of Ilmen where a figure was asked; Mira Silt answered a thin run with Ask the river. A child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool and the clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called.

Under the roof the pan that stood out to dry through Reed was gone with no scrape, crumb, print or word; the boy held its empty place apart from the shelf's two gaps. Sella reached a finger beneath the shelf where the charcoal tally lay, without looking. On the third day Tarn told the kindling, Six counted. The next I don't count, and the ear-sentence came to the word that would call the loan and stopped. No tenth stopping stood up. At dusk she set him on his bed and went to her own bed. In the small hours his file held six hearings and no seventh and nine stoppings with no payer named; one hundred and fifty-two stood exact and unspent as his hand went up an inch toward the note of water and came down.
