\daymark{152}{Tallow 3, Y.H. 813 (Thirdday)}

The third day of Tallow came up over \place{the Clay Yard} in the ordinary way, which is to say that no woman marked it and no slate in the district was the wiser. Morning found \person[Corin]{the boy} set low at the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} work where no slate lay, and \person{Sella Vail} had given the instruction to the wool and the fire and the pot and the broom before she gave it to anybody, and had never given it to the basket. \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed. He took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named, with the small bent nod he gave to every tool that had been handed a purpose, and he said, “Yes, house,” to the ones who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and the ones who wanted it late, agreeing with both so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and going on wanting the late because it puts off the paying. The kiln breathed behind the wall in fours with a gap. Woodsmoke went up the yard and the river wind came down to meet it, and the two passed without speaking, as they had passed on every morning of his life.

His chest had been tight since the dark. The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent in him with its small interval standing at the top of each reach, where the breath should have gone in and had not, and he worked around the absence the way a man works around a loose tooth, without touching it and without pretending it was not there. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His seventh year stood one tend from its end and still held. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check; the seventh stood where it could not, at the lip of the sill, beyond the reach of any thumb but his own. The small knife lay at the very edge of that sill with the unforged collector sentence beside it, and the sentence lay as quietly as a tool laid down after use, waiting for a hand that had not come.

His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without even the half-check. He did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and no collector came. The stones kept the place where the stroke had once stopped, a shallow brightness nobody swept and nobody entered, and the broom went over it as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. He had checked half a beat there once, behind a wheel-keeper's standing finger, and the half-beat stood in the file with the older intervals, an object and not a rule, an interval and not a payment. Two ears had kept their equality under this roof for a hundred and fifty-two days: his, which heard a pot before a knuckle touched it, and the boy's, which turned toward the note before anybody made it. Two silences, one shape, a loan that could hear and had never been called. He swept the road over and over and kept his exact account of what he owed, which was the one thing in the house the house could not check.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with the day's fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, and it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. A thing mixed is a thing somebody put together on purpose; a thing ground is a thing that got there by being broken. He had learned to tell the two before he could have said why, and the telling, exact and complete, stood in the basket and was worth nothing. Jorra kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, because a question put to that dust would want a figure at the foot of it and no figure had been given to anybody. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they lay, gone around and entered by nobody; this house had long ago decided that a thing gone around is a thing kept, and keeping was the one form of accounting it performed without a slate. He did not look toward the basket. The dust went into the creases past the bend of the finger the way dust goes into any crease, without being invited and without leaving, and his hands went on with the work as if the forearms were the forearms he had come in with.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he told the wheel, in the flat voice that went to the tool before the clay and to the clay before a person. He told a pot, “You'll do.” He knew \lore{the full note} of it before it left the wheel --- knew the pitch it would give under a knuckle when it had cooled and been set down --- and he did not say so when a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} on the wrong wind, with Ash the colour of the ridge on his cuffs, said the pot was singing before its time and that a pot which sings early generally sings itself out early. Jorra said nothing. The kiln-master went off with his sentence whole and the pot went on turning. The knowing went on the tray of answers that had come near to full and had not been paid, and not among the hearings. There were six hearings of the full note, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, and there was no seventh and none invented to fill the place where none was owed. A knowing held back is not a hearing given; he set the two on separate hollows and kept his finger between them.

From the basket's low angle the wheel was a stand of wet clay and tallow, and the sparks from the mouth went up beyond his seeing; the heat came to him sideways and not full, the way it comes to a pot set at the edge of the work and not under the mouth. The kiln's breath was the danger of the month, and the house carried it as a woman carries a sleeping child through a door, carefully and without remarks. It went in fours with a gap and nobody gave the gap a figure, because a figure wants a payer at the foot of it and the payer had been unnamed now for a hundred and fifty-two days. He knew the shape of a room that keeps its danger behind a screen and goes on with the work in front of it; the hands that know where the screen stands are the hands that must not go behind it. Here the screen was clay and iron, and his hand was a small hand outside the wool that could not have lifted a corner of it.

The two tellings of the shut counting room stood where they stood, side by side with no joining and no third. Men with their collars high had gone low-voiced before first grey into \place{the back room} off the shed, whose door keeps its finger's width of open, and Sella had carried past at the half-pace and given the house no name for them and had not said the room was cold. The morning gave no third telling to set beside the other two. A second likeness is the beginning of a rule and a third likeness is the beginning of an account, and a man with a clean slate needs only the third to stand the other two up and find a payer at the foot of them. He held the two where they were and would not stand a third up to keep them company. The door kept its finger's width. The yard swept past it and went on with the firing.

\scenebreak

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low, Jorra's bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair's breadth, and left it standing. The cold took the warmth out of the fingertip as iron takes it, without haste and without malice. The end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it: Sella's hands went on to the next pot, Tarn's head stayed down over his work, and nothing in the room turned to look at what had been done. It was an obedience and it was a correction neither of them would name, and the room came level again in the space of a breath.

He read it as one piece of iron with two bites: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, the holding and the taking in the same throat of iron, and no word in \lore{Grathic} that would hold both mercies in one breath. He had known a clamp for what it was in a better-lit room --- a thing you set where the vessel was thin, and thanked for the mercy that stopped the bleed, and hated for the same mercy when it stayed a turn too long --- but a clamp is a clamp and the two bites here were one bite filed on a place of its own, joined to no kiln and worth nothing. A man who calls it a doctrine has already found the payer he wanted. It stood where it stood, and the two tellings of the shut room stood beside it with no third, and the boy could not have reached the iron, and there was no hand in the basket to lay across it.

At the going of the light the coals were banked under the back of a broken pot and the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where the house could see it and the seventh year stood where the house could not. Tarn told the kindling, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it came up in him as it had come up nine times before, and on this day it was not brought to the word that would call the loan. It stopped short of the threshold, the way a man stops short of a step he has learned to know by the feel of the floor under his foot, and no tenth stopping stood up to join the nine. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, said, “Yes, house,” to the fire, and went back to the broom.

Nine objects lay on the widened tray, and he held them apart by hand. Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and Reed 29: nine stoppings of the near-saying, objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. A thing held nine times is still owed. The absence of a tenth was a keeping, not a payment, and he would not stand one up to keep the nine company before the seventh year had been earned. Nine likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a doctrine if nobody keeps a finger between them, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled, the Seed 16 breath unspent, the seventh year one tend from its end and not given. The unforged collector sentence lay beside the knife where it had lain all day, waiting for a hand that did not come.

Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined under the one roof while the kiln went on breathing in fours behind the wall. Her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at a hearth or a wheel-shed, because neither was an opening; it hung where it hung, a thumb-length of \place{Low Kiln} clay under its glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and the rule held with nobody to praise it and nobody to see it broken. In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps and the charcoal under its board waited unread, and the pan's gone place on the wall kept the round shape of its absence. He set each where it stood and wrote nothing across the space between. No third gap came. No fourth likeness made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

Want came up in him, small and importunate as it always was. It wanted the crust two inches from his ear in the other room, stiff and brown-edged and past smelling of anything; it wanted the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand at the hearth. The surgeon in him could have said how long the crust had lain and what had become of the warmth in it, and could have named the exact reach of a hand that had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and now could not lift its own chin from a fold of cloth. The saying would have put neither crumb nor pot within reach. The joke had gone flat with use, and he kept it on a place of its own beside the others, where it cost him nothing because nobody laughed.

One hundred and fifty-two stood exact and unspent in the going of the light, five months and two days, told by nobody, and nobody under that roof would have known what a number was if it had been spoken. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. The stones kept the shallow bright patch where a stroke had once stopped, the nine objects lay in nine hollows with no payer named to any, and the loan that could hear went on being worth more than the debt that held it, uncalled.
