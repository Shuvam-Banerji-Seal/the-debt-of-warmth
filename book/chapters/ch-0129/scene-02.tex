The afternoon of the fourteenth came on without a boundary drawn, a thin light passing over \place{the Clay Yard} the way water passes over a stone --- not falling on it but going across, wet ash and old brick dust under the light, barley wall to wall with no number in it. \person{Sella Vail} had set the basket at the yard's edge where the coming and going of feet did not touch it, and gone back to the racks, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with his ear level with the ground and his hands outside the wool where the light could find them. He took the small sounds first. The small ones did not lie; the large ones were mostly weather. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock that reached the basket before the woman's step did. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp of dry hemp. Barley shifted wall to wall with the low sound of a dry thing moved by a hand. A loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure.

He took the pail's knock apart by the place of each thing. The fist had carried it. The stone had answered with one flat note and no second. Between the going-down and the sound there was a half-beat, and after the sound a space where another knock would have stood and did not. He gave the half-beat no intention and the space no message, because an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The rope told the same story in another material: a cold-stiffened rasp, a knot taking, the small settling after, and then the ordinary quiet with nothing under it. The woman's mouth said nothing, and the absence of a number was not a number kept back; it was a mouth doing the work it had been given. He had once stood at a table and named the instruments before they touched a body and named them again as they came away, so that nothing was left in a field that had not been taken out of it. There was no table here, no field, no second hand to receive the tool from his. He filed the sounds in the order they arrived and did not add them, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut.

The barley went wall to wall and gave up nothing but its weight. A woman lifted a fist of it, judged it by the hand and not by the eye, set it down and went on; the figure that would be read over it come the tenth of the month would not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. The loaf stopped a finger's width from where it had started, and the stopping was a stopping and not a measure. He kept each on a place of its own, the sound on one, the weight on another, the stopped bread on a third, with a finger between every pair, and made no row of them come out even. A likeness was not a cause. A hand going flat over a sleeve was not a reader. A number counted was a number with air around it, and air bought no warmth.

\scenebreak

He went once around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, because a second going-around is how a keeping becomes a habit. The circuit began under \place{the rack end}, passed the bare dark water of \place{the trough}, crossed to \place{the yard well}, and came back along the line where the yard had decided not to step. Sella went around what she went around with the same look and the same wide step, and gave the boy no instruction about it, because the instruction had gone to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the fire and never to the basket. The widest step came at \place{the returning-line place}. A hand had gone in there on Seed 15 and had not come back. There was no name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space and no payer standing under it. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. He gave the place its own ground and did not look at it a second time.

At the trough the bare dark water held the shallow round print where the clay pan had been lifted away, a ring on a place of its own. Level water in a vessel nobody had calibrated is not honest and not a promise; it is level water, and the boy let it stand as that. Beside the well the flat stone kept the pail's own knock and nothing else. The two unswept lines at \place{the Vail corner} kept a finger between them as they had kept it through the dry nights: the clean dark line, and the finer grey beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw}. The grey had thickened in the dry cold and the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} came up off it where no broom had gone in two years. It lay unswept and was called weather. He took it by the place of it and not by the name, took the clean dark line beside it on its own place and the finger between on a third, and did not look twice, for looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit. Nothing under the roof had been mended by the grey, and the grey had not mended anything under the roof; the two statements stood side by side and did not become one.

The rest of the yard's arithmetic went unentered. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost came back with a note folded twice against her palm and not a price. A man brought a number up out of a shut fist toward the water, looked at the light and put it back into his sleeve, and the number arrived nowhere. At the well a neighbour said the rope would take a finger if a body was careless, and another said that cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and a third said the cold had got into the rope and would not come out before the thaw. The words had mouths and the mouths had owners, and the boy gave the words no owner beyond the mouth that had them and the gap no intention. No slate was opened within reach of the basket, no figure was entered, and no money passed anywhere in the yard that afternoon. Costs came as directions and went away as weather; a direction is a thing a hand points and a body does not follow, and it buys nothing and sells nothing.

\scenebreak

The light went off the yard the way it goes off a clay floor, from the edges inward, and Sella came to the basket and carried him up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot and the fire and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the hour was and not told to be still. She said nothing to it of what the afternoon had been, and nothing of the wide step at the returning-line place or of the two unswept lines at the corner. Under the roof at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} she went to \place{the hearth} before the basket and told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone that went into the boy's ear ahead of the warmth. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps, for a figured gap wants a payer in it and none stood. Her finger went along the top of the loaf shelf so that the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count and wanted no answer. One glance went to the cold clay jar standing beyond \place{the pot board} under \lore{lead glaze}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air and went back to the pot.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure; the pot was mended and was not made whole, and the crack was left where a man could see it had been handled. At \place{the rack end} she drew the wool over the dressed grain with the flat of her palm, turning a corner of it for damp with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and gave it no figure and no handful. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}; the half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest, and she did not measure it and did not name it.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open; a Fourthday afternoon was not an opening and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its fourteenth. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn, with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread at his mouth had gone stiff and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. \lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put in the empty place, still a finding and not a placing, with no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle; all three wants were true and useless, the second was louder, and none of them bought another. Nothing under the roof had been mended by anything that had stood on its own place through the afternoon, and nothing had been made a lesson of it.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap. He did not add the findings, because a surgeon who adds before the last is in has already decided what he will find. Six hearings of the \lore{full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three --- the kiln's four-and-a-gap behind the wall, \place{the boards}' four-and-four-and-a-knot, and \lore{the carried fire}'s four low pulls with the fifth missing --- for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule.

The offered figure stood on its place beside its return. The second asking of the twelfth stood on a place of its own beside it, with a finger between; the face from \place{Slate Row} stood on another, and the reported second slate of the sixth, \person{Ilva}'s fear kept in wood, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate unread up the road, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, the hair-thin thaw of the seventh, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and never a sighting, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, the three likenesses of Slate Row, \lore{the burned slate} standing at five, and the four ownerless directions stood each on its own place with a finger between every pair. None was a reader, a witness, a tenth hand, a custom, a payment or a payer. None was made to answer another. A doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood; the hand that will one day lay charcoal and cord and knot-string and inward slate and strip of birch together and call them an account is far off and is not his.

Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up \place{the clay road}: four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer. The twelve of Y.H. 813 were not laid across the four to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and the key went up in a coat pocket that never came back down; \person{Berr Clay}'s box stayed locked, and the figure read was still not the figure paid, with no signature on the space. None of the four was made to answer any kept thing under this roof. The unsent call to \person{Nina} stood where it had been put and was not paid again. \place{High Kiln} and \place{Greybank} and \place{Sill} and \place{Grath} stood where they stood, and nothing came down any road into a day that had earned only its fourteenth.

He kept four hundred and thirty-eight exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-eight days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. The carried fire's fifth went unpaid and \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s name went unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-eight, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Answer four hundred and fifty-two was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine. Silt 20 stood four mornings off as a date and was not brought.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, the note coming from under the covering and not from the covering and ending on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold on answer four hundred and fifty-two as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under the boards the four low pulls kept their silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into the fifteenth not lived and not brought into this one.
