# Chapter 1 — The Stone Going Cold

## Frame

- **Day and date.** Day 1 only — Stone 7, Y.H. 812, a Seventhday. Deep winter: the Ilm under ice, three days before the Five Cold Days. The chapter lives one day from the inside of an infant's body; no jumps, no summary.
- **Places.** The birth room off the \place{Clay Yard} in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} — a two-room clay-daubed house with a trodden-ash floor and a grey ridge along the skirting board. The one breaking-through is the \place{expressway} near \place{Chicago}.
- **Point of view.** \person{Daniel Marchetti}, who is two hours old and called \person{Corin}. Close third-person past tense. Present tense *only* for the Chicago break in Scene 2, and only for the actual arrival of the memory, never for anticipation. \memory{...} only for remembered *words* — none are needed in this chapter; do not tag a line of the death scene with it.
- **Governing image.** A small river stone, warmed at somebody else's fire and wrapped for a newborn's belly, already giving up its heat. Keep it a physical object. Never call it a symbol, never explain who paid to warm it.
- **What changes.** The boy is born and is immediately inside an accounting he cannot read: a stone is cooling against him, a name has been entered on a slate before anyone has seen his face, and a woman's hand has hovered over the bundle without taking it. He learns, without words, that this household arranges its silences around things. Power does not change — he is cracked and unranked — but the clinical ear is established and the first habit of attention begins.

## Scene 1 — The Stone Going Cold (drop capital)

**Day and time.** Day 1, just after the birth, in the small hours before dawn; the room is lit by one banked fire and a tallow dip.

**Place.** The birth room off the Clay Yard in Low Kiln — trodden-ash floor, a grey ridge of Ash along the skirting board, a slate within reach of the midwife's hand.

**Present.** \person{Corin} (Dan, silent), \person{Alda Reed}, \person{Dessa Silt}. \person{Mira Silt} is in and out (establish her presence here; she has the fuller idiolect in Scene 3).

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the stone: warm when Alda put it against him, warm no longer. Establish that it was warmed at a neighbour's fire — a favour, something owed and not entered.
2. Alda's hands: steady, clay under the scrubbed nails, addressing the stone, the slip, the child, then the mother — never the father, who is absent. Her practical instruction: *Warm the stone first. A cold stone is a stone, not a kindness.* She reaches for the slate beside her before she reaches for the child.
3. Dessa in labour's aftermath, counting nineteen steps under her breath — the count she has walked from the slip pit to the drying rack for six years, now counted for a different reason. She rubs the pale sieve-wire scar on her left forearm.
4. Dan's first perceptions are surgical and useless: the angle of Dessa's wrist, the rhythm of her breathing, the exact point at which an afterdrop would begin if this were a rewarming patient. He notes the room's exits without deciding to. He knows the stone's rate of cooling the way he once knew a patient's core temp.
5. The argument of registers: the adult's dry, exact inventory against the infant's immediate, impatient, cold, hungry body. *He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the breast. Both were true and the second was louder.* The body humiliates him — he drools, cannot hold up his head, cannot turn over. Play it for exactness, with the dry joke living in it, never for easy pathos.
6. He cannot speak. He cannot reach a cup. A trauma surgeon who cannot reach a cup.
7. The stone gives up the last of its heat against his belly and nobody in the room feels it but him.

**Emotional turn.** From the cold competence of a clinician reading a body to the humiliating, loud victory of an infant's hunger — and the first small grief that the competence has no power behind it.

**Sensory anchors.** Wet wool, tallow, clay dust, the sour mineral edge of the Ash ridge; the fading warmth of the stone; a low singing flattened by work; the rasp of Dessa's breathing.

**Threads advanced.** T006 (stone), T013 (Ash ridge), T061 (ear, as habit), T071 (counting exits/steps), T072 (attention), T044 (the debt of warmth, unspoken).

**Foreshadowing.** The stone was warmed at somebody's fire — the first payer. The Ash ridge nobody will sweep. The slate reached for before the child. The nineteen steps. Do not point at any of them.

**Exact last image.** The stone under his belly giving up its last heat, the room warm around it and no one the wiser.

## Scene 2 — The Birth-Flower

**Day and time.** Day 1, moments later, still before dawn.

**Place.** The birth room; the Chicago break has no place in Sarn and is written as an intrusion, not a location change.

**Present.** Alda, Dessa, Corin; Mira still in the edges of the room.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Alda lifts the child to clean him and sees the branching red mark across his ribs and down the left forearm — frost-flower, burn map, fire writing itself into the trace.
2. She crosses herself with two fingers at the corner of the mark, notices she has done it in front of a stranger, and covers the gesture with the plain word *birth-flower*. Do not let her explain the sign. When she lies or hides a thing she becomes unusually exact — give her a date or a detail nobody asked for.
3. The seam opens: the tense tightens into the present. Orange tunnels of sodium light on wet snow; the wipers have stopped; the hiss of fuel; the weight of a vibrating phone in a coat pocket with a name on the lit screen he will not look at; the cold leaving his hands; the wordless, complete sentence *I have left the job unfinished.*
4. The grey-coated woman at the edge of the flare light, a slate in her hand, writing; the writing does not stop when the fire takes him. Render the woman only as shape, coat the colour of wet ash, hand moving. No name, no trade, no explanation of what she wrote.
5. Hold the phone-light and the writing together for a breath — the name he will not look at and the name somebody else is writing — then fall back into the clay room. Past tense returns. Do not comment on the shift.

**Emotional turn.** The ordinary wonder of a birth-flower curdles into the unhealed death the infant carries whole inside him; the reader learns the child has a past before the household has noticed he has a face.

**Sensory anchors.** Machine-clean hospital smell and bitter coffee against tallow and clay; orange on snow; the cheap flare's chemical smoke; the small warm weight of a newborn against the sheet.

**Threads advanced.** T001 (birth-flower), T002 (grey coat), T004 (lit phone / unsent call), T020 (hot trace), T007 (Alda's crossed sign), T049 (the receipt, as light), T044 (the boat promise, present only as the weight of an unsent sentence).

**Foreshadowing.** The name written before the face (payoff T003, far off); the woman who writes without stopping (payoff T002, ~Ch 35); the light he refuses to look at (payoff T004, very far off). The reader may think the woman caused the fire. She did not.

**Exact last image.** The writing that does not stop — then Alda's two fingers coming down, and the word *birth-flower* settling over the room like a lid.

## Scene 3 — Counting

**Day and time.** Day 1, later in the same dark, as the room settles after the birth.

**Place.** The birth room; Corin on his back where they have laid him.

**Present.** Corin, Alda, Dessa; \person{Mira Silt} fully on stage now.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Corin on his back counts the nails in the floorboards because there is nothing else to count: four on the near board, four on the next, and a fifth that is not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb. He does not yet know he is counting exits.
2. He reads the room the way he once read a trauma bay: Dessa's pulse at the wrist, the colour under her nails, the scar she rubs when deciding whether to tell the truth. Competence with no business being there.
3. Dessa's nineteen steps run under the scene — counted now for the child, not the pits. She says the difficult thing in proverb form so it can be taken as advice: *A body can be warm and still be gone. You learn that in the pits.* She calls him *the little hot one*, never a name.
4. Mira comes in with eel slime dried on her sleeves and eel-bones in her pocket; she flicks a bone against her thumb while deciding whether to lie. She answers a question with a question and then her own answer, starts in the middle of things, will not sit where a slate is open. She stays standing.
5. The argument becomes physical: the surgeon's dry register wants to assess, the infant's body wants the breast; the head lolls, a blanket will not stay on, a sound comes out that is not a word. The body wins, loudly. The dry joke is allowed here — he has reduced a thousand shoulders and cannot keep a blanket on — but it must cost something.
6. Mira says almost nothing that matters. Under it, the sense that this woman knows more than she will say and has arranged her face to keep it (T058's pattern seeded in the aunt before it is named in the keeper).

**Emotional turn.** Amusement at a humiliating body sours into the suspicion that the adults' silences are arranged, and that he is the subject of them.

**Sensory anchors.** Cold boards under a small back; the flat grey of Ash in the corner; river silt and salted perch off Mira's sleeves; the tick of an eel-bone against a thumb; the dip guttering.

**Threads advanced.** T071 (counting), T072 (attention), T013 (Ash), T008 (Dessa's lie, seeded in the counting), T058 (silence as a wall), T060 (brisk kindness).

**Foreshadowing.** Mira's knot-string habit is not yet shown — keep it to the flick of the eel-bones and the refusal to sit at a slate. Dessa's cough is weather here, not a diagnosis.

**Exact last image.** The knot the width of a thumb where the fifth nail should be.

## Scene 4 — The Slate Within Reach

**Day and time.** Day 1, toward morning; the dip burning lower.

**Place.** The birth room.

**Present.** Corin, Alda, Dessa, Mira.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The birth-slate lies within reach of Alda's hand. On it a name has been entered in a neat, strange hand — written before anyone looked at the child's face. Dan cannot read Grathic; he registers the shape of the writing the way he once registered a chart he was not supposed to have read.
2. Dessa will not say the name. She has called him the little hot one and the flower so long that the habit is a way of not saying what she means. When she is frightened she gets specific — steps, dates, loaves — and the specifics are a handle on a thing too large.
3. Mira will not sit where the slate is open. She says almost nothing; when she gives the child's mother a crust of brown-edged barley bread she does it quickly and briskly, before anyone can make a speech about it. The crust came out of somebody's meal — let the reader be able to name whose.
4. Alda becomes exact about dates nobody asked for, the small exactness of a woman choosing what to write down and what to leave in a second record she has not yet shown. She answers the room's unasked question with the weather — which in Low Kiln is never the subject.
5. The boy is already entered somewhere he has not been seen. The room has arranged itself around this without anyone saying it. He notices the arrangement with the part of him that was a surgeon and cannot say a word about it.
6. The stone goes fully cold. The Ash ridge along the skirting board is there, and nobody will sweep it on the Last Day three days off. Somebody will come on the third day.

**Emotional turn.** From the domestic warmth of bread and low voices to the cold certainty that a record has been made of him by people who have not asked him anything — and that mercy here is a form of entry.

**Sensory anchors.** Slate dust and neat ink; brown-edged barley bread; the guttering dip; cold ash; the crust's warmth against a palm.

**Threads advanced.** T003 (name before face), T005 (whole return, seeded in the neat hand), T008 (the lie to come, in Dessa's specificity), T013 (the Ash), T060 (brisk kindness), T058 (arranged silence), T072 (attention).

**Foreshadowing.** The number forty is never spoken. *Collector* is never attached to the neat hand. The third day is named as a coming, not an event.

**Exact last image.** The cold stone beside the neat hand on the slate — warmth spent, ink dry.

## Scene 5 — The Hand at the Door

**Day and time.** Day 1, morning breaking over the Clay Yard; the ice on the Ilm holding.

**Place.** The threshold of the birth room; the kiln breathing somewhere off the yard.

**Present.** Corin, Alda, Dessa, Mira; \person{Sella Vail} enters as a hand and a presence, not yet a speaking part.

**Beats, in order.**
1. A woman's hand reaches for the covered bundle — the back of it carrying a burn shaped like a little door, which she keeps closed when she carries a cup. It is Sella's. The hand does not take the bundle yet.
2. The pause is the whole scene: a household deciding without words whether to keep a foundling with no house-name, and what keeping him will cost. No one meets anyone's eye. Sella says nothing.
3. The hand withdraws. The slate is closed. Somewhere off the yard a kiln draws breath and lets it out — the first sound of the Vail fire in the book, heard before the house itself appears.
4. Corin counts the kiln's breaths because there is nothing else to count — four, four, and the knot the width of a thumb — and the counting is the only thing in the room that belongs to nobody but him.
5. Close on the cold stone at his belly and the counting, with the third day still ahead. Forward momentum: a hand has hovered and a name has been written, and both will come back for him.

**Emotional turn.** The scene ends on a held breath — not resolution but the first tightening of the household's knot around him. He has been seen and not claimed; the not-claiming is itself a decision.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and wet wool on the hand that reaches; the little door of a burn; the kiln's slow breath; pale morning on clay dust; the cold stone.

**Threads advanced.** T058 (Sella's silence — the first wall, planted), T057 (the second roll, seeded only as a household that keeps its own record), T060 (love as maintenance — the hand that warms and does not yet take), T006 (the stone going cold), T071/T072 (counting and attention, the private record).

**Foreshadowing.** The hand's pause is not explained. The reader is not told why Sella will take him on the third day, nor that she knew his mother. The kiln's breath is introduced before the kiln.

**Exact last image.** The cold stone at his belly and the four-and-four-and-a-knot of the floorboards, with the third day still to come.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 2

The chapter closes on a held breath: Sella's hand has reached and withdrawn, the slate is shut with a name Dan cannot read, the stone is cold, and the third day has not arrived. Chapter 2 ("He's Not Vail," Days 2–3) opens inside the Vail kiln-house whose breath he has just counted, with Garl known only by objects, Jorra at the wheel saying *he's not Vail*, and Sella at last carrying the covered bundle up the Clay Yard. The count he kept in the birth room — four, four, a knot — is the same habit he will use to read that house's hollow wheel-seat and salt-stained coat, so the movement from chapter one to chapter two is from a hand that hovered to a house that takes him in and calls the taking kindness.
