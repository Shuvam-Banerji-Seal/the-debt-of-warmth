The afternoon came down under the Vail roof without a hand to call it, the way a second cloth comes down over a first --- not lifting it, not shaking it, only lying where it is laid. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. She carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at the turn of the landing before the wicker settled, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. A basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. In \place{the Clay Yard}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and went back to the small honest work.

The small sounds came first, and he took them first because they did not lie. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock that had no note in it and wanted none. The rope at \place{the yard well} settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it, going quiet when the weight was taken; the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist and eye and mouth and the half-beat in the gap. The fist was the part that arrived; the eye was the part that checked; the mouth was the part that said or held its peace. Between them stood the half-beat, a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

Into this went a word, and no body stood under it.

It came at the well from mouth to mouth among the bucket-round. A woman at the rail, with her eyes on her pail and her hand already going back to the rope, said low into the next woman's sleeve that the woman who carries the slate had taken up a second. The next said it to the next while the cold-stiffened rope complained on its knot, and the third added the part about the writing, and the fourth had nothing to add and passed it on anyway. \emph{She's carrying a second}, one said. \emph{A narrower one}, said another. \emph{Edge under her arm, the writing turned the same way in}. Nobody asked whose writing. Nobody said whose slate. The word went around the well the way a coal goes from hand to hand in a dark room --- passed on because the hand that had it wanted to be rid of it, not because any of them had seen it warm. When it came back to the woman who had first said it, she was drawing water and did not say it again.

No speaker stood under the word. No name was spoken over either slate. \person{Berr Clay} was up \place{the clay road} with the box locked and the key gone up in a coat pocket that did not come back down, and his slate was in the keeping of a woman who was not here to be asked; the second slate belonged to the same mouth's saying and to no body in the yard. One of the mouths gave the order of it, and gave it as though somebody had stood near enough to see: her hand went to the slates before her eye went to the yard, and her mouth went after both. Between hand and eye stood the half-beat, and in the telling it stood as a place and not a person, the interval where a lesser observer would have put an intention. There was no intention in it. An intention wants a hand under it, and the only hands at the well were busy with pails.

No room was entered in the telling. No threshold was crossed. No slate was opened within reach of the basket, and no figure came off either slate, narrow or wide. The woman who carried the second thing was not made a reader of it by a word at a well; she was a shape in a mouth and no witness stood under the shape. A report is not a sighting. A saying that has gone from mouth to mouth is a saying that has been carried, and carrying is not reading, and nobody in the yard had done the one thing that would have made the second slate an account.

He had worked in a building where a thing said in one room arrived three doors down with the first speaker gone clean out of it, and the chart at the end of the corridor carried no line for it, because a chart is a vessel for what somebody has put into it and an unentered thing has nowhere to stand. He had learned to read such reports by the order of the mouths and not by their heat. This one came in the order hand, eye, mouth, with the half-beat standing between hand and eye, and he took it apart the way he took apart a wound --- by what lay where, not by any meaning it offered.

He filed the second slate on a place of its own.

It stood near, but did not touch, the eighth record-first hand that had gone up the road with its owner and her slate unread; near, but not touching, the offered figure that had come out of a shut fist on Ilmen 26, looked at the light and gone back into its sleeve, arriving nowhere, and its return; near, but not touching, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} as a report and never as a sighting; near, but not touching, the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}. A finger stood between every pair, and the finger was his own attention and nothing that could be entered. It was not a reader made, not a ninth hand, not a tenth, not a witness, not a sale, not a sixth shaping, not a custom and not the calling of the loan. None of those things stood under it, and he did not supply them. Two slates under one arm are two slates under one arm, and the road carries and reads neither.

He did not lay the reported thing across the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road behind one unsigned figure, three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid; nor across the printed one in twelve; nor across the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}; nor across the neighbour's near-reaching at the loaf shelf from Silt 3, which had stopped a finger's width short and stopped there; nor across the two market sightings of Ilmen 30, which stayed on separate trays with no eighth of either. Each kept its own place with a finger between. A joining wants a hand to make it, and the hand that will one day lay the district's private keepings together is far off, and is not his. That was the whole of the turn: the runner's reaching come nearer to a joining than it had ever come, and the refusal of it in the same breath. What the road carried was filed as a shape. It did not become an account because the only person in the yard who could have made one lay outside the wool with a hand that could not close two inches on the rim of a basket. It was not made a cause, not made a rule, not made a payment, and it was not told a second time on the strength of a first. A seventh day would have to earn its own telling, and no seventh day had yet stood under this roof.

The afternoon kept its grammar around the filed shape. One wide circuit went around \place{the seven walked-around places}, once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number; widest around \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back out. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The step was a keeping and not a sign. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf's stopping and not laid across the ridge at \place{the Vail corner}. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated; level is not honest and a level is not a promise.

At the corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up where no broom had gone in two years. That finer grey was called \lore{the thaw}. Beside it the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between; a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. He did not look twice. Through \place{the finger's-width door} the air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge, where the low note of \lore{water over water} ran nearer under the winter skin and the tang came thinner. He found it where put, called it weather, and did not make a sign of it. Costs arrived as directions and went away as weather and left no entry: a man waiting his turn opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve, the number arriving nowhere; a woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price; the figure read over the covered grain did not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between figure and price carried no signature.

At \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones} \person{Tarn} kept six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. He said \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once, and did not say it again; a second would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood. The sentence about an ear under this roof came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist gave back what little light there was. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel and spoke to the wheel before any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. He knew the \lore{full note} a pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite held and one took, and he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed. The two quiet men kept their places under one roof and the boy found both where put with a finger between, their silences maintenance and not a compact.

\person{Ilva} was offstage with her strip of notched birch bark in her sleeve, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between; it was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined, and no figure came off the wood, her hand ninth with no tenth. \person{Hessa Rale} was up the road with her narrow slate unread and her eighth hand eighth and joined to none. Berr Clay was gone up the clay road toward High Kiln with the box locked and his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered each on its own place with a finger between, believed and not owed. The soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate and entered figure stayed a report at the landing, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. None of these was told again. None was made a witness, a reader, a custom or a rule on a day that had earned only its sixth. There was no market, no \place{Cross}, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner. Walk-ons came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, and none looked into the wicker or gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights; a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer.

The going of the light came without anyone calling it. Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order. \emph{It's the thaw}, she told the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that has done this work before and expects to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter, and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Sixthday that had earned only its sixth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, left the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, and gave both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. Garl Vail's laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain, drew the wool back with the flat of her hand, and gave no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth, and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. \lore{The keeping at the fold} in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and thirty days; nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap: the pail's flat knock on the stone, the rope's cold-stiffened rasp, barley wall to wall without a number, the loaf stopped a finger's width; the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest at the returning-line place; the trough's round print on its own place; the two unswept lines at the corner with a finger between; the softening at the river's skin found where put and called weather; the fist arriving nowhere, the note that was not a price, the figure that did not know what the covered grain cost with the ink-neat space unsigned. And beside them, on its own place with a finger between it and every kept thing, the word that had gone around the well: two slates under one arm, the writing turned to the sleeve, hand before eye and mouth after both, the half-beat standing in the gap where no payer stood.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, the thread a small dry nothing, a hand on the pot handle a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. All three were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a verdict, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He finished on his plate what was put before him and could not reach the plate.

Answer four hundred and thirty-six came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer; it was laid across neither the reported second slate nor the eighth hand, the offered figure and its return, the clerk's entered figure, the three likenesses, the twelve arithmetics, the three readings, the fifth shaping, the neighbour's near-reaching, the two sightings, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. It was the fourth of six that this chapter would hold, two to each of its days, and not one a doctrine.

Four hundred and thirty exact, six months and two hundred and fifty days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth, and a number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer where none stood. Silt 10 waited four mornings off as a date and was not brought into this day. Nothing crossed under the roof from the yard's report or the river's weather as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

The hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat against the fold on answer four hundred and thirty-six, as a man puts away a tool where a next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. Forward, into a seventh day not yet lived and not brought into this one.
