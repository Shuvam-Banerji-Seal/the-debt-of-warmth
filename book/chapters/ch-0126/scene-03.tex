\daymark{430}{Silt 6, Y.H. 814, Sixthday}

The small hours of the sixth day came down through the house the way cold comes down a stairwell --- not in a fall but in a settling, gathering first in the corners, lying longest where nothing stirred it, finding the thin places along the boards before it found the middle of the room. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid on the last answer, flat and patient as a tool put away where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and twenty-nine exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-nine days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on under the dark the way a banked fire goes on under ash, with nobody turning it and nobody supplying what it lacked. This was a sixth morning, and a sixth is only a sixth. Nothing had crossed into it from the fifth day behind --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The yard had said what the yard said and the weather had been weather, and both had gone where they went without leaving an instruction under this roof.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the dark thinned by no degree anybody measured. Under \place{the river} the low note of \lore{water over water} went on beneath its winter skin, softer at one edge where the note ran nearer, and nobody in the room gave the softness a name. He had found it where it lay on the day behind and had called it weather, and calling it weather had cost him nothing and bought him nothing, and he did not call it anything again this morning. A finding repeated is still a finding. A name given twice wants a hand that named twice, and a rule wants a third, and both want a payer standing under them where none stood.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would once have tried to eat it and would not try now, and the not-trying was not a lesson in anything. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them. He wanted the crust lifted two inches and put where a mouth could reach it. He wanted the thread wiped from his lip by a thumb that would know to do it. He wanted a hand on the pot handle, where the warmth lived. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. All three were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on in the dark --- the old man's exactness with its instrument laid down, the small animal body with its whole unspeakable impatience, and no verdict between them, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He could not speak a word of \lore{Grathic} and had no way to ask for any of the three; the wanting was therefore complete and therefore useless, and that was the whole of the work.

There had been a room in the other life where a man lay under a sheet and the chart at the foot of the bed said one thing and the man's fingers said another, and the surgeon had read the chart by what it left out rather than by what it set down, because a neat hand can enter a thing neatly and still not have looked at it. He had learned to read a house the same way --- not by what it would not survive, which was the reading any fool could make of a cold room and a banked fire, but by what it would not say. This house would not say the man's name. This house would not say what the fifth pull under the floor was owed to. It would not say why a fold lay in an empty place or where the hand went that had put it there. The refusals stood in the room like objects, exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he filed them where they lay without putting a cause across any of them. That room was in another country and another time and none of it came under this roof; the surgeon's register went on taking the body in its order and wrote down nothing a Court could read.

\scenebreak

First grey came without a bell. It came the way light comes into a room whose shutter has been set against a cold wind --- by degrees nobody marked, settling on the backs of chairs before it reached the floor, showing the edges of things before it showed their faces. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.

\emph{It's the thaw}, she told the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her. She turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them with the small dry sound of a tool that has done this work before and expects to do it again, and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie, because a sponge is a sponge and a needle is a needle and neither of them has a reason to flatter you. Here a woman counted spoons and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Sixthday that had earned only its sixth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this morning. There was a sixth in the counting of the kindling and a sixth in the answer last filed and a sixth in the day, and the three sixes stood on three places with a finger between every pair; he did not lay them across one another to make a row come out even, for a row that comes out even is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, because a line that looks joined can still be a line that will not hold; and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof. \person{Garl Vail}'s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A fist of it was taken up from the board, closed, turned once in the palm to feel its weight, judged by the hand that carried it and set back where it had lain; no figure went over that either. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. The pot stood where she set it, and the warmth in it went the way warmth goes in a cold room --- out, slowly, by the whole of its surface, and faster where the metal met the air.

\scenebreak

She carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; a basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. In \place{the Clay Yard} she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and the yard went on about its small honest work. At \place{the yard well} a rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken; the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. The basket was not told what the sound was and was not told to be still.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and did not turn his head toward the landing. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away the whole of the checking. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. The sentence was found where put and was not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not a hearing and not a rule.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the \emph{Seed 16} breath stayed unspent, the loan uncalled in a term already ended, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. His chest had been tight since the dark. He cut nothing, counted nothing beyond the six, spent nothing; and the boy, who had twice seen a hand withdraw from a field before the dressing went on, found the withdrawal where put on a place of its own and did not make a custom of it on a morning that had earned only its sixth.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold, and the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and he took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time. There was no Grathic word for the two bites of one piece of iron, and he gave the house neither. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, gathering where it lay against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions without being called or paid. The two quiet men kept their places under one roof a third time, and the boy found both where put with a finger between, their silences maintenance and not a compact, no rule stood up out of what he had seen and no joining made that was not his to make.

\scenebreak

The morning kept its grammar and asked nothing for it. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. Women came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Sixthday grammar of hand before eye and mouth after both; none looked into the wicker, and none gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights, for a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. There was no market, no \place{Cross}, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its sixth. The softening at the river's skin stayed where put and was called weather; the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf stayed on its own place from the day behind and was not told again or made a custom; the two sightings stayed on separate trays with no eighth of either. Nothing from the fifth crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

Beside the work, on places of their own with a finger between every pair, stood the things already found: the offered figure and its return, not offered again; the asking at the well answered with the weather; the second man's turned slate and the direction he went; the first man's face; the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} as a report and never as a sighting; the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}; the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}. \person{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood, her strip in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road}, her eighth hand eighth and joined to none. \person{Berr Clay} was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed. None of these was told again. None was made a witness, a reader, a sixth shaping, a custom or a rule. None was joined to another on a day that had earned only its sixth, for a joining wants a hand to make it and the only hand that would have made it lay outside the wool and could not close two inches on the rim of the basket.

One wide circuit went around \place{the seven walked-around places}, once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number; widest around \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back out. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf's stopping and not laid across the ridge at \place{the Vail corner}. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated; level is not honest, and a level is not a promise. At the corner the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years, and the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between; a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. He found both where put and did not look twice, and went back to the small sounds because the small sounds did not lie.

Answer four hundred and thirty-five came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road behind one unsigned figure, the printed one in twelve, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the two market sightings, the neighbour's near-reaching, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. It was the third of six that this chapter would hold, two to each of its days, and not one a doctrine.

Four hundred and thirty exact, six months and two hundred and fifty days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; his name had not crossed a threshold under this roof in four hundred and thirty days and no account had gone up the road to call his debt, and neither the count of his absence nor the count of the missing pull had been laid across the other. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth, and a number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer where none stood. Silt 10 waited four mornings off as a date and was not brought into this morning.

Sella carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Under \place{the boards} the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place. In the small hours \place{the towel} would come out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and the file would turn out exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side, both true, the second louder, neither winning, the quarrel the work. He finished on his plate what was put before him and could not reach the plate. The hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and thirty-five, as a man puts away a tool where a next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. Forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.
