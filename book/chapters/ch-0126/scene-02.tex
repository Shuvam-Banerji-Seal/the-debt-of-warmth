The afternoon came on without a bell and without a hand to name it, settling into the kiln-house the way a second cloth settles over a first --- not lifting it, not shaking it, only lying where it was laid. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. She carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at the turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker, and the instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. A basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. She set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and went back to the small honest work of the yard.

In \place{the Clay Yard} the small sounds came first, and he took them first because they did not lie. The large ones were mostly weather: the wind off \place{the river} going under the winter skin, the timber of the sheds complaining as the dry cold bit into it, a man's voice lifted and lost and lifted again. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock that had no note in it and wanted none. The rope at \place{the yard well} settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining while the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken, and the knot held what the knot was given and asked for nothing back. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A fist of it was weighed in a hand, judged by the hand that carried it and set back where it had lain, and no figure went over it. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure.

He took each apart by fist and eye and mouth and the half-beat in the gap. The fist was the part that arrived. The eye was the part that checked. The mouth was the part that said or held its peace. Between them stood the half-beat, and the half-beat was a place and not a person, an interval with no intention in it, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. A man might have called the gap a hesitation and the hesitation a meaning; the surgeon had seen too many gaps in too many rooms to put a meaning into one simply because it had the shape of a pause. He gave it no intention, and it lay where it lay, exact and complete and worth nothing.

One wide circuit went around \place{the seven walked-around places}, once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it. The space beneath the lowest rack kept its broken birth-slates entered by nobody. The gone pan kept its shallow round ring. The trough kept its water. The field's empty place lay out on the worked strips with nobody standing in it. And the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out, was gone around widest of all. No name went with the wide step, no body, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The step was a keeping and not a sign. He had learned, in a country that had no word for what he had lost, to walk around an empty place without putting a name in it; the wide step was the whole of what could be done and was therefore done exactly.

At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, not laid across the loaf's stopping and not laid across the ridge at the corner. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated. Level is not honest. A level is not a promise. He had read a great many straight lines in his life and knew that a straight line drawn in a vessel says only where the water came to rest, and says nothing at all about the hand that set the vessel down or the cold that made the water hold still.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. Beside it the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place, and a finger's width stood between the two greys. A thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off the river was less cold at one edge, where the low note of \lore{water over water} ran nearer under the winter skin and the tang came thinner; he found it where put, called it weather, and did not look twice. It was not a fourth covering, not a sign, not a payment and not a custom. Nobody swept it, nobody owned the day, and no hand stood under it to make it mean anything.

Costs arrived as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at the well opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The figure read over the covered grain did not know what the grain had cost, and the ink-neat space between the figure and the price carried no signature. He did not write a cause across any of them. A direction is a direction, and weather is weather, and a space with no name in it stays a space.

The women came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Fifthday grammar of hand before eye and mouth after both. None looked into the wicker. None gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights, for a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer. There was no market, no \place{Cross}, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its fifth. Silt 10 waited three mornings off as a date and was not brought into this afternoon.

Beside the work, on places of their own with a finger between every pair, stood the things already found: the offered figure and its return, not offered again and not made a custom; the asking at the well answered with the weather; the second man's turned slate and the direction he went; the first man's face; the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} as a report and never as a sighting; the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}; the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}; the neighbour's near-reaching at the loaf shelf from Silt 3, stopping a finger's width short and stopping there; the two market sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either. None was told again. None was made a witness, a reader, a sixth shaping or a rule. None was joined to another on a day that had earned only its fifth, for a joining wants a hand to make it and the only hand that would have made it lay outside the wool and could not close two inches on the rim of the basket.

\person{Ilva} was offstage with her strip in her sleeve and her ninth hand nine, the notches unread and no figure coming off the wood. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road}, her eighth hand eighth and joined to none. \person{Berr Clay} was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the third subtraction inside, the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down; his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness kept their own places with a finger between, believed and not owed. The soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate and entered figure stayed a report at the landing, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. Nothing from the days behind crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and no slate had been opened within reach of the basket.

The going of the light came without anyone calling it. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. \emph{It's the thaw}, she told the coals, in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; no hand went beneath, no mark was copied, no row was numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

\person{Garl Vail}'s laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, left the hairline showing between the older brass staple and the wire, and gave both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Fifthday that had earned only its fifth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain, drew the wool back with the flat of her hand, and gave no figure and no handful. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth, and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, gone into old horn in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and twenty-nine days; nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river under the door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap: the pail's flat knock on the stone, the rope's cold-stiffened rasp, barley wall to wall without a number, the loaf stopped a finger's width with nobody calling the stopping a figure; the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest at the returning-line place; the trough's round print on its own place; the two unswept lines at the corner with a finger between; the softening at the river's skin found where put and called weather; the fist arriving nowhere, the note that was not a price, the figure that did not know what the covered grain cost with the ink-neat space unsigned. Beside each, on places of their own with a finger between, stood the offered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate and its direction, the clerk's entered figure, the fifth shaping, the three likenesses, the neighbour's near-reaching and the two sightings on separate trays. No eighth stood up anywhere. Six hearings stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and the Drawing's flat knock outside; nine record-first hands and nine stoppings stood with no tenth; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule; three readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure, three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid.

The infant wanted three things and would not be argued out of any of them, and the wanting went on in the dark the same as it had gone on in the morning. The surgeon knew the crust was old horn and would not nourish, that the thread was a small dry nothing, that a hand on a pot handle was a hand arriving at two inches of air and stopping. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a verdict, because a verdict wants a hand to enter it and none stood. He finished on his plate what was put before him and could not reach the plate.

Answer four hundred and thirty-four came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road behind one unsigned figure, the printed one in twelve, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the two market sightings, the neighbour's near-reaching, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. It was the second of six that this chapter would hold, two to each of its days, and not one a doctrine.

Four hundred and twenty-nine exact, six months and two hundred and forty-nine days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth was still unpaid and Garl Vail's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. Silt 10 waited three mornings off as a date and was not brought. Nothing crossed under the roof from the yard's directions or the river's weather as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

The hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat against the fold on answer four hundred and thirty-four, as a man puts away a tool where a next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. Forward, into a morning not yet lived and not brought into this day.
