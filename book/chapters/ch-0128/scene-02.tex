The afternoon of the eleventh came on without anybody announcing it. The light thinned from white to the colour of tallow long kept in a warm room, and the shadows came out from under the racks and pots and lay across \place{the flagstones} as though they had been waiting there since morning. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down \place{the nineteen steps} again at the half-pace from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the fire and never to the basket, and set him where the heat came to him sideways. The yard went on at the work of an afternoon. Nobody looked up to see how the light had changed, and nobody said to the wicker what the hour was or told it to be still.

The low angle of the basket gave him the yard in pieces: the underside of a board, the shadow of a pail, the hem of a skirt going past and the sound of the going before the skirt itself. He had learned to read a room from a low angle in the other life, from the floor beside a gurney where the sheeting hid everything but the hands; a face can lie for a whole shift and the hand at the edge of the field cannot. Here the hands were at the well and the racks and the kindling and the faces were turned away, so he took the hands first. \person{Tarn} was off at the kindling with his keepings found where put, and \person{Jorra Vail} at the wheel-shed edge with his dust white in a fist-crease that was not opened twice; neither came under the basket's small sky and neither was brought. A man under this roof was kept in objects and in the grammar of a house — a salt-stiff coat on its peg, a hollow wheel-seat nobody was allowed to fill, a pot on the board with its hairline showing — and not in a story told to a child, and the telling of it bought nothing.

He took the small sounds first because they do not lie. A pail came down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock, the stone and the wood and the hand that set it down, and no fourth thing standing behind to be asked. The rope at \place{the yard well} settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp it had given all winter, a dry sound with nothing warm in it. Barley lay wall to wall along the rack without a number on it. A loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, with nobody calling the stopping a figure. He took each apart by the place of each part: the fist that came down, the eye that followed, the mouth that did not open, the half-beat lying in the gap between the setting-down and the stopping. The gap was a gap. He gave it no intention, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. A surgeon who writes the name of a cause over a gap has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. The pail's knock was one sound and then no sound; it did not go on speaking after the hand left it. The rope's rasp ended with the settling of the knot. The loaf stopped a finger's width from where a thumb had put it and stayed stopped until another hand moved it. He had worked in a room where a nurse said a number twice and the chart carried it once, and he had learned to ask which of the two was the finding and which the telling. Here there was no chart and no nurse; there was a hand, a knock, an eye that went to the pail and a mouth that stayed shut, and the half-beat between, and he let the half-beat stand as a half-beat and wrote nothing over it.

That was one room and one set of hands, and it paid nothing here. The boy's hand outside the wool lay where the fold had put it and could not close two inches of air. The crust stood at his ear with its corner a hair off the wool, gone into old horn in the dry afternoons; \lore{the keeping at the fold} lay in the empty place, found where put and still a finding; the thread at his mouth was stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. He had the body in the old order without having to ask for it, and the three wants came up in the old quarrel without a verdict: the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. The surgeon read the house by what it would not say and not by what it would not survive, and the reading bought nothing. Both entries were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the file went on with the case open.

He went once around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice. There was the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} and the finer grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up where no broom had gone in two years. There was the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody. There was the shallow round print the gone clay pan had left in \place{the trough}, kept on a place of its own. There was the trough itself, its water standing level in a vessel nobody had calibrated, and level is not honest and level is not a promise. There was the field's empty place out beyond the yard. And there was \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back, no name, no body, no figure for what the month had cost, no accident written across the space, no payer. His look went widest there, and wide is not a sign and wide is not a figure. The strips beyond the yard had gone in with a count kept in glances and armfuls and had come back one hand short of a count nobody had spoken, and the yard had gone around the place in the returning line with a look and a wide step on the day it happened and on every day after, and the wide step had cost nothing and bought no figure. He held a finger between that place and the trough's round print, and another between the print and the two unswept lines, and did not lay any of them together.

The two lines at the corner kept their distance: the clean dark line and its finer grey, unswept, called weather, thickened again in the dry cold with the sour Ash coming up off it. He had found the grey a hair less distinct along one stretch on the seventh and had called it weather then, and he called it weather now, found where put and not made again. Nobody swept. Nobody owned the day or the grey or the space under the rack, and nobody looked twice. A thing left where it lies is still kept; that was a fact under this roof and not an argument, and no hand under it had become a claim.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather, and left no entry. A man waiting his turn opened a shut fist up out of his sleeve toward the light, looked at what was in it, shut the fist and put it back where it came from; the number arrived nowhere, and the notched stick kept its notch and learned no name. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost came back with a note folded twice against her palm and did not open it where a basket could see; the pot on the board kept its hairline showing and said nothing about what it had cost, and the fire said nothing either. A question about a price went to the pot and the weather and came back with neither, and the yard went on with its work as though no question had been asked, which is the way a yard answers when it has no price to give and no wish to invent one. The arithmetic read at home over grain covered under wool did not know what the grain cost, and the ink-neat space between the figure and its price carried no signature. No slate was opened within reach of the wicker. No figure was entered. No money passed at any point in the yard, from the first knock of the pail to the going of the light. The boy kept the directions on their own places with a finger between every pair and added no row and no total, for a total is a thing that wants a reader standing behind it, and none stood.

\scenebreak

The light went at the going of the light the way a pot is taken from a fire: all at once and yet not hurried, the heat left behind in the place it had occupied. Sella came and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and said nothing to the basket of what the day had been. She set him under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} where the sideways heat found him, and went to \place{the hearth} before the basket.

The whole grammar came back at the end of the day as though nothing had turned. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbers and came back to the hairline at the wire. The one grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She told the coals it was the thaw and did not tell it to the basket, turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone, and gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath and no mark was copied. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}, the hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain was turned under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, lifted for damp, laid back where it lay, covered with the flat of a hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. An afternoon, a pail, a well, a basket and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and an eleventh tending day was not made an eighth by the figure that had gone up \place{the clay road} on the tenth. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping was found where put and stayed a finding.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap and did not add them; a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up his answer.

Six hearings of the \lore{full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, four, four and a gap, and three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth. The order of reaching was the same in each: hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both, the half-beat in the gap. He had watched it in a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before the crowd, in a clerk with hand to slate before eyes to stalls, and under this roof in Sella going to the hearth before the basket and in Tarn's hand over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach. The order was a fact about hands and not a custom; nine of it were nine with a finger between every pair. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule.

Beside the small sounds of the yard stood the offered figure and its return, the well-asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, \person{Berr Clay}'s entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, \person{Hessa Rale}'s reported second slate, the hair-thin thaw, \lore{the burned slate} standing at five, and the four ownerless directions, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair. None was a rule, a witness, a tenth hand, a sixth shaping, a doctrine or a payer, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. \person{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood and her ninth hand nine, and the hand that will one day lay shelf and fold and strip and slate together is far off and is not his. Nothing from the reading of the tenth had crossed under the roof as sign or lesson, mending or cause, payment or payer. An eleventh day had earned only its eleventh.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept the low note of \lore{water over water} under \lore{the winter skin}, a low thing over a lower thing, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the covering and the tang thinner, the note coming from under the covering and ending on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it — the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound — took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer four hundred and forty-six was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment. Four hundred and thirty-five exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-five days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-five, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. Silt 20 stood nine mornings off as a date and was not brought. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Forward, into the twelfth not yet lived and not brought into this one.
