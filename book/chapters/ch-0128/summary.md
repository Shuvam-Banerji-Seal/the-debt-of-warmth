## Chronicle

Three mornings came down under the Vail roof like silt settling in a standing well, and the house took them up as though nothing had turned. Sella Vail went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the wire crossed the crack, gave both proverbs to pot and fire, turned the grain under wool, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand. The widow's mark hung where it hung; seven openings in seven months were seven and no tending day made an eighth.

He kept the body in its old order. The crust stood two inches off, gone into old horn; the keeping lay in the empty place, found where put and still a finding; the thread at his mouth was stiff and unwiped, because nobody had ever been told to wipe it. Cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint. He wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle; the surgeon filed all three without verdict. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other.

At the kindling Tarn set six in the worn thumb-mark and the seventh at the lip, hand over blade and sill and away. His sentence about an ear worth more than the debt came to the calling word and stopped; the term had ended and the loan remained uncalled. At the wheel-shed Jorra parted dusty from clean forearms as clot from vessel, led at the cold damper ahead of Sella's sentence, and withheld the full note from any High Kiln master. The promise stayed ungiven. Garl Vail stayed in objects: the salt-stiff coat, the hollow wheel-seat, the visible crack, the unforged sentence, the fifth pull nobody supplied.

On the twelfth a neighbour at the well asked whether a count kept in wood would be taken at the next reading. Ilva's hand went flat over her sleeve-fold before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth after both. “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account,” she said, and went back to her pail; no figure came off the wood. On the thirteenth a man from Slate Row stood at the yard's edge with loose hands and a patient face. Nobody asked what he wanted and he said nothing. The boy filed the face on a place of its own.

Six answers stood on the sixth place, two to each day and not one a doctrine. The count was four hundred and thirty-seven exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-seven days told by nobody. The river kept its low note under its winter skin; the hand came up an inch, took a small intake, held the interval and came down flat on the fold. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment:** the small hours closing Day 437, Silt 13, Thirdday, under the Vail kiln-house roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note (the inch not a reach), took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down flat on the fold on answer 450, as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. No next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust; four low pulls under the boards kept their silence where the fifth should have stood and nobody supplied the pull. Forward into a fourteenth day not lived.
