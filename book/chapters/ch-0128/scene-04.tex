The afternoon of the twelfth came on the way the second half of a firing comes on: without a word, and with the heat already spent that had made the first half. The light thinned from white to the colour of tallow long kept in a warm room, and the shadows came out from under the racks and lay across \place{the flagstones} as though they had been waiting there since morning. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and the pot and the fire and never to the basket. The basket was not told what the hour was and was not told to be still. She set him where the heat came to him sideways and went about the afternoon, and \place{the Clay Yard} went on at the work of an afternoon without anybody looking up to see that the light had changed.

The low angle of the basket gave him the yard in pieces: the underside of a board, the shadow of a pail, the hem of a skirt going past and the sound of the going before the skirt itself. He had learned to read a room from a low angle in the other life, from the floor beside a gurney where the sheeting hid everything but the hands; a face can lie for a whole shift and the hand at the edge of the field cannot. Here the faces were turned away and the hands were at the well and the racks and the kindling, so he took the hands first. \person{Tarn} was off at the kindling with his keepings found where put, and \person[Jorra Vail] at the wheel-shed edge with his dust white in a fist-crease that was not opened twice; neither came under the basket's small sky and neither was brought. A man under this roof was kept in objects and in the grammar of a house --- a salt-stiff coat on its peg, a hollow wheel-seat nobody was allowed to fill, a pot on the board with its hairline showing --- and not in a story told to a child. The telling of it bought nothing.

He took the small sounds first because they do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. The rope at \place{the yard well} settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp it had given all winter, a dry sound with nothing warm in it. A pail came down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with a flat knock: stone, wood and the hand that set it down, and no fourth thing standing behind to be asked. Barley lay wall to wall along the rack without a number on it. A loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, with nobody calling the stopping a figure. He took each apart by the place of each part --- the fist that came down, the eye that followed, the mouth that did not open, the half-beat lying in the gap between the setting-down and the stopping. The gap was a gap. He gave it no intention, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood. A surgeon who writes the name of a cause over a gap has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. The pail's knock was one sound and then no sound; it did not go on speaking after the hand left it. The rope's rasp ended with the settling of the knot. The loaf stayed stopped until another hand moved it.

\person{Ilva} stood at the well drawing with the pail against her knee. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft. People noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred. The bucket came up dark and without a sound, and the water in it moved and stopped moving. The short strip of notched birch lay in the fold of her sleeve, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between. It was not out. It had not been out on any of the days behind, and there was no reason under this sky why it should be out today. The boy had made no reason for it and could not make one now: a hand that finds a keeping is not a hand that places it, and a hand that keeps a count is not a hand that reads it.

A woman waiting her turn shifted a pot from one hip to the other. The word had gone around the bucket-round since Ilmen 26 that a woman waiting her turn had brought a number up out of a shut fist, held it toward the light and put it toward the strip in this one's sleeve, and that the number had looked at the light, gone back into the sleeve and arrived nowhere. The neighbour had heard that word and stood now with the fear of it in her mouth, and a fear carried by another mouth is a different thing from the offer that fed it. It was not the old question repeated and it was not a second offer. It was the fear advanced by somebody else, and the question behind the question was a slate put to the stick.

“It's the next reading I'm thinking of,” the neighbour said. “A count kept in wood. Will they take it?”

The yard went on with its work around the two women. A rope rasped. Somewhere a board was set down and did not ring. The boy took the order of reaching apart by the place of each part, as he took every field apart, before the words had settled in the air.

Ilva's hand went flat over the fold in her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker, and her mouth opened after both. The half-beat lay in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. There was no intention in it. There was an order: hand, eye, mouth, and the interval standing between the second and the third as a door stands open between two rooms, with nobody passing through it. The strip stayed in its fold. It was not taken out, read, copied, counted, shown or joined. No figure came off the wood. No slate was opened within reach of the basket, and no room was entered and no reader made.

“Ask the pot what it cost,” she said. “I keep the count, not the account.”

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail. The neighbour held her look a breath longer than the answer required, then turned the pot on her hip and went back to the round. Nobody turned twice. The figure the neighbour had carried in her head was not the figure that had gone back into a fist on Ilmen 26, and no figure of either kind was given here. The stick kept its notch and learned no name.

\scenebreak

The boy filed the second asking on a place of its own. He did not lay it across anything. Beside it stood the offered figure and its return from the twenty-sixth of Ilmen, her ninth record-first hand with its nine stoppings, and the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, each on its own square with a finger between every pair. The asking was not a second offer, not a custom, not a tenth hand, not a witness, not a sale and not the calling of the loan. It had no figure under it and no payer standing. He kept it expressly unjoined to the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report, to \person{Hessa Rale}'s reported second slate of the sixth, to the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, to the well-asking that had been answered with the weather, and to the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}. Five places and a sixth, all separate, all standing where they had been put, and none of them one account. A joining of any two would want a payer put under both and none stood, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer.

Ilva's fear was advanced and left unpaid. She had not been made a reader by the asking and had not made an account of it, and the hand that will one day lay charcoal and cord and knot-string and inward slate and strip of birch together and call them a count with a payer under it is far off and is not his. He could not bring that hand under the roof or into the yard to answer any of the places waiting there, and did not try. The surgeon's register said the fear was a practical one, small and exact as the woman's speech, and the infant's body, warm from the basket's sideways heat and impatient for the going of the light, said nothing about it at all. Both entries stood. Neither bought the other.

The afternoon went on at the work of an afternoon. He added none of it together. The small sounds came and went and left no entry: the pail's knock, the rope's rasp, the loaf stopped a finger's width along its board. Costs came as directions and went away as weather. A man waiting his turn opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist and put it back into a sleeve, the number arriving nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price. The figure read over the covered grain under this roof did not know what the grain cost, and the ink-neat space between figure and price carried no signature. None of these was the asking, and none of them was joined to it. Six hearings of the \lore{full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and a twelfth tending day was not made an eighth by a question asked at a well.

\scenebreak

The light went at the going of the light the way a pot is taken from a fire: all at once and yet not hurried, the heat left behind in the place it had occupied. Sella came and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and said nothing to the basket of the asking, or of the stick, or of the reading that was to come, or of what the day had been. She set him under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} where the sideways heat found him, and went to \place{the hearth} before the basket.

The whole grammar came back at the end of the day as though nothing had turned. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbers and came back to the hairline at the wire. The one grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and did not tell it to the basket, turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone, and gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath it and no mark was copied. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}, the hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air.

The twice-mended pot came up thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair was not restoration, and she kept them apart the way a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain was lifted for damp and turned piece by piece with a touch that lightened as the hand went in and came out, laid back where it lay and covered with the flat of a palm; no figure went over it and no handful was counted, though a fist of barley weighed in the hand knows what it weighs. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. An afternoon, a well, an asking, a basket and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and a twelfth tending day was not made an eighth by the fear carried up out of the bucket-round. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. \lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put and stayed a finding. Nothing from the well had crossed under the roof as sign or lesson, mending or cause, payment or payer; she had not brought it in to answer the empty place, or the covered grain, or the figure that had gone up \place{the clay road} in the tenth's locked box. Costs went away as weather. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} kept its finer grey hairline called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} where no broom had gone in two years, unswept and called weather, and nobody looked twice.

\scenebreak

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap and did not add them. A surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up his answer.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and the Drawing house's flat knock outside. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, four, four and a gap, and three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth. The order of reaching was the same in each: hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both, the half-beat in the gap. He had watched it in a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, in a thane passing a finger over a figure before the crowd, in a clerk with hand to slate before eyes to stalls, in \person{Berr Clay}'s thumb wiped twice over a figure passed through a sleeve, and under this roof in Sella going to the hearth before the basket and in Tarn's hand over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach. It was a fact about hands and not a custom; nine of it were nine with a finger between every pair.

Beside the small sounds of the yard stood the offered figure and its return, and beside that the second asking of this afternoon, with a finger between the two and another between the asking and her ninth hand. Then came the well-asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, Hessa Rale's reported second slate, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, the hair-thin thaw, the burned slate standing at five, the three likenesses of Slate Row, and the four ownerless directions, each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair. The asking at the well had not made a tenth hand, a reader, a witness, a sale, a custom or a payer of the woman who answered it, and it had not become the reading it feared. It gathered interest toward a far-off calling and was not called here, not on a day that had earned only its twelfth and would not be told again on the thirteenth. Nothing from the tenth's reading had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road, four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid, all locked in that box with the key gone up in a coat pocket that never came back down. He did not lay them across the strip in Ilva's sleeve, which kept its notch on its own place with no figure taken off it.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept the low note of \lore{water over water} under \lore{the winter skin}, a low thing over a lower thing, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the covering and the tang thinner, the note coming from under the covering and ending on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer four hundred and forty-eight was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine. Four hundred and thirty-six exact, and six months and two hundred and fifty-six days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s name had gone unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-six, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth. The keeping at the fold lay where it had been put, found where put and still a finding. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Forward, into the thirteenth not lived and not brought into this one.
