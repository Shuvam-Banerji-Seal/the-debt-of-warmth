The afternoon came on over the Clay Yard the way heat comes up into a banked kiln --- not in a flame but in a settling, more of it in the hollows than on the ridges, the light going from wet ash to old brass and then to a colour that had no name. \person[Corin]{The boy} was set at the yard's edge in the finger's width of shade where no slate lay, the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and the basket not told what the hour was or told to be still. He could not see the whole yard from the low angle. He could see boots, and the lower halves of aprons, and mud going dry on leather that had stood too long in one place. That was enough. A surgeon reads a room from the part of it that has stopped moving.

There was a man standing at the yard's edge who was not of the yard. He had come from the direction of \place{Slate Row} and had not come into the yard; the difference was in the feet, which had stopped on the near side of the ruts, and in the mud on his boots, drying pale at the toe and still dark at the heel, so that he had been standing a while and had not stood all day. The boy took it in the order it came. Boots first. Then hands.

The hands were loose. They were not open and they were not shut, but hung with the fingers neither curled nor spread, the way a man's hands hang who has decided to wait and has not yet decided what for. There was nothing in either hand and nothing under either arm. No slate. No loaf. No cord, no pail, no pot with a figure chalked on its cost. The patient set of them was the whole of what he carried.

Then the face. It was the face of a man who can wait in a doorway. The boy had seen that face on people who had business with a Court and did not want to be seen having it: eyes not fixed on anything in particular and yet missing nothing that moved, mouth closed in a way that was neither kindness nor its opposite, the small muscles about the nose quiet. Nothing in it was angry. Nothing in it was sorry. It was a face with room in it, and the room was for a question.

The question did not come. Nobody asked him what he wanted. Nobody went to him, and he did not come to anybody. A woman passed with a pail and went around him the way the yard goes around a stone it has decided not to move. A man called something to somebody at the well and the something was about the weather. No loaf passed. No slate was shown, no word carried to the basket, no threshold crossed. He was not entered and he did not enter. He stood where he had put himself, with the mud going pale on his boots and his hands loose, and the yard went on being a yard.

A woman came to \place{the yard well} and set her pail down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with its flat knock, and drew, and went. A man carried a coil of rope past him and settled it into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, going around the waiting boots as the yard goes around \place{the returning-line place}, wide and without a number. A girl with her sleeves pulled over her hands looked at him once and looked at the ground, and the look cost her nothing because it was not a question and wanted no answer. Nobody made a figure of him. Nobody gave him an errand or took one from him. The boy took the sideways look apart as he had taken the hands apart: eye to the boots, eye away, mouth not opened, the half-beat in the gap left a gap. A lesser observer would have put a message in that gap. There was no message in it and no messenger, and a message wants somebody to pay for the sending.

The boy watched the mud go from dark to pale at the toe. He had once stood in a hospital corridor with a man in a good coat who waited exactly so, hands loose at his sides, and had known before the man spoke that whatever was coming would be entered somewhere and paid for by somebody who was not in the corridor. That was another country and another pair of hands. It paid nothing here. The thought went through him and out again without stopping, and he did not make the face a danger and did not make it a promise. He took it apart by the place of each part --- boots, hands, face, and the space where a question would have stood and did not --- and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer standing under it and none stood.

He filed the face on a place of its own. Beside it stood the older record-first hands, each in its square with a finger between: the midwife laying slates before she turned to a fold; the thane passing a finger over a figure before the crowd; the keeper's finger beneath a board; the runner's strap and wrist; the clerk with hand to slate before eyes to stalls; \person{Berr Clay} wiping his thumb twice and passing a finger over a figure through the cloth of his sleeve; \person{Hessa Rale} turning her wrist before her eyes went to the yard and going up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln} with the writing turned to herself; \person{Ilva} at the yard well passing a hand flat over her sleeve before her eyes went to the asker and her mouth after both. Further along and still separate stood the watcher's late turn and the first man's face at the edge, kept from an earlier day without a name. The new face went beside them, not among them. A finger went between it and the second asking of the day before, which stood where it had been put with its answer of pot and weather and no figure off the wood. Another went between it and the three likenesses of Slate Row, which were likenesses and were not this man. He made no tenth hand of it, no reader, no witness, no sale, no payment and no payer. He did not lay it across the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and never a sighting, or across the reported second slate, or across the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, or across the fourth reading that had gone up the road in a locked box with the key in a coat pocket that never came back down. He did not bring it under the roof as a lesson. None of those things was made to answer another.

The face stayed where he had put it while the light went on changing. It did not grow a hand, or a slate, or a word; it did not turn toward the basket to check whether it had been seen. Men who wait in doorways have learned that being seen costs nothing and that being asked costs something, and the boy knew the difference as well as the man did, though neither of them had ever said it. He did not make the man's patience a virtue or the patience a threat. It was a set of the face, found where put, like the fine grey beside the clean dark line at the corner, thickened in the dry cold and called weather by everybody who passed it. A thing found is not a thing owed. He had no name for the man and gave him none, and no roll in any room had a line standing open for him under that evening.

The household's exposure went on gathering the way \lore{Ash} gathers in a corner where no broom goes --- by being left, not by being added to. \person{Tarn}'s ear was worth more than the debt that held it; the term had ended and the loan was uncalled, which left him more exposed and not less. \person[Jorra Vail]'s promise of a name was neither given nor withdrawn, and \lore{the burned slate} stood at five with the four ownerless directions gathering interest against it, while \lore{the tile debt} circled against a firing of tiles that would never come. Berr's kindness said low to the block and his \emph{your house}, never \emph{I}, stood believed and not owed; a man who takes himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on has still left the field for somebody else to dress. The Kindling collector's reason stood on its own tray. The clean voice's missing name stood on another. The Long Night story's empty name stood where a neighbour had put it down. No payer stood under any of them. All of them went on gathering toward a calling that was far off and was not made on a day that had earned only its thirteenth.

The going of the light took the yard the way a cloth takes water --- from the edges in, quietly, with no drip and no sound. \person{Sella Vail} came to the basket and lifted it, and carried him up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. She did not tell the basket what the day was. She did not tell it about the face at the edge or about the mud drying on a pair of boots that belonged to nobody under this roof. A basket carried up is a pot carried up and set where the heat comes to it, and no woman explains the hour to a pot.

Under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the whole grammar came back as though nothing had turned. She went to \place{the hearth} before the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, in the voice another woman would have used to a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, a dry scrape of iron over stone. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, and figured neither of the two gaps. Her finger went along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so that the charcoal on its underside stayed unread; no hand went beneath it, no mark copied, no row numbered. Her lips moved on a two-gapped row that was neither prayer nor count and wanted no answer. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand --- two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold --- closed on air and went back to the pot.

At the going of the light she banked what she had turned, and banked it the way a woman puts a child down without waking it. \emph{That'll do till morning}, she said to the coals. \emph{You'll keep}, she said to the pot, and to the fire nothing at all, for the fire had been told the thaw already and did not need telling twice.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack, the hairline of the original break showing between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, to the fire, and never to a person. Repair stayed apart from restoration, as a surgeon keeps a graft apart from a cure. At \place{the rack end} she drew the wool over the dressed grain with the flat of her palm, no figure over it and no handful counted, though a fist of barley weighed in the hand knows what it weighs. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest. She did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung. She touched it before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open. A going of the light was not an opening, and an afternoon at the yard's edge was not an opening, and seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule and no eighth came due. Under \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years, unswept and called weather. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. \lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put and stayed a finding. The crust stood two inches off with its corner a hair off the wool, gone past leather into old horn; the thread at his mouth was stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Nothing under the roof had been mended by what had happened in the yard, and nothing had been made a lesson of it.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap, and did not add them, for a surgeon who adds findings before the last is in has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut.

The boots at the edge came first: mud pale at the toe and dark at the heel, a man who had stood a while and had not stood all day. The hands came next, loose, neither curled nor spread, with nothing in them and nothing under the arm. The face came after, patient, with room in it for a question. The space where the question would have stood came last, and was a space. Beside all of it stood the yard's honest small sounds, which do not lie because nobody has taught them to: a pail down on stone with its flat knock, a rope settling into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, barley wall to wall without a number, a loaf pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped with nobody calling the stopping a figure. He gave each its place and gave the half-beat in each gap its own square, and put no intention in any of them, for an intention wants a payer where none stood.

Under the same skin the body kept its own small order, and he read it because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn through the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool. The empty place beside it held the fold, found where put and still a finding. The thread at his mouth was stiff and unwiped. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught, with the heat coming to it sideways, was the careful rewarming he would choose, and could not ask for it; the knowing was a finding like any other, with no hand under it to make it a claim on anybody.

The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth, wanted the thread wiped, wanted a hand on the handle of the pot that would bring a cup within reach. The surgeon filed all three without verdict. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel went on without a settlement, because a settlement wants a hand to enter it and the only hand in the case was the small one outside the wool that could not close two inches of air.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside on its own place. The heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood; the kiln's four-and-a-gap behind the wall, the boards' four-and-four-and-a-knot, and the carried fire's four low pulls with the fifth missing were three and not one. Nine record-first hands stood with nine stoppings and no tenth. Seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule. The offered figure and its return, the second asking of the day before, the reported second slate, the neighbour's near-reaching, the hair-thin thaw, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and never a sighting, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, the three likenesses of Slate Row, and the face of this afternoon stood each on its own place with a finger between every pair. None was a reader, a witness, a tenth hand, a custom, a payment or a payer. The hand that will one day lay charcoal and cord and knot-string and inward slate and strip of birch together and call them an account is far off and is not his. He did not bring it under the roof to answer any of the places waiting there.

Four readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure up the clay road, four being four and not one account, the first not made a thirteenth by the three that followed, the second supplying no signature, the third no doctrine, the fourth no payer, the figure read still not the figure paid, and the twelve of Y.H. 813 not laid across the four to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. The fourth reading was not made to answer the face at the edge, or the asking at the well, or the near-reaching at the shelf, or the thaw at the corner, or anything else left lying under this roof. The unsent call to Nina stood where it had been put, a small cold rectangle of light with a name on it that he had not paid again and would not pay here. Nothing from any of these places was joined to another.

He had once laid a tray of instruments in the order they would be wanted, and known the difference between a finding and a want without having to think which was which; that was another room and another pair of hands, and it paid nothing here. The file that came out on the towel was in that order now, and he did not add findings before the last was in, because a surgeon who does has already made up the answer he wanted before he cut. Finding was not placing. A likeness was not a cause. A hand going flat over a sleeve was not a reader. Five shapings of a saying were not a sixth, and four readings were not an account that had signed itself. A number counted was a number with air around it, and air bought no warmth; he had kept four hundred and thirty-seven days of it and could not spend one of them.

He kept four hundred and thirty-seven exact, six months and two hundred and fifty-seven days told by nobody who would have known what a number counts. Answer four hundred and fifty was filed on the sixth place, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment and not a doctrine. Six honest answers stood there now, four hundred and forty-five through four hundred and fifty, two to each of the three days, and not one of them was an account that had found a payer. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth went unpaid and \person[Garl Vail]'s name went unspoken the same four hundred and thirty-seven, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one, for a number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under \lore{the winter skin}, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer and the tang thinner; the sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under \place{the boards} the four low pulls kept their silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Forward, into a fourteenth day not lived and not brought.
