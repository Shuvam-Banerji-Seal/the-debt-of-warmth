# Chapter 25 — The Last Tend Holds — beat sheet

**Days:** 113–115. Day 113 = Seed 24, Fourthday; Day 114 = Seed 25, Fifthday; Day 115 = Seed 26, Sixthday; Y.H. 813. No Account-day falls in this chapter (Seed 30 is Day 119). Day 112 is done and is not revisited; Day 116 belongs to Chapter 26. **Mode:** normal, three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and at least 800 words on the page. Six scenes; two carry each day so that nothing is poured into another.

**The chapter's movement.** The planting's last tend holds with the seed up and the hands still counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. A number is carried in a fist toward the well and put back in the sleeve, so it arrives nowhere, and another neighbour at the loaf shelf lays two rooms under one cause in a single breath while the boy keeps three places apart. Under the Vail roof the first fraction of change in the equality is **held, not repeated**: Jorra's finger leads at the damper again and Tarn's stroke goes on without the half-check, leaving the flagstones still keeping yesterday's stopped place. The count goes 113, 114, 115, exact and unspent. Nothing is joined, paid, named or opened. Nothing ends.

**Hard withholdings (carry all three days).** No ending, no payment, no recognition; no opening of tin, box, slate, back room or widow's mark; no third gap and no third crust; no knot counted; no word *forty*; no seventh hearing; no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand; no watcher on stage and no sale or betrayal paid; no name or number from Dessa; no silt-leach, cause or cure for the cold foot; no calling of the loan; no spending of the seventh year; no third telling of the shut counting room; no repeating of Tarn's check. T097 advanced again and still not paid. T023 not paid again. Marra not in Sill; Ulda offstage; Dessa dead and stays dead; Fenna offstage; Orra present only as her knot-string; Berr short and only where noted. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from Dan's eyes. The bell is kept beside the heard bell but is not heard in this chapter and must not be invented.

## Scene 1 — The Figure That Arrives Nowhere
**Day 113, Seed 24, Fourthday, morning.** Vail kiln-house at the loaf shelf, then the Clay Yard and the yard well; the worked strips east of Low Kiln spoken of as the last tend. Sella Vail, Corin (carried), an unnamed woman with wet sleeves at the well, an unnamed man with a fist, yard voices. Berr is not on stage this day.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open at first grey on the last tend: seed up in the worked strips, the hands that went in on Seed 15 still counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. The boy is carried at the edge and his hand goes up an inch toward the direction and comes down. He is one hundred and thirteen days old, three months and twenty-three days, and nobody says so.
2. Sella gives the instruction to the bucket, the broom, the pot and the fire before anybody and never to the basket; says *the house* where another woman would say *I*; keeps the half-pace from every open slate; moves her lips along the loaf row without giving a figure.
3. A woman with wet sleeves asks what the planting cost now that hands have been in the strips. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, leaves the space at the row's end with no figure and sends her to the pot: *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.* The instruction goes to the pot, not to the woman and not to the basket.
4. A man brings a number up out of a fist toward the well, looks at it, thinks better of giving it, puts the fist back in his sleeve and goes off; the figure arrives nowhere. The surgeon's register reads the shut fist as a chart closed with a neat line over a note not opened --- exact, complete, worth nothing --- and the boy cannot lift a hand to his own chin.
5. The ordinary work resumes around the withheld figure: wet sleeves wrung out, a broom going around what the sweep left, loaves pushed along the shelf. No event is made of the fist; nobody joins the number to the strips.
6. Sella does not touch the widow's mark: a well, a shelf and a row of loaves are not openings. End on the work, not on the absence.

**Emotional turn.** The cost of the planting is handled the way every cost in Low Kiln is handled --- shut in a fist, sent to a pot, left on a shelf. The one person who reads the withholding exactly is the one who cannot enter a figure anywhere and has no standing to ask. The dry joke lives in the exactness: he is the only adult in the room and the least use in it.

**Sensory anchors.** Wet sleeves and the wet stone lip of the well; chaff in the creases of a fist; the clay jar cold under its glaze; a bare board worn smooth at the row's end; a hard heel of barley bread; woodsmoke gone flat under the roof; the sour mineral tang in the unswept corner; a pot set down and left.

**Threads advanced.** T077 (number brought up out of a fist and put back, figure arriving nowhere), T058/T079 (instruction to bucket, broom, pot, fire --- never the basket; *the house has said it*; half-pace; jar glanced at, mark untouched), T009/T013/T092 (left things kept; strips worked and uncounted), T071/T072 (113 kept exact).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The figure withheld at its source advances toward the day a slate at the watch will carry a number that is not the number paid; the fist advances toward the harvest of the planting in the Account-days ahead (T098 is planted in Ch 26, not here). Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** The man's fist going back into his sleeve with the number still shut in it, while Sella's lips move along the loaf row and the boy's small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the worked strips and comes down.

## Scene 2 — Two Absences in One Breath
**Day 113, Seed 24, Fourthday, midday at the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house; going of the light under the roof.** Sella Vail, Corin (carried), another unnamed neighbour with a pot. Sella's private file checked at nightfall.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Another neighbour at the loaf shelf reaches past the bare board, her hand going to the board before her eyes go to the boy. She opens for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, closes on the loaf beyond and goes on with her pot.
2. In one breath she calls the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the boy's ear a child's appetite. She does not see that she has laid two rooms under one cause and does not see that the two bare boards are one absence. She speaks to the board, not to anyone.
3. The boy keeps three places apart --- the shelf's two unnamed gaps, the fold's one stiff brown-edged crust with its unnamed empty place, and the neighbour's one-breath naming --- and writes no cause across the space between them. T097 advanced again and **not paid**; no fourth likeness making a cause and no third gap. He does not make the neighbour's joining his own.
4. Sella moves her lips along the row, glances at the clay jar and gives nobody a figure; she does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf is not an opening. The errand stays an errand and is not turned into a walk to Slate Row.
5. At the going of the light she carries him up at the half-pace from every open slate, goes around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped: nobody has been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.
6. 7. He adds the day without entering it: the fist put back in the sleeve, the woman sent to the pot, the neighbour's one-breath naming. Nothing moved, nothing paid, nothing joined.
8. River's note through the gap; kiln's four, four and a gap through the floor under blind ash, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirteen days; two counts in two places that do not meet. Boards overhead: four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb.

**Emotional turn.** A neighbour makes an account of two rooms and the only person who sees the joining is the one who must refuse it. Keeping the places clean is the whole of what he can do, and the keeping buys nothing. The woman at the fire keeps everything by not explaining any of it.

**Sensory anchors.** A hand reaching past a bare board before the eyes arrive; the half-beat of an absence; chaff under fingernails; a hard heel of bread; the clay jar under its glaze; fold put right without a look at the face; frozen thread; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; the pale grey streak in dying light; wet wool riding back from a bare wrist.

**Threads advanced.** T097 (another one-breath naming, advanced, **not paid**), T060/T086 (three absences on three places, no third), T058/T079 (*the house*, instruction to pot and fire, never the basket, half-pace), T061/T082 (six hearings, no seventh, three near-full answers, word forty), T036/T076 (loan uncalled), T088 (carried fire, payer unnamed at 113), T009/T013/T092, T071/T072 (113 exact, unspent).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The neighbour's joining waits for the far-off finger that will lay shelf and fold together and call them an account --- never his hand, not this chapter. ## Scene 3 — The Stroke That Does Not Check
**Day 114, Seed 25, Fifthday, before first grey through midday.** Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard. Sella Vail, Jorra Vail, Tarn, Corin (carried at the edge). Ulda offstage; no foundling turns late at any board.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before first grey men come up the yard with collars high and go into the room off the wheel-shed that stands a finger's width open. The low voices do not carry to the edge of the light where the basket is set. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, ground small as meal, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The boy keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
3. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody. The space above them is not stepped into.
4. Jorra speaks to the wheel before anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.* He tells a pot *You'll do*. He knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
5. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, the iron taking the cold out of his fingertip; the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He has not answered with a word and has not looked at her.
6. **The change held, not repeated:** Tarn takes the broom only after it has been given its instruction. Yesterday his stroke checked half a beat behind the standing finger; today it does not. The bristles go on over the flagstones without a pause and without a turn of the head. The flagstones still keep the place where the stroke stopped the day before, as a thing nobody will enter; the equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to. Neither man names the other's silence and the loan that can hear is not called.
7. The boy reads the iron once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object joined to no kiln and worth nothing. He sets the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat on the interval tray and lays no cause across them; the two tellings of the shut counting room stay joined to no kiln and no third telling comes.
8. *He's not Vail* stays sealed; the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn; nobody looks toward the bed or into the basket. The seventh year is not spent and the held breath from Seed 16 is still unspent.

**Emotional turn.** Two men keep the same house by not saying the same thing, and the one who moved does not move again. The reader is given that the flagstones kept the place; no person in the room will enter it. The boy's exactness sees the holding and the not-repeating as objects, and buys nothing with either.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey dust fine as meal in the creases past the bent finger; damp clay and tallow; the sweetish edge of lead glaze on a bad wind; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; broom bristles going on over the flagstones where they checked yesterday; blind ash under the pot; the small interval at the top of Tarn's reach where a breath should have gone in.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, grey dust, broken birth-slates, sealed sentence, ungiven promise), T062 (damper's double bite filed as an object, joined to no kiln), T036/T076 (the first fraction held and not repeated; loan uncalled; seventh year held; two silences not joined), T058/T079 (instruction to pot and fire, never the basket), T061/T082 (no seventh hearing; two tellings joined to no kiln), T071/T072 (114).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** The convergence under one roof advances toward the day the sentence that would call the loan is said; the unchecking stroke is held for the later near-saying that stops short of calling it (Ch 28–29), and the dust and the unnameable back room still point at the broken birth-slates and the men who burn them. Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's bent finger standing in the cold iron of the damper and Tarn's broom going on over the flagstones without the check, the place where it stopped yesterday still kept on the stones, the boy carried at the edge with the two silences laid on one tray and not joined.

## Scene 4 — Warm and Breathing

## Scene 6 — One Hundred and Fifteen
**Day 115, Seed 26, Sixthday, going of the light.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof; the boy on his bed and never in the Debt Loft. Sella Vail, Corin.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The boy is one hundred and fifteen days old, three months and twenty-five days, and no living person under this roof knows there is a number to be known. He has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it; the want goes on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under blind ash.
2. He turns out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; three intervals; the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn and the weight of a thumb apart; four low things in four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth; the two tellings of the shut counting room joined to no kiln and no third; the damper's double bite filed as an object worth nothing. The told bell stays beside the heard bell; the bell was not heard in this chapter and is not invented.
3. The held breath from Seed 16 is still unspent, the unforged collector sentence kept beside it, the small bench knife at the very edge of its sill with six years in a worn mark and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it does not give here and the loan that can hear is still uncalled.
4. Add the day without entering it: the number shut in a fist, the neighbour's one-breath naming, Jorra's finger leading and Tarn's stroke going on without the check, Alda's date over the sign, Mira's hidden twist, the soft hands at the Cross. He sets them on their places and lays no finger across any of them; a fourth likeness would make a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand is the one object in this world he has spent two lives declining to become.
5. One crust stiff in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; the shelf's two gaps in the other room; below the sill the dark foot inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody will perform, the reading perfect and buying nothing. The clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept and called the thaw; the space under the rack gone around; no opening came, so the widow's mark hangs untouched and Sella's private roll stays unshown.
6. The carried fire under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and giving back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fifteen days. Every warmth in the house has a payer and none of them stands in the room to be thanked.
7. Sella tells the fire it was the thaw and turns the coals with the back of a broken pot; *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*; she stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath that no man could take up as a figure, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak at her temple pale in the dying light off the coals), sets the pot where it could be found in the dark and does not look toward the bed.
8. The door stands a finger's width open. The river's note comes through the gap from the Ilm, water going over water, and through the floor comes the kiln's breath, four, four and a gap under blind ash, two sounds in two places that have not met in a hundred and fifteen days and will not meet tonight. 9. Cold arrives in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the bare joint so the cold finds the place it has come for. He wants the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what a life counted, and can reach or ask for none of them. He was a man who had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that was not this one and he cannot close two inches on the fold.
10. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down. One hundred and fifteen stands where one hundred and fourteen stood, exact, unspent, spoken by nobody, while the river goes over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire misses its fifth breath under the floor. On the morrow one hundred and sixteen will stand in the same place and nobody under the roof will know a number has moved.

**Emotional turn.** The file is complete and useless; the day adds its exact objects and spends none of them. **Sensory anchors.** Fold put right without a look at the face; frozen thread; blind ash and the back of a broken pot; four low pulls and a gap; the pale grey streak in dying light; the little-door burn shut on nothing; wet wool, woodsmoke and river silt in the gap of the door; the small wet sound when the head goes too far; the cold finding the bare wrist.

## Pull into Chapter 26

The chapter closes mid-reach, not at a resolution. The count moves from one hundred and twelve to one hundred and fifteen without anyone under the roof knowing there is a number to move; the last tend of Seed still holds with the seed up and the hands counted in glances and handfuls, and a number carried toward the well in a fist has arrived nowhere. T097 has taken another shape from another neighbour's breath and remains unpaid, with three places still apart and no third gap; the equality under the roof has moved once and held there, Tarn's stroke going on without the check while the flagstones keep yesterday's stopped place, and the loan that can hear remains uncalled. The seventh year is three days nearer its end and has not given. Chapter 26 opens on Day 116, Seed 27, Seventhday, with the boy one hundred and sixteen days old and the last of the hands coming out of the strips at dusk --- and one hand that went in on Seed 15 not coming back with the rest (T098 planted there, no name, no cause, no body) --- the shelf's two gaps and the fold's one still in rooms that do not touch, the word *forty* unspoken, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting, no watcher on stage, Marra not in Sill and Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy: the same held things one day further on, forward and not closed.
