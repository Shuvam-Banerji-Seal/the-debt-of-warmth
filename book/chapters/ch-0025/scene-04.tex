The morning did not so much end as stand a moment while the iron held and the broom went on over the flagstones, and then \person{Sella Vail} lifted the basket from the edge of the light as she lifted a pot filled to the lip, the little-door burn closing first on the handle and then on nothing. \person[Corin]{the boy} rode level under the fold, one hundred and fourteen days old and told by nobody that a number had been reached, looking at the underside of her chin and at the low smoke that lay behind her with no lift in it. She carried him at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, neither between a hand and the writing it was going to nor where a figure lay with its face up, and went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The broken birth-slates kept their place under there. Three doors down the \place{Clay Yard} the day had the smell of soap-water gone cold and lye sour under it, and she set the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door of \place{House Silt}, at the edge of the light and away from every place a slate might lie. The instruction went to the wool. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” It went into the wool and not into the basket and not into the boy. She opened no slate. The errand that had carried her three doors stayed an errand to a room and did not turn into a walk to \place{Slate Row}, though most of what this room held would have read better there. She stood a half-pace off with her burn shut on nothing.

The room kept what it had kept for three years. A bench stood under the door and a chipped cup on the shelf by the wall, and a grey ridge of \lore{Ash} ran along the skirting and under the door into the next room, left where it lay for the Last Day. Above the abandoned board-bed a round pewter tin held its place, with a pale fold showing at the seam of the lid and a thumb's bright rub worn into the pewter. Below it the fold of grey wool lay as it had been laid and held only the hollow where the small pale river stone had once lain. Nobody in the room said that either thing was gone. In the back room a neighbour scrubbed at a board with lye water and did not look up at the writing, or the tin, or the fold pressed too long against a shoulder; a low song came up through the floor with its words flattened out of sense by work. From the height of a basket the shelf is the highest thing in a house, and the boy had entered the tin nowhere in all his days.

Then \person{Alda Reed} came. She came short, the way a woman comes who has been called out of a room where somebody else has just been fed: soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt, lye sour under the soap, her nails scrubbed to the quick with clay still under them. She had her birth-slates under her arm and a narrow slate of her own in the fold of her shawl. She crossed to the bench and laid the writing-down flat before she turned toward the fold. It was the order of a hand that reaches for the record before the person, and it did not vary; he had watched it from a basket at a market's edge and from a bed and had never once seen her reverse it. He had watched a senior man lay his hand on a chart before he looked at the patient under it, and learned there what the order meant: the record was the thing that would still be there when the person was carried out. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for the order and he had no voice to spend on it, and she performed it again now in a room where the person was not going to be carried out at all.

She pressed the wool flat at his shoulder a breath longer than a fold requires. The red edge of the birth-flower showed where the wool had ridden back, and she crossed two fingers at the corner of it before she knew she had done it, the sign made in front of a basket and covered before it could be read. She covered it with the date she had given the woman: \emph{Stone seven. The account went up on the eighth, before the paper went out.} It stood now over a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of, uncollected, and no living person could take it off the sign. “Warm and breathing,” Alda said. “That's all I've set down.” It was exact. It was not true. He had no way to tell her so and no standing if he had, and she did not wait for an answer; she went on with dates after the question had stopped, because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry: a cooper's seventh in \place{Mud End}, a potter's third child in \place{Stonemouth}, the last high water, the exact day of a birth in a house nobody in this room would ever enter. The dates came faster than dates had any business coming, and the room arranged itself around them the way a market arranges itself around a reading it has not understood.

Her hand went once inside her bodice to the little tin on its cord and came away without opening it. She took up the narrow slate from her shawl and wrote on it with a charcoal stub what she did not leave lying about, in the second hand no Court physician had ever seen, and turned it writing-down into the shawl toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name. Six pages, all unread, written one at a time over years and never carried to a door; no seventh had been added and nothing was paid again today. Above the board-bed the pewter tin kept its pale fold and thumb-bright lid shut, and below it the hollow in the wool kept the shape of a stone nobody mentioned. Two private records in two materials stood in one room on two separate places, and a third thing lay at his wrist under a date, and the boy set all three apart and wrote no cause across the space between them. A likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not anybody put one there; he had learned that in a better-lit room and had spent his whole life in this one keeping his finger off the places where it would grow.

She went out as she had come in, short, with the birth-slates under her arm, and Sella asked nothing about the writing or the tin or the fold pressed too long. A half-pace and a turned slate stood between the two women for the whole of the visit, which was the arrangement, and neither had put a word into it in three years. Sella lifted the basket level and carried him back out at the half-pace from every open slate, and did not look into it, and did not turn the errand into a walk to Slate Row on the way back either.

By the going of the light the yard in \place{Low Kiln} had gone the colour of a pot that has stood covered since morning, and Sella came across to the basket with the little-door burn closing on the handle and opening on nothing. She carried him up at the half-pace, going wide around the space where the broken slates lay, and set him on his bed under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. The fold went right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and the thread of drool at his mouth froze where it lay and was left unwiped, because nobody in this house had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. When his head went a little too far there was the small wet sound, and nobody had been given an instruction about that either.

He turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel, each thing on its own place with room between, and found every object where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with no seventh: five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through the wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh had come; the bell was not in this day and he did not invent it, and the told bell stayed beside the heard bell in two places. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid; three intervals stood on another, and a stroke that had gone on without checking went beside the half-beat of the day before without a cause laid across either. The word forty stayed on its own tray, unspoken, with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and neither of the two things beside it was counted. Four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water over water was a fifth on a place of its own and not a hearing.

The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed joined to no kiln and no third telling came; \lore{the damper}'s double bite lay filed as an object worth nothing, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man in one piece of cold iron with no word in Grathic for both mercies. The held breath from Seed 16 was still unspent, and the unforged collector sentence kept beside it; the small bench knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years in a worn mark and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. Something must give at the end of the seventh year. It had not given today, and the loan that can hear was uncalled.

He added what the day gave without entering any of it: men unnamed come up the yard before first grey, fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bent little finger, a wheel-keeper's finger standing in the cold iron ahead of a sentence not finished, and a clay-bound hand's stroke going on over the flagstones without the check. He set them on their places and laid no finger across them; a fourth likeness would make a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one object in this world he had spent two lives declining to become. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; \place{the loaf shelf} in the room that did not touch this one kept its two gaps. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; the reading was perfect and bought nothing, uncured, uncaused and unnamed. The clean dark line and the new finer grey lay unswept and were called \lore{the thaw}; the space beneath the lowest rack was gone around and entered by nobody; no opening had come, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung untouched and the private roll Sella kept stayed unshown.

She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the carried fire under blind ash gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fourteen days. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath that no man could take up as a figure, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, set the pot where it could be found in the dark, and did not look toward the bed. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals, and the house went on holding what it held without an entry in it.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's note out of \place{the Ilm}, water going over water, and through the floor came the kiln, four, four and a gap under blind ash, two sounds in two places that had not met in a hundred and fourteen days and would not meet tonight. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not become one. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what a life counted, and he could reach or ask for none of them. He was a man who had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that was not this one and he could not close two inches on the fold.

Above the abandoned board-bed three doors down the round pewter tin kept its pale fold and thumb-bright rub shut, and the grey wool below held only the hollow where the stone had lain, nobody saying either was gone. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and fourteen stood where one hundred and thirteen had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, while the river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor. On the morrow one hundred and fifteen would stand in the same place and nobody under the roof would know a number had moved.
