The going of the light came down under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof the way ash comes down in a room with no draught --- slowly, and settling on whatever has been left standing. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} up from \place{the Cross} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the basket held level the way a woman carries a bowl filled to the lip and will not let it slop, and she went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The little-door burn closed first on the handle and then on nothing. She set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. It took her a moment longer than the setting needed, the way a hand will rest a half-beat on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot bring itself to let go of, and then the hand was gone and the fold was right and the room was a room again.

He was one hundred and fifteen days old, three months and twenty-five days, and there was not a living person under this roof who knew there was a number to be known. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same thing as not wanting it. The wanting went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under blind ash, giving back nothing a man could see and keeping its heat all the same. A neighbour at a shelf, a clerk at a landing, a woman with her thumb under her apron, a man who put a fist back into his sleeve --- every one of them had carried a number today and not one of them had thought to hand it to the child who could have told them what it counted. He lay with his head going where it wanted and making the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody put it right, because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering.

He turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel, each object going to its own place under a hand that knew where it lay without looking. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh had come on this day: no whole vessel's note had come past the door, no bell had been heard under this roof today and he did not supply one, and the wheel's and the pots' notes were not hearings and never had been. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid, a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot in the yard, each one a thing that had almost said its whole and then stopped short, the way a vessel gives nearly all of its note before the crack takes the rest of it. Three intervals stood in their own row. The told bell stayed beside the heard bell on the place where the two had always lain, the same four and the same missing stroke kept in two rooms that did not touch, and the boy did not bring them together tonight any more than he had on any other night. The word forty stood unspoken, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and neither was entered and neither was called by its name. Four low things kept their four places in four rooms, and the river's note of water over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own, not a hearing and not joined to any of the four. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed joined to no kiln and no third telling came. \lore{the damper}'s double bite --- the clamp that holds a vessel whole and the clamp that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies --- lay filed as an object worth nothing, joined to no house and no kiln. The loan that can hear lay uncalled, and the sentence that would call it had not been said under this roof in a hundred and fifteen days.

The held breath from Seed 16 lay where it had lain a hundred days ago, still unspent in a man's chest somewhere in this house, and the unforged collector sentence was kept beside it, a thing said once to a collector and never entered and never taken back. The small bench knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years in a worn mark and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house that the house could not check, the one account kept by a man who had decided not to spend it and went on deciding every morning. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it had not given today, and the breath had not gone in at the top of the reach where a breath should have gone in, and the loan that could hear was still a loan nobody had called. He set all three on their places and went over none of them with a thumb, because a thing looked at too long in this house grows a figure at the foot of it and there was nobody who would have known what to do with the figure if it came.

Then he added what the day had given, without entering any of it. A number brought up out of a fist toward a well, looked at, thought better of, and shut away again in a sleeve. A neighbour at a shelf who had said two bare boards were one appetite in a single breath and gone off with her pot not seeing what she had done. A wheel-keeper's finger standing in cold iron ahead of a sentence that had not finished, and a broom going on over the flagstones without the half-check it had made the day before, the two silences in one room with no hand joining them. A birth-slate with a dead woman's date laid over a sign nobody alive could correct. A shift of a creel showing a second cord twisted twice beneath the grey with neither knot counted. Soft hands handing a figure to a slate held at the edge where nobody else could see it, and a thumb resting on the corner afterward. He laid each on its place and laid no finger across any of them. They were six objects on six places and they did not touch, and a likeness laid on a likeness in this district grew a cause as surely as silt gathers behind a weir, and a fourth would have made the cause with a clean hand at the foot of it. The clean hand was the one object in this world he had spent two lives declining to become. He kept his own hand out of the field.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place beside it, still unnamed, and no third had come into the fold on this day or any day. The loaf shelf kept its two unnamed gaps in a room that did not touch this one, the old space at the row's end and the newer palm's breadth that had come in the night of Seed 6 without a scrape, crumb, print or word, and the two rooms had not touched in a hundred and fifteen days and would not touch tonight. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core in the order he had learned in a room that was not this one, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. The reading was perfect and bought nothing: no silt-leach, no cause, no cure, no name, only the cold doing what cold does and a man who knew the whole of it and could not close two inches on a fold. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned lay unswept beside the new finer hairline of grey that had gathered next to it, and the house called the two of them \lore{the thaw} and would go on calling them that until someone said what they were, which nobody had. The space under the lowest rack had been gone around again this evening as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered by nobody. No opening had come under this roof on Seed 26, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung, untouched at a basket and a bed, and the private roll the house kept stayed unshown.

The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees the way a thing warms that is trusted to a woman who will not be in the room to watch it. It gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had given on every day of this child's life, and its payer had gone unnamed for a hundred and fifteen days. Every warmth in this house had a payer and not one of them stood in the room to be thanked. The milk, the coal, the seed, the hands in the strips, the loaf that had gone off the shelf on Seed 6, the fire under the pot --- each had crossed into the house from somewhere and left the giver standing outside in the yard with the giving unentered. He had learned early to ask who paid before he asked what a thing was worth, and the asking had never once produced a name.

At the fire she told it it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the way a woman turns a thing that has been trusted to her and will go on being trusted when she is not standing over it. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath that no man could take up as a figure, toward no opening: not a door, not a shelf, not a slate turned writing-down, not the space under the rack, nothing that would have asked for a hand on the little-door mark. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals, a single pale thread in a head of coal-black that she said had come in the winter Garl died and would not discuss further. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark and did not look toward the bed. It was told of her in the yard that she had once sat three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and that such a house has had practice; the story had come from other mouths and never from hers, and she had never once laid it beside the dark foot below this sill, and least of all beside the bed.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's note out of \place{the Ilm}, water going over water, the small steady sound the boy had kept for a fifth thing on a place of its own since the first warm days. Through the floor came the kiln, four, four and a gap under blind ash. Two counts in two places that had not met in a hundred and fifteen days and would not meet tonight, though both went on under one roof and both lacked the same fifth thing. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not become one, and the three counts did not become one either. He had long ago given up trying to lay them edge to edge. A man can hold a great many separate things in his head and keep them separate, and it is the whole of the work and the whole of the loneliness, and it buys nothing.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool rode back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for, and the thread at his mouth was frozen into a small stiff line that nobody had been instructed to wipe. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, and the breast, and the bucket's lip two paces off instead of the empty distance where it stood, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would have known what a life counted when they said it. He could reach or ask for none of them. He was a man who had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room that was not this one, who had decided in ninety seconds which of twenty injured bodies would be given the next breath of air, and he could not close two inches on the fold that smelled of wet wool and nothing else. Both facts were true, and the second was the one that was loud here, as it had been every night since the stone at his belly went cold, and the first was the one he had spent two lives trying to put down and could not.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and fifteen stood where one hundred and fourteen had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody under the roof, while the river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor and the two counts did not meet. Nothing was joined, nothing was paid, nothing was named, nothing was opened, and nothing ended. On the morrow one hundred and sixteen would stand in the same place with the same number of nails above it and the same gap under the floor, and nobody under this roof would know that a number had moved.
