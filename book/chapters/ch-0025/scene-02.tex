The middle of the day came under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof without arriving, the way a pot already on the bench comes to a woman's hand: the light lay flat and the woodsmoke lay flat under it, and between the two flats the room held wet wool and the faint sour tang that came up out of the unswept \lore{Ash} corner. \person[Corin]{The boy} was at \place{the loaf shelf} still, set where his eye could take in the row and his hand could not take in anything, which was the whole of the arrangement --- a direction with no figure at its foot, a row with two bare places in it that nobody called by a name. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less against it than the cold wanted, riding back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound it made when it went too far, and nobody put it right, because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering.

Another neighbour came for a loaf with a pot hooked over two fingers. Her hand went past the bare board before her eyes went anywhere, the way a hand goes to a familiar gap in a shelf it has used for years: it opened for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, that small pause a hand makes over an absence the way a tongue finds a hole in a mouth, and closed on the hard heel of a loaf beyond. Only after that did her eyes come to the basket, the fold, the small curved shape of a head under the wool that was not turned toward her. There was chaff at the base of her thumb and a wet cuff, and she carried the pot the way a woman carries a thing she will set down somewhere and leave.

“That end place came in with the planting,” she said to the board, meaning the gap at the row's end; and then, in the same breath with no stop between the two, “and that there's a child's appetite. It'll want filling come Reed.” She said it to the bare board and to her pot, not to anybody under the roof, the way the yard said its questions when it wanted the house to answer and no house to have answered. A woman who says a thing to a shelf is not asking to be contradicted by a basket.

She did not see what she had done in one breath. She had laid the old gap at the row's end and the empty place at the boy's ear under one cause and called them both the planting's doing, and gone off with her pot thinking she had said two plain things about two plain boards. She did not see that the two bare boards on this one shelf were one absence wearing two shapes. A cause, once laid, is a thing that wants a name at the foot of it; this one went off under her arm with the loaf and stood in no slate that any clerk could read. Her hand had gone to the board before her eyes had gone to him, and the order of that was the order of the whole district --- the record before the person, the shelf before the child --- and she went on with her pot and her loaf as though nothing had passed between them.

The boy kept three places and did not let them touch. On one stood the shelf's two unnamed gaps, in a room that did not touch this one: the old space at the row's end, and the second that had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, a bare board worn smooth by hands reaching past it for something else. On another stood the fold's one stiff brown-edged crust with the empty place beside it that had no name --- a little hard heel of barley gone past smelling of anything, and beside it the shape of the absence where a small pale stone had once lain warm against a belly and was gone and spoken of by nobody, left in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned bed in \place{House Silt}. On a third stood the neighbour's one-breath naming, a shape with the shelf's old absence and the absence at his ear folded together inside it. He set them edge to edge and wrote no cause across the space between them. He had long ago learned to feel an empty place with an exactness that had nothing to do with temperature: he had felt the place of the pale stone in the fold in House Silt with the same faculty he had once used to find a rustled joint under a man's skin, and the feeling bought him nothing here, because there was no hand on the other end of it that he could reach. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer: laid one on another they grow a cause, and a fourth would make the cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one object in this world he had spent two lives declining to become. He did not make her joining his own. He kept her saying on its own tray, the way a man keeps a clamp laid open on a towel --- exact, complete, and not to be closed on anything that was not his to close. A fourth likeness would have joined shelf to fold and fold to appetite and given the whole of it a clean hand and a name, and there was nobody under this roof who had asked him for either.

\person{Sella Vail} moved her lips along the row and came back to the same bare place twice. She did not give the neighbour a figure, and she did not give the board one, and she did not give the woman who had gone off with her pot one. Her eyes went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and came back to the loaves; it was the single look she gave it every day and it was not an opening. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a shelf is not an opening, and a row of loaves is not an opening, and no opening had come under this roof that day. The neighbour's errand stayed an errand. It was not turned into a walk to \place{Slate Row}, and nothing she had said was carried out of the house to be entered anywhere. Her little-door burn shut on nothing as she passed the loaf row and opened on the pot's handle. The loaves went along the board a finger at a time where a thumb had pushed them, the hard heel going where hard heels go, and the two gaps stayed where they were and were not read.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody in this house had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The going-around was a thing he kept on a place of its own, exact and worth nothing, and he made no approach to it with his one free inch of hand.

He turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel, each object going to its place under a hand that knew where it lay. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh came in this day: no whole vessel's note had come past the door, and the wheel's and the pots' notes were not hearings. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid. Three intervals stood in their own row, and beside them the small half-beat of a broom's check went as another place where a stroke should have gone on and did not, laid there and not explained. The word forty stood unspoken, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and neither was entered. Four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water over water was a fifth on a place of its own, not a hearing and not joined to any of the four. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed joined to no kiln, and no third telling came. \lore{the damper}'s double bite --- the clamp that holds a vessel whole and the clamp that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies --- lay filed as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln. The loan that can hear lay uncalled, and the sentence that would call it had not been said under this roof.

He added what the day had given without entering any of it: the number brought up out of a fist, looked at, and put back in the sleeve; the woman with wet sleeves sent to the pot; the neighbour's one-breath naming of two absences as one appetite. He laid each on its place and went over none of them with his thumb. Three things had come into the room and none of them had been given a figure, a name or a standing; the withheld number, the sent-to-the-pot question and the joined appetite stood edge to edge like three instruments laid out that no hand had been given to hold. Nothing had moved. Nothing was paid. Nothing was joined. The file was complete and useless, the way a chart is complete in the moment before the man who needs it is carried out of the room, the neat line drawn straight over the note that nobody opened. He was the only grown man under this roof and had the use of none of it, and the joke of that was exact and cost him nothing, because there was nobody here to tell it to who would know what a chart was worth.

At the fire she told it it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, the way a woman turns a thing that has been trusted to her and will go on being trusted to her when she is not in the room. “The house will keep it,” she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting something under her breath that no man could take up as a figure, toward no opening: not a door, not a shelf, not a slate turned writing-down, not the space under the rack, nothing that would have asked for a hand on the little-door mark. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark and did not look toward the bed. It was told of her in the yard that she had once sat three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and that such a house has had practice; the story had come from other mouths and never from hers, and she had never once laid it beside anything under this roof, and least of all beside the bed.

The carried fire took its coal under blind ash and the back of the broken pot and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirteen days. The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's note out of \place{the Ilm}, water going over water; through the floor came the kiln's four, four and a gap. Two counts in two places that had not met in a hundred and thirteen days and would not meet tonight. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not become one. Cold came in its known order and the wool rode back from the bare wrist, and the crust lay two inches from his ear where he could smell nothing from it and could not close the distance to it.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and thirteen stood where one hundred and twelve had stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody under the roof, while the river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor, and nothing joined, nothing paid, nothing ended.
