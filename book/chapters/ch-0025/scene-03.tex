\daymark{114}{Seed 25, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the yard had the look of a pot left covered overnight, the cold settled into it and nothing yet lifting the cover. Men came up out of the clay road with their collars high and their shoulders drawn in against what the dark had left of the wind, and crossed \place{the Clay Yard} toward \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} with the quiet step of men taking a stair they would rather not have heard. Their boots went through the ruts without speaking. The low voices came after them and went into \place[the back room]{the room off the wheel-shed} and stayed there, and the door of that room kept its finger's width of open, as it had kept it on other mornings that the boy could not now count without wanting a figure he had no standing to ask for. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay in his basket at the edge of the light, where \person{Sella Vail} had set him, because the edge of the light was a place a basket could be set and no slate lay open upon it. The voices did not carry so far as the wool over his ear. What carried was the small settling sound the cold makes in a house before anybody has given the fire its instruction, the iron and the flagstones taking in the night and giving nothing back.

He had learned to hear a room's temperature in its silence the way another man might read a chart by the line of a note laid down and not opened. A room that holds a low voice is a room shut against the yard, and a door a finger's width open is not the same thing as an open door; it is a door with a decision left standing in the gap. Sella gave the house no name for the men. She did not say the room was cold. A woman who says a room is cold has made a statement about a room, and a statement, in this district, is the first thing an account wants to fasten on; she gave her morning to the fire and to the pot instead, and the two of them asked nothing back. The boy kept the finger's width on a place of its own, exact, complete and worth nothing, and laid no other morning beside it to make a row. Three such rows would have made a cause, and a cause would have wanted a hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one object in this world he had spent two lives declining to become.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door with the morning already worked into him. Fine grey slate dust was rubbed into both forearms past the bend of his left little finger, ground small as meal, not the bone-coloured slip that dries pale on the wheel and comes off in flakes. The dust had gone into the creases the way grit goes into a field after rain, and no washing of the yard would bring it out of there before the day was done. He did not say where he had been, and Sella did not ask; a question about the dust would have been a question about the room, and a question about the room would have been the first line of something written down. The boy kept dusty forearms and clean forearms on one tray as two states of one morning, the way a man keeps two slides under the same lens and refuses the comparison until he has both. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. Those were the two questions the dust stood for, and he had learned to leave a question standing where it was rather than put a hand to it and have the hand go down in the record.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had lain, and the yard went around the space above them as wide as a pail of hot water set down where a child might knock it. Nobody entered the space. Nobody swept beneath the lowest board, and nobody lifted the board to see what was under it, and nobody said what had become of the slates or why they were in pieces. The space above them was not stepped into, and the boy was not carried across it; Sella took the long way around with the basket held level, the little-door burn closing on the handle and opening on nothing, and the keeping of the space was a thing she performed with her feet and never with her mouth. A thing gone around is a thing kept, and a thing kept is not a thing explained. He set the going-around beside the finger's width of open door and the dust on the forearms, three objects in three places, and wrote no cause across the space between them.

Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone, as he had spoken to it a hundred mornings and would speak to it a hundred more. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said. “It cares if they're steady.” He said it to the wheel and not to the yard, in a flat voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, the voice a man uses to an animal that must be steadied before it is worked. The wheel took the house's time as it had taken Garl's before it and would take somebody's after it, and the hollow in the seat kept the leftward lean of twenty years that nobody had filled. Jorra put his weight into the hollow as into a thing he had not yet earned and meant to keep. He set a pot on the bench and said, “You'll do,” with no more feeling than a man who passes an instrument across a table. He knew the full note of a \lore{Vail pot} before it left the wheel --- knew it in the hand and the shoulder and the thin place near the foot, before any knuckle had been laid on it and before it had gone under fire --- and he would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call the knowing early and make something of it. A thing said aloud in this district is a thing that can be entered, and the knowing was the one part of the wheel Jorra had kept out of every account.

There was a sweetish edge on the wind that came up off the glaze road from High Kiln, the taste of lead that had been fired badly somewhere and carried on a bad wind into the yard. The boy knew the taste from other mornings and had no word for it in \lore{Grathic} that any grown person here would have used. He filed the knowing --- that Jorra could hear a pot whole before it left the wheel and would not say it to a kiln-master --- on a tray of its own, beside the bent finger and the dust, and let it lie there. The adult in him recognised the shape of a man protecting one exact skill from men who would turn it into a price; the child in him wanted the breast and wanted the crust two inches from his ear and could not have told either want to a person who would understand the first. Both were true. The second was louder, and neither had a standing in this yard.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire ---” she said, and the rest of it never arrived at him. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the sentence, going in first as the finger always went in first, ahead of the hand and ahead of the word, and the iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave a little of it back warmer, the extremity leading the core in the reverse of the way a body loses heat. He moved the tongue of iron a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The end of her instruction went past him and arrived at the pot, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it: the broom, the bench, the basket at the edge of the light, all coming level at once without anybody giving the order to come level. He had not answered with a word. He had not looked at her. Obedience arriving before the instruction that calls it is obedience and a correction at the same time, and neither of them named which of the two had just gone through the room.

The boy read the iron once more as he had read it on other mornings and as he would read it again: the same bite that holds a vessel whole and can take the hand off a man, mercy and mutilation in one piece of cold iron, with no word in Grathic for both mercies. There was a tourniquet in the room he had lived in before this one, a clamp of iron with the same double nature, and he did not bring it across, because there was no door between the two rooms that any person in this yard could have opened and he had no standing in either. He filed the reading as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln, and kept his finger off the place where it would have joined the two tellings of the shut counting room that had come into this yard from eighteen leagues east. Those two tellings stayed on their own tray, joined to no kiln, and no third telling came this morning.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave the instruction to the broom --- to go around what the sweep had left and around the space beneath the lowest rack and over the flagstones toward the road --- and only after that did his hand close on the handle, as a man takes up a tool already named and not a thing he has chosen. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late one the yard was arguing before the light, agreeing so entirely that the argument had nowhere to put its foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; there was a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in and did not, the held breath from Seed 16 still unspent. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

Yesterday, behind the wheel-keeper's standing finger, his stroke had checked for half a beat on the flagstones. Today it did not. The bristles went on over the stones without a pause and without a turn of the head, out into the yard and toward the road, over the place where the stroke had stopped the day before and past it, going on as though the place were stone like any other stone. The flagstones kept that place all the same. They kept the small interval where a stroke had stopped as a thing nobody would enter: no foot set into it, no broom drawn back across it, no hand pointing it out. The equality under the roof held at the place it had moved to, which was not the place it had held yesterday, and neither man made a word of the difference. Jorra did not say that Tarn had gone on. Tarn did not say that Jorra's finger stood in the iron. Two silences stood under one roof, of one shape, with no hand between them, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled, and the sentence that would call it had not been said in this house.

The boy set the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat on the interval tray and laid no cause across the space between them. One was a place where a stroke should have gone on and did not; the other was a stroke that had gone on; they were two objects on one tray and not a rule. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart, and his was the only hand in the yard that would have been clean enough to lay the cause down and the only one that could not close two inches on the fold at his ear. He did not make the keeping his own. The seventh year lay at the very edge of the sill with six in a mark a thumb had worn almost flat and the next nearly gone and held, the one entry in this house the house could not check; the unforged collector sentence lay beside it and neither was entered. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had lifted in three years. The promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and the boy was carried at the edge of the light with nobody looking toward the bed or into the basket and no foundling turning late at any board.

Jorra's bent finger stood in the cold iron of the damper and Tarn's broom went on over the flagstones without the check, the place where it had stopped yesterday still kept on the stones as a thing nobody would enter. Under the Vail roof the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, giving back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and fourteen days. Sella moved her lips along a row in the other room and gave nobody a figure. The boy was carried at the edge with the two silences laid on one tray and not joined, and the count stood at one hundred and fourteen, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, while the men's low voices stayed in the room with its finger's width of open and the yard went on around the space beneath the rack. Nothing moved again. Nothing was paid. Nothing ended.
