## Chronicle

On the twenty-fourth of Seed the last tend stood with the seed up and no figure for the hands that set it; a man brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back in his sleeve, so the figure arrived nowhere. Sella Vail gave the bucket, broom, pot and fire their instructions before anybody and never the basket, and sent a woman to ask the pot what a wet thing costs, because *the house has said it*. Another neighbour reached past a bare board and in one breath called the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the child's ear a child's appetite, never seeing she had laid two rooms under one cause. The boy kept the three places apart.

On the twenty-fifth men came before first grey into the finger's-width-open room off the wheel-shed, and Jorra Vail gave them no name. He came through with grey slate dust in both forearms past the bent finger, spoke to the wheel before anyone, and put that finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, so the household straightened around his silence. The change was held and not repeated: Tarn's broom went on over the flagstones without the half-check of the day before, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped. Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates on the bench before she turned to the fold, crossed two fingers at the birth-flower unknowing and covered the sign with a dead woman's date; *warm and breathing* was exact and not true, and her sixth page stayed face-down.

On the twenty-sixth, at the Fishstairs, Mira Silt answered whether the run would be thin --- *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* --- and put her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a buyer named a figure; a shift of a creel showed a second cord twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted. The clerk sat at the landing for a measure nobody had called. At the Cross a soft-handed man from Slate Row handed a figure to a slate held at the edge, and rested his thumb on the corner afterward.

On his bed at the going of the light the boy turned out his file and found it unchanged: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word forty unspoken, four low things with the river's note a fifth, the shut-room tellings joined to no kiln, the damper's double bite, the loan uncalled. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and said the house would keep it. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. One hundred and fifteen stood unspent and spoken by nobody; nothing was joined, paid, named or opened, and nothing ended.
