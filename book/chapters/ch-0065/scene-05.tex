\daymark{248}{Smoke 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours came on without a stroke drawn, the way a coal comes up through its own ash, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in the room that does not touch the room of the fire, with the fold at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from it. Two hundred and forty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and sixty-eight days told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name. He had ceased to expect a figure spoken over any of it, which was not the same as not wanting one, and the wanting went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under ash. The morning had not earned its grey and there was no bell under the roof, no market and no \place{the Cross}, for a Ninthday has no reading; Smoke 10 (Day 249), the first Account-day and market of the second firing, stood one morning ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

The first smell was hot clay under tallow, and under that wet wool, and under that the sour mineral breath of \lore{Ash} in the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours under the floor, with the gap where the fifth pull should have stood, and it was not a bell. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}; under the floor the fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing them would want a payer put under all three and no payer stood to any. He left the trays where they lay. It was a discipline and not a belief; a discipline is a thing a hand uses, and his could go up an inch and no further.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness entered in a figure is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as though the line it followed had been written out to that length and no longer; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the face a man wears going cold and the face he wears when warmed too fast, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew the warming must come from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it. He wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child's hunger keeps no hours while the register goes on being exact inside a hungry body. The surgeon turned out his file as instruments on a towel and found them exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and entered neither fact anywhere.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at \person{Sella Vail}'s throat and she did not touch it in the small hours, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; a shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon and a dusk are none of them openings. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and she did not look at it again, and no lesson was made of it and no payer named. The fifth asking of the day before stood on its seventh place with five askings and no witness, and he did not bring it down among the hearings or the sayings or the count to make a row look fuller. Two hundred and forty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and sixty-eight days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven.

\scenebreak

In the morning she went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she went every morning, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. She told it it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot at \place{the kiln mouth}, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting-go — a count she had never offered to any ear under this roof, and pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak showed pale off the coals. The burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. The fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument.

At \place{the drying racks} the dressed grain came out from under its wool for damp. She put her fingers into it to the knuckle, felt the damp that had gathered overnight, spread it thin on the flat stone to finish, turned it with the back of a hand and gathered it back by the handful, and set the fold flat again. It changed state and gained no figure. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by the arm that carried it, and the arm knew weight and not cost, which was the whole of what it was asked to know; nothing went on any slate and the weight was not laid across any of the absences the yard went around. A thing covered in wool is a thing kept, and a thing kept is not a thing entered, and the figure that would be read over it come Smoke 10 would not know what it had cost.

\scenebreak

Before the morning was well into the yard a neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. Sella did not price it, refuse it or enter it. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, she said, and the woman went off level with the question still on her and nothing slopped, carrying it away as she had carried the last one, unanswered and ordinary. A man came down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt and brought a number up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it as though to read what was written on the inside of his own hand, and put it back into his sleeve; so the figure arrived nowhere and was not a missing figure, for a sleeve is not a slate and a thing not priced is not therefore free but a thing whose payer has not been named. \place{the trough} stood bare and dark beside the well with its water called weather.

The figure read over the covered grain on Grain 30 was still not the figure paid, and the true subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, going up and down the clay road and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Two slates stood beside each other in the file with no writing laid between them: the told figure and the paid figure, the heard bell and the told bell. A figure that goes past a household twice in one morning and stops at neither has no signature on it, and the boy left the space between them apart and did not average it, because an average is an account that has found a payer and none stood to either.

\scenebreak

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and did not look into it. She moved her lips along the row standing with two gaps and gave nobody a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing and came away; she did not touch the jar or say what it held. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; a mark nobody reads is still a mark kept, and a mark read wants a figure put under it, and a figure wants a payer. She set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting that was not a look toward the bed and was not a look away from it either. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance, and the hand could not close it, and the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered.

In \place{the Clay Yard} the work went around the places it always went around. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the ridge of Ash had turned, and the finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, and neither was swept; there was the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, and the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word; there was the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back. Each got a look and a wide step and no number, and none was entered. The boards fronting the shelf kept their four nails, four and a thumb-width knot, and the yard gave them no figure and took none from them. Above, \place{the terrace} went up toward \place{High Kiln} in the dry light, and the clay road went up it and came down it without bringing a collector and without carrying a figure off the yard, because no collector came up or went down on Smoke 7, 8 or 9 and the debt against the firing of tiles lay where it lay.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf, and nobody straightened it or took it down. The wheel-seat kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and had not been filled. The ridge of Ash had turned the corner and become the clean dark line, and beside it the finer hairline of grey had gathered, called the thaw, and both were left unswept — not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack, and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-eight days. The keeping of him was maintenance; nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would. Somewhere at the kindling a broom was given its instruction before a hand took it up, and somewhere at the wheel a pot was told it would do, and both came to the boy as the ordinary directions of the house — a sound with a place and no person attached to it — and he did not turn toward either.

The last working morning settled into its own small noise the way a coal settles under ash, with nobody turning it. The fifth asking of the day before had not become an answer; it stood on its seventh place with a finger between it and every other keeping, and no witness had stood anywhere to be asked. The grain under wool had not been given a figure, and the figure that Smoke 10 would read over it would not know what it had cost. The boy was where he had been for two hundred and forty-eight days: exact, cold, hungry, worth nothing at two inches of air, and still keeping the tray. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the order of the places. Neither stood up and said what the other was, and he wrote no cause across the space between them, because a cause is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to either.

He put the question to his hand the fifth way of the chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed. He kept it there through the small intake, the hold and the letting-go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, and kept it through the little silence after her count had ended. He did not bring the river's own interval into the inch; he let one rhythm run across the borrowed silence and did not cross a second against it, for one rhythm held across a silence is not two rhythms crossed, and the two-rhythm coordination of the day before was remembered and was not a change made again. Then he brought the hand down on the river's next low note.

It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh of it, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule, and not a sixth asking of the question over the boards. He filed answer seventy-one on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. Seventy-one answers stood there now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and the fifth asking kept its five with nobody to own them. The file lay open on the towel. The hand came down on the river's next low note after the little silence following Sella's count, and answer seventy-one lay on the sixth place while Smoke 10 stood one morning ahead, unbrought.
