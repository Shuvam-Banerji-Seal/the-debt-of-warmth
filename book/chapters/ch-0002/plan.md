## Scene 1 — The House of the Breath (drop capital)

**Day and time.** Day 2, Stone 8, first light in the Vail kiln-house. The one scene away from Corin: he is three doors down and still under the cold stone in House Silt; the reader is inside the thing whose breath he has been counting through the wall. Make the change of room felt — no kiln-warmed domesticity here, only a house holding itself together around an absence.

**Place.** The Vail kiln-house and its wheel-shed, in the Clay Yard. Objects first, people second.

**Present.** \person{Sella Vail}, \person{Jorra Vail}, \person{Tarn}. \person{Garl Vail} is present only as the shape his objects keep and as proverbs in the house's voice. Corin is not on stage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the breath — the low intake, the pause, the long letting-out — now heard from *inside* the kiln rather than through a wall. Connect it to the count without naming a connection: this is the breath Dan counted four, four and a gap. Do not say the boy is listening; he is not here.
2. Know the house by its objects before its people: the wheel-seat worn hollow on the left where Garl sat twenty years, which Sella will not let anyone fill; the salt-stained coat on the peg behind the door, which Jorra has twice reached for in the dark and stopped; the Ash ridge along the skirting board, thicker than in any other house in the district, which nobody will sweep before the Last Day. Garl's proverb in the house's voice: *Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.*
3. Tarn is already sweeping when anyone wakes. He calls Sella *house*, says *I owe* where another man would say *I am*, is six years into a seven-year term and has counted every one. He pockets the small bench knife when he thinks nobody is looking and then says, of his own accord, that he took it and put it back and they can check. His deference has a nail in it. He can hear the full note of a pot and has told nobody — show the ear in the way his sweeping pauses at the sound of a pot being set down, without pointing.
4. Jorra stands at the wheel with hands like a gate hinge; slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms, the permanent bend in the left little finger. He never looks at Sella while obeying her instantly. He speaks to the wheel and the pots first, and only then to people. His short declaratives. He has not decided whether he is with the Broken Slates and will not say.
5. Sella touches the widow's mark at her throat — a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it — before she opens the kiln, and at no other time. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, gives instruction to the pot and the fire, never a finished feeling, and gets quieter when she is angry. She will not let anyone fill Garl's seat or take down his coat.
6. The unspoken business of the house: a foundling with no house-name is to be brought under the roof on the morrow, the third day. No one says what will be paid or why. The warm stone and the barley crust have already crossed this threshold from this fire into House Silt (T074), neither entered. Let the reader be able to name who paid.
7. The line: when Sella says the boy will be brought under the roof, Jorra says *he's not Vail.* Sella does not answer it. She says instruction to the pot instead. The words hang as a form of argument in a house where a thing said is a thing entered.

**Emotional turn.** From the cold order of a house living around an absence to a grievance spoken aloud and refused an answer — the first crack between mother and son, and the foundling is the thing they will not name in the same room.

**Sensory anchors.** Banked coals and ash; woodsmoke and wet wool; the sour mineral tang of the Ash ridge; the hollow of the wheel-seat; salt stiff in a coat; the low intake and long letting-out of the kiln; clay dust on a broom.

**Threads advanced.** T009 (Garl uncollected; the Ash ridge; the proverb about the crack), T031 (the charred slate seeded in the kiln's ash — mention the charred edge lying where a slate was pushed into the fire, do not examine it), T035 (Jorra's *he's not Vail*; his broken slates are not shown, only the temper that keeps them), T036 (Tarn's knife and ear), T057 (the house's own roll is spoken around, never shown), T074 (the stone and bread from this hearth).

## Scene 2 — The Cup Across the Room (drop capital if Scene 1 does not carry it)

**Present.** \person{Corin} (Dan, silent), \person{Alda Reed}, \person{Dessa Silt} on her pallet.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Resume with the body's humiliations: the door will not shut, the grey wool will not stay on his shoulder, turning his head past a point produces the small animal sound. The stone is cold at his belly and nobody thinks to move it. He is two days old and has been awake most of them. The dry joke lives in the exactness — a trauma surgeon who cannot keep a blanket on — and costs something.

## Scene 3 — What the Room Is Arranging

**Day and time.** Day 2, Stone 8, later — sleet turning to a low grey afternoon; the kiln breathing through the wall at measured intervals.

**Place.** The birth room and the threshold of House Silt; the Clay Yard beyond the door that will not shut.

**Present.** \person{Corin}, \person{Alda Reed}, \person{Dessa Silt}. \person{Mira Silt} does not appear in this chapter (she is held for Chapter 5 per `plan/next.md`); the brown-edged crust she gave on Day 1 is still the last warmth that crossed this room from Mira's hands.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The second day wears on: sleet against the oiled-paper window, the Ilm under ice, the Five spoken of as three days off in Low Kiln, which says three days when it has counted what it has and does not want to say it. Dan counts the kiln's breaths through the wall — four, four and the gap where the fifth should be — because there is nothing else to count. The gap, not the breaths, is what he notices.
2. Dessa's cough comes up from the pits; she calls it weather. She rubs the pale sieve-wire scar while deciding how much to say, gets specific about loaves and steps and the day of the month, and will not name the child — the little hot one, the flower. Her pulse comes up when the counting stops. A body can be warm and still be gone, said in proverb form so it can be taken as advice.
3. Alda answers with an exact history nobody asked for and returns to the weather, which in Low Kiln is never the subject. The tin is touched once inside the bodice. She keeps the slate flat so no writing shows.
4. The room's unasked question — who pays to keep a foundling with no house-name — sits in the space nobody fills. Dan reads the arrangement the way he once read a trauma bay at four in the morning: the senior stops giving orders, the nurse stops putting instruments down, and a decision made above them comes down. He is the thing being sorted, and he cannot lift a hand to take part in it.
5. Note the arithmetic of the household without naming it: three of four loaves to the levy, a bed and a warmth for a foundling no one has agreed to pay for, the slip to be paid for in the thaw. Somebody will come on the third day. The room has said it without a word.
6. Close on the door that will not shut with the third day beyond it, sleet coming in at the gap, the cold stone at his belly.

**Emotional turn.** From the small irritations of a humiliated body to the cold suspicion that the adults are not merely ignoring him but arranging something around him — and that the arrangement is a kind of kindness he has no words to refuse.

**Sensory anchors.** Sleet and clay dust; the sour edge of the Ash ridge; the low rhythm of the kiln through the wall; the rasp of Dessa's cough; the sieve-wire scar under her thumb; the brown-edged barley crust already eaten or set aside; the door lifting at the latch.

## Scene 4 — Nineteen Steps Up the Yard

**Day and time.** Day 3, Stone 9, Ninthday, morning. The icy yard, the kiln already breathing.

**Place.** The threshold of House Silt, the length of the Clay Yard, the ground in front of the Vail kiln-house; the Slip Brook running behind the houses.

**Present.** \person{Sella Vail}, \person{Corin} in the covered bundle, \person{Dessa Silt} at the end of the yard, \person{Alda Reed} behind with the lie.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The hand that hovered on Day 1 now takes. Sella comes in ahead of her smell — woodsmoke, wet wool, the sour tang of the Ash — and lifts the covered bundle. Dan reads the grip as a surgeon estimates mass and care: the pressure through cloth, the little door of the burn closing on nothing as it takes the weight, the fingers that know just how much a thing will bear. She says *the boy*, not *my son*, and *the house*, not *I*. She does not look at Dessa.
2. Carried in the bundle up the Clay Yard, Dan counts her steps because there is nothing else to count — different from Dessa's nineteen, a new count laid over the old — and reads what the bundle lets him read: the woodsmoke thickening toward the kiln, the Slip Brook behind the houses, the breath of the Vail fire growing closer, the ice on the Ilm out beyond. He cannot turn his head past a point; the small animal sound comes; he cannot ask where they are going. The head goes where it wants and the body humiliates him one last time before he leaves this house.
3. Dessa stands at the end of the yard. She counts nineteen steps and does not follow. Her pulse is in her throat, the thumb on the pale scar, the door-shut smile. She says nothing that would make the leaving a thing that must be answered. She will believe he went in the levy for three years.
4. As the bundle goes away, Alda's voice comes from behind with the lie: the boy has been entered to a house, and when Dessa asks which, the boy went in the levy, among the children taken for the account, and a mother with a thin return would only draw attention to a slate better left alone. The figure is never spoken — never the word forty — but it lives in the pressure of Dessa's thumb on the scar and in Alda's exact, unasked-for dates. The lie is told as mercy, and must not be exposed by the narration.
5. Dan hears his own life rewritten as a kindness and cannot correct it. He holds the cold stone's absence at his belly and the count she is making with her steps, and understands, with the part of him that was a surgeon, that a woman has just disposed of a child the way an institution disposes of an account: because the truth would make a scene, and the scene would cost more than the lie. He is the thing being accounted and has no voice in it.
6. Close on the yard behind him: Dessa's nineteen steps not taken, the thumb on the scar, Alda's exact dates settling over the space where a name would have been.

**Emotional turn.** From the physical fact of being carried to the knowledge that he is being *given up* by a mother who loves him and told he is dead by a woman who is lying to keep him safe — mercy as erasure, the first betrayal and the pattern of the book.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and wet wool arriving ahead of Sella; the little-door burn closing on cloth; sleet on clay dust; the Slip Brook's clay-coloured water; the cold of the yard against the bundle; the low intake of the kiln growing nearer; Dessa's pulse in her throat.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 3

The chapter closes with the boy installed under the Vail roof, warmer and more watched than when the day began: a bed given, a hand swept, a knife put back, a coat left on its peg, a charred edge of slate in the kiln's ash. He has been taken in and cannot say so; Alda has told Dessa he went in the levy and the number has not been spoken; the flat note of a cup is in his ear and he cannot reach the cup. The market and the Cross are still ahead, the Five spoken of as three days off. Chapter 3 ("One in Twelve," Day 4, Stone 10 — Account-day and the last market before the Five) moves him out of the house and into the district's accounting: Berr Clay wipes his thumb on his apron before he touches the slate and says *one in twelve, as the notice says*, and Dan, in a basket at the Cross, hears the note of Berr's slate as a flat thing — the same flat note he first heard from a cup across a birth room, now carried into the market where the whole district's account is read aloud. The movement is from a household that keeps its own roll to a district that reads its account in public, with the boy who hears the flat note sitting in the middle of it and unable to say a word.
