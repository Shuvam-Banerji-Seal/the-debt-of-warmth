Down into \place{the Clay Yard} she carried him in the afternoon, at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she told the fold, as she had told it on the first of the month and would tell it on the last, for the wind had never once asked anybody to be told anything and had no intention of beginning now. \person[Corin]{The boy} felt the small jolts of her walking come up through the straw into his ribs, and took the yard as a surgeon takes a field before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder through the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle and bring it within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, as it had gone on through all three days of the dark month.

\place{Ash} was going out the way it had come on, without a boundary drawn across it. In the morning there had been no mark set at its beginning and in the afternoon there would be no mark set at its end; the older women named it by a weather that had not done what it promised and the slatemen by their count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words. The month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse. Three hundred and thirty-two stood exact behind him, six months and one hundred and fifty-two days, told by nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}; under that roof \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-two days, and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept on its peg was unspoken the same number. Nothing from the first or second of the month crossed the yard this afternoon as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment. The opening at the kiln mouth stood where it stood, behind the wall, and nobody had named it, and six openings in six months were six and not a rule, and no seventh was made due on a day that had not earned one.

She went around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept, and the finer grey hairline beside it that the yard called \lore{the thaw} lay unswept beside it; the basket went wide of both. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where no broom went and no hand entered. On the stone lip of \place{the yard well} the round ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. The water in \place{the trough} had a skin on it from edge to edge. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field kept its empty place, and in the returning line the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out was gone around with a look and a wide step and no figure spoken over the going. Seven places. No eighth likeness stood up among them as a row, and no cause was laid across any two, for a cause wants a payer put under it and none stood.

Three coverings at the well stayed weather and were entered nowhere. The white that frost-bloom had written on the well's north lip on Bone 3 had gone while the yard looked elsewhere, and its going was not a sign and was not brought into the dark month. The skin on the trough broke under a pail's knock and closed again over the water without a word spoken to it. The plate of ice clouded with silt stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, where she had set it on Frost 9 and told the weather where to stand. There was a finger between every pair. The boy read the three as he would read a covering laid over a depth, then the same covering over another depth, then a piece of the depth lifted and set where no foot goes; a fourth likeness would have stood up as a rule, and a rule wanted a payer, and none stood. He kept them where they were.

The afternoon went about its ordinary work around him. Women put pots down and took them up; a man with a wet pot was stopped out of the draught and set where the heat came to him sideways; somewhere a board knocked and was not mended today. Nobody made a feast of the first firing and nobody crossed himself over the coals that had gone under a broken pot two mornings ago. The boy took the ordinariness as the only honest record there was. A thing said aloud became a thing the Court could enter, and so the yard had no word for what had happened at the kiln mouth, and the boy had no slate; he kept both without making either mean, and the keeping bought nothing at two inches of air.

A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} with a hare across his shoulders shifted the weight on his neck when a woman with a pot asked what the carrying had cost. “Ask the weather,” he said. “It carried the snow. It'll carry the price.” He went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare still across him and nobody weighing it and nobody opening a slate. Another man with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the mending of the month would want and was told to ask the pot; and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came. Two men mending a strip of fence straightened where they squatted, one with a nail held in his teeth and one with the hammer down on his knee. “Early,” said the one with the nail, and the other said, “Late. It'll be late.” \person{Tarn}, going past with the broom, gave each of them \emph{Yes, house}, and kept the late. He agreed so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot; a man who wanted the thaw early and a man who wanted it late had both been answered, and the answer cost nobody anything, and the late suited him because a late thaw puts off the paying. No collector came up the clay road or went down it in the afternoon, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name. These were the month's hunts and repairs, directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never figures. The boy took them apart by what was there and by what was not, and filed the blanks where a number should have stood and went on. A chart left blank where the number should stand was a chart still, and he entered neither the hare, nor the jar, nor the price, nor the seventh year in it.

At \place{the kindling} Tarn kept his six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it, beside a collector sentence that had never been forged and lay there without a name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and without a figure on the debt. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where a man's hand could check it and where a man's hand could also slip it off, and told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. The announcement went to nobody, because the yard had already been told. When the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up in him it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach on Seed 16 and did not spend it, and his chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back, and he lifted the wrist a moment toward the grey as though to see whether the light had come to anything. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot, unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

The thumb's resting on the uncut seventh on Bone 26 was one object on its own place, and he checked it and did not make it again on the first, the second or the third of \place{Ash}. It was not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not a payment, not a fifth held change, not a hearing, not a near-full answer, not a mending and not a rule, and no payer stood under it. His thumb went back down to the six where the house could check, and the knife lay where it lay, at the very edge. No collector came up the clay road or went down it, and the loan that could hear stayed worth more than the debt that held it and was not called.

At the shed's edge \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases where a fist had opened; it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said to the wheel, and to a pot \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not say it before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make a song of it.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot. One piece of iron had two bites in it, the holding and the taking, and there was no word in the district's speech for both mercies, so the house had made do with one word and gone on. He did not look toward the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of a name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year hollow that nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey unswept, the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person, so that repair stayed apart from restoration. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under the roof three hundred and thirty-two days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a lesson; nobody stood up and made a doctrine of it and nobody paid it.

The boy checked the whole tray and left it as it lay. The burned slate stood at five with no sixth, no witness standing anywhere to be asked and no hand at the flame; five tellings were five and not five witnesses to one fire, and if a sixth had come that afternoon it would have wanted a payer and none stood. In the small hours, with the yard quiet and the river's note at its lowest under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it. The infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform.
