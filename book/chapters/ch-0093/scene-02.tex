In the afternoon \person{Sella Vail} carried him down into \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the way a woman carries a pot filled to the lip that must not be slopped. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. \place{Ash} had come on the way a dark room comes on, without a boundary drawn across it. The dark was named and not explained. In the first Ash cold the rope at the well stiffened in a working hand, the skin on the trough lay longer before it broke, and the coals were banked earlier at both ends of the day, and the yard called all of it weather and gave nobody a figure. A man come down from \place{Cold Acre} with a hare across his shoulders shifted the weight on his neck when a woman with a pot asked what the carrying had cost. “Ask the weather,” he said. “It carried the snow. The boy kept the two ears under one roof in their old equality and did not join the debt's stopped sentence to the handful at the rack end or to any of his own gaps. He had spent a life watching institutions make one account out of several hands and had signed one of those accounts himself, and the small exact keeping of things apart was the nearest honest work he had left.
