## Chronicle

Ash came on as a dark room comes on, without a boundary drawn, and neither habit of naming its months was set right. On the first morning the boy came into his three hundred and thirtieth day, taking the room as a surgeon takes a field, by the small sounds first. One crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed, the thread at his mouth frozen, unwiped, cold stopped at cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint with the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. Sella Vail went to the fire before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would have said I. In the yard she went wide of seven places with a look and a step and no number.

On the second morning the kilns were brought up for the dark month's first firing. At the kiln mouth, before any instruction went to the basket and before a word, Sella stood over him with her hands still and put her left hand to the widow's mark at her throat, the cord oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times. She held it one breath, let go, lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot. The fire took, answering four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Six openings in six months were six and not a rule; nobody entered a slate and no seventh was made due. He made no lesson or payment of it, noting only that the fifth pull was still missing.

The day after was an ordinary tending day. At the well Ilva told a woman what Ash had cost: *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, her hand flat on the sleeve where the strip lay unread. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside a collector sentence never forged; Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence.

On the third, Ash went out as it had come on, without a boundary drawn. Each morning and close the hand went up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake and let it go on the same low note, coming down as a man puts away a tool. Six answers were entered, two hundred and thirty-five through two hundred and forty, each worth nothing at two inches of air. At the close three hundred and thirty-two stood exact and spoken by nobody, six months and one hundred and fifty-two days told by nobody, the carried fire's fifth unpaid and Garl Vail's name unspoken the same number. Nothing had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended. The file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door.
