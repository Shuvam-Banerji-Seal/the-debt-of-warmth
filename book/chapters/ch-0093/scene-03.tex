\daymark{331}{Ash 2, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

The second came on in the dark the way the first had come on, without a boundary drawn across it, and nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} told the boy what had changed between one sleep and the next. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on his back at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and took the small hours in as a field is taken before the light, by the sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. Three hundred and thirty had stood exact at the going of the light; three hundred and thirty-one stood now, six months and one hundred and fifty-one days, told by nobody, exact, complete, unspent, and of no use to anybody who might have wanted it. Under the board at his back \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, and that silence had stood three hundred and thirty-one days with no payer named under it. \person{Garl Vail} stayed where he had been kept, in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on the peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in the shed keeping the hollow twenty years of another man's weight had worn into it and that nobody filled, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, the twice-mended pot with both proverbs gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person. His name had not been spoken under this roof three hundred and thirty-one days, and the debt against a firing of tiles was neither paid nor settled nor done with and was worse for being uncalled. Nothing that had happened on the first of \place{Ash} came across the night as sign, lesson, mending, cause or payment; the yard had gone around its own work and come back to the same room, and the room kept no figure for it.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond the stopping, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, the ease cold puts on a face that is no ease, and he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth, the thread wiped away, and a hand that would close on the pot handle and bring it within reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won. The argument went on under the wool without a verdict, the surgeon taking the inventory in its old order and the infant underneath wanting what the inventory could not give, the register reading the room as it had once read a trauma bay, by the small sounds first and the gap in them second. He had reduced a thousand shoulders in the dark and could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him, and the joke of it lived in the exactness and not in the telling; nobody under the roof had ever heard it and he had never once said it aloud.

The kilns had been brought up in the night for the dark month's first firing. Even under the roof the house smelled of it: the hot dry smell of clay giving up its water, tallow under it from \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and, on a wrong wind that came over \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere, the sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze}. The wind crossed the boards and touched the fold and went out the way it had come, a direction and not a second edge paid; a cause would have wanted a payer standing under it and none stood, so the sweetness went on being the sweetness and the yard gave it no figure. In the small hours \person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket, as she went every morning, and told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said I, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening and figuring neither gap. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and gave neither gap a number; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air.

When the first grey came into the yard she carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, the way a woman carries a pot filled to the lip that must not be slopped. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she told the fold, and told the wind nothing, for the wind in this district had never once asked anybody to be told anything. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. At \place{the kiln mouth} she set the basket down level on \place{the flagstones} where no slate lay, a little out of the draught, so that the heat of the opening came to him sideways and not full, the way warmth comes to a thing set beside a fire and not into it. She did not look toward his face. Around them the yard had begun the first firing of \place{Ash}: women putting pots down with the practised set-down of hands that knew what the clay would bear, a man with a wet pot held a little out of the draught while he waited for the yard to tell him where to set it. Nobody made a feast of the firing and nobody crossed himself. It was an ordinary working morning and the yard went about it with no figure spoken over any of it.

Before she gave any instruction to the basket and before any word was spoken, she stood over him a moment with her hands still. The boy felt the stillness come down through the straw. It was not the stillness of a woman deciding anything; it was the stillness of a hand held over a field before the dressing goes on, and it had no more announcement in it than that. The thumb that went down to the cord was the same thumb that had lifted the pot an hour before and would lift it again an hour after, and it knew the weight of the clay the way a working hand knows the weight of its own tool. It was not asking the mark anything and it was not telling it anything, and the holding had no more in it than the one breath it took. Then her left hand went to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat. It was a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, the cord oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, the little-door burn on the back of her hand half-closing on the cord as the fingers took hold. She held the mark one breath and let go. The hand went away from her throat and down to the lid, and the stillness went out of her as completely as it had come, so that a person watching only her hands would have seen one holding and one letting-go and nothing between them that could be entered.

She lifted the clay lid with the back of a hand --- not the fingers, which had been at the mark, but the back, as though the lid were a thing to be turned and not a thing to be grasped --- and tipped the carried coal under the back of a broken pot. The fire took. It answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and thirty-one days, and the heat came up behind the wall the way the heat of any firing came up, giving and taking with the same hand. Six openings in six months stood behind this morning: Tallow 1, Ember 1, Smoke 1, Frost 2, Bone 2 and Ash 2. Six were six and not a rule. No seventh was made due; nobody praised the opening, named what it mended, entered a slate or made a lesson, sign, mending, cause or payer of it; the five behind were not looked at again as signs or payments, because a rule made from five would have wanted a payer and none stood. When she carried him back under the roof she did not touch the mark at the shelf, the well, the rack, the road, the basket, the yard, the morning, the afternoon, the dusk or the going of the light. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and that was the whole of what the house knew about it.

The boy took the opening as a field is taken before the light, by the small sounds and not by the large: the little scrape of the clay lid lifted with the back of a hand, the dry rasp of coal going under the back of a broken pot, the fire taking in fours with the gap where the fifth should have stood. He did not make a sign of it, or a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. Five openings in five months had been five and not a rule, and he had kept them on five places with a finger between; this one went on a sixth place with the same finger's width around it, and no seventh place was made ready for it and nobody stood who could have called it. He did not join it to the five openings that had gone before, or to the six arithmetics locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up \place{the clay road}, or to the gap the bell had left in the square, or to the dressed grain covered under wool, or to his own gaps. There was one thing he did note, and it was not a joining: the fifth pull was still missing. It had been missing before the lid went up and it was missing after the fire took, and the coal had not supplied it and nobody had supplied it, so the carried fire's fifth stood unpaid the same number of days as the name under the roof that nobody spoke. No collector came up the clay road or went down it, and the yard's ordinary work went on around the new fire without a word spoken over either.

A woman putting a pot down asked what the month's first firing would want when the account came, and the nearest rack-hand told her to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. The man with the wet pot asked whether the draught would take it, and was told to set it where the others stood, which was a direction and not a figure. These were the month's hunts and repairs, answers judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never numbers; the boy took them apart the way he took apart a voice over the boards, by what was there and by what was not, and filed the blank where the number should have stood and went on. A chart left blank where the number should stand is a chart still, and he entered neither the firing nor the pot nor the price in it.

When the firing had taken and the yard had gone back around its morning, Sella carried him up at the half-pace and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof, never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. She went to \place{the hearth} and the grammar took up again exactly where it had stood, the way a man takes up a tool he laid down the night before. \emph{The house will keep it}. The coals were told the thaw and turned with the back of a broken pot; a small intake, a hold and a letting-go went toward no opening and neither gap was figured. She lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, said both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and left the crack showing; the hairline of the original break stood between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire, and the boy read it the way he read any wound closed in a place the hand would not have chosen, by what the hand had done and by what it had left, repair one thing and restoration another. The rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The dressed grain lay turned under wool with a lighter touch and no figure and no handful, her fingers going in and coming out without closing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and the grey streak at her temple showed pale, and she still did not look toward the bed. There was no celebration of the opening and no change in any of this. The house kept the same shape it had kept on the first of Ash, and the boy took the sameness in as the only honest record there was.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it; the sixth was still the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the things that had come out of vessels. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; through the finger's-width door \lore{water over water} went low over lower, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and was not numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between. Six arithmetics of the year rode locked up the clay road in Berr Clay's box, one in twelve unchallenged, the figure read still not the figure paid, six readings six and not one account; the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it and was not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain covered under wool, the four held changes, the handful, the thumb's resting, the two small objects of the hand or his own gaps. The edges came near to touching. The finger went between.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every object on its own place and every gap left gap. In the small hours, with the yard quiet and the river's note at its lowest under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early again nor stayed, and the hand did not stay. The inch was not a reach and the intake was not a breath kept for warmth; the interval was not a stopping of the river and the note was not his. He filed it as answer two hundred and thirty-seven: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the six arithmetics, the bell's gap, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the stayed object, the early letting-go, the sixth opening nor his own kept gaps. Two hundred and thirty-seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that had made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field left as found and in an answer entered where nobody could see it. The infant underneath took none of it. One stiff brown-edged crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread lay frozen and unwiped; cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the wrist-joint as at the end of its line and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two registers quarrelled without a verdict in the small hours under the roof. No comfort was given and none was taken. Three hundred and thirty-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and fifty-one days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and the name of the man the salt-stiff coat kept was unspoken the same number. Nothing on the second had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended except the one opening at the kiln mouth, which changed the house's count and changed nothing anybody would enter. The boy's exact noticing was the only record there was, and it was worth nothing at two inches of air and was not for sale. Behind the wall the fire still answered four pulls and a silence; the widow's mark hung back under the working thumb and was not touched again; the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap; and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door while \place{Ash} 2 stood one morning short of \place{Ash} 3 and was not brought toward it.
