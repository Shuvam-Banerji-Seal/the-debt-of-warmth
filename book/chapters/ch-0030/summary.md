## Chronicle

On the first Account-day of Reed the square leaned over its reading block like a pot with the lid not quite on. Berr Clay came up short, his cord folded under his coat and green at one mouth-corner; he wiped his thumb twice, read *one in twelve, as the notice says*, called the levy and said it was not called by him. A man put his fist back in his sleeve. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. Below the bridge the soft-cuffed clerk held his slate for a measure nobody called. One hundred and twenty-nine days old, the boy kept the figure read aloud on a place of its own, a chart closed over a note nobody had opened.

Sella set him low at the Vail kiln-house where the light made a roof of the loaf shelf. On its underside lay short charcoal marks in clusters, some dark, some faint: a keeping put where nobody thought to look. He did not read or count them or lay them beside the two gaps or the crust in the other room. A neighbour who asked what the row would want was sent to the pot with *the house has said it*.

Before first grey men spoke low in the back room and Jorra Vail came out with grey slate dust past his bent finger; he spoke to the wheel before anyone, told a pot *You'll do*, withheld its note from a kiln-master and put his finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's sentence. Tarn took the broom only after it was instructed and wanted the late thaw because it puts off the paying. That night the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt came a third time to the word that would call the loan and stopped with no figure and no name; the boy laid it on his tray as an object and not a rule. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool.

At the Weir a reed-cutter tied one knot with his teeth and put his cord back inside his coat: *You can ask the cord* --- *No more can the Court*. Orra Weir gave a date and Mira Silt kept her thumb on the grey cord. Alda Reed covered a birth-flower with a dead woman's date and said *Warm and breathing*, exact and not true. Sella reached a finger beneath the loaf shelf and brought it away without looking. At dusk she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, left the thread frozen and told the fire it was the thaw. The river's note came through the door, the fire missed its fifth breath, and the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot: three counts, none become one. One hundred and thirty-one days old, he was told by nobody; his hand went up an inch toward the water and came down, the count unspent.
