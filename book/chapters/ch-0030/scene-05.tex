The afternoon went downriver with them the way a coal goes down in a banked fire --- slowly, and giving up its heat by degrees. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} off the clay road and down the way to \place{the Fishstairs} below \place{the north bridge} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around a board set out to dry as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the running water and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, going under the fold where nobody had been told to put a hand. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and no instruction had ever been given about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put, stiff and doing nothing. \place{Low Kiln} thinned behind them into wet wool and tallow smoke; ahead, salt stood white on the posts where the flood had touched them and gone away, and under the noise of the stalls ran the low note of \place{the Ilm}, a fifth low thing he had kept on a place of its own since he was six days old.

She set the basket at the very end of \place{the fish-trade shade} where no slate lay, and stood a half-pace off from it, the little-door burn across the back of her hand closing on nothing. Eel slime had dried in long grey strokes on the boards and on the sleeves of the women at the outer boards, and tallow smoke from \place{the Eel House} lay low over the catch, so that the shade smelled of salt and river mud and something cooking that nobody in it was hungry for. The steps came down four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line, and a whole market went over the landing carefully without making a fall of it. A girl with a creel took it sideways with one hand out to the rail and went on selling without looking at her feet; nobody made an accident of it, and nobody made a step of it either. He lay at the low angle where the shade became a roof and the underside of things became the parts that mattered, and watched what the market did with its hands.

Below the bridge, at \place{the out-of-line landing}, the soft-cuffed clerk was still set for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, the grey of a pot that has been scoured and set back over the fire; his slate lay against his forearm with the writing turned where nobody else could see it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, every time, the way a man passes a finger over a seam before he trusts it and looks at the cloth afterward. He had not been taken off since the month turned, or if he had been taken off somebody had put him back in the dark. A buyer in a coat with the river still in its hem named a figure at the eel boards, and the clerk's hand went to the slate before his eyes went up to find the man who had said it, so the figure was entered before it was looked at and the looking came too late to change anything. The market sold its reeds and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and did not lower its voice. A man shifting a creel went around a pot on the boards and came back the same way; he did not go around the clerk, because a man with a slate is a thing every landing has.

The boy set the washed cuff on one place and the slate held where nobody else could read it on another, because a man who washes his own cuff has a hunger or a habit, and a man who holds a slate where nobody can read the figure has a use, and a use wants a payer. Which of the two this man was he could not say, and did not try to say; a thing seen at the edge of a market and still seen there was a thing seen, and he kept the not-knowing whole rather than fill it. A measure set down before anybody calls it is a measure set down ahead of something. He kept that as a shape and did not make a rule of it, the way he kept the heard bell beside the told bell in two places and had never laid one across the other.

Below the sill, where the shallow water went over the flat stones and went away doing nobody any harm that anybody would name, a woman had wrapped a child's foot in three turns of wool. The foot stayed dark inside the wool. She called it the cold. A neighbour at the tubs up the bank called the water weather, and the two sayings went past each other as two boards laid side by side go past each other without touching, and neither was wrong in the way that matters to a woman with a child, which is a way no Court slate has a word for.

The surgeon in him read the foot before the woman had finished speaking. Extremity going before the core: the dark toe, the slow return, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody in this place would perform, and past that the things he had known in the old life --- that a cold patient cannot clot, that the grimace of severe cold looks like a smile, that you do not rub a frozen foot and do not set it to the fire. The reading came whole and correct and went nowhere. He had no hand to put two paces out, no word in \lore{Grathic} for what the water had left in the foot, and no leave to write cause or cure or recognition across a child. There was no word in the district for what stays in a foot after the water goes, and he did not supply one; three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a cause if there is nobody to hold them apart, and he had spent two lives learning to hold them apart. He filed the three turns of wool and the dark foot on a place of their own beside the withheld number and the clerk's uncalled measure, and asked the woman nothing, and she carried the child away without once looking toward the basket.

The light went off the boards and the market thinned the way a fire thins when the wood is down to its last few pieces. Sella carried him up the clay road at the half-pace, going around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. Then she went to the fire.

At the hearth the coals were banked with the back of a broken pot, leaving one small place where the heat came through, and \person{Tarn} had set the small knife back where it could be checked, on the sill at the very edge, with six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and never cut. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He told the kindling what he told it every night. “Six counted. The next I don't count.”

The sentence came after that, as it had come on Reed 5 and again on Reed 8, in the same order and with the same weight at the end of it: an ear under this roof was worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan --- the word that would put a slate at the watch and set a collector on the road --- and stopped there. There was no figure in it and no name. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the small interval showed where it should have gone in, the same interval that had shown in his chest every morning since Seed 16, and tonight it showed at the hearth where the fire could not see it and the boy could. “Yes, house,” he said to the fire, and went on with the broom.

It was the third time. The boy had watched the first two go to the same word and come back with nothing, and he had laid them on the interval tray as objects and not as a rule; tonight he laid the third beside them with the same care and the same refusal. Three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if a man lets them, and a doctrine is a thing that can be entered on a slate, whereas three intervals lying side by side are only three intervals and belong to nobody who could spend them. He would not let three stoppings stand up. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and it had been brought to the threshold three times and not called once; the seventh year stood at the very edge of the sill and had not given.

\person{Sella Vail}, within hearing of the word that had not been said, gave the end of her instruction to the pot --- keep it where it can be found, the house has said it --- and asked nothing. \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with bone-coloured slip dried to old bone on both forearms and the bent little finger leading, spoke to the wheel and not to the man at the hearth, and did not ask about the ear. Tarn did not ask about the room. The two silences stood under one roof with no hand between them, like two pots set on a bench that have both been judged and neither entered, and the household straightened around them as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, though no weight had been named and no order had been changed.

In the small hours the house kept its three counts in three places and none of them became one. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir}, a fifth low thing never counted among \lore{the full note}; through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty days; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. He had learned to leave the three where they lay. A man who lays three counts together and calls them one account has made a cause out of a room, and there was no room under this roof for another cause.

He turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, in the order he could check them and with nothing added that had not been earned. Six hearings of the full note stood where they had stood, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, from a person, beside and never among, with no seventh coming out of the wheel's low note or the told bell or the clerk's open slate; three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid; the word forty stayed unspoken with the shape of the forty-first double turn still legible and unnamed; the reed-cutter's cord and \person{Mira Silt}'s cords and \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string lay on their separate places; \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung and the charcoal under \place{the loaf shelf} lay where nobody had thought to look for it; the shelf's two gaps and the one brown-edged crust in the fold kept their two rooms that did not touch; the cold foot below the sill was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard; the clerk's washed cuff lay on its own place and his slate on another. He could spend none of it, and he had stopped asking to.

On the interval tray lay the intervals from Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, Tarn's half-beat behind the standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over \place{the flagstones} without the check, his own held breath from the market, and now the three stoppings of the near-saying --- Reed 5, Reed 8, Reed 11 --- lying side by side as three objects and not as three payments. He did not set the third above the first two and did not make a fourth place for it; it went where the others went, on the tray, with no figure in it and no name and no rule standing up out of it. A third stopping is heavier than the first two for the simple reason that it is the third: three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a doctrine if there is nobody to hold them apart, and he was the only person under the roof who knew there was anything to hold. It was an invisible labour and worth nothing to anyone who could have entered it. He kept it, and kept it from becoming a law.

The loan stayed uncalled. The seventh year had not given. The small knife lay at the edge of the sill with six counted where the house could check and the seventh held where it could not, the one entry in this house the house could not check, and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it, neither entered. The sentence about the ear had come three times to the word and spent nothing, and a thing that has been stopped three times has not therefore been settled; it has only been stopped three times. He set that down on the tray too and did not let it stand up.

The carried fire kept its four low pulls under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing its fifth breath. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water beyond the reed beds and came down, with one hundred and thirty exact and spoken by nobody; he had stopped expecting to be told what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it. Tarn's breath stood unspent at the top of the reach, where it had stood at the hearth and would stand again before the light; the small knife lay on the sill with six counted and the seventh held; and the near-saying lay on the interval tray as a third object and not a third payment while the fire under blind ash missed its fifth pull and the payer went unnamed for a hundred and thirty days. Nothing had been entered. Nothing had been paid. The seventh year had not given.
