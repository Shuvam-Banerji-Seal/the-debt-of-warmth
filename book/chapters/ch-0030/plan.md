# Chapter 30 — The Tally Under the Shelf

**Days:** 129–131. Reed 10, Tenthday (the first Account-day of Reed) through Reed 12, Twelfthday, Y.H. 813. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each opened by its own dated heading and lived in full. Day 128 is finished and is not revisited; Day 132 belongs to Chapter 31.

**One meaningful change:** T100 is planted in Scene 2 — a charcoal tally on the UNDERSIDE of the Vail loaf shelf, a private count the house keeps where nobody thinks to look — and carried in Scene 7 when Sella reaches a finger beneath the board without looking at it. The near-saying under the roof comes a **third** time to the threshold of the word that would call the loan and stops again (Scene 5), filed as a third interval and not a third payment. Nothing is paid: T097, T098, T099 and T100 remain advanced or planted and unpaid; the loan stays uncalled and the seventh year has not given.

**Continuity held throughout:** the boy is 129, 130, 131 days old (four months and nine, ten and eleven days), carried everywhere, never in the Debt Loft; drool frozen and unwiped, wool ridden back from the bare joint, cold cheek–temple–wrist, hand up an inch toward the river's note and down. Six hearings of the full note stand beside and never among, no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; the intervals, the unchecking stroke, the held breath and now **three** stoppings of the near-saying are objects on a tray and not a rule. One crust in the fold with its unnamed empty place; two shelf gaps in a room that does not touch; no third gap, no fourth likeness, no joining. One in twelve printed and unchallenged; true subtraction in Berr's locked box. Word forty unspoken; Orra's forty-first double turn legible and unnamed; Mira's second cords and the reed-cutter's cord unread; Alda's six pages and all tins shut; Dessa dead and unnamed to the boy. Marra not in Sill; Ulda, the green-mouthed woman and Fenna offstage and Fenna never named in the narration. No sale, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting, no knot counted, no silt-leach cause, no power, no rank, no present-tense Chicago break and no `\memory{}` tag.

## Scene 1 — One in Twelve, as the Notice Says

- **Day and time.** Day 129, Reed 10, Tenthday, the first Account-day of Reed, morning into mid-morning. First dated heading of the chapter.
- **Place.** The reading block under the moot-post at the Cross; stalls beyond the Cross; the out-of-line landing below the north bridge kept in view.
- **Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), the soft-cuffed clerk, market walk-ons. Orra, Mira and the reed-cutter are not yet on stage this chapter.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries the boy up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, sets him in the moot-post's shade where no slate lies, and stands a half-pace off.
  2. The Account-day crowd leans in. Berr comes up short with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat rather than worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one mouth-corner; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing needs.
  3. He reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the goers-by, calls the levy, and says *The levy is called. It is not called by me*; to a man who brings a number up out of a fist he says there will be a slate at the watch — *your house*, never *I*. The figure goes into the air; the true subtraction stays in the locked box up the clay road.
  4. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself. The heard bell stays beside the told bell.
  5. At the landing the soft-cuffed clerk is still set for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing, slate to his forearm, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells its reeds over the top of him and nobody steps around him.
  6. The yard's absence is carried in one look: the place in the strip where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, gone around with no figure, no name, no body.
  7. The boy is one hundred and twenty-nine days old and nobody under the market says so. He reads the figure read aloud as a chart closed over a note nobody opened: exact, complete, worth nothing.

- **Emotional turn.** From the crowd's appetite for a number to the boy's private, useless exactness — the figure belongs to everybody and nobody says what it counts; the only person who could have named the day is the one person unable to speak.
- **Sensory anchors.** Tallow smoke and wet wool; the sweetish lead smell at Berr's mouth-corner; hot clay carried on the wind; the reading block wet under the slate; the bell longer than a church bell; the market's noise going over a man with a slate as water goes over a stone.
- **Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077 (read figure, twice-wiped thumb, self-removal, true subtraction), T078 (told bell beside heard bell), T090/T080/T083 (clerk set for an uncalled measure), T058/T079 (instruction to wool and wind, never the basket; half-pace; shade where no slate lies), T009/T013/T092 and T098 carried (missing hand's place gone around with a look), T071/T072 (129 kept exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** Nothing paid. T090 advances the Court's bookkeeping sitting ahead of a death; T098 gathers interest with another going-around; Berr's box gathers interest toward the later published deduction. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand; the green at Berr's mouth stays a colour on its own place, not Fenna.
- **Exact last image.** The reading block with the wet mark of the figure already drying where the crowd has stopped looking at it, and the basket in the moot-post's shade with nobody looking into it.

## Scene 2 — The Mark Under the Board

- **Day and time.** Day 129 continued, midday into the afternoon.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house: the loaf shelf seen from the basket's low angle, and the hearth.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail, a neighbour with a wet pot (walk-on).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket at the low angle from which the underside of the loaf shelf can be seen, tells the fire it was the thaw, and gives the instruction to the pot and the broom and never to the basket.
  2. Above the board the row keeps its two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word. The fold at his ear keeps one stiff brown-edged crust and its empty place. Two rooms that do not touch.
  3. From the low angle the boy sees short charcoal marks on the UNDERSIDE of the shelf: grouped strokes of different darkness, some pressed harder than others, kept where nobody thinks to look, above a row kept badly on top. **T100 planted.**
  4. He does not read, count, copy or show them. He does not lay the marks beside the two gaps above, the fold's crust, Alda's tin, Mira's cords, Orra's knots, Dessa's pewter tin or the reed-cutter's cord. He sets the marks on a place of their own, advanced and unpaid, held for the far-off day a hand not his lays the district's records together.
  5. A neighbour comes with a wet pot and asks what the loaf row will want before the tend is out; Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze and sends her to the pot with *The house has said it*, and the woman carries the unanswered thing away level.
  6. Sella does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf and a day are not openings.

- **Emotional turn.** The boy's attention settles on an object nobody has ever thought to hide — a keeping so ordinary it has become invisible — and he refuses, as in two lives he has learned to refuse, to let it join the other absences and make a cause.
- **Sensory anchors.** Clay-cold glaze on the jar; the dry smell of the loaf shelf and the sharper charcoal; the banked coals turning over with the back of a broken pot; the neighbour's wet sleeves; the low angle at which a shelf becomes a roof.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (**planted**), T060/T086 (two shelf gaps, one crust, rooms that do not touch), T097 (carried, unpaid), T058/T079 (instruction to fire, pot and broom; *the house*; jar glanced at once; mark untouched), T077 (a cost answered with a direction), T071/T072.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T100 planted only: no figure read, no stroke counted, no copying hand, no joining to the two gaps above and no slate entered from it. Its payoff stays at the far convergence of the district's second records. Nothing else paid.
- **Exact last image.** From the basket's low angle, the underside of the loaf shelf going dark in the corner where the marks are, the row above it standing with its two gaps, and Sella's back between the boy and the board with nothing counted.

## Scene 3 — One Hundred and Twenty-Nine

- **Day and time.** Day 129 continued, going of the light into the small hours.
- **Place.** The clay road and the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof; the loaf shelf in the next room kept in view.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace, goes around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie, sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen and unwiped, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting.
  2. At the fire she tells it it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale, sets the pot rim out and handle toward the reaching hand and does not look toward the bed.
  3. The clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and are called the thaw. The widow's mark is not touched at a rack, a road, a shelf, a basket or a dusk.
  4. Three counts in three places: the river's note through the finger's-width door, the carried fire's four low pulls and silent fifth through the floor under blind ash (payer unnamed for 129 days), the boards' four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. None becomes one.
  5. He turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel: six hearings from four sources plus the sixth from a person, beside and never among, no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; intervals and two stoppings on their tray; word forty unspoken; cold foot still the cold; greens on their own place; reed-cutter's cord, Mira's cords, Orra's knot-string; shelf's two gaps and fold's one crust in rooms that do not touch.
  6. The charcoal marks from the afternoon go into the file as one more object on a place of its own, unread and joined to nothing. No third gap, no fourth likeness, no seventh hearing. One hundred and twenty-nine stands unspoken.

- **Emotional turn.** The daily inventory should be comfort and is not: the file is exact, complete and worthless, and the one new object is another thing he can never enter.
- **Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke and the sour mineral tang of Ash in the corner; the little wet sound when his head goes too far; the thread at his mouth stiff; cold arriving cheek, temple, wrist; the fire's lost fifth breath.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (carried, unread), T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T088, T061/T082, T060/T086, T097, T071/T072, T004 held offstage (no break, no tag).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** None paid. The charcoal is carried into the file beside the other private records, still unjoined; T088's payer goes unnamed one day further.
- **Exact last image.** The hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water and coming down with one hundred and twenty-nine standing where it stood, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody.

## Scene 4 — The Dust Ahead of the Sentence

- **Day and time.** Day 130, Reed 11, Eleventhday, before first grey through midday. First dated heading of the day.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard.
- **Present.** Corin (carried), Jorra Vail, Tarn, Sella Vail; three or four unnamed men (not followed); a High Kiln kiln-master (walk-on).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Men come up the yard with collars high and speak low in the room off the shed; Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
  2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger, not bone-coloured slip; keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds it from a kiln-master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. The iron is read once more as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no Grathic word for both mercies, filed as an object joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut room stay beside it; no third comes.
  5. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed, says *Yes, house* to an early thaw and a late and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, his seventh year pressed past another tend and still held, the knife at the very edge with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the breath from Seed 16 unspent.
  6. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check while the stones keep the shallow bright patch where it stopped. Neither man names the other's silence; the loan that can hear is uncalled.

- **Emotional turn.** A household moves around two men who will not speak: the wheel-keeper's silence is a correction, the debt's silence is a kept account, and the boy is the only one who can read both and the least use in the room.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust like meal worked into the creases past the bent finger; cold iron taking the warmth out of a fingertip; the wheel's low note and the smell of damp clay and tallow; wet wool and the sour tang of Ash in Tarn's knuckles; the pale clip-ring catching the little light.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, dust past the bent finger, broken birth-slates under the rack, *he's not Vail* sealed, promise ungiven), T062 (damper's double bite, object joined to no kiln), T065/T095 (two shut-room tellings, no third), T036/T076 (seventh year held, knife at the edge, breath unspent, unchecking stroke), T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** None paid. T062's double bite gathers toward the day the Vail room is understood; T035's unnamed room and dust gather toward the Broken Slates; the held stroke waits for the near-saying that night.
- **Exact last image.** The shallow bright patch on the flagstones where the stroke had stopped the week before, with the broom going on over it without the half-check and Jorra's bent finger still standing in the cold iron of the damper.

## Scene 5 — The Word Stopped a Third Time

- **Day and time.** Day 130 continued, late afternoon at the Fishstairs, then the going of the light into the small hours at the hearth under the Vail roof.
- **Place.** The fish-trade shade and the out-of-line landing below the north bridge; the hearth and kindling stack under the Vail roof.
- **Present.** Corin (carried), Tarn, Sella Vail, Jorra Vail (silent, no question); the soft-cuffed clerk; market walk-ons; a child below the sill (not approached).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the landing for a measure nobody called; cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm, hand to slate before eyes to stalls. The market sells over him; nobody steps around him or takes him off.
  2. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool — the cold to one woman, weather to the yard. The surgeon's reading of the extremity going before the core works and buys nothing; no cause, cure or recognition is written across it.
  3. At the hearth, coals banked and the small knife set back where it can be checked, Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*.
  4. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes a **third** time to the word that would call the loan and stops there with no figure and no name. He draws the breath that should go in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom.
  5. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room. The two silences stay unjoined.
  6. The boy files the third stopping on the interval tray beside the first two as a third interval and not a third payment — an object among objects, not a rule. He refuses to let three stoppings stand up and call themselves a doctrine. The loan is uncalled; the seventh year has not given. The carried fire keeps four low pulls and a silent fifth, payer unnamed for 130 days.

- **Emotional turn.** The near-saying has now reached the word three times and spent nothing; the third time is heavier precisely because it is the third, and the boy's whole discipline is to keep it from becoming a law.
- **Sensory anchors.** Grey cuff washed and re-greyed by the day; slate held where nobody else can see the figure; the sour mineral tang under the soap; the heat of the banked coals going down; the small knife on its sill; the unforged collector sentence kept beside the seventh year; the held breath at the top of each reach.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (**third stopping, withheld**), T061/T082 (third interval, not a third payment, not a rule), T090/T080/T083, T093/T094/T096 (uncured, uncaused, unnamed), T088, T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** None paid. The third stopping advances the loan toward its far calling and makes the seventh year's end more inevitable; the cold foot gathers interest for Arc 3 with no silt-leach named; the clerk's uncalled measure gathers toward the day it is understood.
- **Exact last image.** Tarn's held breath not spent at the top of the reach, the small knife on the sill with six counted and the seventh held, and the near-saying lying on the interval tray as a third object and not a third payment while the fire misses its fifth pull under blind ash.

## Scene 6 — Two Boards That Do Not Touch

- **Day and time.** Day 131, Reed 12, Twelfthday, morning downriver at the Weir and midday three doors down in House Silt. First dated heading of the day.
- **Place.** The Weir: the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone, the lower boards; then House Silt.
- **Present.** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Orra Weir, Mira Silt, the reed-cutter, Alda Reed (short); a weir woman, a buyer and a neighbour at the tub (walk-ons).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed work goes on in armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate.
  2. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle and the grey cord inside his coat; sets the bundle on the flat stone, straightens the cord on his thumb, turns it so the knots stand, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back. To the woman who asks what the cord holds: *You can ask the cord* / *No more can the Court*. No knot counted, no name, no joining to the missing hand or the word forty.
  3. Orra is at the lower boards with her board on its side and a cord at her waist, pulls one knot down with her thumb and gives a date where a levy question is asked — the ninth of Ilmen, the collector's boat with six oars, the man at the stern who never touched them; when told it is not the levy she says it is a date and the river takes what the Court does not. Her knot-string lies beside Mira's board and does not touch; the word forty stays unspoken with the shape of the forty-first double turn legible and unnamed.
  4. Mira works the eels with slime dried grey on her sleeves and silt pale in her neck's hollow, answers a thin run with her own answer, moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie, puts her thumb under her apron to the grey cord when a buyer names a figure. The second twist twisted twice stays beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted, the thin child's knot untouched.
  5. At midday Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door of House Silt at the edge of the light and stands a half-pace off, opens no slate and turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row.
  6. Alda lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the wool a breath too long, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower without knowing it, covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa, says *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down* — exact and not true over a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of — touches the little tin inside her bodice without opening it and leaves six pages turned toward a floorboard. No seventh written. The pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright lid shut.
  7. Green-mouthed woman and foundling offstage; no fourth green, no sixth sighting, no sale.

- **Emotional turn.** The Weir and House Silt are full of records kept in materials no Court thumb can spell, and every one of them is visible and enterable by nobody — the day's work is to hold them apart without making a single joined account.
- **Sensory anchors.** Salt dried white on the posts; traps with their mouths upriver; tallow smoke from the Eel House; eel slime dried in long grey strokes and silt pale in the neck's hollow; soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap; a thumb-bright rub on a pewter lid.
- **Threads advanced.** T099 (carried, not paid), T024/T059/T025, T023/T089 and T007/T008 (not paid again), T058/T079, T071/T072, T077.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** None paid. Three private records — the reed-cutter's cord, Orra's knot-string, Mira's two cords — gather beside Alda's tin and Dessa's pewter tin toward the far convergence; all visible, none entered. Orra's date gathers toward the long vigil; the mercy-lie curdles further against a death nobody can correct.
- **Exact last image.** Orra's knot-string lying beside Mira's board without touching it, the reed-cutter's cord gone back inside his coat with its knots unread, and in House Silt the pewter tin above the empty bed keeping its thumb-bright rub shut.

## Scene 7 — A Finger Beneath the Board

- **Day and time.** Day 131 continued, afternoon at the loaf shelf, then the going of the light into the small hours on the bed.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house loaf shelf and hearth; the bed under the roof.
- **Present.** Corin, Sella Vail. Alda, Mira, Orra, Berr, Jorra and Tarn offstage or silent; nothing joined.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella stands at the shelf with her back to the basket, moves her lips along the row of loaves with its two unnamed gaps, glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and once reaches a finger beneath the board where the charcoal tally lies — without looking at it and without a word — and takes the finger away.
  2. From the basket's low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, join the underside to the two gaps above or to the fold's one crust in the other room, or make a cause of the hand's habit. T100 is carried, not paid.
  3. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I* and does not explain the count she keeps; the widow's mark hangs where it hangs, because a shelf and a dusk are not openings.
  4. At dusk she carries him up at the half-pace, goes around the rack space, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting; tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist and does not look toward the bed.
  5. Three counts in three places again: the river's note through the finger's-width door, the carried fire's four low pulls and silent fifth under blind ash (payer unnamed for 131 days), the boards' four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. None becomes one.
  6. He turns out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object in its place: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the three stoppings among the intervals as objects and not a rule, the reed-cutter's unread cord, the charcoal under the shelf on a separate place joined to nothing, the word forty unspoken, the cold foot still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, one crust in the fold and two shelf gaps in a room that does not touch. Nothing is joined, paid, named, opened, called or ended.

- **Emotional turn.** A hand goes under a board in an ordinary kitchen and comes back with nothing said; the boy's whole life is the keeping of such motions without ever being allowed to lay one beside another. The count reaches 131 and is still worth nothing to anyone who could enter it.
- **Sensory anchors.** The dry warm smell of the loaf shelf; charcoal under a fingernail; the clay jar cold under its glaze; woodsmoke and the sour tang of Ash at the corner; the thread frozen at his mouth; the fire's missing fifth breath.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (**carried, not paid**), T058/T079, T060/T086, T097 (advanced, unpaid), T061/T082, T088, T071/T072, T093/T094/T096, T004 held offstage (no break, no tag).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** None paid. T100's finger-under-board is a second keeping of the same hidden count, still unread and still separate from the two gaps above; it gathers toward the far day a district's private records are laid together. The three stoppings gather toward the end of the seventh year without paying it. Nothing else opened.
- **Exact last image.** Sella's finger going beneath the loaf shelf and coming away without a word, the row above keeping its two gaps, and under the roof the river's note through the finger's-width door while the hand goes up an inch toward it and comes down with one hundred and thirty-one unspent.

## How the last moment pulls into Chapter 31

The chapter ends mid-action in the reaching hand, with one hundred and thirty-one exact and spoken by nobody — the same small gesture that closed Chapters 27, 28 and 29, now one day further on and heavier with a new object in the file. Two private records have gathered in this chapter and neither has been read: the charcoal under the Vail shelf (T100) and the reed-cutter's cord at the Weir (T099). The near-saying has been stopped three times with no figure and no name, and the seventh year is still held; something must give at its end and has not given here. Chapter 31 opens on Day 132, Reed 13, Thirteenthday, with the boy one hundred and thirty-two days old, four months and twelve days, the reed month in its middle tend: the missing hand's place still gone around with a look, both private tallies unread, the third stopping on the interval tray and the loan uncalled. Forward, not closed.
