The afternoon came back up the clay road with them the way a pot carried uphill comes back into the light: a little slopped, a little cooler, held level all the same. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come down with them and would go back up with them, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing. \place{Low Kiln} went around them in woodsmoke and wet wool and the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} worked into the corners; cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint so the cold found the bare place and went under the fold at his ear. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody under any roof in the district had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put, stiff and doing nothing. He was one hundred and thirty-one days old, four months and eleven days, and nobody on the road said so.

At \place{the Vail kiln-house} she set the basket low and a little back near the bench leg, at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board that a man standing sees only the top of. It was not a place she chose for him; it was a place the light chose, coming in low through the finger's-width door and running along the grain under \place{the loaf shelf}. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}. She gave the instruction to the pot and to the broom and never to the basket --- feed it, turn it, go around what the water left, it cost enough to come in and would cost more to take out --- and did not look down into the basket. He lay at the low angle with cold iron at his cheek where the bench bracket had been bolted and left, and his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The child in him was cold at the ear and wanted the crust two inches off in the fold; the man in him read the angle of the light and the grain of the board before he read either. Both were true. The second was quieter and the first was louder, and neither could be handed to anybody.

Above him the shelf held its row the way a row is held in a house that keeps its days badly: loaves pushed along it, a glance at the clay jar on the wall, and a space at the row's end nobody called a number. The row kept its two gaps. One was the old space at the end, the one that had come in with the planting and that a neighbour could name in one breath without ever seeing it as a thing that was missing; the other was a palm's breadth of bare board that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word, as if a loaf had lifted itself off in the dark and gone. Sella stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row and gave nobody a figure. Her glance went once to the clay jar and came back; the glaze was cold in the warm room and she did not touch it. “The house has said it,” she said to the row, and the row went on standing with its two gaps.

Then she reached a finger beneath the board.

It was not a searching hand. It went under the front edge where the shelf's underside dropped into shadow, the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again, and stayed there long enough for the boy to see the knuckle set and the thumb come up against the grain; then it came away. She did not look at what her finger had gone to find. She did not look anywhere but at the row above the board, and her lips went on moving along the loaves as though the finger had never left the pot or the fire. No word went with the motion. The dry warm smell of the shelf went up past her and the sharper smell of charcoal went under, and for one breath the two smells crossed the way two hands cross in a dark room without touching.

From the low angle the boy saw the hand go under and come back and saw nothing of what the marks said. He had been set under this board that afternoon and the marks had waited there since Seed 6 or before, and he had not once been given a reason to turn them into a figure. He did not count the strokes. He did not hold the shapes in the dark where he could keep them, or press the pad of a thumb against the grain where her thumb had been, or lay what was under the board beside the two gaps above it, or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or beside any of the other private records the district kept where no Court thumb could spell them. He did not make a cause of the hand's habit. A hand that goes under a board without looking either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look; he had learned to leave the reason where it stood. The marks were a keeping of the same order as the other private records in the district: kept in a material no Court thumb could spell, visible, enterable by nobody. He set them back on the place where they had already been set, advanced and unpaid, held for the far-off day when a hand not his laid the district's records together and an account at last wanted its name. The finger went under and came away, and the count went on being kept where nobody could spell it.

She had put the house into the sentence where another woman would have put herself, and gave no explanation of the count she kept. It was not a count she could have explained. A figure spoken aloud is a figure somebody can carry off, and a figure carried off wants a payer; she had learned to set a record where her own hand could reach it without her eyes going there. The boy did not follow her into that. He had no word in \lore{Grathic} for the inside of a woman's silence and would not have entered it if he had. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it; she touched it before an opening and at no other hour, and a shelf in the middle of a tend was not an opening and an afternoon was not an opening. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned and the new finer hairline beside it lay unswept in the corner and were called the thaw, and she did not look toward the corner. A shelf and a dusk are not openings. The row kept its two gaps and the mark kept its cord and the marks under the board kept their grain, and none of the three was laid across either of the others.

\scenebreak

The light went off the underside of the shelf and the marks went dark in the corner where the grain turned, and Sella lifted the basket without ever looking into it. Dusk came down the clay road ahead of them the way a pot's shadow crosses a table before the pot is set down, so that they walked into the house's evening rather than bringing it in with them. She carried him at the half-pace from every open slate, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, going around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she did not go back to the loaf shelf that night and did not take her finger under any board again. The house kept its day the way a cup keeps the shape of what was poured into it and then emptied out.

At the hearth she set the pot down where the light fell on its rim and turned the handle toward the side of the bench where a reaching hand would find it. The pot gave its low note as it settled, full beneath the small flat sound that lived in it from the wheel. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood where they had stood --- five from four sources and the sixth from a person --- beside and never among, and he did not make a seventh of the pot under this roof. No seventh came with the dusk. The bell had been a told bell and a heard bell in two places; the wheel's note came through the wall from the shed and went where it sounded; the marks under the shelf stayed where they were put. Three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a cause if there is nobody to hold them apart, and he had spent a hundred and thirty-one days being the person who held them apart, an invisible labour that bought nothing anyone could have entered.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening, as she had counted at a shelf with her lips and at a rack with her eyes, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale off the coals. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

He was carried up under the roof and set on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof in the district had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the way a hand rests on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot bring itself to let go of; then it went, and the house straightened around its going as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One stiff brown-edged crust lay in the fold with its empty place beside it, and no third came into either place in the going of the light.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir}, a fifth low thing he had kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing and never counted among the six. Through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-one days. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one: the note through the door that was never a hearing, the fire's four with the fifth missing under the ash, the nails and the knot in the boards above his head. Three counts in three places will stand up and call themselves a single account if a man leaves them alone together for a night; he had spent a hundred and thirty-one nights keeping them apart.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from the small bare joint and going under the fold where nobody had been told to put a hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. It could not reach the crust, the bucket, the pot with its handle turned out, the door's finger's width of light, or any one of the three counts it had spent the day keeping separate. That was the whole of it: a man with a surgeon's eye and a child's reach, lying under four nails and a knot while the fire missed its fifth breath and the river went on being water over water beyond a town that had never once asked him what he heard.

\scenebreak

He turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel, each thing in its place and nothing laid across anything else. It was a habit of the first life that had survived into this one without his ever choosing it: field, exposure, instrument, complication, cost. Here there was no field and no cost he could spend, only the objects. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, no seventh; the told bell stood beside the heard bell; the two tellings of the shut counting room stood side by side, each with its date, and no third telling came. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. The intervals from Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 lay with \person{Tarn}'s half-beat behind the standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over \place{the flagstones} without the check, the breath drawn at the market and not spent, and now the three stoppings of the near-saying --- the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it, brought three times to the word that would call the loan and stopped three times with no figure and no name. Three intervals were three objects on a tray and not a rule. He had refused to let two stand up and call themselves a doctrine; he refused it now with three. A rule is a thing a man can hand to a clerk. These he could not have handed to anyone, and that was why he went on laying them out on a towel in the dark where nobody could see him do it.

The loan stayed uncalled. Tarn's seventh year had not given, the small knife was back on its sill where it could be checked with six counted in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh held at the very edge, and the unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no figure in it. He did not join that to the mark under the shelf, though both were counts kept where a Court thumb could not read them and both had a payer nobody had named. Neither man had asked the other anything, and the two silences had stayed unjoined under one roof. \person{Jorra Vail}'s standing finger in the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, the dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger, the men who came before first grey and were given no name: these lay on their own places with no third telling added. The coat on its peg kept its broader shape and the seat its twenty-year lean; the uncalled debt against the tiles went around the space where a name might have stood, neither paid nor settled nor done with.

The word forty stayed unspoken with the shape of the forty-first double turn still legible on \person{Orra Weir}'s cord and no name laid on it. The cold foot below the sill was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, and the surgeon's reading of the extremity going before the core worked and bought nothing; no silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition had been written across it. The four greens kept their three places with no fourth and no cause; \person{Berr Clay}'s office cord stayed folded under his coat and his twice-wiped thumb stayed on its tray beside the finger that had passed over a figure, the true subtraction gone up the clay road in a locked box. \person{Dessa Silt}'s pewter tin above the abandoned bed kept its pale fold and thumb-bright lid shut, and the number stayed in a thumb gone into the water and the name under the sieve-wire scar, neither spoken to him. \person{Mira Silt}'s second cord stayed beneath her apron, never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} or lifted at a door; \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages stayed turned face-down toward a floorboard, read by nobody; the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the landing for a measure nobody had called. Each sat where it sat. He could see every one and enter none of them.

One crust in the fold and two shelf gaps in a room that does not touch. The marks under the shelf sat on their own place, joined to nothing, and the finger that had gone beneath the board had come away without a word. Nothing was joined, paid, named, opened, called or ended. He was one hundred and thirty-one days old, four months and eleven days, and nobody under the roof had said so or would have known what the number would have counted if somebody had. The river's note came through the finger's-width door; through the floor under blind ash the fire missed its fifth; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. He lay with the fold at his ear and his hand outside the wool, and the hand went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down with the day exact and unspent and spoken by nobody. In the next room the row of loaves kept its two gaps, and under the shelf the marks waited where they had been put, for a hand not his and a day that had not come.
