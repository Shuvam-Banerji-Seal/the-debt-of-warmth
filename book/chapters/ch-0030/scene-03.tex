The going of the light came up \place{the clay road} before the boy did, travelling ahead of him in long thin strips that lay across the ruts and went out one after another as the sun dropped behind the kilns. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the half-pace was not slowness but a measurement: a slate lying open on a bench was a thing to be gone around with the basket held level, and she had gone around so many of them that her walk had become the shape of a question she never asked. The instruction went with them the way the wool went, given to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up, and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The wind had come round to the east and carried tallow smoke and the faint sour tang that had worked into the corners of \place{Low Kiln} and would not come out with scrubbing. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool rode back from the small bare joint and the cold found the bare place and went under the fold at his ear. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody under any roof in the district had been given an instruction about the thread, so it stayed where it had been put, stiff as a stitch set and never drawn out.

He was one hundred and twenty-nine days old, four months and nine days, and nobody on the road said so.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had gone around them since the year turned, entered by nobody, and she went around the space as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. A look, a wide step, no number. The boy read the going-around the way he had learned to read a chart laid face down on a desk: not the thing underneath, but the care taken with the covering, which told him more than the writing would have. He set the space beside the place in \place[the worked strips]{the strip} where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out on Seed 27, and left the two lying side by side without drawing a line between them. Both were absences the yard kept by walking around them. Neither had been given a figure, and he gave neither one.

Inside \place{the Vail kiln-house} she set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. The bed had been his since he came under the roof and the loft had never been offered, and the distinction was kept the way the house kept most of its distinctions, by never mentioning it. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the crust where it lay. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, the way a hand rests on a thing it has carried a long way and cannot quite bring itself to let go of. The little-door burn closed on nothing. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about that sound either.

The surgeon in him noted the half-beat and filed it where he filed the other small excesses of the house: a hand that stayed where a hand had finished, a thumb wiped twice where once would serve, a finger that rested on a slate after the figure had been read. The child in him was cold at the ear and wanted the brown-edged crust two inches off in the fold, stiff and past smelling of anything. Both were true. The second was louder; the first was the one he could not hand to anybody, because there was nobody under this roof who would have known what a half-beat was worth.

\scenebreak

At the hearth she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}. She said it to the coals the way she said everything that had to be heard by something that could not answer, and the fire took the words and gave back nothing a slate could carry. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, feeding the low places and leaving the blind ash over the rest, and said, “The house will keep it,” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The \emph{I} was not withheld out of modesty. A thing said aloud became a thing \lore{the Court} could enter, and the house was a safer word to stand in front of a warmth nobody had paid for in public.

She stood a moment with the back of the broken pot in her hand, counting something under her breath toward no opening. Her lips moved on a row only she could see, the household's arithmetic in loaves and glances and the space at the end nobody called a number; she came back to the same place twice and went on from there. The count was a shape kept for the pot, not a figure given to a man with a slate. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, the colour of the new fine line in the corner that she had not swept and did not intend to sweep. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed.

“Go around what the water left,” she told the broom, giving the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody. “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” The pot gave its low note as it settled, full beneath the small flat sound that lived in it from the wheel, and she left it where a hand groping in the dark would find the handle first.

The \lore{widow's mark} hung on its cord against her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. Her hand went toward it and stopped, and came away to the pot. A mark was touched before an opening and at no other hour; a dusk was not an opening, a fire banked for the night was not an opening, a bed with a boy on it was not an opening. She had lost a husband to the danger and had never described it that way even to herself. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay unswept at the corner with the new finer hairline beside it, and both were called the thaw and would go when they went. She did not look at either. The private roll she kept in her head was not shown to a man from \place{Slate Row}, and a dusk was not an hour for showing it to herself either. She went to her own bed.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the dark came on in the order it always came on, first the light going out of the oiled paper at the window, then the fire's low red, then the sound of water. Through the finger's-width of the door came the river's note of water over water, beyond \place{the reed beds} and \place{the Weir} and the whole length of Low Kiln, a fifth low thing he had kept on a place of its own since he was six days old, never a hearing and never counted among the six. Through the floor under blind ash the carried fire took its coal and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and twenty-nine days. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb.

Three counts in three places, and none of them became one.

He had learned in two lives what three likenesses in a row will do if a man leaves them lying together: they stand up and call themselves a cause. The bell at \place{the Cross} was a told bell and stayed beside the heard bell; the note of water was a fifth low thing and stayed beside the four pulls; the knot in the boards was a knot and had never been a nail. He kept them lying flat where they lay, as a man keeps instruments separated on a towel so that no edge is taken for another edge. The discipline cost him nothing and bought him nothing, and he had done it so long that it had become the way he lay awake.

He turned out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel before anyone calls what the operation is for.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood where they had stood: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} through a wall, the district's slate twice at the Cross, a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh had come in the day that had gone, and he had not made one to fill the place where a seventh would stand. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again. On the interval tray lay the intervals from Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat in a man's broom behind a standing finger, the stroke that went on over the flagstones without the check, his own held breath from the market, and the two stoppings of the near-saying: the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it, brought twice to the threshold of the word that would call the loan and stopped twice there with no figure and no name. They lay as objects among objects and not as a rule. The loan that could hear was uncalled and the seventh year had not given.

\person{Orra Weir}'s cord kept the shape of the forty-first double turn with the word that would have named it still unspoken, and the word forty had not been said under this roof in all his days. The cold foot below the sill was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, and no silt-leach, cause or cure had been written across it. The three greens stood each on its own place, a colour and not a name. The clerk at the landing still held his slate for a measure nobody had called, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. The reed-cutter's cord had gone back inside his coat with its knots unread; \person[Mira Silt]{Mira}'s grey cord and the second twist twisted twice beneath it had not been lifted at a door, her board lying beside Orra's knot-string without touching it; \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s narrow slate held a figure that did not know the hand that had not come back; \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages lay turned toward a floorboard in \place{House Silt} with both tins shut, and a woman more mornings dead than any living person could count lay uncollected with her name unsaid.

\place{the loaf shelf} in the next room kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word; the fold at his ear kept one stiff brown-edged crust and an empty place beside it that had no name. Two rooms that did not touch. A saying of the loaf-shelves of Low Kiln held that two gaps stay two gaps until a finger lays them together and an account then wants a name, and no finger in the district had laid these together. No third gap had come. No fourth likeness had been allowed to stand up and call itself a cause.

There was one new object on the tray tonight, and it was the one he could do least with. The short charcoal marks on the underside of the loaf shelf, seen that afternoon from the low angle where a shelf becomes a roof, went into the file beside the other private records and on a place of their own. He did not read the clusters or count the strokes, and did not hold their shapes in the dark where he could have kept them; he did not lay them under the board beside the two gaps above it, or beside the one crust in the fold in the room that did not touch, or beside the cords and the knots and the tin and the slate. A note turned down behind a chart was still a note turned down, and in the other life he would have gone looking for the hand that had turned it; here there was nobody to ask and no hand of his that could have asked, and the looking had nowhere to go. The marks were a keeping put where nobody expected to be read, and the care of the hiding told him more than the strokes would have if he could have spelled them. He set the object down and left it lying. Three private records now stood separate and unopened in the district, the charcoal under the Vail shelf, the reed-cutter's cord, the knot-string at the Weir, and he would not let three in a row stand up and call themselves a doctrine, any more than he had let three counts become one or three stoppings become a rule. A doctrine was an account that had found a payer. These had none, and he had no hand to give them one.

The inventory was exact and complete and worth nothing. That was the comfort he had expected from it and had never once found: a man lays out his instruments and knows what he has, and here he knew everything and could enter nothing, and the one new object was another thing he would never be allowed to touch. The surgeon's eye had sorted the pressure of the charcoal, the half-beat of a hand, the fifth breath that never came; the child's ear was cold and wanted the crust two inches off. Both were true. The surgeon's voice was the one he heard most now, and that was its own small grief.

He was one hundred and twenty-nine days old, four months and nine days, and nobody under this roof had said so. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. The number stood where it had stood that morning, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody, and the dark went on keeping what it kept while the fire under blind ash missed its fifth pull and the boards overhead kept their knot the width of a thumb.
