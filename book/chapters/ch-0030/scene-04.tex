\daymark{130}{Reed 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey \place{the Clay Yard} had the colour of wet ash, and there was nothing in it yet that \lore{the Court} could have entered. The men came up from the direction of the sill with their collars high, three or four of them, walking in the spaced way of men who have arranged not to arrive together, and went low-voiced into \place[the back room]{the room off the shed}, whose door kept its finger's width of open and did not shut. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} past them at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the half-pace held the basket level the way a hand holds a tray that must not be tilted; she gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, did not turn her head toward the door, and did not say, then or afterward, that the room was cold. \person{Jorra Vail} came out of it with fine grey dust of slate ground small as meal worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, and said nothing about where he had been.

Three or four. The boy could not make a number of what had not been given to be counted, and he did not try. A thing glimpsed at the edge of a yard and gone into a door was a thing glimpsed at the edge of a yard, the way the clean dark line in the corner was a line and the new finer hairline beside it was another line, and neither of them was a figure. He had learned in two lives to count only what was set out to be counted, and the household set out very little; what it did not set out it carried past him in the posture of men who had already agreed on the shape of their arriving. Two tellings of a counting room shut in the war with the men inside dead of the breath had come into this yard in other years, each with a date, each told as a curiosity eighteen leagues east. No third telling came this morning. The men went in, the door kept its finger's width, and the dust came out on the forearms of the wheel-keeper, and the two old tellings stayed where they lay, joined to no kiln and to no dust and to no room standing a finger's width open under \place{Low Kiln} before the light.

He was one hundred and thirty days old, four months and ten days, and nobody in the yard said so.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} she set the basket down where no slate lay, and stood a half-pace off from it, the way she stood a half-pace off from every slate that had ever been left open on a bench. A basket set down was a pot set down and left. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up. He lay at the low angle where the light ran under the bench and the underside of things became their roofs, and the thread at his mouth stayed frozen because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread.

Jorra kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray. Across a hundred and thirty mornings the boy had watched the two states of the same man's arms come and go and had never once seen a hand lay one beside the other and call them one account. Today the grey went past the bent finger and stopped at the crease of the wrist, meal-fine, ground smaller than the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; under the nails it sat in the half-moons like flour that had been breathed on. It was not slip. Slip dried to the colour of old bone and flaked away; this went into the grain of the skin and stayed. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The questions stood where all his questions stood, in a mouth that could not form the \lore{Grathic} for them and a hand that could not have written the answer if one had been handed to him, and he did not make a cause of the not-asking either. Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing, and the one sentence Jorra had spoken on the hour the boy arrived was sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. The promise of a name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the wheel he spoke before anyone spoke to him. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said to the wheel, in the flat voice he kept for tools and pots and never for the basket. “It cares if they're steady.” The wheel gave its low note under his hands as it came round, and with the note came the smell of damp clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze carried up from \place{High Kiln} on a bad wind. The boy turned his head toward the note before anyone tapped anything, the way he had turned toward pots since he was six days old. Nobody under the roof had ever given the turning an instruction, and nobody had ever told him to stop.

A pot came off the wheel and stood on the bench with the thrower's thumb-mark still wet at its shoulder. “You'll do,” Jorra said to it, with no welcome in the voice and no unkindness either, and the boy heard in those two words the whole arithmetic of the house: a vessel judged by the note it gave before it was fired, and the judging kept where a kiln-master would not call it early and make something of it. The full note was in that pot before it left the wheel. Jorra knew it as he knew the bent finger and the hollow in the seat his father had worn, and he would not say so in front of a man who would put a figure to it.

The kiln-master came up the yard from \emph{there} later in the morning, with \lore{Ash} on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of lead glaze coming off him in the little wind, and stood looking at the pots on the bench with the look of a man who would set a figure to a thing if the figure could be made to stand.

“That one's singing before it's fired,” the kiln-master said, not as a question, and put his hand out toward the bench.

“It's dry enough,” Jorra said to the pot.

He did not say what he knew, and he did not put the word the man wanted into anybody's mouth. The knowing was worth nothing anyone could take, and was therefore kept. The boy watched the space between the two men the way he had learned to watch the space between a chart and the neat line drawn over a note nobody had opened: exact, complete, and not to be entered. He did not supply the figure the kiln-master wanted to hear. He had no words in which to supply it, and if he had had them he would not have spent them, because a note called early was a note spent early, and this house had spent enough of its warmth in public already.

\scenebreak

She had told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} when she came in, and the fire took the word and gave back nothing a slate could carry. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low,” she said to the fire, and the rest of it --- whatever instruction would have gone to the boy, or the pot, or the hand at the damper --- stayed in her mouth. Before the end of it came, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, the little finger leading as it had led since he was eleven, and moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing there. The end of her instruction arrived at the pot instead of at him. The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, though no weight had been named and no order had been changed; only a tongue of iron stood a hair's breadth differently from where it had stood a moment before, and the fire took the cold out of the fingertip and gave it back a little warmer.

The boy read the iron once more as he had read it on other mornings and would read it on mornings still to come: the bite that holds a vessel whole is the bite that can take the hand off a man. There was no word in Grathic that held both mercies in one. In the old life there had been a word for such a thing and the word had been a clamp, and a clamp was a clamp and not a doctrine, and you set it where you needed it and did not ask it to mean anything more. He set this one down on the tray as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed beside it where they had stayed for years, each with its date, one with breath through the nose and one made in the throat, and no third telling came to stand up among them. A correction and a keeping were two things, and he kept them on two places and did not let them touch.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave the instruction to the broom before she gave it to anybody --- go around what the water left, it cost enough to come in and would cost more to take out --- and he took the broom up as a man takes up a tool already named, saying, “Yes, house,” to the ones who wanted the thaw early and to the ones who wanted it late. A man come up from the sluice with his sleeves wet to the elbow said the thaw could not come too early for him, and a woman at the pot board said she hoped it held off another fortnight, and he said “Yes, house” to the one and “Yes, house” to the other, and went on wanting the late. He agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and kept wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His seventh year was pressed past another tend and still held. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and never cut, the one entry in this house that the house could not check; he had set it there before the light and told the empty yard where it was, and had not announced it a second time, because a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest was tight with the breath he had decided not to spend on Seed 16, and at the top of each reach there was a small interval where the breath should have gone in and did not. He did not say what an ear under this roof was worth, and nobody asked him. The sentence that would have called the loan lay where it had lain for six years, under the roof and in the room with the broom and the knife, and he went on sweeping around it.

His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} without the half-check. The week before, behind Jorra's standing finger at the damper, the broom had checked for half a beat on the stone and gone on; today there was no check, and the stones kept the shallow bright patch where it had stopped, kept it the way the yard kept the space under the lowest rack and the place in the strip where a hand had gone in and had not come out, by passing over it and not entering it. Neither man named the other's silence. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stood in one yard with no hand between them, like two pots set on a bench that have both been judged and neither entered. The loan that could hear was uncalled.

The boy lay at the edge of the work with the basket level and the thread at his mouth frozen where nobody had told anybody to wipe it. The wool had ridden back from the small bare joint and the cold had come in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood where they stood, beside and never among, with no seventh coming out of the wheel's low note or the pot on the bench or the told talk of kilns; three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid; the intervals and the two stoppings lay among the objects as objects and not as a rule. The word forty was unspoken. The cold foot below the sill was still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard. The short charcoal marks under \place{the loaf shelf} lay on a place of their own, unread and joined to nothing, and the shelf's two gaps and the one brown-edged crust in the fold kept their two rooms that did not touch. He held all of it and could spend none of it. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water beyond \place{the reed beds} and came down, with one hundred and thirty exact and spoken by nobody.

The shallow bright patch lay on the flagstones where the stroke had stopped the week before, and the broom went on over it without the half-check, and Jorra's bent finger stood in the cold iron of the damper.
