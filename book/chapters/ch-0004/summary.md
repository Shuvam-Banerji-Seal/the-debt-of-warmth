## Chronicle

On the eleventh stone the coals held under their ash, and Sella Vail touched the widow's mark at her throat before she opened the kiln and at no other time. The boy was five days old and could not keep the wool on his chin; she put the fold right without looking. Tarn took the small bench knife, set it back where it could be checked, and said so unasked; his broom stopped two strokes when a pot in the shed gave its flat note, and he did not turn his head. Jorra spoke to the pots, not the boy.

Alda Reed came to see the child she had delivered, reaching for a slate before him. A neighbour's child, three days returned, lay grey at the fingertips; Alda touched the tin inside her bodice, gave exact dates where comfort was asked for, and called it a graft settling. Dan knew the afterdrop: the cold that worsens at rescue, the face that smiles when it has given up. The women corrected him without knowing he had spoken. He reached for a pocket with no phone. Her slate went writing-down.

On the twelfth, the Hearth Drawing, Sella set the basket at the edge of the light in a neighbour's house, where he could see the work and not join it. The collector laid his hand on the slate before he looked at the man, took a cold iron tube from its cloth and held it without pressing; his fingers were exact and not cruel. The man gave with the small flat knock --- the fifth hearing, the first from a person. The measure was banked, the account stood, the return was recorded. The boy's knuckle went up an inch and came down.

Later the rekindled child grew worse, and the women worked with the certain kindness of a thing they had known all their lives. Alda turned a second note down under a milk cup. For two sentences the white light of another life took the room --- a nurse holding pressure, a hand going past a turned note toward a signature. He knew what the child was doing and had no sentence for the room. The grey ridge along the skirting board was thicker by a finger's width than the ridge at Vail, and turned the corner without a break.

At dusk the coals were banked without a word, and the fold put right without a look. Tarn swept again and set the knife back; Jorra came in from the back room with slate dust on his forearms and answered nothing. Dessa Silt stood at her threshold three doors down, called her cough weather, and counted nineteen steps into her thumb without looking into the basket. The boy kept four, four and a gap beside the two ridges, the charred slate and the barrow of grey. Sella fed him from the house's own dish, entered nowhere; Low Kiln said three days to the Five without saying what it counted.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Night of Day 6 in the Vail kiln-house. The boy lies on his bed under the roof with the fold at his chin and the cold coming in at the door's finger's width. Sella has banked the coals under ash with the back of a broken pot and put the fold right without looking at his face; she has said nothing about where they went, what was taken, or from whom. Tarn has swept the yard a second time and put the small bench knife back where it can be checked; Jorra has come in from the back room with slate dust worked into his forearms, said *the back room's cold* to nobody in particular, and shifted the damper again before Sella had finished. Dessa Silt is a figure at her own threshold three doors down, counting into her thumb, not looking into the basket. The boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch and comes down. The ridge turns the corner without a break, the door does not quite shut, and the count is four, four and a gap. Forward, not closed.

- **Day and time at the end.** Day 6 complete --- Stone 12, Y.H. 812, Secondday, the Hearth Drawing. Two lived days, Stone 11 (Day 5) and Stone 12 (Day 6), morning of the first through night of the second, with no gap after Day 4. Day 7 is Stone 13, Y.H. 812, a Thirdday. Low Kiln still gives three days to the Five; the Drawing is behind him.

- **Where everyone is now.** Corin/Dan is on his bed under the Vail roof, six days old, fed and banked-in, cold at the chin, awake, silent, with a thread of frozen drool wiped by nobody. Sella Vail is at the fire, having carried him to the Drawing and back without a word or a look into the basket; she touched the widow's mark only before the kiln opened on Stone 11, and not at the bank on Stone 12. She wants the fire kept, the house kept, and the keeping never explained. Tarn is in the yard with the second sweep done, his knife where it can be checked; his broom stopped two strokes at the Vail pot's flat note on Stone 11 and he did not name it. He wants the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and keeps the one thing he owns out of the house's reach. Jorra Vail is in the wheel-shed, slate dust on his forearms from the back room, speaking to pots and answering no questions; he wants the foundling outside the name Vail and the hollow seat his. Alda Reed has left the death-house with her tin still inside her bodice and two written notes turned writing-down, one on the bench at Vail, one slid under a milk cup; she wants the children warm and the account in its proper order, and will lie to keep both. The unnamed collector has gone out with his satchel and slate, one line spoken in the Court's soft passive voice; he believes he keeps the world alive and is not Marra and not the Cross clerk. Dessa Silt is at her threshold, cough called weather, thumb on the sieve-wire scar, believing her son spent among the Forty; she did not look into the basket and does not know he is in it. Garl Vail is absent, kept as coat, seat, ridge and proverb. The neighbour mother sits with her undrunk cup of milk; the rekindled child is worse on the third day; the man taken at the Drawing lies on his board-bed with a pale round place on his arm where the iron was. Berr Clay, Mira Silt, Marra and the wider world do not appear this chapter.

- **Items, money, powers.** Nothing gained, nothing lost. The pale stone remains in the fold of grey wool on the abandoned board-bed in House Silt, unmentioned. The small bench knife is back on the sill; the charred slate is still in the kiln ash; Sella's private roll, Berr's box and Mira's cord remain unshown. No coins change hands; the figures are one in twelve, three days to the Five, and a measure entered in a neat ordinary hand. No power used, no rank change: he remains a cracked, low-capacity, unranked vessel below Thin, unable to move Warm, clamp, speak, read Grathic, stand, roll, or lift a hand to his own chin. His instruments are still the count and the ear. The flat note now has five hearings from four sources --- a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail wheel-shed, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and a man giving at the Drawing --- with the fifth the first from a person. The cost is the same as before and worse: knowing exactly and being unable to act, plus the knowledge that a figure read aloud in a square is a measure taken out of a body. No Grathic word for the sound; nobody tapped anything for him.

- **Facts, lies, debts.** The year's first collection was taken at the Hearth Drawing from a dying neighbour and, before the day was out, the newly born child was worse on the third day of a return; the room called both ordinary. The collector said *the measure is banked; the account stands; the return is recorded* and entered the same figure the Cross had read aloud. Alda gave exact dates that answered no question, called the second chill a graft settling, and wrote what she did not leave lying about; the Court's struck word was never spoken in narration. The death-house Ash ridge is thicker by a finger's width than the Vail ridge and turns the corner without a break; the Vail house keeps its ridge, its charred slate, its hollow seat and its salt-stiff coat, and the debt against the firing of tiles lies uncalled. Sella said nothing about where they had been, and Jorra said nothing about the back room. No bargain, promise or debt was made or settled on stage.

- **For Chapter 5 and after.** Keep him six days old, cracked, mute, unranked, unable to read Grathic or reach a cup; keep the two hands separate, the three pauses unjoined and the gap unexplained. Remember the hearing count: five, four sources, one from a person; a Vail pot gave the note again and Tarn heard it. The Drawing has happened and the year's first measure is taken; nobody in the yard said what it was. Low Kiln still says three days to the Five. Day 7 is the quiet morning after --- Tarn sweeping, the knife back, Jorra at the wheel, the boy turning his head toward a pot before anyone taps it --- before the move down to the Fishstairs at dawn on Day 8. Do not explain the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate, the debt or why Sella took him; do not show the roll, the box, the cord or the tin's contents.
