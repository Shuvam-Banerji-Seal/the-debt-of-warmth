\daymark{5}{Stone 11, Y.H. 812 (Firstday)}

\dropcap{T}{he coals} had held. All night the ash had lain over them in a grey skin the colour of the sky before snow, level as water left to settle, and under the skin the small red lives had gone on the way a pulse goes on under a blanket: not loudly, not for anyone's hearing, but with the stubborn regularity of a thing that has been told to last and has decided to last. First light came into \place{the Vail kiln-house} through the oiled paper at the window and found the room as the night had left it --- the bench, the hollow seat, the salt-stiff coat on its peg keeping a broader man's shape than anyone living under the roof could fill, and the ridge of \lore{the Ash} along the skirting board running to the corner and turning it without a break. The ash was grey at the edges and the coals kept a red heart in the middle, and the red heart had not gone out in the night, which was the whole of the house's wealth and the whole of its condition. Behind the house \place{the Slip Brook} went over its stones with the small dirty sound it had kept all winter. The door at the threshold stood the width of a finger open, as it had stood for a year, letting in the dry cold that lifted the paper and would not let it settle.

\person{Sella Vail} came in before the yard was properly awake. She went to the mark before she went to the kiln. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she took it between finger and thumb with the burn on the back of her hand half-closing as she did it --- two straight sides and a lintel, the skin inside puckered and pale, the little door shutting on nothing because the hand held nothing but the cord. She held it a moment longer than the touch required, longer than opening a kiln had ever required of her, and then she let it go and lifted the lid and fed the coals. The heat came up with the smell of clay and old smoke, and the room took it in the way a cup takes water, slowly, at the edges first. She stood a half-pace off while it drew, counting something under her breath that might have been breaths and might have been spoons. She did not say why she had touched the mark, and there was nobody in the room to ask.

The kiln took its first breath of the day and went on taking it in fours: four, and four, and a gap where the fifth should have been. She put a hand near the damper and did not move it yet. “Feed the fire before you feed yourself,” she said to the fire. “It'll still be there in the morning. You won't.” The proverb had been \person{Garl Vail}'s once, improved by nine years of use, and she said it to the fire the way the house said everything that mattered --- to the thing that could not answer, and never to the person who was listening.

\person{Corin} lay on his bed under the roof with his fifth morning coming in at his ear and no way to close it out. He was five days old and awake, and the fold had ridden back off the ear, and the scrap of wool laid over his chin had gone the way all such scraps went --- up toward the eye or down into the collar --- and lay now across his cheek doing no work at all. He knew the shape of the cold before it arrived: the way it came in at cheek and temple and worked inward, a slow level rising, and what it would do if he let it rise. He had spent five days learning that the body he was in kept its own ledger and would not be argued out of it. It wanted the breast. It wanted the wool up. It wanted the head to stay where it was put, and the head went where it wanted, and the small wet sound came up in his throat when he turned it past a point. The man inside it knew the order in which a body gives up its heat and the exact feel of a patient going down that road, and could do nothing with the knowledge but recognise it. Recognition was the whole of his instrument now. He had once had a tray of instruments and a room with white light; now he had an ear and a count and a hand that went up an inch. That hand went up an inch toward the wool and came down again on the straw with a small sound that was not a word.

She put the fold right without looking at him. The hand came across, found the edge of the wool, tucked it under the chin and along the ear, and went back to the kiln; at no point did her eyes leave the fire. “There's a draught under that door,” she said to the fire. “Keep it off the boy.” The instruction went to the fire, which would not answer, and not to the boy, who lay under the wool and heard every word and was the person it was about and could not say so.

\scenebreak

The household went about its order. \person{Tarn} had been sweeping since before the light, a bent man of twenty-two with the Ash in the creases of his knuckles that no scrubbing lifted and a pale ring round his left wrist where an iron clip had sat six years ago. He moved quickly, the way men move who expect to be told to stop, and he swept the yard as if the day's account depended on the broom going over the same boards in the same order. He called everyone house, including Sella, and said \emph{I owe} where another man said \emph{I am}, and the boy had already learned to hear the difference between the deference and the nail in it. Six years of a seven lay behind him and he would not count the seventh, and the not-counting was the one thing in the house nobody could check. Halfway through the sweeping his hand went to the small bench knife on the sill and put it in his pocket, and came back a moment later without it, and set it down again where it could be checked.

“I took the small knife,” he said, in the direction of the bench. “I put it back. You can check.”

Nobody had asked him to check it. He said it the way another man might have said the weather was holding --- as a fact one person had established and another might verify at leisure --- and went on sweeping. The broom went over the boards. His hands knew where the knife was without looking, which was the whole of the joke and not a funny one.

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} was at the wheel with slip dried to the colour of old bone up both forearms, the left little finger bent permanently inward from a splinter taken at eleven. He did not look toward the house when Sella spoke, and he obeyed her before she had finished speaking, which was how the house knew what he would not say. He had not looked at her since the boy came under the roof, and the household had learned to read the not-looking the way another house reads a door slammed hard. He turned the wheel with his weight in the hollow his father had worn on the left and spoke to the pots as other men speak to animals.

“You'll do,” he said to a jar still soft on the wheel.

He said it with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and said nothing at all to the boy, who lay three paces away under a roof that did not divide itself for him. He shifted the damper before Sella had finished telling him to, which was his way of agreeing with her and refusing her in the same movement, and went back to the wheel with his eyes on the clay.

Then the pot was set down. It went onto the bench in the shed carefully, by a man who knew what the thing would bear, and it gave its small answer through the wall: a flat knock with no body behind it, the shadow of \lore{the full note} stopping at the surface of the clay and going no further. It was not the noise of the pot he heard --- the wood of the bench, the drag of a sleeve --- but the small dry answer underneath it, the note that stopped at the surface of the thing instead of going through it. A sound vessel answered full and went on answering after the hand had gone away; a cracked one gave this, a knock with nothing behind it. He knew the difference the way he knew the difference between pulsing blood and welling blood, and the knowing had never once been of use to him here. \person{Daniel Marchetti} had heard that sound from a cup across a birth room, from a pot under this same roof, and twice from the district's own slate in a market two days gone. He set this hearing beside the others the way he set his numbers down, and did not make them agree. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} for the sound and no need of one, and nobody in the house tapped a pot for him. The knuckle that would have done it lay outside the fold with milky nails on it and went up an inch and came down, and there was no surface within two paces of it that it could have reached.

The broom stopped. It stopped for two strokes --- not a full stop, only the space between one stroke and the next held a fraction longer, as if the man holding it had heard something in the shed and was deciding whether the turning of his head was worth the trouble. He did not turn it. He did not name the sound, or say that it was a sound at all. Then the broom went on, and the two strokes went under the ordinary noise of the house, and there was nobody under the roof who had seen anything in the stopping to remark on. The boy lay with the wall between him and the shed and the fold over his ear, and heard the broom go on, and kept the small interval where it had been put, without a word to put with it and without anyone to say the word to.

\scenebreak

Food came out of the house's own dish. Sella fed whoever was hungry from it, the boy included, and did not call the feeding by any finer name. Barley gruel, bread with the crust left on, a scrap of something salted from yesterday --- these went around the room in the order of hunger rather than the order of claim, and when a neighbour's child came in at the door the dish was handed over without a word about whose it was. At the threshold a woman from two houses down put her head in and asked after the boy without asking after him, the way the district did everything.

“And how's the little one?” she said to the room at large.

“Warm,” Sella said to the fire. “That's all he is.”

The woman went away with the answer she had come for, which was none, and the house went on with its spoonfuls. “The house keeps a fire,” Sella said, when the question of the boy's keep hung in the room without anybody putting it into words. “That's the whole of what I know.” Faith was done in the same domestic way. A cup sat sunk in a little shelf by the door that nobody used and nobody moved, and when the gruel was served Sella's thumb touched its rim for half a beat; a half-sentence went to the pot before the first spoonful, worn by use from whatever it had once been into an instruction about the draught. It was not prayer and it was not not-prayer, and the boy had already learned that in this house the two were the same gesture done quickly and not discussed afterward.

Money was not spoken of directly, which did not mean it was absent. \lore{The levy} went around the room the way a hole in the wall goes around a poor house --- spoken around rather than named, with a lowering of the voice and a glance at the door. Bits were owed and put off to the thaw. A firing of tiles was mentioned and then not mentioned, and the space where it had been was left with the same care Sella used when she set a cup down near the edge of a table. The district was saying three days to \lore{the Five}, and the boy had the date of \lore{the Hearth Drawing} by heart the way he had the shape of the door, and nobody under the roof had said what either number was for, or what would be drawn, or from whom.

Weather was the thing everybody agreed about because it was the only thing that cost nobody anything to name. The dry cold got under the oiled paper and lifted it; \place{the Ilm} lay under its skin of ice a short walk south and the district said without saying that the ice was holding; \place{Low Kiln} went on with its fires and its rolls the way a pot goes on holding water with a crack in it that nobody mentions until the thaw. The Return was present in the house as a habit and nothing more --- a fire banked at night, a roll kept in a careful hand, a coat left on its peg --- and nobody explained it, because nobody in a clay house explains the thing that keeps it warm. The boy, who could not have found \place{the Clay Yard} without being carried, had the whole of the district's arithmetic by heart and no way to spend a single figure of it.

Garl's objects kept their places. The wheel-seat stayed hollow on the left and Sella would not let anyone fill it. The coat kept the salt stiffness in it and the broad shape on the peg, and Jorra had twice reached for it in the dark and stopped. The Ash ridge ran along the skirting board with its sour mineral edge, and the house had not swept it in a year and would not sweep it before the Last Day. In the kiln ash the charred edge of a slate lay black on one side and eaten into the shape of a strip of bark, the writing gone off the end, and the house had swept around it for a year without picking it up. “Leave the crack showing,” the house said to the pot, in the dead man's improved voice. “People trust a thing they can see has been handled.”

The arithmetic was performed again without anyone performing it. The foundling had the bed under the roof, warm and watched and fed from the house's own dish; the clay-bound hand had \place{the Debt Loft} above the shed and a broom and the small knife back where it could be checked. The two facts sat side by side in the room like two cups set down at the same distance from the fire, one of which would be warmer before the morning was out. Nobody called the arrangement a decision and nobody called it kindness, and the two words would have described the same act equally badly. The part of him that had once stood in a bay and sorted twenty bodies into three piles read it in a glance and found the ordinary shape of it, and the part of him that wanted the breast read it as cold and hunger, and neither part had a sentence that could be said in that room. The house straightened around a silence, and the boy was inside the silence, and the warmth went where it had been decided warmth should go, and no entry was made of it anywhere.

The pot's note went into the house's noise and was gone. The coals held under their ash. The boy's hand outside the fold went up an inch toward the wheel-shed and came down, and the door at the threshold stood its finger's width open, and the kiln breathed four, four, and a gap, and the count stayed where it had been put.
