# Chapter 4 — “The Hearth Drawing” — beat sheet (private)

**Days covered.** Day 5 (Stone 11, Y.H. 812, Firstday) and Day 6 (Stone 12, Y.H. 812, Secondday, **the Hearth Drawing**). Two lived days, no gaps, no month jumped. Day 4 is finished and is not revisited as a scene. Five scenes, 13,500 budgeted words (2700 + 2500 + 3100 + 2800 + 2400), comfortably over the 12,000 floor. `mode: normal`; `pov` stays with Dan.

**Governing constraints for every scene.** He is five then six days old, cracked, low-capacity, unranked — below Thin, twelve to fifteen embers at best. No Warm moved, no clamp, no way cut, no rank change, no speech, no Grathic read or written, no hand that can reach a cup two paces off or lift to his own chin. The only instruments are the old-life habits: the clinical ear (now about to hear its first body), the reading of hand-grips and not-lookings, and the count. The body humiliates him in every scene — drool, the head that goes where it wants, the blanket that will not stay on, the hand outside the fold going up an inch and coming down. Play it exact, never for easy pathos. Present tense only for the Chicago rewarming memory in Scene 4; **no remembered voice is tagged with `\memory{...}` this chapter** — Ostrowski's market voice stands alone behind her one appearance in Chapter 3. No recap of the Cross. No recap of Ostrowski's two duties or Vesci's sentence. Let the reader be as lost as Dan is.

**No power growth, honestly accounted.** There is no breakthrough in this chapter and none is faked. The ear acquires a new *object* (a person giving, not a vessel of clay or slate) and that is all; it buys him nothing. The "training" present is the household's — Tarn's ear (T076), Jorra's full note kept unspoken, Alda's craft — all of it planted toward later arcs and all of it invisible to the boy as knowledge he could use. The cost of the chapter is the same as the cost of Chapter 3: knowing exactly and being unable to act, plus the new knowledge that a figure read in a square is a measure taken out of a body. Record no rank change in `memory/state.md` when closing; record only the fifth hearing and the first from a person.

## Scene 1 — “The Coals Under Ash”

- **Day and time.** Day 5, Stone 11, Y.H. 812, Firstday; first light into mid-morning. Start on the house, not on a thought. Open with the `\dropcap{T}{he coals}` paragraph at least three lines long, per `design/AUTHOR-API.md`.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house and wheel-shed, the Clay Yard, Low Kiln. The Slip Brook behind it; the Ilm under ice a short walk south; the door that does not quite shut.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan; Sella Vail; Tarn; Jorra Vail; Garl present as objects and proverbs (hollow wheel-seat, salt-stiff coat on its peg, Ash ridge, *leave the crack showing*). No Alda, no Dessa, no collector.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The banked coals under their ash held through the night. Sella comes in, touches the widow's mark at her throat with the little-door burn half-closing, and only then opens the kiln. Do not say why she touches it; let the Greybank confirmation of Chapter 3 hang in the room unspoken (T079).
  2. The boy on his bed under the roof, five days old, awake, the fold ridden back at his ear and a scrap of wool over his chin that will not stay. He knows the shape of the cold coming in through cheek and temple; he cannot pull the wool up. Sella puts the fold right without looking and says the instruction to the fire or the pot, never to him (T060, T058).
  3. Household work in its ordinary order: Tarn sweeping before anyone wakes, Ash in the creases of his knuckles, the pale ring at his left wrist; he pockets the small bench knife and then takes it out and puts it back where it can be checked, unasked (T036). Jorra at the wheel with slip dried to old bone up both forearms and the bent little finger, speaking to the pots — *you'll do* — and not to the boy. The kiln breathes four, four and a gap (T065).
  4. A pot is set down in the wheel-shed and gives its small flat note through the wall. Dan hears it as the same sound the cup made across the birth room and the district's slate made twice at the Cross; he does not join them and there is no Grathic word for any of them (T061). Tarn's broom stops for two strokes. He does not turn his head, does not name the sound, and goes on sweeping. **The reader sees the second ear in the house; nobody in the house sees it** (T076). Say nothing about the loan that can hear being a loan that can be called — that is for later chapters.
  5. Food, money, weather, faith as domestic fact: barley gruel or bread from the house's own dish, the levy spoken around rather than named, bits owed and put off to the thaw, the dry cold lifting the oiled paper, the Ilm holding under ice. The Return is present only as the household's habit of keeping a fire and a roll, not as a lecture. Sella feeds whoever is hungry from her own dish and calls the boy *the boy* and the house *the house*.
  6. The bed-under-the-roof / loft-above-the-shed arithmetic is performed again without commentary: the foundling is warmed and watched, the debt is given the loft. Dan reads it with the part of him that was a surgeon and cannot say a word about it (T060 triage, withheld as such).
- **Emotional turn.** From the small competence of the banked fire to the humiliating exactness of a man who can hear a flaw through a wall and cannot lift a hand to the thing that made the sound. The house straightens around a silence; he is inside the silence.
- **Sensory anchors.** Coals grey under ash; lead-glaze mark on its cord; little-door burn closing on nothing; wet wool and woodsmoke; slip the colour of old bone; the small flat knock of a pot through a wall; the scrape of a broom stopping; the sour mineral edge of the Ash ridge; dry cold pulling at oiled paper.
- **Threads advanced.** T061, T076, T071/T072, T009, T060, T058, T079, T065, T077.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Paid: nothing yet; this is the setup for the Drawing. Planted: Tarn's ear and his "one year to go" remain unsaid but present in his exactness; Jorra's dust and the back room are held for Scene 5 and later arcs; the kiln's gap stays unexplained. Withheld: why Sella took the boy, the contents of her private roll, the charred slate's writing, the meaning of the flat note.
- **Exact last image.** The boy's small hand outside the fold goes up an inch toward the direction of the wheel-shed and comes down; the pot's note is gone into the house's noise; the coals hold under their ash with the door not quite shutting and the count still four, four and a gap.

## Scene 2 — “Dates Where Comfort Was Asked For”

- **Day and time.** Day 5, Stone 11, mid-morning into afternoon.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house threshold and the bench by the door; the clay yard beyond it. A neighbour's house is spoken of but not entered. House Reed and the birth-flower shrine are named as directions only.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan; Alda Reed; Sella Vail (present, mostly silent); a neighbour mother and her rekindled child (walk-ons, no sheet); the house's ordinary voices.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Alda arrives under the Vail roof to look at the child she delivered — the arrangement between her and Sella is a silence with a door in it; neither opens it. She reaches for the slate habit before the child, checks the fold at his shoulder, and crosses two fingers at the red edge of the flower on his wrist without knowing she does it in front of him (T007). Do not let her say what the sign is.
  2. A neighbour's child is carried in. Three days out of a thin return, worse: the second chill. The mother asks for comfort in the only way the district permits — a practical question about what to do. Alda touches the tin once inside her bodice (T023) and answers with exact dates: which day the account went up, which day the paper went out, what the midwife set down. When she is lying she becomes unusually exact; the reader must hear the exactness, not a verdict.
  3. The women explain the second chill as a graft settling. They know the phrase from their mothers and from the order; they do not know the phrase the Court struck from its record in Y.H. 651. Let the midwife's words do the work and never write the Court's term in narration (T012, seed).
  4. Dan reads the child the way he read rewarming patients: worse before better, the cold that will not clot, the face that looks like a smile when it is dying. He knows the afterdrop. Every woman in the room has a better, kinder, older explanation, and each corrects him without knowing he has spoken — a look, a proverb, a hand moving the child away from his draught. He says nothing (T012).
  5. Alda writes on her own slate and turns the writing down before she sets it anywhere a mother or an officer might read. She gives the mother an instruction about the pot and the door and goes on giving dates after the question has stopped. The tin goes back inside the bodice, unopened (T023).
  6. Dan's hand goes to the place where a pocket should have been. There is no phone, no coat, no lit name he will look at. The gesture is a habit, not a thought, and the wool closes over it (T004, T044).
- **Emotional turn.** The only adult in the room is a man with the hands of a child, and his exact knowledge is not merely useless but unwelcome — the room has arranged itself around a kinder wrong answer. He recognises the shape of a chart signed without reading the nurse's note, but the recognition stays in his interior and is not named as a planted thread.
- **Sensory anchors.** Tin on its cord against warm milk and tallow; slate chalk and the turned-down writing; a child's greyish fingertips (described as colour, never diagnosed); a fold pressed flat too long; the dry cold at the threshold; the exact dates arriving faster than they have any business arriving.
- **Threads advanced.** T012 (planted: two causes, one withheld), T023, T007, T004, T044, T060.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Planted: the second chill and the tin, toward the floorboard discovery years away; the Court's struck word is never said. Paid: nothing (the sign T007 repeats for the fourth time in as many days, still uncovered). Withheld: the second cause, the contents of the tin, Dessa's lie, the word forty, Marra, any Grathic name for the flat note.
- **Exact last image.** The neighbour's child is wrapped and carried out into the yard; Alda's hand closes the tin away inside her bodice and her other hand turns the slate's writing down on the bench; the boy's hand, having reached a place where no pocket is, closes on the fold of wool and stays there.

## Scene 3 — “The Cold Iron Tube”

- **Day and time.** Day 6, Stone 12, Y.H. 812, Secondday — the Hearth Drawing; early morning into mid-morning.
- **Place.** A neighbour's house in Low Kiln, the room where the collection is made. Witnessed from a basket set just inside the door or at the edge of the room — never from inside the collector's table. Slate Row and the Waiting Room are spoken of as directions, not entered.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan; Sella Vail; the collector (one function, one line, no sheet — walk-on); the dying person and the household around them (walk-ons); Alda Reed if the day calls for her as collector's assistant; no Berr, no clerk from the Cross.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries the boy in the basket. She does not set it where a slate lies open. She sets it where he can see the work and cannot be part of it, and says something to the wool or the wind rather than to him. The year's first collection has come to the district; the boy has had the date by heart since the Cross (T077).
  2. The procedure written as surgery is written: the field, the exposure, the cold iron tube against a way, the exact unhurried hand, the response of the vessel, the complication, the cost. Nobody chants; nobody prays over the tube. The room is ordinary — a pot, a bench, a draught under a door, the smell of tallow and wet wool, the same materials as the Vail house. The collector's fingers are exact and not cruel; his cuff is soft at the wrist; he sets his hand on the slate before he looks at the person waiting, the same grammar the Cross clerk used, but **he is not that clerk and not Marra** (T075, T061).
  3. The collector speaks once, in the Court's soft passive voice: the measure is banked; the account stands; the return is recorded. He has a reason, and the reason is the world kept alive. Do not make him a monster and do not let him explain the Return to the room. If he is given a second line, a full sheet must be created at close; the plan is one line, so he goes in `characters/_minor.md` as a walk-on.
  4. The dying vessel gives. The sound is the small flat knock Dan has heard from a cup across the birth room, a pot in the Vail house, and the district's slate twice at the Cross — now the **fifth hearing and the first from a person** (T061). He knows it without being told as the sound a body makes when it gives way: the walls going, the measure going out, the sound stopping at the surface. Do not write the Grathic word for it; there isn't one in the boy's ear. Do not explain the Warm, the Hearth, collection or banking to the reader as fact — only what the room does and says.
  5. The measure is booked. A number on a slate is the same number as the one read at the Cross on Stone 10, but here it came out of a man. He cannot reach the tube, read what is written, or lift a knuckle toward the slate; the hand outside the fold goes up an inch and comes down (T077, T015, T009 by contrast with Garl left where he lay).
  6. **The account read aloud at the Cross has become an account taken from a person.** Let the juxtaposition do the work; do not state it as a theme.
- **Emotional turn.** The clinical ear finally has the correct object and it is a dying body, and the correctness is the whole humiliation: he knows what just happened better than anyone in the room and is the least able to act on it. The man with the reason goes on being reasonable.
- **Sensory anchors.** Cold iron going cold in the hand; a cuff soft from washing; chalk on slate; the small flat knock with no body behind it after it has finished; tallow, wet wool, a pot on a bench; the draught under the door; the grey of Ash in a corner noticed but not read.
- **Threads advanced.** T061 (fifth hearing, first from a body), T077, T009, T015, T075.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Paid: the flat note travels from cup → pot → slate twice → a person; the figure of Stone 10 becomes a taking. Planted: the collector's grammar migrates toward Derren and Wessen years away; the passive voice is a tool, not a protection. Withheld: no explanation of the Return, no joining of the three pauses, no name for Marra or the collector, no one in three, no number forty, no second cause.
- **Exact last image.** The slate lies under the collector's hand with the measure entered in a neat, ordinary hand; the boy's knuckle outside the fold goes up an inch and comes down; the flat knock goes into the room's ordinary noise with nobody tapping anything for him.

## Scene 4 — “Worse on the Third Day”

- **Day and time.** Day 6, Stone 12, later in the morning, still at the neighbour's house; into the afternoon of the same day.
- **Place.** The neighbour's death-house, Low Kiln — the same house as Scene 3. The Vail ridge and House Silt are spoken of in Dan's interior comparison, not re-entered.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan; the rekindled child from Scene 2; its mother; Alda Reed; two or three midwives and household women (walk-ons); Sella Vail (present, at the edge, silent).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The child Alda was called to on Stone 11 is worse on the third day of its return. The room fills with women who know what this is and what to do about it; they do not raise their voices, because this is ordinary work. A graft is settling, they say; that is the second chill; it comes to some and they come through it or they do not.
  2. Alda touches the tin once, inside her bodice, and writes a second note which she turns down and does not leave lying about (T023). She gives dates where comfort is asked for. The sign at a flower's edge, if she makes it, is made unaware (T007).
  3. Dan reads the worsening as the Afterdrop he knew in a trauma bay: the patient gets worse at the moment of rescue; the cold patient cannot clot; the grimace of severe cold resembles a smile; you do not rub a frozen foot; rewarming has its own treachery. Let the Chicago memory break in present tense for two sentences only, with no name and no voice: the white light, the body on the table, the nurse holding pressure in a cavity with both hands, the hand on the chart. Then back to past tense and the clay yard (bible/voice.md, §Person/tense). **No `\memory{...}` this chapter.**
  4. Every woman in the room corrects him — not with an argument, but with the small, certain kindness of a hand moving the child, a proverb addressed to the pot, a date that closes the question. He is the man who decided in a bay and now cannot get a sentence into the room; he says nothing (T012).
  5. His hand goes again to the place where a pocket should have been. The lit rectangle with a name on it is not looked at here either; the debt of nine summers of a boat never bought is carried as an absence, not told (T004, T044).
  6. He looks at the Ash ridge along the skirting board of the death-house. It is thicker by a finger's width than the ridge in the Vail house, and it turns the corner without a break. He puts it with the Vail ridge and the charred edge of the slate in the Vail kiln ash; nobody has explained any of the three (T013, T031).
  7. The plain fact closes the scene: the year's first measure has been taken from the dying and from the newly born on the same day, and the district will go on calling the day ordinary. Do not say what the measure is or where it goes.
- **Emotional turn.** From the recognisable mechanics of a body failing to the certainty that this knowledge will never be admitted in the room — the surgeon corrected by midwives who are kinder and more useful than he is; the old life arriving in the present tense and being useless there too.
- **Sensory anchors.** Warm milk, soap, tallow; a child's shallow breathing; the grey ridge under a skirting board measured by a finger's width; the white ceiling light of the other life for two sentences; slate chalk; the hand reaching for nothing in a fold of wool.
- **Threads advanced.** T012, T023, T004, T044, T013, T007.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Paid: the afterdrop recognition planted in Chapter 1 is exercised again and corrected again. Planted: the second cause of the second chill (withheld as fact), Alda's second note and tin toward the floorboard, the Ash as what is left when warmth is taken (the reader can only see the grey). Withheld: no Court term, no diagnosis, no Second Want named, no explanation of the Return, no joining of pauses.
- **Exact last image.** The grey ridge in the corner of the death-house, thicker by a finger's width than the one under the Vail skirting board, turning the corner without a break; the boy's hand closed on the fold where a phone would have been, the count of the day going into the place nobody can tax.

## Scene 5 — “The Ridge Turns the Corner”

- **Day and time.** Day 6, Stone 12, dusk into early night. Close of chapter.
- **Place.** The Vail kiln-house, the Clay Yard, the doorstep of House Silt three doors down; the Slip Brook behind; the Ilm under ice beyond.
- **Present.** Corin/Dan; Sella Vail; Tarn; Jorra Vail; Garl as absence; Dessa Silt as a background figure at her own threshold (brief, no dialogue with the boy, no recognition).
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella lifts the boy out of the basket at the Vail house and puts the ridden-back fold right without looking. She banks the coals. She does not touch the widow's mark now — it is touched only before the kiln is opened — and says nothing about where they went or what was taken (T058, T079, T060).
  2. Tarn sweeps the yard a second time, puts the small bench knife back where it can be checked, and says nothing about the pot's flat note. The loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; keep that unsaid (T036, T076).
  3. Jorra comes in from the back room with slate dust on his forearms and answers no question about where he has been. He speaks to the wheel or the pot, not to the boy, and not to Sella about the boy. The broken slates under the drying rack remain unshown; the men who come into the back room are not named (T035).
  4. On the walk or from the basket's angle, Dessa Silt is at her own threshold three doors down: cough from the pits that she calls weather, thumb on the pale sieve-wire scar, the *door shut* smile, nineteen steps counted because counting keeps a larger thing out of the room. She does not look into the basket and does not know her son is in it. She believes him spent among the Forty; the figure the boy heard at the Cross has become, for her, a person who is gone (T008, T073). Do not expose the lie; do not let her speak his name or any name.
  5. The boy sets the death-house ridge beside the Vail ridge and the charred edge of the slate in the kiln ash, and files them with the barrow of grey that went toward the north gate on Stone 10. Three facts nobody has explained and nobody asked him to understand (T013, T031, T009).
  6. The number the district gives out is still three days to the Five; the Hearth Drawing is now behind him and the year's first measure has been taken. He keeps four, four and a gap — the kiln, the floorboards, the bell — and the gap is the part nobody has thought to enter (T071/T072, T077).
  7. He is fed from the house's own dish and warmed at the house's fire, neither warmth entered anywhere; the house that paid twice has now taken a measure from the district and goes on keeping the boy without saying so (T060, T044).
- **Emotional turn.** The boy returns from a room where a number was taken out of a person to a house that keeps its own account otherwise — a fire banked without an entry, a fold put right without a look, a debt uncalled. The knowledge has changed the account and not the household; the household's not-knowing is its own kind of keeping. Forward, not closed.
- **Sensory anchors.** Banked coals under ash; the small knife going back to its place; slate dust on a thrower's forearms; wet wool and woodsmoke; a cough called weather and a thumb on a pale scar; grey Ash turning a corner without a break; the charred edge of a slate in kiln ash; the door not quite shutting.
- **Threads advanced.** T013, T009, T031, T035, T036, T058, T008, T073, T071/T072, T060, T077.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid.** Paid: the Ash barrow of Stone 10 is joined to the ridges and the charred slate in the boy's private file of unexplained facts; the uncalled debt sits beside the measure that *was* called. Planted: Jorra's slate dust and back room toward the Broken Slates; Tarn's ear toward his later refusal; Dessa's cough toward the Slip Brook; the charred slate toward the spiral. Withheld: no explanation of the bell, the flower, the mark, the charred slate, the debt, or why Sella took him; no forty; no Marra; no one in three; no joining of the three pauses; Sella's roll and Berr's box stay unshown.
- **Exact last image.** The coals banked under their ash, the little-door burn closing on nothing as the fold is put right, the Ash ridge along the skirting board turning the corner without a break, and the boy's hand outside the wool going up an inch and coming down — the count still four, four and a gap, Low Kiln saying three days to the Five without saying what it counted, the door not quite shutting.

## How the last moment pulls into Chapter 5

The chapter ends on the banked coals and the ridge turning the corner, with the count four, four and a gap and the boy's hand going up an inch and coming down. The year's first measure has been taken from a person and nobody in the yard has said what it was; the house has kept its fire and its boy without entering either. That held, unspoken account is what Chapter 5, **“Nineteen Steps”** (Days 7–9, Stone 13–15), opens onto: the quiet morning after the Drawing, Tarn sweeping the yard a second time with the small knife back in his pocket, Jorra at the wheel, the boy turning his head toward a pot before anyone taps it, and then the move down to the Fishstairs at dawn where Mira Silt sells eels and rumours, flicks her eel-bones and keeps a cord nobody has shown him. The reader leaves a house that counts in coals and gaps and enters a market that counts in knots and bones — the same account, still unentered, still his to notice. No explanation has been given and no arc is closed; the story goes forward into the thaw.
