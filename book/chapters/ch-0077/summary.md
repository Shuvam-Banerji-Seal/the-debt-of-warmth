## Chronicle

Frost 13 came on as an ordinary tending day: no market, no Cross, no bell. Sella went to the hearth before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with a broken pot and said *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*. Her finger stayed above the charcoal, her lips on an unfigured two-gapped row, and no instruction went to the basket. The boy lay with one leathery crust two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed, the thread frozen and unwiped, cold at cheek and wrist, and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and could not close two inches on a pot handle; both were true, and the second was louder.

The yard went around its seven places with a look, a wide step and no number: two unswept lines, the birth-slates under the rack, the gone pan's ring, the skinned trough, the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out. The frost lay white on the well lip and the plate of ice stood on its stone; the yard called both weather. Garl stayed in objects --- coat, hollow wheel-seat, uncalled tile debt --- and his name had not been spoken under the roof for three days. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip; Jorra put his bent finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence.

On the fourteenth afternoon a woman at the well spoke to the boards, not into the basket: the runner who carried Berr Clay's slate had once carried a figure that named a person she knew. No owner stood to be asked, no room, no hand at the writing, no figure, no cause. Ilva drew water beside her, rack dust in a thumb seam; asked what the firings had taken, she answered only *Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account*, and her notched strip stayed in her sleeve. The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes a wound and filed it beside the runner's eighth hand with a finger between: a shaping toward a far-off choosing, not a hearing, not a confession, not paid.

Through three days the hand rose an inch six times and came down on the river's note --- a rhythm carried across a silence, intakes held apart and bridged, a letting-go kept whole, the held interval beside the empty one --- each exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. At the close two hundred and eighty-four stood unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and one hundred and four days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer unnamed. The file lay open on the towel, every gap left gap, the crust two inches off, and the river's note went quiet.
