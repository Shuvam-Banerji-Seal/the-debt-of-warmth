\daymark{283}{Frost 14, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours went off the coals the way a pot goes cold when it is lifted to the stone and left there: not in a single surrender but by slow degrees, the heat drawing back from the rim and sitting behind the pot where a hand would find it still if a hand went there. No hand went there. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, \person[Corin]{the boy} lay carried and awake on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, the hundred and fortieth answer laid down behind him as a man lays a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Outside the wool the dark had the taste of yesterday's smoke in it. Nothing in it announced what the morning would bring; a day brings its own news when it comes.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly; the knowledge was exact and bought him nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and three days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. The room smelled of hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner called \lore{the thaw}. Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have stood. Agreeing any two would want a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any. No fire-sitter stood over the coals and no house count came to be borrowed.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The heard bell's four, four and a gap stayed in \place{the Cross} beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not brought under this roof on a Fourthday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The count crossed from two hundred and eighty-two to two hundred and eighty-three the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. Two hundred and eighty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}; six months and one hundred and three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The house kept its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, and gave nobody a number to carry. The room whose floor does not touch his gave its low counting --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy took only what the hand could take and left the letting-go where it was.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to anything else, as she had come to it on the mornings behind and would come to it on the mornings ahead. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing as she reached the coals, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light. She told them it was the thaw. \emph{You'll go when you go}, she told them, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, and her hand did not go to it at a morning and in a basket, because neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with its face turned away; she did not look at it again for a lesson, and four openings were four and not a rule, and the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for.

At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She set the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, left the thread frozen where it lay, and stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. She gave no instruction at any hour to the basket. He was set under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}; this was a keeping and not a kindness anybody had named. The finding that a house keeps itself by \lore{not-doings} stayed on a place of its own, with the boy's own gaps on another and a finger between, and the morning did not lay one across the other.

Out at \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone. She lifted a corner of the wool and turned the grain once beneath it with a lighter touch than on the day behind, and set the corner down again, and gave no figure to what she had turned. The arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 had not known what it cost; the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it, and the turning of the grain did not sign it now. Frost's first arithmetic stayed locked up the \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}; the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Nothing under this roof had opened the box or copied the slate, and nothing under this roof would bring any of the reading back as a lesson, a figure, a mending or a payment.

Sella carried him down at the half-pace from every open slate. Out in \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The clean dark line at the Vail corner lay where it lay, and the finer grey hairline beside it, which the house called the thaw, lay beside it unswept; beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates kept their place; the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print and no word; \place{the trough} kept its skin over its water and the yard called the water weather; out on \place{the worked strips} the field kept its empty place; and in the returning line a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account. The missing hand was carried, not named, not paid, not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or anything else that waited with its own name kept.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of \place{the yard well}, where the yard had left it and gone on calling it weather, and the plate of ice lifted off the trough stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. The rope had stiffened; nobody had given either a figure and nobody had carried either away. The surgeon's reading of a skin laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, with a finger between; he made no fourth likeness of the three and no cause of any two, and the morning going up over the yard paid none of it. Nobody named a payer to either the white or the ice.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, which nobody filled; the clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept both. The debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled, and his proverb had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-three days. No collector had come up the clay road on the mornings behind and none had gone down it, and the household went around the debt as Sella went around \lore{Ash}, without a figure and without a name.

The held counts stood where they had been stood and none was advanced. The ownerless question kept five askings with one further shaping, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named; the silence was a keeping and not a sixth witness. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth unread in her sleeve a pace away, joined to none and taken for no figure off the wood. Six hearings stood with no seventh. The seventh green stood a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either; the three pot sayings kept their own tray with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place apart and no fourth likeness made a cause; burned slate stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two. No third gap came into the fold and no fourth likeness was made a cause. The held interval and the empty interval lay side by side with a finger between and were not promoted into a reach, a hearing, a gift or a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stands.

Frost 14 came on in its own light and was not brought into the day behind it. No figure from the tenth's reading reached the small hours, and the first reading was not looked at again as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment. There was no market beyond the door, no gathering at the Cross and no bell; a Fourthday was an ordinary tending day and went about its work as a pot goes about cooling. Frost 20 (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood four mornings ahead and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. The light went first along the well's white lip, then across \place{the flagstones} where the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} sat in the seams and the shallow bright patch kept its place since Seed 22 under a broom stroke without the half-check; then the light came in under the shelf where the boy lay, thin and cold and exact as a strip of iron laid on stone. The kiln answered in fours with its gap and not a bell. Nothing in the light foretold a word from the well's direction; the well lay beyond the door as a direction arrived nowhere. He counted this morning without asking anybody what the count was for; the wanting of a number spoken went on under the not-expecting the way a coal goes on under ash, and there was nobody in the room who would have known what a number counted if it had been spoken.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It took the small intake of one count from the room whose floor does not touch his, held it past that count's letting-go and through the little silence that came after it, and came down on the river's next low note of water over water. The little silence was left whole and empty and was not filled to make a row come out even. No house count was borrowed for it and no fifth was supplied to the kiln. It was an honest coordination on the sixth place, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a held change, not a key, not a power, not a doctrine and not a rule. One hundred and forty-one stood beside one hundred and forty. The district's arithmetic had changed on the tenth where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back; the boy's arithmetic did not change and went on carrying one small intake across one small silence on an ordinary Fourthday, and both went on without an argument between them. The surgeon's clean observation of how a count ends was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and eighty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The boy lay with the crust two inches off and its empty place unnamed, the hand laid down on the hundred and forty-first answer, and the file open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap. Beyond the finger's-width door the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool at the rack end, and the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere. Frost 20 (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood four mornings ahead and was not brought into this day. The river's low note went quiet after the hand came down.
