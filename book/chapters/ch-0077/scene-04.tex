The afternoon thinned without ending; the light went off it the way warmth goes out of a pot lifted to the stone and left there, drawing back from the rim and sitting behind the pot where a hand would find it still if a hand went there. No hand went there. Under the Vail roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, \person[Corin]{the boy} lay carried at the edge of the work where no slate lay, and took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because there was no other order he had ever found trustworthy.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and three days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and could not now close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Two hundred and eighty-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}; six months and one hundred and three days were told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The house kept its days in loaves, glances and unnamed spaces, and gave nobody a number to carry. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The heard bell's four, four and a gap stayed in \place{the Cross} beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The room whose floor does not touch his gave its low counting --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy took only what the hand could take and left the letting-go where it was.

At \place{the yard well} a woman of the firing work drew water with the pail against her knee. It was \person{Ilva}, of the yard and not of a house, glimpsed once and not looked at twice. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails; the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub it out. The rope at the well had stiffened in the dry cold and gave a small dry rasp as the bucket came up; the water in \place{the trough} kept its skin; the stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip where the yard had left it and called it weather; the plate of ice lifted off the trough stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. None of it was entered. The yard went around all of it with a look and a wide step and no number.

A woman with a pot asked what the month's firings had taken, now the first reading had gone up \place{the clay road} and the district's arithmetic had changed where two hundred stood and could not be changed back. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of birch bark with its notches was not taken out, not read, not asked about, not given a figure off, not joined to the charcoal under the shelf, the cord, the knots, the pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line. Her eyes went to the asker; her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

She said no figure and gave no name to the thing she kept, and went on with her water while nobody turned to look at her twice. The boy lay at the edge of the work and read the order of her reaching: hand toward the record, thumb over the notches, eyes to the asker, and only then the mouth. He filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed nine on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. She did not know she had been read; she had not reached for the stick on this afternoon any more than on the ones behind, and went on being a woman who drew water in a yard.

The well kept its small commerce. Another woman, waiting her turn with a bucket, spoke not into the basket and not to any person but to the boards and to the well-roof above her, as the yard speaks to the weather. “The runner that carries \person{Berr Clay}'s slate,” she said, “once carried a figure that named a person I knew.” That was all of it. There was no owner of the voice standing anywhere to be asked, no room, no hand at a slate, no name for the person, no figure spoken, no confession and no cause. She said it the way the yard says the weather --- flat, as a thing gone past the telling --- and went on with her bucket, and the bucket went on with the well. Nobody turned twice.

The boy took the saying apart as a surgeon takes a wound apart, by the order in which the facts presented themselves and by what was not there to be found in it. A voice. A direction arrived nowhere. A runner. A figure carried in a slate. A person the speaker had known, unnamed. No hand at the writing, no room in which the writing stood, no owner to the voice, no figure given over, no confession, no cause, no witness under any roof. He did not make the runner a reader. He did not join the saying to the strip in Ilva's sleeve a pace away, or to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, or to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate at the landing, or to the collector who entered a deduction at zero and has paid with her fingers ever since, or to the charcoal under the shelf, or to any private keeping that waited with its name kept. He set it on a place of its own in the file, beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand, with a finger between. It was a shaping toward a far-off day on which the runner might have to choose whether to read what she had carried for years; it was not a hearing, not a seventh, not a tenth hand, not a cause, not a confession, not a joining, not a witness and not paid. He would not let one stray saying stand up and call itself a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The yard said its weather and went on; the boy said nothing and went on.

On \place{the flagstones} before the kiln-house the shallow bright patch kept its place as it had kept it since Seed 22, and the broom went over it without the half-check. The stones still kept the place where the stroke had stopped; they did not give it up because the stroke went on, and nobody made a figure of the stopping. Up in \place{the Debt Loft} the small dry scrape of the sill lay on a place of its own, and the carried man's pale line where a ring had once been worn tight and long lay on another, and neither was laid across the other. The loft came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into; the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold, and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} when the light went thin, with the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six he counted in the worn mark worn almost flat by his thumb, where the house could check; the seventh he kept at the lip where it could not, and that was the one entry in this house the house could not check. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and keeping the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before it stopped, with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and his chest was tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped. Garl stayed in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow that nobody filled, the clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it unswept, and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His proverb had gone years ago to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and eighty-three days, and no collector had come up the clay road in the afternoons behind and none had gone down it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he told it, and he told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. Before \person{Sella Vail}'s sentence had finished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing; the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

\place{The back room} kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices. Men unnamed had gone low into it before first grey, and neither Jorra nor Sella had given the house a name for them, and nobody had said the room was cold. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the three sayings about a pot stood together with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place apart and no fourth likeness made a cause. The ownerless question stood at five askings with one further shaping, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

No sixth asking came over the boards and no further shaping came with it. The silence was a keeping and not a sixth witness. No sixth hearing came, and the empty seventh place was not filled to make a row come out even. The Frost 14 saying stood where it had been said, with no witness under it, on its own place with a finger between it and the eighth record-first hand; no second shaping of it came in the afternoon and nobody stood anywhere to be asked.

At the going of the light Sella came for him and carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate. Her hand went under him the way a hand goes under a pot filled to the lip, making no slop, asking nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on his weight and then on nothing as she lifted. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. She gave the instruction to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. \emph{You'll go when you go}, she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. \lore[The widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at a going of the light, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with its face turned away; four openings were four and not a rule, and the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for.

The finding that a house keeps itself by \lore{not-doings} stayed on a place of its own with the boy's own gaps on another and a finger between, and neither was laid across the other for want of a payer. The stayed frost called weather, the plate of ice on its flat stone, the skinned trough, the coals banked an hour earlier, the dressed grain turned under wool with no figure --- each kept where it had been laid, no sign, lesson, cause, mending or payer made of any. Out at \place{the rack end} the grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone; the arithmetic that had read over it on Frost 10 had not known what it cost, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. Frost's first arithmetic stayed locked up the clay road in Berr's box toward \place{High Kiln}; the printed figure stood unchallenged and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Nothing under this roof had opened the box or copied the slate, and nothing under this roof would bring any of the reading back as a lesson, a figure, a mending or a payment.

In the small hours the room whose floor does not touch his gave its low counting --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and the boy took only what the hand could take. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It took the small intake of one count from that room and the small intake of the next, set them apart with a finger between, and bridged them over the river's next low note of water over water. The bridge was laid down as an object on the sixth place and was not a span crossed: no house count was borrowed for it, no fifth was supplied to the kiln, the letting-go between the two intakes was left where it was, and the saying at the well was left where it had been said. It was an honest coordination, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not a held change, not a key, not a power, not a doctrine and not a rule. One hundred and forty-two stood beside one hundred and forty-one.

The turn was from a voice that carried a figure it would not name to a boy who carried nothing and would not make a witness where none stood. The saying had named a person the runner once carried in a slate, and it had arrived nowhere, from no owner, with no room and no hand at the writing; the boy's refusal to join it to her hand was the same refusal that kept nine hands nine. He had heard a debt and filed it as a shape. The surgeon's clean observation of how two intakes may be bridged was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off; the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.
