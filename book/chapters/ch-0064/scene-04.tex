The afternoon thinned without ending, using itself up the way a banked fire uses a day, not by a stroke but by degrees: the near boards giving back their warmth before the far ones, the heat that had been set down at \place{the hearth} crossing the room behind the pot and settling at last under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, into the fold at his ear, where it came to rest against the crust the way a palm laid flat rests on a wound, holding and not bearing down. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where the light found him at that angle and did not spend the afternoon turning over what the hand had done in the small hours. A man lays a tool on a bench at the end of work and leaves it where the next hand will reach for it; the afternoon was the work. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the house took up its ordinary grammar again, instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one.

Woodsmoke came in low bars through the finger's-width door, and under the smoke the river wind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, and under all of it the sour breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. He took them in the order they arrived, the near before the far, what lay under the hand before what lay behind it, the way he had once taken a room in the dark before anyone had put a light on. Nothing in the order had changed since the morning and nothing in it was new, and that was the keeping.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to lie down there and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, and would stay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it: knew the severe face cold wears is a quiet face, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all while the register goes on being exact inside a body that goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under every one, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. Three instruments that will not read against one another are still three instruments, and an instrument is a thing a hand uses, not a thing a man believes.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf, and nobody straightened the crease and nobody took the coat down. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and no one had filled it. The \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line, and beside it a finer hairline of grey had gathered that \person{Sella Vail} called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}, and was said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-four days. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for it and none had gone down, not on Smoke 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5, and the yard gave the road no figure and the road gave the yard none. The keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

\scenebreak

Sella went to the fire before she went to the basket. She told it the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, fed them, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and the burn across the back of her hand, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, closed on nothing and opened again to the work. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at a hearth or an afternoon, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, the way a woman reads a row of loaves without taking a figure off it. The row stood with two gaps and she gave nobody a figure; her lips went over the gaps and did not stop at them. She glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and shut her hand half over nothing and opened it again to the work. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; this was not an Account-day and no slate came near it. She set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The mark hung where it hung at a shelf, because a shelf is not an opening.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} did not take up the broom until the broom had been told. Sella's instruction went out ahead of her into \place{the Clay Yard}, a sentence addressed to the wood and the stones and only after that to the man who would move them, and he stood with his hands loose at his sides until it had arrived, then took the broom up as a man takes up a tool already named, saying \emph{Yes, house} to nothing that stood near enough to answer. He was bent at the shoulders from six years in the slip pit; the grey Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back; his chest had been tight since the dark.

The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. He set it there with the flat of his hand and then checked it with his eyes, which was the order of the thing and not a second order: six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip of the sill where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. He told the kindling what he told it every evening of this year, \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He did not count the next. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

When the coals had been banked and the instruction given over, the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth. It came the whole way. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name. He had brought it to that word on Reed 5 and 8 and 11 and 14 and 17 and 20 and 23 and 26 and 29, and the stopping had become an object he kept on a tray of intervals, a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood, nine objects, and a tenth had not been stood up before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. Nine stoppings must not stand up and call themselves a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of the nine. At the top of the reach he drew the breath he had drawn on Seed 16 and did not spend it; the small interval showed where it should have gone, and he said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with the broom. The loan was uncalled. The sentence stood where it stood, an interval and not a payment, an object and not a rule.

He swept. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch the stones had kept since Seed 22 without the half-check, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. Above the shed \place{the Debt Loft} settled as the warmth went out of the boards and they gave a little the way an old cup gives when a hand lets go, and came to the boy as a direction and nothing more. The warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died lay cold long since in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. A man who owns nothing should know where the small things are, and he knew where that stone was without ever looking toward the loft.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one afternoon on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks}, where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and had been given no name, and he did not say the room was cold; the room kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices that arrived at the basket as a direction and nothing more. The end of that silence and the end of Tarn's silence stayed side by side under the roof and were not joined, and nobody under the roof gave an order for them to straighten around.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, in the flat voice he used to the clay and the iron and only then to men. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, with no welcome in the sentence and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns ahead of its leaving his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. \lore{the burned-slate word} stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. No name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot had been lost. The sayings about the pot went on being sayings --- that a pot which sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, that the one which sang early last month had not been heard from since, that a pot had gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and none had been named to any of them.

Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish. \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} she said, and the end of the instruction did not arrive at him. Before she had finished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, held while the little silence ran, and then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. The end of the sentence arrived at the pot. The household straightened around his silence without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. He had held the iron first on Day 161 and on the days after, and this was the holding remembered and not a change made again, paired with no other fraction and not joined to the damper fraction of Garl's year or the held beat under the shelf or the half-beat on the flagstones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. He read it as an object worth nothing and joined it to no kiln. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn. Three or more men had gone low into the back room before first grey on Smoke 5 and had got no name; the room kept its finger's width of open and its cold.

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt, and found it the same as he had left it. Among the things laid out, the eight record-first hands lay each on a place of its own with a finger between every pair, and into the space beside the eighth he now put the ninth: the order of a hand that went to a strip of notched bark before it went to a person and before it went to the pot. He did not lay it on the eighth. He did not lay it anywhere near. A finger went between the ninth and the eighth, and fingers went between the ninth and every other private keeping --- between it and the charcoal under the shelf, the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood, the knots tied into a woman's apron, the pages turned down toward a floorboard, the tins with their pale folds and thumb-bright rubs, the missing hand that had gone in on Seed 15, the pale line where something had been worn tight and long. Nine record-first hands were nine. They must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them.

He did not read the stick. He had no right to the notches and had not been given one; the order of reaching was all he had taken from the yard well and he had taken it the way a man takes a temperature without opening a wound. He did not join the notches to the cord, though both were counts kept where no slate could take a figure off them, because two things kept from a slate are not therefore one keeping, and a row of such things will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer. He did not ask the woman what the bark said and did not want to know what a clerk could not take off it. The order of reaching stayed an object on its own tray, not a cause, not a key, not a power and not a rule. He had recognised the shape before he had a word for it and that was the danger; knowing a shape is the first step toward wanting it to mean something, and the wanting is where a payer gets a place to stand. He held the ninth apart.

The file was checked and unchanged. Six hearings stood beside the full note and never among it, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place; an empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings, with no fourth likeness made a cause, for three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule. The fourth burned-slate telling stood on its own place and the two tellings of the room shut in the war stood at two, with no fifth and no third. The told bell stood beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid, with Grain's third subtraction locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up and down the clay road and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth. Three held changes stood on three places --- the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 held beat, the Tallow 22 flagstone half-check --- and the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth, the held inch on a sixth, with sixty-three answers filed there and exactness worth nothing at two inches of air. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth and no name spoken over either. The dressed grain lay covered under wool and unfigured. The returning-line place had been gone around with a look and a wide step and no number. The pan's gone ring stood on a tray of its own. The question stood on its seventh place with four askings and no witness, and no fifth asking had come over the boards and no row had been named. And now the ninth hand stood on its own place with a finger between it and the eighth. The trays did not touch. Nothing was completed to make the file come out even, and nothing in it stood up and said what it meant.

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came behind with the taste of water over water, and between the two she went wide. She went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey called the thaw, around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, around the bare dark \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, around the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and around the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each of the seven got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row are still seven; they are not an account until a payer stands under them, and none stood under any of them. The widow's mark hung where it hung through the going of the light and the setting-down, because a road and a basket and a dusk are not openings; the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and the one opening on Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again.

Under the roof she set him on the bed and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. She banked the coals with the back of a broken pot and went to her own bed before the small hours and left them to keep themselves.

In the small hours there was no breathing number anywhere in the house and no fire-sitter standing over the coals. Fire, river and boards kept their three counts and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and missed its fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. Agreeing them would want a payer put under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so they lay on three trays and the trays did not touch.

He put the question to his hand the fourth way of the chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. It was taken through the small intake, held through the letting-go of one of the breaths Sella had counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, kept to the end of the count and brought down where the count ended. One rhythm borrowed and one rhythm kept: it was the coordination he had made on the sixtieth answer, and this was that same coordination remembered, not a change made again. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Sixty-four answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had taken a clamp to a bleeding vessel in a body that was not his own and now he could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him; exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and neither stood up to say what the other was. The child wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a finding.

The hand came down where Sella's count ended, through one borrowed rhythm and one kept, and answer sixty-four lay beside the ninth hand-shape on the towel, exact, complete and worth nothing. Two hundred and forty-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and sixty-four days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong; there was no market, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell on a Fifthday; Smoke 10 stood five mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The file lay open on the towel with the empty place beside the six hearings still empty, the ninth hand on its own tray with a finger between it and the eighth, and the count went forward into a day that had not yet earned its heading.
