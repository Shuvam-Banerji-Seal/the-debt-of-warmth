The afternoon thinned without ending, using itself up the way a banked fire uses a day, not with a stroke but by degrees: the near boards giving back their warmth before the far ones, the heat set down at \place{the hearth} crossing the room behind the pot and settling under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, into the fold at his ear, where it came to rest against the crust the way a palm laid flat rests on a wound, holding and not bearing down. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where the light found him at that angle and did not spend the afternoon turning over what the hand had done in the small hours. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn in the telling; neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Sixthday. Smoke 10 stood four mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it; a day not yet earned keeps its own counsel, and the yard kept it.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the smells came in the old order, the near before the far, the way a hand takes a wound before it takes the room: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under all of it the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell and not a voice. Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-five days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under every one, and no payer stood to any, so the boy left them on three trays and the trays lay where they lay. Three instruments that will not read against one another are still three instruments, and an instrument is a thing a hand uses, not a thing a man believes.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third came to lie down and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, and would stay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. It went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it: knew the severe face cold wears is a quiet face, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all while the register goes on being exact inside a body that goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf, and nobody straightened the crease and nobody took the coat down. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and no one had filled it. The Ash ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line, and beside it a finer hairline of grey had gathered, called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}, and was said now over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-five days. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for it and none had gone down, not on Smoke 4, 5 or 6, and the road went up past \place{the terrace} toward \place{High Kiln} and gave the yard no figure while the yard gave the road none. The keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before she went to the basket. She told it the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, fed them, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and the burn across the back of her hand, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, closed on nothing and opened again to the work. She did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at a hearth or an afternoon, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The one opening on Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending performed and not as a payment; no slate had been entered and no payer named. A shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon and a dusk were not openings, and on Smoke 4, 5 and 6 she had touched the mark at none of them.

At the loaf shelf she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, the way a woman reads a row of loaves without taking a figure off it. The row stood with two gaps and she gave nobody a figure; her lips went over the gaps and did not stop at them, and no number came out of her mouth. No third gap came. She glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and shut her hand half over nothing and opened it again to the work. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; this was not an Account-day and no slate came near it. She set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The mark hung where it hung at a shelf, because a shelf is not an opening.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been told. \emph{Yes, house}, he said to the wood, and to the stack he said \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and put Garl's unforged collector sentence beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He put the flat of his hand on the knife and checked it with his eyes, the way another man might count a tray of instruments before he closed the case, and went back to the broom.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping and not a new one. Nine stoppings stood, nine objects and not a rule; a tenth had not been stood up before the seventh year, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the Seed 16 breath at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up the clay road or went down it on Smoke 4, 5 or 6. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed \place{the Debt Loft} settled as the warmth went out of the boards and came to the boy as a direction and nothing more; the warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and cold long since, and he had stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt, which is not forgiving.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted dust from slip the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, two states of one afternoon laid on one tray, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks}, where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey on Smoke 4, 5 and 6 and had got no name from him; he did not say the room was cold. The room kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices arriving at the basket as a direction and nothing more.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}, he said, in the flat voice he used to the clay and the iron and only then to men. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, with no welcome in the sentence and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns ahead of its leaving his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. \lore{the burned-slate word} stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. No name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot had been lost. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings and no fourth likeness was made a cause, because a likeness made a cause wants a payer and none stood to any of them.

Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish. \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} she said, and the end of the instruction did not arrive at him. Before she had finished, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, held while the little silence ran, and then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. The end of the sentence arrived at the pot. The household straightened around his silence without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. He had held the iron first on Day 161 and on the days after, and this was the holding remembered and not a change made again, paired with no other fraction and not joined to the damper fraction of Garl's year or the held beat under the shelf or the half-beat on the flagstones. The iron was one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. He read it as an object worth nothing and joined it to no kiln. He did not look toward the bed or the basket. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light the boy turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments and no case, only the order of the laying, sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt with every edge where a hand would look for it first; the discipline was the whole of it, and a discipline is a thing a hand uses, not a thing a man believes.

Six hearings stood beside the full note and never among, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood advanced and unpaid. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings. The fourth burned-slate telling stood on its own place and the two tellings of the room shut in the war stood at two with no third. The told bell lay beside the heard, the told figure beside the paid, and Grain's third subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, going up and down the clay road and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the three held changes --- the Day 161 fraction, the Day 166 held beat, the Tallow 22 half-check --- lay on their own places with the loft scrape, the pale line and the held inch beside them, six places and no joining across any of them.

The ninth record-first hand-shape lay on a place of its own with a finger between it and the eighth and fingers between it and every other private keeping. Nine record-first hands were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. What the notches held was a count of firings, plain as a firing is plain, kept in wood because a slate would want a figure put on it; nothing on the stick waited to be read and no figure came off it that a clerk could enter. He did not read it, had not asked the woman about it and had not joined the order of reaching to the cords, the knots, the pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line. The seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth and no name spoken over either. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured; the pan's gone ring lay on its own tray; the returning-line place had been gone around with a look and no number; and the ownerless question stood on its seventh place with four askings and no witness standing. He drew no finger between the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the empty place in the field and the returning-line place, and he made no fourth likeness a cause. Nothing in the file had moved, and nothing in it had been completed to make the row come out even.

Then he put the question to his hand the sixth way of the chapter.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. In the room that does not touch his, Sella's count had come to its end: small intake, hold, letting go, and the little silence standing after it that she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear. He took the whole of it --- the intake and the hold and the letting-go, and the silence after the count had ended --- and crossed it with the river's next low note in the inch and nowhere else, across a wall that does not touch. Two rhythms crossed in the inch, one borrowed from the other side and one kept on this, and the crossing was remembered and not a change made again. He did not make a longer reach of it or enter a miss as a finding, did not hear a seventh of the full note, did not stand a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, did not pay, did not hold a change, did not make a key, a power or a rule. Sixty-six answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had crossed a trauma bay at a run with a clamp in his fist, and now two inches of air lay between his hand and a crust he could not lift to his mouth; the two facts stood on one tray and neither was promoted into a finding. He filed the answer with no payer named and went back to the crust, which was where he had been all afternoon and where he would be at the close of it.

The hand came down on the river's next low note and lay against the fold like a tool put away. The file lay open on the towel, exact, complete and worth nothing; under the floor the fire stopped at four, and the silence where the fifth should have stood stood where it stood. Two hundred and forty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and sixty-five days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk on Smoke 4, 5 or 6, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went out without a boundary drawn in the telling and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong; there was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Sixthday, and Smoke 10 (Day 249), the first Account-day and market of the second firing, stood beyond the close and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The count went forward into Smoke 7, a day that had not yet earned its heading.
