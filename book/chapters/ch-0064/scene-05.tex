\daymark{245}{Smoke 6, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

A day came into the room without knocking, the way the river came in under the door --- not as an arrival but as the continuation of a sound that had been going on all night and would go on after. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and let it come. He was two hundred and forty-five days old, six months and sixty-five days, and nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said either number over him or within his hearing. The wanting of it had gone on under the not-expecting so long that the two had become one habit, like a man who keeps reaching for a pocket he knows holds nothing and keeps finding wool. Smoke had come on without a boundary drawn in the house and would go out without one; neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong in the telling. No bell on a Sixthday, no market at \place[the Cross]{Cross}. Smoke 10 stood four mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the smells arrived in their old order, the near before the far: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under all of it the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was the kiln drawing in four low pulls and stopping where the fifth should have stood. It was not a bell and not a voice. Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-five days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under every one, and no payer stood to any, so the boy left them on three trays and the trays lay where they lay. Three instruments that will not read against one another are still three instruments, and an instrument is a thing a hand uses, not a thing a man believes.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from the ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third came to lie down and make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, and would stay unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. It went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it: knew the face severe cold wears is a quiet face, knew a cold patient cannot clot, knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all while the register goes on being exact inside a body that goes on being hungry. The surgeon took the inventory and entered it nowhere.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at the throat of the fire's keeper, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and it was not touched in the small hours, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The one opening on Smoke 1 lay behind the household and was not looked at again --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending performed and not as a payment; no slate had been entered and no payer named. A shelf, a well, a rack, a road, a basket, an afternoon and a dusk were not openings. He did not need to be told this and had never been told it; the house kept the hour the way a vessel keeps a seam, without discussion.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before she went to the basket. She told it the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, fed them, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. She pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and the burn across the back of her hand, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold, closed on nothing and opened again to the work. She did not look toward the bed. The mark hung where it hung at a hearth and a morning, because neither was an opening.

The dressed grain came out from under its wool for the damp. She put her fingers into it to the knuckle, lifted it, spread it thin on the flat stone near \place{the hearth} to finish, turned it with the back of her hand, gathered it back by the handful and set the fold flat again. It changed state and gained no figure. She counted it in glances, in handfuls and by the weight of the carrying arm, which knows weight and not cost; there was no slate near the stone and nothing was entered on any day of this firing. It was not laid across the crust in the fold, the two gaps on the shelf, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, the empty place in the returning line or the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The figure read over the dressed grain on Grain 30 was still not the figure paid; the true subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, going up and down the clay road and answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. A figure read is a figure read and a figure paid is a figure paid, and no signature stood between them for a boy who could not have read the signature if it had been put in front of him.

\scenebreak

At \place{the loaf shelf} she stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row, the way a woman reads a row of loaves without taking a figure off it. The row stood with two gaps and she gave nobody a figure; her lips went over the gaps and did not stop at them, and no number came out of her mouth. No third gap came. She glanced once at the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and shut her hand half over nothing and opened it again to the work. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; this was not an Account-day and no slate came near it. She set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed. The mark hung where it hung at a shelf, because a shelf is not an opening.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf, and nobody straightened the crease and nobody took the coat down. The wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and no one had filled it. The Ash ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line, and beside it a finer hairline of grey had gathered that Sella called the thaw, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, \emph{leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled}, and was said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and forty-five days. No collector had come up the clay road for it and none had gone down, not on Smoke 4, 5 or 6, and the yard gave the road no figure and the road gave the yard none. The keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

The house gave its ordinary directions and nothing more. From \place{the kindling} came the stroke of a broom, taken up only after it had been told, and from the edge of the shed the small regular note of the wheel being turned; neither came into the room as a person and neither was given a name. Above the shed \place{the Debt Loft} settled as the warmth went out of the boards and gave a little the way an old cup gives when a hand lets go, and came to the boy as a sound with a direction and nothing besides. The two silences under this roof kept their places side by side and were not joined, and nobody gave an order for them to straighten around.

A neighbour at the threshold hitched a pot against her hip and asked what the keeping would take now the grain was under wool. Sella sent the question to the pot and the weather: \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}. Nothing was priced, refused or entered; the woman carried the question away level, the way one carries a full pot, with nothing slopped and nothing given. She did not look into the basket, and the boy did not take the pot set on the sill for an answer to anything. A man come down the clay road with the dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of his fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve. The boy read the closed fist as he had once read a chart turned face-down on a table: the shape of the paper is not the writing under it, and a number put into a sleeve had not been put there for him. Both readings were exact and neither bought him the crust. The figure arrived nowhere. It was not a missing figure, because a figure kept in a sleeve is a figure kept in a sleeve and a sleeve is not a slate; a thing not priced is not therefore free, it is a thing whose payer has not been named. The neighbour and the man went off about their work with their questions still on them, and the yard gave neither of them a figure.

\scenebreak

The wrong wind got up off \place{High Kiln} in the middle of the morning and came down \place{the terrace} and over the boards. It carried the sweet edge of lead glaze on it, the taste of a glaze room with the door left open, and the edge rode the length of the boards, crossed the sill and touched the fold where the dressed grain lay. It went out on the wind that brought it. It did not settle and it did not return; there was no second edge paid and nothing to pay with. A direction had arrived nowhere, and the boy let it stand as a direction. There was a silent woman at the market's edge with the green of lead glaze in the crease beside her mouth, and he did not lay this sweet edge across that green, because a green kept at a mouth and an edge come down on a wind are two places and not one account, and no payer stood to either. No name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188.

One of the three shapings of the pot saying stood again as a saying: \emph{a pot which sings before its time mostly sings itself out early}. It came over the boards with the weather and went off up the terrace with it, and the other two stayed where they had been laid. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of these. No pot was lost in the yard that morning, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no saying was made true by a wind that had only carried it. The kiln went on with its four low pulls and its gap. The river went on with its note under the door. The boards went on with four nails, four and a thumb-width knot. The wind had passed without a lesson, and that too was a keeping.

\scenebreak

The morning settled back into the ordinary the way a yard settles after a cart has gone through it --- the same ruts, the same stones, the same space where the cart had been. Sella came in from the boards with the fold of her work still on her hands and set the pot down where she set it, and the house took up its grammar again: instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. She did not look toward the bed. Under the low angle of the shelf the boy kept his place with the crust two inches off and the empty place beside it still without a name, and the wrong wind went on being a direction that had arrived nowhere.

He had been two hundred and forty-five days at this, and the register had learned to say the same thing in the same order without hoping it would come out different: one crust, one gap, one frozen thread, cold at three places and the afterdrop waiting past all of them. He knew the shape of a man going cold and knew the shape of a man coming back too fast, and neither shape was his to treat. Two inches of air lay between the hand and the crust. Two inches was not a long distance to a man who had once crossed a trauma bay at a run with a clamp in his fist, and it was the whole world to a body that could not close it. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in him and neither stood up to say what the other was. He wanted the crust. He kept the tray. The tray was what he had; he had kept it for two hundred and forty-five days and would keep it tomorrow.

The file lay where he had laid it, open on the towel that was not a towel and sharp by sharp and blunt by blunt in the order he had given the instruments. He did not turn it out again in the morning; a man does not re-lay instruments he has already counted to make sure they are still there. The sixth place was where the next answer would go, and the empty place beside the six hearings was empty still, and the ninth record-first hand from the yard well lay on a place of its own with a finger between it and the eighth and fingers between it and every other private keeping. Nine hands were nine, and he had held them apart on Smoke 5 and held them apart now, because a row of likenesses will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer, and an account wants a payer and no payer stood to any of them. The wrong wind had brought no fourth likeness and taken no pot; the grain under wool had changed state and gained no figure; the number in the man's sleeve had arrived nowhere. Nothing in the file had moved, and nothing in it had been completed to make the row come out even.

Then he put the question to his hand the fifth way of the chapter.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door. In the room that does not touch his, Sella's count had come to its end --- small intake, hold, letting go, and the small silence after it that she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear. It was that silence he took, not the breath: the little interval standing where it stood after her count had ended, with no rhythm of hers in it to cross. He held his hand through it on the river's own low note, one rhythm held across an interval borrowed from the other side of a wall that does not touch. He did not cross two rhythms in the inch this time. He kept one, and the keeping of it was the whole of the thing. The hand came down on the river's next low note, down before the note had finished and after the interval had stood where it stood.

It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Sixty-five answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feeling for the joint before the light came, and now he could not lift a crust two inches to his mouth; the two facts stood on one tray and neither was promoted into a finding. He filed the answer with no payer named and went back to the crust, which was where he had been all morning and where he would be at the close of it.

The hand came down on the river's next low note after the little silence following Sella's count, and answer sixty-five lay on the sixth place while the sweet edge of glaze was already gone out on the wind that brought it. Two hundred and forty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill and Grath; six months and sixty-five days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. Smoke went on without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong; there was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Sixthday; Smoke 10 stood four mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The file lay open on the towel, exact, complete and worth nothing, and the count went forward into a day that had not yet earned its heading.
