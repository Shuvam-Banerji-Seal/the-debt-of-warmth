The afternoon thinned without ending. It went the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals and left on a cold stone --- not all at once, not with a word spoken over it, but by degrees, the warmth drawing back from the outside inch by inch and settling somewhere behind the pot where nobody could come at it. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the heat drew back from the fold in just that manner and sat behind the pot and the wall. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the day going off him and two hundred and eighty standing exact and complete and spoken by nobody, six months and one hundred days told by nobody, the fire's fifth unpaid the same number with its payer without a name. He had stopped expecting a number and had not stopped wanting one; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever trusted. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth. Both were true and in a body six months and one hundred days old the second was louder, and the surgeon entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. The bell's four, four and a gap heard at \place{the Cross} on the tenth stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place stood empty and was not filled to make a row come out even.

\person{Sella Vail} set him in the finger's width of shade at the well where no slate lay, and went about the running water with the little-door burn on the back of her left hand shut on nothing. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wind. “It's the river wind.” The instruction went to the wool and the water and never to the basket. She carried him as a thing set down is carried, level, and gave no reason and no instruction to the thing in it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at an afternoon or a well, because neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Beyond the door the stayed frost lay white on the north lip of the well and the broken plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it; the yard called both weather, and no figure, sign, cause, lesson, mending or payer was made of either.

Out at \place{the yard well} \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of \place{the drying racks} was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. She was of the firing work and not of a house, and went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all. The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and watched the order of her reaching. A woman with a pot asked what the month's firings had taken now the first reading had gone up the road and the district's arithmetic changed for a new month that could not be changed back. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of birch bark was not taken out, read, asked about, taken a figure from or joined to the charcoal under the shelf, the cord, the knots, the pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it. “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.” She said no figure and gave no name to the thing she kept, and went on with her water while nobody turned to look at her twice.

The boy filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its place of its own beside the eighth with a finger between it and every other private keeping, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. The count stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. He did not read the stick, ask her about it or take a number from it, and she did not know she had been read. Two private keepings stood side by side under this roof and neither was opened: the notches in her sleeve and the charcoal beneath the loaf shelf. His curiosity about the stick was checked by the rule that a count kept in wood is still a keeping and is not his to read, and the check was not envy and not virtue, only the exact order of another person's reaching, filed apart. He had once laid a chart face-down on a desk and passed his hand to the signature without reading what lay under it; the grammar was the same and the reaching was the same and he made nothing of the likeness, because two hands that go to the record before the person are two hands and not a doctrine.

On \place{the flagstones} before the house the shallow bright patch lay where it had lain since Seed 22, and the broom stroke went over it without the half-check it had once made, the stones still keeping the place where the stroke had stopped. Nobody entered the place and nobody said what had stopped there. The small dry scrape of the loft sill lay on a place of its own in the boy's file and the carried man's pale line lay on another, and he did not lay either across the other or across the bright patch, for want of a payer standing under any of them. Three kept marks in three places and no fourth likeness stood up among them.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife set at the very lip of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the very edge where it could not, the one entry in this house the house could not check. \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather. To the ones who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and the ones who wanted it late he said “Yes, house,” agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on keeping the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and his chest was tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His broom went over the bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it, and no collector came up \place{the clay road} on this day and none went down it.

Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold. Tarn had kept it two years and could no more reconcile it with the debt than he could the house's kindness with the account he kept of it, and he had stopped trying, which was not forgiveness. The scrape on the sill lay where it lay and the stone sat cold in its pot and neither was carried down or named.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger, not bone-coloured slip, and white in the creases of a fist that had opened. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and he withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished he put his bent finger into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no Grathic word for both mercies, an object joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The cold iron took the warmth out of his fingertip and gave nothing back.

\place[the back room]{The back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices and was given no name and not said to be cold; it was a direction and a sound and the narration did not go into it. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. No sixth asking came over the boards and the silence that stood where it might have stood was a keeping and not a sixth witness; the five askings and the one shaping stayed with no owner under any of them and no row named. No sixth hearing of the full note came either, and the empty place kept its emptiness, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

At the going of the light Sella came up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate and carried him in. She set him on his bed under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along the row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold on the wall under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. At the fire she told it it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}; the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals as she pushed the coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist. She stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening and did not look toward the bed. The widow's mark hung where it hung at a going of the light, because a going of the light is not an opening. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed, and gave the basket no instruction at any hour. The house kept itself by \lore{not-doings}: the fifth pull unsupplied, the charcoal unread, the two lines unswept, the loan uncalled, the grain unfigured, the thread unwiped, the crust unlifted. Nothing from the reading had come back under this roof as a lesson, a figure or a payment, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged up the clay road with the figure read still not the figure paid.

The staying frost lay where the yard had left it on the well's north lip and the broken plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet, called weather and entered nowhere; the staying and the lifting had taken no figure and found no payer. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, read over the day before by a number that did not know what it cost and still did not, with the space between the figure and the price unsigned.

In the small hours the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed, every gap left gap and every tray parted from its neighbour and nothing that had moved. He had once laid out instruments on a towel for a night's trauma and known each by its weight before the eye fell on it; the finding here was exact in the same way and worth as much, which was nothing at two inches of air. The house's not-doings stayed on one place and his own gaps on another with a finger between --- the two gaps above the shelf, the one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place --- no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; nine record-first hands stood with a finger between every pair and Ilva's ninth in her sleeve unread; Frost's first arithmetic sat locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box up the clay road and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Nothing had opened, named, called, paid or ended.

Beyond the finger's-width door the afternoon had gone on with Ilva at the well drawing with the pail against her knee, her hand in her sleeve and nobody turning to look at her twice, the count staying in wood where no slate could take a figure off it. In the small hours, from the room whose floor does not touch his, a woman's low counting came through in threes --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and went on without anybody asking what she counted. She did not count the boy, and nobody under the roof counted the days in anything a slate could take. His hand outside the wool went up an inch. It took two of the intakes from that room and held them apart with a finger between, the first left where it was and not carried into the second, and it bridged them over the river's note coming low through the finger's-width door, the low thing over lower under the door and here in the open. The bridge was kept as an object on the sixth place and not a span crossed, one hundred and thirty-six beside one hundred and thirty-five with a finger between, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. No house count was borrowed for it and no fifth supplied to the kiln, and he made nothing of it, would not let it stand up as a gift or a keeping or a rule, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and the river's low note went on under the going of the house while Frost 20 stood eight mornings ahead and was not brought into this one. Forward went the day without a line drawn under it.
