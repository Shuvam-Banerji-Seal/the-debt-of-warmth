\daymark{281}{Frost 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

Frost came on its own morning, earned by the light in the door and not brought into a day that had not earned it. In the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand laid back against the fold where the last answer had been put away, a tool left where the next hand would find it without looking, and the night going off him inch by inch the way heat goes out of a bowl set upon a cold stone. He came into his two hundred and eighty-first day. Six months and one hundred and one days were told by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; the number stood exact and complete against a house that kept its days in loaves, in glances and in the space where a crust should have stood and did not. The count had gone from two hundred and eighty to two hundred and eighty-one the way a man lays one board beside another without measuring the seam. There was no market, no gathering at \place{the Cross} and no bell on a Secondday; Frost 20 stood seven mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it, and the reading of the tenth was not looked at again as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever trusted. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth. He could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and one days old the second was louder, and the surgeon entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust to his mouth; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; its payer had been without a name for two hundred and eighty-one days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any; no fire-sitter stood over the coals and no house count came to be borrowed. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on the tenth, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not a seventh of \lore{the full note}.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place stood empty and was not filled to make a row come out even, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Five askings of the ownerless question stood on a seventh place of their own with one further shaping beside them and a finger between every pair; no witness stood under any and no row was named. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth in her sleeve unread and no tenth. The seventh green stood a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either. The two gaps above the shelf, the one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place stayed on separate trays, no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause. Every tray kept its neighbour parted by a finger's width, and nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket and before any word, as she had gone on the mornings before and would go on the mornings after; the fire came first in her house and no instruction was ever given to the thing in the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals as she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the burn shaped like a little door with a lintel and a threshold and the skin inside it puckered and pale, closing on nothing. She carried him at the half-pace from every open slate, level, as a pot filled that must not be slopped, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind --- \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} --- and never to the basket. At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along the row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold on the wall under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, leaving the thread frozen where it lay. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at a morning, a hearth or a rack, because none is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

On the cool stone at \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from \place{the hearth}, changed in state and given no figure. She lifted a corner of the wool and turned it once for damp with a touch lighter than the morning before, the grey dust of \place{the drying racks} working into the seam of a thumb she did not scrub out, and laid it back where it had lain. The arithmetic that had read over it on the tenth did not know what it cost and still did not, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature upon it. Frost's first arithmetic sat locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with the printed one in twelve unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid. Nothing of the reading had come back under this roof as a lesson, a mending or a payment; the house kept its own count in loaves and glances and unnamed spaces, and the boy kept his in objects on a sixth place, and neither was entered anywhere.

Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the house went around its seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer hairline of grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie; the round ring where the shallow pan once stood with no scrape or crumb upon it; \place{the trough} skinned over and its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust. Seven were seven and not an account. The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of \place{the yard well} and the broken plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it; the yard called both weather and gave neither a figure, sign, cause, lesson, mending or payer. On \place{the flagstones} the shallow bright patch lay where it had lain since Seed 22 and the broom stroke went over it without the half-check, the stones still keeping the place where the stroke had stopped. \person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects with his name unspoken two hundred and eighty-one days --- the salt-stiff coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} keeping its twenty-year lean and its hollow, the tile debt neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled --- and his proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. No collector came up the clay road on Frost 10, 11 or 12 and none went down it. \place{The back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices, given no name and not said to be cold; above it \place{the Debt Loft} came to the boy only as a sound with a direction, and he was never taken into it.

In the small hours the low note of the river came under the finger's-width door, water over water, and from the room whose floor does not touch his a woman's low counting went on in threes --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- with nobody asking what she counted and nobody counting the boy. The hand outside the wool went up an inch. It took the small intake of one of those counted breaths and held it; the count let go, and the hand stayed past the letting-go and through the little silence that came after it, and came down on the river's next low note. The little silence was left whole and empty, not filled to make a row come out even, not crossed by a rhythm that belonged to another house and not borrowed from the fire's missing fifth. He held a count the way a man holds a field open to see how the bleeding ends, and the knowing bought nothing; it was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. One hundred and thirty-seven stood beside one hundred and thirty-six with a finger between, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The boy lay with the grain still covered under wool at the rack end beyond the door, the crust two inches off with its empty place unnamed, and the river's low note going on after the hand came down. Frost 20 stood seven mornings ahead and was not brought into this one. Forward went the day without a line drawn under it.
