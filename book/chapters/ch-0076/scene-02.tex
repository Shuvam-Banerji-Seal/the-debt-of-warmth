The yard came up under the basket in the thin light that frost leaves when it goes, and \person{Sella Vail} carried at the half-pace she had kept since the first market, one ear open to the slates that stood propped here and there in \place{the Clay Yard} and the other to nothing anybody could have written down. She went wide around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline beside it, around the space under the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay, around the round ring where the shallow pan had stood with no ring left but its place, around \place{the trough} with its skin and the plate of ice standing on its flat stone out of the path of feet, around the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven places, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number; seven were seven and not an account, and the basket crossed the ground between them the way a pot is carried across a room without slopping. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wind. “It's the river wind.” She gave the basket no instruction and no reason, and carried it as a thing set down is carried, level.

\person[Corin]{the boy} came into \place{the Cross} at the low angle of the basket, where the world was boots and hems and the cold iron of \place{the moot-post} at the edge of the shade. Two hundred people leaned over the reading block under it, and the square had four ways out of it --- the road down to the south bridge and \place{Slate Row}, the steep street up to \place{High Kiln}, \place{the north bridge}, and the road toward \place{the Bone Orchard} --- with a Court table set at the Slate Row fringe where business was done in the open air. Sella set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay and stood half a pace off, her little-door burn shut on nothing, and did not look into it. The cold iron came at his cheek and stopped there as the cold under the roof had stopped at cheek and temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back. He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread of drool frozen in the dry heat and unwiped; the cold at the three places; the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody here would perform. He wanted the crust lifted two inches. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-nine days old the second was louder; he entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. There was nobody in the square who would have known what a number counted if it had been spoken, and nobody spoke one to him.

Eight pigs went over the wet weighing board and an assessor of \place{Sill} wrote them down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail. A bell struck through the morning, longer than a church bell, and went on striking; nobody crossed himself at it. The boy heard four, four and a gap, and kept the heard bell in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not under the roof, not folded into the kiln's four low pulls or the river's \lore{water over water} or \place{the boards}' four nails and four and a thumb-width knot, and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Agreeing any two of those counts would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any. The gap stayed a gap. He had once stood in a bay where a monitor wrote a number for a body on a table and the number was correct and the body was gone; the square did not need him to tell it what a correct number was worth. He kept the thought on its own place and went on with the seeing.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road}, as he had come up short on Smoke 10 and Smoke 20 and Smoke 30, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which was the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His large clean hands wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, and he held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing turned in. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a surgeon lets his eyes rest on a chart before he turns it to the light. Then he read low into the top of the two hundred, \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says}, and the figure went over the basket once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and still the boy was not looked into. The levy was called for Frost. “The levy is called,” Berr said. “It is not called by me.” To a man who brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the month's firing would take now the first reading had gone up, he said, “your house,” and left the rest of the sentence standing where it was; he never said \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the crowd leaned in to hear him because he did not raise his voice. He went off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the first true subtraction of Frost inside it, believing he had done a kindness. The printed figure stood unchallenged. A figure read aloud was still not the figure paid.

The district's arithmetic changed for the new month at the reading block under the moot-post, and could not be changed back. The boy registered the change the way he had registered the three in Smoke, without a word for it and without a hand to enter it. It answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, for whom no figure, collector or slate knew the cost; it answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back; it answered nothing for the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end} at home, which would be read over this morning by a number that did not know what it cost and would leave the space between the figure and the price unsigned. Three absences stood in three places and none rose into another, and no fourth likeness was made a cause of any two. Berr had wiped his thumb twice and passed a finger over a figure and gone away in the belief that the account stood; the boy had seen the order of the reaching and filed it where such things were filed, with no payer named and no signature on the space between what was read and what was paid.

\person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round; the shorter of the two straps had been let out for the long road, and the dried pale scurf of mud stood unbrushed on her skirt where it had been shortened for the wet. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd, and nothing pressed where the writing was. To a woman who asked what she carried she said, “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the one who wanted more she said, “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” answering the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology. No figure came off the slate. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand in the district on a place of its own, not joined to Berr's twice-wiped thumb, the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate, \person{Ilva}'s strip in a sleeve far off, the charcoal under the shelf, the missing hand or any other private keeping; eight were eight and not a rule. A runner carried and did not read, and the road under her kept its own count in the legs.

Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. A buyer's number went in before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and he did not rise out of it. The boy took the order of it and left the man where he was set: a clerk at a landing, not \person{Marra}, not the grey-fingered collector, not the soft-handed man at a Court table, not the woman with the slate, not any of the hands filed on their own trays. He made no tenth of it and no joining.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip and a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long; the green stayed a colour and was not named. At the market's edge a small shape in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one turned half toward a pot set down near the eel shade and went back; the man's shoulders on a child's frame, the breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver of lead glaze on a string at the throat that caught the light on the turn and went dull going back. The half-turn came after the knock and was a turn after the knock, not a hearing and not joined to the boy's own turning; neither was named, neither was given a loaf, a slate or a sale, and no eighth of the colour or the shape stood up to call either a cause. Nobody spoke to either and neither spoke to anybody. The boy kept the sightings apart from every hearing and from every romance the square might have made of them, and left them where they stood.

The whole square performed the same gesture at different scales. A thane passed a finger over a figure and took himself out of his sentence. A runner set her hand to a slate before her eyes took the crowd. A clerk entered a buyer's number before he looked up to find the man who had said it. A woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back. A child in cast-offs turned after a knock. A widow carried a basket at a half-pace and stood off from every open slate. Each hand went to its own object first and to the person afterward, and the boy registered each without promoting any into a cause. The turn was from a district's arithmetic changed by a reading to his own arithmetic changed by nothing at all; a figure had gone into a slate over the top of the basket and no figure had come into the basket, and the one count that had moved that morning was the one nobody entered.

The stayed frost on the north lip of \place{the yard well} and the plate of ice standing on its flat stone were carried where the yard had left them; the yard had called both weather on Frost 8 and Frost 9 and called them weather still, and the reading that counted over the district took no figure off either and set no payer under either. No sign, lesson, cause, mending or payer was made of the white lip or the clouded plate. The boy kept them on places of their own with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness of the three appearances of white and no cause of any two. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at Sella's throat and she did not touch it at a market, a road or a going of the light, because none of these is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with its face turned away and no lesson made of it; four openings were four and not a rule, and the next stood in the first firing of a later month, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. Frost 20 stood eight mornings ahead and was not brought into a morning that had earned only this one reading.

At the going of the light she carried him home, up the yard at the half-pace, around the seven places with a look and a wide step, and said nothing of what had been read over him. She set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. She told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal remained unread, her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure, and she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, and gave no instruction at any hour to the basket. Two hundred and seventy-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}; six months and ninety-nine days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The surgeon knew the value of a clean coordination and the value of a number that cannot be spent, and both were exact and both bought nothing at two inches of air.

In the small hours the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap and every tray parted from its neighbour, each thing known by its weight before the eye fell on it. The hand outside the wool went up an inch. It took the small intake of the heard bell's gap and the hold of one counted breath from a woman counting turnips at the edge of the shade, set them side by side with a finger between, borrowed no house count, supplied no fifth to the kiln, agreed no two counts by putting a payer under them, and came down on the river's remote note. The heard bell's gap stayed in the square beside the told bell, not agreed with the figure read over the basket and not made a seventh of the full note. The answer came down as one hundred and thirty-four, beside one hundred and thirty-three with a finger between, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not promoted into a reach, a hearing, a gift, a keeping, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule, and he made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything.

The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Beyond the finger's-width door the river kept its low note over lower, and the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and the plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it unnamed, and there was no third. The district had changed its arithmetic for the month and could not change it back; the boy had changed nothing, and his was the count nobody entered. Forward went the days without a line drawn under them.
