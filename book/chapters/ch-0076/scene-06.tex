Frost went out of its second working day without a boundary drawn upon it, the afternoon thinning the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals and left upon a cold stone. It did not end; it drew back. The heat drew back from the fold and sat behind the pot. The district called Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and went on calling them so, and \person[Corin]{the boy} laid the two sayings each on its own place and set neither right nor wrong in so many words. Nothing had been made of Frost's first reading under this roof and nothing was made of it now; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule, and a rule wants a payer and none stood to any of them.

He came into the afternoon with the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because there was no other order he had ever trusted. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth. He could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and one days old the second was louder, and the surgeon entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; its payer had been without a name for two hundred and eighty-one days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail would have stood. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any; no fire-sitter stood over the coals and no house count came to be borrowed. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on the tenth, stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, not folded into the three and not a seventh of \lore{the full note}.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down into \place{the Clay Yard} in the afternoon at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer hairline of grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lie; the round ring where the shallow pan once stood with no scrape or crumb upon it; \place{the trough} skinned over and its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps or the fold's one crust. Seven were seven and not an account.

The stayed \lore{frost-bloom} lay white on the north lip of \place{the yard well} past the going of the light of the day before and into this afternoon, and the yard called it weather and gave it no figure; the rope had stiffened and the trough kept its skin and nobody put a cause under either. The broken plate of ice stood where it had been set on \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it; it was a covering laid over a depth and the yard said it was weather and let it stand. On \place{the flagstones} the shallow bright patch lay where it had lain since Seed 22 and the broom stroke went over it without the half-check, the stones still keeping the place where the stroke had stopped. The boy kept the stayed white and the standing plate and the skinned water each on a place of its own, with a finger between, and gave none of them a fourth likeness and made no doctrine out of three. Frost 20 stood eight mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. \person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects with his name unspoken two hundred and eighty-one days --- the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} keeping its twenty-year lean and its hollow that nobody filled, the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it, the debt against a firing of tiles neither paid nor settled nor done with and worse for being uncalled --- and his proverb went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. No collector came up \place{the clay road} on Frost 10, 11 or 12 and none went down it.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} kept six where the house could check, in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, and the seventh at the very lip of the sill where it could not, beside the unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He had taken the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” To those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late he said “Yes, house” to both, agreeing so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, his chest tight as it had been since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over the bright patch without the half-check. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which came to the boy only as a sound with a direction and never as a room he was taken into, the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness.

At the edge of the shed \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of her instruction arrived at the pot; and the boy read one iron with two bites --- the holding and the taking --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

\place[the back room]{The back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices; men unnamed had gone low into it before first grey, and neither Jorra nor Sella gave the house a name for them, and nobody said the room was cold. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together on their own tray with the premature Smoke 14 note on a place apart and no fourth likeness made a cause. The ownerless question stood at five askings with one further shaping, a finger between every pair, no witness standing under any and no row named; no sixth asking had come and no second shaping, and the silence was a keeping and not a witness. “He's not Vail” stood sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, set him on \place{the bed} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} and not in the loft, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it lay. At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment counting under her breath toward no opening. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals as she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the burn shaped like a little door with a lintel and a threshold and the skin inside it puckered and pale, closing on nothing. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along the row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold on the wall under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed, and gave no instruction at any hour to the basket. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and her hand did not go to it at a going of the light, because a going of the light is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

In the small hours the file was turned out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments are turned out for a man who will never be called to use them, each thing in its own place with room between, known by weight before the eye fell on it, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He walked the inventory in the order it lay.

Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place stood empty and was not filled to make a row come out even. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at the Cross on the tenth, lay beside the told bell of the Long Night and was not folded into the three counts and not made a seventh of the full note. Three pot sayings lay together on their own tray, each a saying and not a cause; the premature Smoke 14 note lay on a place apart and no fourth likeness stood up among them. Burned slate stood at four from Slate Row, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling. The two shut-room tellings stood at two and had not become three. The ownerless question lay on a seventh place of its own with five askings and one shaping, a finger between every pair, no witness under any and no row named. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Nine record-first hands stood with \person{Ilva}'s ninth in her sleeve unread and no tenth to make them a rule. The seventh green stood a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either. Frost's first arithmetic sat locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box with the printed one in twelve unchallenged, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end} beyond the door, and the arithmetic that had read over it still did not know what it cost, the space between figure and price unsigned.

The small dry scrape of the loft sill lay on a place of its own and the carried man's pale line on another, and neither was laid across the other. Garl stayed in his objects with his name unspoken two hundred and eighty-one days and the tile debt worse for being uncalled. The stayed frost, the plate of ice, the grain turned for damp and the week's small movements lay each where they had been laid, with no fourth likeness standing up and no doctrine made of three. The finding that a house keeps by \lore{not-doings} stayed on its own place and the boy's own gaps on another with a finger between --- the two gaps above the shelf, the one crust in the fold and its unnamed empty place, the pan's gone ring, the field's empty place and the returning-line place where a hand went in and did not come out --- no third gap and no fourth likeness made a cause. Every tray kept its neighbour parted by a finger's width; nothing was opened, named, called, paid or ended, and nothing that had moved.

The hand outside the wool went up an inch. It took the held interval and the empty interval from the file, set them side by side with a finger between, and came down on the river's next low note. Neither was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a gift, a keeping, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. One hundred and thirty-eight stood beside one hundred and thirty-seven, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Two hundred and eighty-one stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sill}, \place{Grath} and Low Kiln; six months and one hundred and one days were told by nobody, and the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The boy lay with the crust two inches off and the empty place beside it unnamed, the hand laid down on the hundred and thirty-eighth answer, and the file open on the towel with every gap left gap. Beyond the finger's-width door the frost lay white on the well lip where the yard had left it, called weather and entered nowhere, and Frost 20 (Day 289), the month's second Account-day, stood eight mornings ahead and was not brought into this day. The river's low note went quiet after the hand came down.
