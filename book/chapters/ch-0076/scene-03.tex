\daymark{280}{Frost 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

Frost 11 came on its own morning and was not brought into the day that had gone before it. It came the way light comes up a yard wall, by degrees, with no announcement and no number spoken. There was no market on a Firstday, no \place{the Cross}, no bell. The reading of the tenth stood where the yard had left it, eight mornings short of the twentieth, and nothing of it came back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof as a lesson, a figure or a payment. In the small hours \person[Corin]{the boy} lay carried on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, awake and silent, with the hundred and thirty-fourth answer put down behind him the way a tool is laid where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and eighty stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}; six months and one hundred days were told by nobody. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the fifth was unpaid two hundred and eighty days with its payer unnamed. He had stopped expecting anybody to speak a number, which was not the same as not wanting one; the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

The room smelled of hot clay cooled under tallow, wet wool, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw} and would go on calling the thaw until it chose to go. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing. Behind the wall the kiln answered in fours with a gap, and it was not a bell. A bell had struck at the Cross on the tenth and gone four, four and a gap, and that hearing stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; on a Firstday no bell struck at all. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail would have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. The seventh place stood empty and was not filled to make a row come out even.

He took the body as it presented itself, in the order the facts arrived, because that was the only order he had ever found trustworthy. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. The knowledge was exact and bought nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket or to anything else, as she had come to it on the mornings behind and would come to it on the mornings ahead. The little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on nothing as she reached the coals, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light. “It’s the thaw,” she told them. “You’ll go when you go.” She turned them with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool. “It’s the river wind.” She gave the basket no instruction and no reason, and carried it as a thing set down is carried, level. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, and her hand did not go to it at a morning and in a basket, because neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with its face turned away; she did not look at it again to make a lesson of it, four openings were four and not a rule, and the next stood in the first firing of a later month, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

At the loaf shelf her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal beneath remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold on the wall under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. Then she set the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, left the thread frozen where it lay, and stood the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the setting and the arrest of it; he had measured it before and would measure it again, and it was an object on a place of its own and not a lesson anybody had been taught. She did not look into the basket, and she did not say whose child he was. He was set under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}; this was a keeping and not a kindness anybody had named.

Out at \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured under wool on the cool stone, and in the morning light she lifted a corner of the wool and turned it once for damp with a lighter touch than the turn before, and set it back where it had lain. No figure went with the turning. The number that had read over it at the Cross the day before did not know what the grain had cost and still did not, and the space between the figure and the price had no signature on it. Frost’s first arithmetic sat locked in \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}’s box going up \place{the clay road}, out of reach of every hand under this roof and unable to be changed back, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged. The figure read was still not the figure paid. She gave the wool its instruction and went on with the pot, and the boy kept the seeing on a place of its own and made no lesson of it.

She carried him up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number. There was the clean dark line at the house corner where the ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline beside it called the thaw, unswept both, and neither swept now; there was the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring on the paving where the shallow clay pan had once stood with no ring left but its place; \place{the trough} with its skin and the pale scurf of the rope, and the white fronds on the north lip of \place{the yard well}; the field’s empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven places, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number; seven were seven and not an account. The clean dark line and its finer grey neighbour lay in the house’s corner; the rack space kept its broken slates; the pan’s gone ring stayed on a tray of its own; the skinned trough and the white lip stayed where the yard had left them; the field’s empty place and the returning-line place stayed out in the strips with nobody naming what they lacked. Grey rack dust lay worked into the seams of \place{the drying racks} where hands had passed, and she went around it as she went around the rest. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf’s two gaps, the one crust in the fold or the dressed grain under wool.

The \lore{frost-bloom} that had written white fronds on the north lip stayed there past the going of the light and into this morning, and the yard called it weather still and put no figure on it. The plate of ice that had been lifted off the skin on Frost 9 stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it. Nobody gave either a figure and nobody carried either away; no sign, lesson, cause, mending or payer was made of the white lip or the clouded plate. The surgeon’s reading of a covering laid over a depth stayed on a place of its own beside the gone white of Frost 4, with a finger between; he made no fourth likeness of three appearances and no cause of any two, and the morning coming up over \place{the Clay Yard} did not pay any of it.

\person{Garl Vail} stayed in objects and not in a story, his name unspoken under this roof two hundred and eighty days: the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} keeping its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it unswept, and the debt against a firing of tiles neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His sayings lived in the house’s mouth, spoken to the pot and the fire and never to a person. The unforged sentence about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford lay where it had been laid at \place{the kindling}, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. No collector had come up or down the clay road for the reading, and none came today. No lesson had been made of any of it, and the morning going on over the yard did not make one now.

Nothing had come over the boards in the small hours to make a sixth asking, and the silence that stood where it might have stood was a keeping and not a witness; the five askings and the one shaping stayed as they stood with no row named and no owner under any of them. No sixth hearing of the full note had come either, and the empty place kept its emptiness, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; nine record-first hands stood with a finger between every pair and no tenth; the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth of either. He did not lay any of them across another, and he did not call any of them a rule.

The house kept itself by \lore{not-doings}. It kept the fire unbreathed at the fifth pull, the charcoal unread beneath the shelf’s edge, the two lines unswept, the debt uncalled, the grain unfigured, the thread unwiped and the crust unlifted. From the room whose floor does not touch his, a woman’s low counting came through in threes --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and went on without anybody asking what she counted. She did not count the boy, and nobody under the roof counted the days in anything a slate could take. The boy kept the house’s keeping on one place and his own gaps on another, with a finger between, and did not lay either across the other. He had once laid out instruments on \place{the towel} for a night’s trauma and known each by its weight before the eye fell on it; the finding here was exact in the same way and worth as much, which was nothing at two inches of air.

Sella set the basket down in the finger’s width of shade at the well where no slate lay, and went about the running water with her little-door burn shut on nothing. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wind. “It’s the river wind.” She gave the basket no instruction and no reason, and carried on as a thing set down is carried and left, level. The counted breath from the room whose floor does not touch his had gone on under the roof with its small intake, hold and letting-go, and the boy took only what the hand could take and left the letting-go where it had been left. Nothing under the roof had opened the box up the clay road, and nothing under the roof would.

The plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet, clouded where the silt had risen into it, and the boy lay in the basket set in the well’s shade with the crust two inches from his ear and its empty place beside it unnamed. The hand outside the wool went up an inch. It took the small intake of the river’s own going, the low thing over lower under the door and here in the open, and let it go on the same note without holding, without carrying it across a silence that belonged to another person’s counting and without borrowing a house breath to close it. It came down. The letting-go stayed whole. It was not an ending made early, not a freedom and not a gift, for a freedom wants a taker and nobody under this roof had taken anything. It was not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule; it was an object on the sixth place, one hundred and thirty-five beside one hundred and thirty-four with a finger between, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand laid back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Frost 20 stood eight mornings ahead and was not brought into this one. Forward went the day without a line drawn under it.
