The afternoon thinned in the \place{Clay Yard} the way heat goes out of a pot lifted off the coals and set on a cold stone. It did not end; it drew back. The warmth that had stood in the fold at his ear since first light went out of the wool by degrees, and \person[Corin]{the boy} felt the going of it before he felt the cold, the way a man feels the pressure ease before he understands the wound that was under it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and could not close two inches on the pot handle set beyond his reach. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and seventeen days old the second was louder; the surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-seven days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. At the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and behind the wall the kiln breathed in fours with the silence where the fifth should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to any. Two men were under the roof and only one of them had a body that could be warmed; the older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other, and the naming did not warm him.

\person{Sella Vail} carried him down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, and the half-pace was not haste and not fear but the distance a woman keeps from a thing that any man with a slate in his hand could enter. She gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to the wind, \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, to the wool and not to the child who could not have answered, and the boy watched the going of her burn-wrist the way he had once watched a surgeon's hand leave a field --- for what it did not touch. The yard had the look of a room somebody had swept around a sleeping animal: a wide border left where the feet went, and the animal undisturbed in the middle of it. She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, and the seven stayed seven and did not stand up and call themselves an account, for an account wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood under any of them.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay beside the finer grey hairline the house calls \lore{the thaw}, and she swept neither, giving the broom its instruction before anybody --- \emph{Go around. It cost enough to come in; it'll cost more to take out}. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken \lore{birth-slates} kept their place in the dust and the yard went around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and no hand picked up a piece and no slate was entered. On the stone at \place{the rack end} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept its shape with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; a pan was gone from it years ago and the ring had outlasted every account that might have said what became of it. \place{The trough} beside \place{the yard well} had a skin over its water from edge to edge, and out on \place{the worked strips} the field's empty place lay where the planting had not filled it, and in the returning line another place lay where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. The boy carried the missing hand the way he carried the crust in the fold and the man carried out on Day 188: as an object on its own place, not named, not paid, not joined to anything that stood near it.

At the well the white \lore{frost-bloom} stayed on the north lip where it had stayed since Frost 8, past noon and past the going of the light and onto days that had no business keeping it; the rope gave its dry note as a woman drew, and the water in the trough held its skin. On \place{the flat stone by the well}, set where no foot went, the plate of ice stood clouded with silt, lifted out of the trough on Frost 9 and told where to stand. The yard called all of it weather, and weather was entered nowhere. The boy kept the white lip, the trough's skin and the plate on three places of their own with a finger between, and made no fourth likeness between them and no cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and nobody stood. Three coverings laid over three depths were three coverings; a fourth likeness would have been an account with no payer, which is the kind of account this house has learned to leave where it lies.

At the rack end she lifted one corner of the wool from the dressed grain to feel for damp, and laid it back with a touch lighter than the one before, and no figure came off the grain and no cost was named to it. The arithmetic standing two mornings off would read over a thing whose price it did not know, and the space between the figure and what the grain had cost would stay unsigned. He had learned to recognize that space the way another man recognizes a familiar face across a room, and he did not greet it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the yard's failing light, and she did not look toward the basket.

A wrong wind came up off \place{High Kiln} and brought the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} over \place{the boards}, and it touched the fold at his ear and went out again on the wind that brought it --- a direction arrived nowhere, not a second edge paid, not a sign, not a mending. He kept it on a place of its own and did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings} or his own gaps, for want of a payer under either. The keeping stayed a finding and not a rule.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill. Six stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip where it could not; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He said \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, and said \emph{Yes, house} to a woman who wanted the thaw early and \emph{Yes, house} to a man who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts of \place{the clay road} and had stood there since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered stopping, the ninth, and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it had stopped, and from above came only a sound with a direction out of \place{the Debt Loft}, where the warm stone in its unmarked pot was cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road and none went down it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. When Sella's sentence came low and unfinished his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of the end of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. There is no \lore{Grathic} word for the two bites of that one iron, the holding and the taking, and the boy set it on its own place joined to no kiln and entered in no slate; a clamp is a clamp. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; three sayings about a pot stood together with the Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. He did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined under one roof.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. His salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and Sella let no one fill it. The clean dark line at the corner kept its finer grey beside it, unswept; the proverb had gone years ago to a pot and the fire and never to a person --- feed the fire before you feed yourself, leave the crack showing, people trust a thing they can see has been handled --- and the debt against a firing of tiles stood neither paid nor settled nor done with, and was worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and ninety-seven days. No collector came up the clay road and none went down it; Frost's first arithmetic and Frost's second rode locked up the road toward High Kiln in \person{Berr Clay}'s box, and the keeping of a man by not-doings stayed on its own place, maintenance and not a story, not joined to any shaping that had come over the boards and not made to answer for a name a district had spent. The house went around his absence the way it went around the line on the floor, and there was no lesson in it for anybody who cared to learn one.

The yard offered three private keepings in one afternoon, and he kept his finger between them. The short strip of notched birch lay in a sleeve a pace away from the well, unread and unfigured; the charcoal tally lay beneath \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge where a woman's finger rested on top of the board and never went under it; and the red wool cord of an office lay folded square under a coat up the clay road, where a man had passed his thumb twice on an apron before touching nothing. Three keepings were three and not one account; a joining would have wanted a payer standing under all three and none stood. The restraint was not an attainment and did not make a man freer than the cold that held him. It was the only honest work available to two inches of air.

At the well \person{Ilva} drew with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey rack dust worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and were not soft, and the district would not have turned its head twice for her; she was of the yard in the way the rack and the trough were of the yard. A neighbour with a pot, falling in beside her at the bucket-round, asked what the month's firings had taken now the month was nearly out, and an open slate lay on the stone lip with the writing turned toward its owner and nobody reading it aloud.

Her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The flat of it came down on the place where the strip lay as a woman lays a hand flat on a thing she has no mind to take up; the notches were not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted, not asked about and not joined to the open slate a pace away, and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the woman with the pot after the hand had settled and her mouth opened after both with a half-beat between, and she answered the question put and not the one behind it: \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.} She put the flat of her hand down once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned to look at her twice.

The boy, lying at the edge of the work where no slate lay, took the order of her reaching apart the way he took a wound apart, by what was there and by what was not: hand to the record, thumb over the notches, eyes to the asker, mouth after both. He filed it as the ninth record-first hand on a place of its own beside the eighth, with a finger between every pair, and filed no tenth, and made no figure of the slate's nearness and kept that nearness as an absence rather than a joining. He made her no reader of her own keeping, no witness and no party, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked and none of this stood anywhere to be asked. Nine were nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. She did not look toward the basket and did not know she had been read, and nothing of the exchange came under the roof as lesson, figure, mending, cause or payment.

The light went out of the yard without a boundary drawn, and neither of the district's two habits of naming its months was set right or left wrong in so many words: the older women named Frost by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days, and the two went on side by side like two hands on one rope. Frost 28 went the way Frost 1 had come, without a lesson left on the step. She carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on \place{the bed} under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. The thread at his mouth lay frozen where it had lain since morning and was left there; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a going of the light is not an opening. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next opening stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

At \place{the hearth} she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale in the going light, and set \place{the pot board}'s rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with the handle turned toward it. There was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. The half-beat was not a gift and the handle turned toward the hand was not an instruction to take; a gift wants a taker, and the boy was not given the means to close two inches of air. Her finger stayed on top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The instructions went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. The basket was set down and left, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the next room. At the very end of the day a small intake came from the room whose floor does not touch his --- the breath of a woman counting what she will not say aloud --- and he took it at its top and let it go on the same note, whole and unjoined. He did not carry it into the carried fire's missing fifth, did not cross the room's letting-go with the river's low note of water over water, did not borrow a house count, did not supply a beat under the floor to make the row come out even, for a fifth supplied to please a total is a finding forged and a payer invented to stand under it. The letting-go went on belonging to the woman who let it go and was not crossed or borrowed; it was her breath and her count and not his.

The hundred and seventieth answer was filed exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. It was not promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. The clean satisfaction the surgeon felt at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's plain want of the crust two inches off, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue; nothing bought by the checking could reach two inches of air. He had once spent a life finding the one error that makes a set of numbers agree, and here the finding was to leave them as they stood.

The hand laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. One hundred and seventy honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing. Two hundred and ninety-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and seventeen days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. Frost 30 stood two mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it or made a sign of anything. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended; no thread was opened and none was closed. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. The river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door after the hand came down. The crust lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed, and out on the flat stone by the well the plate of ice stood clouded with silt out of the path of feet while nothing under the roof had bought the lifting of the crust.
