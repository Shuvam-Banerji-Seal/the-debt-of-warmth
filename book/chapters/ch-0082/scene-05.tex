\daymark{299}{Frost 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

Frost 30 came on its own morning, as the last loaf in a batch comes out not by its own choice but because the fire has burned down to it. It had stood three mornings ahead and unbrought, and now it was here in the small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof with its market folded inside it and its reading not yet spoken and two hundred people not yet leaning in. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his two hundred and ninety-ninth day with nobody under any roof to tell him so. Two hundred and ninety-eight had gone to two hundred and ninety-nine as one board of \place{the boards} gives place to the next, with a nail between; six months and one hundred and nineteen days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its fifth unpaid two hundred and ninety-nine days with its payer unnamed, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. There would be a market today and a bell today, but neither had come into the small hours to say so. A day earns its own noise. This one had not yet earned a stroke of it.

He took the body in the order it presented itself, crust, gap, thread, cold and the waiting after, because that order had never lied to him and no other had promised to. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on \place{the bed}, gone past the point where a child would eat it, its empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire; the ease cold puts on a face is not ease. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body six months and one hundred and nineteen days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter; the older kept the inventory and the younger kept the wanting, and neither had authority over the other, and the naming bought nothing at two inches of air.

Through the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have stood; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood to any. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, a heard thing beside a told thing; no bell had rung yet this morning and he would not lend an ear one had not earned, for a bell comes on its own morning the way a month comes. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the burned-slate word stood at four, the shut-room tellings at two; the three shapings of the Frost 14 saying stood beside the eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair, and a strip of notched wood stayed unread in a sleeve not under this roof, and nine must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to these.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. That was the grammar of the house, and it held on an Account-day as strictly as on a tending morning: the instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} when the sour breath came off the unswept corner, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her finger stayed on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally beneath remained unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps, and neither gap got a figure; her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze}, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the low light. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a reading is not an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. The dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}; she lifted a corner and turned it once with a touch lighter than the one before, put the wool back, and gave it no figure, though a number was already on its way up \place{the clay road} that would go over it before noon and would not know what it cost. The pot rim stood two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward the bed. Behind her the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing moved.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note and stayed for a small intake, and came down without filing anything, because the intake wanted a place put under it and none stood. This morning the work of the hand was only to be ready; the answer it would give had not yet happened to it. She carried him down at the half-pace from every open slate, in the basket under the fold of wool like a pot filled that must not be slopped, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it's the river wind. The way up ran past doors where slates lay, and she went wide of every one as of a sleeping animal she had no wish to wake. Above, \place{the terrace} climbed toward \place{High Kiln}, and the road that came down from it was the clay road, dried pale in its ruts and scuffed with the passage of feet; a wrong wind off the kiln brought the sweet edge of lead glaze into the square and took it out again on the wind that brought it, a direction arrived nowhere and not a second edge paid. At \place{the Cross} she set the basket under \place{the reading block under the moot-post}, in the finger's width of thin shade where no slate lay, with the cold iron at his cheek and the shade lying on him like a cloth laid over a loaf to keep the dust off, and stood half a pace off through the reading, giving no instruction and no reason to the basket.

\scenebreak

The square was full of people leaning in. Two hundred of them stood shoulder to shoulder over the wet boards where eight pigs had already gone and would go again, and the reading block under the moot-post stood at the centre of them like a stone set out to be leaned upon. The market smelled of wet pig, of tallow smoke from the stalls, and of the clay dust that had come down off the terrace in the soles of two hundred pairs of feet. Two half-loaves went from hand to hand at the edge of the crowd and came back with a bite out of each that nobody had paid for; a girl with a creel took the out-of-line steps below the bridge sideways and did not fall. The boy lay in the finger's width of shade and let the noise go over him, not counted and not entered. Today there were two arithmetics already behind the figure that would be spoken and two readings that had stood where he now lay, and a third coming on, and not one of them had known the weight of the grain lying covered at the rack end at home.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road a little before the top of the morning, with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted lead glaze at one corner of his mouth. Under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath, and the two hundred leaning in did not know they were smelling it. His hands were large and clean; he wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks a stitch he has set before he cuts the thread. Then he read \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says} low into the two hundred, and the sentence went over the top of the basket without ever going into it.

He sent the figure over the basket twice: once for the moot, where the two hundred stood, and once for the households that had come late and had not heard the first. He called the levy for Frost and denied it in the same breath, \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me} --- never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the figure to stand on its own with nobody's hand under it, and the two hundred leaned in to hear it. To a man at the rail who brought a number up out of a fist he said \emph{A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch} --- \emph{your house}, never \emph{I} --- and said it to the rail and not to the man, the way a man speaks to a pot he means to have obeyed. The man put the fist back into his sleeve with the number still in it. The notice stood pinned to the moot-post with one in twelve printed on it in a clean hand, and nobody took it down and nobody set a mark against it, because a printed figure is a thing a man can be seen reading and the reading of it is not the paying of it. The crowd went back to the pigs.

The boy knew the small movement by which Berr had put himself out of his sentence. He had made it himself once over a man opened on a table in a white room, drawing his hands back before the closure so that the chart would hold what the room had done and nothing the room had felt; the knowledge came without a picture and without a name, as the feel of a clamp comes to a hand that has not been told it is remembering. Here the field was a square of wet boards and the dressing was a figure that would be entered against households who had not stood there to hear it. Frost's third true subtraction went up the clay road in the locked box behind the first two, and behind it the district's arithmetic changed a third time for the month and could not be changed back. The printed figure stood unchallenged. The figure read was not the figure paid, and the space between them had no signature on it, and nobody in the square asked who had signed it. Berr went off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the third subtraction inside, believing he had done a kindness; a kindness is a thing a man can carry up a road without ever looking into the basket he has just read over.

The reading knew nothing of what it read over. No figure in the square, no slate on the block, no reader with a thumb wiped twice, answered for the man carried out on Day 188, whose name had not been spoken over the basket; none answered for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, still gone around at the returning line with a look and no number; none knew the dressed grain lying covered under wool on the cool stone at the rack end at home, now read over for the third time that month by a number that did not know what it cost. The boy kept Frost's first arithmetic, Frost's second and Frost's third on three places with a finger between every pair, three and not one account, and kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it. He very nearly laid the three readings across the house's \lore{not-doings} --- the two lines unswept at the corner, the widow's mark untouched, the charcoal unread beneath the shelf's front edge, the fifth pull unsupplied under the floor, the loan uncalled at \place{the kindling}, the crust in the fold not lifted two inches to a mouth that wanted it --- and across his own kept gaps beside them. Three readings and three omissions stood edge to edge in the mind, and the edges nearly touched. Then the finger went between them and stayed there. A joining would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood under the readings and none stood under the not-doings, and a payer invented to make the two agree would have been a finding forged about the square as surely as one forged about the floor. It was not a lesson. It was not a grievance he could carry up the road and set down at a door, nor a mending, for nothing had been mended, nor a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of it. The finding was exact and worth nothing at two inches of air, and he did not call it a virtue and did not spend it.

The bell went over the square in the turn of the morning, longer than a bell in a temple yard, and came from somewhere above the reading block and went out over the pigs and the stalls and the fish-trade shade below the bridge: four strokes, and four, and then the place where the fifth should have stood. The square went on with what it was doing. Not one hand in two hundred went to a face. The boy held his inch through the stroke that did not come and came down on the sound after it, supplying nothing to fill the hollow. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night, a heard thing beside a told thing; it was not folded into the three counts under the roof, not laid across the fire's missing fifth, the boards' thumb-width knot or the river's low over lower, and not heard as a seventh of the full note. Four was four in the fire, four in the nails and four in the bell, and the gap stood where a fifth should have stood in each of the three places and rose up in none of them. He lent the bell no more than an ear it had already earned and let the silence after it be a silence and not a summons.

\person{Hessa Rale} took the narrow slate up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} before noon, edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve and nothing pressing where the writing was. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round; the second strap was let out for the long road, and the dried pale scurf of road mud on the skirt she had shortened for the wet stood unbrushed, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd. To the women who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}; and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}. No figure came off the slate. She did not look toward any woman at \place[the yard well]{the well}, and the three shapings of the Frost 14 saying stood where they stood, beside the eighth record-first hand with a finger between every pair; they were not joined to her slate and did not make her a reader, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and nothing of that saying stood anywhere to be asked. Eight was eight and was not a rule, and the road under her kept its own count in the legs.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} for a measure nobody had called, a fifth step's worth of ground where no step stood. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, and he held the slate to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it; his hand had gone to the slate before his eyes found the man who had said the buyer's number, and the number was entered before the looking. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and he did not rise out of it, and nobody took him off.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a silent woman stood over pots with the sweetish green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth, clay dried pale under her nails in the small crescents, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood. At the market's edge a shape in cast-off clothes stood the way a uniform stands on a boy who was never measured for one, a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver of glaze on a string at the throat that caught the light on the turn and went dull going back. The half-turn came half a beat after a pot was set down with a small flat sound, and the boy filed it as a turn after a knock, not a hearing and not joined to his own turning. The green was a colour and the shape was a shape, standing on places that did not touch; each was the seventh of its kind and no eighth came into the square to join them, and neither was named, neither joined to loaf, slate, sale, hearing, cause or romance. He read the small shoulders as a fracture set in the wrong position, an old hurt nobody in the square was setting, and put the reading on a place of its own with a finger between it and everything else.

The market thinned without ending. It went out of the square the way heat goes out of a pot lifted off the coals and set on a cold stone, not all at once but by degrees, the belly of it holding longest and the rim going first, until the stone had taken what the pot had held and neither was the richer for the exchange. Eight pigs went over the wet boards and were eight still; the shallow prints they left filled with water and went flat and glossy, and the wet boards kept the place where the reading had stood without keeping the reading. The boy lay in the finger's width of shade with the cold iron at his cheek, and the afternoon went over him as the morning had, a low thing over a lower thing.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch into the quiet interval. There, between the going-back to the pigs and the next number nobody brought up out of any fist, a small intake stood whole --- the space between the moot's reading and the late households' reading, the first going into the two hundred who had stood and the second into the doorways of people who had not --- and he held it beside the twice-spoken figure with a finger between, carrying into it neither the bell's gap nor a figure to stand under either. The hundred and seventy-third answer was exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: a told figure beside a paid figure, and the two sayings kept as two sayings and not one account. The surgeon's clean satisfaction at the exact coordination was checked by the infant's louder want of the crust two inches off at home, and the checking was the work and he did not call it a virtue. Nothing bought by the checking could reach two inches. Three readings are three and not one account, and a payer is wanted under each of them and none stands under any.

Two hundred and ninety-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and nineteen days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under the roof two hundred and ninety-nine days. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in the square: no third reading into one account, no figure into a price with a signature under it, no bell's gap into a count, no green into a name, no shape into a story, no boy's kept gap into the house's not-doings. The account had gone up the clay road in Berr's locked box with Frost's third true subtraction inside and could not be changed back, and the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end under a number that did not know what it cost.

At the going of the light Sella lifted him out of the finger's width of shade and carried him home at the half-pace from every open slate, saying nothing of what had been read over the basket and giving no instruction to it. The bell's gap of four, four and a space stayed in the square behind them, and not one hand in two hundred went to a face.
