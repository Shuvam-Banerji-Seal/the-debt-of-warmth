\daymark{319}{Bone 20, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

The twentieth of Bone came up out of the dark the way a pot comes to the top of a kiln, from the inside, by a change in the air that no hand had made and that nobody under the roof lifted a finger to mark. It was the month's second Account-day and there would be a market at \place{the Cross} before the light was well up, and a bell over it at the middle of the morning, but none of that had crossed the threshold in the night and none of it came in now as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment for anything the nineteenth had held. Bone went on the way a river goes under ice, without a line drawn across it. The older women of the district named their months by the weather and the slatemen named them by the count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and nineteenth day with nobody saying so, and six months and one hundred and thirty-nine days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. The count went from three hundred and eighteen to three hundred and nineteen exact, complete, unspent, spoken by no man under any roof. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what the number counted, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the not-asking the way a coal goes on under ash.

Under the fold the body was taken in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had and he had learned to trust it before he had learned to trust anything else in this house. The crust lay two inches from his ear, stiff and leathery in the dry kiln heat, its browned corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place keeping its name to itself and no third coming to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, for nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody here would perform. He knew the composed face for what it was, the ease a cold face puts on that is not ease, and he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread at his mouth wiped and a hand that would close on a pot handle; both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two voices argued without a word between them as men argue in their sleep, and the argument was the work.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. The coals breathed under blind ash and she told them the thaw, turned them with the back of a broken pot and stood a moment with her lips moving along a row whose two gaps she did not figure, small intake, hold, letting-go, and gave neither gap a number. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf} so that the charcoal underneath stayed where it was and unread, and one glance went toward the cold clay jar on its shelf and the hand shut half over nothing. She lifted the twice-mended pot with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle, saying both proverbs to the pot and the fire and never to a person, the crack left showing. The pot rim went down two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. Then she came for him.

She lifted the basket and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket: \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}. She set him level the way a pot is set level on a board and carried him down \place{the clay road} at the half-pace she used from every open slate, with the burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing and his face turned to her shoulder. The market was already stacking its noise in the square when they came into it: poles going up, a pig complaining somewhere, the wet slap of boards being laid over mud, women calling to one another in the flat voices of people who have walked this road in every weather and expect nothing new of it. Under the fold the one crust kept its two inches and the river wind kept its cold against the wool and the wool kept it off. She carried him past the stalls and set the basket down in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade, where no slate lay and where no copy of anything had ever been set. She stood half a pace off from it, not over it and not beside it, and gave the wool and the wind the instruction once more and gave nothing at all to the basket.

\scenebreak

The reading came before the pigs. A space opened at \place{the reading block} in the way a space opens in a crowd before a man the crowd has decided to hear, and \person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat instead of worn across it. The green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth, sweetish and patient, and under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing turned inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks a dressing he has already set and knows he will not have to lift again.

\emph{One in twelve, as the notice says}, he read, low into the two hundred, and the figure went twice over the basket in the moot-post's shade, once for the moot and once for the households that had come late. He did not look into the basket. He never looked into the basket. \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me}, he said, and to a man who brought a number up out of a fist toward the block, \emph{Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch} --- your house, never I, taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. The man put his fist back into his sleeve and the figure arrived nowhere. Berr said something more low to the block about kindness being entered, and the crowd leaned in to hear it and went back to the pigs, and he went off up the clay road with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, Bone's second true subtraction riding inside beside the three of Frost and the first of Bone, believing he had done the district a kindness. The soft voice of the reading stayed in the square after him like smoke stays in a coat. The arithmetic of the district had changed for the second month running and could not be changed back.

Five arithmetics of the year now stood behind one printed figure: four readings from before and this fifth one from Bone, one in twelve unchallenged in every printed notice, the figure read still not the figure paid, and no signature standing on the space between. Five were five and not one account. A rule made out of five would have wanted a payer put under all five and no payer stood under any of them, and the boy kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, not a rule. The reading answered nothing for the hare that had gone into the weather on the nineteenth with nobody weighing it; it answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; it answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, whom no figure, collector or slate had ever known the cost of; it answered nothing for the dressed grain turned under wool at the rack end by an arithmetic that had not known what it cost and had gone up the road without learning. He did not lay the finding across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the grain under its wool, or across the four held changes on their four places, or across the stayed-at-the-inch object, or across the early letting-go of Bone 17, or across his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching, so near that a less careful hand would have pushed them together and called the touching a cause, and the finger went between.

The bell went over the square, four, four and a gap, and nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood in its own square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and the gap in the heard was not fetched in to make a seventh of the hearings of \lore{the full note} or a payer for the silence. Six hearings stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go, and the seventh place stayed empty and was not filled to make the row come out even, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled is a finding forged. The stayed-at-the-inch object stayed one object and the early letting-go stayed one object; neither was brought to the reading as a sign or a lesson, and the letting-go was not made a second time in the shade of the moot-post. The river kept its note of \lore{water over water} under the finger's-width door at home, a low thing over a lower thing, not numbered among the hearings and not laid across the bell's gap. Three counts in three places did not become one, and agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

\scenebreak

\person{Hessa Rale} took Berr's slate up the road toward \place{Greybank} before noon, edge-on to her forearm with the writing turned to her sleeve, the second strap let out for the long stretch and the dried pale scurf of road-mud standing unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it. Her hand arrived at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Two women by the rail asked what she carried. \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}, she said, and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}, answering the question put and not the one behind it and going on with the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. The fifth arithmetic had changed the district's figures below her on the square and had not made her a reader of what she carried; her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand in the district, eight and not a rule, kept on a place of its own and joined to none, and the three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood beside it with a finger between every pair and did not make her a witness, a cause or a confession. She was not the soft-handed man from Slate Row, not the clerk at the Court's table, not Ilva with her count in wood, and not the woman who had carried the saying at the well.

Below \place{the north bridge}, at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by the middle of the morning; his slate lay against his forearm with the writing inward, and his hand went to it before his eyes went to the stalls, and a buyer's number went into it before he looked up to find the man who had said the number. The market sold over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. Across the square at the pot board a silent woman with clay dried pale under her nails in the small crescents and a pale line across the back of one hand turned a cup a quarter and set it back exactly where it had stood, the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip; at the market's edge a small shape in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one stood with a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver of lead glaze on a string at the throat that caught the light on the turn and went dull going back. A pot was set down nearby with a small flat sound and the small shape turned half a beat after the knock and stopped in the middle of the turn. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, and no eighth of either came into the market on the twentieth of Bone. Neither was named in the square, neither was given a loaf, a slate or a hearing, and neither was joined to the reading that had gone over the basket twice or to the box going up the road.

\scenebreak

In the moot-post's finger's width of shade the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole, let the intake go on the same low note, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was not early and the hand did not stay. He filed it as answer two hundred and thirteen: exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, not laid across the five arithmetics, not made a hearing of the bell's gap, not a sign of anything the reading had done. Two hundred and thirteen honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Sella stood half a pace off through the reading and through the bell and did not look down at the basket, and gave it no reason and no name. The crowd went back to the pigs. Eight pigs went over the wet boards and were entered before anyone could ask which eight, and the two hundred went on with their morning the way water goes on over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the figure that had been read twice over the basket went up the road in a man's box and was not left standing in the square where anybody could ask who had paid it. The boy's count did not rise into the district's arithmetic. It stood at three hundred and nineteen, exact and unspent and told by nobody, a thing kept in a body that could not lift the wool from its own ear, and the keeping of it borrowed no house count and supplied no payer. The surgeon in him took the measure of the morning the way he had once taken the measure of a field before the light, by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie: the soft voice of the reader, the pig's complaint, the wet slap of boards, the gap in the bell where a stroke should have stood, the low note of the river under a finger's-width door far off. None of them came together. He had spent a life learning what an answer costs when three things are put into one sentence in a room with better light, and here there was no room, no sentence and no hand to put it in. He kept his attention whole while the account was entered somewhere else, and it was the only thing in the square that nobody could tax.

The figure went up the clay road in Berr Clay's locked box, five arithmetics standing behind one printed figure with no signature on the space, the figure read still not the figure paid, five readings five and not one account; and the bell's gap stayed standing in the square with nobody fetching it in. In the moot-post's finger's width of shade the hand lay back against the fold on the two hundred and thirteenth answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, while the district changed its arithmetic for the second month running over a basket that could not answer.
