\daymark{119}{Seed 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, Account-day and the last market of Seed}

The last market of Seed came up over \place{the Cross} with a thin light the colour of skimmed slip, the kind that shows everything and warms nothing. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down from \place{Low Kiln} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, going around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay. The instruction went to the wool. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” It went into the wool and into the wind off \place{the Ilm} and never into the basket, and she stood her half-pace off with the little-door burn closing first on the handle and then on nothing. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody at the moot had been given an instruction about that sound. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had wiped it. He was a man who could have told you by the feel of the back of a hand on a brow how far a body had gone, and he could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him. Both things were true. The second was the one that was loud.

Across the square the reading block stood with the day's mud drying on it in the shape of boots. Two hundred people leaned toward it in the way a crowd leans when a man is about to speak low, because if he speaks low they must come close and being close is itself a kind of having been told. Eight pigs went over the weighing board with the assessor's hand on the slate before his eyes went to the rail and his mouth went afterward, entering them before anybody could ask which eight he meant. Beyond the north road \place{the worked strips} stood with the seed up, and one place in the line was still gone around with a look and no figure. The hands had come out of it the day before. One of the hands that had gone in on Seed 15 had not come out with the rest, and the yard had gone around the space the way a woman goes around \lore{Ash} left in a corner, and gone around it again this morning on the way to the reading. No body had come up. No cause had been said. No name had been given the space and no figure set at the foot of it. The planting month ended on this day and Reed came on the morrow, and the space went into the new month still held and still unentered.

\person{Berr Clay} came up to the block short, off the north road, with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth. He smelled of hot clay and, under it, nothing at all, which was the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean, the hands of a man who had once carried clay and had then stopped carrying it and kept the carrying in the way he held things. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched anything, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm while he passed a finger over what was written and let his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required. It was the check of a man setting down a dish already sure it will not slop, a small careful passing of the thumb that cost nothing and would be entered nowhere. The boy set the twice-wiped thumb on a place of its own. He had watched a midwife reach for a slate before a child, a neighbour reach for a bare board before she looked at him, a clerk behind a Court table put hand to paper before eye to face, a man price a basket without looking into it, Jorra speak to the wheel before the pots, Sella give the instruction to the broom before anybody, Alda lay her writing-down on the bench before the fold. Berr's thumb made another shape on another place, and the boy laid no finger across any of them, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause as surely as silt gathers behind a weir, and the clean hand at the foot of a cause was the one object he had spent two lives declining to become.

Berr read low, and the crowd leaned in.

“One in twelve, as the notice says.”

He said it into the top of the two hundred the way a man says a thing to a pot, without raising his voice and without looking to see who had heard. The notice had been printed through two bad winters and a war of deductions that he called, when pressed, a disagreement about arithmetic; a man could stand under a printed figure all morning and never think to ask whose arithmetic it agreed with. “The levy is called,” he said. “It is not called by me.”

A man come up from the strips brought a number up out of a fist and held it a moment in the open palm for the yard to see, the way a man shows a caught thing before he decides whether to keep it. “What did the planting cost,” he said, “now the hands have been in it?”

Berr did not look at the palm. He passed his finger over the figure on his slate again, the way a surgeon goes back to the chart before he goes back to the patient, and the boy knew the order before the man's mouth had finished closing.

“A number goes into a slate, not into a strip,” Berr said. “If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.”

Your house. Never I. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field open and clean and nobody's, and the man with the number stood with his palm still half open and nowhere to put it. He shut the fist and put it back into his sleeve, and the number went from the fist to Berr's mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere. That was the district's whole arithmetic, done in the open air over worked ground: a figure read aloud that was not the figure paid, a figure put into a slate that was not a figure put into a strip, a figure withheld in the very act of planting withheld like any other figure. The true subtraction sat in a locked box in a house up the road and had not been shown to anybody. The figure that went into the slate did not know the hand that had not come back out of the strip. It did not know there had been a hand. It did not know there had been a strip.

The boy kept Berr's twice-wiped thumb and his finger passing over the figure on a tray of their own and did not join them to any other record-first hand. A figure read over the top of a basket was a figure read over the top of a basket, and the basket had stood in the moot-post's shade since morning with a child in it who had no name any slate wanted. Nobody had looked in. Nobody had entered him. The crowd leaned a little closer at the reading and went back to its pigs when the reading was done, and the moot went on being a moot with a space in it shaped like the hollow of a wheel-seat that nobody would fill.

The sentence went around the square the way a bucket goes around a yard of thirsty people, passed from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder, and nobody thought to ask who was carrying it or where it had been filled. A woman with a shawl over her head said something about the thaw and the late rains, and Berr answered her with an instruction about the notice, which was how he apologised, and she went away with the answer as though she had been given one. The boy had learned the shape of that grammar in the better-lit room of his other life: the speaker taken out of the sentence, the figure left standing in it, the cost carried by nobody. He had once let a chart go by with a note turned down under his own signature, and the shape of it had stayed with him for two lives since. Here the chart was a narrow slate held unopened against a forearm, and the signer was a man wiping his thumb clean, and the not-reading was done in the open air before two hundred people who leaned in to hear it.

The bell went four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself. The boy kept the heard bell on a place of its own beside the told bell of the Long Night, two counts in two places that did not touch, because a bell heard in a market and a bell told in the dark in a house with no fire are the same four strokes and the same missing stroke and not the same thing. Below \place{the north bridge} at \place{the Fishstairs} the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at the out-of-line landing with his cuff grey from washing and his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. The market sold its eels over the top of him as though a man with a slate were a thing a landing always had. The boy kept the grey of the washed cuff on one place and the slate held at the edge on another. A man who washed his own cuff had a hunger or a habit; a man who held a slate where nobody else could see the figure had a use. The two readings stood edge to edge and did not touch, and he made no rule of either, because a measure set down before anybody called it was a shape and not yet a cause, and the day it wanted a name would come from another direction and another mouth. The steps came back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line where a step should have been, and a whole market walked over it carefully without making a fall of it: a girl with a creel took it sideways and caught the rail and went on selling, a man with a pot stepped over and did not look at what he had stepped over, and nobody called it an accident. The boy set the out-of-line landing beside the missing fifth stroke and laid no finger across them. A landing out of line and a bell with a gap were two things in two places, and the market walked over the first and went on with the second the way it went on with weather.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof at the going of the light he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found every object where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, and no seventh came; no whole vessel's note had come past the door today, the bell was not a hearing, the wheel's and the pots' notes were not hearings, and he did not invent one to fill the place where a seventh would have stood. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid: a cup across a birth room, a pot under the wheel-shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, three answers to a question nobody had asked aloud. Three intervals kept their places, with the half-beat of a checked stroke and the stroke that went on without it lying beside them as two objects and not a rule. The told bell stayed beside the heard bell in two places that did not touch. The word forty stayed unspoken with the readable double turn of a cord twisted twice beneath another cord beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both. Four low things kept four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth, and the fifth was not a hearing and had never been counted among the four. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed joined to no kiln, and the damper's double bite stayed an object filed and spent on nothing, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take a hand off a man in one piece of iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies. In the fold one stiff brown-edged crust lay where it had lain since Stone fifteen with the empty place beside it still without a name and no third; on \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room two gaps stood unnamed in a room that did not touch this one. The clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner and the new finer grey beside it lay unswept and were called \lore{the thaw}; the space under the lowest rack was gone around and entered by nobody; the held breath in a man's chest was still unspent and the loan that could hear was still uncalled. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. He knew the whole of it the way he had once known it under the lamps of a bay, and the reading worked perfectly and bought nothing.

No seventh hearing came today. No sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came. No knot was counted. Nothing was opened. He added what the day had given: the month's printed figure read over the absence nobody had entered, the number shut back into a sleeve and carried nowhere, a clerk still set for a measure nobody called, the steps walked over without a fall. He laid no finger across any of them. Keeping the places apart was the whole of the craft and the whole of the uselessness: a line drawn through the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust and the field's empty place would make a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the one thing in this world he would not be. The account stood advanced and not paid, toward a far-off day when it might want a name, and no name came today.

Sella told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said the house would keep it where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark and did not look toward the bed. The fold lay where she had put it, one crust and the empty place, and the thread at his mouth had frozen again in the going light and nobody under the roof had been given an instruction about the thread. The carried fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees, its payer unnamed for a hundred and nineteen days. Every warmth in this house had a payer somewhere and no slate in the district carried the name.

The fire's four, four and a gap came up through the floor and the river's note of water over water came through the finger's-width door, and the two counts in two places did not meet, and the three counts did not become one. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note and came down. One hundred and nineteen stood where one hundred and eighteen had stood in the small hours, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody who would have known what a number of days counted when they said it. The planting month went out on the tally and Reed came on in the dark beyond the door, a direction spoken of as work and not as a figure. Nothing was joined, named, paid or ended; the place in the returning line went on being gone around with a look and no figure, the two gaps on the shelf stayed in the other room, and the fold kept its one crust and its unnamed place. On the morrow one hundred and twenty would stand in the same place with the same four nails above it and the same knot the width of a thumb and the same gap under the floor, and the boy would be carried into a month whose work had not yet been given a figure.
