Back under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the going of the light came down through the oiled paper in thin grey squares, and the house took \person[Corin]{the boy} in the way a cup takes water poured against its inside: carefully, and with a little left over that nobody entered. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down where no slate lay and gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody.

“Round the lip,” she said to the bucket. “No slops on the ash.” To the broom she gave the next: “Go around. It cost enough to come in; it will cost more to take out.” To the pot she gave the one about the door, and to the fire the one about its own cold mouth, and to the basket she gave nothing at all, the way a woman sets a bowl filled to the lip on a table and does not speak to it in case it should slop. The words went to the things and the things took them; the words did not go to the basket, and the basket did not expect them. She said *the house* where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and nothing in the room turned its head at that, because the house had been saying it for three years and the boy had heard it since before he could hear anything else.

She went around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and kept her half-pace from every open slate between the door and the bench. The river wind came under the eaves off \place{the Ilm} with the sour edge of mud in it, and she gave the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it is the river wind. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth of the fire and came down. The fire was two paces off and the hand had as much chance of reaching it as it had of reaching the year before last. Both facts were true, and the second was the one that was loud here.

Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool had ridden back from the bare joint so the cold found the place it had come for, and the wind under the eaves went under the fold at his ear and found the other place. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody put it right, because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering. He wanted the stiff brown-edged crust two inches from his ear and the breast and the bucket’s lip two paces off with its cold iron handle, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would have known what a number of days counted when they said it. He got none of them and could ask for none of them. He was a man who had once held pressure in a cavity with both hands while a student counted the pulse out loud, and he could not close two inches on a fold that smelled of wet wool and nothing else.

\place{the loaf shelf} stood in the next room, and the house kept its days there badly: loaves pushed along a board, a glance at the clay jar on the wall, and a space at the row’s end nobody called a figure. A neighbour came in with a pot under her arm, come up from \place{the worked strips} where the seed stood up and the hands had come out with one place gone around. There was chaff dried in the creases of the fist that held the pot’s handle, and mud on the skirt-hem where she had come across the low ground, and she set the pot down on the bench with the care of a woman who has carried it a long way and means to carry it away again. Then she reached past the bare board before her eyes found the basket, the way a hand goes to a well-lip before the face goes to the water, opened for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, and closed on the hard heel of a loaf beyond. The half-beat was small enough to be nothing. It was the same smallness as the shut fist in the field and the two fingers with chaff worked into the creases, exact, complete and worth nothing.

“The end place came in with the planting,” she said to the board, or to her pot; it was not said to the boy and did not want an answer from him. “And this one is the price of a wet thing set down wrong.” She shaped the second saying with her mouth as she said it, the way a woman shapes a thing she has said before and expects to say again, and put the loaf under her arm with the pot and went off without once seeing that she had laid two bare boards under one cause, or that the two boards were one absence, or that there was a third empty place in a room her feet had never entered. The saying went out with her through the door and left the shelf standing with two gaps and no figure over either.

Sella moved her lips along the loaf row without a figure, the way a woman counts what she will not say, and did not look toward the basket while she did it. The loaves went along the board in the shape of the days to Kindling, and her lips came back to the same place twice and went on, and the shape stood where a figure would have stood if a figure had been wanted. She had heard the neighbour’s two sayings and heard them as two sayings and not one absence, and the private roll she kept stayed where it was kept and was not opened for a neighbour with a pot.

The boy set the shelf’s two gaps on a place of their own, and the one stiff brown-edged crust in the fold with its unnamed empty place beside it on another, and the neighbour’s one-breath joining on a third. Three places that did not touch. He allowed no third gap and no fourth likeness; a likeness laid on a likeness grew a cause as surely as silt gathers behind a weir unless a hand held them apart, and the clean hand at the foot of a cause was the one object he had spent two lives declining to become. He kept his own hand out of it. That the neighbour’s hand had gone to the board before her eyes had gone to him was the order of the house laid bare — the record before the person, the pot before the child — and it was the truest thing said under this roof today and the least spendable. He had learned in a better-lit room what a neat line drawn over a note does to the note, and he did not draw one here.

Once, and only once, Sella’s glance went to the clay jar on the wall shelf, cold under its glaze, and went back to the loaves. Her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat, because a shelf is not an opening and she had been given no instruction to open anything. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned under the corner lay where it lay, and the new finer grey hairline beside it lay where it lay, and neither was swept; the house called both \lore{the thaw} and the thaw would go when it went. No opening had come under this roof in all his days, and the roll that would have entered one stayed unshown.

At the going of the light she carried him to his bed under the roof and not to \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him down the way a woman sets down a pot she has carried a long way and cannot bring herself to let go of. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted, and here they counted the breaths at his ear and entered the count nowhere. The hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. The half-beat was the whole of what the house would allow itself, and it went out with her hand.

She told the fire it was the thaw, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, her lips moving the way they had moved along the loaf row, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals, a single pale thread in a head of coal-black that she said had come in the winter \person[Garl Vail]{Garl} died and would not discuss further. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark and did not look toward the bed. The little-door burn closed on nothing as the hand came away.

The boy turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel, and found every object where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person; no seventh had come and he had not invented one. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on their own tray, and three intervals on another, and beside the intervals both the half-beat of a stroke checked and the stroke that went on without it, two objects and not a rule. The told bell lay beside the heard bell in two places that did not touch. The word forty lay unspoken with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb a little apart from both, and he had not said it and no thumb had counted the knot. Four low things stood in four places with the river’s note of water over water a fifth, and the two tellings of the shut counting room lay joined to no kiln, and the double bite of \lore{the damper} lay filed as an object joined to no firing. The held breath from Seed 16 lay where it had lain, unspent, and the collector sentence nobody forged lay beside it, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled. The figure the yard had not given for the hands that went into the strips lay on a place of its own, a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody had opened.

To these he added what the day had given: the place in the returning line that the yard went around with a look and no figure, and the neighbour’s one-breath joining of two bare boards. He set them on places of their own and laid no finger across either. He did not lay the field’s gap beside the shelf’s two, or the neighbour’s saying beside the crust in the fold, or any of them beside the word forty; a line drawn through the places would have made a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent two lives declining to become. The file lay complete and exact, and there was nobody under the roof who could have read a word of it. His exactness was the only arithmetic in the district and the more exact it grew the more it isolated him, and he went on keeping it because there was nothing else to keep.

The carried fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixteen days. It took its coal and gave back its four and kept the fifth where it had always kept it, and the house said the thaw and went on. Every warmth in this room had a payer somewhere, and no slate in the district carried the name.

The door stood a finger’s width open. Through the gap came the river going over itself with the low note of water over water, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; through the floor came the kiln’s four, four and a gap. The two counts did not meet. The three counts did not become one. Above him the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the boy lay with the count where the count had always been.

His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and sixteen stood where one hundred and fifteen had stood in the small hours, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody in the district, while the river went over water beyond the finger’s-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor and the two counts did not meet. The fold smelled of wet wool and tallow and nothing else, the crust lay stiff two inches from his ear with the empty place beside it unnamed, and the thread at his mouth froze again in the dark and was not wiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread.
