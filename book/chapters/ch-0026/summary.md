## Chronicle

On the twenty-seventh of Seed the last hands came out of the worked strips, counted in glances and in a barrow-man's half-shaped word. One hand that went in on Seed 15 did not come out with the rest, and the yard went around the empty place in the line with a look and no number. Sella Vail carried the boy at the half-pace from every open slate, instructing the wool and the wind and never the basket. At the loaf shelf a neighbour called the old gap a planting and the new one the price of a wet thing set down wrong, without seeing she had joined two rooms. The boy kept three places apart.

On the twenty-eighth, before first grey, men came up the Clay Yard into the finger's-width-open room, and Jorra Vail gave them no name. He came through with grey slate dust in both forearms past the bent finger and put that finger into the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read the iron as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes a hand off a man. Tarn's seventh year stood pressed to the sill and still held, his breath unspent; his stroke went on over the stones without the half-check, and neither man named the other's silence. Across the market a woman looked at pots with green at one corner of her mouth and said nothing.

On the twenty-ninth a woman at the well asked what the planting cost, and Sella sent her to the pot: *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs. The house has said it.* At the loaf shelf a second neighbour called the old gap a planting and the empty place at his ear a child's appetite, without seeing she had joined two rooms. The boy set her saying beside the first and made no joining of his own. Mira Silt put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was named, neither knot counted. Alda Reed laid her birth-slates before the fold, covered the birth-flower with a dead woman's date and said *Warm and breathing*. Below the sill a child's foot stayed dark in three turns of wool.

On the thirtieth, Account-day, Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice and read *one in twelve, as the notice says*; called the levy and denied it, and told a man with a fist of numbers that a number goes into a slate, not into a strip --- *your house*, never *I*. The figure read was not the figure paid and did not know the missing hand. Under the roof the boy found his file unchanged --- six hearings and no seventh, the word forty unspoken, one crust and two shelf gaps --- and Sella told the fire it was the thaw. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. One hundred and nineteen stood unspent; nothing was joined, named, paid or ended.
