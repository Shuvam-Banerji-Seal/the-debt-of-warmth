## Scene 1 — The Place in the Line

**Day and time.** Day 116, Seed 27, Seventhday, at the going of the light.

**Place.** The edge of the worked strips east of Low Kiln, where the planting's last tend gives up its hands.

**Who is present.** Corin, carried; Sella Vail; unnamed field workers going out of the strips; the man with a barrow; a woman lifting two fingers with chaff in the creases. Berr Clay, Jorra, Tarn, Alda, Mira, Orra and Ulda are offstage. Dessa stays dead and unnamed to the boy.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open on the field at the end of the light. The seed stands up in the strips and the last hands come out of the worked brown ground. The district counts them the way it has counted everything this month: in glances and handfuls, a woman lifting two fingers with chaff worked into the creases, a man with a barrow shaping the number of stooped backs with his mouth and then shutting it, nobody putting a figure to the row. The weather will have the last word; the row comes up short and a body gets used to it.
2. One place in the line is gone around with a look. The hand that went in on Seed 15 does not come back with the rest. Nobody names it, calls an accident, sends for anybody or enters a figure; the yard simply goes around the space the way it goes around the clean dark line and the space under the lowest rack. **Plant T098 here** --- an absence entered in no slate and gathered in glances, held for the far-off day a body is found or a figure is finally given for what the month cost. No name, no cause, no body, no identification of which hand, no payment. Forward, not closed.
3. Sella Vail carries the boy at the very edge of the work, the basket held level the way a woman carries a bowl filled to the lip, at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate. The instruction goes to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket; she says *the house* where another woman would say *I*. She does not look into the basket toward the strips.
4. The boy, one hundred and sixteen days old with nobody saying so, reads the shut line the way he read the shut fist on Seed 24: a chart closed with a neat line over a note nobody has opened --- exact, complete, worth nothing. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the worked strips and comes down. He wants the crust, the breast, the bucket's lip and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted; gets none of them.
5. Close the scene on the yard turning away from the strips into the dusk, the empty place in the line still gone around, Sella's basket held level as she starts back toward the kiln-house.

**Emotional turn.** From the ordinary, ending work of a month to the quiet arrival of an absence the whole district can see and none will enter. The boy's exactness meets the district's glance-counting and is worth nothing to either; the reader is made to feel the gap before anybody gives it a shape.

**Sensory anchors.** Turned brown ground with seed up; chaff in the creases of fists; the barrow's wooden rim; wet wool; river wind off the Ilm; the going light; the half-pace; the cold arriving cheek, temple, wrist; the small wet sound of the wandering head.

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T098 planted only, for the later body or figure; it gathers interest through Chapters 27–30 and is not named, counted or paid here. Nothing paid off.

## Scene 2 — Two Gaps and a Row of Loaves

**Day and time.** Day 116, Seed 27, continued: evening into the going of the light.

**Place.** The Vail kiln-house, the loaf shelf in the next room, then the boy's bed under the roof.

**Who is present.** Corin, Sella Vail, one unnamed neighbour at the loaf shelf with a pot.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{116}{Seed 27, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}` at the opening of this first interior scene. Sella gives the bucket, broom, pot and fire their instructions before she gives them to anybody and never the basket; she goes around the space beneath the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, stands a half-pace from every open slate and moves her lips along the loaf row without a figure.
2. A neighbour reaches past the bare board, opens for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, closes on the loaf beyond and calls the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the new palm's breadth the price of a wet thing set down wrong --- still without seeing that the two bare boards are one absence. She goes off with her pot. The boy keeps the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one stiff brown-edged crust with its unnamed empty place, and the neighbour's saying on three places that do not touch; no third gap, no fourth likeness allowed to grow a cause. He does not make her joining his own.
3. Sella glances once at the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and gives nobody a figure. She does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf is not an opening. No opening comes under this roof, so the private roll stays unshown and the clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and called the thaw.
4. At the going of the light she sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof has been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rests a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it goes away.
5. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, stands a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn (the grey streak pale in the dying light), sets the pot where it can be found in the dark and does not look toward the bed.
6. The boy turns out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object where he left it: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, three intervals, the told bell beside the heard bell, the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn and the weight of a thumb apart, four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth, the two tellings of the shut room joined to no kiln, the damper's double bite filed as an object, the held breath from Seed 16 still unspent, the unforged collector sentence beside it and the loan that can hear uncalled. He adds what the day gave --- the place in the returning line and the neighbour's one-breath joining --- on places of their own and lays no finger across either. The carried fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and sixteen days.
7. The door stands a finger's width open. Through the gap comes the river's note of water over water; through the floor comes the kiln's four, four and a gap; the two counts do not meet and the three counts do not become one. Above him the boards keep four nails, four, and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb.

**Emotional turn.** The household's arithmetic absorbs the missing hand without ever entering it; the boy's keeping of separate places is the only exact account in the district and the more exact it is the more it isolates him. Tenderness arrives as the fold put right without a look at the face and the hand that will not let go quite soon enough.

**Sensory anchors.** Woodsmoke, tallow, clay dust, wet wool; the dry brown-edged crust two inches from his ear; the back of the broken pot turning coals under blind ash; the little-door burn closing on nothing; grey streak pale in the dying light; the half-pace; the river's note through the gap and the missing fifth breath through the floor.

## Scene 3 — The Bite with No Word

**Day and time.** Day 117, Seed 28, Eighthday, before first grey through midday.

**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and the back room that stands a finger's width open (not entered), the damper, the Clay Yard.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried and set at the edge of the light); Jorra Vail; Tarn; Sella Vail; unnamed men who come before first grey (not followed, not named); a kiln-master from High Kiln. Alda, Mira, Berr and Ulda offstage.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{117}{Seed 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}`. Before first grey men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and speak low in the room off the wheel-shed. Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold; the boy does not enter it and no name is taken down.
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger --- not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel --- and keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around and entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone --- it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady --- tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
4. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted; the end of her instruction arrives at the pot. The boy reads the iron as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object worth nothing, joined to no kiln.
5. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction. His seventh year is pressed to the very edge of the sill and still held, the small bench knife at the very edge with six years in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone; his chest has been tight since the dark, the breath from Seed 16 still unspent, with a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in. To the kindling the account stays *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and the unforged collector sentence stays where he keeps the seventh year.
6. **The change holds:** Tarn's stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check it made behind Jorra's standing finger on Seed 22, while the stones still keep the place where the stroke stopped; neither man names the other's silence and the loan that can hear is uncalled. The boy sets the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat on the interval tray and lays no cause across them.
7. Jorra does not look toward the bed or into the basket; *he's not Vail* stays sealed and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn. Close on the two silences in one room with no hand joining them.

**Emotional turn.** Obedience arriving before the instruction that calls it is both obedience and a correction nobody will name; the household straightens around a silence. The equality under the roof holds at the place it moved to, and the boy's reading of the damper --- exact, clinical, worth nothing --- is the truest thing in the room and the least spendable.

**Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal ground into the forearms; damp clay and tallow; the wheel's low note; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; broom bristles on flagstones; the small interval at the top of a reach where a breath should go in; the pale clip-ring holding what light there is; Ash worked into the knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, grey dust past the bent finger, broken slates under the rack, sealed sentence, promise ungiven), T062 (damper's double bite filed as an object joined to no kiln), T036/T076 (seventh year held, breath unspent, unchecking stroke held and not repeated, loan uncalled), T061/T082, T071/T072 (117).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T062 toward the later day the danger under the Vail roof is named; T036 toward the near-saying that stops short of calling the loan and the day something must give at the seventh year's end. Nothing paid.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's bent finger standing in the cold damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence while Tarn's bristles go on over the flagstones without the check, the stones still keeping the place where the stroke stopped the day before, and neither man naming the other's silence.

## Scene 5 — A Second Mouth Makes the Cause

**Place.** The yard well and the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried and set near the shelf where no slate lies); Sella Vail; a second unnamed neighbour at the well; the same kind of unnamed neighbour with a pot at the loaf shelf, now a second mouth making the joining. Berr, Jorra, Tarn and the rest offstage.

**Beats, in order.**

Sella glances at the clay jar cold under its glaze and sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. 2. In one breath she calls the old space at the row's end a thing the planting brought in and the empty place at the child's ear a child's appetite. No joining made his own, no fourth likeness allowed to grow a cause, no third gap and no finger laid across the places. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause as surely as silt gathers behind a weir unless a hand holds them apart, and the clean hand at the foot of the cause is the one object he has spent two lives declining to become.

5. Close with the neighbour going off with her pot and the two bare boards still apart in her wake.

**Emotional turn.** A second mouth makes the same joining without seeing it, and the reader feels the cause trying to grow where the boy will not let it; his refusal to be the clean hand is at once his whole dignity and the proof that he has no standing in this house.

**Sensory anchors.** Mud on the well rope; water on the stone lip; chaff under a fingernail; a dry heel of loaf and the palm's breadth of bare board; tallow and wet wool; the half-beat of an absence; a pot carried off unanswered.

**Threads advanced.** T097 (advanced, not paid), T060/T086, T058/T079, T061/T082, T071/T072 (118).

## Scene 6 — Two Records That Do Not Touch

**Day and time.** Day 118, Seed 29, continued: dawn at the Fishstairs, midday three doors down, going of the light under the Vail roof.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried); Mira Silt; Sella Vail; Alda Reed; an unnamed weir woman with salt white at her wrists; an unnamed buyer; an unnamed neighbour scrubbing at a board with lye water in the back room (heard, not looked up at). **Beats, in order.**
1. Mira works the eels at the outer boards with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt dried pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. When the buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted by any thumb but her own and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody will perform. The reading is perfect and buys nothing: no silt-leach, no cause, no cure, no name, only the cold doing what cold does and a man who knows the whole of it and cannot close two inches on a fold.

The boy's surgical reading of the cold foot works perfectly and is the most useless thing in the room.

**Sensory anchors.** Eel slime and river silt; salt dried white at a wrist; lye sour under soap; soap-and-milk gone cold; grey wool with only a hollow in it; the pale fold and thumb-bright rub of a pewter tin; the low song flattened by work; tallow and wet wool under the roof.

## Scene 7 — One Hundred and Nineteen

**Day and time.** Day 119, Seed 30, Tenthday, Account-day and the last market of Seed: morning at the reading block and the worked strips, going of the light under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The Cross and the edge of the worked strips; the Vail kiln-house roof and the loaf shelf in the next room.

**Who is present.** Corin (carried and set in the moot-post's shade where no slate lies); Sella Vail; Berr Clay (short appearance); the two hundred at the Cross; a man bringing a number in a fist; the soft-cuffed clerk at the out-of-line landing; unnamed market voices. Jorra, Tarn, Alda, Mira and Orra offstage or present only as their objects; Ulda offstage; Dessa dead and unnamed; Marra not in Sill.

**Beats, in order.**
1. `\daymark{119}{Seed 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813, Account-day and the last market of Seed}`. The planting month ends and Reed comes on. The hands came out of the strips the day before and one place in the line is still gone around with a look and no figure; T098 carried, not paid, and no body, cause, name or figure for what the month cost.
2. Berr comes up to the block short, with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, hot clay over the smell of nothing at all, the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands are large and clean; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required.
3. He reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the crowd over the worked ground, calls the levy and says *It is not called by me*. To the man who brings a number up out of a fist and asks what the planting cost now that hands have been in it he says *A number goes into a slate, not into a strip; if the house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch* --- *your house*, never *I* --- taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. The figure read aloud is not the figure paid; the true subtraction sits in the locked box up the road; the figure that goes into the slate does not know the hand that did not come back. The boy keeps Berr's twice-wiped thumb and his finger over the figure on a tray of their own and does not join them to any other record-first hand.
4. The bell goes four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the heard bell stays beside the told bell in two places that do not touch. Across the market the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing with his cuff grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells its eels over the top of him as though a man with a slate were a thing a landing always had. The steps come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the whole market walks over carefully without making a fall of it.
5. At the going of the light under the roof the boy turns out the file and finds it unchanged: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, three intervals plus the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat, the told bell beside the heard bell, the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn and a thumb's weight apart, four low things in four places with the river's note a fifth, the two tellings of the shut counting room joined to no kiln, one crust in the fold with no third, the shelf's two gaps in a room that does not touch this one, the clean dark line and the new finer grey unswept and called the thaw, the space under the lowest rack gone around, the held breath from Seed 16 unspent and the loan that can hear uncalled. No seventh hearing came, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand came, no knot was counted and nothing was opened.
6. Sella tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, stands a moment counting under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist, the grey streak pale in the dying light, sets the pot where it can be found in the dark and does not look toward the bed. The carried fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and nineteen days.
7. Close on the count and the forward motion into Reed.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 27

The last image is the same reaching that closed Chapter 25, now one day further on and one month nearer its end: the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the river's note and comes down with the count at one hundred and nineteen and nobody under the roof knowing a number has moved. Chapter 27 opens on Day 120, Reed 1, Firstday, with the boy one hundred and twenty days old, four months, and the planting month behind him. Forward motion is carried in the objects this chapter planted and held, not tied off: the seed is up and the hands are out of the strips, and one place in the returning line is still gone around with a look and no figure (T098 carried, not paid); the loaf shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and the fold one crust and its gap in a room that does not touch them; six hearings stay six beside and never among with no seventh, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word forty unspoken, the loan uncalled, the clerk still set for a measure nobody called, the cold foot still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard. The yard turns toward the reed beds and the Weir while the house keeps its days in loaves, glances and an unnamed space. Nothing was joined, paid, explained or ended here, so Chapter 27 opens on the same held things one day further on and a new month's work spoken of as a direction and not a figure.
