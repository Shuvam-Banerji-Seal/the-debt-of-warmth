Late morning came on over the yard and went down the clay road ahead of them, and \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} to the stalls beyond \place{the Cross}. She set the basket at the outer edge of \place[the market stalls beyond the Cross]{the stalls}' shade, where the boards gave up their shadow and no slate lay, the way a woman sets a pot where no passing hip will knock it. The instruction went to the wool and to the running water and never to the basket: keep it off his ears, it is the river wind; keep it dry, it is running water. She said it twice, the way a woman tells a pot a thing and then tells it again because the pot has not moved. To a woman who asked whose boy it was she said \emph{the house}, and the answer went where answers given to a house go and did not come back to the basket. She stood her half-pace from every open slate between the basket and the pots, and did not look into the fold.

The market had the sour edge of river silt on its air under the smell of hot clay and lead, and under that the sweetish breath of glaze that came down on a bad wind from \place{High Kiln}. Pigs went over the weighing board at the block and came off it with their sides marked in a hand that would not keep the mark; eels lay in their grey slime on the boards below the bridge; a girl with a creel went over the out-of-line landing sideways, caught the rail, and went on selling as though a landing had always had a step's width missing from it. The weigher put his hand to the slate before his eyes went to the pig, and the buyer put her thumb to her purse before her eyes went to the eels, and a man with a slate under his arm set the slate against the turnip heap before he looked at the turnips; three orders of reaching in three parts of one market, and the boy set them on three places and did not lay a finger across them. Voices came up out of the stalls and went down into them again, and he took them in the order he had taken everything in since the stone at his belly went cold: the sound first, the sense of it after, and the thing itself never. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist. The wool had ridden back from the bare joint in the night and the wind under the boards went under the fold at his ear and found the place it had come for. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody put it right, because the instruction had gone to the wool and not to the wandering.

Across the market a woman stood looking at pots. She had come to the row with two other women and had stopped where the shallow bowls sat in their dust, and she turned a cup with the fingers of one hand, not lifting it, only moving it a quarter turn so the light went along the rim and came off it. There was green at one corner of her mouth, the sweetish green of lead glaze, the colour that gathers on a glaze-taster after years of putting the brush's tip to the tongue, and it lay in the crease beside her lip as a small thing lies in the corner of a cup and is not wiped away. The wet of it caught the little light there was and gave it back dull; it had been put there by a thumb that did not know it was putting it there, and nobody would wipe it off before it had become a habit and then a face. She said nothing. Nobody spoke to her and she spoke to nobody, not even to the pots; she turned the cup another quarter, set it back exactly where it had stood, and went on looking at the row. Her nails had clay under them and there was a pale line across the back of one hand where a cord had once been. A pot-seller went past with a tray balanced on her hip and the woman did not look up, and the pot-seller did not look at her, and the two went on in the ordinary way of a market where everybody sees everything and takes nothing in.

The boy watched the green and put it on a place of its own. He had seen \person[Berr Clay]{Berr} with the same sweetish green at the corner of his own mouth, the clean thumb wiped twice before it touched nothing, the figure passed under a finger and left where it lay; and he had seen a green across this square on the last market of Silt, on a woman who stood and did not speak and was gone by dusk. And here was a third green on a third mouth in a third place. Three shapes in three places that did not touch. He called it green and nothing else. He did not call it a trade, or a price, or a name, or an ear; he did not join it to the eel boards below the bridge or to the pot-seller's tray or to the box gone up the clay road. A likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause as surely as silt gathers behind a weir unless a hand holds them apart, and the clean hand at the foot of a cause was the one object he had spent two lives declining to become. He kept his own hand out of it. The green was a colour laid beside two other colours, exact and worth nothing, filed in silence where no Court thumb would ever read it.

A man came up out of the crowd with a number in his fist. It is the district's way of carrying a figure: shut, with the thumb over it, the way a man carries a coal cupped against the wind. He held the fist out toward the pot-seller and opened it a finger's breadth, and shaped the number with his mouth.

“What's it come to, now the hands are out of the strips?” he said, and the question went to the pots as questions went in this yard, and not to any person who might have answered it.

The pot-seller took the number into her mouth and passed it on with a jerk of her chin toward a porter going up the road with a crate on his shoulder. “Ask the road,” she said. The porter took it on the shoulder with the crate and went up toward High Kiln, and the figure stood on the road behind him and arrived nowhere, because a shoulder is not a slate and a road is not an account. The man shut his fist again, put it back into his sleeve, and went off with the number still in it, which was the same as carrying it and the same as not having it, and the two were the same thing in this district.

Word went along the stalls that the Thane had gone up the road since first grey with his box.

“Took the box,” a woman said to the girl at the turnip heap, shifting her pot from one hip to the other.

“He takes it of a market-day.”

“Aye. And leaves the figure here.”

The girl looked at the turnips and did not ask what figure, and the woman went off with her pot, and the saying went into the noise of the stalls and was gone. The boy kept the green and the withheld figure on separate places. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and a figure put into a box and carried up a road is not the figure left standing in a market; the true subtraction sat in the locked box and the number that had gone from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder was the one the district would go on keeping. He knew the shape of it perfectly and could not spend a word of it, and the green on its own place was the only thing the day had added that he had not already seen twice before. The two grew side by side and did not touch, and that was the whole of what he had got for a hundred and seventeen days of keeping them apart.

Sella did not look into the basket at the stall's edge, and the woman across the market did not look at the basket either; the two not-lookings were turned the same way and meant opposite things, one keeping and one pricing, and the boy set them on separate places and wrote no cause across them. His small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the cups and came down. The crust two inches from his ear was stiff and past smelling of anything, and the breast was nowhere, and the number of his days was spoken by nobody who would have known what a number of days counted when they said it. He had once held pressure in a cavity with both hands while a student counted the pulse out loud, and here he could not close two inches on a fold that smelled of wet wool and nothing else. Both facts were true, and the second was the one that was loud. He had stopped expecting to be told the number and had not stopped wanting it.

By the going of the light she had carried him back up the clay road at the half-pace, gone around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and come under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof. She set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}; then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak pale in the dying light off the coals, set the pot where it could be found in the dark, and did not look toward the bed. The little-door burn closed on nothing as the hand came away.

He turned out the private file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found every object where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, from a person; no seventh had come and he had not invented one. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on their own tray, and three intervals on another, and beside the intervals the half-beat of a checked stroke and the stroke that went on without it, two objects and not a rule. The told bell lay beside the heard bell in two places that did not touch. The word forty lay unspoken with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb a little apart from both. Four low things stood in four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth, and the two tellings of the shut counting room lay joined to no kiln, and the double bite of \lore{the damper} lay filed as an object joined to no firing, the bite that holds and the bite that takes in one piece of iron with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both. The held breath from Seed 16 lay where it had lain, unspent, and the collector sentence nobody forged lay beside it, and the loan that could hear was still uncalled. One stiff brown-edged crust lay in the fold with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third; \place{the loaf shelf}'s two gaps stood in the other room and did not touch this one; below the sill a child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool, cold to one woman and weather to the yard, the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. The place in the returning line that the yard went around with a look and no figure stood where it had stood, entered by nobody.

To these he added the green at the corner of the silent woman's mouth, the second sighting of a colour he had already filed twice, and set it on a place of its own beside the other two greens. He laid no finger across them and named nothing. Three places that did not touch; no name for the mouth, no word spoken, no ear, no price, no joining to the eel trade or to the road or to the box. The file lay complete and exact, and there was nobody under the roof who could have read a word of it. His exactness was the only arithmetic in the district and the more exact it grew the more it isolated him, and he went on keeping it because there was nothing else to keep. The carried fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and seventeen days. Every warmth in this room had a payer somewhere, and no slate in the district carried the name.

Across the market the green went out of the light as the stalls gave up their shade, the woman turning the cup a last quarter and setting it back where it had stood and going off with the other women, and nobody naming the colour or the mouth or the woman. The boy filed it where he had put it and went back under the wool. His small hand outside the fold went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. One hundred and seventeen stood where one hundred and sixteen had stood in the small hours, exact, unspent and spoken by nobody in the district, while the river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor and the two counts in two places did not meet. Nothing was joined, named, paid or ended; the place in the returning line went on being gone around, and the green went on being green.
