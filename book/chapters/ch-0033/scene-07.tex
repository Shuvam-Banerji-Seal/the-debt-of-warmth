The river road gave onto the clay road without a seam, the way one worn thing gives into another, and the half-pace did not change. Woodsmoke went before \person{Sella Vail} and the river wind came up to meet it, and the two met somewhere about her shoulders and went on together, each keeping its own shape. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket --- keep it off his ears, it had come all the way down with them and would go all the way back up --- and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with his cheek to the side of it and the small wet sound coming and going as his head wandered and was not set right. The light went off the reeds behind them and came on the clay ahead, and the clay took it up red and gave nothing back. \place{Low Kiln} stood with its backs to the road, and the road went up between the houses the way a scar goes through a hand, known by feel and not looked at. A carrying arm is a record in muscle: it can say how often it lifted and never what the lifting cost. He had learned that before he could hold up his own head, and he did not need to learn it again.

At the bend she went around a pan set out to dry, tilting her shoulder to pass it, and around the trough by the well where the skin of ice had stood since morning and was called weather. She did not break the ice and did not lift the pan; a thing set down is a thing set down, and a woman carrying a basket has no second hand for either. Farther up she went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack}, as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. A hand had gone in there on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest on Seed 27, and the yard had gone around the place every morning since with a look and a wide step and no number, no cause, no body and no name. The broken birth-slates lay under the rack where they lay and were entered by nobody. \lore{Ash} had turned the corner at the house and left a clean dark line behind it, and beside it ran the new finer hairline the house called \lore{the thaw}, and neither line was swept. She carried past at the half-pace, gave the house no name for the space, and turned no head toward it. The boy watched the ground go by under the basket's edge and noted the order of the going-around without deciding to: the shoulder went first, the eyes came after, and the place got no more than the wide step it had been given for a year.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she lifted him out of the basket without looking into it and set him on \place[the bed]{his bed}, and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, setting him down as a woman sets a pot down where it will not be slopped --- rim level, weight settled, the whole of the thing arrived before the hand lets go. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, and the thread of drool at his mouth froze where it lay and was not wiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away. He had learned that half-beat as other men learn a hand's pressure through a dressing: it told what was carried and not what it cost, it was exact, complete, and worth nothing to anyone who wanted a figure. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf and glances at a jar cold under its glaze, and it had no use for his number.

She went to the fire and told it it was the thaw. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the pot's edge took the ash off and left the red underneath, and she fed them and said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals. She set the pot where it could be found in the dark, rim out and handle toward the reaching hand --- the handle standing two inches beyond the hand that would have reached for it and would not close on it --- and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and she did not touch it, because a dusk is not an opening, and neither is a basket, a bed, a rack, a road or a fire gone down under blind ash. She went to her own bed and left the house to keep what it kept.

In the small hours he turned out his file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins: handle out, edge in, every piece in the place its own wear had made for it, and nothing laid across anything else. That last was the whole of the work. A file in which one object touches another is an account, and an account wants a payer, and no payer had been named to any of these.

There were six hearings of \lore{the full note}, and they lay beside one another and never among: a cup across a birth room, a pot under \place[the Vail wheel-shed]{the wheel-shed} wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}, and the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her. Five came from four sources and the sixth came from a person, and the sixth stayed where it was put, beside and not among. No seventh had come in three days, and he had not made one to fill the place. There were three near-full answers, advanced and not paid, each on its own place and none delivered: a cup across the birth room, a pot under the shed wall, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set again. There were the older intervals: the half-beat held behind a standing finger, the stroke that had gone on over \place{the flagstones} without the check while the stones kept the bright patch where it had stopped, his own breath drawn at the top of a reach and not spent. They lay as objects do, each complete, each exact, each worth nothing to a thumb looking for a figure.

Then the six stoppings of the near-saying, laid out as the first five had been laid and the sixth laid the day before: Reed 5, Reed 8, Reed 11, Reed 14, Reed 17 and Reed 20. Each had come all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. Each had cost a breath that went in where it should not have gone and was not let out where it should have been, and a man had gone on with his broom afterward, which is the shape his help takes when he is frightened. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house could check and the seventh where it could not; the unforged collector sentence lay beside it, and neither was entered. They were objects, six of them, and not a rule. He held them apart by hand because he had learned what six likenesses will do if a man stops holding them: they stand up together and call themselves a doctrine, and a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. No payer had been named to any of the six. The seventh year pressed nearer its end and had not given; the loan stood uncalled. Six intervals are six things that happened, and nothing in the counting made a seventh.

Beside them lay \lore{the damper}'s double bite, filed as one piece of iron joined to no kiln: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood together with no third, and the heard bell stood beside the told bell in two places that neither agreed nor disagreed. None of them touched. He did not lay the iron across the six stoppings, or the bell across the two tellings, or any of them across the cold foot below the sill, which was the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to a neighbour with a pail, read exactly and worth nothing. A likeness is not a cause, and a colour is not a face, and an absence is not an absence of the same thing; he had bought those three sentences with three errors and would not spend them now.

In the other room \place{the loaf shelf} kept its two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- and the charcoal marks under the board stayed on a place of their own, unread. She had reached a finger beneath the front edge again that day without looking and without a word, the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again, and brought it away, and the marks had not been counted and the finger had not been explained. Such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason stood between the two, where it had stood for a hundred and forty days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the two rooms did not touch. He did not lay the charcoal under the shelf across the two gaps above, or either of them across the one crust in the fold, because a hand that keeps its own record by hiding it is not an invitation to another hand, and three absences in three places are three absences until somebody joins them, and nobody had.

The word forty stayed unspoken with \person{Orra Weir}'s readable double turn beside it, and the boy kept the shape without the name as he had kept it since the year of the flower. The fifth green that had come to a silent woman's mouth across the square was filed as a colour and not a name, joined to no ear and to no pale line on the back of a hand. The reed-cutter's cord lay back inside the coat with its knots unread; \person{Mira Silt}'s second twist lay twice beneath her apron, never carried upriver to a door; Orra's knot-string kept its last double turn, tail tucked, tied left over right with the small weight of a thumb pressed in. Three doors down in \place{House Silt}, \person{Alda Reed}'s six pages still lay turned toward a floorboard and both tins stood shut, and Dessa's pewter tin above the abandoned bed kept its pale fold and thumb-bright lid closed. Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk was still set for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his hand to his slate before his eyes to the stalls. Every one of them was where it had been put, every one visible and none enterable, and every one worth nothing to a man who could only look.

Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the six hearings and never would be, because it was water and not a vessel. Through the floor under blind ash the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty days now and not one day less. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them became one. He had long ago stopped trying to make them. A surgeon who reads three counts in one body knows better than to add them; the pulse and the breath and the output are three accounts kept by three instruments, and the addition of them is a figure that cures nobody. Here there was not even a body to add, only a house keeping its warmth in the dark and a river keeping its note under the door, and the keeping of them apart was the only honest work in the room and the only work no slate would enter.

Cold arrived in its known order: cheek, temple, wrist, the wool riding back from the small bare joint where the hand could not hold it. He had learned the order in this body and in another, and the two knowings lay side by side and did not join. One hundred and forty days old, four months and twenty days, and told by nobody; if somebody had said the number aloud under this roof, nobody would have known what it counted, and the house would have gone on keeping its days in loaves and glances and the space at the row's end nobody called a figure. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear and could not reach it. He wanted the number spoken by somebody who would know what a number was, and there was nobody in the room who could speak it and nobody under the roof who would know. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. In the next room the charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand not his and a day that had not come, and the count went on.
