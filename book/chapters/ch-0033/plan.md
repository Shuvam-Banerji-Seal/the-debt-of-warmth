# Chapter 33 — beat sheet: *Six at the Teeth*

**Days covered.** Days 138–140, Reed 19–21, Y.H. 813, Ninthday to Firstday, with Reed 20 the second Account-day of Reed. A **normal** chapter of three lived days, each with its own `\daymark` and each lived in full, at least eight hundred words to the day. Day 137 is finished and not revisited; Day 141 belongs to Chapter 34. The boy is one hundred and thirty-eight, then thirty-nine, then forty days old — four months and eighteen, nineteen and twenty days — and nobody under the roof says so. The reed month stands in its last tend; at the chapter's close the second Account-day (Reed 20) has stood and the third (Reed 30, Day 149) is nine days off.

**Arc.** Arc 1, *The Cold Stone* — *a warm stone, already going cold*.

**Chapter's one meaningful change.** Under the Vail roof Tarn brings the sentence about an ear worth more than the debt that holds it **a sixth time** all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there, with no figure and no name, drawing the breath he does not spend. The boy files it on the interval tray beside the first five as a **sixth interval and not a sixth payment**, an object among objects and not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh, refusing to let six likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine — for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer. The loan stays uncalled; the seventh year presses nearer its end and has still not given. A second gathering, not a resolution: on the second Account-day a **fifth green** comes to a silent woman's mouth across the square and is filed as a colour, not a name, beside the four earlier greens and Berr's own glaze — five at mouths and six shapes of the colour, still joined to no ear and never Fenna in the narration.

**What is withheld everywhere.** No calling of the loan, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, no knot counted, no word forty spoken, no name or number from Dessa, no sale, no opening of tin or box, no body or cause for the missing hand, no reading of either private tally, no joining of the two empty places, no fourth likeness made into a cause, no sixth green, no silt-leach or cure, no Fenna named in narration. T097 stays advanced and unpaid; T098, T099 and T100 stay carried and unpaid. The old life stays offstage: no present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no departure from the boy's eyes, no Court, collector, Waiting Room, back-room interior, Ulda on stage, Marra in Sill, watcher on stage, present-tense break or ending.

## Scene 1 — *The Last Bundles*

- **Day and time.** Day 138, Reed 19, Ninthday, Y.H. 813, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Clay Yard and the reed beds below Low Kiln; the edge of the worked strips east of the yard.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; reed-cutters going downriver and coming back; a girl with a creel; a woman at the racks; yard voices. Walk-ons only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Open on the morning of Day 138 with the reed month in its last tend and no boundary drawn at its entering; the boy is one hundred and thirty-eight days old and four months and eighteen days and nobody under any roof says so. Sella carries him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket — *Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.*
  2. The last bundles come downriver and go back at dusk-shaped armfuls judged by the arm that carries them and by nothing set on a slate. A cutter tells the girl with a creel to lift her bundle and let her arm tell her what went into it, *and that's the whole of what it knows*; to a second question he says it doesn't know cost and asks how heavy it is (T071/T072, T077).
  3. A woman working the racks asks what the cutters' hands will be paid now the reed is in its last tend; she is answered with the weather and the river, never a figure, and shuts two fingers out of a fist as she goes (T077, T058/T079).
  4. The yard goes around the clean dark line where the Ash ridge turned, the new finer hairline of grey called the thaw, the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out — a look, a wide step, no number (T009/T013/T092, T098 carried, not paid).
  5. The reed-cutter's grey cord goes past along the boards with the knots unread, straightened and turned where no slate lies; it is not laid beside the charcoal under the shelf, the empty place in the line, Mira's cords or Orra's knot-string (T099 carried, not paid).
  6. The boy reads the carrying arm as a record in muscle that tells what was carried and not what it cost: exact, complete and worth nothing. The surgeon's register notes the order of reaching without giving him standing to enter it anywhere (T061/T082).
  7. His small hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down against the fold; he cannot reach the bundle, the pot or any of the places gone around. The count goes on without anybody's thumb upon it.
- **Emotional turn.** From the ordinary last work of the reed month to the exactness of a record that cannot be spent: competence here is helplessness, and the day's arithmetic stays in arms and weather where no Court thumb will read it.
- **Sensory anchors.** Wet wool and woodsmoke going before Sella; grey reed ends pale where frost bit the tips; dry mud worked into wrists; river wind at the ear; the small wet sound of the wandering head; a trough by the well with a skin of ice that was not there at morning.
- **Threads advanced.** T071/T072 (138 kept exact and unspent), T077, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T098 (carried), T099 (carried), T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T098 gathers interest with another going-around, no name, body, cause or figure; T099 goes past unread a second day running; T077's cost asked as a direction is answered with a direction and leaves no entry. Nothing paid.
- **Exact last image.** The woman at the racks goes back to her strips with two fingers shut out of a fist; the reeds stand grey in the ruts uncounted; the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down.

## Scene 2 — *The Finger Under the Board*

- **Day and time.** Day 138 continued, midday into the afternoon.
- **Place.** Under the Vail kiln-house roof, at the loaf shelf and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, the boy in the basket; a neighbour come up from the strips with a pot. Walk-on only.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella sets the basket low and a little back near the bench leg, at the angle at which a man lying on the floor sees the underside of a board a man standing sees only the top of; she gives the instruction to the wool, the pot, the broom and the fire and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  2. Above, the shelf keeps its row the way a house keeps its days badly: loaves pushed along, a glance at the clay jar cold under its glaze, and two unnamed gaps — the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word — in a room the fold does not touch (T060/T086, T097 carried and unpaid).
  3. Standing with her back to the basket and moving her lips along the row, she glances once at the clay jar and gives nobody a figure.
  4. Still without looking down at the board and without a word, she reaches a finger BENEATH the front edge where the charcoal tally lies — the little-door burn closing on the wood's lip and opening again, the finger going under far enough for the knuckle to set and the thumb to come up against the grain — holds it a breath out and back and brings it away, and does not go back to the shelf (T100 carried, not paid, unread).
  5. From the low angle the boy sees the hand go under and come back and sees nothing of what the marks say. He does not count the strokes, press a thumb where her thumb had been, lay the underside beside the two gaps above or beside the one stiff crust in the fold in the other room, or make a cause of the habit — such a hand either knows the board by heart or has been told not to look, and the reason is left where it stands, between the two (T100, T097).
  6. A neighbour come up from the strips with a pot asks what the row will want before the tend is out; Sella sends her to the pot with *Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it*, and the woman carries the unanswered question away level, nothing slopped (T077, T058/T079).
  7. The widow's mark hangs where it hangs and is not touched, because a shelf and an afternoon are not openings; the clean dark line and the new finer grey lie unswept and are called the thaw (T009/T013/T092, T079).
- **Emotional turn.** A second private record is handled in front of him and stays unread — care that hides itself tells more by the hiding than the writing would — and the boy refuses to be the hand that joins it to anything.
- **Sensory anchors.** The dry warm smell of the shelf crossed for one breath by the sharper smell of charcoal from under the board; the cold iron of the bench bracket at his cheek; the grey streak at her temple showing pale off the coals; the little-door burn closing on nothing; the clay jar cold under its glaze.
- **Threads advanced.** T100 (carried), T060/T086, T097, T058/T079, T077, T009/T013/T092, T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T100 gathers interest toward the far-off convergence of the district's private records; the finger-under-board habit stays unexplained, held for a hand not his. Nothing read, counted, copied, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The shelf keeps its two gaps above and its charcoal marks below, the finger has come away without a word, and the two rooms of the house do not touch.

## Scene 3 — *One in Twelve, As the Notice Says*

- **Day and time.** Day 139, Reed 20, Tenthday, the second Account-day of Reed, Y.H. 813, market morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The reading block under the moot-post at the Cross, the market stalls, and the out-of-line landing below the north bridge.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; Berr Clay; the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing; a silent woman with green at her mouth; the Account-day crowd and stall-keepers. Walk-ons except Berr and Sella; the green-mouthed woman is unnamed, silent and given no dialogue.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The crowd leans over the reading block the way a pot leans with its lid not quite on. Berr comes up short with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing (T015/T041/T047/T080).
  2. He holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and lets his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, then reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the goers-by and calls the levy, denying it with *your house*, never *I* (T015/T041/T047/T077).
  3. The figure goes into the air and not into any house; the true subtraction stays in the locked box up the clay road. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and the boy sets the two on places that do not touch (T041/T077).
  4. Across the square a **fifth** woman's mouth shows the green — clay under her nails, a pale line across the back of one hand, a cup turned a quarter, not lifted, so the light goes along the rim and comes off it, then set back exactly where it stood. She is unnamed, silent and given no dialogue; the boy files the fifth at a mouth and the sixth shape of the colour counting Berr's own glaze beside the Silt 30, Seed 28 and Reed 7 greens, a colour and not a name, never Fenna in the narration, joined to no ear, no cord-mark, no pots and no box up the clay road (T050/T051, advanced, not paid).
  5. The bell goes four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The heard bell stands beside the told bell of the Long Night in two places that neither agree nor disagree, and it is not added to the hearings of the full note (T078, T061/T082).
  6. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, slate to his forearm with the writing turned inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells over the top of him and nobody takes him off (T090/T080/T083).
  7. Sella stands a half-pace from every open slate, sets the basket in the moot-post's shade where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket; she does not turn toward the reading and does not repeat the figure (T058/T079).
- **Emotional turn.** The account is read aloud and the truth of it stays locked where the reader cannot be seen touching it; a colour shows itself five times without once standing up and saying a name. What the square gives is a performance of arithmetic, and the boy can read the performance and not enter it.
- **Sensory anchors.** Hot clay over the smell of nothing at all; green glaze at a mouth corner and in the crease beside another woman's lip; a slate held flat to a forearm; the pale line across the back of a hand where something was once worn tight and long; the bell's four, four and a gap; wet wool and river silt at the landing.
- **Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077, T050/T051 (fifth green, advanced not paid), T078, T090/T080/T083, T058/T079, T071/T072 (139 kept exact), T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T050/T051 gathers a fifth green toward the far day an ear will be spoken with that colour; the woman stays unnamed, silent and never Fenna in the narration. T041 keeps the true subtraction in the box. T078 keeps the heard bell beside the told. T090 holds a Court presence set ahead of something. Nothing named, paid, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** Below the bridge the clerk's hand goes to his slate before his eyes go to the stalls, and above him the market sells its reeds over the stone where he sits; across the square the green goes off with the light, still a colour.

## Scene 4 — *The Sixth Stopping*

- **Day and time.** Day 139 continued, afternoon at the market stalls and below the sill, then the going of the light into the small hours at the hearth and kindling under the Vail roof.
- **Place.** The market stalls at the Cross; the fish-trade shade below the north bridge; the hearth and kindling stack under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Present.** Sella Vail, Tarn, Jorra Vail, the boy in the basket; Berr Clay at the stalls; a stall-keeper, a woman winding wool about a child's foot, market goers. Walk-ons except the three household figures and Berr.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Berr buys two pair of eels and pays two marks short, and the stall-keeper puts the marks in her palm without counting them where anyone can hear; he says *A number goes into a slate, not a bundle*, takes himself out of his own sentence with *your house*, never *I*, and goes off up the clay road with the cord folded square under his coat and the box locked, believing he has done the district a kindness (T015/T041/T047).
  2. Below the sill a child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to a neighbour with a pail. The surgeon's reading — the extremity going before the core, the almost-smile that is not peace, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody will perform — works perfectly and buys nothing; no silt-leach, cause, cure or recognition is written across it (T093/T094/T096).
  3. Under the roof, with the coals banked and the small knife set back where it could be checked, Tarn tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and sets the knife at the very edge of its sill with six counted where the house can check and the seventh where it cannot, the unforged collector sentence beside it and the breath from Seed 16 still unspent with its small interval at the top of each reach (T036/T076).
  4. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that holds it comes **a sixth time** — after Reed 5, 8, 11, 14 and 17 — all the way to the word that would call the loan and stops there with no figure and no name. He draws the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and does not spend it, says *Yes, house* to the fire and goes on with the broom (T036/T076).
  5. Sella, within hearing, gives the end of her instruction to the pot and asks nothing; Jorra, within hearing, does not ask about the ear and Tarn does not ask about the room, and the two silences stay unjoined (T058/T079, T035/T031).
  6. The chapter's one meaningful change: the boy files the sixth stopping on the interval tray beside the first five as a **sixth interval and not a sixth payment** — an object among objects, not a rule, not a hearing and not a seventh. He refuses to let six likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer; no payer has been named to any of the six (T036/T076, T061/T082).
  7. The carried fire keeps its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and thirty-nine days (T088). The loan stays uncalled and the seventh year has still not given.
- **Emotional turn.** A thing stopped six times is still a held thing, not a settled one: the chapter's change is a further interval, bought by a breath a man does not spend and recorded by a child who cannot spend it either. Six likenesses must be held apart by hand or they will stand up by morning as an account with a payer.
- **Sensory anchors.** Banked coals under blind ash; tallow and wet wool; the small knife's worn thumb-mark at the edge of the sill; the pale clip-ring at Tarn's left wrist holding what light there is; the small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in; three turns of wool around a dark foot.
- **Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (sixth stopping withheld, loan uncalled, seventh year held), T061/T082 (sixth interval as object not payment), T015/T041/T047, T093/T094/T096, T088, T058/T079.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T036/T076 advances the loan one interval further; something must give at the end of the seventh year and has not given here. T093/T094/T096 carry the cold foot uncured toward Arc 3. T041 keeps the true subtraction in the box and a kindness believed in. Nothing paid, named, opened, called, cured or ended.
- **Exact last image.** The sixth stopping lies on the tray among five others and none of the six has a payer; the fire gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and Tarn's stroke goes on toward the road.

## Scene 5 — *The Dust Before the Sentence*

- **Day and time.** Day 140, Reed 21, Firstday, Y.H. 813, before first grey through midday.
- **Place.** The Vail wheel-shed, its finger's-width-open back room (not entered), the cold damper, the Clay Yard and the flagstones.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; Jorra Vail; Tarn; three or four unnamed men who speak low in the back room; a High Kiln kiln-master. Walk-ons except Jorra, Tarn and Sella.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Before first grey three or four men come up the Clay Yard with their collars high and go low-voiced into the room off the shed, whose door keeps its finger's width of open; Sella carries the boy past at the half-pace, turns no head toward the door, gives the house no name for the men and does not say the room was cold (T035/T031).
  2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates under the lowest rack are entered by nobody (T035/T031, T009/T013/T092).
  3. He speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows its full note before it leaves the wheel and withholds the knowing from a High Kiln kiln-master who says it is singing before its time and that a pot that sings early sings itself out early (T062).
  4. Before Sella has finished telling him the fire is low his bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper, moves the tongue a hair's breadth and leaves it standing; the household straightens around his silence and the end of her instruction arrives at the pot (T062).
  5. The boy reads the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies, and files it as an object joined to no kiln, worth nothing. The two tellings of the shut counting room stand beside it with no third (T062, T065/T095 carried).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after it has been instructed and wants the late thaw because it puts off the paying; his seventh year presses nearer its end and is still held, the knife at the very edge of its sill, the unforged collector sentence beside it, the breath from Seed 16 unspent (T036/T076).
  7. His stroke goes on over the flagstones without the half-check while the stones keep the shallow bright patch where it stopped; neither man names the other's silence. The boy files the unchecking stroke beside the earlier half-beat with no cause across them (T036/T076, T061/T082).
- **Emotional turn.** The household's equality holds at the place it moved to by fractions: an iron that is both mercy and mutilation, a dust that names nothing, a year the debt cannot bring himself to count, and two silences that are not joined.
- **Sensory anchors.** Fine grey slate dust like meal worked past the bent finger and under the nails; bone-coloured slip on clean forearms; cold iron of the damper taking the cold out of a fingertip; wet clay and tallow and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on the kiln-master's coat; Ash worked into Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line; the shallow bright patch on the flagstones.
- **Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed, dust, broken birth-slates unentered), T062 (damper's double bite filed as an object), T065/T095 (two tellings carried, no third), T036/T076 (seventh year pressed nearer its end, breath unspent, unchecking stroke), T009/T013/T092, T058/T079, T061/T082, T071/T072 (140 kept exact).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T035 points at the Broken Slates without a name given; T062 keeps the double bite for the far-off day it is understood rather than merely read; T065/T095 carry the two shut-room tellings with no third telling owed; T036's year is pressed toward its end without giving. Nothing paid, named, entered, called or joined.
- **Exact last image.** Tarn's stroke goes on over the shallow bright patch on the flagstones without the half-check, the stones keeping the place where it stopped, and the door off the shed standing a finger's width open.

## Scene 6 — *Twice Beneath the Grey*

- **Day and time.** Day 140 continued, morning going into midday.
- **Place.** The Weir, the plank walk on posts, the flat blessing stone and the lower boards; below the sill at the fish-trade shade.
- **Present.** Sella Vail carrying the boy; the reed-cutter; Orra Weir; Mira Silt; a weir woman who cannot read a cord, a woman who asks about the levy, a buyer, a woman winding wool about a child's foot and a neighbour with a pail. Walk-ons except Sella, Orra and Mira; the reed-cutter stays unnamed.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. The reed-cutter comes along the boards with a bundle and the short length of grey wool or reed-fibre cord gone grey with the oil of a working thumb; he sets the bundle on the flat stone, straightens the cord, turns it once so the knots stand in the small light, ties one low with his teeth and a pull of the thumb, bites the end off and puts it back inside his coat, drawing the coat closed over it with the flat of his hand (T099).
  2. To the weir woman who asks what the cord holds he says *You can ask the cord*; to *I can't read a cord*, *No more can the Court*. He goes to lift a trap with the question going down into the water. No figure, knot, name or joining; the cord is not laid beside the charcoal under the shelf, the empty place in the returning line, Mira's cords or Orra's knot-string (T099 carried, not paid).
  3. Orra stands at the lower boards with her board on its side and her hands like old rope, pale eel scars uncovered across both forearms, and pulls one knot down with her thumb. To the woman who asks what the levy will take she gives a date — *The ninth of Ilmen. The collector's boat came round the bend on the flood and had six oars. One man stood at the stern and never touched them* — and to *That isn't the levy*, *It's a date. The river takes what the Court doesn't. You can shout at either one. I've tried.* The forty-first double turn stays legible with its name unspoken (T024).
  4. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried in long grey strokes on her sleeves and silt pale in the hollow of her neck, her head held a little right where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch. Asked whether the run will be thin now the hands are in the reeds she answers her own answer — *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river* — and flicks eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie, putting one away (T059/T025).
  5. When a buyer names a figure her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays longer than the figure required; shifting a creel shows the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's and does not touch it (T059/T025/T024).
  6. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to a neighbour with a pail. The boy reads it exactly and it buys nothing, and he does not lay it beside the green that went off with the light across the square or any other absence (T093/T094/T096).
  7. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the work where no slate lies, gives the instruction to the wool and the running water and never to the basket, and carries the boy home at the going of the light (T058/T079).
- **Emotional turn.** Three private records stand within a dozen paces of the basket — a cord in a coat, a knot-string on a board, a second twist beneath an apron — and every one is visible and none is enterable; the boy keeps them side by side and not across.
- **Sensory anchors.** Eel slime dried in grey strokes; silt pale in the hollow of a neck; the grey cord oiled by a working thumb; the small flat knock of a knot pulled down; river silt and cold fish and tallow smoke; three turns of wool around a foot that stays dark.
- **Threads advanced.** T099, T024/T059/T025, T093/T094/T096, T058/T079, T071/T072, T061/T082.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T099, T024 and T025 gather with the charcoal under the shelf, Alda's six pages and both tins and Dessa's pewter tin toward the far day a hand not his lays the district's private records together; all visible, none entered. T096 carries the cold foot toward Arc 3. Nothing counted, read, named, cured, opened or joined.
- **Exact last image.** The buyer goes off with the eels, Mira's thumb comes out from under her apron, and the reed-cutter's cord is back inside his coat with its knots unread while the child's foot stays dark in three turns of wool.

## Scene 7 — *One Hundred and Forty*

- **Day and time.** Day 140 continued, the going of the light into the small hours.
- **Place.** The clay road up from Low Kiln; under the Vail kiln-house roof at the loaf shelf, the bed and the hearth.
- **Present.** Sella Vail and the boy only at the last; the boy alone at the final line.
- **Beats, in order.**
  1. Sella carries him up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her and the river wind coming up to meet it, and gives the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket (T058/T079).
  2. She goes around a pan set out to dry and the trough by the well with its skin of ice, and goes wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lie as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it (T009/T013/T092).
  3. Under the roof she sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, lifting him out of the basket without looking into it and setting him down as one sets a pot down where it will not be slopped; she puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, leaves the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and lets her hand rest a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it goes away (T058/T079).
  4. She tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot, says *The house will keep it* where another woman would say *I*, counts under her breath toward no opening, pushes a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, sets the pot rim out with its handle toward the reaching hand — the handle standing two inches beyond the hand that would have reached for it and would not close on it — and does not look toward the bed or touch the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening (T058/T079, T079).
  5. In the small hours he turns out his file as a man lays instruments on a towel and finds every object in its place and nothing laid across anything else: six hearings of the full note beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; the older intervals with the half-beat behind the standing finger, the stroke that went on without the check and his own held breath; **six stoppings** of the near-saying as objects and not a rule, an interval and not a payment, no payer named to any; the damper's double bite filed joined to no kiln; the two shut-room tellings with no third; the told bell beside the heard bell (T061/T082, T036/T076, T062, T065/T095, T078).

## Pull into Chapter 34

The sixth stopping lies on the tray with five others and none has a payer, and the reed month has entered its last week with the loan uncalled and the seventh year pressed nearer its end than it has ever stood. Chapter 34 opens on Day 141, Reed 22, Secondday, with the boy one hundred and forty-one days old and nobody saying so: the yard still going around its two lines and the place where a hand went in and did not come out, both private tallies still unread, a fifth green filed as a colour and not a name, and a seventh stopping still owed at the threshold of the word that would call the loan. Nothing is joined, paid, named, opened, called or ended; the count goes on unspent, forward, not closed.
