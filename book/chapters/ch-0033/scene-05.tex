\daymark{140}{Reed 21, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the men came up \place{the Clay Yard}. There were three or four of them, and the boy could not have said which, because a man with his collar held high gives the world less of himself to count than a man who goes with his throat bare; and these went with their collars high. They came up out of the dark toward \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the wet of the yard on their boots, and went low-voiced into \place[the back room]{the room off the shed}, whose door kept its finger's width of open as a door keeps a habit and not an invitation. No name went with them. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} past at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke thin above the basket and the river wind coming up to meet it, and turned no head toward the gap of light under the door. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool, as she told it going up and would tell it coming down, and gave the house no name for the men, and did not say that the room was cold.

The boy saw the width of the opening and the boots and the collars and the way the voices dropped as a body drops over a sill. He had learned to read the order in which a hand goes to a record before it goes to a person, and here there was no hand at all, only the low voices going in and the door keeping its finger's width, which is a record of a kind: it tells that a door was left so, and tells nothing of who set it or why, and is exact and worth nothing. He did not enter the room. He had no standing to enter it and no hand that could have pushed an inch past the doorway, and the surgeon's register in him noted the width and the hour and went no further, the way it noted the tiny giving of a pupil before a man speaks and kept the note for a day that might never come. The room stayed on its own place. The two tellings of the shut counting room stood beside that place and did not grow a third out of this morning, though men had gone into a room and the door stood a finger's width open; he would not lay one room across the other and call the two one cause.

By the time the light came properly onto the boards \person{Jorra Vail} had come through from the shed. Fine grey slate dust was ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, so fine that it lay in the creases like a second skin and not like the bone-coloured slip of the wheel that dried pale on other mornings and could be brushed off at the bench. He kept the two states of himself on one tray without setting one across the other: dusty forearms and clean ones, this morning and another morning, both his and neither asking about the other. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack in \place{the drying racks}, where the yard went around the space as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and nobody entered them, and Jorra did not look toward them as he passed. The boy wanted the answer to both questions with a hunger that had no Grathic word and no hand to put it into, and kept the hunger where questions last for years. The dust was a record that named nothing, ground finer than the clean dark line in the corner and finer than the new hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}; it told what room a man had stood in and told nothing of what he had done there, and that was the whole of what it knew.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.”

He said it to the wheel before he said anything to a person, in the flat voice that went to the wood first and only afterward to the air. A pot turned under his hands with the slip going smooth and the shoulder coming up, and he looked at it a moment as a man looks at a thing he has already judged, and said, “You'll do.” No welcome in it and no unkindness. He knew the full note that was in that pot before it left the wheel, knew it three turns before the clay would be set down and give it to the room, the way a man knows the note of a vessel under his knuckle before the knock has sounded. He kept the knowing where he kept other things he would not say. He would not say it before a kiln-master.

A kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} stood at the shed door with \lore{Ash} grey on his cuffs and the sweetish edge of lead glaze on his coat, brought up from the terrace on a bad wind. “That one's singing before its time,” the man said, nodding at the pot. “A pot that sings early sings itself out early. You want it slow.”

Jorra did not look at him. “You'll do,” he said again to the pot, and went on turning.

The boy set the man's saying and Jorra's silence on two places that did not touch. The pot's note had not yet gone into the air; it was shut in the clay where Jorra knew it and the kiln-master guessed at it, and a knowing held back from a man who would make something of it is an answer advanced and not paid, filed where the near-full answers lay and not among the hearings of \lore{the full note}. He did not let it stand up as a sixth hearing. Six were six and stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among; the wheel and the pots under the roof were notes left where they sounded and were not added to that row. Nothing had been given to the air here that anyone could hear and enter. The kiln-master went off up the yard with his cuffs grey and his certainties about him, having been told nothing, and the pot stayed on the wheel with its note still shut inside it.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low.

“Jorra, the fire's—”

His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of his hand, ahead of the last of her sentence, the little finger leading as it had led for three years and would lead again. The iron took the cold out of the fingertip and gave a little back in reverse. He moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing. She shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the end of it to the pot, and the household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, Sella with her hands on the basket, the boy with his head going where it wanted in the wool, the wheel going on turning because a wheel does not straighten and does not care. Nobody said the room was cold. Nobody said the men had names. The end of the instruction arrived at the pot, which is where instructions go in this house when the person they were meant for has answered with a finger in iron.

The boy read the iron again as he had read it on other mornings and would read it on mornings still to come. It was one piece of iron with two bites. The bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man are the same bite, set the same way, turned by the same bent finger; there is no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, because the house calls it a holding and never a taking, and a man who has lost a finger to it calls it something else in a voice nobody in this yard has heard. He filed it as an object joined to no kiln and worth nothing anyone here would pay. The two tellings of the room shut in the war with the damper closed and the men inside dead of the breath stood beside it, one from a bucket at the well and one from a man come downriver, each a dated curiosity eighteen leagues east, differing only in breath through the nose against breath made in the throat; they stood beside the iron and kept their places, and no third telling came this morning, though three or four men had gone low into a room whose door kept its finger's width of open. He did not write the joining. A room in this yard and a room shut in a war eighteen leagues east are two rooms until a hand not his lays them together, and his hand could not close two inches on the rim of the basket.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. Sella gave it to the broom first — go around what the yard left, go around the clean dark line and the new finer grey beside it, go wide around the rack space — and only then let the broom go to him, and he took it up as a man takes up a tool already named, with a “Yes, house” that had a nail in it.

“Early, they want it,” he said to the flagstones, of those who wanted the thaw early. “Late, they want it.” He agreed with both so entirely that the argument had nowhere to put its foot, and went on wanting the late one himself, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

The boy knew where the knife was without looking. It lay on its sill at the very edge, with six years standing in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The unforged collector sentence lay beside it where Tarn kept it, and the breath from Seed 16 was still unspent in his chest, a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in and did not. The seventh year pressed nearer its end on this morning than it had ever stood and had still not given. The boy could not call what he was owed, could not spend the interval, could not say the word that would call the loan even if a Grathic word for it had been put into his mouth, and the word was not said under the roof. Something must give at the end of a seventh year and had not given here.

His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones}. It went on past the shallow bright patch the stones kept where the stroke had stopped on the first fraction of change, and it did not check this morning, not for the standing finger in the damper, not for the low voices going into the room, not for anything. The stones kept the place where it had stopped and went on keeping it, a place nobody entered and nobody named, and Tarn did not turn his head along the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and Jorra did not ask about the ear while Tarn did not ask about the room. The two silences lay side by side under the one roof and were not joined, and the equality held at the place it had moved to by fractions.

The boy took the unchecking stroke out of the morning and set it on the interval tray beside the earlier half-beat. The half-beat was there from Seed 22, the stroke that checked behind the standing finger and went on; the unchecking stroke was this morning's object, brought all the way to the place where the check had been and not stopping there, laid beside the first with no cause written across them and neither standing up to be the other's reason. It was an object among objects, not a rule and not a payment. He would not let two likenesses call themselves an account, because an account is a thing that has found a payer and no payer had been named to either stroke. The sixth stopping lay where he had set it the night before among five others, all six held at the teeth and none paid, and he did not let the morning's stroke lie down with them either. It was not a hearing and not a seventh.

The fire under the kiln-house went on at four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and forty days. Through the finger's-width door came the river's note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the full note. The dust on the wheel-keeper's forearms was finer than meal and named no slate; the iron in the damper held and took with one bite and was filed joined to no kiln; the men in the room off the shed kept their names and the room kept its finger's width of open. Above the boards four nails stood, four, and a fifth that was a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none became one.

The boy was one hundred and forty days old and four months and twenty days, and nobody under \place{Low Kiln} said so. He could not lift the tray to any table, could not reach the pot with its handle turned out, could not ask which slate had been broken, could not push the door an inch wider or put a name to the men. His hand outside the wool went up toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold, exact, complete and worth nothing. Tarn's stroke went on over the bright patch without the half-check, the stones keeping the shallow place where it had stopped, and the door off the shed stood a finger's width open.
