The market went on after the reading as a pot goes on after the spoon is taken out: a small remaining sound, a leaning with nothing left to lean toward, the last reeds of the month going off in armfuls judged by the arm that carried them and the eel tubs keeping their skin of cold water at the rim where the morning's ice had turned. The flat light came down off the boards and found the dust in the hollows of \place{the pot board}, and every cup on it looked like a cup set down by somebody who meant to come back for it and had not yet come. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} a little way along \place{the market stalls} and set the basket where the boards ran level and no slate lay, and stood a half-pace off with the little-door burn shut on nothing. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool, as she had told it coming down and would tell it going up, and told the wind nothing at all, because a wind has no instruction to receive.

\person{Berr Clay} came among the stalls a little after the reading with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat and the green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth, hot clay going in ahead of him and the smell of nothing at all following, the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before he touched nothing, as a man wipes an instrument before he lays it on a towel, and took two pair of eels from the tub with those same clean hands and laid two marks on the stall-keeper's outstretched palm. The woman put the marks in her palm without counting them where anyone could hear, the way a woman puts a hot coal in a pocket she means to empty later, and shut her hand on them; the eels went into a basket with a small wet slap and a flick of the tail that caught the light off the boards.

Two pair. Two marks short. There was no figure in the air over the tub, only the slap and the flick and the closing of a hand, and the boy set the whole transaction on a place of its own as a record that told exactly what had been carried and not what it had cost.

“A number goes into a slate, not a bundle,” Berr said. “If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” \emph{Your house}. Never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence the way a man takes his hand off a chart before somebody asks what is written under it, and the sentence stood afterward with nobody inside it, which is the way this district likes its arithmetic to stand. To the stall boards, in a voice lower than the one he had used at the block, he let fall that the \lore{levy} was called and was not called by him, one in twelve as \lore{the notice} says; nobody leaned in at that hour, so the words were spent on the boards and went nowhere any ear could follow.

The stall-keeper went on wiping the tub rim with a rag gone grey with slime and did not count the marks, and did not seem to mind the two eels that had not come with them. A kindness performed in the form of a subtraction is still a subtraction, and a man can believe he has done good with the very hand that carries the shortfall into a slate where nobody will look at it. The boy had learned to read the order in which a hand goes to a record before it goes to a person, and he read it now in the large clean hands going back to the eels. There was nothing cruel in the hands. That was the difficult part of them, and the reason he kept them on a tray of their own, apart from the midwife reaching for her slate before the child and the clerk going to his slate before his eyes went to the stalls.

Berr went off toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded square under his coat, the narrow slate under his arm and the green at his mouth where it had been, and up \place{the clay road} to the house whose door had never been opened in the boy's sight, where the true subtraction sat in a locked box and had sat there since the Account-day before this one and the one before that. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid. The one had gone into the air over the pig rail in the morning under \place{the Cross}; the other went up the road under a coat with the cord folded under it, and the two did not travel together and did not arrive at the same house. He believed he had done the district a kindness, and the belief was a clean thing in him, like the slate he wiped before he touched it. The boy kept the belief where he kept the twice-wiped thumb — on its own place, joined to no figure and no payer.

Below \place[the Singing Sill]{the sill}, at the end of \place{the fish-trade shade} where the boards ran out into the tubs, a woman wound a child's foot in three turns of grey wool. She worked with her lips shut and her eyes on the foot, the way a bandage is folded over a thing you do not want to look at twice, and when a neighbour came down with a pail and asked, the woman said it was the cold. The neighbour said it was weather, and that weather would go when it would go, and set the pail down where the boards were level and went back up the steps. Between the two sayings the foot stayed where it was: dark inside three turns of wool, the wool ridden back from the small bare joint, no colour coming into it.

The boy read the foot in the order his trade had taught him for any cold extremity: the part before the whole, the surface before the centre. The extremity goes before the core. The small vessels at the surface close down first and the blood goes where the warm organs can use it, so that a foot is given up to save a chest, and a child with a foot like that is further gone than a woman winding wool in a market can be expected to know. The face of severe cold is not a peaceful face; the almost-smile sits on it like a wrong lid on a jar, and the woman winding the wool would take it for sleep if she had not been looking at the foot. And the afterdrop waits on the far side of any warming anybody might perform, when the cold blood comes back to the heart and the core falls a degree before it rises, which is the moment a man is lost by people who have only just begun to be kind.

He knew the order. He knew the almost-smile. He knew the afterdrop and what it would do and at what hour it would do it, and there was not one thing in the shade he could put into the foot or lift out of it or carry up the steps. No silt-leach had been spoken over the tubs, no cause had been given, no cure was going up with the pail, and nobody had said what the grey was or where it had come from or whether it had been in the winter or come in with the flood. The boy did not supply the absence with a name. The foot was the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to the neighbour with the pail and nothing at all to the market selling over the top of both of them. He set the reading on the place where he kept such readings and left it there: exact, complete and worth nothing anybody in the shade would pay for. The wool stayed dark. The small wet sound of his own head going too far was the only other thing in the basket.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the going of the light came in through the finger's-width door as a note of water over water, low and fifth and never counted among \lore{the full note}. Sella carried him up at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, woodsmoke going before her out of the clay road and the river wind coming up to meet it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. She went around a pan set out to dry and the trough by the well with its skin of ice, and went wide around the space beneath the lowest rack where the broken birth-slates lay as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, lifting him out of the basket without looking into it and setting him down as one sets a pot down where it will not be slopped, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face. The thread at his mouth stayed where it had frozen, and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread.

The coals were banked under blind \lore{Ash} with the back of a broken pot, and she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}. The small knife lay on its sill where it could be checked, at the very edge with six standing in a worn thumb-mark and the seventh nearly gone and never cut. \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, and measured the kindling with his eyes as a debt is measured: four, four, and a place where the fifth should have stood. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood, and did not fill the place. He set the knife back where the house could check it and kept the other count where the house could not, the seventh year standing at the very edge of the sill with the unforged collector sentence beside it and the breath from Seed 16 still unspent, a small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in and did not. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

Then the sentence came.

It came in the shape it had worn for six years, a small plain sentence about an ear under this roof and what it was worth against the debt that held the man who owned it. It had come on Reed 5 and Reed 8 and Reed 11 and Reed 14 and Reed 17, and now on Reed 20 it came a sixth time. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan. The word stood with a figure behind it and a name behind the figure, and Tarn stopped there. He did not say the word. He did not say the figure. He did not say the name. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, said “Yes, house” to the fire, and went on with the broom.

Sella, within hearing, gave the end of her instruction to the pot and asked nothing, as she had given it to the pot on five nights already and would give it again. \person{Jorra Vail}, within hearing, did not ask about the ear; Tarn did not ask about the room off the shed whose door kept its finger's width of open. The two silences lay side by side under the one roof and were not joined, and nobody in the room made one of them the other. Jorra came through from the shed with the dust of the day still worked into both forearms past the bent little finger and told a pot it would do, and did not look toward the bed.

The boy laid the day's objects out in the order they had arrived, the way a man lays his tools back in a box, each in its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else. He took the sixth stopping out of the air where Tarn had left it and set it on the interval tray beside the first five. It lay there as an object among objects: Reed 5, Reed 8, Reed 11, Reed 14, Reed 17 and now Reed 20, six stoppings of the near-saying, each brought all the way to the word and stopped there with no figure and no name.

It was a sixth interval and not a sixth payment. It was not a hearing and not a seventh. The six hearings of the full note stayed six, five from four sources and the sixth from a person, beside and never among, and he did not let the stopping lie down with them. He would not let six likenesses stand up and call themselves a doctrine, because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer had been named to any of the six. A thing held six times at the teeth is a held word still. Six times owed is not six times paid, and a row of held things does not become a rule because the row has reached a number a man can count on his fingers. The tray held six objects and no account.

Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and nobody had named a payer for it in a hundred and thirty-nine days. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled. The seventh year stood at the very edge of the sill and had still not given. Tarn's stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch the stones kept, without the half-check, toward the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, and he did not turn his head along it.

The sixth stopping lay on the tray among five others and none of the six had a payer. The fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and his payer stayed unnamed a hundred and thirty-nine days. The boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold; he could not call the loan, could not spend the interval, could not lift the tray to any table in \place{Low Kiln}.
