Dawn came up off \place{the Ilm} under \place{the north bridge} and put the market in shadow before it put it in light. \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace from every open slate and set the basket at the very end of \place{the fish-trade shade}, where the boards ran out into the mud and no slate had ever been laid. She set it the way a woman sets a pot where it may be seen and not slopped, tucked the fold under his ear, took her hands off it, and gave the instruction to the wool and to the running water. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” No instruction went to the basket. The river wind came up \place{the Fishstairs} in four pulls with a landing where a fifth should have been, and she stood a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing, which is the stance of a woman who has set a thing down and will not be the one to say what it is.

Below the boards \person{Mira Silt} worked the eels. Pale slime had dried on her sleeves in long grey strokes, and silt had dried pale in the hollow of her neck where the collar rubbed, and she held her head a little to the right, as a woman holds it when the place where an ear should be is cold. The top of her left ear had been taken by a knife in a Fishstairs quarrel when she was nineteen; she said a hook did it, and the scar said otherwise, and nobody in fifteen years had made anything of the difference. She smelled of river silt and salted eels and tallow smoke and the cold that came up off the water before the sun got to it. Her small smile sat on her face the way a door sits on the latch, able to shut without anybody seeing the movement. She did not sit where a slate lay open, and she had not sat at one in fifteen years.

A woman come down from the weir with salt white at her wrists asked whether the run would be thin now the planting had taken hold of the month. Mira did not look up from the eel in her hand. “Thin?” she said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.” The question had been answered by the person asked it, in a form that left the answer where nobody could take hold of it, and the woman with the salt wrists went off with nothing. Mira moved two eel-bones against her thumb, back and forward and back, while she decided whether to lie, and put one away in her pocket when the deciding was done. The boy watched the order of it from the fold: the hand to the bones before the words, and the words before the lie, and the lie put away where no slate could reach it. He had known men who reached for a chart before they looked at the patient under it, and women who laid a date over a face, and here was a third order in a material he could not read, and he kept the three on three places without laying a line between them.

A buyer in a coat with the river still in its hem named a figure for two pair of eels. Mira's thumb went under her apron to the grey cord at her waist and stayed there while she decided, and the deciding took longer than the figure required. Shifting a creel showed the second cord twisted twice beneath the grey, the cord that had never been carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots} and never lifted at a door, and the knot on neither cord was counted by any thumb but her own, and the knot for the thin child upriver was not touched. Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and did not touch it. Orra had come down from \place{the Weir} before light with silt on her boots and the grey streak showing first through her black hair, and she stood where she stood and watched what there was to watch, and got no answer. The shape of the last knot on her cord was legible to the boy without its name, but he did not count it, and the word stayed unspoken in a market where a great many figures went past him that morning and none of them had his number in them.

Mira did not look into the basket. That was the keeping of it, the opposite of the pricing he had watched over the worked strips, where a man had read a figure off ground that would not give one and had gone away believing he had done a kindness. One woman kept a child by not looking at him, and another man priced a district by looking at it too hard, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held both kinds of not-looking, so he set them on separate places and wrote no cause across them. No crust came into the fold that morning. The one brown-edged crust already in it was stiff where it lay, and its empty place beside it had no name and no third was coming into it in this shade.

At the market's edge a foundling stood in cast-off clothes worn as if they had been measured for him: a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of his wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string at his throat that caught the light when he turned and went dull when he turned back. He was unnamed in the market and said nothing all morning. He watched the grown faces with the patient attention of a child who has no place in a thing and means to stay in it all the same, and whenever a pot was set down on a board his hand went first to the sliver at his neck, though he never seemed to know he had moved it.

A kiln-hand set a pot down on the boards near the eel shade. The boy heard \lore{the full note} of it under the sound of the water, the low note a sound vessel gives when a hand leaves it and the clay settles into itself, and his head went toward it. The foundling's head went toward it a half-beat after the boy's, after the knuckle and after the note, and then stopped in the middle of the turn. He rubbed the sliver twice with his thumb. He could not have said why he had turned, and if he had been able to say it there was no word in the market's language for the thing that had made him turn, which was not the note and came after it. A kiln-hand at the edge said the tail of a sentence and left it hanging, and the foundling's mouth opened on the end of it the way a child's mouth opens to finish a thing he has heard a hundred times, and nothing came out that any clerk could have taken down. No loaf passed. No slate was opened within reach of the basket. No secret changed hands that a slate could read.

The boy put the delay on a place of its own. It came after the knock and not before it; it was not a hearing and it was not a seventh, and his own turning he kept beside it without letting the two touch. A thing that comes after a sound is a different object from a thing that comes before it, and a delay is not a cause, and a cause written across two turns would have been a clean hand at the foot of a figure, which was the one thing he had spent his whole life declining to be. He set the watcher's turning beside the two tellings at \place{the loaf shelf} and joined none of them. A neighbour had called an old space at a row's end a thing the planting brought in, and put an appetite at a boy's ear in the same breath, and had gone off with her pot without seeing what she had said, and here was a third shape at the edge of a market turning half a beat late toward a pot. Three tellings, three places, no finger laid across them. Somewhere a long way off a hand would come and lay them together and call the joining an account; it was not his hand, and it was not today.

The steps below the bridge had come back the way the water had left them: four, four, and a landing a hand's breadth out of line where the fifth step should have stood, and a whole market walked over it carefully all morning without anybody making a fall of it. A girl with a creel went over it sideways, caught the rail, and went on selling as though the rail had always been there. The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing with his cuff grey from washing and his slate held to his forearm for a measure nobody had called, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. The boy filed that order apart from the pause in the woman's hand at the edge of the flare-light, apart from the grey-fingered man at \place{the Drawing} and the soft-handed man at the Court's table, and took no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand away from the market. A measure set down before anybody called it is a measure set down ahead of a death; he kept that as a shape and did not make a rule of it, because a rule is a thing a man can carry into a room and hand to somebody who will write it down.

At the going of the light \person{Sella Vail} carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because a market and a shade and a step and a bed are not openings, and she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. Her private roll stayed unshown.

He turned out the private file the way a man turns out instruments on a towel and found it unchanged. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a woman the yard had lifted, and no seventh came on Seed 19; three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid; three intervals lay on another; the word forty remained a saying with the readable double turn of a cord twisted twice beneath a grey one beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water over water was a fifth on a place of its own and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut counting room, nose-breath against throat-breath, both saying not the war's hand, stayed joined to no kiln with no third; the damper's double bite lay filed as an object; and the loan that can hear was still uncalled. To these he added the watcher's turn, after the note and after the boy's own turning, on a place by itself and beside the two tellings of the shelf, and he joined none of the three. He added one hundred and eight to the count and spent none of it.

She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had for a hundred and eight days, its payer unnamed. The door stood a finger's width open. The river's low muttered note came through the gap from the Ilm, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred and eight days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and could reach or ask for none of them. The turn at the market's edge was a thing that had come after a note and had stopped before it became a cause, and it lay where he had put it, worth nothing and joined to no hand. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. The count stood at one hundred and eight, unspent, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
