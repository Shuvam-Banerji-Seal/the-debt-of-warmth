## Chronicle

On the eighteenth of Seed Tarn's knife lay on its sill with six years worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, one entry the house could not check; to the kindling he said “Six counted. The next I don't count.” His chest had been tight since the dark, the breath unspent. He took the broom only after Sella Vail had instructed it, said “Yes, house” to an early thaw and a late, and swept the collector's road without once turning along it. No collector came. The planting went on in glances and handfuls, never a figure. The boy read the held breath as a clamp's, worth nothing, and added one hundred and seven.

Three doors down Alda laid her birth-slates on the bench before the fold, pressed the wool too long, crossed two fingers at the flower unknowing, and covered the sign with a dead woman's date. “Warm and breathing” — exact and not true. At the loaf shelf a neighbour named the old gap as a thing the planting brought in and went off without seeing the empty place at his ear. On the nineteenth men spoke low in the room off the wheel-shed and Jorra Vail gave them no name; he came through with grey dust past the bent finger, put it to the cold damper ahead of Sella's sentence, and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read the iron as the bite that holds and the bite that takes, with no word for both in Grathic.

At the dawn market Mira answered whether the run would be thin with her own answer, put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was named and showed a second twisted twice, no knot counted, her board beside Orra's. A foundling turned toward a pot half a beat after the boy's, after the note, and rubbed the green sliver. On the twentieth, Account-day, Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve over the worked ground: “The levy is called. It is not called by me,” and “A number goes into a slate, not into a strip.” The figure read was not the figure paid; the subtraction sat in a locked box up the road. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself.

Under the roof at the going of the light the boy turned out his file and found it unchanged — six hearings and no seventh, the word forty unspoken, the loan still uncalled. One crust lay stiff in the fold beside its unnamed empty place; the thread at his mouth was frozen. He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. The river's note came through the door and the kiln's missing breath through the floor, two counts that did not meet. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count stood at one hundred and nine, unspent and spoken by nobody.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Scene 6 ends at the going of the light, Day 109, on the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof. Corin lies alone in the room; Sella has told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it” where another woman would say “I,” stood a moment with her hand on the back of the broken pot counting something under her breath, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn (the grey streak at her temple showing pale), and did not look toward the bed. The boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down; the count stands at one hundred and nine, unspent, and the dark under the roof keeps it without a figure. Mid-action only in that small reaching; no opening, joining, payment or ending.
