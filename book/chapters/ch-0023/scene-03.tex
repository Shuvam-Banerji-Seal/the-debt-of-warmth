\daymark{108}{Seed 19, Y.H. 813}

Before first grey the room off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} gave back its dust the way a shut hand gives back nothing, and the door of it kept the finger's width of open it had kept since the year before this one. Men had come up \place{the Clay Yard} in the dark with their collars high and gone in, and the low voices that came out of the room did not carry as far as the edge of the light where \person{Sella Vail} had set \person[Corin]{the boy} in his basket. She set him the way a woman sets a pot where it may be seen and not slopped, the fold tucked under his ear and the wool ridden back from one wrist, and took her hands off him. The instruction went to the wool. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” None went to the basket. None went to the men, who were not called men or anything else under this roof.

\person{Jorra Vail} gave the house no name for them. He did not say the room was cold. He came through the shed door with his eyes going where they always went --- to the door, to the fire, to the wheel, and back without reporting --- and the boy read the not-saying the way he had once read a chart turned face-down at the foot, where the note lay under the signature and the signature covered it. There was a shape to a keeping like that: it admitted no question because it had answered none. No word in \lore{Grathic} held that particular silence, and he had no voice in any language to spend on it. He kept it as he kept the rest, on a place of its own, exact and worth nothing.

The dust came in on Jorra's forearms. It was fine and grey and worked into the creases past the bend of his little finger, ground small as meal and rubbed in with the sweat of a hand that had been splitting slate; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. Slip dried pale and sat on top of the skin where any man could see it. This had gone into the skin and stayed. The boy kept the dusty forearms and the clean forearms of other mornings on one tray together, two states of one man's working life, and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He had learned early in this house that a question spoken would travel further than the answer, and that the answer, if it came, would arrive in a hand he could not read and over a name he could not see. There was no reach in the small hand outside the wool for the wheel, no voice to put the question into the yard, and no standing from which to put it. He sorted the two states and filed them without a cause, the way he had once sorted twenty injured people by the single thing each of them would not say for himself.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had been laid, and the yard went around the space above them as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Nobody entered the space. Nobody lifted the slates. Sella, carrying him down at the half-pace from every open slate, went around it without looking and gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody, and the broom went where it was told and not into that space, and the household kept the place by leaving it alone, which is a kind of keeping no slate can read. It cost the yard a step each time and nobody counted the steps. The boy counted them and put them on the same tray as the dust.

Then Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” He said it as he had said it a hundred mornings, to the wood and the clay and not to any person who might have used it, and the wheel gave back its low note under his hands and went on taking clay up out of the basket. To a pot going past on a bench he said, “You'll do,” flat, with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it. The shed smelled of damp clay and tallow and, on a bad wind off the kilns, the sweetish edge of lead glaze. The seat at the wheel kept its hollow on the left where \person{Garl Vail} had leaned for twenty years, and Jorra's weight had not filled it in three, and nobody called attention to the difference. The boy knew the knowing in the young man's hands the way he knew the bent finger: a thrower could hear a sound vessel before it left the wheel, and this one kept the hearing to himself because a man who said such a thing before a kiln-master would have it entered against him as a claim. He kept the knowing where questions could last for years, which in this house was most of them.

\scenebreak

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low. It wants --- ” She did not get the rest out, because his bent finger had gone into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, ahead of his hand and ahead of the word, and the iron took the cold out of his fingertip and gave it back a little warmer. By the time she could have finished, the instruction had arrived where it was going, at the pot and at the fire, and she gave the end of it there instead of to him. “Feed it,” she told the fire, and that was the whole of the sentence that had begun with her son's name.

The household straightened around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. Nobody said the room off the shed was cold. Nobody said the fire had been low. The correction had been given and taken in one movement with no word in it, an obedience that was also an answer neither of them would ever name, and the yard went on with its work as if nothing had been said, because nothing had. He had not answered her with a word and had not looked at her. The boy watched the order of it from the edge of the light: the instruction to the fire first, then to the pot, and the son last, and the son moving before the instruction ever reached him, so that the order was kept whole and overturned at the same time without a word spent on it. He had once watched a senior man do something like it with a chart and a resident, and had never seen it done more cleanly.

His head went where it wanted under the fold and made the small wet sound it made when it went too far, and nobody put it right, because the fold was already right and the wool was already off the ear and there was no instruction left over for a head. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the bare place at the wrist found the wind before the wool could be pulled back over it. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear and the breast and the bucket's lip, and got the note of the wheel instead, which was worth nothing and went in anyway.

The boy read the iron once more as any iron that stops a thing from running. It was the same bite in the damper as in a clamp set across a vessel in a lit room: the bite that holds a thing whole is the bite that can take the hand off a man, and one piece of iron carried both mercies while the language he heard every day had no word to hold them together. Here they called it the damper and left it at that, which was not a poverty of the shed but of every mouth in it. He filed the reading as an object, exact and worth nothing, joined to no kiln. A man could know what the bite was and still not be able to spend the knowing, and there was nobody under this roof who would have paid him for it if he could have handed it over.

The promise of the foundling's name to the men who burned slates was neither given nor withdrawn. It had stood in this house since the boy came under the roof and Jorra said the one sentence that sealed it: \emph{He's not Vail}. He had not looked toward the bed or into the basket this morning, and the sentence stayed where it had been put, as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. Two things lived in the same young man without either troubling the other --- the most careful keeper of the Vail fire in four generations, and a boy who let the men who wanted every slate in \place{Sill} burn come into his mother's back room and speak low. He had never once thought they could not live together. The boy, lying where a pot is laid and not addressed, kept the two facts on separate places and wrote no cause across them, because a cause drawn here would be a clean hand at the foot of a figure, and he had spent his whole life declining to be that hand.

\scenebreak

Midday went over the yard without any change in its arithmetic. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and the new finer grey beside it, finer than the old, lay unswept and was called \lore{the thaw} and would go when it went. Sella stood over both lines with a broom in her hand and swept neither. She gave the instruction to the broom and the pot and the fire and never to the basket, and said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at any hour, because a wheel-shed and a rack and a yard and a basket are not openings, and she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. Her private roll stayed unshown. The yard went around the space under the rack again, and the steps were not counted by anybody else.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The one crust in the fold stayed stiff with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the new grey in the corner stayed where it lay, unswept and called thaw.

He turned out the private file the way a man turns out instruments on a towel and found it unchanged. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person the yard had lifted, and no seventh came on Seed 19; three near-full answers stood on a tray of their own, advanced and not paid; three intervals lay on another; the word forty remained a saying with the readable double turn of a cord twisted twice beneath a grey one beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; four low things kept their four places and the river's note of water over water was a fifth on a place of its own and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut counting room, nose-breath against throat-breath, both saying not the war's hand, stayed joined to no kiln with no third; the damper's double bite lay filed as an object; and the loan that can hear was still uncalled. He added one hundred and eight to the count and spent none of it.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had for a hundred and eight days, its payer unnamed. The door stood a finger's width open. The river's low muttered note came through the gap from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred and eight days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and could reach or ask for none of them. The reading of the iron was perfect and valueless in the dark, and the household had straightened around an unfinished sentence that nobody would ever enter. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. The count stood at one hundred and eight, unspent, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
