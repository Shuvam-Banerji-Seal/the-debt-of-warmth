The door gave onto the room the way any door gives onto a room it has long been opened into: a breath of colder air, the smell of wet wool drying where nobody had hung it with care, woodsmoke gone flat under the roof, and under all of it the sour mineral tang of a corner no broom had touched. \person{Sella Vail} came through with \person[Corin]{the boy} held level in the basket, the little-door burn shut on nothing where her hand had nothing to hold, and set him down under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof. She had carried him the whole way up at the half-pace from every open slate and had gone around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it; here she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, the way a woman sets a pot where it will not be slopped and does not look into it afterward. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours out at the Cross and she left it frozen and unwiped, because nobody had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The house took him in the way a cup takes a spoon: it closed around him and gave nothing back.

He laid the private file out in his mind as a man lays instruments on a towel, and found each where he had left it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a woman the yard had lifted from the brook --- and no seventh came on Seed 20. Three near-full answers stood on a tray of their own, advanced and not paid: the cup across a birth room, the pot under the wheel-shed wall, the rack pot in the yard. Three intervals lay on another tray, and between the trays the word forty remained a saying with no mouth to speak it, the readable double turn of a second cord twisted twice beneath a grey one beside it, and the weight of a thumb on wicker set apart from both. Four low things kept their four places, with \place{the Ilm}'s note of water over water a fifth on a place of its own and not a hearing. The two tellings of the shut counting room, nose-breath against throat-breath, both saying not the war's hand, stayed joined to no kiln and no third telling came to stand with them. \lore{the damper}'s double bite lay filed as an object, the bite that holds a vessel whole and takes the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies in one. The loan that can hear was still uncalled, and the sentence that would call it had not been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, like a tool set down in a dark room against a morning a man means to take it up.

To these he added what the day had given without entering any of it. The bell at the Cross went on one place as a thing heard, four, four and a gap, and the told bell of the Long Night stayed on another with the same four and the same missing stroke, the two kept apart although they were the same sound. Berr's twice-wiped thumb over the figure had gone up the clay road with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, and the figure read aloud had not become the figure paid. A neighbour's two sayings at the shelf --- the old gap named as a thing the planting brought in, the empty place at his ear called a child's appetite --- lay on two places and he had not made her joining his own. The watcher's turn at the market's edge, half a beat after the note and after the boy's own turning, lay on a place by itself beside the two tellings of the shelf and joined neither. Nothing had moved. Nothing had been paid. Nothing had been joined.

He was the only adult in the room and the least use in it. A man lays his instruments out in order because he means to put his hands into a body; the order is a promise of use, and the promise is most of the discipline. He had laid out a whole district's kept things in order and there was no field to open, no clamp to set, no person who would let him carry any of it to a door. He knew what each thing was and what it would cost if a hand ever moved it, which was the whole of his usefulness and the whole of his punishment. The file was exact, complete, and worth nothing.

There was no word in Grathic in which anybody under this roof could say what a life would count. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a board and in glances at a jar and in a space at the row's end, and a boy carried under the roof for one hundred and nine mornings had no name for the thing he kept and no person to keep it with. He had learned long ago, in a room whose light was better than this, that a figure at the foot makes the note above it difficult to argue with and no truer. Here there was no chart and no foot and no signature, and the count went on being exact for nobody. Exactness kept in a room with no use for it is not knowledge. It is a habit with nowhere to put its hands.

In the fold one brown-edged crust lay stiff with its empty place unnamed and no third beside it. It had gone past smelling of barley and had not gone out of the fold; nobody had lifted it and nobody had said it was there. On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end that had come in with the planting and the new palm's breadth that had come in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word, in a room that did not touch this one. Three absences, three places, no line drawn through them. A line drawn through three absences is a cause with a clean hand at the foot of it, and the clean hand was the thing he had spent two lives declining to become. He kept his finger off all three without thinking about it now, the way a man keeps his finger off a wound he has decided not to open.

Below the sill the child's foot stayed dark inside three turns of wool. The cold was the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed, and the two words had never once been let into the same sentence. He read the extremity going before the core as cleanly as he had ever read it across a table: the dark flesh, the almost-smile that is not peace, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform. The reading was perfect and bought nothing. There was no hand in the room long enough to wind the wool tighter, and no word he could make that would tell one woman the thing she already knew and had chosen not to call by its name.

The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} turned its corner lay where it had always lain, and the new finer grey beside it lay unswept and was called \lore{the thaw} that would go when it went. He had watched a woman stand over the two lines with a broom in her hand and sweep neither; the broom went around them the way water goes around a stone set in a shallow pan. The space under the lowest rack had been gone around again that morning as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and the broken birth-slates beneath it were entered by nobody. No opening had come in three days, so \lore{the widow's mark} hung at her throat untouched and her private roll stayed unshown. A strip, a shelf, a basket, a road, a door and a bed are not openings; the rule held on Account-day as exactly as it held on any morning, and the mark was not for a reading, a carrying or a silence.

Before she went to the fire Sella moved her lips along the loaf shelf in the next room and gave nobody a figure. The clay jar stood cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and she glanced at it once, the way a woman checks a pot set down where it may be seen, and took nothing from it and said nothing about it. Her hand went over the bare board where the row had lost its two places and came away with no loaf and no number, and she went around the clean dark line and the finer grey on her way back with a care that looked like not seeing them at all. From the bed the boy watched the order of it: the hand to the shelf, the eye to the jar, the broom to the corner, and the fire last of all. It was the order he had watched all day in other rooms, from a man with a fist at the Cross to a clerk with a slate at his forearm, and here it ended in a woman counting what the house was owed in loaves and glances and a space nobody called a figure. She gave the instruction to the wool and the pot and the fire and never to the basket, and the basket lay under the roof with its fold put right and took no instruction at all.

Sella went to the fire. “It was the thaw,” she told it, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed them the way a woman answers a child who has asked the same question a third time. The fire took the coal under blind ash and gave back four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, as it had given for a hundred and nine days. Its payer had no name in this house and no figure on any slate she had ever shown him. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and did not look toward the bed. The little-door burn shut on nothing as her hand came away from the pot, and the household, which was only a bed and a fire and a shelf in two rooms that did not touch, went on being kept.

She stood a moment with her hand on the back of the broken pot, counting something under her breath that no man could have taken up as a figure, and then set the pot where it could be found in the dark. The grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light off the coals and she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and went on counting. Three nights she had once sat up with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind; the story had come into this yard from other mouths and never from hers, and she had never once told it near a bed. Tonight she told the fire the thaw and nothing else, and did not look toward the bed, and the room went on being a room in which nothing was said.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from the Ilm, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, water over water with no vessel in it and no crack to find; and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot. The two sounds came in two separate places and had come so for a hundred and nine days, and he kept them apart without effort now, the way a man keeps two charts on two tables and will not let the corners touch. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet and never had.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. The small hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off, and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted; he could reach none of them and ask for none of them, and the wanting was exact and went nowhere the way a figure written on a slate goes nowhere toward the strip it was taken from. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody under this roof had been given an instruction about that sound either.

He had watched a man reach for a figure and take himself out of his sentence, and a woman reach for a slate before she turned to a fold, and a neighbour reach for a shelf before her eyes went to a child, and a clerk set his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody had called. Four orders, kept apart, no two joined. He had set them all down and carried none of them to a door, and now there was no door in this room except one standing a finger's width open for the river and no person on the other side of it who would take a figure from a child who could not speak. The wanting sat where the hand could not go, which is the ordinary condition of a man who knows the order of things and has no standing to change it. He did not try. Not trying was the last exact thing he had left.

The dark kept the count without a figure. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third; the shelf in the other room kept two gaps; the thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped; the wool rode back from the bare wrist. The river went over water beyond the finger's-width gap and the fire missed its fifth breath under the floor, and the two counts did not meet. One hundred and nine stood where one hundred and eight had stood the day before; on the morrow one hundred and ten would stand in the same place, exact, unspent, and spoken by nobody. Somewhere off the bed the small bench knife lay on its sill with six years in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check, and the loan that could hear waited under the roof for a sentence no man here would say.
