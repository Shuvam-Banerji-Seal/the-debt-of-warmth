**Meaningful change.** The planting crosses into its last tend, and Account-day reads the printed figure over the very ground the hands went into on Seed 15: the number the field withheld in fistfuls now arrives from a slate at a date, but the figure paid is still not the figure read and the true subtraction stays in Berr's locked box. Time moves, the tend turns, the pressure increases, and nothing is joined, named or paid. That is the chapter's whole movement.

## Scene 1 — At the Edge of the Sill

**Day and time.** Day 107, Seed 18, Eighthday, before first grey through midday; going of the light under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The yard well and the Clay Yard, the road toward the Fishstairs, the kindling sill in the wheel-shed; under the Vail kiln-house roof. The worked strips east of Low Kiln are spoken of as the place the planting goes on, not entered again as a new scene.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Tarn. Yard voices: those who want the thaw early and those who want it late; a field voice or two carrying word of the planting. Walk-ons only for the yard and field voices. Jorra is offstage; Alda, Mira, Berr and the watcher do not appear.

**Opens with.** `\daymark{107}{Seed 18, Y.H. 813}`. No recap: open inside the work, on the sill and the held breath, not by referring back to Chapter 22.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before first grey the small bench knife is on its sill with six years standing in a mark Tarn's thumb has worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check. To the kindling he says *Six counted. The next I don't count.* His chest has been tight since the dark and the breath he decided not to spend is still unspent.
2. He takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction; he says *Yes, house* to those who want the thaw early and those who want it late, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went.
3. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back. He calls everyone *house* and says *I owe* where another man says *I am*.
4. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. He was sent once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year, entering neither. No collector comes. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence that would call it is never said under this roof.
5. Word comes up that the planting goes on in the worked strips: hands in the brown ground counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. Sella keeps her half-pace from every open slate, goes wide around the space under the lowest rack and gives the instruction to the bucket and the broom before she gives it to anybody, never to the basket. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I*.
6. The boy, carried at the edge, reads the tight chest and the held breath as a breath held against a clamp --- the same iron that holds a thing in and will take the hand off a man --- exact and worth nothing. He adds one hundred and seven to the count and spends none of it. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it does not give here, and the breath presses to its edge.

**Emotional turn.** From the ordinary work of a morning at the well to the recognition that the whole house is holding one breath: the deference with a nail in it, the one account the house cannot check, and a man frightened enough to be helpful doing three things at once to keep the fourth from being seen. The pressure rises; nothing gives; the keeping is the whole of it.

**Sensory anchors.** Worn thumb-mark on the knife handle; Ash in knuckle creases the colour of the unswept line; pale clip-ring catching the little light; broom bristles over the rutted collector's road; tight chest and the small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in; river wind off the Ilm; woodsmoke and wet wool under the Vail roof. Food: nothing within reach; the house's loaves go along the shelf unnamed. Work: kindling, broom, bucket, the planting carried as a direction. Weather: the thaw argued early and late. Faith/money: the unforged sentence kept beside the seventh year like a tool set down in a dark room.

**Threads advanced.** T036/T076 (six counted, seventh held at the very edge, breath unspent, two ears and two silences, loan uncalled); T071/T072 (107 exact against the house's bad arithmetic); T058/T079 (instruction to broom and bucket, never the basket, half-pace, *the house*); T009/T013/T092 (road swept without turning, no collector, worked ground and the left things kept); T088 (carried fire, payer still unnamed); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing; the held breath filed as an object, not a hearing).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T036 advanced toward the day something must give at the seventh year's end and the loan can be called; the held breath and the unforged sentence are carried, not spent. T009's plain fact that a thing left where it lies is still kept is carried in the unturned road and the unforged sentence. **Nothing paid**: no seventh year counted, no collector sentence entered, no turn along the road, no ending.

**Exact last image.** The small bench knife on its sill with six years in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, Tarn's chest tight with the breath still unspent and the broom set away after going over the collector's road without once turning along it; under the roof the river's note and the kiln's four, four and a gap come in two separate places, and the boy's hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down with the count at one hundred and seven, unspent.

## Scene 2 — The Date Over the Sign

**Day and time.** Day 107, Seed 18, continued: midday three doors down in House Silt; afternoon at the loaf shelf in the Vail kiln-house; going of the light on the bed under the roof.

**Place.** House Silt, three doors down the Clay Yard, with its finger's-width-open door, bench, board-bed and grey skirting; the Vail loaf shelf, clay jar, corner lines and drying racks; under the Vail roof.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Alda Reed, Sella Vail, a neighbour at the loaf shelf (walk-on, the T097 witness shape, not named as such in the narration). Dessa stays dead and offstage; nothing of her name or number is spoken to the boy.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every open slate; she opens no slate and turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row, and stands a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing.
2. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, her nails scrubbed to the quick with clay under them. She lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, the order of a hand that reaches for the record before the person, and presses the wool flat at his shoulder a breath longer than a fold requires.
3. Her hand goes to the red edge of the birth-flower at his wrist and crosses two fingers there before she knows it; she covers the sign at once with the date she gave Dessa --- the seventh of Stone, the account up on the eighth before the paper went out --- laying a date where a name should have stood over a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of and uncollected. She says *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down*, exact and not true, and goes on with dates after the question has stopped because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry.
4. Her hand goes once into her bodice to the little tin and comes away without opening it. The sixth narrow slate stays face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name, six pages in a second hand all unread, no seventh written and T023 not paid again. Above the abandoned board-bed the round pewter tin keeps its pale fold at the seam and thumb-bright rub on the lid unopened, while the grey wool below holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone lay; nobody says either is gone. The boy sets the two private records in two materials on two places and writes no cause across the space.
5. At the loaf shelf a neighbour reaches past the bare board, opens for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, closes on the loaf beyond and says in one breath that the old space at the row's end came in with the planting; her hand has gone to the board before her eyes went to the boy. She goes with her pot. The boy files the saying beside the one already waiting there --- T097 advanced and not paid --- and keeps the shelf's two unnamed gaps in the next room from touching the fold's one stiff crust and its unnamed empty place.
6. Sella moves her lips along the loaf row, glances at the clay jar on the wall shelf and gives the house no figure; she touches the widow's mark at no hour, because a shelf, a strip and a bed are not openings. She tells the fire it is the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and feeds it; the fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and seven days. At dusk she carries him up at the half-pace, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen and unwiped.

**Emotional turn.** A near-joining refused twice over: Alda lays a date where a name should stand and the neighbour lays a planting beside an appetite in one breath, and the boy's whole work is to keep his finger off both places. The keeping buys him nothing and keeps the places clean, which is his exact, unspendable dignity. The mercy-lie has curdled past correction while the two records in tin and slate stay shut and unread.

**Sensory anchors.** Soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt; lye sour under the soap; nails scrubbed to the quick with clay under them; slates laid writing-down on the bench; the grey ridge running to the skirting; the pewter tin's pale fold and thumb-bright rub; the hollow in the grey wool; loaves pushed along a bare board and the palm's breadth of the new gap; the clay jar cold under its glaze; woodsmoke, wet wool, the sour tang of the unswept corner; the fire's four low pulls and the missing fifth; river water over water through the door gap. Bodies: the fold pressed a breath too long, the unaware two-finger sign, the frozen thread at his mouth, cold cheek–temple–wrist, the little-door burn closing on nothing. Faith: the date laid over the sign and the widow's mark held while no opening comes.

**Threads advanced.** T023/T089 (Alda's sixth page and both tins shut, not paid again); T007/T008/T073 (unaware sign, date over the flower, mercy-lie curdled to fact); T060/T086 (one crust and its empty place, two shelf gaps, rooms that do not touch); T097 (witness advanced, **not paid**); T058/T079 (half-pace, *the house*, instruction to the fire, never the basket, mark untouched); T088 (carried fire, unnamed payer); T009/T013/T092 (two unswept lines, left things kept); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing); T071/T072 (107).

## Scene 3 — The Wheel Before the Hands

**Day and time.** Day 108, Seed 19, Ninthday, before first grey through midday; a short dusk under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The Vail wheel-shed and its finger's-width-open back room (heard, not entered), the damper, the drying racks and the Clay Yard; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Jorra Vail, Sella Vail. The men in the back room are unnamed, unentered, unfollowed. Tarn is offstage this scene; Alda, Mira, Berr and the watcher do not appear.

**Opens with.** `\daymark{108}{Seed 19, Y.H. 813}`. No recap: open on the dust coming out of the room, not on yesterday's shelf.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Men have come in before first grey and spoken low in the room off the wheel-shed that stands a finger's width open; Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room was cold.
2. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel; he keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody.
3. He speaks to the wheel before he speaks to anyone: *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.* He tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it.
4. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low. His bent finger goes into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her sentence, the iron taking the cold out of his fingertip, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He has not answered her with a word and has not looked at her.
5. The boy reads the iron once more as any iron that stops a thing from running: the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man are one bite, and no word in Grathic holds both mercies. He files the reading as an object, exact and worth nothing, joined to no kiln. The promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed.
6. Dusk under the roof: the new grey stays unswept, the one crust stays stiff in the fold, the file is unchanged and his hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down.

**Emotional turn.** The house straightens around a silence rather than a word --- an obedience that is also a correction neither will name --- while the boy's reading of the iron is perfect and valueless: he knows what the bite is and cannot spend the knowing or say it in the one language he hears every day.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust in the creases past a bent little finger; bone-coloured slip dried to old bone on other mornings; damp clay, tallow, the sweetish lead edge carried up from High Kiln on a bad wind; the wheel's low note; cold iron taking the cold out of a fingertip; the damper's bite; the yard going wide around the space under the rack. Work: wheel, pots, damper, broken slates left where they lie. Bodies: the bent finger leading, the boy's small hand outside the wool. Faith: the household's silence as a weather signal; the back room's door kept a finger's width open.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031 (men unnamed before first grey, grey dust past the bent finger, broken birth-slates under the rack, promise ungiven, *he's not Vail* sealed); T062 (damper's double bite filed as an object, joined to no kiln, not paid); T076/T036 (two ears and two silences under one roof, loan uncalled); T061/T082 (no seventh hearing, file unchanged); T058/T079 (Sella's unfinished instruction arriving at the pot); T071/T072 (108).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T035/T031 advanced toward the day Jorra hands the foundling's name to the men who burn slates; T062 toward the day the house's danger is named. **Nothing paid**: no name for the men, no opening of the back room, no slate examined, no birth-slate read, no seventh hearing, no name given.

## Scene 5 — One in Twelve, As the Notice Says

**Day and time.** Day 109, Seed 20, Tenthday, Account-day, morning at the Cross reading block and the worked strips beyond; midday under the Vail roof.

**Place.** The Cross reading block and the market around it, the worked ground east of Low Kiln spoken of as the thing read over; the Clay Yard and the Vail roof at midday.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short). The two hundred at the Cross, the man who brings a number in his fist and asks what the planting cost, and the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (reported still set) are walk-ons. No watcher on stage this scene; no Marra, no sixth sighting.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him down at the half-pace from every open slate and sets the basket in the moot-post's thin shade, away from any open slate, where he can see and not join.
2. Berr comes up with the red wool cord of his office folded under his coat instead of worn across it, green of tasted glaze at one corner of his mouth and hot clay over the smell of nothing at all, the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands are large and clean; he wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing and holds a narrow slate unopened against his forearm, passing a finger over the figure and letting his eyes rest on it a breath longer than the writing required.
3. He reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the top of the crowd over the worked ground where hands went in on Seed 15, calls the levy and says *It is not called by me*. To the man who brings a number up out of a fist and asks what the planting cost now that hands have been in it, he says *A number goes into a slate, not into a strip; if the house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch* --- *your house*, never *I* --- taking himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.
4. The figure read aloud is not the figure paid. The true subtraction goes over the strips unspoken and sits in the locked box in his house up the road; nothing in the ground changes its figure because a man has looked at it. The hands that went in by the look do not become a count. Garl's uncalled debt against the firing of tiles goes around the space where a name might have stood, and is not called.
5. The bell at the Cross goes four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody crosses himself; word comes up that the soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the Fishstairs with his slate for a measure nobody called. The boy keeps the heard bell beside the told bell in two places and adds no seventh hearing.
6. Berr never looks into the basket and goes off toward High Kiln with the box still locked and the true deduction inside, believing he has done the district a kindness. Sella carries the boy home at the half-pace and puts the fold right without looking at his face.

**Emotional turn.** A figure read over ground that refused to give a figure: the district's withholding met by an institution that has already written the answer down, in a voice so low the crowd has to lean in. The boy sees the clean hand go to the record before the person and knows the shape of it, and the knowing buys nothing.

**Sensory anchors.** Red wool cord folded under a coat; lead-glaze green at the corner of a mouth; hot clay over the smell of nothing; large clean hands and a thumb wiped twice on an apron; a finger passed over a figure and eyes resting a breath too long; the printed notice; the bell's four, four and a gap doing a temple's work with a Court's hand; the moot-post's thin shade; river wind and the smell of the worked strips. Food: the market goes on around the reading; nothing eaten within the boy's reach. Money: a number from a fist sent into a slate at the watch, the true subtraction locked away. Faith: *as the notice says*, the speaker taken out of his own sentence.

**Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077/T078 (one in twelve read as the notice says, figure into a slate and not a strip, self-removal, true subtraction unspoken, bell four-four-and-a-gap with nobody crossing); T090/T080 (soft-cuffed clerk still set for the uncalled measure, filed apart); T009 (Garl's uncalled debt left where it lay, left thing still kept); T061/T082 (six hearings beside and never among, no seventh, heard bell beside told bell); T071/T072 (109); T058/T079 (half-pace, moot-post shade, *the house*).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T015/T041 advanced toward the later day the true deduction is published; T090 toward the uncalled measure becoming a standing charge on a district that has spent its dead. **Nothing paid**: no true figure, no opening of the box, no seventh hearing, no sixth sighting, no naming of the planting's cost in hands, no ending.

## Scene 6 — One Hundred and Nine

**Day and time.** Day 109, Seed 20, Account-day, continued: midday through the going of the light under the Vail kiln-house roof.

**Place.** Under the Vail roof --- the bed, the loaf shelf in the next room, the corner lines, the drying racks and the door standing a finger's width open; the wheel-shed and the road heard beyond.

**Present.** Corin/Dan, Sella Vail. All others offstage; Dessa stays dead and uncollected and her name and number are not spoken. No Marra, no watcher, no collector.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him home at the half-pace from every open slate, sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because nobody has been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.
2. The boy turns out the private file the way a man turns out instruments on a towel and finds it unchanged: six hearings of the full note --- five from four sources and the sixth out of the woman in the brook --- filed beside and never among with no seventh; three near-full answers advanced and not paid; three intervals on another tray; the word forty unspoken with the readable double turn of a second cord twisted twice beneath a grey one beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; four low things in four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut counting room, nose-breath against throat-breath, both saying not the war's hand, joined to no kiln with no third; the damper's double bite filed as an object; the loan that can hear still uncalled and the sentence that would call it still unsaid under this roof.
3. In the fold one brown-edged crust lies stiff with its empty place unnamed and no third. On the loaf shelf in the next room the row keeps its two unnamed gaps --- the old space at the row's end that came in with the planting and the new palm's breadth that came in the night of Seed 6 with no scrape, crumb, print or word --- in a room that does not touch the fold's. Below the sill the child's foot stays dark inside three turns of wool, the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed; the boy's reading of the extremity going before the core works perfectly and buys nothing.
4. The clean dark line where the Ash turned its corner lies where it has always lain and the new finer grey lies beside it unswept and is called the thaw that will go when it goes. The space under the lowest rack is gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and the broken birth-slates beneath it are entered by nobody. No opening came in three days, so the widow's mark hangs at her throat untouched and Sella's private roll stays unshown.
5. Sella tells the fire it was the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and feeds it; the fire answers four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, the way it has for a hundred and nine days, its payer unnamed. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I* and does not look toward the bed.
6. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note comes through the gap from the Ilm, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor comes the kiln's breath, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred and nine days. The boards overhead keep four nails and four and a fifth that is not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts do not meet. Cold comes in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rides back from the wrist so the cold finds the bare place. He wants the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counts, and can reach or ask for none of them. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water and comes down. The count stands at one hundred and nine, unspent, and the dark under the roof keeps it without a figure.

**Emotional turn.** The check comes back clean: nothing has been moved, paid or joined, and the keeping produces nothing. He is the only adult in the room and the least use in it; the file is exact, complete and worthless, and the count goes on one more day into a new tend.

**Sensory anchors.** Wet wool, woodsmoke, the sour tang of the unswept corner; the fold put right without a look; the frozen thread; the clay jar cold under its glaze; loaves pushed along a shelf with two empty places; grey wool in three turns around a dark foot; the clean dark line and the finer grey; the fire's four low pulls and the missing fifth under blind ash and the back of a broken pot; the river's note through the door gap and the kiln's breath through the floor. Bodies: cold cheek–temple–wrist, the wool riding back, the hand up an inch and down, the small wet sound when the head wanders. Food: one stiff crust, no third. Money: none; every warmth in the room has a payer nobody names. Faith: the widow's mark untouched, the marks covered with dates.

**Threads advanced.** T061/T082 (six hearings beside and never among, no seventh, near-full answers advanced, intervals, heard bell beside told bell, half-beat after the knock kept apart); T060/T086 (one crust and its empty place, two shelf gaps in a room that does not touch); T093/T094/T096 (cold foot still the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused, unnamed, no silt-leach); T023/T089 (sixth page and both tins shut, not paid again); T059/T025/T024 (word unspoken, readable double turn and thumb's weight kept apart); T036/T076 (loan uncalled, seventh year held, two silences); T009/T013/T092 (two lines unswept, left thing kept, rack space gone around); T088 (carried fire, payer unnamed at 109); T071/T072 (109, unspent); T058/T079 (*the house*, instruction to the fire, never the basket, half-pace).

**Foreshadowing planted / paid off.** T093/T094/T096 carried toward Arc 3, where the cold foot will be found again and named as a beast with a habit; **not paid** and no cause or cure spoken. T036 held for the day something must give at the seventh year's end. T023/T089 held for the convergence of second records after Alda is gone. T097 remains planted and unpaid. **Nothing paid**: no seventh hearing, no joining, no third gap, no silt-leach named, no opening, no ending.

**Exact last image.** The door a finger's width open with the river's low muttered note of water over water coming through the gap and the kiln's four, four and a gap coming through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places; the boards overhead keeping four nails and four and a fifth that is a knot the width of a thumb, the two counts not meeting; one brown-edged crust stiff in the fold with its empty place unnamed and no third, the shelf's two gaps in the other room; the thread frozen at his mouth and the wool riding back from the wrist; and his hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water over water and coming down. The count stands at one hundred and nine, unspent, and the dark under the roof keeps it without a figure.

## Pull into Chapter 24

The chapter ends on the hand going up an inch toward the water and down, the count at one hundred and nine unspent, no opening, no joining and no payment anywhere. That exact forward motion carries straight into Day 110, Seed 21, Firstday: the planting month enters its last tend, the seed stands up in the strips the boy first carried as a direction, and the hands that went in on Seed 15 are still counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. Sella will go around the two lines, the two shelf gaps and the fold's gap without laying them together; a woman at the loaf shelf will reach for the place that is no longer there and call the old gap a planting and the new one the price of a wet thing, still without seeing they are one absence, while the boy keeps three places apart (T060/T086, T097 advanced and still unpaid). Tarn's seventh year will press past the end of the third tend and still be held with the unforged collector sentence beside it; Jorra's dust and the damper's double bite will stay joined to no kiln; the clerk will still sit at the Fishstairs for the uncalled measure. Nothing has been closed here, so Chapter 24 opens on the same held things, one day further on, with the count exact and unspent and the last tend of the planting month ahead.
