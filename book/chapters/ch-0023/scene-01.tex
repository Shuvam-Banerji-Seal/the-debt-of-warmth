\daymark{107}{Seed 18, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{B}{efore first grey} the small bench knife lay on its sill in \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, blade to the wall and handle out, the order \person{Tarn} kept even in the dark, and six years stood in a mark a thumb had worn almost flat while the seventh was nearly gone and still held. \person{Sella Vail} had carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down before the yard stirred and set him at the edge of the light with the fold tucked under his ear and the wool riding back from one wrist, the way a woman sets a pot where it may be seen and not slopped. The house had not yet decided whether the day would be cold. Behind him the carried fire kept its own quiet account, four low pulls and the space where the fifth should have been, and nobody had yet told it the morning was come. On the sill the knife held the one entry in this house the house could not check.

Tarn came in with the dark still on his shoulders. He took the small knife up, turned it in his fingers and put it back where it could be found, telling the empty shed where it was as a man states the weather; a small tool went into his pocket and came out again, because a man who owns nothing should know where the small things are. Then he bent to the kindling and set his thumb along the worn mark the way a man sets a finger on a pulse to count it, and said to the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” The words went into the kindling and stayed there. His chest had been tight since the dark. When he reached up to the stack the breath did not go in at the top of the reach; there was a small interval there, the width of a breath, and he went on through it without spending the breath he had decided not to spend. The boy watched the interval come and go the way he watched a pot's note come up to the rim of hearing and go back down, and put it on the place where such things were put.

Presently a vessel went past the shed door in somebody's hands and gave its flat note --- a whole pot, air under the note and nothing cracked in it --- and Tarn's head did not turn and he did not name the sound, any more than he had named it on the mornings when it came through the wall. The boy had once turned his own head before a pot was set down, before the note and before the knuckle that gave it; he had learned since to stop the turn where it started. He kept his stopping on one place and Tarn's not-turning on another and wrote no word across the space between them. Two ears kept their silence under one roof, and an ear that can hear is worth more than the debt that holds a man, which is why the debt keeps it and why nobody under the roof says so.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line out at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what light there was and gave it back, so that the wrist looked warmer than the hand. He was narrow and bent from six years in the slip pit, and people noticed first how quickly he moved and last that he stopped moving altogether when he was told he was doing well. He called Sella \emph{house} and the yard \emph{house} and the kindling \emph{house}, and said \emph{I owe} where another man would have said \emph{I am}. When a woman at \place{the yard well} asked how long he had been with the house he said, “I owe six,” and shut his mouth on the seventh as a man shuts a fist on seed.

Sella did not give the broom to anybody until she had given the broom its instruction. “Over the road first,” she said to the broom, “then \place{the Clay Yard}, and let the line lie where it lies.” Only then did she let Tarn have it in his hands, and he took it as a man takes an instrument that has already been named. The yard had two voices on \lore{the thaw} that morning. “It'll come,” a man said, “and the strips want the sun on them before the tend's out.” A woman at the racks said, “Let it come late. There's a week yet before the paying, and a late thaw's a longer week before it.” “Yes, house,” Tarn said to the early one, and “Yes, house,” to the late one, and agreed so entirely that the argument had nowhere to put its foot. He did not take either side; he stood with the broom in his hands while the two voices went past him the way weather goes past a wall, and the only thing that moved in him was the wanting of the late, which stayed where it was, because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went and had not stirred out of them.

The bristles went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, and Tarn went over it with the broom and did not once turn along it. The ruts held the old water in their hollows and gave it back muddy at the edges. The road led down toward \place{the Fishstairs} and the north bridge and the houses where a man with a slate and a cold tube would come from; the bristles crossed the mouth of it again and again and went back across, and no collector came. He had been sent once in \lore{the Long Cold}, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year, entering neither. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. The sentence that would call it had not been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, like a tool set down in a dark room against a morning a man means to take it up.

\scenebreak

Word came up from the worked strips east of \place{Low Kiln} that the planting went on: hands in the brown ground again, counted in glances and handfuls and never in a figure. A woman come up from the strips had silt dried pale on her shins and held up two fingers with chaff in the creases to show the seed was in. “They went in yesterday,” she said, and would not say what the fingers counted. “The strips want another tend,” the man beside her said, and looked at the ground a moment longer than the ground required, and the woman said the weather would have the last word. Neither put a figure to a hand or a strip, and the word went around the well as a direction goes, arriving at every ear and at no slate. Sella went at a half-pace from every open slate, gave the instruction to the bucket and the broom before she gave it to anybody and never to the basket, and went wide around the space under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and did not take a broom to the two lines at the corner, because a strip and a well and a rack and a basket are not openings.

The basket sat at the edge of the work with the fold over him and the river wind off \place{the Ilm} coming under the wool. He could see the road and the yard and the strip beyond it as a direction carried and not yet a number, and kept them on the places he had made for them and did not lay one across another. A woman drawing at the well let the bucket down and said something about the cost of a wet thing set down in the wrong place. “Stand there,” Sella said to the bucket, “and come up quiet.” She gave the woman no figure. “The house has said it,” she said, which was true and was not a number. In a house that kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf and in glances at a clay jar on a wall, the boy had learned to tell when a sentence had been given an instruction and when it had been given an answer. Nobody that morning gave either to the basket. His head went where it wanted under the fold and made the small wet sound, and the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and no hand had been instructed about the thread.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire took the coal and gave back four low pulls with the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for a hundred and seven days. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I} and did not look toward the bed.

Tarn's chest came back to him as an object laid on its own place: tight since the dark, with a breath held behind it and the small interval at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in. He read it as a breath held against a clamp, which is an exact thing and worth nothing --- the same iron that holds a vessel whole and will take the hand off a man, one bite with no word in Grathic for both mercies. He had spent his life reading what a held thing costs: a breath stopped behind a clamp is a breath spent whether it goes in or not, and the price is in the hand that holds the iron, not in the vessel it holds. He had known the order of a dozen such holds and had never once been the man who paid for the iron. Knowing the shape of the thing bought him nothing here. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and the breath behind the tight chest was kept where the seventh year was kept, and neither was spent under this roof.

Somewhere off the bed the small bench knife lay on its sill with six years in a mark worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. The broom was set away after going over the collector's road without once turning along it. Something must give at the end of the seventh year; it did not give here, and the breath pressed to its edge.

The door stood a finger's width open. The river's low muttered note came through the gap from the Ilm, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred and seven days. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool rode back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, the breast, the bucket's lip two paces off and the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted, and could reach or ask for none of them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down. He added one hundred and seven to the count and spent none of it, and the dark under the roof kept it without a figure.
