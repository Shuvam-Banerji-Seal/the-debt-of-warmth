\daymark{414}{Ilmen 20, Y.H. 814, Tenthday}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay flat against the fold where the going of the light had left it, and the small hours came on under the roof the way dry cold comes through a wall that has no crack named for it --- not arriving, only being there. Answer four hundred and two lay under the hand, a small intake held whole on the river's note's own finishing and let go as the note ended, exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air; four hundred and thirteen days stood behind it, six months and two hundred and thirty-three of them told by nobody. This was the tenth of \place{Ilmen} and the month's second Account-day and market, earned by its own morning, and nothing from the nineteenth had crossed under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. First light was still a direction beyond \place{the finger's-width door} and not a thing in the room. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and the heat came to him sideways from \place{the hearth} and not where he would have chosen. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool, gone past the point where a child would still eat it and hardened through the dry nights into old horn. Beside it \lore{the keeping at the fold} lay folded once where it had been put, found where put on the mornings and goings of the light of the seventeenth, eighteenth and nineteenth, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name and no gift over it. The thread of drool at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

Taking the body in its old order was the only work the room allowed him, and he took it: crust, fold, thread, cold, the waiting heat. He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, that the grimace of a cold man wears the shape of a smile and the smile means nothing, that pink returning is the surface still lying warm over a core still cold, and that a cold patient cannot clot and must be rewarmed from outside, slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. A basket set level out of the draught where the warmth came sideways was the rewarming he would have chosen and could not ask for. He had once sorted twenty people by what they would not survive, and that knowing had hands two paces long and a body that could not lift a crust to its own mouth. Under all of it, louder than the register, the wanting went on: the crust lifted, the thread wiped, a hand on the handle of the pot. He wanted the three things without knowing that wanting them was a want. He got none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and no verdict was looked for; the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor does not touch his.

First light came up \place{the clay road} side of the room without a boundary drawn across the dark, and the count became four hundred and fourteen exact, six months and two hundred and thirty-four days, told by nobody. He did not stand the number up as a threshold, a doctrine or a figure; a number stood up as a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and none stood. \place{Ilmen 30} stood eight mornings off, a date and not a day that had earned anything, and he did not bring it into a morning that had earned only its twentieth. The clerk's figure entered before a face was looked at stayed on its own place beside his inward slate at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, with a finger between it and \person{Tarn}'s degree toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard's edge filed among the older record-first hands and not counted among them: hand, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap, reported and not witnessed. Tarn's sentence, withheld on the eighteenth and said again on the nineteenth, was found where put and not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a payment and not a rule. The softening at the river's skin and the air less cold were found where put and called weather, on a place of its own beside the clean dark line and the called-gone grey, not a sign, not a fourth covering, not a sixth shaping and not spent on a reading that had not yet been read. The fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying} sat on its own tray between \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth record-first hand up the road and the saying of the road from \place{the Cross} to \place{Greybank}, with a finger between every pair and a third between it and the four ownerless directions; her narrow slate stayed unread up the road and \person{Ilva}'s strip of notched birch stayed in its sleeve away from this yard with her ninth hand nine, and no figure came off any wood.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the hearth before the basket, as she had at both ends of every day since he came under the roof. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps. \emph{The house will keep it} went to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally on its underside stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air, and went back to the pot. Her thumb went first to where the tighter wire crossed the crack, and only then did the weight come on the handle; the hairline showed between the old-brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side. Both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration; a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves the hand showing. At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain piece by piece under the wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each down where it had lain, took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. \person{Garl Vail} stayed where Garl stays, in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder on the peg, the hollow wheel-seat under \place{the Vail wheel-shed} nobody was allowed to fill, the clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} with its finer grey called the thaw, the hairline in the pot, and the unforged collector sentence beside the seventh notch at \place{the kindling} with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. His name had gone unspoken four hundred and thirteen days and \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth had gone unpaid the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; the fifth pull under \place{the boards} was unsupplied and nobody lifted the floor.

Before first light she carried him down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; she did not tell the basket what the day was and did not tell it to be still. Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the dark still held \place{the seven walked-around places}, gone around with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place} where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come out. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign; a figure set against it would want a payer standing and none stood. At the kindling Tarn kept six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside Garl's unforged sentence, the small knife at the edge of its sill; his hand went over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach, the coming away the whole of the checking. He said nothing of the count to the wood on this morning, and the sentence of the nineteenth was found where put and not made a habit. At the wheel-shed edge \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger, parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, and spoke to the wheel before any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. He told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness, and kept the full note it would give three turns ahead from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence and came out without the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time; \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

At the Cross the market had begun to assemble in the cold before the top of the morning: boards going up, a rope of pigs somewhere behind the square, the first of the two hundred finding places to lean. \place{The reading block under the moot-post} stood with its finger's width of thin shade on the north side, and Sella set the basket there, where no slate lay, the cold iron of \place{the moot-post} at his cheek, and stood half a pace off through the reading and the bell. She gave no instruction and no reason to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and nobody under any roof instructs a basket about what goes over it.

Eight pigs went over the wet boards and no figure was called aloud for any of them. From the low angle of the basket the square came by its small sounds first --- the rope under a pig's belly, the rasp of a slate laid flat on a board, the small flat knock of an iron rim on stone, a cooper's mallet checking a hoop. The small sounds did not lie and the large ones were mostly weather. Figures came to a heap of turnips and were left standing side by side without being made to agree; nobody made a row of the two. A neighbour passed the bell from mouth to mouth along the rail without making a number of it, and the number that was coming was not yet in anybody's mouth.

By the time the top of the crowd had begun to lean, \person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road to the block with the red wool cord of his office folded square under his coat and the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks that a number is still where he put it.

Then he read low into the goers-by, \emph{One in twelve, as the notice says}, and sent the figure twice over the basket --- once for the moot and once for the households that came late --- never looking into it. He called the levy and denied it. \emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me}; \emph{Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch}; \emph{If your household has heard otherwise, your household has heard wrong} --- \emph{your house}, never I --- taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the work clean and the hand that did it nowhere in it. To a man who had brought a number up out of a shut fist and asked what the levy would take he did not give the number back: the man was sent to a slate at \place{the watch}, and the number went from the fist to a mouth to a shoulder going up the road and arrived nowhere.

Something low he said to the block about kindness being entered, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs, and the block answered nothing. There was no anger in it and no triumph, and no payer stood under it for the boy to file; the sentence went into the stone and stayed there as a stain stays in a board. He went off up the clay road toward High Kiln with the cord folded, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked, the second true subtraction of \place{Ilmen} riding inside, believing he had done a kindness. That belief, not anger, was the part of him that would one day be remembered against him; the boy filed it on a place of its own without anger and without triumph, because a finding filed in anger is a finding forged.

He took Berr's order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart --- the thumb wiped before the slate was touched, the finger over the figure through the sleeve before the eyes to the goers-by, the sentence said with the speaker taken out of it --- and filed it on a tray of its own beside the other record-first hands, with a finger between it and the face of the man from \place{Slate Row} at the yard's edge that was filed and not counted, and between it and the runner's eighth that would come up the road before noon. No tenth stood up in the empty place, and he made no doctrine of the twice-wiped thumb and no payer of the man who wiped it.

He filed the arithmetic. Two readings of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure: the first of Y.H. 814 gone up the road on the tenth and this, the second, gone up this morning, and behind them the twelve of Y.H. 813, twelve and not one account. Two were two and not one account; the first was not made a thirteenth by the second, and the second supplied no signature. The figure read was still not the figure paid, no signature stood on the space between, and no doctrine came out of two. He did not lay the twelve old findings across the two new ones to make a row come out even, for a row made even by a hand that has no key is made even on paper and paper is not a payer; the key had gone up in a coat pocket that never comes back down and there was no key under any roof and none in his hand. Berr's low saying about kindness entered stayed on a place of its own with a finger between it and the twelve findings and another between it and the two readings.

The bell went four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the strokes striking through the morning and the afternoon and longer than a church bell. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, its gap not folded into \lore{the carried fire}'s four and a gap under the floor, into the boards' four and four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not, or into the readings behind one unsigned figure; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and \place{the Drawing house}'s small flat knock outside on its own place, and neither the knock nor a told gap was made a seventh hearing.

Hessa Rale went up the clay road toward Greybank before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was, the second strap let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud unbrushed on the shortened skirt. The small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist came first, her hand to the slate before her eyes to the crowd. To a woman at the rail who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it: \emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which}; and to the one who wanted more, \emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk}. She gave no figure, read nothing aloud and did not look into the basket, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. This second arithmetic of the year did not make her a reader; her eighth record-first hand stayed eight with no tenth, joined to none.

Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing, where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called, his cuff grey from washing, his slate turned inward and his hand to the slate before his eyes to the stalls. He was a shape brought up by word and not a sighting from the basket, reported and not witnessed, and nobody under any roof had seen his face; the figure he had entered before he looked up to find the man who gave it stayed on its own place beside the inward slate with a finger between it and the degree of the head toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard's edge, and no man stood under the figure, no room was entered and no reader made. Across the square at the pot board a silent woman turned a cup a quarter and set it back where it stood, the sweetish green of taste at one corner of her mouth staying a colour joined to no ear; at \place{the market's edge} a small shape in cast-offs caught the light on a green sliver and went dull, a shape with no name and no house. Neither was given a loaf, a slate or a sale. There was no eighth of either, and he never called the colour by a woman's name or the shape by a child's.

At the going of the light she carried him home, saying nothing of what had been read over the wicker, up the nineteen steps and never into the Debt Loft, correcting the tilt at every turn of the landing, and set him on the bed under the roof. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold; the thread at his mouth stayed frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread. Garl's twice-mended pot stood on the board with the hairline showing between the older brass staple and the tighter wire, and nobody under the roof had carried the square's figure back to it or brought the price of the dressed grain into the square. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at a market, a shelf, a yard, a basket and a bed and was not touched; a market and a reading are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its twentieth.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing and left every gap a gap: the figure sent twice over the basket and not into it; the man's number that went from fist to mouth to shoulder and arrived nowhere; the kindness said low to the block on a place of its own; the two readings behind one unsigned figure beside the twelve, two and not one account; the bell's gap in its square; the runner's eighth going up the road unread; the clerk's entered figure reported from below the bridge; the fifth shaping found where put; the softening under the river's skin kept as weather; the colour and the shape with no eighth of either; the covered grain read over by a number that did not know its cost. Every one lay where it lay, advanced and not paid, and none stood up and called itself a rule. The district's arithmetic had changed for the second time this year and could not be changed back, and nobody in the square had addressed the boy it would carry.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note under the winter skin. The inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the covering, let it go as the note ended --- neither early nor stayed --- and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Answer four hundred and three came out and the hand came down. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve readings, the first and second of the new count, the printed figure, the fifth shaping, the clerk's entered figure, Tarn's degree, the face at the yard's edge, Jorra's ungiven promise, Tarn's withheld sentence, the softening at the river's skin, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the winter skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers began on the day the month took its second figure, four hundred and three the first of them, and not one was a doctrine. The count came to four hundred and fourteen exact, six months and two hundred and thirty-four days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number and its payer unnamed the same number; Garl's name was unspoken the same number, and the fire and the man stood side by side without becoming one. Nothing was opened, joined, named, called, paid, forgiven or ended; no slate lay open within reach of the basket, no figure was given for the boy, no door was drawn across the tend, and Ilmen 30 stood eight mornings off and was not brought into a day that had earned only its twentieth. Under the exactness, louder, the infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot board's handle, and got none of the three; both were true, the second was louder, neither won, and the quarrel was the work. The crust's corner stood a hair off the wool and the thread was frozen and unwiped; four low pulls breathed below the floor with the fifth unsupplied; the second arithmetic of the year was up the clay road in a locked box with no key in his hand; the bell's gap stayed in its square. The house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place and the fire kept --- forward, into a morning not yet earned and not brought into this one. Count four hundred and fourteen exact, and no number stood up as a threshold.
