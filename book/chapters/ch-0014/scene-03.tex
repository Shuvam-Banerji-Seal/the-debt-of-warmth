\daymark{63}{Silt 4, Y.H. 813}

The wheel had been turning since before he was lifted out of the basket, and \place{the back room} off the shed had kept its finger's width of open all morning. \person{Jorra Vail} came through that gap with fine grey slate dust worked into the creases of both forearms past the bend of his little finger, and the dust was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. It lay in the seams of his skin the way \lore{the Ash} lay in a corner nobody swept, grey and fine and particular, and he said nothing about where he had been. Two men had come into that room since first light and spoken low; the boy had heard the sound of them through the wall and had no names to set against the voices, and the house kept no roll in which to enter them. They went out again the way such men go, without being spoken of, and the dust came back into the shed on the wheel-keeper's forearms as though a wind had carried it there.

\person{Corin} lay at the edge of \place{the drying racks} in the basket under the fold, carried level as a pot filled is carried, and he was sixty-three days old. Nobody under the roof gave a figure for the days before the month changed its name, so he kept the count himself, as he had once counted the injured in a field before he put a hand into it, and the count went on and could not be spent. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted; the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. From the low angle he could see the two states of the same forearms: grey dust in the creases this morning, old bone on other mornings, both of them Jorra's, both of them one man's having been in one place or another. He set them on the same tray as two entries and did not let one become an account of the other. He had once read a resident's hands before the resident opened his mouth --- a smear at the cuff, a habit of wiping on a coat tail, the thing a man had been holding a moment before he was asked --- and knew that hands told the truth about a day that a face would arrange differently. The question of which slate had been broken stayed where it was. The question of whether it had been a birth-slate stayed beside it. Neither was asked. Questions could last for years in this house; they had lasted for years already.

Under the lowest rack was the space where whole slate should have stood. The yard went around it with pots and buckets and brooms, around it as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over, and entered it into nothing. Two years of broken slates lay buried there and no slate above ground gave them back. The boy watched the going-around from under the fold and kept the space as an empty place on its own tray, apart from the dusty forearms and apart from the clean ones, and wrote no cause between them. A repair that pretends the break never happened was the district's ordinary kindness, and it had a shape a man could learn to see: the stool shifted an inch, the broom stroke shortened, the word that stopped before it reached the thing it named.

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} Jorra spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands,” he said, in the flat voice that went to the clay and the iron before it went to a person. “It cares if they're steady.” He set a pot on the wheel and told it, “You'll do,” with no welcome in the sentence and no unkindness either, the way a man might tell a tool it would serve. The wheel took up its rhythm again, damp clay and the sweetish edge of lead glaze coming off it on a bad wind from \place{High Kiln}, and the house went the way a house goes when someone who knows what he is doing has begun to do it.

The boy knew a thing about that wheel which nobody had told him and which he could not have said aloud. The full note was in a Vail pot before it left Jorra's hands; he had heard it through the wall on mornings when no one tapped anything, and Jorra, who could feel the note in the turning clay before it was a sound, would not have said so in front of a kiln-master. A kiln-master would have called it early and made something of it, and a thing made something of in this district acquired a figure, and the figure went into a slate. So the knowing stayed shut in the wheel-keeper as it stayed shut in the boy, two keepings under one roof with no word between them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six: five from four sources and one out of a person, filed beside and never among, and no seventh came into the shed that morning. Knowing what the pot would give before it gave it had bought him nothing he could lift a hand to. He kept Jorra's knowing beside his own on a place where neither touched the other, as he kept the dust beside the slip and the clean dark line beside the new grey.

\person[Sella Vail]{Sella} had not finished telling him the fire was low. “The fire's low, Jorra. Don't set that wet --- ” The bent finger went in ahead of his hand at the damper and moved it, and the end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him. She shut her mouth on the instruction and gave the rest of it to the clay. The household straightened around his silence without an order being given, the way a room straightens when a man who has been patient has stopped being patient and nobody wishes to find out what he will do next. Iron took the bend of the finger first, then the whole hand; the boy watched the order of it from under the fold, the way he had once watched which instrument went into a field before the hand went in after it. A man's anger in this house did not raise its voice. It moved a damper a half-turn early and let the sentence go where it would.

\person{Tarn} came to the shed end with his broom and stood there a moment. \lore{the Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line where the ridge had turned its corner, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light the morning had and gave it back. He said “Yes, house” to whatever the household had said to him, and did not look at the dusty forearms and did not look into the basket, and went back to his sweeping. He had once been sent for a collector and had come back without one, and the thing he carried for two years was the sentence Sella had not said. Two ears kept the same silence under the same roof. The loan that could hear was still a loan that could be called, and the sentence that would call it lay where it had always lain, unspoken by the man who owned the ear and unspoken by the man who owned the house's account. The boy set Tarn's not-looking on a tray of its own beside Jorra's not-saying and let the two lie near without touching.

Jorra did not say the back room was cold. He had said it once, years ago, to nobody in particular, shaped so that nobody could take it further, and Sella had shifted the damper instead of answering, and the house had learned from that hour what it was allowed to hear. He did not look toward the bed where the foundling lay and did not look toward the basket. *He's not Vail* stayed sealed as tightly as a lid; it had been sealed on the hour it was spoken and nothing in three years had lifted it, not the taking in, not the bed under the roof, not the wheel turning with a boy who was not the wheel-keeper's to keep. The promise of a slate with the foundling's name on it, made to the men who came into the room and spoke low, stayed where it had always stayed, ungiven and not withdrawn. A boy with no house-name was a coat anybody could wear; he had said as much in another room and had not said it here. The nearer the wheel's hollow seat came to being filled by his own weight, the tighter the sentence about the foundling closed, until it held more than a name could have held and would not let go. The carried boy went on under the roof unwritten, and the house went on around him as though that were the whole of the arrangement, which it was, and as though it cost nothing, which it did.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the day went the way the days went. Sella told the fire it was the thaw and fed it from the house's dish. She gave every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, and said *the house* where another woman would have said I. There had been no opening that day and \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat stayed where it hung. \person{Garl Vail} was kept as he had been kept for two years: the salt-stiff coat holding a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the hollow seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year lean that nobody filled, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner and been burned and called dealt with, and the new hairline of grey that had gathered beside it and lay there still, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw. His proverb about leaving the crack showing went to a pot with no crack in it, said over the corner where the repair had been rubbed out, and cost more now than it had when he was alive to say it. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came.

The body went on with its small humiliations while the count went on in the part of him that had nothing to do with the body. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders in a room kept warm by other people's money, and he could not keep a blanket on his own chest. He wanted the crust in the fold and could not reach it, and the empty place beside it was carried through another day without a name; he wanted the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted, and nobody under the roof had any use for it. The adult in him called the wanting a failing of the field and the child in him called it hunger, and the hunger was louder. He had no \lore{Grathic} word for the note in the water behind the racks, and if he had had one he could not have said it.

At dusk Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up the yard at a half-pace from every open slate, and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. One brown-edged crust lay in the fold where two had lain, stiff past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it went through another day without a name. Six hearings stayed six and no seventh had come; three near-full answers stayed advanced and not paid; the word stayed a saying on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; the intervals from Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 stayed objects; the new grey lay beside the clean dark line and was called thaw. He wrote no cause across any of them. A cause written too early was a thing he had once watched a whole institution sign, with the note beside it unread.

The door stood a finger's width open, and through the gap came the low note of the water going over itself behind the racks, and through the floor came the fire's missing breath, and the two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months. What he kept last was the bent finger going in ahead of the hand at the damper, moving the iron before the instruction had finished, the end of it arriving at the pot; and under that, his own hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the warmth and coming down. The count went on.
