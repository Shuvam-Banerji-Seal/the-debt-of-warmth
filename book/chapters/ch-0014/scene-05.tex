\daymark{65}{Silt 6, Y.H. 813}

The slip had stood on \place{the drying racks} all night and came back in the morning dry and pale, the colour of old bone laid out in rows, each strip held at its ends by the same two pegs the year before had used. It was a dry that did not last. Behind the racks \place{the Slip Brook} ran clay-heavy and a little higher than it should, going over itself with the low note that had no word in the district, and the women at the near ends of the rows kept their skirts drawn up and their backs to the water as if a back turned were a wall built. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket at the rack's edge where the boards gave a little lee, turned it with her knee the way she would turn a pot that must not be slopped, and left it level. She gave every instruction to the wool at the boy's ear and to the fire at the house's end of the yard and to the bucket under the bench, and never once to the basket. *Keep it off him,* she said to the wool. *It's the river wind.* There had been no opening that morning, and \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat hung where it hung and was not touched. She said *the house* where another woman would have said I, and went away about the fire.

\person{Corin} was sixty-five days old. Nobody under any roof gave a figure for his days, so he kept the count himself, as he had once counted the injured in a field before he put a hand into it, and the count went on and could not be spent. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted; the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. From the low angle of the basket the row-ends stood above him like the legs of a table in a room he could not get up to, and the dry strips shifted a little with the wind, all of them moving together, none of them going anywhere.

A rack-woman came along the near row with a pot in her hands and set it down out of her own shadow, on the bare board where the light fell, the way a woman sets a thing down when she means to come back for it and not before. She did not knock it. No knuckle went to its belly, no thumb tested the rim, nobody asked it anything. She turned to the row-end and went to work there with her back to it.

And the pot answered.

It gave a note nearly full, with air under it and a want in the note. Not the flat little knock of a vessel cracked all the way through, not the full clear answer of a sound one, but something with its hand half-open: almost the thing, reaching for the thing, the space where the fullness should have been kept inside the sound itself. The woman did not turn. The strip she had in her hands went on bending to her thumb. The note went on standing in the yard with nobody's knuckle under it, as a smell stands in a room after the person who made it has gone out.

The boy filed it beside the two answers from Ilmen 6 and Ilmen 23. That made three. All three were advanced and not paid. *Nearly* and *full* were still two sounds, and he had no hand to tap the pot and ask it to speak again, and no word in \lore{Grathic} for the space between them; he had the two sounds and the space and nothing that could be done with any of them. He had spent twelve years being paid to tell a true answer from a false one, and here was a pot that did neither and both, under a rack where nobody had asked it a question. \lore{The full note} had six hearings and six stayed six: five from four sources, the cup across the birth room, the pot through the wheel-shed wall, the district's own slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at the Drawing, and the sixth small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them, from a person and not a seventh. This was not a hearing. It went on the near-full tray and stayed there.

The three intervals from Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25 stayed objects on the place where he kept such objects, each with the shape of a sound that had not quite come, the third of them still at the empty spot in the yard where a pot had cracked and been swept around. They were not the near-full answers and the near-full answers were not hearings, and the hearing tray touched neither. He did not let one tray touch another. A cause written across three likenesses was a thing he had once watched a whole institution sign, and the note in the file beside it had gone unread; he had kept the lesson and had no file of his own to put it in, only the trays, and the keeping of them was the only work available to a body that could not lift a hand.

\person{Tarn} came up the yard with the broom under his arm and set to sweeping the trodden ash in front of the rack-ends. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the house corner, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His head went a degree toward the pot. It went no further. The broom went on over the ash, and he said nothing about the note and did not once look toward the basket at the rack's edge.

“Yes, house,” he said to the ground. “I'll sweep it. I swept it this morning. I'll sweep it again.”

Six of the seven years were counted and the seventh was held back, the one entry in this house the house could not check. He had heard the note as the boy had heard it, and had put his head a degree toward it, and had gone back to his broom; the boy kept the turning on the place beside his own hearing and did not join the two. Two ears under one roof, one of them lent and one of them owned, and a loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. Neither man had ever said the sentence. The rack-woman went on bending her strip. The pot stood where it had been set, and nobody tapped it.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the afternoon went the way afternoons went. Sella came back with the basket level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, carried at a half-pace from every open slate, and set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without looking at his face. She told the fire it was the thaw and fed it from the house's dish, and gave every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket, saying *the house* where another woman would say I. There had been no opening, and the mark was not touched. The clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner lay where it had lain for a year, and the new hairline of grey beside it lay there still, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw. \person{Garl Vail} was kept by his salt-stiff coat on its peg, his hollow seat, his proverb said to the fire and never to the basket, and the debt against the firing of tiles that lay where it lay and was neither paid nor settled nor done with.

The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came. The boards kept the same count above as below, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two different places under one roof. The house kept its days badly in loaves and glances and a space at the end of the row where a loaf should have stood, and nobody called the space a number; the boy kept his own number exactly and could spend none of it. The woman who kept this house had gone another year around Garl's debt without calling it and without paying it, and the boy had learned by now that a debt left lying was not the same as a debt forgiven; it was only a debt nobody had picked up. What a house could not measure it called weather or mercy, and went home warm on the word.

He wanted the crust in the fold and could not reach it. One brown-edged crust lay where two had lain, stiff past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it went through another day without a name. The adult in him called the wanting a failing of the field and the child in him called it hunger, and the hunger was louder. He had once put a chest back together with his hands under lights that somebody else paid for; here the wool slid off his own chest and the cold came in at cheek, temple, wrist, and the thing he most wanted was an absence in a fold of cloth two inches from his ear. A man can be the only adult in a room and have the hands of an infant, and the joke of it had stopped being funny some fifty days ago, though he had never found anybody to tell it to who would know what it meant.

The pot on the rack had answered nearly full under nobody's knuckle, and that was all it had done. Nobody had tapped it. No word in Grathic had been found for *nearly*, and if there had been, no mouth under this roof would have put it to the sound. The ear that could hold six hearings from four sources and one out of a person, that could keep a flat note and a full note and the air under a nearly-full one on three separate trays without letting them touch, had bought him exactly what it had bought him on Ilmen 6 and Ilmen 23: the keeping of an object he could not lift a hand to. Almost was its own kind of answer. He set the third almost beside the first two and wrote no cause across any of them. The sealed room eighteen leagues east, the told bell in the dark, the heard bell in the square, the early note under the boards, the kiln's gap beneath him, the word on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both --- all of them stayed where they were, and none had acquired a cause by being laid near another. Six hearings. Three near-full answers. One crust. An empty place without a name. The count went on and bought nothing.

What he kept last, as the light went off the boards and the river's low note came through the finger's width of the door, was the pot standing on the rack in the yard beyond the wall with its nearly-full note under nobody's knuckle, the rack-woman gone to the other end of the row and the ash already trodden over the place where Tarn had swept. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward it and came down.
