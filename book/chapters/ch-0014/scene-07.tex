\daymark{67}{Silt 8, Y.H. 813}

First light came thin and the colour of dishwater over \place{the yard well}, and the stone lip of it was wet where the day before it had been dry. Mud had got into the rope. It was ground into the twist of it, the colour of old rope gone dark, and it came up under the fingernails of every woman who drew and went back down again with the bucket, so that the well drew mud out of itself and drank it again in the same motion. \person{Corin} was carried down in the basket under the fold and set in the little roof's thin shade, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped, where the shadow of the well-house lay across him and the draught went the other way. \person{Sella Vail} turned the basket with her knee until his face was out of the wind, then gave every instruction to the bucket and the wool and the fire at the far end of \place{the Clay Yard}, and never once to the basket. “There's mud in it,” she said to the bucket. “Rinse it. Keep it off him,” she said to the wool, “it's the river wind.” There had been no opening that morning, and \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat hung where it hung and was not touched. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and went to the bucket-round with her burn shut on nothing.

The women spoke of the skin on the water to the water and to the bucket and never to the boy under the fold. One of them, letting the bucket down on a rope that had mud in it to the last strand, said the skin had thickened overnight and would hold a poured spoon. Another said it would not hold a spoon and came up anyway, and the two of them went on with the drawing as if the thing had been settled. A man come back from looking at the ice stood with his hands in his armpits and told it to the boards and to the well-roof and to whoever stood within hearing, the way a man says a thing he does not want owned.

“It's honeyed all through,” he said. “Not just at the edge. All through. You could cut it and find it yellow in the middle.”

“Weather,” a woman said from the tubs, and the man nodded as though she had given him an answer, and went off toward the road.

Nobody joined it to any house. The boy lay under the fold and took the words in as he took everything: as objects to be set down, not as a cause to be written across anything. \emph{Honeyed} was a word the district had had for a month for the taste in the water and the colour at the edge of the ice, and now the same word had gone all the way through the sheet and meant the same thing it had meant before, which was nothing a person had to own. A man had said it to the boards. The boards had no house in them. He set the saying down on the place where he kept the early note and the told bell and the heard bell, and he wrote no cause across any of them, and the word bought nobody a figure.

The argument went the way it always went. A man come up from the brook said the month had come early, meaning the thaw, meaning the levy that fell in it, meaning the figure that nobody said aloud; and a woman at the tubs said it came as it always came. Neither gave a number. The man said the ice had gone soft before its hour and the woman said it had not, and both of them wanted the late one in the end, because a late thaw puts off the paying, and they went home over it warm. The boy had learned what that warmth was for. It was not agreement. It was permission to stop looking.

He lay in the well's thin shade and kept the count. Sixty-seven days. The number sat in him with the weight of a small stone he could neither set down nor lift, and he had no one to give it to and no word in \lore{Grathic} in which to give it even if he had. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The bucket stood on the wall of the well within a hand's reach of him --- a thing with water in it, a thing with a lip and a rope --- and he wanted the bucket and wanted the brown crust in the fold beside his ear, and he could reach neither. He had once stood at the head of a table and decided which bodies had already gone beyond help. Here he lay in the shade of a well and could not lift a hand two inches to the water.

The adult in him called the wanting a failing of the field and read the cold in his own wrists the way he had once read it in somebody else's, the small vessel going at the extremities before the core admitted to it, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of any warming. The child in him was hungry and cold and wanted the breast. Both were true and the second was louder.

\scenebreak

Later Sella carried him out beyond \place{the drying racks} to the edge of \place{the Ilm} and set the basket where the boards gave a lee out of the draught, turning it with her knee the way she would turn a pot so the light fell where she wanted it. The racks stood behind him with slip drying on them in the colours of old bone, and the Slip Brook went clay-heavy somewhere out of sight, and the river was in front of him under a skin so thin that the dark current showed through it in veins. He had never seen the current show through before except as a rumour in words. Now it lay under him in long dark lines that moved without any wind moving them, the way a bruise moves under thin skin when the light changes, and the ice above it was not white any more but the colour of grey slip-water standing in a shallow dish. The man's word came back to him with the sight of it. Honeyed all through. Not just at the edge. There was no edge to be seen on the water in front of him, only the veins going under and the skin over them, and the skin was thinner here than it had been three doors down at the well.

More hairlines had run into the ice since the last time he was set here. They came in from the bank and went a little way and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the open water, every one of them stopping at the same distance as though a hand had drawn them and lifted before the last stroke. He kept them where he kept the other stopping hairlines, on a place of their own, and did not let them touch the widening of the day before or the honeyed-all-through saying or the early note out of the stone under the falls. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six: five from four sources and one out of a person, filed beside the five and never among them, and no seventh had come. Three answers stood on the near-full tray with air under the note and a want in it, advanced and not paid, and nobody had tapped a pot to ask any of them to speak again. The clerk was still set at \place{the Fishstairs} with a slate for a measure nobody had called, and the weak board in \place{the plank walk} was still there and trodden by nobody. A weak board was a gap in a place that would one day have to carry weight; a missing step was a gap in a flight of stairs; a hairline stopping short was a gap in a sheet of ice. Three gaps in three places, kept apart. He set the widening hairlines beside them and still let nothing touch. A likeness was not a cause. He knew what a cause written too early would do to four honest witnesses, and he wrote none across any of them.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the afternoon went its ordinary way. There had been no opening and the mark was not touched. Sella fed the fire from the house's dish and told it it was the thaw, and gave every instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals. “The house keeps it.” \person{Garl Vail} was kept by his salt-stiff coat on its peg, his hollow seat, his two lines in the corner, and the proverb said to the pot and not to a person; the debt against a firing of tiles lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with. In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner for a year lay as it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey beside it lay there still, finer than the old line, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes. A line left where it was was still a line kept, and a new line left beside it was kept the same way, and the boy set the two lines in two places and did not let one become the other.

The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came. Above it the boards kept a count of their own, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two different places under one roof. The told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, given in a dark room where no fire was lit and taken on trust; the heard bell at \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap, under which nobody crossed himself; the early note out of the stone under the falls; the kiln's gap through the floor. Four low sounds in four places, kept apart for two months. He had kept them apart and he kept them apart now, with the river's own note coming up through the boards and called nothing, because to call it anything would have been to write a cause across four separate places. One crust lay in the fold. The empty place beside it went through another day without a name.

\scenebreak

At dusk Sella carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at a half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without looking at his face. One brown-edged crust lay where two had lain, stiff past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it went through the last of the day without a name. He wanted the crust and wanted what had been taken out of the fold and got neither; the hunger was louder and the older man's sorrow had nowhere in this house to put its hands, so it went where all of it went, into the counting. He was sixty-seven days old and nobody under any roof gave a figure for it, so he kept it himself. Sixty-seven. The count went on and could not be spent.

The wool had ridden back from his wrist and the cold found the place. He wanted to pull it up and could not. The dry crust pressed at his ear with the stubbornness of a thing put there by somebody else and left to stay until somebody else decided otherwise. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and he could not keep a blanket on. There was a dryness in the small of his back that had nothing to do with the cold and everything to do with the day, and he entered it nowhere, because there was no slate in this house that would take it and no hand of his own to put it on one.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the low note of the river moving under the ice, nearer than it had been at the well and nearer than it had been under the boards in the afternoon, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap. The two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months, as the told bell and the heard bell and the early note and the kiln's gap had kept theirs. The river had said its word to the boards in the yard that morning and the word was honeyed all through, and the word had not crossed the gap into this room and would not have meant the room if it had. The ice was thinner beyond the racks than it was three doors down. The hairlines had run in further and stopped where they stopped. The new grey lay beside the old line and neither was swept. The clerk was still set at the Fishstairs and the weak board still lay in the plank walk and no collector came up the road. None of it had been joined to any house, and this house was not joined to it, and under the roof the fire kept its own account and the door gap kept another.

The count crossed toward Silt 9 unspent. Nothing had been joined, nothing paid, nothing explained, nothing ended. The low note came up through the boards beneath his basket, and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down.
