\daymark{62}{Silt 3, Y.H. 813}

Before light came up over the slip pits \person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill and told the empty yard where it lay. “It's back. You can check.” He said it to the sill and the boards and whatever part of the yard might be thought to answer, and nothing answered, as nothing had answered for six years. The knife lay with its handle uppermost, the mark on it worn almost flat by a thumb that had scraped \lore{the Ash} out of the same creases every morning of a term no man had asked him to keep. A man who owned nothing should know where the small things are, and Tarn knew where this one was even in the dark, with his eyes shut in the loft above the shed. Before he swept he told the kindling what he told it every morning: six were counted, and the next he did not count.

He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to the little light there was and gave it back. The ring had sat in his skin since Y.H. 806, when the iron went on and he was bound to the house, and the place under it held what light the morning had the way a cup holds a little water. Six years. The seventh was nearly gone and he did not count it. It was the one entry in this house the house could not check, and he kept it where he kept the small knife, in a place nobody entered. He said *I owe* where another man would have said *I am*, and said it to the floor, and the floor took it as it took everything else.

\person{Corin} came into \place{the Clay Yard} in the basket under the fold at the half-pace \person[Sella Vail]{Sella} kept from every open slate, carried level as a pot filled is carried. He was sixty-two days old. Two months and two days, and nobody under the roof gave a figure for the days before the month changed its name, so he kept the count himself, as he had once counted the injured in a field before he put a hand into it. The count went on and could not be spent. Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted; the thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

The yard argued the thaw as it argued most things, standing over the pots with their sleeves to the elbow. One woman wanted it early and another wanted it late, and both said it as though the weather had been asked and had given a proper answer. The first said it would break before its time, as it had the year before; the second said it would hold. Neither gave a figure for what had risen behind them, because the yard had one word for a thing a person could go home warm on, and the word was weather.

“Yes, house,” Tarn said to the first, and “Yes, house,” to the second, and agreed so entirely that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot. He went on wanting the late one all the same. A late thaw put off the paying, and a man who has spent six years putting off a payment knows the exact weight of each day's reprieve. The wanting had an edge to it this morning. \place{the Slip Brook} ran higher behind \place{the drying racks} than it had run yesterday, brown water gritted with clay, dragging at the racks as though the boards were being asked to carry more than they had been made for, and the water had a note in it that went over itself without stopping. The paying came with the water whether a man wanted it early or late. Tarn swept toward the racks with his head down and said nothing about the note, and when Sella told him he had done well he stopped altogether for two breaths before he said “Yes, house” and went on.

By mid-morning the yard was laying wet pots along the boards and turning each one a quarter turn so the slip would not run to one side, and the boy lay at the rack's edge where the basket had been set out of the draught. Tarn's broom went over the trodden ash in long level strokes. At the place where a pot had cracked all the way through on Stone 25 and been called a pot gone wrong and swept around, the broom checked half a stroke. The yard had gone around the place ever since and entered it into nothing; the small flat knock had been a sound for one breath and an empty spot in the boards afterward, and Tarn swept the empty spot as he swept the rest. It checked and went on. He did not turn his head. He did not name the small flat sound the pot had made two months before, and he did not supply a \lore{Grathic} word for it, and he said nothing at all to the boards.

The boy kept that check as a fact on a place of its own. It was the third interval, and it lay on its own tray with the intervals of Stone 13 and Stone 17 --- objects, not hearings, silences where a note had been expected and had not come --- kept apart from the tray of near-full answers and apart again from the tray of hearings. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot through a shed wall, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, a man giving at the Drawing; and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman the brook had taken, from a person, filed beside the five and never among them. No seventh came into the yard that morning. He did not call the cracked-place check a hearing, and he did not call Tarn's silence an answer, and he did not let either tray touch another.

A vessel gave its note past the yard door as a woman carried it by, and Tarn's head did not turn. It was the same note he had heard through the wall on other mornings and had never called a note; the woman went on toward the racks without knowing she had carried a thing worth more than a day's wage past the only two ears under the roof that would have known what it was. The boy watched the not-turning from under the fold. Two ears kept the same silence under one roof, and the loan that could hear stayed uncalled; the sentence that would have called it lay under the day where it had always lain, unspoken by the man who owned the ear and unspoken by the man who owned the house's account. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called, and neither man said it, and the yard went on turning its pots as though nothing had been carried past the door at all.

\person{Jorra Vail} came up from the wheel-shed with slip dried to the colour of old bone on both forearms and no grey worked past the bend of his little finger, and went to the damper with that bent finger ahead of his hand. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low; the end of her sentence arrived at the pot instead of at him, and the household straightened around his silence without an order. He told the wheel it did not care whose hands worked it, only if they were steady, and told a pot it would do, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He did not look toward the basket.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the day went the way the days went. Sella fed the fire from the house's dish and told it it was the thaw. She gave every instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, and said *the house* where another woman would have said I. \person{Garl Vail} was kept as he had been kept for two years: the salt-stiff coat holding a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, the hollow seat in the shed keeping its twenty-year lean that nobody filled, the clean dark line where the Ash ridge had turned its corner and been burned and called dealt with, and the new hairline of grey that had gathered beside it and lay there still, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw. A barrow of the same grey had gone out toward \place{the Bone Orchard} at the last market and nobody looked twice, and the clean dark line was not swept, because a line left in place is still a line kept. His proverb about leaving the crack showing went to a pot with no crack in it, said over the corner where the repair had been rubbed out, and cost more now than it had when he was alive to say it. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and \lore{the kiln's breath} went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came.

The body went on with its small humiliations while the count went on in the part of him that had nothing to do with the body. A thread of drool froze at his mouth and was not wiped; his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far; cold arrived at cheek and temple and wrist and stayed longest at the wrist. He had once reduced a thousand shoulders in a room kept warm by other people's money, and he could not keep a blanket on his own chest. He had wanted the breast in the first days and wanted the crust now, and the two wants were the same want wearing different clothes; the adult in him called it a failing of the field and the child in him called it hunger, and the hunger was louder. He wanted the crust in the fold and could not reach it, and he wanted the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted, and nobody under the roof had any use for it. He had no word in the district's tongue for the note in the water, and if he had had one he could not have said it.

At dusk Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up the yard at a half-pace from every open slate, and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right without looking at his face. One brown-edged crust lay in the fold where two had lain, stiff past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it went through another day without a name. There had been no opening that day and \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat stayed where it hung. Six hearings stayed six and no seventh had come; three near-full answers stayed advanced and not paid; the word stayed a saying on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both; the three intervals stayed objects; and the new grey lay beside the clean dark line and was called thaw. He wrote no cause across any of them. A cause written too early was a thing he had once watched a whole institution sign, with the note beside it unread. The door stood a finger's width open, and through the gap came the low note of the water going over itself behind the racks, and through the floor came the fire's missing breath, and the two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months.

Out in the yard Tarn's broom went once more over the place where the pot had cracked and checked half a stroke at the empty air where it had stood, and then went on. Under the roof the boy's hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the warmth and came down. The count went on.
