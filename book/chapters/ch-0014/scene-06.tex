\daymark{66}{Silt 7, Y.H. 813}

Before light the edge of \place{the Ilm} beyond \place{the drying racks} was a place of two cold waters, the river under its skin of ice and the air above it, and neither belonging to the other. \person{Corin} was carried there in the basket under the fold and set down where the boards gave a lee, level as a pot filled that must not be slopped. \person{Sella Vail} turned it with her knee and left it facing the water, then gave every instruction to the wool at his ear and to the fire at the far end of \place{the Clay Yard} and to the bucket under the bench, and never once to the basket. “Keep it off him,” she said to the wool. “It's the river wind.” There had been no opening that morning, and \lore{the widow's mark} at her throat hung where it hung and was not touched. She said *the house* where another woman would have said I, and went away to the tubs.

The yard came awake around the basket in the order it always did: the tubs first, then the racks, then the fire at the house's end drawing smoke that lay low in the cold air and would not rise. There was tallow in the smoke and wet wool everywhere and the flat mineral smell of the cold off the water. The boy took the yard in from the height of a basket, where the tubs stood above him like the legs of a table in a room he could not get up to, and the slapping of the linen went over his head and the cold went around him, and both were ordinary.

Then the note came.

It came first in the teeth. A low vibration in the roots of the teeth and against the hard roof of the mouth, before the ear had anywhere to put it, so that for one breath it was inside his own skull and not out in the water at all; only afterward did the ear find it out there in the dark. \place{the Singing Sill} was giving its note under the fall earlier than it should, the same low thing the ledge had kept all year but a week ahead of its hour. The women at the tubs stopped their beating. One of them, with her sleeves wet to the elbow and the steam going up off the linen, said, “It runs fast under the ice, that water does. A man could hear it run from here.” And then, in the same breath and with the same flat face, “Weather.” She wrung her linen, took up her basket, and went home warm on the word. A second woman, younger, with her hair bound in a wet rag, called after her that it would come early this year. The first woman was already at the gate. “It'll come with the mud,” she said over her shoulder, and that was the whole of what was said about it.

He had learned by now what that word was for. It did not name the water; it named the permission to stop looking at it. A district that had one word for a thing could let a person go home warm without owning a day of it, and there was no word in \lore{Grathic} for the note before its hour and no word for the failure in a stone that sang too soon. He set the early note down in the place where he kept such things.

It was the fourth low sound in four places, and none of the four touched. There was the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, given in a dark room where no fire was lit and taken on trust by everyone who had heard it; there was the heard bell at \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap going over the market, under which nobody crossed himself; there was \lore{the kiln's breath} under his own roof, four, four and a gap coming up through the floor; and now there was this, out of the stone beneath the falls. He had kept the four apart for two months and wrote no cause across any of them, and he kept them apart still. A likeness was not a cause. Four sounds in four places were four sounds, and a man who had once signed a chart that made two different accounts agree knew exactly what a cause written too early would do to four honest witnesses.

Cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody wiped it, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left; his head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. He was sixty-six days old and nobody under any roof gave a figure for it, so he kept the count himself. Sixty-six. The number sat in him with the weight of a stone he could neither set down nor lift.

\scenebreak

Later a man come up from \place{the Weir} stood at the yard's end with salt white at his wrists and river mud dried the colour of old rope on his boots. He did not come to the basket. He set one foot on the bench rail and took it off again without saying why, and said it to the ground and to the boards and to whoever stood within hearing, the way a man says a thing he does not want owned. He had the look of a man who had walked the plank walk in the dark and knew by the give under his boot which boards would hold.

“That board's still there,” he said. “In \place{the plank walk}. Won't carry a man when the river comes up. Nobody's trodden it.”

“And the clerk's still set at \place{the Fishstairs},” a woman said from the tubs.

“For a measure nobody called,” the man said. “Cuff gone soft with the washing. Hand to his slate before his eyes go to the stalls. Nobody's taken him off.”

“Weather,” the woman said, and the man nodded as if she had settled it. A man at the racks asked what measure it was they had come to take, and the man from the Weir said there wasn't one, and the man at the racks said weather, and that was the end of the asking. The two men went off toward the road with their hands in their armpits.

The boy set the board beside the missing step at the Fishstairs and the hairlines that ran into the ice and stopped a thumb's breadth short of the edge, and he set the new grey in the Vail corner beside those, and let none of them touch. A weak board was a gap kept in a place that would one day have to carry weight. A missing step was a gap in a flight of stairs. A hairline stopping short was a gap in a sheet of ice. Three gaps in three places, heard-of and not trodden, entered in no account he could reach. The clerk's order of reaching went on its own place beside the other record-first hands he had kept: the midwife who went to her slate before she turned to a child, a neighbour at a well, a man behind a Court table, a man who priced him over a basket, a Thane passing a finger over a figure, a woman moving eel-bones while she decided whether to lie. He did not join the clerk to any of them. Six hands were six hands, and a hand going to a slate before a person was the oldest grammar in the district, spoken by people who would have been shocked to be told they had said anything at all.

He kept the count exactly as he had kept it for two months. Six hearings of \lore{the full note}: five from four sources, the cup across the birth room, the pot through the wheel-shed wall, the district's own slate twice at the Cross, a man giving at the Drawing, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed beside the five and never among them, from a person and not a seventh. No seventh had come. Three answers stood on the near-full tray, advanced and not paid, each with air under the note and a want in it, and nobody had tapped a pot to ask any of them to speak again. The word lay on its own tray with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and the word was not spoken in this yard and would not be spoken under this roof. An almost was still an answer of a kind, and the third one went where the first two went, on a tray with no hand to lift it; the ear that could keep a flat note and a full note and the air under a nearly-full one in three places without letting them touch had bought him exactly what it had bought him on Ilmen 6 and Ilmen 23 and again yesterday: the keeping of an object he could not raise a finger to.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the afternoon went its ordinary way. There had been no opening, and the mark was not touched. Sella fed the fire from the house's dish and told it it was the thaw, and gave every instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. “That'll hold,” she said to the coals. “The house keeps it.” \person{Garl Vail} was kept by his salt-stiff coat on its peg, his hollow seat, his proverb said to the pot and not to a person, and the debt against a firing of tiles that lay where it lay, neither paid nor settled nor done with. The carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees, and the kiln's breath went four, four and a gap through the floor, keeping the missing breath as carefully as the four that came.

\scenebreak

At dusk Sella carried him up the yard at a half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without looking at his face. One brown-edged crust lay where two had lain, stiff past smelling of anything, and the empty place beside it went through another day without a name. He wanted the crust and wanted the breast and got neither; the adult in him called the wanting a failing of the field and the child in him called it hunger, and the hunger was louder. The wool had ridden back from his wrist and the cold found the place, and he wanted to pull it up and could not. The dry crust pressed at his ear with the stubbornness of a thing put there by somebody else and left to stay until somebody else decided otherwise. He had once stood at the head of a table and decided which bodies had already gone beyond help. Here he lay under a roof and could not lift a hand to the one thing in the world that was two inches from his ear.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the low note of the river moving under the ice, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap, and the two kept their separate places as they had kept them for two months. In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner lay where it had lain for a year, and the new hairline of grey beside it lay there still, unswept, because the house said it was the thaw and would go when it goes.

The low note came up through the boards beneath his basket, the same low thing the boards had kept all year; and the boards kept a count of their own above it, four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, coming as they did from two different places under one roof. He called the note nothing. To call it anything would be to write a cause across four separate places: the told bell in the dark, the heard bell in the square, the kiln's gap through the floor, the early note out of the stone under the falls. Four, in four places, with no cause written across any of them, and the word that would have joined them left where it lay. The count went on and could not be spent. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the sound and came down.
