## Chronicle

For seven days the Slip Brook went over itself behind the racks with a weight no one could name, and the boy carried under the fold heard the river's own note and could not reach it. He was sixty-one days old on the first morning and sixty-seven on the last; nobody gave a figure, so he kept it himself. The yard gave the rising water one word --- weather --- and went home warm on it. Sella Vail said it was the thaw, gave every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket, and touched the widow's mark only before an opening.

At the Fishstairs Mira Silt answered a question with her own answer, moved eel-bones while deciding and put her thumb to the grey cord when a figure was spoken, showing a second cord twisted twice beneath it, never to Nine Pots or a door, neither knot counted. Berr Clay wiped his thumb twice, said a number goes into a slate, not into a well, and took himself out of his own sentence; the soft-cuffed clerk put hand to slate before eyes to the stalls, silent. Tarn set his knife on its sill, six years counted and the seventh held back, wanting the late thaw, because it puts off the paying; his broom checked where a pot had cracked, and his head did not turn at a vessel's note.

Jorra Vail came from the room off the wheel-shed with slate dust past his bent finger, moved the damper ahead of Sella's sentence and did not look toward the bed. Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates writing-down before turning to the fold, touched the tin in her bodice unopened, crossed two fingers at the flower and covered the sign with the date she had given Dessa, saying warm and breathing, exact and not true; then she wrote what she would not leave lying and turned the slate toward a floorboard in a house the boy could not name. Five pages became six, read by nobody.

A pot in the yard answered nearly full under nobody's knuckle, a third answer beside Ilmen 6 and Ilmen 23; six hearings stayed six, five from four sources and one from a person, no seventh. The Singing Sill sang early; a woman who said water ran fast under the ice said weather and went home. Word came that the weak board was trodden by nobody and the clerk still set. On the last day the ice was honeyed all through, the current showed in veins, mud in the rope; he wanted the bucket and crust and could reach neither. At dusk Sella set him on his bed, one crust in the fold and the empty place unnamed; the door stood a finger's width open, the river's note through the gap and the fire's gap through the floor kept apart. Nothing joined, paid or explained. His hand went up an inch toward the water and came down.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment / day and time.** Dusk of Day 67, Silt 8, Y.H. 813 (Eighthday). The boy lies on his bed under the Vail kiln-house roof, not in the Debt Loft; Sella has carried him up at a half-pace from every open slate and put the fold right without looking at his face. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low note comes through the gap, nearer than at the well or under the boards in the afternoon, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor, the two sounds still in separate places. One brown-edged crust lies in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down. Only Sella is with him; no one else is in the room. Nothing joined, paid, explained or ended. The count crosses toward Silt 9 unspent.
- **Where everyone is / condition.** Corin, sixty-seven days old, carried everywhere, mute, cracked, unranked, below Thin. Sella Vail at the fire and the bed, keeper of the house and the fold; no opening in any of the seven days, so the widow's mark was not touched all chapter. Tarn in the yard and shed, knife on its sill, Ash in his knuckles, pale clip-ring at his wrist; six years counted, the seventh nearly gone and still held; his broom checked at the cracked-pot place on Day 62, his head went a degree toward the nearly-full note on Day 65, and he went back to sweeping without naming either. Jorra Vail in the wheel-shed and the room off it (not entered), grey slate dust past the bend of his little finger on Day 63, the bent finger leading at the damper, knowing the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and not saying it; he did not look toward the bed, and *he's not Vail* stays sealed. Alda Reed was in House Silt on Day 64 and is offstage at the close; Berr Clay and Mira Silt appeared only at the Fishstairs on Day 61, with Berr going off toward High Kiln; the soft-cuffed clerk was glimpsed silent on Day 61 and reported still set on Day 66. Garl Vail is absent and kept by his salt-stiff coat, hollow seat, clean dark line and two proverbs. Dessa Silt is dead, twenty-nine mornings gone at the close, uncollected; Orra Weir, Marra, Rusk, Fenna, the Greybank man and all Earth figures are offstage.

The spent crust from Stone 15 lies stiff in the fold; the smoky crust from Day 46 and the coal taken by the brine-gull on Day 45 remain gone and unnamed. **Unchanged:** the pewter tin above the empty bed keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened; Alda's little tin stays inside her bodice, touched and unopened; Berr's box locked with the true deduction inside; Mira's two cords with knots uncounted, never to Nine Pots or a door, her board beside Orra's knot-string and not touching; Sella's private roll unshown; the bench knife on its sill; the charred slate in the kiln ash; the basket and wool are Sella's; the clay jar on the shelf is the house's; Garl's debt against the tiles neither paid, settled nor done with. No money at any point.

Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift. Six hearings of the full note stay six --- five from four sources and the sixth, the flat knock out of Dessa, from a person, filed beside and never among --- and no seventh comes. Three near-full answers now stand on their own tray (Ilmen 6, Ilmen 23 and the Day 65 rack pot), advanced and not paid; the boy has no hand to tap a pot and no Grathic word for *nearly*. Three intervals (Stone 13, Stone 17, Stone 25) stay objects on a separate tray. Water-over-water, the early Sill note, the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's gap each kept their own place; no cause was written across any tray.
- **Facts revealed / lies told / promises and debts.** No secret is revealed to any character. In the world: the Slip Brook runs higher with heavier clay; the Ilm's ice is honeyed all through, its current shows in veins, and hairlines run in and stop a thumb's breadth short of the edge; the well rope has mud in it; the weak board and the uncalled clerk are still set; Alda has written a sixth page. Alda again says *warm and breathing*, exact and not true, and covers her unaware two-finger sign with the date she gave Dessa; the mercy-lie has curdled through to a woman twenty-nine mornings dead and no living person can correct it. Jorra's promise of the foundling's name to the men in the back room is ungiven and not withdrawn; Garl's tile debt stays uncalled; a debt left lying is not a debt forgiven, only a debt nobody has picked up. No one says who took the smoky crust or that the stone is gone.

- **Chapter 15 must not forget.** Day 68 is Silt 9, Ninthday; open before light at the Fishstairs with water over the lower steps and coming up the road in a thin brown sheet, and the boy hearing the note of a whole district under strain --- a vessel of enormous capacity with a slow, certain failure --- filed and not explained, with no word and no hand to point. The weak board and the honeyed ice must arrive at the flood still on separate trays; do not make an accident of the board, do not pay T023 again, do not solve the crust, the stone or the coal, do not name Dessa, open a tin, count a knot, speak forty, join the trays, or let a seventh hearing come. Nothing has been explained or closed; forward, not ended.
