## Chronicle

Sixthday came under the Vail roof with no bell, only the kiln drawing in fours and stopping where the fifth should have stood. Hot clay came first under tallow, wet wool under that, and beneath all three the sour breath of the corner the house called the thaw. The boy lay at the loaf shelf's low angle, two hundred and twenty-five days old by a reckoning no slate had entered, with a crust two inches from his ear. Fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places; agreeing them would want a payer and none stood to any.

The year's first grain was taken out of its covering on the first afternoon. A flail of ash-wood beat the ears open on the flagstones, the chaff went up on the river wind, and the dressed grain was poured back near the hearth under wool. The next mornings spread it thin to dry and gathered it back under the fold. Bundle became dressed grain and gained no number. The yard counted it in glances and handfuls, by the carrying arm's weight that knows weight and not cost; no slate was opened. With the hands dressing the grain the yard went around the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come out, with a look and a wide step and no number.

Sella Vail gave every instruction to wool, fire, pot, broom, water and wind, never to the basket. She set the pot handle two inches beyond the hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, and sent cost-questions to the pot and the weather. A fist's number went back into a sleeve; the figure arrived nowhere. Tarn kept six where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, stopped the ear-sentence at the calling word and held nine with no tenth. Jorra parted dust from slip as clot from vessel, withheld the note with air under it and a want, and grew quieter under the burned-slate word, now told four times; a wrong wind carried a pot saying over the boards and the sweet edge of glaze touched the fold and went out on the wind.

In the small hours he asked his hand six times --- a miss, an inch to the small intake, a coming-down before the counted breath, an inch to the river's low interval, a coming-down short of its aim, and lastly an inch carried through a breath counted in a room that did not touch his, down on the river's next low note: two rhythms across a wall, remembered, not a change made again. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing: six hearings with no seventh, nine stoppings with no tenth, the loan uncalled. He could not lift the crust or speak a believable number. Two hundred and twenty-seven stood exact and unspent; the grain lay covered with no figure over it, and the returning-line place went around still without a number.

Sella has gone to her own bed and left the coals to keep themselves; the two rooms do not touch.
