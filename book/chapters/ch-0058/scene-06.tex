The afternoon thinned without ending. It did not go out so much as wear away, the way a hem wears at the edge of a long walk, the light losing its heat a breath before it lost its hold, and the low angle under \place{the loaf shelf} kept the day longer than the middle of the room, where it had gone flat and grey an hour before. Heat settled in the hollow under the shelf and went behind the pot and came to rest at last in the fold, as though it had been given an order to find the warmest place in the house and stop there and ask nothing about who had paid to put it there. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at that angle with the wool at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from it, and the empty place beside the crust kept its unnamed place, and no third came to stand beside it and make the row come out even.

The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. Nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left; the day's work had gone over him and nobody had thought to wipe it, which was not cruelty and was not neglect but simply the order the house kept, in which a thing not spoken of is a thing not done. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it and could go neither further nor away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and he knew that a vessel which has cooled cannot clot and must not be rubbed back too fast. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in a body of two hundred and twenty-seven days the wanting was the louder, and the knowing sat behind it like a man behind a closed door who has not been told the door is shut.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-seven days. Through the finger's-width door the river held its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged; he had learned that at two paces and had never once been asked for the lesson.

The dressed grain lay under its covering near \place{the hearth}, dressed and stored, and the work of it was finished. It had come off the strips as cut stalks, had gone under wool as a bundle, had been beaten out on the stones and winnowed on the river wind, had lain thin to finish drying, and now lay covered again with the fold set flat; three changes of state and no figure over it anywhere. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight and not what the grain would have cost to buy back; the yard had counted it in glances and handfuls and in the space a hand's scoop left in the fold, and no slate had been opened and no hand entered it. He took it apart in the order he took everything within two paces: what it was, grain; where it had stood, in \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln}; who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof; what it asked of him, nothing. He filed it on the place of its own where the dressed grain already lay and laid nothing across the gap to the fold's one crust, nothing across the two unnamed gaps in the other room, nothing across the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood. Kept things standing in a row are kept things and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its places as the women came off the strips with the last of the day's grain in their arms. It went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, neither swept; around the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood; around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called \lore{weather}; around the field's empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number. A woman crossing toward the racks looked at the returning-line place and stepped wide, and the girl behind her looked and stepped wide, and neither said what the step was for. No name went up over it, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. The absence gathered interest the way a pot gathers heat, by being left where it is, and the boy kept it on the place where it lay and did not make it stand up with the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its empty place, the pan's gone ring or the field's empty place. Seven walked-around things stood in a row and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did. The dressed grain under the wool and the place in the returning line were two keepings, and he did not lay them together or draw a finger between them.

Grain 20 stood two mornings ahead with the second Account-day and the market, and it was not brought early into an afternoon that had its own work. The printed figure stood at one in twelve, unchallenged; the figure read over a basket on Grain 10 was still not the figure paid; the true subtraction lay locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up toward \place{High Kiln}; \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate lay unread up the road toward \place{Greybank}, the writing turned to her sleeve; and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. All of it stood in the boy's file and none of it reached back under the Vail roof. A district's arithmetic can go twice over a basket in the square without once coming into a room where a woman keeps a child under wool and gives nobody a figure.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row the way a woman moves her lips over the days of a week without saying them. The row stood with two gaps: the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word. She gave nobody a figure, though the dressed grain lay stored under wool within her hearing and her hand had turned it. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and no instruction had ever been given about what lay under this board.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in the same motion. A half-beat is an interval and an interval kept is an interval; it is not a promise, not a key, not an opening. On an afternoon of Grain she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-seven days, because an afternoon and a shelf and a basket and a bed are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person{Dessa Silt}'s cost stood unentered, and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder, the seat with its twenty-year lean and hollow on that side, the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it that the house called the thaw and had not swept, a proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and twenty-seven days; the keeping of him was maintenance and not a narrative, and no collector came up \place{the clay road} for the debt. No lesson was made of any of it. A line left where it is is still a line kept.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled.

His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up the clay road on Grain 18. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. He let the three pot sayings stand as sayings and made no fourth likeness of them and no lesson, and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and lost no pot in this yard. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door.

When her voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The old iron took the cold out of the fingertip it touched and gave nothing back, and he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up from the edge of the work at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight of the dressed grain and not what it would have cost to buy it back. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw, around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, around the bare trough and the rack space with its broken birth-slates, around the field's empty place and around the place in the returning line, each with its look and its wide step and no number. She set the basket on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. The dressed grain lay covered near the fire with its figure still nowhere, and the fold lay flat with its one crust and its unnamed empty place, and the two keepings were not laid together.

She went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-seven days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The two rooms did not touch.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of Dessa Silt as the men lifted her, from a person; no seventh had come in two hundred and twenty-seven days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying and the fourth burned-slate telling, all sayings and directions arrived nowhere, no fifth telling of the slate and no third telling of the counting room shut in the war, no fourth likeness made a cause. No pot was lost and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188.

Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered seven mornings ago on places that do not touch: a silent woman's mouth at \place{the pot board} with the sweetish green of \lore{lead glaze} in the crease beside her lip, and a shape in cast-off clothes at the market's edge with a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull. No eighth had come of either, no second look had been joined to the first, no name had been spoken over either, no loaf had passed, no slate had come near and no sale had been made; six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it, and none stood under either. He did not call either by a name.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket on Grain 10 and still not the figure paid. The true subtraction lay locked in Berr Clay's box going up toward High Kiln, Hessa Rale's narrow slate lay up the road unread toward Greybank, and the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at the out-of-line landing below the north bridge for a measure nobody called; none of it reached back under the roof to be opened, copied or paid. The nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 as objects and not a rule, with no payer named to any and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places --- the Day 161 fraction at the damper, the Day 166 beat beneath the shelf and the Tallow 22 half-check on the flagstones --- with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line on the carried man on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth with thirty honest answers on it, the thirtieth gathered tonight. No payer was named to any of them and nothing stood up out of them to call itself a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a key, a power or a rule.

He put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and stayed. In the other room her breaths had gone small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward no opening and had never offered to any ear under this roof; the wall stood between that count and his ear and he did not hear it and was not asked to. He took the hand through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of those counted breaths, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended and brought it down on the river's next low note. Two rhythms held against each other across a wall that does not touch, remembered, not a change made again: not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. He filed it on the sixth place with no payer named and did not spend it and did not explain it. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in a small hand and both are true and neither is a sign; once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The hand lay down again against the fold.

Two hundred and twenty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid for two hundred and twenty-seven days; the seventh year stood at its very end and had not given; the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered and unjoined. Grain 20 stood two mornings ahead, the second Account-day and the market, and was not brought early into these small hours. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, the thread at his mouth was frozen and unwiped, and the dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with the place in the returning line still gone around and still without a number. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof; the hand came down on the river's next low note and the count went forward exact and unspent into Day 228, Grain 19, Ninthday.
