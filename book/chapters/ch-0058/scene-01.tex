\daymark{225}{Grain 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{H}{ot clay} came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the hand outside the wool lying down again against the fold. It lay where a man lays a tool on a bench at the end of the work, and there was no word for the lying-down under this roof and nobody had watched it to give it one. In the small hours just gone the hand had gone up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door, been taken through the small intake and the hold and the letting go of a breath counted in a room that did not touch this one, kept through the little silence after that count had ended, and come down on the river's next low note. Two rhythms held against each other across a wall that does not touch, remembered, not a change made again. Then the hand lay down. That was the whole of it. It was not a sign, not a growth, not a reaching, not a gift, not a power, and it did not stand up to ask for a payer. The room that warmed him and the room he could not enter kept their old wall between them, and the fire went on in the one and the river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

He was two hundred and twenty-five days old and six months and forty-five days, and nobody said either number in his hearing or over his head; a house that kept its days in loaves and glances had no use for a figure that belonged to a child it had taken in without a slate. The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping went on being a stopping with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-five days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and kept its empty place beside it unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. An empty place kept empty is a finding; a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. He had set that rule down on other days and it held on this one without his having to touch it. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under this roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was the louder in a body of two hundred and twenty-five days. He could lift his chin two inches and lift the crust two inches and put the one to the other, and he could do neither. The exactness of it did not make it easier. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone, and now the whole reach of him was two inches of air and the fold's warm smell.

With the light he turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel --- no towel, no instruments, no case, but the order of the laying the whole of the discipline --- and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among: five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt]{Dessa} as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in two hundred and twenty-five days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, a wrong-wind voice having let it stand again on the last morning. None had been promoted into the hearings, no cause stood up, no lesson was made, no name had been given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, no pot had been lost in this yard; and the burned-slate word stood on a place of its own with a hand between the three sayings and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve read twice over a basket on Grain 10 and still not the figure paid, the true subtraction locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box going up toward \place{High Kiln}, \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate gone up unread toward \place{Greybank}, the soft-cuffed clerk still set at the out-of-line landing below \place{the north bridge} for a measure nobody called. Grain 10 stood a morning behind; Grain 20 (Day 229), the second Account-day and the market, stood three mornings ahead and was not brought early. A figure read twice is still not the figure paid, and none of it reached back under the roof to be opened, copied or paid.

The nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 as objects and not a rule, with no payer named to any and no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places --- the Day 161 fraction at \lore{the damper}, the Day 166 beat beneath the shelf, the Tallow 22 half-check on \place{the flagstones} --- with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line on the carried man on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth with twenty-four honest answers on it, each an object and not a power, with no payer named to any.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} came to the fire before she came to the basket. The instruction went first to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket; she said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the boy under the low angle was not addressed at all. “It's the thaw,” she told the fire, and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion. He put it where it belonged, laid nothing across it and named no payer to it. A half-beat is an interval, and an interval kept is an interval; it is not a promise, not a key and not an opening. At a morning and a shelf she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-five days, because a shelf and a morning are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and Dessa's cost was unentered, and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere.

Her lips moved along the row as a woman's lips move over the days of a week, and the row stood with two gaps --- the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word --- and she gave nobody a figure, though the first grain lay covered behind her near the fire. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called \lore{weather} by people who did not taste it, around the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, around the clean dark line and the finer hairline called the thaw, around the field's empty place and the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each had its look and its wide step and its no number: no name, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. Five walked-around things stood in a row and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did. The going-around was a way of keeping, not a way of counting, and the yard had been doing it for years.

The neighbour at the door had a pot hitched against her hip. “What'll the dressing take, now it's out of the stalk?” she asked, and looked past the basket as a woman looks past a thing she has decided not to see.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” She carried the answer away level, so that nothing slopped, and the question went off toward the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere, neither priced, refused nor entered.

A man come off \place{the clay road} with road-dust on his boots brought a number up out of a fist toward \place[the yard well]{the well}, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. It was not a number kept back and not a number paid; it was a number that had not been given, and the boy filed the fist and the sleeve on a place of their own and drew no finger between them and the grain under the fold.

The first grain of the year lay where it had been set after the cutting, near \place{the hearth} under a fold of wool, covered so that a man might step around it without seeing what it was. Nobody had opened a slate over it. The hands that cut it had read its weight in their arms and let their arms keep the weight, a record in muscle that knows what a thing weighs and not what it would take to buy it back; the yard counted it in glances and handfuls the way it counted loaves, and the count arrived nowhere and was entered by nobody. A bundle set down is a pot set down and covered. He took it apart as he took apart everything that came within two paces: what was it, grain; where had it stood, in \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln}; who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof; what did it ask of him, nothing. He filed it on a place of its own and laid nothing across it, not across the two unnamed gaps in the row, not across the one crust in the fold, not across the pan's gone ring or the field's empty place. A harvest housed under a roof is a harvest housed; it is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand beneath it.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail]{Jorra} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them.

The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere --- a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot --- and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. He let the three sayings stand as sayings about a pot with no lesson made in them and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. When her voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The iron was one piece with two bites, and he had no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, and joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and \person{Tarn} did not ask about the room.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} Tarn took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled. No collector came up the clay road on Grain 16.

His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it; the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped, and nothing stood up out of the keeping to call itself a rule. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

So the morning went, and under the low angle he lay two inches short of the pot handle with the cover of the first grain warm behind him and the neighbour's question gone off toward the pot and the weather and the man's fist back in his sleeve. Nothing had been entered and nothing had been joined; the hundred small keepings of the house stood each on its own place and none of them had found a payer. He put the question to his hand, as he had put it on other mornings and as he would put it again, under conditions that had once answered and had as often refused. The hand went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door and came down at once.

It was an honest miss. He filed it on the sixth place beside the twenty-four before it, the twenty-fifth answer, not spent and not explained. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping before the seventh year at its very end, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Two hundred and twenty-five stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid for two hundred and twenty-five days; the seventh year stood at its very end and had not given. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof; the grain lay covered near the hearth with no figure over it, the place in the returning line went around still without a number, and the hand lay down again against the fold.
