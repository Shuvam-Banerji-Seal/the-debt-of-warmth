\daymark{226}{Grain 17, Seventhday, Y.H. 813}

Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the smells arriving in the order a man lays out instruments on a towel before he closes the case. It was a new day and nobody under the roof had said so. He was two hundred and twenty-six days old and six months and forty-six days, and neither number had been spoken in his hearing or over his head; a house that kept its days in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood had no use for a figure that belonged to a child it had taken in without a slate. The first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping went on being a stopping with nobody to put a name to the thing that stood in it. There was no market on Grain 17 and the clay road kept its ordinary quiet; the district's arithmetic was two mornings behind and two mornings ahead and did not come under this roof to be read.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-six days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged; he had learned that at two paces and had never once been asked for the lesson.

The body went on with its humiliations as the body will, without consulting the man inside it. One leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and its empty place beside it remained unnamed, and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under the Vail roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He knew what a cold patient looks like when the grimace of severe cold puts a smile on a face that is not smiling, and he knew that a vessel that has cooled cannot clot and must not be rubbed back to warmth too fast. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was the louder in a body of two hundred and twenty-six days.

Then the morning came on, and with it the second working of the grain.

The dressed grain was taken out of its covering near \place{the hearth} and carried to \place{the flagstones} at the yard's edge and spread thin to finish drying, the way a surgeon spreads a field open under light to see what the dressing has left in it. It had come out of the ear smaller than it had gone in and now it lay in a thin uneven scatter on the stone, grain grit bright in the seams between the flags. A woman turned it with the back of her hand, lifting and letting fall, and her hand read it by weight. She did not count it. Her glance went over it and came back, and her fingers closed on a handful and judged it, and the arm that had carried the bundle up from the worked strips knew the weight of what had been carried and the lighter weight of what was left, and kept both and kept no figure. Grit worked into the creases of her palm and she did not brush it away, and nothing was written down. The chaff had gone off on the river wind the day before and had not come back, and the grain lay thinner than the bundle had lain and was no nearer to a number.

He watched her from the two paces that were the whole of his country, and took the thing apart in the order he took everything apart. It had changed state a second time: cut, then dressed and covered, now spread thin to the sun and the wind. What was it, grain. Where had it stood, in \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln}. Who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof. What did it ask of him, nothing. He filed it on the place of its own where the dressed grain already lay and laid nothing across the gap to the fold's one crust, nothing across the two unnamed gaps in the other room, nothing across the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, nothing across the field's empty place. A keeping is a keeping by being covered, and this one was being kept by being spread; the two were the same grain in two postures and neither had acquired a figure by changing posture. Four absences standing in a row are four, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had found none and had not looked for one.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} a rack woman drew her bucket up with the rope going wet through her fingers, and \lore{slip} had dried pale in the creases of her wrists where the racks had left it, the colour of old bone set into the lines of the skin. She stood a moment with the bucket against her knee and asked \person{Sella Vail} what the storing would take now the grain was under wool and out on the stone.

Sella did not turn from the pot she was scouring. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.” The instruction went to the pot and the weather and away level, and the question went with it and arrived nowhere, and nothing was priced and nothing was entered. The woman took her bucket up and went off with her wrists still pale with slip, carrying the unanswered question the way a woman carries a full bucket, level, so nothing slops.

A man come down \place{the clay road} with the dust of the road on his shoes stopped short of the well and brought a number up out of a fist toward the water, the way a man brings up a coin he has been holding and is not sure he means to spend. He looked at it. He looked at the well and at the woman with the pale wrists and at the pot under Sella's hand, and then he put the number back into his sleeve and went on toward the yard with his fist shut again. The figure arrived nowhere. It had not been paid and it was not a figure kept back for later; it was a number that had come up out of a fist and gone back into a sleeve, and under this roof such a number is entered nowhere and asked about by nobody.

The district's arithmetic went on without them. The printed one in twelve stood unchallenged on the notice where it had stood since the slates opened, and the figure read on Grain 10 over the top of a basket was still not the figure paid, though it had been read twice and the second reading had gone into more ears than the first. \person{Berr Clay}'s true subtraction stayed locked in his box and was going up the road toward \place{High Kiln} with the red cord folded square under his coat; \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate had gone up unread toward \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve; and the soft-cuffed clerk sat still at the out-of-line landing below \place{the north bridge} where a fifth step should have stood, set for a measure nobody had called and entering a number that did not ask to be entered. None of it reached back under the Vail roof. It was the arithmetic of \place{Sarn}, of \place{Sill}, of \place{Grath}, and the boy held it on its own tray as a thing he could not enter and had not been asked to enter. He had seen a thane pass a finger over a figure before he lifted his eyes to a crowd, and a runner settle her strap and turn her wrist before she looked up, and a clerk write a buyer's number down before he looked to find the man who said it; three records going inward are three records going inward, and a row of such hands will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer. He had left no payer standing under any of them.

Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, on places that did not touch. Seven mornings had now gathered them without joining them: the sweetish green of \lore{lead glaze} in the crease beside a silent woman's lip at the pot board, and a green sliver on a string at the throat of a shape in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one. He had given neither a name. He did not call the woman by a name and he did not call the child by a name, and neither name was spoken over either in the telling of them. No eighth green had come and no eighth shape, no second look had been joined to the first, no loaf had passed, no slate had come near, no sale had been made. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had put none under either row.

\scenebreak

The yard went around its places as it went around them every morning. It went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, neither swept; around the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood and stood no longer; around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called \lore{weather} by people who did not taste it; around the field's empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number. A woman crossing toward the racks looked at the returning-line place and stepped wide, and the man behind her looked and stepped wide, and neither said what the step was for; no name went up over it, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. Seven walked-around things stood in a row and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did. The going-around was a way of keeping and not a way of counting, and this yard had been doing it for years without once being asked what it kept.

\scenebreak

Sella stood at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row as a woman's lips move over the days of a week. The row stood with two gaps: the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word. She gave nobody a figure, though the dressed grain lay spread thin on the flagstones within her hearing and her hand had turned it that morning. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A basket set down is a pot set down and left; a shelf with a finger on its front edge is a shelf with a finger on its front edge, and no instruction had ever been given about what lay under it.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-six days. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion. A half-beat is an interval, and an interval kept is an interval; it is not a promise, not a key and not an opening. On a morning of Grain she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-six days, because a morning and a shelf and a basket and a bed are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person{Dessa Silt}'s cost stood unentered, and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere. The dressed grain lay thin on the flagstones behind her and did not become a figure because a woman had moved her lips along a row.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and gave the sill a small flat tap with the flat of his finger as he set it down; \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain, and no collector had come up the clay road on Grain 17. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled.

His chest had been tight since the dark; \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it; the stones kept the place where an older stroke had stopped, and nothing stood up out of the keeping to call itself a rule. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. A wrong wind came up off High Kiln with the sweetish edge of lead glaze at its tail and carried one of the three shapings of the pot saying over the boards --- a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and he let it stand as a saying and made no fourth likeness of it and no lesson, and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and lost no pot in this yard. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere, a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door.

When her voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The old iron took the cold out of the fingertip it touched and gave nothing back, and he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up from the edge of the work at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight of the dressed grain and not what it would have cost to buy it back. She went wide around the clean dark line and the finer grey, around the round ring and the bare trough, around the rack space with its broken birth-slates and the field's empty place, and around the place in the returning line, each with its look and its wide step and no number. She set the basket on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with its figure still nowhere, and the fold lay flat with its one crust and its unnamed empty place, and the two keepings were not laid together.

She went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-six days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. The file lay where it lay, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces: six hearings beside and never among with no seventh, three shapings of the pot saying and the burned-slate word at four, nine stoppings with no tenth and the Seed 16 breath unspent, the held inch on its sixth place, no payer named to any of it. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, gathered to seven mornings and unjoined; the dressed grain lay thin under its keeping and the returning-line place was still gone around with a look and a wide step and no number. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.

He put the question to his hand. There was no count in the room to hold it against and he did not borrow one from the room that did not touch his. The hand went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door, and came down before the counted breath had finished, down short of the letting go and short of the little silence after it. It was an honest miss and he kept it as honestly as a hit: the twenty-seventh answer on the sixth place, filed on its own tray with no payer named to it, not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in a small hand; once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches on the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The hand lay down again against the fold.

The clay jar stood cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand had shut half over nothing without a touch. The dressed grain lay thin on the flagstones under its own keeping, spread to the wind and counted in glances and handfuls and the carrying arm's weight, entered on no slate and given no figure. Two keepings under one roof and no finger of his drawn between them, and no name, no number and no payer standing over either. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof; the count stood two hundred and twenty-six, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath, and went forward into the next morning without a figure laid on it.
