The afternoon came on under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} without a boundary drawn anywhere, the heat settling behind the pot and into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay with one leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from his ear and its empty place beside it still unnamed. No third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had reached the end of an order written out for it; it went no further and it did not go away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was the louder in a body of two hundred and twenty-five days.

Then the grain was taken out of its covering.

Two hands came to it where it lay near \place{the hearth} under its fold of wool and lifted the wool away the way a woman lifts a cloth from a pot she means to look into, and the bundle came up in their arms, cut stalks the colour of old straw, dry enough that the dry smell of them went over the room and stood for a moment over the smell of hot clay and tallow. They carried it out to \place{the flagstones} at the yard's edge. The boy, set at the edge of the work, watched them as he watched everything that came within two paces, which was not far but was the whole of his country.

The flail was two sticks of ash-wood joined by a thong of leather. One of the women took it by the longer end and brought it down on the stalks laid thin on the stone. The sound was dry and small and went up into the yard on the river wind. Again. Again. The ears broke open and the grain came out smaller than it had gone in, and the chaff went up on the wind in a thin brown cloud that went off toward the river and was entered by nobody. Grit got into the creases of the woman's palm and she did not brush it away; her arm knew the weight of the bundle and the lighter weight of what was left on the stone, and the arm kept both and kept no figure. The chaff going up was a thing that had been taken out of the grain and put onto the wind, and neither the taking nor the putting was written down.

When the beating was done the dressed grain was gathered up by the handful and poured back into its cover near the hearth and covered again with the wool. Bundle had become dressed grain and had gained no number. It lay where the bundle had lain, counted in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, entered on no slate. A pot covered is a pot kept, and the keeping of it is the covering and not the count.

He took it apart as he took apart everything within two paces, in the order a surgeon takes apart a wound. What was it, grain. Where had it stood, in \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln}. Who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof. What did it ask of him, nothing. He filed it on a place of its own and laid nothing across it --- not across the two unnamed gaps in the row in the other room, not across the one crust in the fold and the empty place beside it, not across the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, not across the field's empty place. Four absences standing in a row are four, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had found none and had not looked for one. The dressed grain was a keeping and the crust's empty place was a keeping, and the two did not become one keeping because a man had carried them under the same roof.

\scenebreak

With the hands dressing the grain the yard went on going around the empty place in the returning line, the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. A woman crossing toward the racks looked that way and stepped wide, and the man behind her looked and stepped wide, and neither said what the step was for. No name went up over it, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. The place went around with a look and a wide step and no number, and the absence gathered interest the way a coal gathers heat under ash, without anybody leaning in to call it a fire. He had set a rule down on other days and it held on this one: an empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Nothing had come to fill this one. He would not fill it to make the yard's arithmetic tidy, and no fourth likeness stood up out of the grain to make a cause of it.

Around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and the finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, and the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates, and the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called \lore{weather} by people who did not taste it, and the round ring, and the field's empty place, and the place in the returning line --- around all of them the yard went with a look and a wide step. Five walked-around things stood in a row and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did. The going-around was a way of keeping and not a way of counting, and this yard had been doing it for years.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row as a woman's lips move over the days of a week. The row stood with two gaps: the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word. She gave nobody a figure, though the dressed grain lay behind her near the fire and had been beaten out that afternoon within her hearing. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-five days. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of his life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward the bed.

He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion. A half-beat is an interval, and an interval kept is an interval; it is not a promise, not a key and not an opening. At an afternoon and a dusk she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-five days, because an afternoon and a shelf and a basket and a bed are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and Dessa's cost was unentered, and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere. The dressed grain lay under its fold behind her and did not become a figure because a woman had moved her lips along a row.

\scenebreak

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where the weight of a man had once settled and would settle no more. The seat kept its twenty-year lean and hollow on that side, and a body sitting down there would have found the place already made for it, which was a keeping and not an invitation. The clean dark line lay where the ridge had turned and the new finer grey lay beside it unswept, and neither had been touched since the flood went out. The proverb had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out, and nobody had made a lesson of it. The debt against a firing of tiles was circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled than it would have been called, because she could not pay it and could not settle it and could not stop owing it. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and twenty-five days, and the keeping of him was maintenance, not mourning and not a story. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for the tile debt on Grain 16.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before it went to the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled.

His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it; the stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped, and nothing stood up out of the keeping to call itself a rule. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person[Jorra Vail] came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open back room before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. The broken birth-slates lay under the lowest rack where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. He let the three sayings stand as sayings about a pot --- a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, the pot that sang early last month not heard from since, and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw --- and made no fourth likeness of them and no lesson, and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and lost no pot in this yard.

The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of \place{Slate Row} and arrived nowhere --- a direction with no hand at the flame and no figure at its foot --- and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When her voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The iron was one piece with two bites, and he had no word in \lore{Grathic} for both mercies, and joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the clay road from the edge of the work at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight of the bundle and not what it would have cost to buy it back. She went wide around the clean dark line and the finer grey, around the round ring and the bare trough, around the rack space with its broken birth-slates and the field's empty place, and around the place in the returning line, each with its look and its wide step and no number. She set the basket on \place{the bed} under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with the chaff gone into the river wind, and the fold lay flat with its one crust and its unnamed empty place, and the two keepings were not laid together.

She went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-five days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays stay apart. The six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among with the sixth out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted her and no seventh, three near-full answers and three shapings of the pot saying, nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent and the loan uncalled, three held changes on three places with the loft-sill scrape fourth, the pale line fifth and the held inch sixth. Exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, with no payer named to any of it.

He put the question to his hand. It went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door and stayed for the small intake only, and came down before the letting go. He was two hundred and twenty-five days old and six months and forty-five days, told by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; the count stood two hundred and twenty-five, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. It was the twenty-sixth answer on the sixth place, an interval and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Nothing was laid across it and no payer was named to it. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in a small hand; once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches on the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe.

Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with the chaff gone into the river wind and no figure over it. The fold lay flat with its one crust and its empty place unnamed and no third. The yard still went around the place in the returning line with a look and a wide step and no number, no name, no cause, no body and no payment. Two keepings under one roof and a third place gone around, and no finger of his drawn between them. The hand lay down again against the fold.
