The afternoon thinned without going out. It wore at the edges the way wet wool wears thin at the cuffs, the light losing its heat before it lost its hold, and the low angle under \place{the loaf shelf} kept the day longer than the middle of the room, where it had already gone flat and grey. Heat settled in the hollow under the shelf and went behind the pot and came to rest at last in the fold, as if it had been given an order to find the warmest place in the house and stop there. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at that low angle with the wool at his ear and one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from him, and the empty place beside it kept its unnamed place, and no third came to stand beside it and make the row come out even.

The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of an order written out for it and could go neither further nor away. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it, and he knew the order in which a body takes heat back, the small joint first and the core last, and he knew that a vessel which has cooled cannot clot and must not be rubbed back too fast. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in a body of two hundred and twenty-six days the wanting was the louder, and the knowing sat behind it like a man behind a closed door.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the fifth, its payer without a name for two hundred and twenty-six days. Through the finger's-width door the river held its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth should have stood. Fire, river and boards kept three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them. He kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. An empty place kept empty is a finding, and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged; he had learned that at two paces and had never once been asked for the lesson.

The dressed grain was turned once more on \place{the flagstones} before the light went, a woman's hand lifting and letting fall, reading the scatter by weight and not by number. The chaff that had been winnowed out the day before was long gone on the river wind; a little more went up now, light as the dust off a dried pot, and went the same way and was entered by nobody. What was kept was kept by being covered, and the count of it stood nowhere. He watched from the two paces that were the whole of his country and took it apart in the order he took everything within reach: what it was, grain; where it had stood, in \place{the worked strips} east of \place{Low Kiln}; who had paid a figure for it, nobody under any roof; what it asked of him, nothing. He filed it on the place of its own where the dressed grain already lay and laid nothing across the gap to the fold's one crust, nothing across the two unnamed gaps in the other room, nothing across the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood. Kept things standing in a row are kept things and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did.

\scenebreak

A kiln-hand come up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind let one of the three shapings of the pot saying stand over the boards as he passed the yard mouth, where the wind had turned against itself and brought the smell of the upper kilns down into the clay: a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw. He did not stop, and the saying went on with him toward the price of barley and the weather, and nobody under the Vail roof took it up.

The boy took it apart as a surgeon takes apart a wound, in the order the layers lie. A saying is not a finding. It had no owner, no room, no hand at its foot and no figure under it; it had a date in it, the thaw, and dates are what sayings use to stand still. It had been turned three ways already: a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early, the pot that sang early last month has not been heard from since, and this one, gone quiet on every row come the thaw. Three likenesses on one tray, and a fourth would be the one that stood up and called itself a cause. He did not make it. He gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and no pot was lost in this yard, and the saying stayed a saying about a pot. To make a fourth likeness of it would be to find a payer for it, and there was no payer under it, only a wind that had come the wrong way and would go back the way it came.

The burned-slate word came over the boards on the same breath, out of \place{Slate Row}, and went the length of the yard and arrived nowhere. It was the fourth telling of it and no fifth, and it still had no hand at the flame, no room it had stood in, no man who had set it and no reason. The two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and did not become three, and a hand's width lay between the burned slate and them, and he kept the hand there. The word was a direction, and a direction is not a cause. He let it go over the boards as he let the wind go over the fold, and neither stopped.

The yard went around its place again as the women came off the strips with the last of the day's grain in their arms. It went around the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned and the finer hairline of grey beside it that the house called \lore{the thaw}, neither swept; around the rack space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates; around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood; around the bare dark \place{the trough} whose water was called \lore{weather}; around the field's empty place; and around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got its look and its wide step and no number. A woman crossing toward the racks looked at the returning-line place and stepped wide, and the girl behind her looked and stepped wide, and neither said what the step was for. No name went up over it, no cause, no accident, no body, no identification of which hand, no figure and no payment. The absence gathered interest the way a pot gathers heat, by being left where it is, and the boy kept it on the place where it lay and did not make it stand up with the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its empty place, the pan's gone ring or the field's empty place. Seven walked-around things stood in a row and a row is not an account until a payer stands under it, and none did.

The sweetish green edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up on the wrong wind behind the kiln-hand and went over the boards with the barley price, and touched the fold where the boy lay, and went out on the wind that had brought it. \place[the pot board]{The pot board} stood across a square he had never crossed, and a silent woman's mouth there had held the seventh green since Grain's first market. It was a direction arrived nowhere, like the saying, like the burned slate, like the number that had come up out of a fist and gone back into a sleeve. It was not a second edge paid and it did not become a rule. The seventh green stayed a colour on its own place, the sweetish green at the crease beside that woman's lip, and no eighth came to stand beside it, and no second look was joined to the first, and no name was spoken over it. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it; he had put none under it. The taste of it went off the edge of his mouth as quickly as it had come, and left nothing behind but the sweetness of a kiln he had never entered.

\scenebreak

Sella stood at the loaf shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row the way a woman moves her lips over the days of a week without saying them. The row stood with two gaps: the old space at the end and the palm's breadth that had come in years back without a scrape, a crumb, a print or a word. She gave nobody a figure, though the dressed grain lay finished on the flagstones within her hearing and her hand had turned it. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it, the little-door burn opening, and shut half over nothing without a touch, the burn closing on air. Her finger lay on top of the shelf's front edge where any woman in the district could see it lying, and the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, because a woman's back turned is not the same thing as a finger beneath it and the reason stood between the two, unspoken. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and no instruction had ever been given about what lay under this board.

At the hearth she told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. “The house will keep it,” she said, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- the count arriving nowhere as it had arrived nowhere on every day of this boy's life. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and she did not discuss the streak or the winter it had come in. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}.

He lay two inches short of the handle and watched the half-beat go by as a man watches a hand come out of a field before the dressing goes on: a thing done and a thing withheld in one motion. A half-beat is an interval and an interval kept is an interval; it is not a promise, not a key and not an opening. On an afternoon of Grain she did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; it hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since, and there had been no opening in forty-six days, because an afternoon and a shelf and a basket and a bed are not openings. Her private roll stayed unshown and \person{Dessa Silt}'s cost stood unentered, and the cost of the man carried out on Day 188 stood where it had stood, entered nowhere.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story: the salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder, the seat with its twenty-year lean and hollow on that side, the clean dark line where the ridge had turned and the new finer grey beside it that the house called the thaw and had not swept, a proverb gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and twenty-six days; the keeping of him was maintenance and not a narrative, and no collector came up \place{the clay road} for the debt.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed, the instruction going to the tool before the man, and he took it up as a man takes up an instrument already named. “Yes, house,” he said, and told the wood “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He set the small knife at the very edge of its sill, six in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it where it had lain. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name; he filed the stopping as a remembered stopping and not a new one, because he had brought it to that word on other days and the stopping was the same object each time. He counted nine behind it with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. He drew the breath from Seed 16 at the top of the reach and did not spend it, and kept the loan uncalled.

His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went over the flagstones past the shallow bright patch kept since Seed 22 without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. Above the shed the warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which was not forgiving.

\scenebreak

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave them no name and did not say the room was cold; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and no third came. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone.

“It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} the pot would give three turns before it left his hands and the knowing had air under it and a want in it, and he kept it back, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. He let the three pot sayings stand as sayings and made no fourth likeness of them and no lesson, and gave no name to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188, and lost no pot in this yard. The burned-slate word had come over the boards four times out of Slate Row and arrived nowhere, and he grew quieter whenever it went around; the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the quiet going before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door.

When her voice went low at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her hand and held until the sentence reached the pot, then moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again. The old iron took the cold out of the fingertip it touched and gave nothing back, and he joined it to no kiln and entered it in no slate. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed and did not ask about the ear, and Tarn did not ask about the room.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up from the edge of the work at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water. The carrying arm was a record in muscle that remembered the weight of the dressed grain and not what it would have cost to buy it back. She went wide around the clean dark line and the finer grey, around the round ring and the bare trough, around the rack space with its broken birth-slates and the field's empty place, and around the place in the returning line, each with its look and its wide step and no number. She set the basket on the bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen. The dressed grain lay covered near the hearth with its figure still nowhere, and the fold lay flat with its one crust and its unnamed empty place, and the two keepings were not laid together.

She went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth, its payer unnamed for two hundred and twenty-six days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any of them.

He put the question to his hand. There was no count in the room and he did not borrow one from the room that did not touch his. The hand went up an inch toward the note through the finger's-width door and stayed. It stayed for the river's own low interval, the drawing out and the slackening and the little silence before the next note came; there was no intake and hold and letting go to measure it against, only the water going over water and the next low note arriving when it arrived. He brought the hand down on that next low note. It was one rhythm and no second, the twenty-eighth answer on the sixth place, filed on its own tray with no payer named to it, not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the three held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Exactness and humiliation are one motion in a small hand; once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close two inches on the pot handle, lift his chin two inches to the crust or speak a number that anybody under any roof would be asked to believe. The hand lay down again against the fold.

The dressed grain lay spread thin on the flagstones with the chaff gone off on the river wind, and the river's note came through the finger's-width door, and the hand came down on the river's next low note, and nothing stood up out of it to call itself a rule. Fire went on in the room that warmed him and the river in the room he could not enter, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof; the count stood two hundred and twenty-six, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, and the file lay where it lay, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.
