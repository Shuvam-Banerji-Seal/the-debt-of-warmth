The afternoon came on the way afternoons came on under the Vail roof, without a word said over it and without a mark anywhere to show a boundary had been crossed. The light left \place{the pot board} and went onto \place{the boards}; \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, kept its heat in the old way, carefully and with one crack nobody mentioned. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay where the heat came to him sideways, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid at the last answer, and nothing from the morning crossed into the afternoon as a sign or a lesson or a cause. The yard's small sounds took the place of the morning's larger ones, and he took the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather.

A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. The rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp the dry cold puts into hemp. Barley passed from wall to wall between a woman's two hands, and no number came out of it. A loaf went a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure, and nobody put a name beside the place where it stopped.

Triage had been the sorting of a crowded door into those who would live and those who would live only if a hand chose them and those who would die whatever a hand did; here the sorting was quieter and ran the other way, into what a yard would not say. It bought him the order of things and nothing beyond the order of things, and the order of things was the only instrument he had.

He went once around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number: the stone lip of \place{the yard well}, the worn boards, the strip of hard mud, the place where a wheel had stood, the two narrow gaps between the stacked pots, and \place{the returning-line place}, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. A figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood, so he gave the space its width and went on. He did not look twice; looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there.

At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own. He did not lay that ring across the loaf's finger's-width stopping or across the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. The water gave back the weather and kept its print, and the print was not a level anybody had calibrated.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, the one the house called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold, with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years. Beside them the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the skin, and the tang came thinner on that edge. He found the softening where put and called it weather, and did not look twice, and did not make it a sign, a fourth covering, a custom or a payment on a day that had earned only its twenty-ninth. The note came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the covering had no hand under it.

Costs came to the yard as directions and went away as weather, and left no entry anywhere. A woman waiting her turn with a pot against her hip brought a number up out of a shut fist and held it toward the light the way a woman at a market board holds a weight before the weigher calls it; she put the fist toward a sleeve, looked at the figure a breath longer than the light required, shut her fist on it and put it back where it came from, and the number arrived nowhere. He gave the fist no figure and the sleeve no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. The boy took the order of it apart by the place of each part --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat lying in the gap --- and found no intention standing in the gap; a lesser observer would have put one there and called it a reason, and a reason wants a payer where none stood. This was a fresh arrival of the afternoon, on a place of its own, and not the figure that had been offered and taken back at the yard well on a morning three days behind; that one kept its own place and was not offered again and was not this.

A woman with a basket went to a display board and put her hand toward a pot. “What'd you give for it?” she said.

The neighbour at the rail did not look up from her pail. “Ask the pot.”

“I asked you.”

“The pot's got a note. It hasn't got a price.” The neighbour set her pail down and went back to the round, and the woman went off with a scrap of note and no price, and nobody turned twice.

The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at \place{the rack end}. A figure had been read over it in the morning and the figure did not know what the grain cost, and no signature stood on the space between the figure and the price. He set none there. A number counted is a number with air around it, and air buys no warmth.

The things that had been kept kept their places. The offered figure and its return stayed where put on a place of its own beside \person{Ilva}'s ninth record-first hand and the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, with a finger between every pair; Ilva was offstage with her strip of notched birch in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth, not made a reader or a speaker by a reach in an empty yard. The offered figure was not offered again, not spent, not named, not made a second reaching by being remembered, and not joined to the fist that arrived nowhere, the note that was not a price, the asking answered with the weather, the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure reported at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, the second man's turned slate and direction down the north bridge road, the face at the yard's edge or \person{Tarn}'s degree toward the landing. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road} with her eighth hand eighth, and \person{Berr Clay}'s box had gone up the same road locked with the year's subtractions inside and the key in a coat pocket that never came back down; neither supplied a figure, a payer or a payment to any kept thing. The clerk's figure, the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row} and the fifth shaping kept their separate places with fingers between and no witness, reader, sale or tenth hand standing. Three likenesses are three and not one account; an account wants a hand to lay them together and a payer under all of them, and none stood.

Tarn was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill; his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away was the whole of the checking. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. His chest had been tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was. The loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended, and his valuable ear was exposure and not safety; he owned nothing and knew where every small thing was. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it stayed stopped at the word that would call the loan, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth, and the boy found it where put and did not make a habit of the silence.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness, and he knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. \person{Sella Vail} had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without the extra holding of Ash 26, gathered once and not made again. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

At the going of the light she came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as it had at every going of the light behind them. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay, and the coals gave off the dry smell of a fire banked and not forgotten. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A going of the light, a hearth, a shelf, a pot and a basket are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Ninthday that had earned only its twenty-ninth.

She said to the fire, in the tone of a woman who had told the same thing to the same fire for three winters, \emph{The fire doesn't know you're sorry. Feed it}, and put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, and did not look to see whether the basket had heard. She did not say it a second time; a second saying would be a custom and a third a way, and both want a payer where none stood. Her hands went on counting when they had nothing to do --- three spoons, a nail, the small breath a child makes that nobody numbers --- and came back to the hairline at the wire as hands come back to a wound they have dressed.

Then she lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She turned the dressed grain under wool at the rack end with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

At the carrying-up she went up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. She went wide around the seven walked-around places once and not twice, widest around the returning-line place, and set him on his bed under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf, never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

\place[The towel]{The towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and he went over the field by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for, and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside them on its own ground, not made a hearing. The bell heard from \place{the Cross} kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and that gap was not folded into \lore{the carried fire}'s four low pulls and the silence below the boards, or into the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, and nine stoppings with no tenth, and he gave no name to the gap after the ninth.

Answer four hundred and twenty-two came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road, the printed figure, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the winter skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, 415 through 422, two to each of the three days behind and two to this day, and not one was a doctrine. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the two readings across one another to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key to Berr Clay's box came under any roof.

Four hundred and twenty-three exact, six months and two hundred and forty-three days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was still unpaid and Garl's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one. No collector came up or went down the clay road; no sixth shaping, seventh hearing, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic or eighth opening stood on a day that had earned only its twenty-ninth, and no slate was opened within reach of the basket. Ilmen 30 waited one morning off as a date and was not brought into this day as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer. Ilva, Hessa Rale, the soft-cuffed clerk at the landing, the first man's face at the yard's edge and the second man's turned slate and direction --- all kept their places where put and did not cross under the roof.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under its winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note, the inch not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor did not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended --- neither early nor stayed --- and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The well gave back the weather, the house went on, and the hand had come down --- forward, into a thirtieth not yet lived and not brought into this twenty-ninth.
