The afternoon went on after the bell's fourth stroke as a market goes on after any stroke --- by selling. The gap closed over nothing. The two hundred went back to the boards, and \person[Corin]{the boy}, set in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where \person{Sella Vail} had put him and had not moved him, took the square by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A cup went down on a board with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a stall-board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He had once sorted twenty people by what they would not survive; here he sorted the square by what it would not say, and the sorting bought him the order of things and nothing more.

The large sounds came and went --- pigs over wet boards, a man's shout, an assessor's slate, the wind off \place{the river} under its winter skin. Weather tells a man where he is and nothing about who owes what; he gave the wind no errand and the pigs no number. Three likenesses were three likenesses and he did not lay them together to make a row come out even, for an account wants a hand under it and none stood in the square. The cold came to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; he had stopped reading the cold as a thing to be mended weeks ago, and the reading had never once bought him a warming.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a woman stood looking at pots. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and on the back of one hand a pale cord-mark lay where something tight and long had once been worn; she did not scrub it out, and nobody noticed the hand, because people notice the pail first and the sleeve not at all. She took up a cup, turned it a quarter without lifting it from the board, and set it back exactly where it had stood. She said nothing. She went off with the light.

To the boy the cord-mark was a pale line in a place where a pale line had been read before --- at the well on a day of mud, and on a man carried out of a kiln alive and breathing --- but he did not lay this line across either of those, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. He filed the quarter-turn of the cup and the pale line on a place of its own, a colour and a shape, joined to no ear, no hearing, no cause and no name. He never called her Fenna, whose ear and whose refusal to be entered were years ahead and unspent, and the green at her mouth, if green there was, stayed the green of \lore{lead glaze} and not a mark entered in any slate. A surgeon reads a hand before he reads a face; here the hand gave him clay in the nail crescents and a pale line, and he let it give him no more, for a name laid over a hand wants a person standing under it and none stood.

At \place{the market's edge} a foundling in cast-offs stood with silt dried pale in his wrists and a gap in his grin, a green sliver at his throat catching the market light and going dull. A pot was set down on a stall-board; the knock went out and the note of the pot after it, and the boy's head went toward the sound before he had decided to turn it. The foundling's head went a half-beat after --- after the knock and after the note --- and stopped in the middle of the turn. No loaf passed. No slate was opened within reach of the basket. No word went from the board to the edge, and no sale was paid. The boy gave the foundling no dialogue and no name, and never called him Ulda, whose loaf and whose sale to a clerk in a Waiting Room were years ahead and unspent. He noted the order of it --- the knock, the note, the half-beat in the gap --- and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stands.

He filed the two as sightings on separate trays: a colour and a shape, with the whole square of noise between them and no finger laid from one to the other. No fourth likeness was made a cause. The green at the pot board and the shape at the market's edge stayed facts from older days, and nothing in the third reading of Ilmen had made a fourth of either or paid either. No eighth came; two sightings were two and not one account, and an account wants a hand and a payer and neither stood in the square. This was the first reaching of a warmth the child's body could not want and the surgeon could only file, and he filed it as a man files a finding: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air. Nothing was consummated and nothing was named. The turn was a half-beat and the cup a quarter-turn, both stopped before they could become an account.

The square pressed around the two of them with eight pigs and two hundred people and a bell gone quiet, and neither of the two on the separate trays knew the other stood there. He kept the file open a moment longer than the finding required and then shut it, for a file left open wants a reader and none stood in the square. He had learned in another life to sort a room by its exits and its bleeding; here the room had no bleeding and the exits were weather, and the sorting went on anyway, because it was what the hands of a surgeon did when the case was finished and nobody had called the next. The old life rode the register and nothing else; no voice from it broke into the square, and the page over the speakers and the hands that had stayed in the field went on gathering interest where they lay, unspent and not brought.

Beside those two trays he set the older keepings, each with a finger between it and the next. The three likenesses of \place{Slate Row} went on their own places: the first man's patient face at the yard's edge, soft hands with no clay ground into the creases, held loose at his sides; the second man's direction down the road toward \place{the north bridge}, his narrow slate turned to his sleeve; the asking after a house-name answered with the weather, a word brought up the yard with nobody standing under it. Three likenesses were three and not one account. Below \place{the out-of-line landing}, where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called, his entered figure reported on a place of its own --- entered before his eyes went up to find the man who gave it, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. The well-asking stood beside the second man's direction and his turned slate and was expressly not the calling of the loan. The fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying} kept its own place between \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand and the road saying, and three figures going up the road did not make a runner into a reader; her slate went toward \place{Greybank} with the writing turned to her sleeve and unread.

\person{Berr Clay} had gone up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln} with the box locked and Ilmen's third true subtraction inside, and the key had gone up in a coat pocket that never comes back down. Three readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure, three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid; he did not lay the twelve old arithmetics across the three of this year to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer. Berr's low saying about kindness being entered and his \emph{your house} stayed each on a place of its own with a finger between, and neither was joined to the figure in the inward slate going up the road or to the two sightings in the square. A belief can go up a road and gather interest there without ever coming back to stand under the thing believed.

The offered figure at the well kept its place beside \person{Ilva}'s ninth record-first hand and the fifth shaping: a reaching from a shut fist toward the strip in a sleeve, held toward the light, gone back into the sleeve and arrived nowhere. A reading did not make a price of it or a custom of it; it was not offered again, not named, not spent, not made a second reaching by being remembered, and Ilva, offstage with her strip in her sleeve, was not made a reader or a speaker by three figures going up the road. Her ninth hand stayed nine. A hand that only reads adds no placing, and a tenth would want a payer where none stood.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man at a stall brought a number up out of a shut fist and held it toward the light as a woman at a board holds a weight before the weigher calls it, looked at it, shut the fist and put it back into his sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price. The boy gave the fist no figure and the note no payer, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat and the half-beat in the gap is a gap with nothing standing under it.

At the kindling \person{Tarn}'s sentence stayed found where put --- \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} --- said once to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather and not made a habit or a second silence on a day that had earned its thirtieth. The small knife lay at the edge of its sill and his hand had gone over blade and sill and away with no breath held at the top of the reach; the coming away was the whole of the checking. The loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended and his valuable ear was exposure and not safety. He was offstage now and found where put, and the reading had not come to the kindling as a cause. Under the wheel-shed \person{Jorra Vail} had kept dusty forearms apart from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate; he had withheld the full note three turns ahead from any master come up from High Kiln who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. His bent finger had gone into the cold damper ahead of an unfinished sentence with one iron's two bites, the holding and the taking, out again without the extra holding, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed. The promise to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and the green and the shape stayed sightings with no eighth and no name.

The market thinned and Sella lifted the basket out of the thin shade without a word to it and went up the clay road at the half-pace from every open slate. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place} where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back --- no name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what the month had cost, only the empty place in the returning line that the yard went around with a look and a wide step. The widest step of the seven was a keeping and not a sign; an absence with a name would have wanted an account, and this one had none, and a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and she did not lay it across the loaf's finger's-width stopping or across the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up where no broom had gone in two years; beside it the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between --- a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off the river was less cold at one edge where the low note of \lore{water over water} ran nearer under the skin, and the tang came thinner on that edge. He found the softening where put and called it weather. He did not look twice, did not make it a sign, a fourth of \lore{the three coverings}, a payment or a custom, and on a day that had earned its thirtieth he did not make it again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it.

At the going of the light she came to \place{the hearth} before the basket in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under lead glaze beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning, a hearth, a market, a reading, a basket and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Tenthday that had earned its thirtieth.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She turned the dressed grain under wool at \place{the rack end} with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

She carried him up \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace and set him under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. She said nothing to the basket of what had been read over it, nothing of the bell's gap, nothing of the clerk's figure reported at the landing, nothing of the cup turned a quarter across the square or of the head that stopped in the middle of its half-beat at the edge. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket. The thread stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for, and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock stayed outside them on its own ground. The heard bell kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, with both gaps in their own squares; \lore{the carried fire}'s gap below \place{the boards} and the boards' four, four and a thumb-width knot stayed three counts in three places, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth and nine stoppings with no tenth; no name was given to the gap after the ninth. The offered figure and its return kept their own place beside the ninth hand and the fifth shaping with a finger between every pair. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had gone unspoken four hundred and twenty-four days and the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number, the two counts standing side by side without becoming one; the crust stood hard as old horn two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool, and \lore{the keeping at the fold} was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, name, gift, payment or payer. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust.

It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of the year gone by, the three readings of this one, the printed figure, the fifth shaping, the offered figure, the clerk's figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under \place{the finger's-width door}, the inch not a reach; he took a small intake, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There is no next hand in the room. Across the square of the day behind him a cup stood a quarter turned on a market board and the foundling's head stopped in the middle of a half-beat, both on places that do not touch, with no name and no eighth of either.
