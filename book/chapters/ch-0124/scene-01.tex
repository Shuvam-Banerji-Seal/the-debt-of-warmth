\daymark{423}{Ilmen 29, Y.H. 814, Ninthday}

\dropcap{S}{mall hours} came and went under the Vail roof without anybody naming them, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid at the last answer of the day gone by. Four hundred and twenty-two exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-two days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, and no voice under the roof to tell it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen; the room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. It was a new day. Nothing had crossed into it from the one before --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. The dark at \place{the finger's-width door} thinned by no degree that anybody measured, and above and behind him the loft kept whatever small sound it kept, and did not repeat it.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would have tried to eat it three nights ago and would not try now. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put in the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind him, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. The Afterdrop waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it.

The infant wanted three things without knowing that wanting them was a want. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, and a hand on the handle of the pot. Both registers were true; the second was louder; neither won. It was not a verdict but the work, and the work went on in silence in a room whose floor did not touch his.

\place[the towel]{The towel} lay at the head of \place{the bed}, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke, and he went over the field by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and not dug for, and \place{the Drawing house}'s flat knock outside them on its own ground, not made a hearing. The bell heard from \place{the Cross} kept its square beside the told bell, four, four and a gap, and that gap was not folded into \lore{the carried fire}'s four low pulls and its silence below \place{the boards}, or into the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth, and nine stoppings with no tenth, and he gave no name to the gap after the ninth.

The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side and neither bought the other. He had reduced a thousand shoulders and he could not keep a blanket on; he had sorted twenty people by what they would not survive and here he sorted a house by what it would not say. The file was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Ilmen 30 waited one morning off, a date and not a day earned, and he did not bring it into the small hours of a day that had earned only its opening.

\scenebreak

First light came the way light comes under a roof that keeps its days in loaves and glances and an unnamed space at the row's end: not as an announcement, but as a greying at the finger's-width door and a thinning of the dark on the boards. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as it had at every morning of the days behind. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay, and the coals gave off the dry smell of a fire banked and not forgotten. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning, a hearth, a shelf and a basket are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Ninthday that had earned only its twenty-ninth.

She said to the fire, in the tone of a woman who had told the same thing to the same fire for three winters, \emph{The fire doesn't know you're sorry. Feed it}, and put two fingers of coal where the coals had thinned, and did not look to see whether the basket had heard. Her hands went on counting when they had nothing to do --- three spoons, a nail, the small breath a child makes that nobody numbers --- and came back to the hairline at the wire as hands come back to a wound they have dressed. At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay covered under wool. She turned it piece by piece with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece down where it had lain, took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand.

Then she lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

At the carrying-down she went down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. Out in \place{the Clay Yard} the small sounds came first, because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather: a pail set on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set, the rope settling into its knot with the stiff dry rasp that cold puts into hemp, barley passed from wall to wall and never made into a number. Nothing in the yard had been told what the day was, and nobody in it was counting on behalf of a basket.

He had once stood at a bay door in a country that would not have understood a yard kept in loaves, and sorted twenty people by what they would not survive; here he sorted the yard by what it would not say. The sorting bought him nothing but the order of things, and the order of things was the only instrument he had. A cost arrived as a direction and went away as weather: a woman sent to ask what a pot cost was sent to the pot and got a note and not a price; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, and the arithmetic read over it did not know what it cost, and no signature stood on the space between figure and price. He took each arrival apart by the place of each part --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat lying in the gap --- and gave the fist no figure and the sleeve no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. He looked for an intention in the gap and found none standing; a lesser observer would have put one there and called it a reason, and a reason wants a payer where none stood.

He went around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, with a look and a wide step and no number: the stone lip of \place{the yard well}, the worn boards, the strip of hard mud, the place where a wheel had stood, the two narrow gaps between the stacked pots, and \place{the returning-line place}, widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign, for a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. He did not look twice; looking twice is how a finding becomes a habit, and a habit wants the hand that put the thing there. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away from it, a ring on a place of its own; he did not lay that ring across the loaf's finger's-width stopping or across the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey beside it, the one the house called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where no broom had gone in two years; beside them the called-gone grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge where the low note ran nearer under the skin, and the tang came thinner on that edge; he found the softening where put and called it weather, and did not look twice, and did not make it a sign, a custom, a fourth covering or a payment on a day that had earned only its twenty-ninth. The note came from under the covering and not from the covering.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away was the whole of the checking. He cut nothing, counted nothing, spent nothing; he said \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. His chest had been tight since the dark, Ash worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was; the loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended and his valuable ear was exposure and not safety. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness; he knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies, and he took the cold out of the fingertip and came out without the extra holding of Ash 26, gathered once and not made again. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

Answer four hundred and twenty-one came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the two readings of the year gone up the road, the printed figure, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the winter skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Seven honest answers stood on the sixth place, 415 through 421, two to each of the three days behind and one to this morning, and not one was a doctrine. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the two readings across one another to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key to \person{Berr Clay}'s box came under any roof.

Four hundred and twenty-three exact, six months and two hundred and forty-three days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The carried fire's fifth was still unpaid and Garl's name still unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one. \person{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood, the strip of notched birch in its fold in her sleeve and her hand ninth with no tenth; the figure that had been offered and taken back kept its own place beside her hand and the fifth shaping, with a finger between every pair, not offered again, not made a custom and not joined to anything. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road} with her eighth hand eighth, and the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure stayed a report brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. No sixth shaping, seventh hearing, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic or eighth opening stood on a day that had earned only its twenty-ninth, and no slate was opened within reach of the basket. Ilmen 30 waited one morning off as a date and was not brought into this morning as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

Sella carried him back up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. The pot stood where she had set it, its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with the half-beat unmeasured and no look toward the bed. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor to supply it. The well gave back the weather, the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place, and the hand lay flat on the fold in a room whose floor did not touch his --- forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.
