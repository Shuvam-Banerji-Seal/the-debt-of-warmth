\daymark{424}{Ilmen 30, Y.H. 814, Tenthday}

Small hours came on under the Vail roof without a word said over them and without a mark anywhere to show a boundary had been crossed. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}; the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been laid at the last answer, and nothing from the day behind him crossed into this one as a sign or a lesson or a mending or a cause or a payment or a payer. Four hundred and twenty-three exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-three days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. The dark at \place{the finger's-width door} thinned by no degree anybody measured, and \place{the river} beyond it kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it. He found the softening where put and called it weather. Above and behind him the loft kept whatever small sound it kept and did not repeat it, and he left it where it was.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool; a child under this roof would once have tried to eat it and would not try now. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind him, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; a face composed by cold is not composure, and a cold patient cannot clot. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew that a basket set level out of the draught, where the heat came sideways, was the rewarming he would choose, and he could not ask for it. The surgeon took the inventory and found it exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air.

Under the inventory, louder, the wanting went on. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. All three were true. The second was louder. Neither won, no verdict was looked for, and the quarrel was the work. A grown man could have named the Afterdrop and done nothing about it; an infant could want a thumb against a hard crust and have no thumb to lift it. Both lived in the same small body under the same fold, and the floor of the room did not touch his.

First light came the way first light came under this roof, grey at the door and then the pot board, and the household straightened around the order of it as though nothing in the district had a date to keep. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter.

Her finger lay along the top of \place{the loaf shelf} so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row with neither gap figured. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}. A morning, a hearth, a market, a reading, a basket and a going of the light are not openings; seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Tenthday that had earned its thirtieth.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. She turned the dressed grain under wool at \place{the rack end} with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and took no figure from it and no handful, and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill; his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. His chest had been tight since the dark, \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist catching and giving back what little light there was. The loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended, and his valuable ear was exposure and not safety; he owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the meal-fine grey of slate dust ground small past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened; it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He parted dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness, and knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. He took the cold out of the fingertip, moved his tongue a hair's breadth and came out without the extra holding of Ash 26, gathered once and not made again. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn.

At the carrying-down she went down the \place{nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the day was and was not told to be still. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place} where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign; a figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and she did not lay it across anything. The air off the river was less cold at one edge, and she did not look twice. Then she was going up \place{the clay road} with the basket level at her hip toward \place{the Cross}, and the two hundred were already leaning in, and the pigs were being driven over \place{the wet boards}.

\scenebreak

The Cross had been entered only as a sound and a report from under the roof; on this morning he was carried into it in the basket and set down at \place{the reading block under the moot-post}, in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay. The shade was thin as a worn thread and cold as the iron that cast it; his cheek lay against that cold edge and the square opened in front of him in boards and mud and the backs of people. Eight pigs went over the wet boards with a wet slap and a squeal and a man's shout, and a Sill assessor wrote them down with his hand to the slate before his eyes went to the rail and his mouth after both; the figure he entered belonged to the eight and to nobody else, and nobody asked which eight he meant. Figures came to a heap of turnips and were left standing side by side without being made to agree. Three likenesses are three and not one account; an account wants a hand to lay them together and a payer under all of them, and none stood.

Sella set the basket where the shade fell and stood half a pace off. She gave no instruction to the basket and no reason for the place, did not look into it, and did not touch the mark at her throat. Half a pace is a short distance to stand from a thing you carry; it is also the whole distance between a woman's hand and a reader's. She had carried him to readings under other months and had never once made the carrying a lesson. A market and a reading are not openings, and the keeping at the fold stayed where it lay, two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool, with no name spoken over it and no third crust.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road to the block. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the sweetish green of tasted lead glaze stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, the most expensive smell in Grath. He was heavy in the way a wagoner is heavy, with a back that had carried clay, and his hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing --- once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it --- and held a narrow slate unopened against his forearm with the writing inward. A finger went over the figure through the cloth of his own sleeve, and his eyes rested on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he was ready, and the crowd leaned in, and the reading began.

The boy watched the order of it the way he had once watched a hand go into a field: hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both, the half-beat lying in the gap where a lesser observer would have put an intention. There was no intention standing in the gap. A surgeon draws a hand from a field before the dressing goes on, and the hand's going is the whole of what can be seen; Berr took himself out of his own sentences by the same small motion, and the boy filed the order where the other record-first hands lay, with a finger between this one and every other, and no ninth or tenth made of it.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” Berr said low into the goers-by, and sent the figure twice over the basket --- once for the moot and once for the households that came late --- never looking into it. The figure went over the top of him as weather goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. It did not touch the crust, the fold, the thread or the two inches of air at the pot rim at home; it did not know the hand that had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back, or the man carried out on Day 188, or the grain lying covered under wool where a figure read over it did not know what it cost. The crowd took the figure and went back to the pigs. Nobody crossed himself.

“The levy is called. It is not called by me.” He said it without raising his voice, which in a moot is more frightening than shouting because two hundred people have to lean in to hear it, and the leaning is half of the obedience. “Your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at \place{the watch}.” \emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. “If your household has heard otherwise, your household has heard wrong.” He took himself out of each sentence as neatly as a hand goes out of a wound before the clamp is put on, and nothing in the square could fasten him to the thing he had said.

A man at the edge of the rail brought a number up out of a shut fist and held it toward the light the way a woman at a market board holds a weight before the weigher calls it. He asked what the levy would take now that hands had been in the planting, and Berr did not give the number back. “A number goes into a slate, not into a fist. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.” The number went from the fist to his mouth and from his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} with a slate under his arm, and arrived nowhere. The boy gave the fist no figure and the shoulder no motive, for a motive is a payer wearing a coat. The half-beat in the gap between the man's asking and the thane's answering was left a gap, and no intention was set under it.

Something low Berr said to the block about kindness being entered, in the tone of a man setting a cup where a cup belongs. The stone answered nothing. There was no anger in the saying and no triumph, nothing a crowd could have carried away and repeated; only the belief, which was the part that would one day be remembered against him. He said it once and did not say it again, for a second saying would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood.

Then he went off up the clay road toward High Kiln with the cord folded square under his coat, the narrow slate under his arm and its writing turned to his sleeve, and the box locked, Ilmen's third true subtraction riding inside behind the twelve arithmetics of the year gone by and the two readings of this one. He went at the pace of a man who has finished a morning's work, and he believed he had done a kindness. The key went up in a coat pocket that never comes back down, and there was no key under any roof where the boy lay. He did not look into the basket at any point, and had not entered this anywhere, including in his own mind. The great clean hands went up the road with the figure on the inward slate and the hot clay smell of him going off behind them, and the crowd went back to the pigs as water goes back to its level after a stone has been dropped in.

Three readings of the year now stood behind one unsigned figure: the tenth, the twentieth and this thirtieth of Ilmen, each earned by its own morning. Three were three and not one account. The first was not made a thirteenth by the second, and the second was not made a thirteenth by the third; each was a reset and not a signing, a reading and not an account, a fresh arithmetic of the new year and not a doctrine out of three. The figure read was still not the figure paid. No signature stood on the space between the figure and the price, and no hand under any roof had put one there. The boy did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the two earlier readings across the third to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and the key had gone up in a coat pocket that never comes back down. A row made even by a hand that has no key is a row made even on paper, and paper buys no warmth. Berr's low saying about kindness being entered stayed on a place of its own, and his \emph{your house} stayed beside it with a finger between, and neither was joined to the other or to the figure in the inward slate going up the road. Three readings gathered interest where they stood and did not become a fourth of anything.

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve, the second strap let out for the long road and dried pale scurf of road-mud unbrushed on the shortened skirt. To a woman at the rail who asked what she carried she answered the question put and not the one behind it. “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the one who wanted more: “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.” No figure came off the slate, and she did not look into the basket. She was a runner and not a reader; her eighth record-first hand stayed eighth and joined to none, and three figures going up the road did not make a reader of a woman who had carried one. She went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking, and the slate went up with the writing to herself.

Below \place{the north bridge} at \place{the out-of-line landing}, where a fifth step should have stood, the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set for a measure nobody called, his slate turned inward and his hand to it before his eyes to the landing. His entered figure was a word brought up the yard and never a sighting; it stayed reported on a place of its own with a finger between it and Tarn's degree toward the landing and another between it and the face at the yard's edge on an older morning. He was not a witness, a tenth hand or a reader, and no figure came off his slate into the square. Below the bridge the market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

The bell went through the middle of the morning, longer than a church bell, four strokes and four and a gap, and nobody crossed himself. The boy heard it from the basket set in the thin shade and kept the heard bell in its square beside the told bell, with both gaps in their own squares. The gap after the fourth was not folded into the silence of \lore{the carried fire}'s four low pulls under \place{the boards} and the silence where the fifth should have stood, or into the boards' four nails, four and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places stayed three, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. The note ended on its own finishing, and he did not make the finishing a hearing or the gap an answer.

Answer four hundred and twenty-three came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of this year, the printed figure, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the degree, the face, the withheld sentence, the softening, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, 415 through 423, two to each of the three days behind and one to this day, and not one was a doctrine. Four hundred and twenty-four exact, six months and two hundred and forty-four days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. He did not stand the number up as a threshold and borrowed no payer from the arithmetics gone up the road, for paper is not a payer.

At the going of the light she came to the hearth before the basket and went through the whole grammar of the order as though nothing in the square had touched either of them, and carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace from every open slate, going wide around the seven walked-around places once and not twice and widest around the returning-line place, and set him under the roof and never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. She said nothing to the basket of what had been read over it, nothing of the bell's gap, nothing of Hessa's slate going up the road, nothing of the clerk's figure reported at the landing. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket. The thread stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

Up the clay road Berr's narrow slate went with its writing turned to his sleeve and Ilmen's third true subtraction locked in the box under his arm, and below in the square the bell's fourth stroke fell into a gap nobody filled.
