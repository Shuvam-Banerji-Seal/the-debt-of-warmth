\daymark{95}{Seed 6, Y.H. 813}

The loaf shelf had a pale oval worn into its board where a loaf had stood long enough for the wood to take the bread's shape and keep it. The oval was the width of a man's palm and lighter than the dark around it, with a small hard ridge at one end where a crust had caught and held through many bakings. \person{Sella Vail} stood before \place{the loaf shelf} in the doorway of \place{the Vail kiln-house} and moved her lips along the row the way a woman counts spoons into a drawer --- one, two, three --- without ever letting the counting become a figure in the mouth. She had done it every morning of the boy's life and had never once said the number aloud. Today the row answered her differently.

There were two gaps in it now. The old one stood at the end of the rank where a loaf had once stood and nobody called it a number, a space the house kept the way it kept \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s hollow seat and the salt-stiff coat on its peg, going around it and leaving it standing. The other was new. It sat within the rank, not at the end, a palm's breadth of bare board where the pale oval showed and the bread did not, and beside it the next loaf had settled a finger's breadth toward the empty place, the way a stack leans when the sheaf under it is taken away. Sella's thumb went over the new gap, felt the little ridge at its end, and came back. She said nothing about either.

Another loaf's worth had gone off the board since the going of the light yesterday and nobody had marked it. There was no scrape on the shelf, no crumb on the floor, no print in the grey, no word said over the row. A loaf had stood here at dusk on Seed 5 and did not stand here at the coming of the light on Seed 6, and the house had gone on around the absence the way water goes around a stone and leaves it where it lies. Carried at the edge in a basket under a fold of wool, \person{Corin} read the two gaps as he had once read a column of figures with a neat line drawn through the middle of it: a thing entered, a thing taken away, both of them accounted for, and nobody in the room any the wiser for what the line had covered. It was a clean reading. It was worth nothing.

A neighbour came to the shelf with her basket on her arm and reached for a loaf. Her sleeve was pushed above one elbow and her hands were red from the tubs. Her hand went out at the level of her own hunger and passed over the new gap without stopping, the fingers opening for the half-beat it takes to find what is not there, and then closing on the loaf beyond it. She did not look at the place her hand had crossed. She said something to Sella about the pot she had come to fetch, and Sella told the pot to come up level and leave its lip as it found it, and the woman went on with the pot, and the gap went on standing in the row. It was ordinary work. Nothing had happened. No figure passed between them and no hand was laid on the bare place to make it mean anything.

Sella gave the instruction to the pot and to the fire and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and when she spoke of the day's baking she spoke of what the house had said and not of what she had decided. The little-door burn on the back of her hand closed on the basket's edge and then on nothing, because her hands had already gone back to the work. Once, in the middle of the telling, her eyes went to the clay jar on the wall shelf and rested there a moment, and came back. The jar was cold under its glaze, the colour of skimmed milk gone off, and it held nothing the boy could see from where he lay. She did not touch it and did not say what it was for.

Under the fold the boy set the shelf's new gap down on one place and the empty place beside the crust at his ear on another, and kept the two places apart. He had learned, in a better-lit room than any in Low Kiln, what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer: lay like on like on like and a cause grows up between them that no one of the three would have given by itself, and the man goes home satisfied with the thing he has made. He had watched a man do it with three reports and be paid for the doing, and the reports had not been lying. Here were two absences in two rooms, with nobody putting a hand to either and nobody asking what had gone. He wrote no cause across the space between them. The keeping of the two places apart was the whole of the work available to him, and it bought him nothing.

He wanted the number of his days said aloud by somebody who would know what it counted. Ninety-five was exact in him and had been exact on every morning since the first, and there was nobody under this roof who knew he had a number, and nobody who could have been told what it counted in \lore{Grathic} that would have understood him to be speaking of a life. He could not ask. The pot got its instruction and the fire got its instruction and the broom got its instruction and the basket got none, and the boy got none either. He was the only adult at the shelf and the least use in the room.

\scenebreak

The small bench knife lay on its sill when they came out into \place{the Clay Yard}, with six years standing in a mark \person{Tarn} had worn almost flat with his thumb and the seventh nearly gone and still held. He had told the sill where the knife was before he told the yard, and the yard had not answered, and he was satisfied. He took the broom only after Sella had given it its instruction, and went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the new hairline of grey finer than the old lying beside it and the space under the lowest of \place{the drying racks}, going as wide around the last as around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. He swept none of the three.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. When a woman at the well said the thaw would come early and a man coming up the path said it would come late, Tarn said \emph{Yes, house} to each and agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on. He went on wanting the late one because it put off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. He did not say what the paying was. The yard did not ask. A vessel gave its note somewhere past the door and his head did not turn and he did not name the sound, and the boy did not call it a seventh hearing.

Somebody had carried a loaf out of this house since the last light. He had seen the cloth go under a woman's arm and had not seen her put anything back, and there was no slate in the yard that would have it entered and no word spoken over it that a slate could have read. He set it on the shelf's tray and did not carry it to the fold. It might have been a gift; he had no word for the thing in \lore{Grathic} and no standing to give it one, and a gift that cannot be answered is a gift that cannot be refused. He left it where it was, unentered and unnamed, beside the other things the house put down and went around.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been given an instruction about the thread, so it lay stiff against his chin and did not go away. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold came at cheek and then temple and then the bare place at the wrist where the wool rode back; the hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note of water beyond the door and came down. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, and no third came. He wanted the crust and could not reach it, and the wanting was louder than the reading.

The private file was checked the way a man checks instruments laid out on a towel, and it was unchanged. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six beside and never among, five of them from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman who had been warm that morning, from a person; no seventh had come. Three near-full answers stood on their tray, advanced and not paid. The three intervals stayed on another. The word forty was unspoken, and the readable shape of the double turn stood beside it on its own place, and the weight of a thumb on wicker stood apart from both. Four low things kept their four places, with the river's note of water going over water a fifth thing and not a hearing. The two tellings of the room shut in the war stayed joined to no kiln, likeness kept and difference kept, breath through the nose against breath made in the throat, both men saying \emph{not the war's hand}. The loan that could hear stayed uncalled.

Sella told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. Its payer had gone unnamed for ninety-five days, and the coal that went up with the brine-gull was entered in no slate and missed in none. In the corner the clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and was called the thaw and would go when it went. Ash was worked into a man's knuckles in the colour of that line, and nobody fetched a broom for either.

She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a shelf, a basket and a bed are not openings. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}, because no opening had come into this house on Seed 6; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. Her private roll stayed unshown. Tallow smoke and wet wool and the faint sour mineral tang of the unswept corner came down under the roof with the dark, and the house kept them the way it kept everything else, without an entry and without a name.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came \lore{the kiln's breath}, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for ninety-five days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. The loaf shelf in the kiln-house held its two unnamed gaps and the fold at his ear held its one stiff crust and its one unnamed empty place, in two rooms and not touching, with nobody putting a hand to either and nobody saying what had gone. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. Nothing had been joined, paid, explained or ended. The boy was ninety-five days old and nobody had told him so, and the count stood at ninety-five, unspent.
