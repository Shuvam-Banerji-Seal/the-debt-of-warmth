\daymark{100}{Seed 11, Y.H. 813}

The hundredth morning came up over \place{the Clay Yard} the colour of wet slate, with the mud the water had left set into a brown skin that held the print of a boot and gave it back slowly as the light went across it. There was no frost on the racks. Out east of \place{Low Kiln} the seed stood in \place{the barley fields} where the fists had let it out, not green yet, only worked ground with the earth drawn over it in a line that stopped where the next strip began, and the women who had put it there went around it with their brooms as though a line drawn in dirt were a thing a man might step over and spoil. Nothing in the ground said a figure. A fist had opened and a fist had closed, and the strip had taken what it was given without a mark to show whose hand had paid it. The morning went on in the small sounds of bristles and boots, and a man at the edge of the strips said the work would want hands before the week was out, and a woman at the racks said the weather would have the last word, and the two sayings passed one another in the yard without either putting a number to the hands or the strips, the way two carts pass on a road and neither leaves a mark of how far the other has gone.

\person{Sella Vail} had set \person{Corin} at the edge of the work with the wool at his ears and had given the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “Go around,” she said to the bristles. “It cost enough to come in. It'll cost more to take out.” The women took it up as though it had been said to them and went on with their sweeping; the basket got no instruction at all. The little-door burn across the back of her hand closed on the wicker's edge and then on nothing. He lay with cold iron at his cheek where an old bracket had been bolted and left, and the cold came in its known order, cheek, temple, wrist, with the wool riding back from the wrist so the cold found the bare place. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had been told to wipe it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the light and came down. Nothing within two paces could be reached.

He was one hundred days old. Three months and ten days. The count had gone edge to edge and come to a round place where a man might have set a mark and shut a column, if there had been a column, or a slate, or anybody who knew what the column counted. A round number is a handsome thing at the foot of an account; it asks to be entered and asks it in a clean hand. The house kept its days in loaves pushed along a shelf, in a glance at the clay jar on the wall, in the space at the row's end that nobody called a figure. Nobody entered his hundred. Nobody said three months and ten days in the yard, or over the strips, or anywhere within the sound of his ear. He kept it where he kept the rest of it, edge to edge and exact, and the exactness was the whole of what he had and bought nothing at all.

Under \place{the drying racks} the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had always lain, and beside it the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay unswept and was called the thaw that would go when it went. Sella stood over both with the broom in her hand and swept neither, and went around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. The broken slates lay beneath that space and were gone around by every foot in the yard and entered by none. A woman pushing her bristles around a stone that had come up with the flood stopped and looked at the ground a moment longer than the stone required, then went on with her sweeping and did not say why she had looked. He kept the boot-print, the clean line, the finer grey and the empty space on four places that did not touch, because a likeness laid on a likeness grows a cause unless a hand holds them apart. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and that count met none of the others either.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after Sella had given it its instruction, and went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning to look along it. The small knife lay on its sill with six years standing in a mark his thumb had worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. To the kindling he said, “Six counted. The next I don't count.” To a woman who wanted the thaw early and a man who wanted it late he said, “Yes, house,” to each, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot anywhere in the yard, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He had been sent for a collector once in \lore{the Long Cold}, had come back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and kept that sentence where he kept the seventh year and entered neither. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. The sentence had never been said under the roof and lay under it all the same. Something must give at the end of the seventh year. It did not give on the hundredth morning.

From \place{the Vail wheel-shed} came the wheel's low rhythm and a word given to a pot and not to a person. “You'll do,” said \person{Jorra Vail}, and the boy heard him and did not see him. No figure came out of the shed onto the yard; the back room was not mentioned, and the men who had come in before the light were not named. Jorra knew the \lore{full note} of a Vail pot before it left the wheel and would not say so before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln}, who would call it early and make something of it; the boy kept the knowing where questions are allowed to last for years. The bent finger went to \lore{the damper} ahead of a sentence nobody had finished, the cold iron taking the cold out of the fingertip, and the household straightened around the silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. He did not look toward the basket, and “he's not Vail” stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years. The promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He was the only adult at the edge of that yard and the least use in it: a surgeon who could read the order of a failing body and could not keep the wool on his own wrist, a man of forty who could not lift his hand to his own chin.

\scenebreak

In the afternoon Sella carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof while she went about the house. The private file was checked the way a man checks instruments laid out on a towel, and it was unchanged. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six beside and never among --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person --- and no seventh came. Three near-full answers stood on their tray, advanced and not paid --- one out of a cup across a birth room, one out of a pot under the wheel-shed wall, one out of a rack pot in the yard --- and each had gone as far as it could go without being carried to any door. Three intervals lay on another. The word forty was unspoken, with the readable shape of the double turn beside it, a cord twisted twice beneath another cord where no hand had counted either knot, and the weight of a thumb on wicker set apart from both. Four low things kept their four places, and the river's note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed on their tray joined to no kiln, likeness and difference kept and no third telling: breath through the nose against breath in the throat, both saying \emph{not the war's hand}. The \lore{damper}'s double bite stayed filed as an object, read perfectly and worth nothing --- the same iron that keeps a vessel whole can take the hand off a man, and no word in \lore{Grathic} holds both mercies in one. The cold foot below the sill stayed the cold to one woman and weather to the yard, uncured, uncaused and unnamed. The half-beat after the knock lay on another place, after the sound and not before it, not a hearing and not joined to his own turning. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled. Nothing in the file was paid.

On \place{the loaf shelf} in the next room the row stood with two empty places where one had stood, and nobody had named either or laid a hand to either; the space at the end of the row had no figure. In the fold at his ear one brown-edged crust lay stiff and gone past smelling of anything, with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Two rooms, two absences, and no opening between them that any hand had made. He set the shelf's two gaps together on the shelf and the fold's one gap beside the crust in the fold, and kept the rooms from touching by the only instrument he had, which was attention, and it bought him nothing. No third crust came. A woman reaching for the shelf that afternoon passed her hand over the new gap and went on with the pot she had come for, and did not look a second time at the place her fingers had crossed.

Every warmth in the house had a payer and none was entered. The carried fire had gone unnamed for a hundred days; the coal the brine-gull took upriver was in no slate and missed in none; the crusts, the gruel, the milk, the seed, the hands that went into the strips and the loaves that went off the board were all entered nowhere that a man could read. He had learned in a better-lit room to ask who paid before asking what a thing was worth, and there was nobody under this roof who would have understood the question, and no word in \lore{Grathic} in which to put it. Sella moved her lips along the row at the wall, glanced at the clay jar, and gave the instruction to the pot and the fire and never to the basket. She said the house where another woman would have said I, and had not once in a hundred days said whose house, or whose fire, or whose hundred. To the wool she said, “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” and went on with the pot as though the hundredth day were the afternoon of any other day of the year and the boy in the basket were a thing set down to keep. He wanted the number of his days spoken by somebody who would have known what it counted, and got no figure for it from any part of the day.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on his bed under the roof, not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a strip, a shelf, a bed and a door are not openings. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark}; she had lost a husband to that danger and had never once described it that way herself. Her private roll stayed unshown.

She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and fed it, and the fire answered with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. \lore{the kiln's breath} came through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed for a hundred days. In the corner the clean dark line lay where it had always lain and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept and would go when it went. Tallow smoke and wet wool and the faint sour tang of the unswept corner came down under the roof with the dark. One brown-edged crust was carried into the night with the empty place beside it, and on the shelf in the next room two gaps stood in the loaf row, and none of the three had a name.

The door stood a finger's width open. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire's four, four and a gap, the two sounds in two separate places as they had come for a hundred days. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. Nobody had said a hundred in the yard or under the roof; nobody had said three months and ten days; nobody had set a mark at the foot of anything that would have known what it counted. He kept one hundred exactly against a house that kept its days badly, and he was the only adult in every room of this day and the least use in all of them. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water going over water and came down. The count stood at one hundred, unspent.
