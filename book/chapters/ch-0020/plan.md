## Scene 1 — "What the Fist Held"

- **Day and time:** Day 94, Seed 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813. First grey through midday on the barley fields east of Low Kiln; midday at the yard well and drying racks; going of the light on the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Place:** `\place{the barley fields}` → `\place{the Clay Yard}` / `\place{the yard well}` / `\place{the drying racks}` → `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`.
- **Who is present:** Corin (carried in Sella's basket), `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Tarn}`; walk-ons — the rack-woman with the broom, the man with the barrow, a woman at the well. Jorra in the wheel-shed, offstage; Alda, Mira, Berr offstage.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Open mid-stroke on the worked strip: broom bristles go around a stone in the brown skin the flood left, and a woman's fist lets seed out into the ground and comes back empty with chaff in the creases. Nothing is counted aloud. No recap of the first planting week.
  2. Sella gives the broom its instruction before anybody — *Round it. It cost enough to come in; it'll cost more to take out* — and never gives one to the basket. She says *the house* where another woman would say *I*.
  3. The man with the barrow says the strips want hands; the rack-woman says the weather will have the last word; neither puts a figure to the hands, the strips or the seed, and the two words meet nothing in the middle.
  4. Carried at the edge, the boy reads the emptied fist the way he once read a chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot — a figure entered and not paid — and the reading is exact and worth nothing. The body interrupts: drool freezes, the head goes where it wants with the small wet sound, cold arrives cheek–temple–wrist, the wool rides back from the wrist, the hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water and comes down.
  5. At the well a woman asks what the planting will cost now that seed is in the ground. Sella moves her lips along the loaf shelf, glances at the clay jar, leaves the space at the row's end with no figure, and tells her to ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there, because *the house has said it*.
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction; he goes around the clean dark line, the new finer grey and the space under the lowest rack, then over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. His knife is on its sill with six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held.
  7. Dusk under the roof: the private file is checked and unchanged; the new grey lies beside the clean dark line unswept and called the thaw; the carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed for ninety-four days; one crust stiff in the fold, the empty place unnamed, no third.
- **Emotional turn:** the work is done and still no figure appears; the boy's one exactness has no object left but its own number. Competence curdles into loneliness — he can read the emptied fist perfectly and cannot spend a stroke of it.
- **Sensory anchors:** chaff in a fist's creases, broom bristles on set mud, barley dust in the light, the cold iron of the well handle two inches beyond his hand, the little-door burn closing on the wool and then on nothing, woodsmoke and wet wool, tallow, the back of a broken pot turning coals.
- **Threads advanced:** T071/T072 (94), T077, T058/T079, T060/T086, T009/T013/T092, T088.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** plants the emptied fist as a chart closed over a figure (the record-first grammar that will be named much later) and sets up the absence that will appear on the shelf on Day 95; pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** under the roof the fire answers with four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; the door stands a finger's width open with the river's note and the fire's gap in two separate places; one brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed and no third; his hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down, ninety-four unspent.

## Scene 2 — "The Shelf Keeps Nothing"

- **Day and time:** Day 95, Seed 6, Sixthday, Y.H. 813. Morning and midday in the Vail kiln-house at the loaf shelf and the clay jar; dusk on the bed under the roof.
- **Place:** `\place{the Vail kiln-house}` (loaf shelf, clay jar on the wall shelf), the edge of `\place{the Clay Yard}`, the bed under the roof.
- **Who is present:** Corin, `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Tarn}` in the yard, a neighbour woman reaching for the shelf (walk-on). Jorra in the wheel-shed, not seen.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Open on the shelf: Sella moves her lips along the loaf row and the row now stands with **two empty places where one used to stand** — another loaf's worth has gone off the board with nobody marking it, beside the old space at the row's end that nobody calls a number. She says nothing about either.
  2. A neighbour reaches for a loaf; her hand passes over the new gap, she takes what she came for and goes on with the pot. The absence is ordinary work, not an event, and no figure passes.
  3. Sella gives the instruction to the pot and the fire and never to the basket, glances at the clay jar, and says *the house*. The boy wants the number of his days spoken by somebody who would know what it counted; he gets none.
  4. The boy sets the shelf's new gap and the empty place in the fold on two places that do not touch and writes no cause across the space between them. He has learned what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, and here are two absences in two rooms with nobody putting a hand to either.
  5. Tarn's knife is on its sill with six counted in a mark his thumb has worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held; Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it. He says *Yes, house* to the early thaw and the late, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went.
  6. The body humiliates him in its known order: one crust stiff at his ear, the empty place beside it, cold cheek–temple–wrist with the wool riding back, the hand up an inch and down. No third comes.
  7. Dusk: the fire keeps four, four and a gap with its payer unnamed for ninety-five days; the new grey in the corner is unswept and called the thaw; Sella puts the fold right without looking at his face, leaves the thread frozen and does not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf, a basket and a bed are not openings.
- **Emotional turn:** a house that already kept its days badly has quietly doubled its absence without noticing; only the boy counts the loss, and the count is the one thing nobody can tax and nobody wants.
- **Sensory anchors:** the worn pale place on the shelf board where a loaf stood, a thumb passed over the gap, the clay jar cold under its glaze, tallow smoke, the faint sour mineral tang of the unswept corner, the thread frozen at his mouth, the little-door burn shutting on nothing.
- **Threads advanced:** T060/T086 (the second empty place planted and held apart), T009/T013/T092, T071/T072 (95), T058/T079, T088, T036.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** plants the second empty place for the later day a house is left where it is and still kept and for the gift entered on no slate (~1500, T009/T013/T086/T091). The two absences must **not** be laid together in Chapters 20–24; nothing solved or named now. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** the two empty places — one on the shelf in the kiln-house, one in the fold against his ear — lying in two rooms and not touching, with the count at ninety-five unspent and the fire missing its fifth breath.

## Scene 3 — "The Bite That Holds"

- **Day and time:** Day 96, Seed 7, Seventhday, Y.H. 813. Before first grey through midday in the Vail wheel-shed and the Clay Yard; dusk under the roof.
- **Place:** `\place{the Vail wheel-shed}`, the room off it (finger's width open, not entered), `\place{the Clay Yard}`, `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`.
- **Who is present:** Corin (carried to the shed door), `\person{Jorra Vail}`, `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Tarn}`; walk-ons — a kiln-master from High Kiln, two men who came in since first light and spoke low in the back room (not seen by the boy, not named).
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Before light the room off the shed stands a finger's width open; men came in since first grey and spoke low; Jorra gives the house no name for them and does not say the room is cold. He comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel.
  2. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it and entered by nobody. The boy keeps dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate.
  3. Jorra speaks to the wheel before anyone — *It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady* — tells a pot *You'll do*, knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master from High Kiln.
  4. When Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low, his bent finger goes into the damper ahead of her sentence, the cold iron taking the cold out of his fingertip, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted.
  5. The boy reads the damper as any iron that stops a thing from running: the same bite that keeps a vessel whole can take the hand off a man, and no word in Grathic holds both mercies. He keeps the reading to himself — an anatomy of the house's danger, exact and worth nothing (T062 filed as an object, not paid).
  6. Tarn takes the broom only after Sella has given it its instruction, then goes over the collector's road over and over without once turning along it; he was sent for a collector once in the Long Cold, came back at the frozen ford and told the house it had been done, and keeps that sentence where he keeps the seventh year. Knife on its sill, six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held; to the kindling, *Six counted. The next I don't count.* The pale clip-ring holds what light there is and gives it back.
  7. A vessel gives its note past the door; Tarn's head does not turn and he does not name the sound; the boy does not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences keep their equality under one roof, the loan that can hear still uncalled. *He's not Vail* stays sealed and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn.
  8. Dusk: fire four, four and a gap, payer unnamed; fold put right without a look at his face; new grey unswept.
- **Emotional turn:** the most dangerous thing in the house is a piece of iron everybody uses and nobody names — mercy and mutilation in the same bite — and the three people who can read it keep it to themselves for three different reasons, so the house straightens around silences instead of words.
- **Sensory anchors:** grey slate dust in the creases past the bent little finger, the cold iron of the damper taking the cold out of a fingertip, bone-coloured slip dried to old bone, the wheel's low rhythm, tallow, the finger's-width-open door, Ash in the knuckles the colour of the clean dark line, the pale clip-ring catching light.
- **Threads advanced:** T035/T031, T062 (read and filed, **not paid**), T065/T095, T036/T076, T061/T082, T058/T079, T071/T072 (96), T057/T084.
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** damper's double bite carried toward the first clamp and the later taking-apart of the bite (T062, ~330/~1300); dust, unnamed men and broken birth-slates toward the Broken Slates (~160) and Jorra handing over the name; T036 still held — do not spend the seventh year; no third telling of the shut counting room. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** the damper cold under Jorra's bent finger and the room off the shed still a finger's width open with no name given to the men in it, while under the roof the fire breathes four, four and a gap and the boy's hand goes up an inch toward the water and comes down; ninety-six unspent.

## Scene 4 — "Twice Beneath the Grey"

- **Day and time:** Day 97, Seed 8, Eighthday, Y.H. 813. Dawn market at the Fishstairs; midday three doors down in House Silt; dusk under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** `\place{the Fishstairs}` below the north bridge → `\place{House Silt}` → `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`.
- **Who is present:** Corin, `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Mira Silt}`, `\person{Alda Reed}`; walk-ons — a weir woman with salt white at her wrists, an eel buyer who names a figure, a neighbour scrubbing in the back room of House Silt, the soft-cuffed clerk at the Fishstairs. Orra offstage, her board present; Dessa dead and stays dead.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Sella sets the basket at the edge of the fish-trade shade where no slate lies and gives the instruction to the wool and the water, never to the basket.
  2. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held where the top of her left ear had been, her small smile kept like a door on the latch; she moves eel-bones against her thumb while deciding whether to lie. Asked whether the run will be thin: *Thin? Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.*
  3. A buyer names a figure; Mira's thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and stays; shifting a creel shows the **second cord twisted twice beneath it**, never carried upriver to `\place{Nine Pots}` or lifted at a door, neither knot counted and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it.
  4. The steps come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks carefully over without a fall; the soft-cuffed clerk still sits with his cuff gone grey from washing and his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls, filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector and the Court-table clerk with **no sixth sighting** of the neat dark hand.
  5. The boy sets Mira's not-looking, a keeping, in a place that does not touch the pricing he saw at the strips — two faces turned the same way that mean opposite things, with no Grathic word for either.
  6. Midday, House Silt: Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light away from every open slate, opens no slate and turns no errand into a walk to `\place{Slate Row}`, standing a half-pace off.
  7. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay under them. She lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the wool a breath too long, crosses two fingers at the red edge of `\lore{the birth-flower}` before she knows it and covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa, a woman more mornings dead than any living person keeps count of. *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down* — exact and not true — and she goes on with dates after the question has stopped. Her hand goes once to the tin in her bodice without opening it; her sixth narrow slate stays face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name, six pages unread, **no seventh written and T023 not paid again**.
  8. Above the abandoned board-bed the round pewter tin keeps its pale fold and thumb-bright rub unopened; the grey wool below holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone lay, and nobody says the stone is gone. Two private records in two materials stand apart in one room, read by nobody and carried to no door. The boy keeps them apart with the only instrument he has, which is attention, and it buys him nothing.
  9. Dusk: private file unchanged, six hearings stay six; one crust, its unnamed empty place; new grey unswept; fire four-four-and-a-gap.
- **Emotional turn:** the district's real records are kept in grey wool, tin and cord by people who will not say what they are keeping; the boy can read every one of their shapes and cannot enter a single one of them.
- **Sensory anchors:** eel slime pale on sleeves, silt in the hollow of the neck, eel-bones flicked against a thumb, grey wool cords twisted twice, salt white at wrists, the out-of-line landing, soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt, lye sour under soap, scrubbed nails with clay under them, the chipped cup, the thumb-bright rub on the pewter lid.
- **Threads advanced:** T059/T025/T024, T023/T089 (carried, **not paid again**), T090/T080, T007/T008/T073, T058/T079, T071/T072 (97), T061/T082.

## Scene 5 — "A Foot the Water Left"

- **Day and time:** Day 98, Seed 9, Ninthday, Y.H. 813. Morning below the sill; midday at the edge of the Fishstairs market; dusk under the Vail roof.
- **Place:** below the sill by the low houses and the `\place{the plank walk}` at `\place{the Weir}` (spoken of, not entered) → `\place{the Fishstairs}` market's edge → `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`.
- **Who is present:** Corin, `\person{Sella Vail}`; walk-ons — a woman below the sill with a child whose foot stayed cold, a neighbour at the tubs, a kiln-hand whose sentence is not finished; `\person{Ulda}` (unnamed in the narration); the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk glimpsed at the landing.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Open below the sill: the water has gone and left a child with a foot that stayed dark and cold; a woman wraps it in wool and calls it the cold; a neighbour at the tubs calls the water weather, so the two facts still never meet.
  2. The boy reads it exactly — the extremity going before the core, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody will perform, a body that can be warm and still be going — and he has no word in Grathic and no hand two paces long. The reading works perfectly and is worth nothing. **No silt-leach, no cause, no cure, no recognition.**
  3. Sella carries him at the half-pace from every open slate, gives the instruction to the wool and the water and never to the basket, and does not go down to the child; the boy does not lay the two together. (It is told of her that she once sat three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind.)
  4. Midday at the market's edge: a small foundling in cast-off clothes worn like a uniform, a gap where a front tooth had been and a green sliver of lead-glaze on a string at his throat, unnamed in the narration. When a pot is set down his head goes toward it a half-beat after the boy's — after the knuckle and after the note — then stops; he rubs the sliver and cannot say why.
  5. He opens his mouth on the tail of a kiln-hand's sentence and does not finish it; **no new loaf passes, no slate is seen, no secret changes hands that any slate could read**; the warm loaf from the man from Slate Row on Silt 16 goes on gathering interest in an account the child cannot read. Do not join the soft hands to the boy or to the loaf as a proved sale; the sheet's dialogue is years hence.
  6. The boy files the half-beat on a place of its own — after the knock, not before, not a hearing and not a seventh — and keeps his own turning beside it without letting the two touch, because a thing that comes after the knock is a different object from one that comes before it. The foundling is not Marra, not the Fishstairs clerk and not the Court-table clerk; no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand.
  7. No vessel gives a seventh hearing: six hearings stay six from four sources plus the one from a person; the word *forty* is not spoken.
  8. Dusk under the roof: fold right without a look, thread frozen, fire four-four-and-a-gap, one crust and its unnamed empty place, new grey unswept; the cold foot stays filed on its own place, uncured.
- **Emotional turn:** the one thing he can do better than anyone in Low Kiln — read a body that is failing — is the one thing he is least allowed to do; beside it, a child whose attention can be bought turns his head half a beat late and cannot say why, and neither of them can spend what he notices.
- **Sensory anchors:** wool wrapped around a dark foot, river wind off the `\place{the Ilm}`, silt and salt at the wrists, a cast-off coat with a man's shoulders, the green sliver rubbed with a thumb, the small flat note of a pot set down, the gap in a grin, tallow smoke under the Vail roof.
- **Threads advanced:** T093/T094/T096, T033/T034, T061/T082, T058/T079, T071/T072 (98).
- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** the cold foot carried to the silt-leach of Arc 3 (~900); the half-beat held for the day the watcher's sale lands as betrayal (~330); the loaf from Silt 16 gathering interest. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** the child's foot in wool below the sill on its own place, the foundling's half-beat after the knock on another, and under the roof the fire's four-four-and-a-gap with the boy's hand going up an inch toward the water and coming down; ninety-eight unspent.

## Scene 6 — "One in Twelve, Twice"

- **Beats, in order:**

  2.   3.   4. `\lore{Account-day}`'s bell goes four, four and a gap over the market, longer than a church bell, and nobody crosses himself; the boy keeps the heard bell beside the told bell in two places; Sella does not look into the basket and says nothing about what was read over it.

  6. - **Emotional turn:** the boy lies inside an account that was read over him twice without once addressing him, and the instant the number is spoken it changes nothing — the district goes back to its pigs and the figure on the slate stays cleaner than the people it counts.
- **Sensory anchors:** the reading block wet with market mud, eight pigs on the weighing board, a thumb wiped twice on an apron, green of tasted glaze at a mouth, red wool cord folded under a coat, the bell's four-four-and-a-gap, soft hands on a clean slate, the moot-post's thin shade, cold iron at his cheek, the back of a broken pot in the kiln ash.

## Scene 7 — "One Hundred"

- **Day and time:** Day 100, Seed 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813. Morning in the Clay Yard and on the strips east of Low Kiln; afternoon and going of the light on the bed under the Vail kiln-house roof.
- **Place:** `\place{the Clay Yard}` → `\place{the barley fields}` → `\place{the Vail kiln-house}`.
- **Who is present:** Corin, `\person{Sella Vail}`, `\person{Tarn}`, `\person{Jorra Vail}` (heard at the wheel, not seen); yard voices (walk-ons). Alda, Mira, Berr and Ulda offstage.
- **Beats, in order:**
  1. Open on the hundredth day: the boy is one hundred days old, three months and ten days, and nobody marks it, names it or gives him a figure for it. No recap of the count's earlier days.
  2. Morning in the yard and on the strips: seed stands where the fists let it out; the yard goes around the brown skin holding a boot-print, the clean dark line, the new finer grey and the space under the lowest rack with a broom and a glance and no figure spoken; Sella gives the broom its instruction before anybody and never to the basket.
  3. Tarn's knife is on its sill with six years standing in a mark his thumb has worn almost flat and the seventh nearly gone and still held; to the kindling, *Six counted. The next I don't count.* He agrees with the early thaw and the late and keeps wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts since the water went. The loan that can hear stays uncalled; something must give at the end of the seventh year and it does not give here.
  4. Jorra is heard at the wheel and not seen, a low note and a word to a pot, with no figure coming out of the shed onto the yard; the back room is not mentioned and *he's not Vail* stays sealed, the promise of the foundling's name neither given nor withdrawn.
  5. Afternoon under the roof: the private file is checked the way a man checks instruments laid out on a towel and is unchanged — six hearings of the full note stay six beside and never among (five from four sources, the sixth out of a person), with no seventh; three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid; three intervals on another tray; the word *forty* unspoken with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart; four low things in four places with the river's note of water over water a fifth and not a hearing; the two tellings of the shut room on their own tray joined to no kiln, likeness and difference kept — no third telling; the loan uncalled.
  6. The loaf shelf keeps its two unnamed gaps and the fold keeps its one stiff crust and its one unnamed empty place, in two rooms that do not touch; nobody names either gap or lays them together; no third crust comes.
  7. The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer unnamed for a hundred days; Sella tells it the thaw, turns the coals with the back of a broken pot and does not touch the widow's mark, because a strip, a shelf, a bed and a door are not openings. She sets him on his bed under the roof and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right without looking at his face and leaves the thread frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left.
  8. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note from the Ilm comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor, the two sounds in two separate places as for a hundred days. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water going over water and comes down. Nothing joined, paid, explained or ended; the count stands at one hundred, unspent.
- **Emotional turn:** the hundredth day is a number only the boy keeps, and keeping it exactly is both his whole dignity and the proof that he has no standing in the house he has never left — the only adult in every room and the least use in all of them.
- **Sensory anchors:** seed standing in the strips, broom bristles around the boot-print, the worn thumb-place on the knife's handle, Ash in the knuckles, the pale clip-ring holding what light there is, the back of a broken pot on coals, woodsmoke and wet wool, the thread frozen at his mouth, the river's note through the finger's-width-open door.

- **Foreshadowing planted / paid:** the two gaps held for the later day a house is left where it is and still kept and the gift entered on no slate (~1500); the seventh year toward its end (Chapters 23–24); the count toward one hundred and one. Pays nothing.
- **Exact last image:** the hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the note of water going over water and coming down, with the door a finger's width open, the river's note and the fire's missing fifth breath in two separate places, one crust and two unnamed gaps carried into Day 101, and the count standing at one hundred, unspent.

## How the final moment pulls into Chapter 21

Under the Vail roof at the going of the light on Day 100, the boy reaches one hundred days exact with nobody to say it, the carried fire misses its fifth breath under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, and the river runs low under the finger's-width-open door — the count carried, not paid, across into Seed 12. Chapter 21 opens on Day 101 with the boy one hundred and one days old and the count still the only exact arithmetic in Low Kiln: the seed is in the ground, the new grey lies unswept in the Vail corner, and the house still goes around the two empty places — one on the loaf shelf, one in the fold — without naming either. A neighbour will speak of the space at the loaf row's end as a thing that came in with the planting, and of the empty place in the fold as a child's appetite, in the same breath and without seeing they are one absence; the boy will keep the two tellings apart (T060/T086, T071/T072). The two empty places stay apart. Nothing is joined, paid, explained or ended.
