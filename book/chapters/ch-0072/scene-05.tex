\daymark{269}{Smoke 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

Smoke 30 came on its own morning, earned by the light that made it and by nothing that had gone before it. The oiled paper at the window took the colour of skimmed milk, and the dark went off the rafters by degrees, the way heat goes off a pot set aside from the coals. In going it carried two hundred and sixty-eight away and brought two hundred and sixty-nine in. Six months and eighty-eight days became six months and eighty-nine. Nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said so. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, and the hand outside the wool lay where the hundred and twelfth answer had been put down in the small hours, as a man lays a tool on a bench where the next hand will find it without looking. He did not ask it to remember. An inch was an inch, and the little silence behind the last count had been an object and no more. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its fifth unpaid day to a payer without a name, and the payer had now gone two hundred and sixty-nine days without one. There was no bell under this roof and no market. This was the third and last Account-day and market of the second firing; it stood here by its own light and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it, and the month had three tends and three Account-days and would spend no fourth. The first firing of Frost stood far off and unbrought.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves, and entered none of it. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would have eaten it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, neither going forward nor going away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and sixty-nine days old the second was the louder; the surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

Three counts stood in three places. Under the floor the fire pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. None rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any; a knot is not a nail, a river is not a fire, and a house that keeps a count it cannot spend is not therefore keeping an account. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels. No seventh stood in the empty place and none had been invented to fill it, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1, held one breath, let go, and touched at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, market, morning, afternoon, dusk or going of the light on Smoke 28, 29 or 30, because a market and a reading are not openings; the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour. The one opening before Smoke 1 stayed behind the household with no lesson, no sign, no mending and no payer named to it. Three days had gone under this roof and the mark had hung through all of them the way a nail hangs in a wall that nobody looks at, which is not the same thing as a nail that nobody has driven.

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she went on every morning of the house, and gave the fire its instruction before she gave one to anything that breathed. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, turned them with the back of a broken pot at \place{the kiln mouth} so the ash broke over them in fine grey dust and the heat came up again without a flame to show for it. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- setting a row of tools in order on a board and would not have the row called a number. Then she pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the shelf by the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing and came away.

At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath it stayed unread, and moved her lips along a row of loaves standing with two gaps without giving either gap a figure. The old space at the row's end and the newer one beside it stood where they stood, and she did not join them to the empty place at his ear in the room that does not touch this one. Then she set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, rested her hand a half-beat longer than the setting needed, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance and the hand could not close it; the half-beat went where half-beats went, on a place of its own with no cause and no owner.

Then she lifted the basket in the crook of her arm and turned toward \place{the clay road}. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she said to the wool and to the wind, and never to the basket. She carried him at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, out of the yard and up the road, giving no instruction to the thing she carried and asking it nothing about the figure that would go over it before noon. The dressed grain lay covered under wool on the cool stone at \place{the rack end} behind her, well away from the hearth, changed in state and carrying no figure.

\scenebreak

\place[The Cross]{The Cross} had earned its morning and kept it badly, the way a shallow bowl holds water, spilling it on every side. Sella set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, set it down with the back of her hand the way a pot is set out of a draught, and stood half a pace off. The cold iron of the post came through the wicker to his cheek and stopped there where the order written out for cold runs out. No instruction would be given to the basket all morning and no reason either, and none was given now. The crust lay two inches from his ear with its empty place beside it, the thread at his mouth stood frozen, cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare wrist-joint, and the afterdrop waited past a warming nobody here would perform. He took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it.

Two hundred people leaned in over \place{the reading block}, shoulder against shoulder, each bringing a figure or a fist that no slate standing in the square had been made to enter. Eight pigs went over the wet boards while the light was still coming up, nose to tail, driven by a boy with a stick who did not look at the crowd; the boards were slick and the pigs slipped and caught themselves and went on, and each gave out a small flat grunt that arrived nowhere. An assessor of \place{Sill} wrote the eight down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail. A man set a figure beside another on a heap of turnips and left them standing like two pots on a board, neither owing the other anything.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off the clay road before the top of the crowd had settled. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward, passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the crowd.

\emph{One in twelve, as the notice says.}

He read it twice. The figure went over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late.

The boy lay under the wool with the figure going over him and did not make it the paid figure, because the figure read aloud was not the figure paid and there was no signature anywhere between the two. He had heard the same figure go over the same basket on Smoke 10 and again on Smoke 20, and now a third time on Smoke 30; three times in one month, and the month had no fourth reading to spend. The arithmetic of the district changed for the third time where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back, and the printed figure stood unchallenged through all three. A change made in a square walks home with two hundred people and sits at two hundred tables, and not one of them knew there were two figures in it, one printed and one paid, with a space between nobody signed.

This was the thing he kept, and he kept it as a finding with no payer. A figure read a third time over the same basket was no nearer the covered thing it was read over than a chart laid over a covered body is nearer the wound beneath it. The reading knew the cover and not the thing; it knew the wool and the shape the wool made, and it knew none of the grain lying under it at the rack end, which had been lifted and turned and covered again through all three readings and had never been given a figure. The arithmetic had changed three times and the price of that grain had not moved. The not-knowing was as complete after the third reading as after the first, and three readings were three and not one account. He set the finding on a place of its own and did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the dressed grain, or across his own kept gaps, for a joining would want a payer standing under it and none stood. It was not a lesson, not a grievance and not a doctrine. Nobody under the roof had found it remarkable; nothing under the roof had been settled by it. He kept the not-remarkable along with the rest, as he kept the crust and the thread and the cold and the afterdrop, which nobody had asked him to keep.

The reading answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, or for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, or for the row whose pot had gone quiet. No figure, collector or slate knew what any of them cost. A voice in the crowd asked what the levy would take now the second firing had spent its last market and the grain lay under wool. Berr took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.

\emph{The levy is called. It is not called by me. A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.}

\emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. The low voice went into the top of the crowd where the people who needed it least could lean in and hear it best. Berr went off up the clay road with the cord folded square, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the third true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The kindness was entered nowhere except in the box, and the box was locked.

\scenebreak

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road toward \place{Greybank} with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. Her hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd at the rail; the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Two straps crossed her chest, the shorter let out for the long road, her thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing on the writing. She was of \place{Slate Row}, a carrier with working hands, and the road between the Cross and Greybank was the whole of her account; she joined the two ends without joining what was written at either.

To a woman who asked what she carried she said,

\emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.}

To the one who wanted more she said,

\emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.}

She answered the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, advanced and not paid, with \person{Ilva}'s ninth on another and a finger between them; no tenth was filed in the square that morning. The boy did not make three carriers into a rule, for a rule wants a payer and none stood to any of them. She was a runner and not a reader of anyone's private keeping, and she was not made one here to answer what the square had left standing.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls. A buyer's number went in before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody took him off. He was not the neat dark hand, not the Drawing collector, not the runner just gone up the road, not the woman at the well and not any of the hands filed on their own trays. He was a clerk set at a landing, and the measure he kept stayed where nobody had called it.

The bell went over the market longer than a church bell, four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and the gap was a gap in a bell and not a hearing, not a fifth stroke of the full note and not a seventh; the six hearings kept their places with an empty place the boy did not fill to make the row come out even. He did not fold the bell's four-and-a-gap into the fire's four-and-a-gap under the floor or into the boards' four nails and thumb-width knot overhead. Three counts in three places and a fourth sound in a square, and none was made to agree. The gap stayed in the square where the bell had struck it, and it was not brought under any roof.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a silent woman stood looking at pots and turned a cup a quarter without lifting it, then set it back exactly where it had stood. The sweet green of \lore{lead glaze} lay in the crease beside her lip, clay dried pale under her nails in the small crescents, and a pale line crossed the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. The green stayed a colour on a place of its own, and no name was spoken over it; no eighth green came to the square, and the pale line was not joined to the cup or to the green or to any ear. She was not Fenna in the telling of it. At the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one turned its head a half-beat late toward a pot set down near the eel shade. A man's shoulders sat on a child's frame, silt had dried pale in the creases of the wrists, and a gap showed where a front tooth had been; a green sliver of lead glaze on a string at the throat caught the light when the shape turned and went dull when it turned back. It was a shape and not a name, joined to no loaf, no slate and no sale; no eighth shape stood up beside it and the half-turn was not made a cause. It was not Ulda in the telling of it, and nobody spoke to it and it spoke to nobody. The square went on buying and selling over both, and the bell's gap stayed unfilled under the noise.

He put the question of the third reading to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's low note and held for the small intake; it did not borrow the bell's four, four and a gap to mark the length of the hold, and it did not come down with the bell's gap. The river kept its own spacing through the finger's-width door, low over lower, and the bell kept its own in the square, four, four and a gap; between them lay a finger's width of air and no stroke supplied to agree them. The hand came down on the river's next low note, down on the sound and not past it, while the market noise and the bell's gap went on where they were.

It was one hundred and thirteen, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twelve with no payer named, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination, not a longer reach, not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of it, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift. The inch was still an inch; it had grown by three honest objects across three readings and had not once closed two inches of air.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella lifted the basket out of the post's thin shade and carried him home at the half-pace, giving the instruction to the wool and the river wind and saying nothing of what had been read over him or of the figure that had gone twice above his head. She went wide around the seven places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the ridge of \lore{Ash} had turned and been burned and called dealt with, the finer grey hairline the house called the thaw, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay and the yard entered them into nothing, the round ring on the boards where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word in it, \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} beside it bare and dark and its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven in a row were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid, not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain lying covered under wool at the rack end. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Smoke 28, 29 or 30 and none had gone down it; the tile debt stood circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right without a look at his face, left the thread frozen, told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, counted under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and went to her own bed. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally lying beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along the two-gapped row without giving either gap a figure. The widow's mark hung where it hung, touched once before the kiln on Smoke 1 and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, yard, market, morning, afternoon, dusk or going of the light on Smoke 28, 29 or 30, because a bed, a basket, a market and a going of the light are not openings. Smoke went out without a boundary drawn and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong; Frost came on in the dark beyond the door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

The dressed grain still lay covered and unfigured on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth. The third arithmetic had been read over it in the square and had gone up the road in a locked box, and it did not know what the grain cost; the space between the figure read and the price of the grain stayed unsigned. Nobody under the roof had found this remarkable, and the finding he kept of it was his own and worth nothing to anyone here. He did not lay it across the house's not-doings or across his own kept gaps, for a joining would want a payer standing under it and none stood.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water. Under the floor the carried fire stopped at four with its silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed for two hundred and sixty-nine days. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. In the square the bell's gap stood where the bell had struck it, and it was not brought under the roof and not heard as a seventh of the full note. No fire-sitter stood over the coals to supply a count and no house count was there to borrow.

Two hundred and sixty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and eighty-nine days were told by nobody. One hundred and thirteen honest answers stood on the sixth place and none of them was a reach. The figure read was still not the figure paid, the printed figure stood unchallenged, and three readings were three and not one account. The seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given. The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking, and the day went on around him without a boundary drawn in it, the covered grain at the rack end keeping its price to itself and the third arithmetic gone up the road.
