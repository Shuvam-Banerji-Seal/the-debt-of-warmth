\daymark{193}{Ember 14, Y.H. 813 (Fourthday)}

The kiln month's second working week ran on without anybody drawing a boundary under the first. The district went on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month, and neither name was corrected, because a name left standing is a name nobody has to answer for. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} and was one hundred and ninety-three days old and six months and thirteen days, and nobody under any roof said so. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and the space at the end of a row, and a day begun was a day begun whether anybody counted it or not. The count stood where he had set it in the dark, one hundred and ninety-three exact, complete and unspent, a thing laid out on a towel that did not exist and could not now be put down. He had stopped expecting to be told a number, which was not the same as not wanting one. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-three days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing that had never been counted among the hearings and had never been invented to stand up as a seventh; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under them and no payer stood to any of them.

The morning came by the nose before it came by the eye. Hot clay lay under tallow, and wet wool lay under that, and below both came the sour mineral breath of the corner that no broom in Low Kiln had been given an instruction about in two years; the household went around that corner and gave the broom no word about it. He knew the order of the smells before the grey showed at the window. The crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was still frozen, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it too had reached the end of its own instruction; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. The body was allowed to humiliate him. A man who had once reduced shoulders in the dark could not lift a crust two inches to his own mouth, and the dry joke of it had long since stopped being funny enough to mention.

\person{Sella Vail} came in with the grey, bringing woodsmoke and wet wool ahead of her, and gave the morning its instruction before she gave it to any person. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she told the wool. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, told the pot to stand where it stood, told the broom what it would be wanted for before anybody had asked for the broom, and told the running water to come when it was called. She gave no instruction to the basket and no reason to the basket, and she said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She kept her half-pace. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the low light, and the burn closed on nothing when her hand had nothing to hold.

She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, and wiped the spoon on her sleeve and did not wipe his mouth. At \place{the hearth} she turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. Her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and then went away. She did not look toward \place{the bed}.

\lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go; nobody had praised what it mended and nobody had seen it broken, and there had been no opening since. A morning was not an opening and a shelf was not a door. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred.

A woman come up from the racks with \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the pots would want before the kiln month was out. Sella, standing half a pace from an open slate with the basket low at her side, did not give her a figure. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” she said. “The house has said it.” The woman carried the question away level with nothing slopped, priced, refused or entered. A cost asked as a direction went to the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere, and this was the household's whole method for staying warm; the boy filed the answer on a place of its own and made no cause of it. No third gap came into the fold, no likeness stood up out of the yard's absences and called itself a cause; he took them down one at a time as he had taken down all the others, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

In the middle of the morning she carried him out, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The arm had carried him a hundred and ninety-three mornings and kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and gave no account of cost; it would have said, if a man could ask it, that the weight was the same on this morning as on the first, which is true, and that the cost was not the same, which is also true and entered nowhere. In \place{the Clay Yard} the kilns stood at their heat in the kiln month's second working week, and woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys while the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the morning as they had passed every morning of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either.

Three or four men unnamed had gone low into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} before first grey and had got no name from the house, and what passed beyond the door the boy did not see. The two shut-room tellings stayed two with no third. Beyond \place{the Vail wheel-shed} the wheel turned at \person{Jorra Vail}'s hand, its low note coming through the wall and going out into the yard without anybody making a cause of it; the shed door kept its finger's width of open and was entered by nobody the boy could see.

She went around the places as she would go around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody entered them, and the yard went around the space as wide as if a hand had been set there and told to keep it. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years lay along the skirting of the Vail wall, and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction; the household went around both and gave the broom no word about either. On the wall the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer; its round ring waited in the dust with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no chip of a rim, and she went around the round place as if the pan still held water a child might spill. At \place[the yard well]{the well} the \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. In the returning line she went around the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and around the place the two carriers had crossed with the glaze-room hand between them, each with a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The yard had learned to carry every absence with a glance and a wide step, and the boy read the space a hand did not go into as carefully as the space it did.

The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind again, was taken as a direction and not a name and not a cause, and went out on the same wind. The pot that had sung itself out early stood swept around where it stood and was not made an omen and not made true as a lesson. Nobody named what had ailed the man carried out of this yard in the kiln month, no collector was called, no figure was given and no slate was opened; his pale line lay where it had been laid, on a fifth place across nothing, and the cost of his carrying stood entered nowhere. He had gone out of the yard breathing and had gone on up \place{the clay road}, and a breath kept is not an ending. No sickness was named in \lore{Grathic} and no lesson was made of the glaze.

\scenebreak

Jorra came through from the shed with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the boy read the dust parted from the slip the way a surgeon parts a clot from the vessel that holds it, two things that had lain near one another and were not the same thing, and wrote no cause across either. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. The man took Jorra's silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying; the knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid, and no pot was lost in the yard. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket and did not say the room was cold.

At \place{the kindling} under the wall \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took up the broom only after the broom had been told what it was wanted for. The small knife lay at the edge of its sill with six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, the sentence of the man who turned back at the frozen ford. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” Tarn told the wood. To a neighbour wanting the thaw early and another wanting it late he said “Yes, house” to both, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it.

The breath from Seed 16 stood unspent at the top of every reach, with the small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and to the threshold of the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it. Nine stoppings stood as objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch and back without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped, joined to no cause and to no ear. Above the shed in \place{the Debt Loft} the warm stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving; from below the boy kept only the remembering of the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, a sound from a room he could not enter, filed on a place of its own and not made a cause.

Back under the roof she set the basket low at the angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and moved about the room with the ordinary work of a quiet morning. The charcoal tally waited under the front edge for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come; her finger did not go beneath it, and the beat that had stayed under the board on the night of Day 166 stayed where it had been left. An afternoon was not that night and a woman's back turned was not the same as a finger held under a board for a counted breath; he had spent a hundred and ninety-three days taking likenesses down one at a time and did not let this one stand up and call itself a return of that one. She moved her lips along the row above him, standing with her back to the basket, and gave nobody a figure; the row stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze without a touch. The one stiff brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. He did not lay the three across one another. The six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed beside and never among, the sixth out of a person filed where a thing out of a person must go, and no seventh had come in a hundred and ninety-three days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid; the knowing Jorra kept back stood beside them and was not promoted into a hearing, and no pot was lost. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth and the carried man's pale line on a fifth, and no word was spoken over any of them and no payer named. The told bell stood beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid, and the shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust and its gap, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. He wanted all of it to mean something and would not let it stand up and mean something, because a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of it.

Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold, as it had every day of his life, and one hundred and ninety-three stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool. The sweet edge of lead glaze went out on the same wind that had brought it, and the yard answered it with work instead of a cause: the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, the grey dust on Jorra's forearms going past the bend of the little finger, no name and no number left behind.
