The afternoon went on thinning after the dust had passed out of the yard, losing its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun. Heat left the high air first and went down into the low places, behind the pot, beneath the shelf, into the fold of wool where the boy lay. It came down the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} in a slow slant --- that roof which stopped at the edge of his reach --- and stopped there of itself, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. He lay at the low angle under it with the crust leathery two inches from his ear, its empty place unnamed and no third, and the thread of drool frozen at his mouth, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. The kiln drew breath in fours behind the wall with the gap where the fifth should have been; through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-three days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; overhead the boards kept four nails, four and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under them and no payer stood to any of them. \person[Corin]{The boy} was one hundred and ninety-three days old and six months and thirteen days, and nobody under any roof said so. He had stopped expecting to be told a number, which was not the same as not wanting one.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the row standing with two gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6, and gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge where the charcoal tally waited, so the marks lay unread, uncounted, uncopied, and the beat that had stayed under the board on the night of Day 166 stayed where it had been left; an afternoon was not that night, and a woman's back turned was not the same as a finger held under a board for a counted breath, and he had spent a hundred and ninety-three days taking likenesses down one at a time and did not let this one stand up and call itself a return of that one. “It's the thaw,” she told the fire. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, fed them, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale in the dying light. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on the first morning of Ember and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since; nobody had praised what it mended and nobody had seen it broken, and an afternoon was not an opening.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} under the wall the small knife lay at the edge of its sill with six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, the sentence of the man who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been told what it was wanted for. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire that was low and to the thaw that would come early and the thaw that would come late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line along the skirting, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the small interval standing where the breath should have gone in. Nine stoppings stood as objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch and back without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped, joined to no cause and to no ear. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it. No collector came up \place{the clay road} and no collector went down it.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}; he kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up on a wrong wind who said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early; the man took the silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying, and the knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. No pot was lost in the yard. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket.

When Sella's voice went low and unfinished at the hearth --- “The fire's low. It wants---” --- his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and stood there while she finished \emph{feeding} and gave the end of the instruction to the pot. Then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing in the iron. It was a remembered repetition, not a change made again, and it lay on neither of the other two places; he did not pair it with the fraction at the iron on the morning of Day 161, nor with the beat held under the board on Day 166, nor with the half-check on the flagstones on Day 171, and no word was spoken over any of them. To the boy at the low angle the damper was one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, the bite that keeps a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man; there was no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful. It was an object joined to no kiln and entered in no slate, worth nothing and exact, and he set it down where it stood and wrote no cause across it.

He lay where the underside of the shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in its slow slant and stopped at the same place. The two adults under the one roof had each held something back at a threshold they would not cross: a breath drawn at the top of a reach and not spent, a finger standing in cold iron until a sentence arrived at a pot. He had watched them do it all the days of his life without a word passing between them, and had filed each holding on a place of its own because three likenesses standing up together will call themselves a rule if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer. Now his own hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It did not come down at once. It stayed up for the length of the small pause in the kiln's breath before the next pull, that little gap where the fifth should have been, and only then came down against the fold. It was a fraction held on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes, not a payment, with no payer named and no cause allowed to stand up out of it. He could not close the two inches to the pot handle. He could not lift his chin to the crust. A man who had once reduced shoulders in the dark could hold one hand an inch above a fold for one small pause, and the body was allowed to humiliate him even as it moved. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the number somebody would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and did not stop wanting them.

At the going of the light she carried him home up the clay road into \place{Low Kiln}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-three mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and gave no account of cost; it would have said, if a man could ask it, that the weight was the same on this evening as on the first, which is true, and that the cost was not the same, which is also true and entered nowhere.

She went around the places as she would go around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. On the wall the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer; its round ring waited in the dust with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no chip of a rim, and she went around the round place as if the pan still held water a child might spill. At \place[the yard well]{the well} \place{the trough} stood bare and dark and its water was called weather. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody entered them, and the yard went around the space as wide as if a hand had been set there and told to keep it. In the returning line she went around the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and around the place the two carriers had crossed with the glaze-room hand between them, each with a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind and was taken as a direction and not a name and not a cause, and went out on the same wind; nobody revived the carried man as a cause, called a collector or gave a figure, and the cost of his carrying stood entered nowhere. She carried past the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it without sweeping either and without giving the broom an instruction about either.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was the thaw, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot, and did not touch the widow's mark, because a dusk is not an opening and there had been no opening since before the kiln on the first morning of Ember. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country, older than the house and older than the woman asleep in the room that does not touch this one. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in a hundred and ninety-three days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on the wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard.

On the widened tray nine stoppings stood --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any of them, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stands to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given; the loan that can hear was uncalled; the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach with the small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not. The damper lay beside them as one piece of iron with two bites, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction of the morning of Day 161 at the cold iron, the beat that stayed under the board on the night of Day 166, the half-check on the flagstones on Day 171 --- and the loft-sill scrape stood on a fourth and the carried man's pale line on a fifth, exact, complete and worth nothing, with no word spoken over any and no payer named. The told bell stood beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid; the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps, the pan's gone ring and the field's empty place lay on trays that did not touch. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause, and no finger of his drew the line: three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and ninety-three days taking them down one at a time, with nobody to help and nobody to see.

And on a place of its own, laid down last and not laid across anything, lay the fraction of the afternoon: the hand going up an inch toward the note of water over water, holding for the length of the small pause in the kiln's breath, and coming down against the fold while in the other room a woman counted under her breath toward no opening and the two rooms did not touch. It was not a reaching. A reaching would have wanted the handle standing two inches off, and a want with a payer under it is an account; this was an inch held and let go, filed where a thing that has no name must go, worth nothing, exact, and the only thing in the room nobody could enter. He had spent three days watching other people hold their breath at thresholds they would not cross, and now his own body had held something back for the length of a small pause and let it go without a word, and the file had gained one small physical object that it refused to promote into a rule.

One hundred and ninety-three stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. The charcoal waited under the shelf for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come; the stone lay cold and unreconciled in its pot in the loft; the mark was touched once before the kiln and at no other hour; the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The count stood where it had been set, edge to edge with the day that had gone before it and ready for the one that had not yet begun, and the boy's hand lay outside the wool with the inch still in it, going forward by that much and by no more.
