\daymark{194}{Ember 15, Y.H. 813 (Fifthday)}

The morning came up over \place{Low Kiln} and into the \place{Vail kiln-house} the way heat comes up through a banked fire: not all at once, and not by any door anybody had opened. \person[Corin]{The boy} woke in the fold with the smell of hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and beneath both the sour mineral breath of the corner that had not been swept in two years. First sound was the kiln drawing in fours behind the wall, three pulls and a fourth and then the gap where the fifth should have been, a breathing that was not a bell and had never been taken for one under this roof. Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and kept the silence where its fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-four days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under them and no payer stood to any of them. He was one hundred and ninety-four days old and six months and fourteen days, and nobody under any roof said so. He had stopped expecting to be told a number, which was not the same as not wanting one.

The one stiff brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third. The thread of drool had frozen in the small hours and was still there, unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down was a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the handle of the pot standing two inches beyond his hand, and wanted a number somebody would have known what a number counted; he got none of them and could ask for none of them. At the edge of the wool his hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold at once, as it had come down every morning of his life. He was a trauma surgeon who could not lift his own chin to bread. The body was allowed to humiliate him, and the morning was allowed to go on.

\person{Sella Vail} came in with the basket against her hip and gave the morning its orders before she gave it to anybody, to the wool to keep the wind off his ears, to the fire that it was the thaw, to the pot to stand where it was told, to the broom to go around, to the running water to keep to \place{the trough}, and to the river wind at the door's finger's width to keep its note where it was. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and held the half-pace she kept from every open slate though there was no slate open under this roof. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without touching it. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, wiped the spoon on her sleeve and did not wipe his mouth, and gave no instruction to the basket for any of it. “Keep it off his ears,” she told the wool. “It's the river wind.”

She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, told the fire it was the thaw and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Then she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand. Her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore[the widow's mark]{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, touched once before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, morning or dusk since; nobody had praised what it mended and nobody had seen it broken, and a morning and a shelf were not openings. There had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1.

\scenebreak

Before first grey the kiln month's second working week was drawing toward its end with no boundary drawn under the first; the district went on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and neither was corrected in the yard. In \place{the Clay Yard} the kilns stood at their heat and the dry smell of clay giving up its water lay over everything, with \lore{slip} ground small as meal under the nails of every hand that had worked there since the fires were brought up. Sella carried him out at the edge of the work and set the basket where no slate lay, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. A woman come up from \place{the drying racks} with a wet pot in her hands and the dry heat already pulling at its rim asked what a wet thing cost set down in that heat. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” Sella told the pot, and the woman carried her question away level, with nothing slopped, priced, refused or entered. A man come off \place{the clay road} with the morning's figure still moving in his mouth asked what the kiln would want now the figure had gone up the road. “The house has said it,” she said. He went away level too. The printed one in twelve stood where it had stood, unchallenged by anybody under this roof; the true subtraction sat in a locked box somewhere up the road, and no hand in the yard could reach back into it. A figure said twice over a basket was not a figure entered, and a figure entered was not a figure paid. Nothing of either went into the basket.

The yard went around its places as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Along the wall \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and left a clean dark line, and beside it lay the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw}; neither had been swept and neither had been given an instruction. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where nobody entered them, and the yard went around the space as wide as if a hand had been set there and told to keep it. On the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed there was now a round ring in the dust with no scrape, no crumb, no print and no chip of a rim. At \place[the yard well]{the well} the trough stood bare and dark, and its water was called weather. In the returning line there was the empty place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, and beyond it the place the two carriers had crossed with the glaze-room hand between them; each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation given to the basket. The third quiet morning was the fullest yet. The yard had learned to carry every absence with a look and a wide step, and the boy had learned to read the space a hand did not go into as carefully as the space it did; he set the dark line, the round ring, the rack space, the field's place and the carriers' place on trays that did not touch, because a row of absences left standing will call itself an account if a man leaves it long enough to want an answer.

A sweetish edge came up off \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind, the taste of \lore{lead glaze} carried over the terrace and into a yard where no Vail pot had asked for it. It was taken as a direction and not a name and not a cause, and went out again on the same wind. Nobody revived the man who had been carried out of the kiln and across this yard as a cause, called a collector, gave a figure or put a word to what had ailed him; \lore{Grathic} had no word that would carry it, and if it had, nobody in the yard would have asked him for it. He had gone out of the yard breathing and had gone on breathing up the clay road, and the pale line at his gum lay on a place of its own, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, not joined to the sweet wind, the dust ground small as meal, the pot that had sung itself out early or any absence in the yard. That pot stood swept around and was not made into an omen, and the saying from High Kiln that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early was not made true as a lesson so soon after the carrying-out.

Before first grey three or four men with their collars high had gone low through the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, and nobody under the roof gave them a name and nobody said the room was cold. The two tellings of the shut counting room stayed two with no third, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. \person{Jorra Vail} came through from the shed with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip; he kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that the pot would give three turns before it left his hands, and withheld the knowing from a man come up on the wrong wind who said a pot singing early generally sang itself out early; the man took the silence for agreement and went off up the yard with his saying, and the knowing went to the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. No pot was lost. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates stayed ungiven and not withdrawn. He did not look toward the basket.

At \place{the kindling} under the wall the small knife lay at the edge of its sill with six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, and \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence beside it, the sentence of the man who turned back at the frozen ford. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been told what it was wanted for. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood. He said \emph{Yes, house} to an early thaw and a late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His chest had been tight since the dark; Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and all the way to the word that would call the loan, and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath he should have spent at the top of the reach and did not spend it, the Seed 16 breath standing where the breath should have gone in. Nine stoppings stood as objects with no tenth before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, and the loan that can hear stayed uncalled. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch and back without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and no collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it.

\scenebreak

All morning the file went with him and came back with him, exact and worth nothing: six hearings of the full note beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, with no seventh and none invented to fill the empty place; three near-full answers on their own tray, advanced and not paid; nine stoppings as objects with no tenth standing up before the seventh year was earned; three held changes on three places with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth and the carried man's pale line on a fifth, no word spoken over any and no payer named. The told bell stood beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid. Beneath \place{the loaf shelf} the charcoal waited unread for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come, and Sella's finger did not go beneath its front edge, so the marks lay uncounted, uncopied and unread. The shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed 6; the fold kept its one crust and its empty place; the pan's gone place kept its round ring. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line, because three likenesses standing up together will call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing long enough to want an answer, and he had spent a hundred and ninety-four days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

The stone lay cold and unreconciled in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and not looked at in a year; from below he kept only the remembering of the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone, filed on a place of its own and not made into a cause. When the morning's work had gathered itself into the heat of the day, Sella carried him back up the short length of the clay road under the roof, with the instruction going to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars and the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing across the yard as they had passed every morning of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-four mornings set the basket down at the height it had used on the first and with the same turn of the wrist at the end of a step, and gave no sign of counting any of them. She went wide around the place the carriers had crossed with a look that asked for nothing and found nothing. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was the thaw, banked the coals with the back of a broken pot and did not touch the widow's mark, because a shelf and a morning are not openings and there had been none since before the kiln on Ember 1. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name.

He lay at the low angle where the underside of the loaf shelf made a roof that stopped at the edge of his reach, and the heat came down that roof in a slow slant and stopped at the same place, as if it too had been given an instruction and had come to the end of it. The crust was leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the handle of the pot stood two inches beyond his hand. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door. It came down against the fold at once, as it had come down every morning of his life; there was no holding in it yet, no fraction kept on a place of its own, only the old small motion and the old small humiliation of a man who could not close two inches on a pot handle. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the number, and got none of them and did not stop wanting them. One hundred and ninety-four stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; through the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others. The charcoal waited, the stone lay cold, the mark was untouched, the loan was uncalled, and the cost of the man carried out stood entered nowhere. Nothing had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. One hundred and ninety-four stood exact in the wool, and the hand went forward into the rest of the day by an inch and by no more.
