# Chapter 66 — The Figure Over the Wool

Days 249–251, Smoke 10–12, Tenthday to Secondday, Y.H. 813. Normal chapter: three lived days, six scenes, two entries to a day. Every day has its own `\daymark` in the first scene of that day only — `\daymark{249}{Smoke 10, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}`, `\daymark{250}{Smoke 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}`, `\daymark{251}{Smoke 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813}` — and each is lived in full in two scenes of well over eight hundred words. No day skipped, poured into another or summarised. Weekdays checked against *t* = 336 + *N* − 1 and the tend rule: Smoke 1 (Day 240) = Firstday, so Smoke 10 (Day 249) = Tenthday, the first Account-day and market of the second firing; 250 = Smoke 11, Firstday; 251 = Smoke 12, Secondday. Smoke 20 (Day 259), the second Account-day, stands eight mornings beyond the close and is not brought early. There is no market, no Cross and no bell on Smoke 11 and 12; Smoke 10 is that morning's work and is not brought into the small hours of Scene 1.

## The one meaningful change

Smoke's first reading comes to the Cross and the district's arithmetic changes for the first time in Smoke and cannot be undone (T015/T041/T047, T077 advanced, not paid). Berr Clay comes up short off the clay road with the red wool cord folded square under his coat and the green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner, wipes his thumb twice before touching nothing, holds a narrow slate unopened to his forearm, passes a finger over the figure and reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the crowd, the figure going twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for those who came late; he calls the levy, denies it with *your house*, never *I*, and goes off toward High Kiln with the box locked and the true subtraction inside, believing he has done a kindness. The printed figure stands unchallenged and the figure read aloud is still not the figure paid. It answers nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or the hand that went in on Seed 15, and the figure read over the dressed grain lying covered under wool at home does not know what that grain cost; the space between the two figures has no signature. Nothing under the Vail roof is settled by the reading: the grain stays covered and unfigured, the five askings stay five with no witness, the ninth hand stays nine, the charcoal stays unread and the loan stays uncalled. Hessa Rale carries the slate up the clay road edge-on and unread, the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing, the bell goes four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and Sella carries the basket home at the going of the light saying nothing of what was read over it.
