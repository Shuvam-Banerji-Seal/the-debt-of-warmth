\daymark{250}{Smoke 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

A day comes onto a house the way a loaf comes onto a board: it is set down among the others and nothing about it says which of them it was cut from. The count went forward into two hundred and fifty with nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} to say so, and six months and seventy days stood where six months and sixty-nine had stood, exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. \person[Corin]{the boy} kept it anyway, as a man keeps a blade oiled through a winter when there is nothing in the room to cut; keeping it was a habit of the hands and not a belief, and he had long ago given up asking who would have put a voice to it. The wanting under the not-asking went on the way a coal goes on under ash, which is a thing a house can go around without ever sweeping.

Yesterday's reading had gone up the road in a locked box and the arithmetic of the district had changed with it, and the figure read over the basket was still not the figure paid. Nothing under this roof had been made wiser by the change. The dressed grain lay where it had been set, covered and unfigured, and the space between the number spoken over it at the Cross and what the grain had cost kept its own counsel and had no signature. Smoke's first subtraction rode up \place{the clay road} in \person[Berr Clay]{Berr}'s box with the true figure shut inside, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188 or for the hand that went in on Seed 15; a box locked is a keeping, and a keeping is not a debt called in. The boy did not open it by thinking about it. He had learned that the mind can lift a lid as neatly as any clerk, and that a figure taken without a payer is only a theft done more quietly.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the first smell was hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and under both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall \lore{the carried fire} drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood there as it had stood for two hundred and fifty days with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it, its payer without a name. There was no market on a Firstday, no \place[the Cross]{Cross} and no bell; the square and its pigs and its two hundred leaners belonged to yesterday and had gone home with it. Smoke 20 (Day 259), the second Account-day and market of the second firing, stood eight mornings beyond the close and was not brought into a morning that had not earned it. A day not yet arrived does not answer to a hand that reaches early for it, and a figure not yet read is not a figure owed.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing going over a lower thing and never counted among the hearings; under the floor the fire kept its four and its silence; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, for agreeing any two of them would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed where it had sounded yesterday, beside the told bell and not folded into the three. He kept the trays apart without congratulating himself on it. A discipline is a thing a hand uses; a belief is a thing a man stands under, and he had only the hand, and it could go up an inch and no further.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to lie down and make the row come out even, because a kindness entered in a figure is the beginning of an account and this house gave without entering. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as though a line had been written out for it to that length and no longer; it neither went further nor went away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the severe face cold wears and the face a man wears when he is warmed too fast; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and that the warming must come from the outside and slowly. He knew all of it and wanted the crust. Both were true and the second was louder, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all while the register goes on being exact inside a body that cannot lift a crust two inches to its mouth. The surgeon took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. She told it the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting go --- and pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn so the one grey streak showed pale off the coals; the burn shaped like a little door closed on nothing and opened again to the work. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and she did not touch it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a road or a basket on this morning, because the hand goes to it before the kiln and at no other hour. The one opening before Smoke 1 lay behind the household and she did not look at it again and made no lesson of it and named no payer under it. The fire answered four low pulls and kept its fifth where it had always kept it, and she fed it as if it had given her no reason to count what it lacked.

On \place{the drying racks} she lifted the dressed grain out from under its wool for the damp and put her fingers into it to the knuckle, spread it thin on the flat stone, turned it, gathered it back by the handful and set the fold flat again. It changed state and gained no figure; she counted it in glances and handfuls and by the weight of the carrying arm, which knows weight and not cost, and entered it on no slate and laid it across none of the absences. The figure spoken over it yesterday went up the road with the box and did not know what the grain had cost, and the boy did not turn the weight of the fold into a number to make the two agree. A thing counted by handfuls is a thing kept by handfuls, and a handful is not a measure any clerk has thought to enter.

\scenebreak

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the keeping would take now the grain was under wool. Sella answered the question put and not the one behind it, and sent both to \place{the hearth}. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}. The woman went off level with her question still on her, carrying it the way a person carries a thing that has not been given a figure. A thing not priced is not therefore free; it is a thing whose payer has not been named, and a payer not named is not a payer forgiven. The pot sat where it sat and the weather went on being weather, and neither took the question off her hands, which was the answer and the whole of it.

A man come down the clay road with dried pale mud-scurf on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. He was not refusing it and he was not giving it; he was keeping it where no thumb could read it, and a sleeve is no slate. The boy read the shape of the paper without the writing on it, a chart turned face-down, and did not call it a missing figure, because a figure kept is not a figure lost and the difference is the whole of the keeping. A fist closed on a number and opened on nothing; a sleeve took it back; the road went on up toward \place{the terrace} and \place{High Kiln} and gave the yard no figure while the yard gave the road none. No collector came up or went down the clay road on Smoke 11.

At \place{the loaf shelf} Sella stood with her back to the basket and did not look into it. She moved her lips along the row standing with two gaps and gave nobody a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing and came away; she did not touch the jar and did not say what it held. Her finger stayed on top of the shelf's front edge, not beneath it, so the charcoal tally under the board stayed unread and uncounted; a mark nobody reads is still a mark kept, and a mark read wants a figure put under it, and a figure wants a payer. She set the pot rim out and turned its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting that was neither a look toward the bed nor a look away from it. Two inches of air was the whole of the distance, and the hand could not close it, and the setting went on being a setting and not a kindness entered.

The yard went around its walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the ridge of \lore{Ash} had turned, and the finer hairline of grey beside it called the thaw, neither swept; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, gone now with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place[the trough]{the bare dark trough} beside the well with its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand went in on Seed 15 and did not come back. Seven places gone around with a look and a wide step, and they were not an account until a payer stood under them, and none stood. He did not draw a finger between the pan's gone ring and the shelf's two gaps, or the fold's one crust and its unnamed gap, or the field's empty place; four absences on four trays with a finger between every pair, and a fourth likeness made a cause would want a payer and none stood to any. The yard gave each thing its own room and went on.

\scenebreak

At the yard well \person{Ilva} drew water with the pail against her knee, clay dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the grey rack dust worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out. She was of the yard and not of the house, and nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. A woman with a pot asked what the firings of Smoke had cost.

Her hand stayed where it was. It did not go into her sleeve; the short strip of birch bark with its notches stayed where it was kept, pale where a thumb had rubbed it and dark in the uncut grain between, and her eyes went to the asker and her mouth answered the question put and not the one behind it.

\emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.}

She said no figure and gave no name to the thing she kept, and went on with her water, the pail knocking the rim of the well and the water in it called weather.

The boy did not file a tenth hand-shape. The ninth stayed where it had been set, on a place of its own beside the eighth with a finger between it and every other private keeping; nine likenesses are nine and must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any. The order of reaching was a shape and the shape was all he had taken the first time, and he did not take a second shape from a hand that did not reach, because a shape taken from a not-reaching would be a shape made to make the row come out even. He had spent two hundred and fifty days learning what a shape is worth when it stands alone and what it is worth the moment a man puts two of them under one cause. Eight hands had reached to their records before the person asking; a ninth had kept her hand in her sleeve and answered with her mouth, and the two orders stood on separate trays with a finger between even these. The notched birch stayed in her sleeve, not read, not asked about, not joined to the reed-cutter's cord turned so its knots stood, or the knots, or the six pages, or the tins, or the missing hand, or the pale line where a ring had been worn, or any other private keeping. She was not the woman with the neat dark hand, not \person{Hessa Rale}, not the soft-cuffed clerk set below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, not an officer, and not joined to any of them. The woman went on drawing water, and the count of the firings stayed in wood where no slate could take a figure off it.

Then he put the third question of the chapter to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and was held for the small intake only; it came down before the letting-go of the counted breath, while the breath was still going out and before the silence after it had begun. It was a fraction of air and not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a hearing of \lore{the full note}, not a seventh of it, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule; it did not cross two rhythms and borrowed no silence from the room that does not touch his. Answer seventy-five lay on the sixth place beside seventy-four with no payer named, and the surgeon turned out the file as instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Three ways of asking the same inch stood as three and not one, and none of them had grown a reach that had not been there in the small hours.

Under the floor the fire stopped at four and the silence where the fifth should have stood stood where it stood, its payer unnamed two hundred and fifty days; the count went forward into a day that had not earned its heading. The thumb stayed inside the sleeve and the woman answered the question put with her mouth and went on drawing water; the boy's hand went up an inch, held for the small intake only and came down before the letting-go; the ninth hand-shape stayed on its own tray and no tenth was filed.
