\person[Corin]{The boy} came down to \place{the Cross} inside a fold of wool, carried at the half-pace Sella keeps from every open slate, and was set in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay. \person{Sella Vail} set the basket down the way she would set a pot down out of a draught, with the back of her hand and without looking into it, and gave it neither an instruction nor a reason. The instruction went to the wool and to the river wind that came up off the boards, found the shade and went around it. \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind}, she said, and stood half a pace off. A basket set down is a pot set down and left, and a thing left owes nobody an account of itself.

The cold iron of the post came through the wicker to his cheek and stopped there as the cold always stopped, at the place where the order written out for it ran out. The crust in the fold lay two inches from his ear, stiff and leathery and brown at the edge, with the empty place beside it keeping its name to itself and no third coming to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry morning and was unwiped. He took the inventory in the order the facts arrived and entered none of it. Under the shade the warm side of him was going thin; over the square the sun was on two hundred backs at once, and the square held its heat badly, the way a shallow bowl holds water, spilling it on every side.

Eight pigs went over the wet boards while the light was still coming up. They came nose to tail, driven by a boy with a stick who did not look at the crowd; the boards were slick and the pigs slipped and caught themselves and went on, and each gave out a small flat grunt that arrived nowhere. Two hundred people leaned in over \place{the reading block}, shoulder against shoulder, each carrying a figure or a fist that no slate standing in that square had been made to enter. An assessor of Sill wrote the eight down before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand going to the slate before his eyes went to the rail, and the pigs went on over the boards and the figures went into the slate and the two things did not agree because nobody had asked them to. All morning the square stood with every withholding in the district gathered under the sun and not one of them read. A man came out of the press with a figure in his mouth and set it beside a figure a neighbour had left on the turnip heap, and the two stood there side by side the way two pots stand on a board, each whole, neither owing the other anything, and the crowd went on leaning. It was the first Account-day of the second firing, and it had not been brought into the small hours; it had earned its morning and come into it. The boy knew the shape of a thing brought to be counted and left uncounted: an instrument laid on a towel and not used, a chart turned face-down, a number spoken into a room and carried home to a shelf. A figure spoken aloud in a crowd becomes a thing the crowd will set down and go on keeping, exact and worth nothing, until somebody puts a payer under it.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} before the top of the crowd had settled. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped the thumb twice before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required. Then he read low into the crowd.

\emph{One in twelve, as the notice says.}

He read it twice. The figure went over the basket twice, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and the second reading was for people who had not yet turned their faces toward the block. The boy lay under the wool with the figure going over him and did not make the figure the paid figure, because the figure read aloud was not the figure paid and there was no signature anywhere between the two. Berr did not look into the basket. The boy thought of the man carried out on Day 188, whose cost no figure, collector or slate had entered, and of the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and not come back; the reading knew neither, and knew the cover of the thing under the wool and not the thing. The surgeon opened the thing the way he would open a wound, layer under layer, and found the cost where the cost always was, under the reading and out of reach.

A man at the rail brought a number up out of a fist and asked what the levy would take now the kilns had been brought up. Berr took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on.

\emph{A number goes into a slate, not into a bundle. If your house wants it again, there'll be a slate at the watch.}

\emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. He said the levy was called and that it was not called by him, and the low voice went into the top of the crowd where the people who needed it least could lean in and hear it best. The number went from the fist to his mouth to the shoulder of a man going up toward \place{High Kiln} and arrived nowhere, and it was not a missing figure. He went off up the clay road with the cord folded square and the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The kindness was entered nowhere except in the box, and the box was locked, and the arithmetic of the district had changed for the first time in Smoke and could not be changed back.

The boy kept the twice-wiped thumb and the finger passed over a figure on a tray of their own. He did not make a tenth shape of them and did not join them to the assessor's hand going to the slate before the pigs, or to any other order of reaching in the square; a shape is a shape, and two shapes laid together do not make a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them. Berr's proverb walked in his mouth with the Court's passive grammar braided through it, and the boy heard the two voices in the one sentence as he heard the two hand-orders in the one morning: \emph{the notice}, \emph{the figure}, \emph{the levy}, \emph{your house}, and never \emph{I}. The man had taken himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the sentence stood where he had left it with nobody in it at all.

\scenebreak

The printed figure stood unchallenged. At home the dressed grain lay under wool near the hearth, turned for damp and gathered by the handful, changed in state and given no figure; the figure read in the square would go home to that roof and no number under it would know what the grain had cost. The space between the figure read over covered grain and the price of the grain had no signature. Nothing under the Vail roof was settled by the reading: the grain stayed covered, the charcoal under the shelf stayed unread, the debt against the firing of tiles stayed neither paid nor settled nor done with, and the loan stayed uncalled. Two hundred people had stood in one square and changed the arithmetic, and the change went up the clay road in a locked box and answered nothing that any household here could ask of it. A change made in a square walks home with two hundred people and sits down at two hundred tables, and not one of them will know that there are two figures in it, one printed and one paid, with a space between them nobody signed. He did not try to join the two. Joining them would want a payer put under both and none stood to either, so the told figure lay beside the paid figure and the read figure beside the cost, each on its own tray with a finger between, and the arithmetic went on being changed without anybody under this roof being made the wiser or the poorer by the knowing of it.

The bell went over the market longer than a church bell, four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The heard bell stood beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night} and he did not lay one across the other, because agreeing them would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. The gap was a gap in a bell and not a hearing; it was not the fifth stroke of \lore{the full note} and not a seventh of it either, and the six hearings kept their places with an empty place among them that he did not fill to make the row come out even. The town went on buying and selling under the sound, the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies.

He put the second question of the day to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the bell and was held there while the strokes came, four and four, and was held on through the gap where the next stroke did not come, an interval kept against the absence of a stroke and against no sound that stood in its place. It came down on the next sound in the square, a board knocked under a pig and the grunt after it. He filed answer seventy-four on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a hearing of the full note, not a seventh of the full note, not a longer reach, not a miss entered as a finding, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. It was an inch of air held through an absent stroke and a down-stroke on what came after, the same honest fraction it had been in the small hours. Seventy-four objects lay on the sixth place now, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air.

\person{Hessa Rale} went up the clay road before noon with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm, the writing turned to her sleeve. Her right hand went to the slate before her eyes went to the crowd at the rail; the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round, and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. To a woman who asked what she carried she said,

\emph{I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.}

To the woman who wanted more she said,

\emph{Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.}

She answered the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate. \place{Greybank} lay eighteen leagues east at the end of her road and the Cross at the other end, and she joined the two places without joining what was written at either. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth record-first hand on a place of its own, advanced and not paid, held apart with a finger between it and the clerk's inward slate and with a finger between it and every other private keeping in the district; eight likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to any of them. She was a runner and not a reader, and was not made a reader here to answer what the square had left standing.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, and a buyer's number went in before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold over him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies and nobody took him off. He was not the neat dark hand, not the Drawing collector with her grey fingertips, not the soft-handed man at the Court's table, not the runner who had just gone up the road and not any of the hands filed on their own trays; he was a clerk set at a landing, and the measure he kept stayed where nobody had called it.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella took the basket up at the half-pace from every open slate and carried him home. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and said nothing of what had been read over him. The way up went around the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the new finer hairline of grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, went wide under \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, went around the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape or chip or word left in it, around \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} bare and dark beside it and its water called weather, out past the field's empty place on \place{the worked strips} and past the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come back. Each got a look and a wide step and no number. Seven in a row are still seven and not an account until somebody stands under them to pay, and nobody stood. \lore{Ash} lay in the colour of the clean dark line in the creases of a man's knuckles somewhere ahead of her, and she did not turn toward it or name it.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set the basket on \place{the bed}, not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear without looking at his face and without wiping the thread. The crust lay where it had lain and the empty place kept its name. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth should have stood; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and kept the fifth where it had always been kept, its payer without a name for two hundred and forty-nine days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing going over a lower thing. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under all three and none stood to any. The bell's four, four and a gap stayed in the square where it had sounded, beside the told bell and not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of the full note. The figure read in the square stayed beside the figure paid, and Smoke's first arithmetic was already up the clay road in Berr's locked box with the true subtraction inside, unread under this roof.

The hand outside the wool had come down on the next sound in the square after the bell's gap, and answer seventy-four lay beside seventy-three on the sixth place with no payer named. Two hundred and forty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and sixty-nine days were told by nobody. The file lay open on the towel, and the count went forward into a day that had not yet earned its heading.
