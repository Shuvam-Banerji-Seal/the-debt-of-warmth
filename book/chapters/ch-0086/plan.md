Normal chapter, three lived days, six scenes of two entries each, every day opened by its own dated `\daymark` in the first scene of that day and lived in full in two scenes well over eight hundred words each. ## Scene 2 — The First Reading of Bone

- **Day and time:** Day 309, Bone 10, Tenthday, late morning through the going of the light and into the small hours.
- **Place:** the Cross — the reading block under the moot-post, the pot board, the market's edge, the square's Slate Row fringe; below the north bridge at the out-of-line landing; then home under the Vail roof (bed, boards, towel).
- **Present:** Corin/Dan carried in the basket; Sella Vail; Berr Clay; Hessa Rale; the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk; the silent green-mouthed woman and the foundling (sightings only, no sheets); the two hundred at the Cross; unnamed sellers and buyers. Tarn, Jorra and Garl remain offstage under the roof.
- **Beats in order:**
  1. Sella sets the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of shade where no slate lay, stands half a pace off through the reading and the bell, gives the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and keeps him out of every open slate's reach.
  2. Two hundred lean over the reading block while eight pigs go over the wet boards. Berr comes up short off the clay road, red wool cord folded square under his coat, green of tasted lead glaze at one mouth-corner; wipes his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing; holds a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward; passes a finger over the figure; reads *one in twelve, as the notice says* low into the crowd, the figure going twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that came late.
  3. He calls the levy and denies it with *The levy is called. It is not called by me* and *your house* — never *I* — taking himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. He goes off up the clay road toward High Kiln with the box locked and Bone's first true subtraction inside beside Frost's three, believing he has done a kindness. The printed figure stands unchallenged, the figure read is still not the figure paid, and no signature stands on the space between (T015/T041/T047).
  4. **THE MEANINGFUL CHANGE:** four arithmetics of the year now stand behind one printed figure. Four are four and a rule made from them is refused, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The finding sits on a place of its own with no payer, not a lesson, grievance, mending or doctrine, not laid across the house's not-doings, the covered grain, the four held changes or the boy's own kept gaps (T061/T082).
  5.   6. The bell goes four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The heard bell stays in its square beside the told bell of the Long Night; its gap is not made a seventh hearing, a fifth pull or a payer (T078).
  7. Hessa Rale takes Berr's slate up the clay road toward Greybank before noon, edge-on, writing to her sleeve, her hand to the slate before her eyes to the crowd. To the women who ask what she carries: *I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which* and *Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk*. No figure comes off the slate; the eighth record-first hand stays eight (T080).
  8. Below the north bridge the soft-cuffed clerk stays set at the out-of-line landing for a measure nobody called, cuff grey from washing, slate to his forearm writing inward, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; the market sells over him as water over a stone. He is filed apart from Marra, the Drawing collector, the Court-table clerk, Berr and Hessa (T090, T080).
  9. Sightings only: across the square at the pot board a silent woman with clay under her nails, a pale line on the back of one hand and the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip turns a cup a quarter and sets it back exactly; at the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy never measured for one, a man's shoulders on a child's frame, silt pale in the wrist creases, a gap where a front tooth, a green sliver on a string that catches the light on the turn and goes dull going back, turns half a beat after a pot is set down with a small flat sound.   10. The fifth burned-slate telling is carried through the reading untouched — no witness, no sixth, no hand at the flame, no payer — and the claim gathers interest against Jorra's ungiven promise while he neither pays nor refuses (T035/T031/T065/T095).
  11. Sella carries him home at the going of the light saying nothing. In the small hours outside the wool the hand takes a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his and lets it go whole on the river's note: **answer 194**, a restraint and not a payment, not carried into the fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap or the river's low over lower.
- **Emotional turn:** the district's arithmetic changes and cannot be changed back, while the boy's own keeping does not move — the reading is an event done to the square, not to the basket; the surgeon's ear hears the self-removal in Berr's grammar the way it once heard a chart signed past the note.
- **Sensory anchors:** wet boards under eight pigs; the moot-post's finger's width of cold-iron shade; the square of printed notice soft at one corner; red wool cord folded under a coat; glaze-green at a mouth-corner; thumb wiped twice on an apron; slate face turned to a sleeve; a bell longer than a church bell with a gap in it; clay pale under nails; a green sliver catching light and going dull; the river wind under the fold; the wool over the ears.
