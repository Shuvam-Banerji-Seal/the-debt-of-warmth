In the afternoon of that Firstday \person{Sella Vail} carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down through \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace she kept from every open slate, with the fold drawn over his ears against the river wind and the instruction going to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and she carried him the way a pot is carried that must not be slopped: level, with the whole of her attention in the forearm and none of it in her face. The wind came under the fold in small cold fingers and went out again. Above her the sky was the colour of \lore{lead glaze} over a grey dish, and the chimneys of \place{Low Kiln} stood with no smoke worth the naming.

He took the afternoon as a wound is taken, in the order its parts arrive. The crust lay two inches from his ear, leathery now and brown-edged, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the days behind to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen where it lay and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it; \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody here would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure and the ease that cold puts on a face which is not ease. He wanted the crust lifted two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip. Both were true and the second was louder, and the older man took the inventory while the infant kept the want, neither winning, the argument itself the work.

The yard came to him the way a field comes to a man checking it before the light: by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie. Rope knocked against a well-rim somewhere. A pot was set down out of sight and gave its small flat knock and went quiet. A broom moved over stone. Under the fold he heard the river, and under that the floor of the house not yet under him, and he kept the sounds on separate places, as he kept everything that nearly touched.

She went around the places the way she went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. At the Vail corner the clean dark line lay where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner and been burned and called dealt with long since, and beside it the new finer hairline of grey called \lore{the thaw} had gathered, finer than the old line and not the old line. She looked at both and swept neither and stepped wide around them, and the boy set the two lines on two places with a finger between, because a line left where it is is still a line kept and a new hairline beside it is a new hairline and not the same thing said twice.

Under \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where no broom went. She did not look down at them and did not look away from them, but took the wide step that was the looking in this yard, and carried him past; the space beneath the rack kept its own shape. A shallow clay pan had gone from its place months ago and left a round ring where it had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word in it, and the ring was the whole of what was left and the yard put no figure to the absence of a pan. The water in \place{the trough} had skinned over from edge to edge again in the dry cold, a skin thin enough to break under a finger and not broken. Out on \place{the worked strips} the field had an empty place, and farther along the returning line was the place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; the work went around both the way water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies, and the boy carried the direction of them in his ear and gave them no number. That was seven places gone around with a look and a wide step and no number. No eighth likeness was stood up beside them to make a row, and nobody in the yard said the word for what the seven were, so that seven stayed seven and did not become a rule.

At \place{the yard well} the stayed frost had gone off the north lip while the yard looked elsewhere; \lore{frost-bloom} had written white there, then grey under the bucket, then nothing, and the yard called the whole of it weather. The skin on the trough was a second covering over a second depth, and the plate of ice clouded with silt, which she had lifted on Frost 9 and set on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, was a third covering with the depth lifted up and set where no foot went. Three coverings in three places, all called weather and entered nowhere. She told the weather where to stand. The boy kept a finger between every pair of them, because the same hand that covers a trough covers a well-lip and the same cold had made the plate, and a cause made out of three likenesses would want a payer put under all three and none stood to any. No fourth likeness was made. The plate stayed on its stone, clouded and out of the path of feet, and the river went on under the yard with no number put to it.

\scenebreak

The month was a hunting month and a repairing month, and its hunts and repairs went on as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A man came down \place{the clay road} with a hare across his shoulders, the hind legs along one collarbone and the head tucked against the other, hill frost grey in the fur at the throat; he judged his own carrying by the ache in the arm that did it and went off toward \place{the north gate road} while the yard went on with its work. A woman with a pot asked what the month's carrying had taken now, and Sella told her the weather and pointed with her chin toward the worked strips, a direction that left no entry behind it.

“That isn't a figure,” the woman said.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” She said it to the pot and the weather and not to the woman, and went around the trough with the basket held level.

The order of it was what the boy read: the cost asked before the work was done, the question arriving ahead of the carrying arm. A neat hand can write a cost down before anybody has lifted a tile, and the neatness does not make the cost true; he had learned to read past the tidy figure to the place where the payer ought to have stood, and in this yard the place was empty. No man pricing a repair stood up to be asked. A roof wanted mending two doors down and the men who would mend it talked of the weather and the carrying of the tiles, and the tiles went by in armfuls judged by the arm, and nobody wrote what they weighed. He kept the cost question and the weather's answer on two places with a finger between and did not make the one a cause of the other. Keeping by not answering was a kind of care in this yard. He would not make a doctrine of it, because the keeping was real and the payer had no name and the two facts must not be joined.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the flagstones} with the broom when they came past, and his stroke went over the shallow bright patch where the stone had kept the place his stroke had once stopped, and went on without the half-check. The second checking had been returned on Bone 8 and set down on the flagstone's own place, and it stayed there beside the Day 161 fraction at \lore{the damper}, the Day 166 beat beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, and the Frost 5 returned beat --- four held changes on four places, four and not a rule, no fifth likeness made a cause and no payer named to any. A thing kept by not being made again is still kept. He said \emph{Yes, house} to one who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where it could be checked and where it could slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark and the seventh at the lip beyond it, and beside it \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} and put the knife back where it could be checked, and announced it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it, and the loan stayed uncalled.

The small keepings stayed small and were not brought forward. At the loaf shelf, passing with the basket, Sella laid her finger on top of the shelf's front edge for a breath and brought it away; the charcoal tally lay on the underside unread, and she did not go back to it, and the two gaps in the row above were not figured. On this afternoon no woman of the firing work came to the well with her hand in her sleeve, and the notched strip of birch bark that \person{Ilva} keeps there stayed where it stays, not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined, her ninth record-first hand nine with no tenth and no figure coming off the wood; the boy filed no tenth and made no likeness of a strip he did not see. The \place{Frost} 14 well-saying stood at three shapings, beside \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth hand with a finger between every pair, and three shapings were three and not a witness, for a witness stands somewhere and can be asked. Two shut-room tellings stood at two. The row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. The green at the mouth of a silent woman across a market was a colour and the shape at the market's edge was a shape, a seventh of each and no eighth, given no name by anybody in the yard, joined to no loaf, slate, sale, hearing, cause or romance. The reed-cutter's cord lay where no Court thumb had read it, and the soft-cuffed clerk's slate lay inward at \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}. None of these was a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment; none came under the roof with the basket. \person{Jorra Vail} gave the men who went low into \place{the back room} no name and did not say the room was cold, and the fifth telling of \lore{the burned slate} stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one, and the promise of the foundling's name neither given nor withdrawn. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up at the half-pace and set him on \place{the bed} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, with the fold put right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. She went to \place{the hearth} before she went to him, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening and giving neither gap a figure. On \place{the pot board} the pot stood with its handle mended twice, the older brass staple and the tighter wire crossing the crack with the crack showing dark between them; she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,} she said to the pot, and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,} she said to the fire, and to neither did she say what the mending had cost or who had paid for it. She set the pot down and the half-beat between the set-down and her going away stood on its own place in the boy's ear, a small interval with a shape and no owner. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because an afternoon and a going of the light are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule.

Under the roof the three counts kept their three places and none rose into another. \lore{The carried fire} gave four low pulls beneath the floor and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; the fifth had been unpaid three hundred and ten days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Through the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and put no number to itself. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Four and a gap in three different materials, and agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either. The house kept itself by its \lore{not-doings} --- by the coals left at one hour and turned at another, by the shelf's front edge with a finger kept on top of it, by the two gaps in a row nobody figured, by the unswept lines at the corner and the pan's gone ring and the plate on its stone --- and the boy kept his finger between those omissions and his own kept gaps, and did not lay one across the other, because the edges came near to touching and the finger was the whole of the work. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty, and none was invented to fill it; the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure stayed beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between. He kept all of it where it lay, on places of its own, and asked nothing of any of it to stand up and mean.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note and took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and set it beside a small letting-go with the finger brought down between, and laid the half-beat of the pot's set-down beside the two and did not join any of them. Nothing was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment or a rule. It was the hundred and ninety-sixth answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; no payer was named to it and it stood up as none of the things it refused. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip; both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument was the work. The hand came down on the river's note and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by the width of a finger and nothing moved: the half-beat of the pot's set-down standing on its own place beside the two small hand-movings, the seven gone-around places with no number, the three coverings called weather and entered nowhere, the four held changes on four places, the small keepings kept small, and the payer of none of them named. Three hundred and ten stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sill}, Low Kiln or \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and thirty days were told by nobody. The river went low over lower under the finger's-width door with no number put to it, and the silt-clouded plate of ice stood on its flat stone out of the path of feet, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
