\person{Sella Vail} carried him down from \place{the Vail kiln-house} in the last of the morning with the fold drawn over his ears against the river wind, and the instruction went to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear where it had lain since first light; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped and could close two inches on nothing, and the wanting went on underneath the inventory without once interrupting it.

The square came to him the way a room comes to a man who steps into it in the dark: by the small sounds first. A cup was turned upon a board and set back. A pig grunted. A slate went down on a rail with the flat wooden sound of a thing laid where somebody meant to find it again. Above all of it the moot kept up its low arguing, never loud, the way people arguing about arithmetic are never loud, because the arithmetic is the part they are afraid of. The small sounds did not lie. The large ones could be made to say one in twelve as easily as one in three, but a cup set down was a cup set down, and a man's voice with a number in it was neither.

At \place{the Cross} she set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, with his cheek turned against the cold iron, and stood half a pace off. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and gave none to the basket, and she kept him out of the reach of every open slate that came near the rail. A man with a slate under his arm passed and did not look in. A woman with a market basket passed and looked at the iron rather than at what the shade held. An assessor's boy came down the steps with a heap of slates and turned them writing-down, and the shade was a finger's width and held exactly what a finger's width of shade will hold, which was the boy and nothing else.

Two hundred leaned in over \place{the reading block}, their backs making a wall with a gap in it that opened and shut as people shifted. Behind them eight pigs went over the wet boards, driven with a stick that caught the mud and let it go with a small sucking sound; a Sill assessor wrote the eight down in a slate before anybody could ask which eight he meant, his hand arriving at the writing before his eyes arrived at the rail. A printed notice was square on a post at the block's edge, soft at one corner where many thumbs had worried it, and the soft corner was the only part of the notice anybody could have read without being told what it said. A number went from a fist to a mouth to a shoulder in the crowd and did not arrive anywhere; the figures stood side by side on a heap of turnips and nobody made them agree. Somebody's hand put a slate down near the basket's shade and took it up again, and the boy kept his finger between the shade and the slate without either becoming a place the other occupied.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road}. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay that came off him was the smell of nothing at all, the most expensive smell in Grath. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man checks a dressing he has already set and knows he will not have to redo.

“One in twelve, as the notice says,” he read low into the top of the two hundred. The figure went twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that had come late, and neither of those households was standing under the figure when it went over them. He called the levy and denied it with “The levy is called. It is not called by me,” and to a man who had brought a number up out of a fist he said “your house,” never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as a surgeon takes a hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, leaving the field with nobody's fingers in it and the dressing neat and the patient none the wiser. The boy had once done that himself in a room with white lights and a chart turned down, and had thought nothing of it. Here the grammar had a green mouth and a clean apron, and went on standing where it stood as if a sentence with nobody in it were a sentence that had been honestly spoken.

The two hundred went back to the pigs. The assessor's eight went into a slate with the ninth still unclaimed and nobody claiming it. The printed figure stayed on its post with its soft corner, and the man who had brought a number up out of his fist put the fist back in his sleeve and went off toward the turnips, and the turnips remained turnips with the figures standing beside them unjoined. Berr went up the clay road with the cord folded and the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked behind him, Bone's first true subtraction going up inside it beside the three from Frost, believing he had done a kindness.

The small sounds came back first when the reading was over. A pig shook itself and the mud came off with a slap. A woman set a pot down on the edge of the rail and the pot gave its note and held it, and the boy's head went toward it a degree before the knuckle arrived to check what it was: the note was whole, and a whole note was the sort that could be trusted to tell the truth about a vessel, and he filed nothing because nothing had been broken. A goer-by with a slate under his arm came too near the shade and Sella went half a pace around him the way she went around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and came back to her place without a word and without looking toward the basket. The instruction had gone to the wool and the wind and never to the basket, and the shade kept what the shade held. Cold iron at his cheek drew the heat out of the skin in a slow even line, and he let it, because two inches of air were two inches of air whether the air was a pot handle or the side of a moot-post, and the crust lay where it lay. Three hundred and nine days stood in him exact and complete and unspent, and nobody in the two hundred could have told him what the number counted if they had tried, which they did not.

The printed figure stood unchallenged. The figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between them. Four arithmetics of the year now stood behind that one printed figure --- the three of Frost locked in the box and Bone's first going up the road behind them --- and four were four. The district's arithmetic had changed for the month in a way that could not be changed back, and the change was done to the square and not to the basket: nothing in the shade had moved, and the crust lay two inches off and the thread lay frozen and the cold had stopped where it had stopped. A rule made out of four readings wanting a payer put under all four, and no payer stood under any of them. A doctrine was an account that had found a payer; this account had found nobody, and so the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain lying covered under wool at the rack end, the four held changes on their four places, or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between. He had carried such findings a long way already without any of them turning into a rule, and a finding with no payer can be carried a long way further.

At \place{the rack end} at home the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth, turned in these mornings with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure. The arithmetic that had just gone over the square went over that covered grain as well, being the kind of number that travels without a hand to put it down, and it did not know what the grain had cost. It answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, whose cost went into no slate and whose name stood in no figure; it answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out of the worked strip; it answered nothing for the hare gone off toward the north gate road, which had been answered with the weather. Three absences and a covered heap of grain, and the number that passed over them did not know the weight of any of them. The boy set the unknowing on its own place and did not make a cause of it, because a cause wants a payer standing somewhere and none stood here either.

The bell went over the square after the reading, longer than a church bell, and the boy counted it as he had counted it at Frost's last market: four strokes, four strokes, and a place where the fifth should have stood. Not one hand in two hundred went to a face. The gap had a shape in the body, an inch held over nothing with the breath kept whole against it, and he kept the shape and did not fill it. He held his inch through the stroke that did not come and came down on the stroke after it, supplying no stroke to fill the gap, making the gap neither a seventh hearing of \lore{the full note} nor a fifth pull of \lore{the carried fire} nor an agreement with the boards' thumb-width knot. Three counts in three places and none rose into another, for agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to either. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, and the square went on with its pigs.

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} went up \place{the clay road} toward \place{Greybank} with \person{Berr Clay}'s narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve. The second strap was let out for the long road and the dried pale scurf of mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for it; her thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was. Her hand arrived at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd, and the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist brought the edge round as she walked. To the women who asked what she carried she said “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.” To the one who wanted more she said “Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk,” answering the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology. No figure came off the slate. The boy filed the order of reaching once on a place of its own and did not file it twice; her hand stayed the eighth such hand in the district, eight and not a rule, joined to none.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} where a fifth step should have stood, for a measure nobody had called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday, and the slate lay against his forearm with the writing inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. His hand went to the slate before his eyes found the man who had said the buyer's number, and the number went in before the looking, and he did not rise out of the market when it sold over him. It sold over him the way water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies; the stone keeps its place and the water keeps its own, and neither is entered to the other. The boy filed that order apart from \person{Marra}, from the Drawing collector with his grey fingertips, from the clerk at the Court's table, from Berr's twice-wiped thumb and from Hessa's edge-on slate. Five record-first hands on five places with a finger between every pair, and no sixth likeness stood up and called itself a cause.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a woman stood saying nothing with clay dried pale under her nails in the small crescents and a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long. The sweetish green of lead glaze lay in the crease beside her lip. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, and went on looking at the pots. The boy called the colour green and nothing else. It was the seventh green at a mouth and there was no eighth of it: no name, no slate, no sale, no hearing and no cause. At the market's edge a small shape stood in cast-off clothes worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one, a man's shoulders set on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been. A green sliver of lead glaze on a string at his throat caught the light as he turned and went dull again as he turned back. A pot was set down nearby with a small flat sound, and the small shape turned half a beat after the knock, not before it. That half-beat was a turn after a sound and nothing more, filed on its own place beside the boy's own turning toward such sounds and not joined to it. The seventh shape stayed a shape. Neither was named, neither was spoken to, neither spoke, and no loaf, slate, sale, hearing, cause or romance came off either of them across the whole length of the reading.

The fifth telling of \lore{the burned slate} went through the reading untouched, as it had come up from \place{Slate Row} on the fifth of Bone: a word on the same breath as the price of barley and the weather, with no owner standing under it, no room, no hand at the flame and no reason. The reading changed the district's arithmetic for the month and did not touch that word. No sixth voice came up over the boards, no witness stood to be asked, no hand was named at any flame, and no payer was put beneath it. The claim went on gathering interest against \person{Jorra Vail}'s promise to the men who burn slates, the promise neither given nor withdrawn, while Jorra under the roof neither paid nor refused and said nothing of the word at all. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed. The boy kept the fifth telling on its own place with a finger between it and the four, five tellings and not five witnesses to one fire, and made no sixth due on a day that had not earned one.

At the going of the light she carried him home up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate, saying nothing of what had been read over him or of what had gone up the road in the box. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The twice-mended pot stood on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the old crack showing dark between the staples, and she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack before she let the weight come on the handle, saying both sayings to the pot and the fire and never to a person. The fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and nine days and nobody under the roof supplying the pull. Under the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and the bell's gap in the square was a fourth thing kept somewhere else again.

She set the basket back on \place{the bed} under the roof and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. The widow's mark hung where it hung on its cord and was not touched: a reading and a going of the light are not openings, and five openings in five months are five and not a rule. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, and the crust still lay two inches off with its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note, took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and let it go whole on the same low note --- a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, carried into neither the fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square nor the river's low over lower. The hundred and ninety-fourth answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it stood up as no reach, hearing, seventh, stopping, payment, held change, gift, doctrine or rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away; both were true and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument was the work. The hand came down on the river's note and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Three hundred and nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sill or Grath, and Bone's first reading was already a direction up the clay road with the printed figure standing unchallenged over a square gone quiet after the bell.
