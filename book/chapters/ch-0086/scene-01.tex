\daymark{309}{Bone 10, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold the way a tool is laid down where the next hand will find it without looking, and under it the hundred and ninety-second answer kept its place and asked nothing of the dark. \person[Corin]{The boy} had set it there in the small hours and had thought about it no more than a man thinks about the last instrument on a tray once the list is finished and the tray wiped; there was no reward in the keeping and no payer named to it, and he had stopped looking for one a long way back. Three hundred and eight days stood behind him, exact, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; and here under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the count went to three hundred and nine. Six months and one hundred and twenty-nine days were told by nobody. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood, so the number lay in him alone, carried in the mouth the way a surgeon carries staples between the lips, always there, almost never spat out.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Its payer had been unnamed for three hundred and nine days and nobody lifted the floor to find one: the fire answered as it always answered, four and a gap, and the gap stayed a gap. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken beneath this roof the same number of days. He was kept in objects and not in a story --- the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat keeping its twenty-year lean and the hollow nobody would fill, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both. The keeping of him was maintenance, and no lesson had been made of it in nine days or in three hundred.

This was the tenth of Bone, the month's first Account-day, and the reading and the market and the bell belonged to it by right. They stood ahead of the small hours like a pot set on a high shelf, and he did not reach for what the morning had not yet earned. The day would earn them in its own time: \place{the Cross} would hold its crowd when the light came and the bell would go over the square after it, and neither was brought into the dark to keep a boy company. Bone had come on without a boundary drawn. The older women of the district named the months by what the weather did, the slatemen by the count of Account-days gone into them, and the two habits lay side by side, neither set right nor left wrong in so many words. Nothing from the nine days behind had followed him in as a sign, a lesson, a mending or a payment; the yard's small keepings were left where the yard had put them, and a day that has not earned a thing does not get to keep it.

Then the body, taken in the old order. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. It had come out of a woman's hand in another house and had gone from warm to cold without anybody saying so, and now it kept the shape of a thing that had once been bread and had long since stopped being food. Beside it the empty place kept its name to itself, and no third had come into the nine days of Bone to make the row come out even; he did not ask one to. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was left unwiped. No one under any roof had ever given an instruction about the thread; a basket set down was a pot set down and left, and the instruction went to the wool and the wind and never to the basket, and the thread lay where it lay. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew what the ease on a cold face meant. It was not ease. A cold patient could not clot and would seem better before he was worse, and had to be warmed from the outside and slowly --- never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He had once said such things to people who wrote them down, and had thought nothing of saying them. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away, and he could not close two inches on the pot handle somebody had set beyond his reaching hand. Both were true. The second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, unnamed place, thread, cold, the waiting beyond --- and entered it nowhere, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter; and the infant underneath wanted, and the wanting had no words and did not need any, and the two voices argued without either winning, which was the work and not a virtue.

Three counts stood in three places and none rose into another. Under the floor the carried fire kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; under the finger's-width door the river went \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming and never numbered among the hearings; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail should have stood. Four and a gap, water over water, four and a knot: three counts, and agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and no payer stood to any of them. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth --- the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her --- filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came out of vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it. The bell he had heard on the last market of Frost, four strokes and four and a gap with not one hand in two hundred going to a face, stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}. It had not been heard again and had not been lent an ear it had not earned.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. That was the grammar of the morning under this roof and had been the grammar of a thousand mornings: the coals told it was the thaw when the sour mineral breath came off the unswept corner, and turned with the back of a broken pot, and given the instruction before anybody gave it to a person. “The house will keep it,” she said to the fire, where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She was a broad woman with forearms the colour of dried \lore{slip} and a burn across the back of the left hand shaped like a little door --- two straight sides, a lintel and a threshold --- and the burn shut on nothing as she worked, then closed on the pot's weight when she lifted it. Her hair was coal-black with one grey streak at the temple that she had said came from the winter Garl died and would say nothing more about; she pushed it back with the wrist that carried the burn, and the streak showed pale in the low light.

She kept her finger on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge while she worked, so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and she moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither gap. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf, and her hand shut half over nothing and did not touch it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it: a market-day morning under this roof was not an opening, and five openings in five months were five and not a rule. The hand went to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour, and this hour was a morning, a hearth and a pot, none of them an opening.

The twice-mended pot stood on \place{the pot board} with the hairline of the old crack showing dark between the staples. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle; and she said both sayings to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one} and \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them. She set the rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with its handle turned toward it, and there was a half-beat between the setting and her going away, and she did not look toward \place{the bed}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay.

\scenebreak

Out at \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where the house could check it and where it could slip. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the lip beyond the mark where it could not, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. He called everyone house, even her, and said \emph{I owe} where another man would say \emph{I am}; he had said \emph{Yes, house} to the early thaw and the late and had kept wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stood there. He did not say the word. It came as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven, and the breath he drew at the top of the reach did not go in and was not spent; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with it. His chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what little light there was and gave it back. Up in \place{the Debt Loft}, which reached the boy only as a sound with a direction, the stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled, and no collector came up \place{the clay road} or went down it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and told a pot “You'll do” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. The men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey had no name from him and the room was not called cold; to the boy it was a direction and a sound.

He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not say it before a kiln-master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would have called it early and made something of it. Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot; the boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The burned slate stood at five tellings and he put a finger between the fifth and the four and supplied no sixth; the two tellings of \lore{the counting room} shut in the war stood at two and had not gained a third; the three pot sayings stood with the Smoke 14 note on a place of its own and no fourth likeness made a cause; the row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

In the small hours, with the first light beginning to come under the finger's-width door and find the edges of things, he turned out the file as he had once turned instruments out on \place{the towel} at the end of a list, laying each thing on its own place and asking nothing of any of them. The held interval lay where it had been laid; the empty interval lay empty; the missing fifth under the floor was missing and nobody had supplied it; the two shelf gaps stood unfigured; the ninth hand stood nine with the strip in a sleeve and no tenth; the fifth telling stood at five with the finger between it and the four. Nothing had been spent to make a row come out even. Nothing had stood up out of its place as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule.

The told figure stood beside the paid figure with no signature on the space between, and Frost's three readings with their three true subtractions rode locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward High Kiln, one in twelve printed and unchallenged; three readings were three and not one account, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and not laid across the house's \lore{not-doings}, the dressed grain lying covered under wool at the rack end, or his own kept gaps. The edges nearly touched and the finger went between. A finding with no payer is a thing a man can carry a long way without its becoming a rule.

The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note and took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his. He let it go whole on the same low note, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, carrying it into neither the fire's missing fifth, the bell's gap left behind in the square nor the river's low over lower. The hundred and ninety-third answer was exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it did not stand up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a key, a power, a freedom, a gift, a doctrine or a rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped away; both were true and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument was the work. The hand came down on the river's note as it always did and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

Three hundred and nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and twenty-nine days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl Vail's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. One hundred and ninety-three honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine. The file lay open on the towel at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, the hand laid back against the fold on the one hundred and ninety-third answer, and the morning light came under the finger's-width door without anybody having said what the day would read.
