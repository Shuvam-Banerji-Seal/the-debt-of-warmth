\daymark{311}{Bone 12, Secondday, Y.H. 813}

The twelfth of Bone came on in the small hours the way a twelfth of any month comes, with no door opened anywhere and no hand lifted to mark it, and under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the count went from three hundred and ten to three hundred and eleven exact. Six months and one hundred and thirty-one days were told by nobody. There was no market on a Secondday, no \place{the Cross}, no bell; the reading of two mornings ago had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box and had not come back, and nothing of it came now as a sign or a lesson or a mending or a cause or a payment. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} and did not know the day had changed its name; he knew only that the number had gone over once more, the way a man knows a page has turned without looking at the page. In \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, nobody could have said what three hundred and eleven counted, and he had stopped a long way back expecting anybody to say it. The not-knowing was its own small room, and he had lived in it so long that it felt less like a deprivation than like a place he had been put and had made warm.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Its payer had been unnamed three hundred and eleven days and nobody lifted the floor to find one; the fire answered as it always answered, four and a gap, and the gap stayed a gap. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number of days. He was kept in objects and not in a story --- the salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat keeping its twenty-year lean and the hollow nobody would fill, the clean dark line at the corner with the finer grey beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both --- and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a lesson, and made no rule.

He took the body as a wound is taken, in the order its parts arrive. The crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery and brown-edged, stiff now to the point where its corner stood a hair off the wool, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in all these days to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen where it lay and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away; \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that was not composure, and the ease cold puts on a face that is not ease, and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He had once read such a face in a bay with fluorescent light in it and a chart turned down on a clipboard, and had gone past the face to the note, and had signed past the note himself, and the knowledge had never once since made a warm place for anybody. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip. Both were true and the second was louder, and the older man took the inventory in its old order --- crust, unnamed gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- while the infant kept the want, neither winning, the argument itself the work.

The finding he had come by two mornings ago was not brought up now and set beside anything. Four arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure, the printed figure stood at one in twelve, unchallenged, the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between; the four true subtractions had gone up the clay road in a locked box and nobody under this roof had seen the face of them since. That was all it was. He kept it on a place of its own with no payer under it, and it was not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine and not a rule, and he did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings} or the dressed grain lying covered and unfigured under wool at \place{the rack end}, or the four held changes standing on four places, or the returned half-check on \place{the flagstones}, or the small gaps he kept in his own file. The edges of these things came near to touching; the finger went between them and did not spend itself joining them. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, and four likenesses will do it more surely; a finding with no payer can be carried a long way without becoming a rule, and this one was carried, light, on its own place, and asked nothing of any of the others to stand up and mean. He took a clean satisfaction in that, the satisfaction of a man who has laid an instrument down without pretending it had healed anybody, and underneath it the small animal want of the crust went on, louder, unsatisfied, and was not corrected by it.

By first light \person{Sella Vail} had gone to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she went before anything. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening and giving neither gap a figure. On \place{the pot board} the pot stood with its handle mended twice, the older brass staple and the tighter wire crossing the crack with the crack showing dark between them; she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot, and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the fire, and to neither did she say what the mending had cost or who had paid for it. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. At the loaf shelf she laid a finger on top of the front edge for a breath and brought it away, the charcoal tally lying on the underside unread, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither of them; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. The instruction had gone to the wool and the pot and the broom and the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it. She did not touch it at a shelf, a well, a rack, a morning, a hearth or a bed, because none of these was an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood far off in a later month's first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward, and she did not hurry the morning to it or make a figure of the days between. The burn shaped like a little door on the back of her left hand shut on nothing as she reached for the pot and shut on the pot's weight as she lifted it, and the boy, who could not have named the shape, kept the two shuttings on separate places and made nothing between them.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where it could slip, with six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it could not, and beside it Garl Vail's unforged collector sentence with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what little light there was and gave it back, and he held the wrist up a moment to the grey as though to see whether the light had come to anything. His chest had been tight since the dark and the breath from Seed 16 was still unspent, a small empty place at the top of each reach where the breath should have gone in. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather and put the knife back where it could be checked, and announced it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Above him, in \place{the Debt Loft}, the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot; he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, and that was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and would not have said it before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make something of it. Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, reading one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes the hand off a man, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies and no slate entered. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He grew quieter as the saying about the burned slate went around, the way he always did, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The fifth telling of \lore{the burned slate} stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one, and the seed waiting on the far-off calling-in of his promise, not a confession and not a payment. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

Under the roof the three counts kept their three places and none rose into another. The carried fire gave four low pulls beneath the floor and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull; through the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and put no number to itself; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Four and a gap in three different materials, and agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either. The house kept itself by its not-doings, and the boy kept his finger between those omissions and his own kept gaps and did not lay one across the other; six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it; the bell he had heard in the square stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure stayed beside the paid figure with no signature on the space. He kept all of it where it lay, on places of its own, and asked nothing of any of it to stand up and mean.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note and took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and held it against the kiln's fourth low pull as it gave, and let it go whole on the same low note, not carried into the missing fifth and not lent to the silence where the fifth should have stood. Nothing was promoted into a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a gift, a doctrine or a rule; no payer was named. It was the hundred and ninety-seventh answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap and nothing moved. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip; both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument was the work. The hand came down on the river's note and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The kiln's fourth pull faded into the silence where the fifth should have stood, and three hundred and eleven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill or Grath.
