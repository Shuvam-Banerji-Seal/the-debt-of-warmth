\daymark{310}{Bone 11, Firstday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof went as small hours go in a house that keeps its warmth by never speaking of it: the fire gave what it gave under the floor and said nothing of what it did not, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with his face turned into the fold and one crust lying two inches from his ear. The room smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool and, under them, the sour mineral breath of the Ash ridge along the skirting board that had not been swept in two years. A clay jar stood cold on the shelf by the wall under its skin of \lore{lead glaze}; the oiled paper at the window let the dark through in a grey square. He had come into his three hundred and tenth day and nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} had said so. Six months and one hundred and thirty days were told by nobody. Three hundred and ten stood in him exact and complete and unspent, the count going over from three hundred and nine as quietly as water goes over water under the finger's-width door, and under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer unnamed three hundred and ten days. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. Firstday came on without a market in it and without a bell, and brought nothing in with it: the day came the way a pot comes to the wheel, on its own weight, with no hand lifting yesterday into it.

The body was taken in its old order, the way a wound is taken in the order its parts arrive, because the order was the only honesty available. One stiff brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone leathery in the dry heat since the morning behind, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come to make the row come out even, and a row of one is a row nobody has to answer for. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen where it lay and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that was not composure, the ease that cold puts on a face which is not ease, the patient who will seem better before he is worse because a cold patient cannot clot; he knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. He wanted the crust lifted to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip and could close two inches on nothing --- not on the pot handle set beyond his reach, not on the wool folded under his chin. Both were true and the second was louder. The older man took the inventory; the infant kept the want; neither won, and the argument between them was the work and not a virtue. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not lift a crust to his mouth; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small economy.

The room came to him as a field comes to a man checking it before the light: by the small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie. Under the floor the carried fire gave its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Through the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and put no number to itself. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood to either. The house kept itself by its \lore{not-doings} --- by the coals left at one hour and turned at another, by the shelf's front edge with a finger kept on top of it, by the two gaps in a row that nobody figured --- and the boy kept his finger between those omissions and his own kept gaps, and did not lay one across the other, because the edges nearly touched and the finger was the whole of the work.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she had gone every morning of this month and every morning of the months behind it. The instruction went to the wool and to \place{the pot board} and to the broom and to the running water, and never to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the first of the light. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under \lore{lead glaze} with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood far off in a later month's first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward.

On the pot board the pot stood with its handle mended twice, the older brass staple and the tighter wire crossing the crack with the crack left showing dark between them; she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,} she said to the pot, and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one,} she said to the fire, and to neither did she say whose crack it was or who had paid for the mending. The sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. The little-door burn on the back of her hand shut on nothing as she went away.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from the hearth, and she turned it beneath the wool with a lighter touch than the day before and gave no figure to it, because the grain had a weight and the weight had no number standing under it. A number had gone over that covered grain on the day behind without knowing what it cost; it had answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188, whose cost went into no slate and whose name stood in no figure; it had answered nothing for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out of \place{the worked strips}; it had answered nothing for the hare gone off toward \place{the north gate road}, which had been answered with the weather. The boy kept the unknowing on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, and did not lay it across the house's not-doings or the returned half-check standing on \place{the flagstones} in its own place among four held changes or his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between. Four readings were four and not one account, and a rule made out of them wanted a payer standing under all four, and none stood; the printed figure stood unchallenged up \place{the clay road} with no signature on the space between figure and price, and a finding with no payer can be carried a long way without becoming a rule.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} when Sella carried the basket past on her way to the rack, the fold drawn over his ears against the river wind, and the instruction went to the wool and to the wind and never to the basket. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where it could be checked and where it could slip, and six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beyond the mark where it could not. Beside the knife lay Garl's unforged collector sentence --- the man sent for a collector who had turned back at the frozen ford --- with no name on the man who turned and no figure on the debt. \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count,} he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked and announced it to nobody because the yard had already been told.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what little light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark, and the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach did not go in, the Seed 16 breath staying unspent with it --- a small interval showing where it should have been, and the man refusing to spend it. The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the one who wanted the thaw early and to the one who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. No collector came up the clay road and no collector went down it; the loan stayed uncalled, and the warm stone in his clay pot in the loft lay cold in its unmarked pot, unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiveness.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, where she set the basket out of the draught and stood half a pace off with her burn shut on nothing, \person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, not bone-coloured slip; three men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.} To a pot on the tray he said \emph{You'll do,} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and he knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from any kiln-master out of \place{High Kiln} who would have called it early and made something of it, singing before its time and singing itself out early.

Before Sella had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read one iron with two bites --- the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. Behind that standing finger the fifth telling of \lore{the burned slate} stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing to be asked, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one; the claim went on gathering interest against his promise to the men who burn slates, the promise neither given nor withdrawn. The shut-room tellings stood at two and not three; the pot sayings at three with the note from \place{Smoke} 14 on its own place apart; the row at five askings with one shaping and no witness; and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken. He did not look toward the bed or the basket.

Outside the wool the hand came up an inch toward the river's note and took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and set it beside a small letting-go with the finger brought down between --- an empty interval laid beside a held interval, the one kept open, the other kept whole, and the finger between them so neither should learn the other's shape. It was the hundred and ninety-fifth answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air; it stood up as no reach, hearing, seventh, stopping, payment, held change, gift, doctrine or rule, and no payer was named to it. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped from his lip; both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument was the work. The hand came down on the river's note and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, the empty interval and the held interval lying side by side on it with the finger withdrawn and neither spent, and the first light of the day after the reading going off the pot board without a word having been said about \place{the Cross}. Three hundred and ten stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sill or Grath; six months and one hundred and thirty days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; Garl's name had not been spoken the same number. Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
