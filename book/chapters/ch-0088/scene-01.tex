\daymark{315}{Bone 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold as a tool is laid down where the next hand will find it without looking. It had gone up an inch toward the low note under the finger's-width door, taken its small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, let it go whole on that same note, and come straight down; and there it lay, answer two hundred and four, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Put away in the dark. Not taken up again. Nothing under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had seen it go up and nothing had seen it come down, and the keeping of the number was the only honest work there was to do and it bought nothing at two inches of air.

The small hours went on the way water goes on under ice, without a line drawn across them and without anybody standing at the near side to say that the sixteenth had begun. The older women of the district named their months by the weather and the slatemen named them by the count of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong under this roof; the month carried both names the way a pot carries two handles and is none the worse for it. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his three hundred and fifteenth day with nobody saying so. Six months and one hundred and thirty-five days were told by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}. The count went from three hundred and fourteen to three hundred and fifteen exact, complete, unspent, spoken by no man under any roof. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. No market, no Cross, no bell on a Sixthday; nothing in the dark had lifted a hand to mark the turning. He had stopped expecting anybody to tell him what the number counted, which is not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

He read the room by its small sounds first, because the small sounds do not lie. The coals breathed under blind ash with the dry, sour smell of hot clay gone cold and tallow and wet wool over it all; the river went on \lore{water over water} under the finger's-width door, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; the little door of the fire's warmth stood shut on air, and under it all lay the faint sour mineral breath of the \lore{Ash} ridge along the skirting board, which nobody had swept in two years and nobody had named in three and which he set down as a smell and nothing more. He took the inventory in its old order, the way a man reads the numbers at the edge of a chart before the writing in the middle, and entered it nowhere, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone stiffer in the dry heat of the kiln, its corner standing a hair off the wool. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third had come in to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, for nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

The surgeon in him took the room as he had once taken a field --- by the small sounds first, then the shape of the loss, then the question of what could be done and how little of it there was --- and the infant in him took the same room as a warmth that was not where he wanted it. The two readings were of one room and agreed on nothing, and both were true, and the older of the two had long since learned to keep his hand steady while the younger one wanted.

He knew the ease a cold face puts on for what it is not --- the composed face that is not composure --- and he knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never rubbed, never set near the fire. The knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread at his mouth wiped, and the wanting had no words and did not need any. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the two voices argued without a word between them the way a man argues in his sleep, and the argument was the work.

Three counts stood in three places and did not become one. Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. At the finger's-width door the river was a low thing over a lower thing and was not numbered among the hearings. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to any, so the three stayed three. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth: the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came out of vessels; the seventh place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The told figure stood beside the paid.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she went to it before everything, and told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}. \emph{It's the thaw. Go when it goes}, she said to them, and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and fed them. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said, where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter and she had decided long ago what she would not give the Court. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails, the breaths of a sleeping child, and the counting was not a prayer and went to no opening.

At \place{the loaf shelf} she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally stayed unread beneath it, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither gap, hearing two sayings and not one absence. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} on the wall shelf and shut her hand half over nothing; the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air, two straight sides and a lintel and a threshold with the pale puckered skin inside it, and nobody asked what the jar was for. The jar was not a door and a morning was not an opening, and she gave no instruction to either.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot stood with its repair showing: two staples, one the colour of old brass and the second a wire drawn tighter and left longer than it needed, with a hairline of the original break showing dark between them. She lifted it with her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot; and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the fire; and never said either to a person. The sayings were a man's who mended pots for a living and would not let anyone mend him; they had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as he had said them. The pot's foot came down on the board with the small flat sound of a vessel set where it belongs, and she gave neither the repair nor the half-beat after it a figure. Repair is not restoration, and a mended vessel carries its break, but she did not say that either, because it would have wanted a person standing under it to be asked.

She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, handle toward it, with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}; then said \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} to the fold and the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and went and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. The grey streak at her temple showed pale where she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried the burn. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of clay under lead glaze with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months are five and not a rule; the next stands far off in a later month's first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward.

Garl stayed in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door with the crease at the left shoulder where the wheel had leaned on him for twenty years; the hollow seat in the shed kept its twenty-year lean and no one filled it; the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey beside it called the thaw stayed unswept both. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled, and no collector had come up \place{the clay road} or gone down it. His name had not been spoken under the roof three hundred and fifteen days, and the keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson was made of any of it.

\scenebreak

Four arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure. The printed one in twelve was unchallenged, the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between; four readings were four and not one account. The four true subtractions rode locked up the clay road in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, unread under this roof. He kept the finding on a place of its own with no payer under it, not a lesson, grievance, mending, doctrine or rule, and did not lay it across the house's \lore{not-doings}, or across the dressed grain covered under wool at \place{the rack end} and read over by a number that did not know its cost, or across the four held changes on four places, or across the stayed-at-the-inch object, or across his own kept gaps. The edges came near to touching and the finger went between, because a finding with no payer can be carried a long way and a doctrine is only an account that has found a payer, and none stood to this one.

The stayed-at-the-inch object stayed one object on its own place on the sixth, one staying and not a habit, not made again and not a fifth held change and not a rule. One staying is a keeping; two would stand up and call themselves a habit, and a habit would want a payer put under it, and none stood. The four held changes on four places were four and not a rule, and the returned half-check kept its own stone with a finger between it and every other object on the tray. \person{Tarn}'s six at the kindling sill and \person{Jorra Vail}'s dusty forearms on their own tray stayed where they had been left, and the house kept both silences without an order; neither man was under the roof this morning and neither silence was fetched in to make a fifth of anything. He turned all of it over the way a man turns instruments on a towel at the end of a list and asks nothing of any of it, and nothing stood up as a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a gift, a power, a doctrine or a rule.

Then, in the first light, the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his and let it go whole on the same low note, a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer; it came straight down and laid itself back against the fold. The small intake was no larger than the one before it and no smaller, and the note it went out on was the note it came in on; the hand did not stay at the inch and did not reach the two inches to the crust, but came down as it had come up, without witness and without haste. He filed it as answer two hundred and five: exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, standing up as none of the things it refused and with no payer named to it. The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number, and the infant underneath wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument between them was the work. He who had once reduced a thousand shoulders in a room with hard light could not close two inches on the handle of a pot set beyond his reach, and the knowing and the wanting went on under the same roof without a word between them.

Two hundred and five honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed, turned out the way instruments are turned out on a towel at the end of a list, every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing moved. The row stood at five askings with no witness, the well-saying at three shapings, the burned slate at five tellings with a finger between the fifth and the four, the shut-room tellings at two, the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note on its own place, nine hands with the strip unread in a sleeve and no tenth, and nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth, and the fifth opening stood where it stood with the next far off. Three hundred and fifteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill or Grath; six months and one hundred and thirty-five days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the sixteenth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended, and the keeping of the number was the only honest work there was to do and it bought nothing at two inches of air.

The hand lay back against the fold on the two hundred and fifth answer, and the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap, and the river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door while the morning came on without a boundary drawn.
