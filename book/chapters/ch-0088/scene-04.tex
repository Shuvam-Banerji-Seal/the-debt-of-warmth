The afternoon came on without drawing a line across itself, the light leaving the wall for the floor and then the floor for the yard, and the seventeenth went on in the same unmarked way it had come on under the door. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the edge of it under a fold of wool, carried, silent, the crust two inches from his ear and the thread at his mouth frozen in the dry heat where nobody had thought to wipe it. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the day kept the fire and kept the silence and put the fold right without a look at the face inside it. \person{Sella Vail} came for the basket after the middle of the day. She gave no instruction to the basket. The instruction went past it to the wool and the river wind --- \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} --- as though the wool had ears and the wind could be told where to stand. She took the basket up as she took up the twice-mended pot, settling the weight before the move, carried it down at the half-pace she kept from every open slate into \place{the Clay Yard}, set it level where no slate lay, and left it there as a pot is left.

The half-pace was not haste and it was not fear of slopping. It was a distance she kept from open slates the way another woman keeps a distance from a sleeping dog, not because the dog will wake but because a thing left alone is worth leaving alone. She did not look into the basket and did not say whose child it was; by the time the yard went back to its work he had been set down level, out of the draught, and given nobody's name.

The yard went around its seven places with a look and a wide step and no number.

At the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge turned there lay a clean dark line, and beside it a finer grey hairline that the older women called \lore{the thaw} and the slatemen would have called something else if they had called it at all; both lay unswept, the old dark under the new fine as a seam lies under a later stitching, and a broom went around the two of them together and took neither. Beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they had been laid, their edges paler than the rack dust sifted over them, and the sweeping went wide of the space beneath as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. On the stone beside the well a round ring marked where a shallow clay pan had once stood, and the ring held no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip, no word --- only the darker circle where the pan's bottom had kept the dust off --- and nobody pointed at it and nobody swept it and nobody said what had been set there. Then there was the water in \place{the trough}, skinned from edge to edge; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, which was a direction and not a thing standing; and the returning-line place, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, which the yard went around wider than all the rest and entered with a look and a wide step and no number over it.

Seven places, and no eighth stood up to make a row. The boy took them in the order the carrying arm took them --- the corner, the rack, the ring, the trough, the strips, the returning line --- and kept each where it lay with a finger between every pair. He did not number them. A number spoken over seven absences would have wanted a payer put under them and none stood; a row of eight likenesses would have stood up and called itself a rule, and there was no account in the district that had not already found a payer for a rule. The yard went around and went on, and nothing was mended by the going around, and nothing was paid.

At \place{the yard well} three coverings stood and were called weather.

The stayed frost had gone off the north lip while the yard looked elsewhere --- gone in the way a thing goes when no hand takes it and no word is spent on its going --- and the north lip was bare, and the yard said the thaw had taken it, and entered it nowhere. The trough kept its skin, a grey membrane drawn tight from edge to edge, and when a pail knocked it the skin broke and closed again and was called weather. On \place{the flat stone by the well}, out of the path of feet, Sella had set a plate of ice clouded with silt, lifted off the trough with the back of a broken pot and carried three paces and set down where no foot went, and the plate stood there clouded and going nowhere and was called weather. Three coverings, and a finger between every pair: the frost that had gone without a payer, the skin on the water, a piece of the depth lifted and set where nobody would step. No fourth likeness was made a cause and no figure came off any of them. The boy kept the three as three acts of three different hands and would not let them stand up as one hand's habit, because a habit wants a payer and none stood to any of them.

At \place{the rack end} the dressed grain lay under wool away from the hearth, and Sella turned it with a progressively lighter touch --- a corner lifted, a handful let fall back, the wool drawn over again --- and gave it no figure. It had been read over on Bone 10 by a number that did not know what it cost; the number had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box and had not come back as a lesson or a mending, and the grain lay as it lay, changed in state and uncounted. Repair is not restoration and a count is not a cost, but she did not say either, because a saying would have wanted somebody standing under it to be asked.

The afternoon's hunts and repairs went on as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, and never as figures. A man came up from the strips with a jar whose handle had been mended and asked what the repair would want, and a woman at the well told him to ask the pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing, and the man went off with his jar and no entry standing behind him. Another, shifting a burden from one collarbone to the other, said the cost was the weather --- it had carried the snow, it could carry the price --- and went off toward the clay road with nobody weighing what he carried and nobody opening a slate. The boy watched the shoulders rather than the burden, because the shoulders told the truth and the burden was only what the man chose to show; both tellings arrived nowhere and neither was entered.

\scenebreak

At the edge of the work \person{Ilva} drew at the well with the pail against her knee. The rope was stiff in the dry cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board, and had never been soft, and went on knowing these things without reporting on them to anybody. A slate lay open on the stone lip a pace away with the writing turned toward its owner, and nobody reading it aloud.

A woman with a pot asked what the firings of Bone had taken.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of notched birch bark, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, was not taken out, read, copied, counted or joined to the charcoal under the shelf, the cord, the open slate a pace away, the knot-string or the ownerless saying, and no figure came off the wood. It stayed where it stayed, a count kept where no slate could take a figure off it.

Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word. She answered the question put and not the one behind it.

\emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account}, she said.

She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the order of reaching apart as he takes apart a reaching --- by what was there and by what was not. Hand to the record before eyes to the person; eyes before mouth. He filed it as the ninth record-first hand on a place of its own with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth, and filed no tenth. He made no figure of the slate's nearness and did not make her a reader of her own keeping, which she was not and had never wanted to be. She was not \person{Marra}, not the soft-handed man, not the soft-cuffed clerk, not Hessa, not the green-mouthed woman at the pot board; no reading had come to her well. Nine hands stood nine and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. He did not spend the seeing to bring the far-off hand nearer, and she did not know she had been read.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} worked at \place{the kindling} with the small knife lying at the very edge of its sill, where a house could check it and where a man's hand could also slip it off. He had put it there before the light and had not announced it, because the yard had already been told and a man does not say the same thing twice to a house that does not answer. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check, and the seventh stood at the lip beyond the mark where the house could not, and beside the seventh lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence --- the one about a man who had turned back at a frozen ford --- with the loan uncalled and no collector come up the clay road or gone down it.

\emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the kindling, in the tone of a man stating the weather. He set the knife back where it could be checked and did not look toward the house.

The ear-sentence came up again as he reached for the wood: the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it. It came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name on the man who had turned back. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; the small interval had stood at the top of every reach since Seed 16, and his chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line at the corner, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was under the shed edge and gave it back, and he held the wrist up a moment to the grey as though to see whether the light had come to anything. A ninth stopping, remembered, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven; the loan that could hear was worth more than the debt that held him and was unsafe for that, and he said neither sentence to any man.

The boy, carried past at the edge of the work, took the stopping apart as he takes apart a reaching --- by what was there and by what was not --- and filed it as an object on its own place, an interval and not a payment, a ninth stopping and not a tenth, with no payer standing under it and no rule standing up out of nine. He did not look toward Tarn's wrist and Tarn did not look toward the basket. Two ears kept their equality under one yard, a loan that could hear and had never been called, and the two silences were of one shape with nobody putting a word between them.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, and asked neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the bench he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not say it before a kiln-master out of \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make something of it, singing before its time and singing itself out early. A thing held back whole is one thing; a thing spent to make a row come out even is another, and he had never spent one.

Before Sella had finished the sentence she was saying to the pot, his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot. The boy read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, and the iron was joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

On \place{the flagstones} Tarn's stroke went over the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped and gave nobody a figure. The returned half-check of Bone 8 stayed on the flagstone's own place with the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat and the Frost 5 returned beat beside it --- four held changes on four places and not a rule, no fifth likeness made a cause and no payer named. The stayed-at-the-inch object was not made a fifth of them, and the early letting-go of the morning was not made one either. One staying is a keeping and four held changes are four, and a finger went between them rather than letting the edges touch and stand up as a habit with a payer under it. A thing kept by not being made again is still kept, and nobody under any roof had made a lesson of it.

Then the boy checked the tray the way he once checked a field at the edge of the light, by the small absences first, because the small absences do not lie. The burned slate stood at five with no sixth and no witness and no hand named at any flame; the row at five askings with one shaping and no witness; the well-saying at three shapings; the shut-room tellings at two; the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note on its own place; nine hands with Ilva's ninth unread in a sleeve and no tenth; six hearings of the full note standing beside and never among, the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came out of vessels, and the seventh place kept empty; the heard bell in its square beside the told; the seventh green a colour and the seventh shape a shape with no eighth. Five askings are five and not one witness; three shapings are three and not a confession; five tellings of a burned slate are five tellings and not five witnesses to one fire. Nothing on the tray stood up and called itself a rule, and no payer stood under any of it.

The staying stayed one object on its own place on the sixth, and the early letting-go of the morning stayed one object on its own place beside it with a finger between. Neither was made again. Neither joined the four held changes on four places, and neither was laid across the boy's own kept gaps or the house's \lore{not-doings}. Neither stood up as a rule: two would stand up and call themselves a habit, and a habit is an account that has found a payer, and none stood. One letting-go early is a letting-go and nothing more; to make it twice is to make a way of it, and a way wants a payer under it. He left them where they lay and asked nothing of either.

\scenebreak

The light went out of the yard without a boundary drawn, and Sella came for the basket at the half-pace and carried it up and set it on \place{the bed} under the roof, never in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, her hand resting a half-beat longer than the setting needed before it went away, and left the thread where it lay. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and a basket set down under a roof is a pot set down and left. Under \place{the boards} four nails stood on the near board and four on the next, and a thumb-width knot stood where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not; under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for three hundred and sixteen days; at the finger's-width door the river kept \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, and was not numbered among the hearings. Three counts in three places that did not become one, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and none stood.

Then the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his. It let the intake go whole on the same low note, exact and complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and came straight down and laid itself back against the fold. He filed it as answer two hundred and eight. The letting-go was not early a second time: the note had done before the hand came down, and the hand came down on the silence after it as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The stayed-at-the-inch object stayed one object and the early letting-go stayed one object, and neither was made again. The answer was a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

Two hundred and eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and the file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed, turned out the way instruments are turned out on a towel at the end of a list, every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing moved. The early letting-go sat on its own place with a finger between it and every other object there.

The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping and the infant underneath wanted the crust lifted two inches and the thread wiped, and both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument between them was the work. The house kept what it kept by not-doing and the boy kept what he kept by not-making-again, and neither was a kindness entered on any slate. Three hundred and sixteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} and \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and thirty-six days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; and Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. Nothing on the seventeenth had been opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended, and the keeping of the number was the only honest work there was to do and it bought nothing at two inches of air.

The hand came straight down on the two hundred and eighth answer with the letting-go not early a second time, and the early letting-go of the morning sat on its own place with a finger between it and everything else, and the file lay open on the towel with every gap left gap. The river's low note went quiet under the finger's-width door while the seventeenth went on without a boundary drawn.
