\daymark{375}{Stone 16, Sixthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been put at the close of the day before, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-four exact. \person[Corin]{The boy} did not carry it forward by an act of will; a man's attention goes where his field is, and his field was two inches of air above a barley crust and the small sounds of a house warming in its sleep. He lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, and this morning had a date no roof under that sky would speak aloud: \place{Stone} 16, the sixteenth day of the deep-winter month. Three hundred and seventy-four days, six months and one hundred and ninety-four days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under this roof the same number. The file had come out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, and he went over it by the place of each thing with every gap left gap.

There were a hundred things in the room he could not change and one he could only read. He took the body in the old order, because a field is checked in an order and a surgeon does not trust an impression that has not been made to stand in sequence. First the crust: one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear, gone harder and browner in the dry kiln heat, its corner standing a hair off the wool. It had stood two inches off for three hundred and seventy-four days and would stand two inches off today; the distance was not a wish and had no giver. Then the cold. It had come cheek, temple, the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and it had stopped there as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it with neither an order to go farther nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. The thread of drool at his lip had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof has ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

Then the place beside the crust. It held the fold of wool where a hand had put it on the fifteenth, and he found it there in the small hours, folded once, pale in the dry heat, changed by nobody in the night. There was no second fold laid over the first. There was no third crust. No name had been spoken over the place in the dark, no look had bent toward his face, no instruction had gone to the basket. The wool lay where it had been put and had not been put again.

He took the interval apart by what was there and by what was not, as a surgeon takes a wound apart by the tissue present and the tissue missing. In the first small fear of the morning he had thought the keeping might have been a mistake of the evening, a hand's careless extra that would be corrected or repeated by daylight, and the fear had been small because it was not a fear for himself. But the wool lay unchanged, and a thing that is found where it was put and not made again is a keeping still. A hand that finds is not a hand that places. A second placing would make a habit and a third a way, and a habit and a way both want a payer standing under them, and none stood. The satisfaction that came to him was clean and exact, the satisfaction of a field taken as found and left as found; it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace.

He filed the keeping on a place of its own beside the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps in the room whose floor did not touch this one, the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood on \place{the flagstones}, and the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}. He kept a finger between it and every other, and between it and the pot-crack resting, the hand-at-the-sill breath and the holding in the damper, so that no row came out even by his doing. It was expressly not a joining of rooms, though the likeness between a fold and a shelf's two gaps was plain enough to make a man reach across with his finger; he did not reach. It was not a third crust, for no crust had been put there. It was not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave. It was not a payment and there was no payer. It was not a sixth held object and not a seventh or an eighth, not a hearing, not a mending, not a sign, not a cause, not a rule. The far-off finger that would one day lay shelf and fold together and call the joining an account was far off still and was not his and had not come. The keeping gathered interest where it lay and was not paid and did not stand up and call itself a want.

He knew a face composed by cold is not composure, and that pink returning to a face is the surface still cold and not the core; that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that the order in which heat comes back is the reverse of the order in which it goes. He knew a basket set level out of the draught where the heat came sideways was the careful rewarming he would choose and could not ask for. All of it was true and all of it was useless to a body that could not lift a crust two inches, wipe a thread, or close a hand on a pot handle. The infant wanted the crust lifted to his mouth, the thread wiped, and a hand on the pot rim. The surgeon read the wool as a warmth laid against the cold side of a vessel that would not hold it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work; he had stopped expecting the quarrel to end, which was not the same as being used to it.

\person{Sella Vail} came to the hearth before she came to the basket, as she had come at both ends of every day for three years. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. Then she stood a moment with her hands gone still, counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure, so that the count went into the room and not onto a slate.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle, and the proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person. “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled.” And: “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” They were improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them, who had said them to a pot and never to a person; he was present in the house in objects and not in a story, in the salt-stiff coat with its left-shoulder crease on its peg behind the door, in the hollow wheel-seat nobody filled, in the clean dark line at the corner with its finer grey called \lore{the thaw} unswept and thickened in the month's dry cold, with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where it had not been swept in two years. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it, and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}, the half-beat the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift, for a gift wants a giver and nobody gave.

She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, and lifted no figure and no handful. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze}, and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closing on air. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung, untouched, because a morning and a hearth and a shelf are not openings, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth was made due.

Under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and seventy-five days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood.

The held things were checked without standing up, as a man checks a field by its small sounds before he lets the light into it. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, the sixth the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources; the seventh place stayed empty and was not dug for or filled. The flat knock of \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} stood on its own place outside them, not a seventh and not a filling of the empty place, for a hearing wants a source standing and that room had been seen from a basket and not entered. The heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap, and was not folded into any of them.

Nine record-first hands stood with no tenth: \person[Hessa Rale]'s eighth at \place{the Cross}, \person{Ilva}'s ninth at the well, the collector's ordinary hand on its own tray joined to none, and no hand stood to lay them together and call them a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood. Nine stoppings kept no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the loan uncalled and the Seed 16 breath unspent. Ten arithmetics stood behind one unsigned figure — the three of \place{Frost}, the first, second and third of \place{Bone}, \place{Ash}'s first, second and third and \place{Stone}'s first — the figure read still not the figure paid, no signature on the space and no doctrine come out of ten; their ten true subtractions rode in \person[Berr Clay]'s box up \place{the clay road} locked, unread under this roof, the key gone up in a coat pocket and not coming back down. Four rooms kept the same gap in their count — the birth room in \place{House Silt}, the Vail kiln-house, the room where the slate had been read on market-day and \place{the Drawing house} seen two doors down — held apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account, an account wanting a hand to lay them together and a payer under all four, and no such hand standing and no such payer.

No sixth hearing, seventh opening, sixth held object, sixth telling, third shut-room telling, sixth asking, fifth shaping, tenth hand or tenth stopping was supplied to make a row come out even, for a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The burned slate stood at five, the shut-room tellings at two, the row at five askings with no witness and the well-saying at four shapings, and none was made a sixth of on a day that had not earned one. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson. The keeping at the fold sat on its own place beside all of these and was not laid across any of them.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over it from bank to bank lay \lore{the winter skin} the cold had put on and the cold would take off, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this morning and not made again, not spent on the day's work or the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. The skin sat on its own place beside the note and not upon it, a covering put on by the weather and entered nowhere, and he entered it nowhere and kept a finger between it and the note it muffled.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note, the way a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed and the hand did not stay; the note's own finishing under the covering ended the interval and not any decision of the hand. The intake was not a breath kept for warmth, the interval not a stopping of the river, the note not his.

He filed it as answer three hundred and twenty-five: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the river's skin, the widow's mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and twenty-five honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

The wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the leathery crust with its corner a hair off the wool; no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The hand lay flat on the fold on answer three hundred and twenty-five, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-five exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-five days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \place{Stone} kept its sixteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held both its names, the older women calling it by the weather and the slatemen by the count of Account-days, neither habit set right or left. The fold was warm where a hand had been and the place beside the crust had not been filled again and had not been named, and the boy lay under the roof with the day's finding gathered on its own place and not paid, and nothing in the house stood up to call it an account. Forward, not closed.
