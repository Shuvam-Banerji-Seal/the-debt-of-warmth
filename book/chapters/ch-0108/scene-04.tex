The afternoon came on in the dry cold without a boundary drawn, and \person{Sella Vail} carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace she keeps from every open slate, the basket level as a pot that must not be slopped. The instruction went to the wool, to the river wind, to the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never to the basket. \person[Corin]{The boy} had no use for the date and had never needed one. Three hundred and seventy-six days stood exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-six days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted, and the seventeenth of \place{Stone} went about its work in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and out in \place{the Clay Yard}. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth stood unpaid the same number with its payer unnamed, and \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken under that roof the same number. Nothing that had happened in the night crossed into the afternoon as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause, a payment or a payer. A night is a thing that happens to a house and not a hand that writes in it.

The yard went around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number. The step was widest around \place{the returning-line place}, where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, wider by nothing that could be measured and once and not twice, because a second going-around would have made a figure of once. At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey hairline beside it, the thing the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the month's dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up off it. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay entered by nobody. At \place{the rack end} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word.

The three coverings stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair. The gone white frost off the north lip of \place{the yard well}, the skin drawn edge to edge across \place{the trough}, and the silt-clouded plate of ice on \place{the flat stone by the well} a little out of the path of feet were not a fourth covering and not a sixth low thing. Through \place{the finger's-width door} \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over it from bank to bank lay \lore{the winter skin} that the cold had put on and that the cold would take off. It was found where put beside the note and on its own place, not spent on the afternoon's work and not made to buy a lesson under any roof. He kept the covering on its own place and put a finger between the covering and the note it muffled, so that no row came out even by his doing.

In the afternoon \person{Hessa Rale} went up \place{the clay road} out of Low Kiln in the boy's sight, at an hour when the road lay long ahead of her and no reading waited at its end. The small flat correction of the second strap came first, let out for the long walk, and the turn of the wrist brought the edge of the slate round; only after both did her eyes go to the yard. Her hand had gone to the slate before either. \person{Berr Clay}'s narrow slate lay edge-on to her own forearm with the writing turned toward her sleeve, the thumb along the grain of the frame and nothing pressing where the writing was. That was not concealment but the manner of carrying a slate so the writing was not rubbed and no goer-by took a figure off it without a hand to put there. Dried pale scurf of road-mud stood unbrushed on the skirt she had shortened for the road; a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake.

A woman waiting with a basket of kindling asked what she carried.

“A slate,” she said. “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.”

The woman wanted to know what was written on it.

“Ask the road. It knows the way and I know the weight. That's the whole of what either of us knows.” She settled the second strap again, not for the woman and not for the yard, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. She gave no figure. She read nothing aloud. She did not look into the basket.

Sella carried him past at the half-pace and said nothing of the runner and gave the house no name for the slate.

The boy took the order of her reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart: hand to the record before hand to the person, eyes after the hand, mouth after the eyes, the flat correction of the strap in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention and find none. He filed the turning of the writing toward herself on a place of its own \emph{beside} the Ash 6 sighting, with a finger between. A runner going up the road with the writing turned toward herself was not a voice standing in a doorway and not a saying fulfilled. He made no fifth shaping of the Frost 14 well-saying, no witness, no reader and no payment, and joined her neither to \person{Ilva}'s strip, nor to Berr's thumb, nor to the charcoal under \place{the loaf shelf}, nor to the soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate, nor to any other private keeping. Her eighth record-first hand stayed eight with no tenth. She was a runner and not a reader, and the road did not care which. He did not make a reader of her early, and the far-off day she might choose to read what she had carried was not brought nearer by a single step of her walking.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} kept the account as he had kept it on the sixteenth. Six pieces stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it, beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. His hand went over blade and sill as a man passes over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away when the checking was done with no breath held at the top of the reach. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark, the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and his stroke went over \place{the flagstones}' shallow bright patch without the half-check.

“Six counted. The next I don't count,” he said to the wood, and cut nothing, counted nothing and spent nothing.

The held breath he had left at the sill on Ash 14 was where it had been put, found where put and not made again; the Bone 26 thumb's resting was likewise where it had been left. Both sat on places of their own beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting, with a finger between every pair — not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not the seventh year given, not the calling of the loan, and with no payer standing. To a woman with kindling asking what the seventh year would want, and to two fence-menders saying the thaw would be early and the thaw would be late, he said “Yes, house” to each and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot. The hare-carrier down from \place{Cold Acre} answered the price with the weather that had carried the snow and could carry the price, and went off toward the clay road with the hare unweighed. The cracked-jar man in his rope sling was sent to a pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing.

The ear-sentence came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, the ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, the Seed 16 breath unspent at the top of the reach and the loan uncalled. Two ears, two silences, a loan that can hear and has never been called, with nobody to enter it. He did not look along the road. A man who owns nothing keeps the exact account of what he is owed and what he owes, and the account stood where it stood without rising into a rule.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey; he gave the house no name for them and no word that the room was cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate, while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, knowing the \lore{full note} it would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and keeping the knowing where nobody could make a song of it, withheld from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. The boy kept the knowing on a tray of its own beside the hearings and never among them, and no seventh hearing stood up in the empty place.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \place{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word covering both mercies. He took the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once on a place of its own and not made again, for a second would be a habit and a third a way and both want a payer and none stood. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

Four rooms kept the same gap in their count. The birth room in \place{House Silt}; the Vail kiln-house; the room where the slate had been read on market-day under \place{the Cross}; \place{the Drawing house} seen two doors down and not entered. He held them apart with a finger between every pair, four likenesses and not one account, for an account wants a hand to lay the places together and a payer under all four, and no such hand stood and no such payer stood. The keeping at the fold sat on its own place beside these and was not laid across any of them. Neither the runner on the road nor the wheel in the shed nor the six at the kindling brought a hand near it.

At the going of the light Sella carried him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}, and set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of the loaf shelf. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and did not add a second fold to the empty place beside the crust. Her hand did only what the setting needed and went away; the little-door burn closed on air; the thread of drool at his lip was left frozen in the dry heat and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; she said nothing of where they had been. No ownerless voice came over \place{the boards} on this day of all days. The Ash 5 joining, the Ash 17 direction and the Ash 23 direction stood each where it had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson.

Under the roof the hearth grammar was renewed as it had been at both ends of every day for three years. Sella came to \place{the hearth} before the basket, told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure; her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung, untouched, because a morning and a hearth are not openings and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule and no eighth was made due.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle; both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person — “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” and “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” They were improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and lifted no figure and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, the half-beat the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift.

The wider file was checked without standing up, as a man checks a field by its small sounds before he lets the light into it. In the small hours the file came out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done, and the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the low note under the door as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed, and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and twenty-eight: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the child's grey fingertips, the mother's cup, the covered grain, the handful, the thumb's resting, the held breath, the two bites of iron, the river's skin, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Three hundred and twenty-eight honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel was the work.

The narrow slate went up the clay road with the writing turned toward the sleeve, and the boy made no reader of the woman who carried it. The wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the leathery crust with its corner a hair off the wool; no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The hand lay flat against the fold on answer three hundred and twenty-eight, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-six exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-six days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. Stone kept its seventeenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held both its names, and nothing under the roof stood up to call the day an account.
