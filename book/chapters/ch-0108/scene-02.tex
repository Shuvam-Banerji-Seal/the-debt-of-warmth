The carry down came before the yard came into view, and it came before every open slate. \person{Sella Vail} went down the nineteen steps at the half-pace she kept from all slates that lay unwritten, with the instruction going ahead of her into the wool, the river wind and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and never into the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she said to the wool. She held \person[Corin]{the boy} level as a pot is held that must not be slopped, the whole of her attention on the level of him and none of it on his face, and set him down out of the draught where the heat came sideways. He felt the change of air through the wool before he felt the cold on his cheek, the way a hand laid on a field feels the edge of a draught before the field itself goes cold. It was the careful rewarming he would have chosen for a patient who could not shiver, and he could not ask for it and had no need to, for it was already made.

\place{The Clay Yard} went about its afternoon as a pot goes about its cooling, by degrees and without an eye on itself. There were seven places the household walked around, and she went around them with a look and a wide step and no number; the look was not a counting and the step was not a measurement, and neither of them stood up and called itself an account. She went widest around the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out, wider by nothing that could be measured, and she went around it once and not twice, because a second going-around would make a figure of once and a figure wants a payer standing under it, and none stood.

At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line kept its finer grey hairline beside it, the thing the house called \lore{the thaw}, unswept and thickened in the dry cold, with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up off it where it had not been swept in two years. Under \place{the lowest rack} the broken birth-slates lay where they lay and were entered by nobody; at \place{the rack end} the round ring where the shallow pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word. The gone white frost off the north lip of \place{the yard well}, the skin drawn over \place{the trough} from edge to edge, and the silt-clouded plate of ice set on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet stayed weather entered nowhere, with a finger between every pair: not a fourth covering laid over the three, not a sixth low thing set beside the five held objects, not spent on the day's work or the going of the light and not made to buy a lesson under any roof.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; over it from bank to bank lay \lore{the winter skin} that the cold had put on and that the cold would take off, found where put on the first day of the month and found again this afternoon and not made again. He kept it on its own place beside the note and put a finger between the covering and the note it muffled, so that no row came out even by his doing. A line left where it is is still a line kept. A sign is a thing that points to a payer, and no payer stood under that ground. Nothing under that roof had laid the skin and nothing under that roof would take it off; the cold did both and entered neither into a slate. He had learned to tell the difference between a thing done to the world and a thing the world had done to itself, and the difference was the whole of the keeping: a hand that finds is not a hand that places, and a sign without a payer is weather and not a promise.

\person{Ilva} was at the well drawing with the pail against her knee, and the rope stood stiff in the Stone cold and gave its small dry rasp as the bucket came up. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out; her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board and had never been soft, and the people going about the yard noticed the pail first and the sleeve not at all, which was the order she preferred.

A woman waiting her turn asked what \place{Stone}'s firings had taken, and the order of Ilva's reaching went before the answer as a surgeon's hand goes to the chart before it goes to the patient. Her hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there; the short strip of notched birch bark lay in the fold where it always lay, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, and it was not taken out, not read, not copied, not counted and not joined to the open slate a pace away on the stone lip, or to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, or to the cord, or to the knot-string, and no figure came off the wood. Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both with the half-beat in the gap where a lesser observer would put an intention. “Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.” She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

The boy took the order of reaching apart as a surgeon takes a field apart and filed it on a place of its own as the ninth record-first hand, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth; he filed no tenth, made no reader of her keeping, joined her to none and made no figure of the slate's nearness. She was expressly not the speaker of any voice. The Ash 5 joining and the Ash 17 and Ash 23 directions stood where they had been put and did not repeat and bought no lesson on this afternoon of all afternoons.

The yard's hunts and repairs came and went as directions and never as figures. A man down from \place{Cold Acre} shifted a hare across his shoulders and, to a woman who asked what the carrying had cost, said “Ask the weather. It carried the snow. It can carry the price,” and went off toward \place{the clay road} with the hare unweighed and nobody opening a slate. A man with a cracked jar in a sling of rope asked what the month's mending would want and was sent to a pot, and the pot, being a pot, said nothing. A woman with a basket of kindling asked what the seventh year would want when it came, and two men mending a strip of fence said the thaw would be early and the thaw would late; to each of them \person{Tarn} said “Yes, house,” and kept the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, agreeing so thoroughly that there was nowhere for an argument to find a foot.

At \place{the kindling} the account stood in its old exactness. Six pieces lay in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check them and the seventh lay at the lip beyond it where the house could not, and beside the seventh lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence about the man who turned back at the frozen ford, with no name on the man and no figure on the debt. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where a hand could also slip it off. Tarn passed his hand over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and told the wood, “Six counted. The next I don't count,” and cut nothing, counted nothing and spent nothing. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught what little light there was and gave it back; his chest had been tight since the dark, and the Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch of \place{the flagstones} without the half-check.

The Ash 14 held breath and the Bone 26 thumb's resting were where they had been put, found this afternoon and not made again, and they lay beside the five held objects and the pot-crack resting with a finger between every pair: not a sixth held object, not a seventh, not a tenth stopping, not the calling of the loan, with no payer standing under any of them. He brought the ear-sentence up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, as a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent and the loan stayed uncalled, and he did not look along the road. Two ears, two silences, a loan that can hear and has never been called, with nobody standing to enter it. The boy took the equality as he took the rest, as a field taken as found and left as found, and made no fifth shaping of it and brought no word of it under the roof.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, and it was not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. Three or more men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey, and he gave the house no name for them and said nothing about the room being cold. He kept his dusty forearms and his clean ones on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate while the broken ones lay under the lowest rack and were entered by nobody. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot he said, “You'll do,” with no welcome in the voice and no unkindness either in it, knowing the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and keeping the knowing where no master come up from \place{High Kiln} could call it early and sing a pot before its time.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low when his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of her sentence, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and was left standing there, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the waiting as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. One piece of iron had two bites, the holding and the taking, and no Grathic word covered both mercies. He brought the finger out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time; that holding had been gathered once and stayed where nobody had entered it, for a second would make a habit and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, gathering interest against the burned slate at five and the three Ash directions, which stood where they were put and did not repeat.

At the going of the light she carried him up the nineteen steps under the roof and never into \place{the Debt Loft}, and the instruction went before her to the wool, the pot and the fire she would bank against their coming back, and not to the basket. She set him on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and did not tuck a second fold into the empty place. Her hand did only what the setting needed and went away; the little-door burn on the back of her left hand closed on air; the thread at his mouth was left frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread; and she said nothing of where they had been.

The boy filed the keeping found where put and not made again: not a habit, for a habit wants a second placing; not a way, for a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood; not a sign, a hearing, a mending, a gift, a payment or a rule. A gift wants a giver and nobody gave. It sat on a place of its own beside the crust's gap, the shelf's two gaps in the room whose floor does not touch this one, the round ring at the rack end and the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, with a finger between it and every other, gathered once and not made again, gathering interest against the far-off hand that was not his and had not come.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, the hearth grammar was renewed at the going of the light as it was renewed at every end of every day for three years. Sella came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket. “It's the thaw,” she told the coals, and turned them with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said where another woman would have said I. She stood a moment counting a small intake, a hold and a letting-go toward no opening, and gave neither gap a figure, so that the count went into the room and not onto a slate. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread; her lips moved along a two-gapped row and figured neither gap; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on air. The cord of her \lore{widow's mark} was oiled where a working thumb had touched it ten thousand times, and the mark hung where it hung, untouched, because an evening and a hearth are not openings and seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule and no eighth was made due.

On \place{the pot board} the twice-mended pot kept the hairline of the original break showing between the old-brass staple and the tighter wire. Her thumb went first where the wire crossed the crack before the weight came on the handle; both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person — “Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled,” and “If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one.” They were improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. Repair stayed apart from restoration: a restored thing hides the hand that handled it and a mended thing leaves that hand showing. She turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and lifted no figure and no handful, and set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward the bed, the half-beat the distance between intention and arrest, bought at nothing and not a gift.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name three hundred and seventy-five days, and nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near one, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not. In the small hours the file came out on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done, and the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note under the finger's-width door as a hand goes up in a field it has learned not to disturb. It did not reach, and the inch was not a reach. He took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing under the winter skin, let the intake go as the note ended and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. The note had done before the hand came down, so the letting-go was neither early nor stayed and the hand did not stay.

He filed it as answer three hundred and twenty-six: exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, laid across neither the ten readings, the bell's gap, the flat knock, the river's skin, the widow's mark, the keeping at the fold nor his own kept gaps. Three hundred and twenty-six honest answers stood on the sixth place and not one was a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The surgeon took his small clean satisfaction in a field taken as found and left as found, and it cost him nothing, which was how he knew it was not peace. The infant wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won, and the quarrel between them was the work.

The wool lay folded once in the empty place beside the leathery crust with its corner a hair off the wool; no second fold, no third crust and no name spoken over the place. The hand lay flat against the fold on answer three hundred and twenty-six, and the count stood at three hundred and seventy-five exact, six months and one hundred and ninety-five days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \place{Stone} kept its sixteenth day without a boundary drawn, the deep-winter month held both its names, and nothing under the roof stood up to call the day an account.
