The afternoon came on over \place{Bone} 14 without a boundary drawn, the light going off \place{the pot board} and lying along the floor in a bar that shortened while nobody watched it shorten. \person{Sella Vail} lifted the basket from the low angle of the shelf and carried \person[Corin]{the boy} down at the half-pace she kept with a full pail, level, with nothing slopped. Three hundred and thirteen stood in him, exact, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and one hundred and thirty-three days were told by nobody under a roof that kept its days in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood. The fifth pull of \lore{the carried fire} had gone unpaid that number of days with its payer unnamed, and the name under the roof had gone unspoken the same number. The fold went over his ears before the door had finished closing, and the instruction went to the wool and the river wind --- \emph{Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind} --- and never to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot set down is left.

Woodsmoke went before her in low bars that the wind broke and gathered again, and under it came \place{the Clay Yard}: wet clay, the cooled smell of tallow, the sour mineral breath off the corner that had not been swept in two years. He took the place in the order a field is taken before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. A pot set down flat out of sight. A broom drawn over stone. The low note of \lore{water over water}. The small dry scrape of a boot shifted on grit. A child in a basket sees the ground first and hears the small sounds before he hears the voices that would explain them, and the small sounds had never once explained anybody.

There were seven places the yard went around, and she went around all seven with a look and a wide step and no number. At \place{the Vail corner} the clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned lay beside a finer grey hairline called \lore{the thaw}, and both were unswept; she set her foot past them as a woman sets a cup past the edge of a table she knows by heart. Beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} the broken birth-slates lay where the yard had put them, and the space above them was gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it. Near \place{the yard well} the round bare ring where the shallow clay pan had once stood kept no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word --- a place named from the shape it had left and not from anything remaining in it. \place{the trough} wore its skin. Far out on \place{the worked strips} a field's empty place stood where a field should have stood. In the returning line a place stood where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven; she made no eighth likeness stand up as a row and put no figure to any of them, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood under any of these. The boy kept the seven on seven places of his own and drew no line between them and the house's \lore{not-doings}, or between them and the small gaps in his own file. The edges came near to touching, and the finger went between.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it in the yard or at the well or in the going of the light, because an afternoon and a yard and a well are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

At the well the stayed frost had gone off the north lip while the yard looked elsewhere --- gone without a word spoken over it and without a hand to say where it had gone. The skin on the trough remained. The silt-clouded plate of ice stood on \place{the flat stone by the well} out of the path of feet, where somebody had lifted it and set it down and told the weather where to stand. Three coverings in three places, and the yard called all of them weather and entered none of them. He put a finger between the gone lip and the skin, and another between the skin and the clouded plate, and made no fourth likeness stand up as a cause and named no payer to any of the three. He had once read a covering laid over a depth in a room with hard light in it, reading it by what it hid and by what the hand that laid it had chosen to leave showing; the reading had been exact and had never once told him who had paid for what lay beneath. Here the covering was called weather, which was a word that let the yard carry on with its work, and he did not call the word a lie and did not call it honest, and kept it on its own place.

\person{Ilva} was at the well drawing with the pail against her knee, the rope stiff in the dry cold and giving its small dry rasp as the bucket came up. Clay had dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey rack dust from the drying racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, which she did not scrub out. Her hands knew the weight of a pail and the edge of a wet board, and had never been soft, and went on knowing these things without reporting on them to anybody. She was of the firing work and not of a house, and went on with her water without looking to see who had noticed her, which was the order she preferred: the pail first, the sleeve not at all.

A woman with a pot stopped at the well lip and asked what the firings of Bone had taken. It was a question asked the way cost-questions are asked in that yard, out toward nobody in particular, so that if the answer turned out expensive it would belong to whoever chose to pick it up. Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there. The short strip of notched birch bark, pale where a thumb had rubbed the notches and dark in the uncut grain between, did not come out. It was not taken out, read, copied, counted, given a figure off or joined to the charcoal beneath \place{the loaf shelf}, the cord, the open slate lying on the stone lip a pace away, the knot-string or the ownerless saying over the boards, and no figure came off the wood. A slate lay open near enough for a hand to reach, and the hand did not reach to it, and nearness was not made into a reading.

Her eyes went to the asker and her mouth opened after both, with a half-beat between the looking and the word. The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay and counted the half-beat because it was there to count, and gave it no name and made no claim on it. \emph{Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account}, she said, and answered the question put and not the one behind it. She put the flat of her hand once more on the sleeve, and went back to the pail, and nobody turned twice.

He took the order of her reaching apart as he had learned to take a reaching apart in a better-lit room, by what was there and by what was not: hand toward the record before eyes to the person, eyes before mouth, and the mouth opening last on the question that had been asked. It came out the same as it had come out on Bone 7, which was not a second finding but the same finding returned, and he filed no tenth. The ninth record-first hand stayed nine on a place of its own, with a finger between it and \person{Hessa Rale}'s eighth, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. He made no figure of the slate's nearness and did not make her a reader of her own keeping, which she was not and had never wanted to be.

She was not \person{Marra}, not the soft-handed man who sat at a table with his figure held at the edge of a slate, not the soft-cuffed clerk set below the north bridge for a measure nobody called, not Hessa with her edge-on road to Greybank, and not the green-mouthed woman at the pot board; the green at that board came off \lore{lead glaze} and belonged to nobody and was not hers. Bone 10's reading had gone up \place{the clay road} in a locked box and had not come to this well, and no figure from the square, printed or paid, had reached her pail. The count stood where she had put it, in wood, where no slate could take a figure off it, and the far-off hand that would one day lay the district's private keepings together was still far off and was not his.

Bone's hunts and repairs went on as directions judged by the carrying arm and the weather, never as figures. A woman come up from the strips with a jar whose handle had been mended asked what the mending had cost now the light went early. Sella, going a step past with the basket level, sent the question where such questions went in that yard. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, she said, and said it low and toward the pot rather than to the woman, and the woman went off with the direction still in her mouth and no figure in her hand. No entry was left. The pot, being a pot, said nothing, and no man priced a repair and stood up in the yard to be asked.

\person{Garl Vail}'s sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one} --- improved in the keeping and very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said them. His name had not been spoken under the roof three hundred and thirteen days, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. No collector came up the clay road or went down it.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife at the very edge of its sill where it can be checked and where it can slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside the unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach did not go in, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with its small interval at the top of every reach, and his chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. The warm loft stone lay cold in its unmarked pot and was unreconciled by not trying, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} while the light was still in the yard, with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them; he would not have said it before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make something of it. Before anyone had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing. He read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies and no slate entered. He grew quieter as the saying about \lore{the burned slate} went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The fifth telling stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one; the two shut-room tellings stood at two. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name was neither given nor withdrawn.

The small keepings stayed small and were not brought under the roof. The three shapings of the Frost 14 well-saying stood beside Hessa's eighth hand with a finger between every pair and were not a sixth asking and were not a witness. The row stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness, and no sixth asking came on a day that had not earned one. The burned slate stood at five; the two shut-room tellings at two; the three pot sayings with the Smoke 14 note on its own place apart; the nine stoppings with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end. Ilva's strip stayed in her sleeve with no tenth hand and no figure coming off the wood. The seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape a shape, with no eighth of either, and the green at the pot board was not carried into the yard and joined to anything. The soft-cuffed clerk's inward slate stayed at \place{the out-of-line landing} and the reed-cutter's cord stayed inside his coat. None of these came under the roof as a sign, a lesson, a mending, a cause or a payment. The edges of them came near to touching the kept things of the house and the boy's own kept gaps, and the finger went between.

Under the roof behind them the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The carried fire's payer had been unnamed three hundred and thirteen days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its low note, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and put no number to itself. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Four and a gap in three different materials. Agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either; he kept the three counts on three places and let them lie there, and the floor of the room his bed stood on did not touch the floor the fire was under. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it; the told figure stood beside the paid with no signature on the space, and four readings were four and not one account, the finding kept on a place of its own with no payer under it and not laid across any of these.

The light went off the yard while he was being carried up. She came at the half-pace and set him under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face. The instruction had gone to the wool, the wind, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. One crust lay two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool, its empty place unnamed and no third to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was left where it lay, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond cheek, temple and the bare wrist-joint where the wool had ridden back, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and knew that such a patient must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. Both registers were true --- the surgeon taking the inventory in its old order, the infant wanting the crust lifted the two inches and the thread wiped --- and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument was the work.

In the small hours the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door, took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and let it go whole on the same low note. Then it laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. It was the two hundred and second answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. It was carried into neither the carried fire's missing fifth, nor the bell's gap left behind in the square, nor the river's low over lower; it stood up as none of the things it refused --- not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a gift, a doctrine or a rule --- and no payer was named to it.

The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number, the satisfaction of a man who has laid an instrument down and does not pretend it healed anybody. He had entered a great many exact things in a room with hard light and had never seen an exactness that paid a debt or warmed a body; he entered this one anyway, because it was the only honest work available to a hand that could not close two inches. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and the wanting was louder than the satisfaction and went on underneath it the way a coal goes on under ash. Both were true. Neither won. The argument was the work.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of \place{the bed} with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Two hundred and two honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and the ninth hand lay on its own place with a finger between it and the eighth. Three hundred and thirteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath; six months and one hundred and thirty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. Neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The strip went back into the sleeve under the flat of a hand with no figure coming off the wood, and the file lay open on the towel with a finger between the ninth hand and the eighth. The river went low over lower under the finger's-width door with no number put to it, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn. Nothing on the fourteenth of Bone was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended.
