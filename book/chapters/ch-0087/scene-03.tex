\daymark{313}{Bone 14, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

The dark under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof had a grain to it, the way a board has a grain, and he lay along that grain on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with his face turned into the fold and took the room the way a field is taken before the light, by the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. Three hundred and twelve had gone out of him in the small hours without a sound, the way water goes out of a vessel filled to its mark and left unwatched, and three hundred and thirteen came in to take its place, exact, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. Six months and one hundred and thirty-three days were told by nobody. Nobody under this roof kept days in numbers; the house kept them in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood, and a day was no more entered on a slate here than a breath is entered on a chart. He had carried the number so long that it had ceased to feel like a thing he had found and had come to feel like a thing handed to him to hold, which was not the same as being told what it counted. The wanting of that telling went on under the not-expecting, the way the fire went on under its ash.

Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood. Its payer had been unnamed three hundred and thirteen days, and the silence had kept its shape all that while without anybody lifting the floor to look for one. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken beneath this roof the same number of days, and the keeping of that silence was maintenance and not a story, a thing done each morning the way a hand is washed before it touches a wound, and done without a word about why. \person[Corin]{The boy} did not lay the two kept things together. The edges of them came near to touching in the dark and the finger went between.

He took the body in the old order, the way he had once taken a field in the old order, because the order was the only part of it that belonged to him. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool; in the dry heat of the room it had gone stiffer through the night, and its edge had the glossy toughness of a thing that had been wet once and had given up being wet without asking anybody's leave. Beside it lay the empty place, unnamed, and nothing had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen where it lay and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as though it had reached the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past all three, beyond a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease, that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that such a patient must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped. The knowing bought nothing at two inches of air. Both registers were true --- the surgeon taking the inventory in its order, the infant underneath wanting --- and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument between them was the work rather than a virtue anybody could have entered on a slate. He had once stood in a lit room and decided which bodies were worth warming; here he could not close two inches on the rim of the basket that held him, and the wanting was as exact as the knowing and as little use.

Not far off, on a place of its own, lay the finding that four arithmetics of the year now stand behind one printed figure. He kept it there and did not lay it across anything. It was not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a doctrine, not a rule; a doctrine is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood under this finding. The four true subtractions had gone up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box toward \place{High Kiln}, behind the three of Frost, and the figure printed at one in twelve stood unchallenged where it had been printed, while the figure read was still not the figure paid and no signature stood on the space between. Four readings were four and not one account. To make one account of them would have wanted a payer put under all four, and none stood.

The edges of the finding came near to other kept things and the finger went between: near the house's \lore{not-doings}, kept by not doing them and not confessions; near the dressed grain covered under wool at \place{the rack end}, turned each morning with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure, read over on \place{Bone} 10 by a number that did not know what it cost; near the four held changes on four places, the returned half-check of the eighth among them, four and not a rule; near the small gaps in his own file, each left gap. None of them touched. He took a clean satisfaction in not promoting the finding into a doctrine, the satisfaction of a man who has laid instruments out and does not pretend the list was a cure. That satisfaction belonged to the older man. Under it the infant wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and the wanting went on without words and without needing any.

\scenebreak

The first light came under the finger's-width door and went along the floor without anybody having said what the day would count. \person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she had gone to the fire before anything else every morning of this house's keeping, and the instruction went out into the room ahead of her hands: to the wool, to the river wind, to the pot, to the broom, to the running water, and never to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down and left. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. The twice-mended pot stood on \place{the pot board} with the wire drawn across the crack and the hairline of the original break showing dark beside the staples; she put her thumb where the wire crossed before she let the weight come on the handle, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then lifted. Both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person --- \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled} --- improved in the keeping and very likely not as good as the man had said them. The crack was left showing.

Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge a breath and came away with the charcoal tally underneath unread; her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither. She glanced once at the clay jar standing cold on the shelf by the wall under its skin of \lore{lead glaze}, and shut her hand half over nothing. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the one grey streak at her temple showed pale in the thin light. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at shelf, at hearth, at rack or at bed, because a morning and a hearth and a bed are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward.

Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat between the set-down and the going away of her hand and no look toward the bed. Two inches was the whole distance. He counted the half-beat because it was there to count and gave it no name and made no claim on it, and she put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread where it lay.

At the rack end the dressed grain lay covered under wool away from the hearth, turned that morning with a progressively lighter touch and given no figure. The number that had gone over it on Bone 10 did not know what it cost and was not brought back now as a lesson, a mending or a cause. She went around the covered fold as wide as she was carried around anything, and he kept the not-knowing where it lay.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife at the very edge of its sill where it can be checked and where it can slip. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh stood at the lip beyond it, where it could not; the unforged collector sentence lay beside it with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach did not go in, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with its small interval at the top of every reach, and his chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back, and he held the wrist up a moment to the grey as though to see whether the light had come to anything. The warm loft stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot in the loft, two years gone, and he had stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt, which was not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it, and the debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing from a kiln-master out of High Kiln who would have called it early and made something of it; the knowing stayed on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them.

Before anyone had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot. He read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies and no slate entered. He grew quieter as the saying about \lore{the burned slate} carried from Bone 5 went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The fifth telling stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one; the seed waited on the far-off calling-in of his promise, and the telling was not a confession and not a payment. The promise of the foundling's name was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

\scenebreak

The morning light went off the pot board and the room went on being a room with nothing opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended in it. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door, took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and let it go whole on the low note of \lore{water over water}. Then it laid the half-beat of the pot's set-down beside the small intake on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed and brought the finger down between them, and neither was spent. It was the two hundred and first answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. It stood up as none of the things it refused --- not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a gift, a doctrine or a rule --- and was carried into neither the carried fire's missing fifth, nor the bell's gap left behind in the square, nor the river's low over lower, which stayed each in its own place. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among with the seventh place empty; the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; the told figure stayed beside the paid with no signature on the space. None of them came together to make the half-beat mean anything it had not meant when the pot was set down.

The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number and in the finger brought down between the two small things, the satisfaction of a man who has laid instruments out on a towel and found the tray exact and is not fooled into thinking the list was a cure. He had entered a great many exact things in his first life and had never once seen an exactness that paid a debt or warmed a body; he entered this one anyway, because it was the only honest work available to a hand that could not close two inches. The infant underneath wanted the crust lifted and the thread wiped, and the wanting was louder than the satisfaction and went on underneath it the way a coal goes on under ash. Both were true. Neither won. The argument was the work.

The file lay open at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, and the half-beat and the small intake lay side by side with the finger withdrawn and neither spent. Three hundred and thirteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill or Grath; six months and one hundred and thirty-three days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. Two hundred and one honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The first light went off the pot board without a word having been said about \place{the Cross}, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn. Nothing on the fourteenth of Bone was opened, joined, named, called, paid or ended.
