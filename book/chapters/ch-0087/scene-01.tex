\daymark{312}{Bone 13, Thirdday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold the way a tool is laid down where the next hand will find it without looking, and under it the hundred and ninety-eighth answer kept its place and asked nothing of the dark. \person[Corin]{The boy} had set it there in the small hours and had thought about it no more than a man thinks about the last instrument on a tray once the list is done and the tray wiped; there was no reward in the keeping and no payer named to it, and he had stopped looking for one a long way back. Three hundred and eleven days stood behind him, exact, complete, unspent, spoken by nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}, and here under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the count went to three hundred and twelve. Six months and one hundred and thirty-two days were told by nobody. The house kept its days in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood, so the number lay in him alone, carried in the mouth the way a surgeon carries staples between the lips, always there, almost never spat out. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood; its payer had been unnamed for three hundred and twelve days, and nobody lifted the floor to find one. \person{Garl Vail}'s name had not been spoken beneath this roof the same number of days.

The small hours went as small hours go in a house that keeps its warmth by never speaking of it. The fire gave what it gave under the floor and said nothing of what it did not, and the dry heat of it came up through the boards and took the wet smell of the wool almost away but not quite. He lay on \place{the bed} at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with his face turned into the fold, and the room smelled of woodsmoke and tallow and, under them, the sour mineral breath of the Ash ridge along the skirting board that had not been swept in two years. A clay jar stood cold on the shelf by the wall under its skin of \lore{lead glaze}; the oiled paper at the window let the dark through in a grey square. Nobody came in the night to count him or to warm him, and he had long ago stopped expecting anybody to; the expectation had gone and the wanting under it had not, the way a coal goes on under ash and gives no light about it.

Bone came on without a boundary drawn. \place{Bone} was the month the household was living and the older women called it by the weather and the slatemen by the number of Account-days, and neither habit was set right or left wrong in so many words; the two names stood side by side on no slate anybody showed, with a finger's width between them and no third name made to agree them. Thirdday came on an ordinary Thirdday, with no market in it, no Cross and no bell. Bone 20 (Day 319), the month's second reading, stood five mornings ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it; a morning that has not earned a reading does not get one early, any more than a pot gets taken off the wheel before it has done turning. The first light came under the finger's-width door and went along the floor and up the leg of the bed the way water goes, quiet, without asking leave, and put a thin edge on everything it touched. He tracked the edge of it with his eyes as it crossed the flagstones of the room that were not the flagstones of the yard. It reached the pot board and stopped there, and the day had begun without anybody saying what it would count.

The body was taken in its old order, the way a wound is taken in the order its parts arrive, because the order was the only honesty available. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone harder in the dry heat since the day before, and its corner stood a hair off the wool where his cheek had pressed it and come away; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third had come to make the row come out even. A row of one is a row nobody has to answer for. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and he could not close two inches on anything; the wanting arrived before the knowing, as it always did, and the knowing came in after it and took the inventory and entered it nowhere, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen where it lay and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go further nor an order to go away. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, the ease that cold puts on a face being no ease at all; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and that such a patient must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. The knowledge was exact and it bought nothing at two inches of air. Both were true --- the surgeon taking the inventory in its old order, the infant wanting the crust lifted and the thread wiped --- and the second was louder, and neither won, and the argument between them was the work and not a virtue.

\scenebreak

Under the floor the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. Its payer had been unnamed for three hundred and twelve days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and put no number to itself. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Four and a gap in three different materials. Agreeing any two of them would have wanted a payer put under both, and none stood to either; the boy kept the three counts on three places and let them lie there without agreeing, and the floor of the room his bed stood on did not touch the floor the fire was under.

He checked the small sounds first because the small sounds do not lie. The fire's fourth pull came and went and did not become a fifth; the river went low over lower and did not become a hearing; a nail held a board and the knot held nothing and neither became a row. There was a satisfaction in the keeping of that, dry and exact and worth nothing, the satisfaction of a man who has laid an instrument down without pretending it had healed anybody. He took it and set it on its own place and asked nothing of it to mean.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket, as she went to it before every basket under that roof. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}; the instruction went to the wool, the wind, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure. Her finger lay on top of \place{the loaf shelf}'s front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither of them, and she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and shut her hand half over nothing. The burn shaped like a little door on the back of her left hand shut on the air and shut on nothing else, and the boy kept the two shuttings on separate places and made nothing between them.

At \place{the hearth} the twice-mended pot stood on \place{the pot board} with the crack showing between the staples, and she put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack and felt what the thumb had to feel before she let the weight come on the handle. \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the pot; \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the fire. Both sayings went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and the sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good a sentence as the man had said them. She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward the bed. The crack stayed showing. A repair that holds mends nothing anybody will name, and the house kept its shape by that and by other not-doings it did not enter.

She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at a morning or a hearth, because neither was an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule; the next stood far off in a later month's first firing, not hoped for and not counted toward.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} had the small knife at the very edge of its sill, where it could be checked and where it could slip. Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh was at the lip beyond it where it could not, and beside it lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist, in place since Y.H. 806, held what little light there was and gave it back, and he held the wrist up a moment to the grey as though to see whether the light had come to anything. His chest had been tight since the dark and the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach had stayed unspent, a small empty place at the top of every reach where the breath should have gone in.

\emph{Six counted. The next I don't count}, he told the wood, in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked and announced it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. He said \emph{Yes, house} to one who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. In the loft above the shed the warm stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot, and he did not reconcile it with the debt and did not try, which is not forgiveness. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up \place{the clay road} or went down it. The boy kept that stopping on its own place as an object and not a payment, and the debt circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and would not have said it before a kiln-master from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and make something of it, singing before its time and singing itself out early.

Before anyone had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot. He read one piece of iron with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that takes the hand off a man, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies and no slate entered. Men unnamed had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. He grew quieter as the saying about \lore{the burned slate} went around, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. The fifth telling stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one; the shut-room tellings stood at two, the pot sayings at three with the Smoke 14 note on its own place apart, and the row at five askings with one shaping and no witness. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name was neither given nor withdrawn. The claim went on gathering interest against that promise while he neither paid nor refused it, and the boy put the gathering on a place of its own with no payer under it.

\scenebreak

In the space between the going of the light and the full of the morning the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the river's low note, took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, and let it go whole on the same note --- a restraint and not a payment, borrowing no house count and supplying no payer, carried into neither the carried fire's missing fifth nor the river's going low over lower. It was the hundred and ninety-ninth answer, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, and it stood up as none of the things it refused: not a reach, a hearing, a seventh, a stopping, a payment, a held change, a gift, a doctrine or a rule. No payer was named to it. The hand came down on the river's note and laid itself back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking.

The older man took a clean satisfaction in the keeping of the number; the infant underneath wanted the crust lifted the two inches and the thread wiped from his lip, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument was the work. He had once read such a face under hard light in a room that did not smell of woodsmoke and gone past the face to the note and signed past the note himself, and the knowledge had never once since made a warm place for anybody, and he did not ask it to now.

The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap, every tray parted by a finger's width and nothing that had moved. Three hundred and twelve stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill or Grath; six months and one hundred and thirty-two days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed; Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. One hundred and ninety-nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, and neither the number nor the small hand-movings that made it stood up and called themselves a doctrine, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood. The morning light went along the pot board without a word having been said about what the day would count, and Bone went on without a boundary drawn.
