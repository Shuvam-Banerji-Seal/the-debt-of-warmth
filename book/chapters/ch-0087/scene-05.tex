\daymark{314}{Bone 15, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours came on under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the way water comes under a door, without an instruction and without a hand to say where one thing ended and the next began. \place{Bone} 15 arrived in the dark and went on arriving into the grey, and \person[Corin]{the boy} lay in the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and one crust two inches from it, and nothing in the room had been set right for the new day and nothing had been set wrong. There was no market on a Fifthday, no Cross and no bell, and the household's arithmetic went on in loaves and glances and the space where a loaf should have stood, which counted the days without ever saying what a day counted. Three hundred and thirteen had gone into three hundred and fourteen in the dark the way a pot goes from one hand to another, level, with nothing slopped, and nobody in \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} was the wiser for the passing.

Six months and one hundred and thirty-four days were told by nobody under a roof that kept its days in bread and weather. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and the fifth had gone unpaid three hundred and fourteen days with its payer without a name, and nobody lifted the floor and nobody supplied the pull. The name of the man who had once kept that fire and would not let anyone mend him had not been spoken under the roof the same number of days, and the keeping of him had gone on all that while as maintenance and not as a story. \person{Garl Vail} was a coat on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder, a wheel-seat with its twenty-year lean and hollow that nobody was allowed to fill, a clean dark line with a finer grey hairline beside it called \lore{the thaw}, unswept both, a proverb gone to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and a debt against a firing of tiles that circled without a name and was worse for being uncalled. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} or gone down it, and the debt lay where it lay.

He took the body in the old order, because the old order was the only thing that had never failed him and because there was nothing else to take it in. The crust had gone stiffer in the dry heat, leathery and brown-edged, two inches from his ear with its corner standing a hair off the wool, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool had ridden back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to go away, and \lore{the Afterdrop} waited beyond all three past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, knew that the ease cold puts on a face is not ease, knew that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse and must be warmed from the outside and slowly, never hurried, never rubbed, never set too near the fire. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth and the thread wiped, and the surgeon's knowing bought nothing at two inches of air; he who had reduced a thousand shoulders in a room with hard light could not close two inches on the handle of the pot set beyond his reach. Both were true and the second was louder and neither won, and the argument between the two registers was the work, as it had been every morning since the first.

The house kept by its \lore{not-doings} and he kept his finger between what it omitted and what he omitted, and neither keeping was laid across the other. The coals were turned and the crack left showing and the mark not touched and the thread not wiped, and these were acts done so often that the household had stopped calling them acts at all. Under the floor four; through the finger's-width door the river going \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming; overhead \place{the boards} keeping four nails on the near board, four on the next and a thumb-width knot where a fifth nail might have been driven and was not. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would have wanted a payer put under both and no payer stood to either. The bell's gap left behind in the square was not fetched in to make a fourth, and the heard bell stayed in its square beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and none invented to fill it.

Four arithmetics of the year stood behind one printed figure, and the finding sat on a place of its own with no payer under it. The printed figure was one in twelve and unchallenged, the figure read was still not the figure paid, and no signature stood on the space between; four readings were four and not one account, and the four true subtractions rode locked up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s box toward \place{High Kiln}, unread under this roof. He kept the finding where it was and did not lay it across the house's not-doings, or across the dressed grain lying covered under wool at \place{the rack end} under a number that did not know what it cost, or across the four held changes on four places with the returned half-check of the eighth standing on \place{the flagstones} beside the Day 161 damper fraction, the Day 166 shelf beat and the Frost 5 returned beat. The edges of these came near to touching, and the finger went between, because a finding with no payer can be carried light and a doctrine is only an account that has found a payer, and none stood to this one. Not a lesson, not a grievance, not a mending, not a rule; an object on a place, and the place left open.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} went to \place{the hearth} before she went to the basket, as she had gone on the fourteen mornings behind this one and would go on the mornings after. She told the coals it was the thaw and turned them with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and gave the instruction to the wool, the wind, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down, and a pot set down is left. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting something under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting-go --- and gave neither gap a figure, and pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale.

On \place{the pot board} the pot with the handle mended twice stood with its repair showing: the dark hairline of the original crack under the old-brass staple, the tighter wire crossing it, the whole thing left visible as a wound is left visible when a man has no other honest way to close it. She put her thumb first where the wire crossed the crack, felt what the thumb had to feel, and only then let the weight come on the handle. \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}, she said to the pot, and \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}, she said to the fire, and the sayings had improved in the keeping and were very likely not as good as the man had said them. The proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person, and nobody made a lesson of the repair, and the crack stood showing.

At the loaf shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally underneath stayed unread, and moved her lips along a row standing with two gaps and figured neither gap, and glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face, and left the thread where it lay. The half-beat was the distance between a hand arrested and a hand arriving, and she did not spend it and he did not name it, and the two inches between the rim and his fingers stayed what they had always been.

\lore{the widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and she did not touch it at shelf, hearth, pot board or bed, because a morning and a hearth and a bed are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Five openings in five months were five and not a rule, and the next stood in a later month's first firing, far off, not hoped for and not counted toward. The household had learned to straighten around this the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted, and the boy had learned to keep the keeping on a place of its own and not make a doctrine of it, for a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and none stood.

\scenebreak

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} with the small knife at the very edge of its sill where it can be checked and where it can slip, six years standing in the worn thumb-mark where the house can check and the seventh at the lip beyond it where it cannot, and Garl's unforged collector sentence lying beside the seventh with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather and put the knife back where it could be checked, announcing it to nobody because the yard had already been told. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, with no figure and no name, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach did not go in, and the Seed 16 breath stayed unspent with its small interval at the top of every reach, and his chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what little light there was and gave it back. The warm loft stone she had given him in the winter Garl died lay cold in its unmarked pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, which was a sound with a direction to him now and not a room he entered, and he did not reconcile the stone with the debt, and not trying was not forgiveness. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the ones who wanted the thaw early and the ones who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. The loan stayed uncalled and no collector came up the clay road or went down it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and kept the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, and would not have said it before a kiln-master from High Kiln who would call it early and make something of it. Before anyone had finished telling him the fire was low his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper}, took the cold out of the fingertip, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around the quiet without an order. He read one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, and there was no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies and no slate entered. He grew quieter as the saying about \lore{the burned slate} went around, and the fifth telling stood at five with a finger between the fifth and the four, no witness standing, no hand named at any flame, no sixth due on a day that had not earned one, no payer. The two shut-room tellings stood at two, and the promise of a name to the men who burn slates waited on the far-off calling-in and was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years.

\scenebreak

At the end of the morning, with the light gone flat on the pot board and the river going under the finger's-width door, the hand outside the wool came up an inch toward the note. It took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his and let it go whole on the same low note, the way it had let small intakes go whole on the mornings behind it. Then, for the first time, it did not come straight down to the fold. It stayed at the inch for the space of the note's fading, held and not reaching, while the two inches to the crust remained what they had always been, unclosed.

The surgeon read it the way he had once read a clamp left a moment after the bleeding had stopped, or a tourniquet not yet released, and he knew what both meant and what both cost. The infant underneath read it as a hand that had not given up wanting the crust, and that reading had no words in it and did not need any. Both were true and neither won, and for the first time the argument between them had a body and a place in the air, a fraction of an inch where the two registers stood and looked at each other without agreeing. The staying was not progress and he refused to call it progress, and the wanting was not answered and went on wanting, and the arc between them stayed exactly as wide as it had been.

He filed it as the two hundred and third answer: a letting-go followed by a staying at the inch, a new object laid on a place of its own on the sixth place with a finger between it and the two hundred and second. It closed none of the two inches. It was not a reach, not a gift, not a power, not a seventh hearing, not a tenth stopping, not a fifth held change, and no payer stood under it. It was not laid across the house's four held changes on four places, which stayed four, and it was not laid across the covered grain or the four arithmetics in the locked box or his own kept gaps, and he made no fifth likeness of it and no rule. One staying was a keeping; two would be a habit, and a habit would want a payer, and none stood. At the going of the light it would not be made again, and the file would hold one object where it had held none before and every gap would still be a gap.

The river's note went quiet under the door while the hand stayed where it was, above the fold and short of the crust, and nobody under the roof had seen it and nobody had given an instruction about it. Three hundred and fourteen stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Low Kiln, Sill and Grath, and six months and one hundred and thirty-four days were told by nobody, and the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed, and Garl's name had not been spoken under the roof the same number. The file lay open on \place{the towel} at the head of the bed with every gap left gap and the new object on its own place, and the hand stayed at the inch while the morning went off the pot board without anybody having said what the day would count.
