\daymark{190}{Ember 11, Y.H. 813 (Firstday)}

The account had gone up \place{the clay road} in \person{Berr Clay}'s locked box in the dark, and there was no hand under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof that could reach into it and take the true subtraction back out. The printed figure stood where the notice had put it --- one in twelve --- and nobody in \place{Low Kiln} had leaned in to challenge it on the first Account-day of Ember. \lore{The levy} had been called and denied in one breath, the calling and the denial going off up the road together with the man who made them, and the district's arithmetic had changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back by any hour of this morning. A figure said over a basket is not a figure entered in a slate, and a figure entered in a slate is not a figure paid. Nothing in this house had been paid. The boy came into his hundred and ninetieth morning one hundred and ninety days old and six months and ten days, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, in all of \place{Sarn}, said so.

Heat came into the room before the grey did, as it had come every morning of his life: hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and below both the sour mineral breath of the corner no broom in Low Kiln had been given an instruction about in two years. He lay on \place{the bed} with \place{the hearth} at his back and his face turned a little to the boards. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and missed the fifth, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety days; through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places that did not become one. He had held them apart every morning of his life with hands that could not lift a spoon, and he held them apart this morning too, because a row of such counts will stand up and call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer.

The body had its own account and kept it badly. The one stiff brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third; the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and no instruction had been given about the thread, so it stayed; cold had come in its known order to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note and came down against the fold. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, and wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water, and gave none to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would say \emph{I}, and kept her half-pace as she crossed to the bench, the way a woman keeps a pace from a thing that might spill. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. She fed him barley gruel from the house's own dish with two fingers behind his ear, tipped the spoon and wiped it on her sleeve, and did not wipe his mouth; the mouth was a basket set down and left, and nobody had been given an instruction about a mouth. She set the pot rim out on \place{the loaf shelf} with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed. The little-door burn shut on nothing when her hand had nothing to hold. Coal-black hair went back with the burn-wrist and the grey streak showed pale off the coals. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat and she did not touch it, because a morning and a shelf are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

A woman come up from the racks with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the month would cost now the reading had gone up. Sella glanced once at the clay jar and sent her to the pot.

“Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it.”

The woman carried the question away level, so nothing slopped. No figure was priced, refused or entered; the cost went to the pot and the weather as it had gone every morning of the kiln month, and arrived nowhere. Money, work and weather stayed in arms, loaves and glances in this house, and never in a number a clerk could take off a slate. The boy lay at the low angle where the underside of the shelf becomes a roof and heard a cost asked as a direction and answered with a direction, and filed nothing, because there was nothing to file.

Before first grey three or four men came up the yard with their collars high and went low-voiced into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off the shed, and neither Sella nor the boy gave them a name and nobody said the room was cold. The two tellings of a counting room shut in the war stayed two, one from a man at a well and one from a man going up the road with a narrow slate turned to his sleeve, and no third telling came to stand beside them. The door kept its finger's width of open. The household straightened around the men the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted and set down again elsewhere.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The dust was parted from the slip as a surgeon parts a clot from the vessel that holds it --- one the material of a room, one the material of the work --- and the boy, carried past the edge of the door, took the two apart without a word spoken over either. The broken birth-slates lay beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} and were entered by nobody. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, flat and low, the way a man says a thing to an animal he means to work with, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it, and kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. A man had come up on a wrong wind with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner, and Jorra withheld the knowing from him; no pot was lost in the yard, and no saying of a pot singing before its time was made true as a lesson so soon after the carrying-out of the day before.

When Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and held there until the sentence reached the pot, and then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The boy read the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, and filed it as one object worth nothing joined to no kiln. The holding was remembered, not made again; it lay on its own place and was not paired with the beat under the loaf shelf or the half-check on the stones, and three likenesses standing in a row would call themselves a rule if he left them standing, and a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to these.

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill where a man could put a thumb and find it where it should be. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood what he told it at every going and coming of the light. “Six counted. The next I don't count.” Six years stood in the worn thumb-mark, in the place where the house could check and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back.

He said “Yes, house” to a neighbour who wanted \lore{the thaw} early and to another who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the stopped place and were not given it again; the change made on an older morning stayed where it was laid, held and not repeated. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the road and no collector went down it. The loan that can hear stayed uncalled.

The ear-sentence came to his teeth and stood there with no figure and no name. It went to the word that would call the loan and stopped at the threshold, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, with the small interval from Seed sixteen showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He did not say the sentence, did not call the loan and did not stand a tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. The ninth was the last, and the tenth had not stood up. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He said “Yes, house” to the fire and went on with the broom.

In \place{the Debt Loft} the warm stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot, cold and unreconciled by not trying, which is not forgiving; a man had taken it out on an afternoon two days gone, held it, set it on the sill in the light, looked at it a long while and put it back, and the debt had not moved by the width of a hair. From below, the boy heard only the small dry scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone from a room he could not enter, and filed it on a place of its own --- not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping and not a payment --- and kept it a remembering, not a holding made again. Beneath the front edge of the loaf shelf the charcoal tally waited unread, uncounted and uncopied, and Sella's finger did not go beneath it this morning, so the held beat stayed where it was left. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line.

The account of Ember had gone away up the road and the house had closed over the space it left as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. Nothing had changed hands at home either: the same six hearings of the full note beside and never among, the same three near-full answers advanced and unpaid, the same nine stoppings, the same cold stone in its pot, one more day exact, complete and unspent. The boy lay at the low angle with the crust two inches from his ear and the empty place beside it unnamed, and wanted a number nobody under any roof would speak, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold.

At the edge of the sill the small knife stood where no thumb of the house had gone, six counted in the worn mark where a man could check them and the seventh at the lip where he could not, and the seventh year stood there with it, nearer its end than it had ever stood and not given. At the top of Tarn's next reach the Seed sixteen breath stood unspent, with the small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not.
