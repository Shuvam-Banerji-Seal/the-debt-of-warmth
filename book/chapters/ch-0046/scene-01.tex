\daymark{189}{Ember 10, Y.H. 813 (Tenthday)}

\dropcap{H}{eat came} into \place{the Vail kiln-house} before the grey did, as it had come every morning of the boy's life, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that and, below both, the sour mineral breath of the corner no broom in \place{Low Kiln} had been given an instruction about in two years. He lay on \place{the bed} with \place{the hearth} at his back and his face turned a little to the boards, one hundred and eighty-nine days old and six months and nine days, and nobody under any roof in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, in all of \place{Sarn}, said so. The first sound was the kiln drawing breath in fours with a gap. It was not a bell. No bell had been rung under this roof in a hundred and eighty-nine days, and one would be rung today in the square, later, when the light had come and the pigs were over the board; that was still ahead of the morning and the boy did not borrow it. Ember 10 was an Account-day and a market-day, the first of the kiln month, and the district went on calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month without a word spent between the two names.

Through the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, and its payer had no name and had had none for a hundred and eighty-nine days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the six hearings of \lore{the full note} and never made to stand up as a seventh, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none rose up and became the others.

The surgeon's register laid them out the way it laid out everything that could not be touched: three towels, three instruments, nothing resting across anything else. A man who had once read a trauma bay in the ninety seconds before he chose which body to work on now read a ceiling from a basket, and read it correctly, and could do nothing with the reading. Four and a gap under the floor, five low over water through the door, four and a knot above his head. A likeness in a row will stand up and call itself a rule if a man leaves it standing, and a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of these. He had spent a hundred and eighty-nine days taking such likenesses down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see, and the work had made him no friends and bought him nothing he could reach. The told bell of the Long Night lay on a shelf of its own among these, four, four and a gap spoken in the dark, kept apart from any bell the morning might ring; the two would stand side by side before the day was out and neither would be asked to account for the other. It was a keeping like the others, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.

The crust lay two inches from his ear, one stiff brown-edged barley cake gone leathery in the dry heat, with its empty place beside it unnamed and no third. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and nobody had wiped it, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far, and nobody had been given an instruction about that sound either. He knew the sound. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark and counted the seconds between a breath and its consequence; now the consequence was his own ear going damp against the fold and he could not lift a hand to his own chin to stop it. The adult and the infant quarrelled in the dark, and the infant was louder. He could not roll. He could not reach the crust two inches off. He could not close two inches on the pot handle standing where it stood. He had put a thousand shoulders back where they belonged and could not keep a blanket on himself, which was a joke with a blade in it that cost him nothing and was not funny.

Cold had come in its known order to cheek, temple, the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the handle standing where it stood, wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them, and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

\person{Sella Vail} came in with the basket against her hip and gave the instruction to the wool before she gave it to anything with ears. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind,” she said; and the fire got its own instruction, and the pot got its own, and the broom leaning by the door got its, and the running water at \place[the yard well]{the well} got its, and the basket got none. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and kept her half-pace from every open thing, and did not once look into the fold. Her eye went once to the clay jar standing cold under its glaze and her hand shut half over nothing and came away without touching it. She pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and set the pot rim out on \place{the loaf shelf} with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. It would have said, if a man could ask it, that the weight in the basket was the same on this first Account-day as on the first morning of his life, which was true, and that the cost was not the same, which was also true and entered nowhere. Her hands were never still; when they had nothing to do they counted spoons, nails, the breaths of a child who could not speak for himself, and gave nobody a figure.

At the hearth she told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it,” and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. She did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the first morning of Ember, held one breath, let go, and at no shelf, well, rack, road, basket, afternoon or dusk since, because a morning and a market and a reading are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. Nobody had praised the keeping and nobody had seen what it mended, and the fire still answered four pulls with the fifth missing. People noticed the burn first and the mark last, which was the order she preferred. She kept a roll of the house in a careful hand and showed it to no man come down from Slate Row, and what went into it and what stayed out was the same thing to the basket: a door without a handle on this side.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} before the light with the small knife back at the edge of its sill, six years in the worn thumb-mark where the house could put a finger and verify them and the seventh at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, \person[Garl Vail]{Garl}'s unforged collector sentence lying beside it and entered in neither. He took the broom only after it had been instructed; the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to his teeth and stood there with no figure and no name, and he drew the breath he should have spent and did not spend it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. At the finger's-width door of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, and gave the wheel its instruction before he gave anything to a person, and did not look toward the basket. The two rooms under the roof went on not touching, and the boy in the fold heard the household going about its ordinary morning without being told what any of it cost.

\scenebreak

She lifted him with the basket against her hip and gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” Woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the morning as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-nine mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and gave no account of cost; it would have said the weight was the same and the cost was not, which were both true and neither entered.

At the edge of \place{the Clay Yard} she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim; around \place{the trough} at the well, bare and dark, whose water was called weather; wide beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The clean dark line where Ash had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called the thaw beside it lay unswept in the corner, neither with an instruction, and the household went around both. The yard kept its arithmetic in arms and loaves and glances: a woman with slip dried pale in the creases of her wrists lifted a pot and set it down, a man counted what he carried with his hands and not his mouth, and nothing in the walk needed a figure to be done correctly.

Down \place{the clay road} the market was assembling before the reading: two hundred people leaning toward \place{the reading block}, eight pigs going over the wet boards, a Court table set at the fringe of \place{Slate Row} spoken of as a direction and not as a place anybody meant to go that morning. The square smelled of wet boards and pig and turnip tops and woodsmoke flattened into the low air, and under all of it came the same mineral sourness the house kept in its corner, carried down in the wool. People brought figures to a heap of turnips and left them standing side by side without making them agree. Slates lay open on barrels and knees and the tops of walls, and she kept her half-pace from every one of them, so that the basket went through the square at the edge of the light where no figure could fall into it and none could be taken off it. The boy read the crowd the way he had once read a room he was not allowed into: by the exits, by which hands were full and which were empty, by the weight people carried and where they put it down. Two hundred bodies leaning the same way made one shape, and the shape had a hole in it where the answer would stand, and nobody in the square was looking into the basket to see whether the basket had an answer of its own. There would be a figure said twice before the afternoon, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and the figure would go into a slate that knew nothing about the small humiliating warmth the boy had been carried out of. A reading is a thing said twice over a body that cannot answer; he knew the shape of that before the first stroke of the bell, and knowing it bought nothing.

The square was cold in a way the bed had not been cold. The bed had held a warmth he could not reach and did not want to leave; the square offered nothing and asked for attention. He gave it. The infant wanted the breast and the crust. The surgeon wanted the number. The two wants lay one over the other in the fold and did not become one want, because a likeness stood beside a likeness will call itself a truth if no hand holds it apart, and he had learned to hold them apart with hands that could not lift a spoon. Across \place{the pot board} a cup stood turned a quarter and set back where it had stood, and he called the colour at a mouth corner green and nothing else, and gave the shape no name and no ear. The bell would be kept where such bells are kept, beside the told bell of the Long Night, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and neither hearing would be made to answer for the other; that keeping belonged to later in the morning and he did not spend it before it came.

She had set him at the edge of every open slate for a hundred and eighty-nine mornings and had never once set him in one, and this morning was like the others in that respect if in none other: the reading would go over his head and not into him, and the house would carry him home and put him on his bed and say nothing about what had been said. She set the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade where no slate lay, with the cold iron of the post near his cheek, and stood half a pace off with her burn shut on nothing. She gave the wool its instruction and the river wind its instruction and gave the basket none and no reason for the setting. The shade was thin and did not hold; it was a place to set a pot and not a place to keep anything, and she had set him there as she set every vessel down, base first, where the light would not fall wrong on it. The bell went over the square, longer than a church bell, and the first stroke came down into the boards and the pigs and the open slates. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. One hundred and eighty-nine stood exact, complete and unspent, spoken by nobody under any roof, and the fire's fifth was still missing under the floor of a house up the clay road with its payer unnamed, and the river kept its fifth and the boards kept their knot, three counts in three places that did not become one. The hands of the square went on reaching for their slates, and the count went forward into the noise.
