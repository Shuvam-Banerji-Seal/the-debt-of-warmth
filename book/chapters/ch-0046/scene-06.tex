The afternoon thinned without ending. It lost its edges the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun all day, the heat going first out of the high air and staying longest in the low places, under the shelf, behind the pot, in the fold of wool where the boy lay. Hot clay came up under tallow and wet wool came under that and, below both, the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that no broom in \place{Low Kiln} had been told to take. He lay at the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} made a roof that did not reach the corner, and the afternoon came down that roof toward him in a slow slant and stopped at the edge of his reach as if it, too, had been given an order and had come to the end of it.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket. She moved her lips along a row that stood with two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six, and she gave nobody a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. Her finger did not go beneath the front edge of the shelf where the charcoal tally lay, and so the tally stayed unread, uncounted, uncopied, and the beat that had stayed under the board on Day 166 stayed where it had been left --- an afternoon and a woman's back turned not the same as that night, and neither of them an opening. He had learned to read the space a hand did not go into as carefully as the space it did, and had spent a hundred and ninety-one days taking such likenesses down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw} and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn so the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because a shelf and an afternoon are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay back at the very edge of its sill, where a man could put a thumb and find it where it should be. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark, in the place where the house could check and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. The boy read the knife the way another man might read an instrument laid out for a case he will not be suffered to assist at: the count that can be checked and the count that cannot, held apart on one board and never stood together to call themselves a whole. A whole is a thing that wants a payer under it, and no payer stood under the seventh at the lip.

\person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. The ear-sentence came to his teeth and stood there with no figure and no name. It went all the way to the word that would call \lore{the loan} and stopped at the threshold, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, with the small interval from Seed sixteen showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He did not say the sentence, did not call the loan and did not stand a tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any of them, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found its payer and no payer stands to the nine. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire and went on with the broom.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped and were not given it again and joined it to nothing. The knife lay at the edge of its sill with the seventh year standing at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, and the boy went on holding the small account in the dark of the wool.

\scenebreak

The stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. No hand under the roof had lifted it since the afternoon it came out, was set on the sill in the light, looked at a long while and put back, and the boy had no room in his account for a room he could not enter. From below he kept only the remembering of a small dry scrape of a pot's foot on stone, filed on a place of its own: not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not laid across the three held changes and not stood up with them and the loft-sill scrape into a rule. A fourth likeness standing up would call itself a cause, and a cause would want a payer under it, and no payer stood under any of them. He let the remembering lie where it lay rather than take it up and make it hold a thing it could not carry.

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}. The woodsmoke went before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind came up behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The instruction went to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The arm that had carried him a hundred and ninety-one mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and not cost; it would have said, if a man could ask it, that the weight was the same on the first morning and on this one, which was true, and that the cost was not the same, which was also true and entered nowhere.

At the edge of the yard she went wide around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, whose water is called weather; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed fifteen and did not come out; and around the place the two carriers had crossed with the glaze-room hand between them. Each turn had a look and a wide step and no number, and she gave none of them to the basket and no explanation of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. A cost asked as a direction went to the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere, and the carrying-out of a man breathing went on being entered in no slate.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face, left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and has never told anybody, and would not call kindness now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure. She told the fire it was the thaw, banked the coals with the back of the broken pot and did not touch the mark, because a dusk is not an opening and there had been no opening since before the kiln on Ember 1.

Then she went to her own bed. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name. Nothing spoken in the one arrived in the other.

\scenebreak

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country, older than the house and older than the woman asleep in the room that does not touch this one. He has no towel and no instruments and no case, and lays them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} lie beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person{Dessa Silt} as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in a hundred and ninety-one days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged, and a forged finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed.

Three near-full answers stand on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on a wrong wind who said a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. The older intervals lie at Stone 13, Stone 17 and Stone 25, with the half-beat behind a standing finger, the unchecking stroke that went on over the bright patch, and his own held breath from a market morning. The nine stoppings stand where they have always stood --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. Beside them lies \lore{the damper}, one piece of iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate.

Three held changes stand on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161, the hand that waited at the iron for a sentence to finish; the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166; and the movement in the yard when a bent finger stood in the cold iron and a broom checked half a beat behind it and went on without a word or a turn of the head. Three held changes, not a pair and not a doctrine, no word spoken over any and no payer named. A fourth place holds the loft scrape. The two shut-room tellings stand side by side --- one from a man at a well, one from a man going up the road with a narrow slate turned to his sleeve --- with no third come to stand beside them. The told bell lies beside the heard, four, four and a gap, with nobody crossing himself, and the told figure lies beside the paid figure, and neither pair was made one.

On a fifth place lies the pale line at the gum, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces. The man had been read as a man with lead in him: the wet white line where the colour should have been, the hand held curled and then held limp by another man, the fingers gone the colour of old rope and the nails pewter-pale, the breath in short and out shorter with a small pause at the top where something hurt, the wrist that set the dipper down with care because the fingers had stopped telling the man where it was. Each thing lay in its own hollow of the register, and the whole of it was worth nothing without a hand to carry it. It is not a hearing, not a seventh, not a near-full answer, not a stopping, not one of the three held changes and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to this. It is not joined to the sweet of \lore{lead glaze} on the wrong wind, the dust ground small as meal, the pot that sang itself out early, the sixth green, the foundling's sliver, the pan's gone ring or any absence in the yard. The man went out of the yard breathing and went on breathing up the clay road; a breath kept is not an ending.

The sixth green is a colour joined to no ear and the foundling a shape with no sale, both held and not advanced. The sliver at his throat went dull when he turned and caught the light when he turned back, and his head went toward a set-down pot half a beat after the boy's, after the knuckle and after the note, and then stopped in the middle of the turn. No loaf had passed, no slate had been seen near the basket and no sale had been made. The shelf's two gaps, the fold's crust with its empty place, the pan's gone round ring and the field's empty place in the returning line lay on trays that do not touch. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line; three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and ninety-one days taking them down one at a time.

He wants the crust two inches from his ear, wants the breast, wants the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand, wants a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and gets none of them. He has stopped expecting to be told, which is not the same as not wanting it. Through the finger's-width door the river keeps its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh; through the floor \lore{the carried fire} gives four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for a hundred and ninety-one days; overhead the boards keep four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places, and none of them rises up and becomes the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under them, and no payer stands to any of them. Ash lies thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line and the new finer grey called the thaw lies beside it, neither swept and neither with an instruction. Cold has stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rides back, and the afterdrop waits on the far side of a warming nobody in this house will perform.

One hundred and ninety-one stands exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody under any roof. Nothing has been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended. The nine stoppings are still nine with no tenth, the Seed sixteen breath is still held, the charcoal still waits under the shelf for a hand that is not hers and a day that has not come, the mark was touched once before the kiln and at no other hour, and the cost of the man carried out stands entered nowhere. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and comes down against the fold, and the count goes forward into Day 192, Ember 13.
