\daymark{191}{Ember 12, Y.H. 813 (Secondday)}

The kiln month's second working week opened without anybody drawing a boundary under the first. One morning stood edge to edge against the next the way two bricks stand in a wall with no mortar shown between them, and the household went on as if a week were a thing that happened to the weather and not to a slate. The district kept calling Tallow the firing month and Ember the kiln month and spent no word reconciling the two names, and neither was corrected from the basket. The account had gone up \place{the clay road} the day before in a locked box, and there was no hand under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof that could reach into it and take the true subtraction back out; the printed figure stood at one in twelve where the notice had put it, and a figure said over a basket was still not the figure paid. The boy came into his hundred and ninety-first morning one hundred and ninety-one days old and six months and eleven days, and nobody under any roof in \place{Low Kiln}, in \place{Sill}, in \place{Grath}, in all of \place{Sarn}, said so.

Hot clay came into the room ahead of the light, as it had come every morning of his life: hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that and, below both, the sour mineral breath of the corner that no broom in Low Kiln had been given an instruction about in two years. He lay on \place{the bed} with \place{the hearth} at his back, and the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} made a roof over him that did not reach the corner. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and missed the fifth, and its payer had had no name for a hundred and ninety-one days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings and never invented to stand up as a seventh. Overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places. None of them rose up and became the others, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under them, and no payer stood to any of them.

The body went on with its small humiliations, exact and unashamed. The one stiff brown-edged crust lay leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third, and the thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the small hours and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own order and had no further instruction to work from. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note and came down against the fold. The afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He knew the afterdrop the way he had once known the back of his own hand: the cold that goes on deepening at the very moment a man is being warmed, the core still giving its heat to the shell, the shallow vessels opening and taking the warmth away from the places that needed it. A body could be handed from the dark into a warm room and go on getting colder in the hands of people who were being kind to it. He had watched that happen to others and had never found a way to make it clean. He wanted the breast. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder, and there was no third thing in reach that wanted anything at all.

\person{Sella Vail} gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom and the running water, and never to the basket. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and kept her half-pace as though the floor were a thing that must not be hurried across. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. Coal-black hair went back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. She told the fire it was \lore{the thaw}, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing. She did not touch it. It had been touched once, before the kiln on the morning of Ember 1, held one breath, let go, and the clay lid lifted and the carried coal tipped under the back of a broken pot; nobody had praised the opening and nobody had seen what it mended. There had been no opening since. A morning and a shelf are not openings, and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She set the pot rim out on the board with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward the bed.

\scenebreak

She carried him down to \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace from every open slate, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. The yard went around its unswept places as a woman goes around a pail of hot water set down where a child might knock it. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years lay where it lay, and the new finer grey called the thaw lay beside it, and neither had been given an instruction and neither was swept. She went wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim; around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, whose water is called weather; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed fifteen and did not come out; and around the place the two carriers had crossed with the glaze-room hand between them. Each turn had a look and a wide step and no number, and none of them went into the basket.

A woman come up from the racks with \lore{slip} dried pale in the creases of her wrists asked what the pots would want before the tend was out. Sella glanced once at the clay jar and sent her to the pot. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it,” she said, and the question went away level in the arm that had carried it, nothing slopped, nothing priced, nothing refused and nothing entered. Money, work and weather stayed in arms, loaves and glances where they had always stayed, and never came out as a figure within reach of the basket. A man with a barrow said the firing would want two hands by the end of the week and then said it was only a direction, and the yard went on around the Ash as water goes around a stone that lies where a stone lies.

The sweetish edge of \lore{lead glaze} came up from \place{High Kiln} on a wrong wind and went out again on the same wind, spoken of as a direction and answered with the pot and the weather. The pot that had sung itself out early on the day before stood swept around where somebody had set it, with no chip and no crack shown and no word said over it. Sella did not make an omen of it, and the kiln-master's saying that a pot singing before its time generally sings itself out early was not made true as a lesson so soon after the carrying-out of a man breathing between two carriers. No second edge was paid, and no High Kiln saying was stood up as a cause. The boy turned his head a little toward the wind and filed the sweet where a direction goes, not among the hearings, not with the near-full answers, not as a fourth likeness waiting to be promoted. Three likenesses in a row will call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and ninety-one days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

\scenebreak

Off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, \place{the back room} kept its finger's width of open all morning, and the men who went low through it before first grey got no name from the house and were not followed by the narration. The two shut-room tellings stayed two --- one from a man at a well, one from a man going up the road with a narrow slate turned to his sleeve --- and no third came to stand beside them.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. He did not say the room was cold. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, flat and low, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it, and kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. A man had come up on a wrong wind with the sweet edge at one mouth-corner, and Jorra withheld the knowing from him; no pot was lost in the yard.

When Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and held there until the sentence reached the pot, and then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The boy read the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, and filed it as one object worth nothing joined to no kiln. The holding was remembered, not made again; it lay on its own place and was not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones, and no payer stood under it to call it a cause.

At \place{the kindling} the small knife stood at the very edge of its sill, back where a man could put a thumb and find it where it should be. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark, in the place where the house could check and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He said “Yes, house” to a neighbour who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

The ear-sentence came to his teeth and stood there with no figure and no name. It went to the word that would call \lore{the loan} and stopped at the threshold, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, with the small interval from Seed sixteen showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He did not say the sentence, did not call the loan and did not stand a tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. The ninth was the last, and the tenth had not stood up. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He said “Yes, house” to the fire and went on with the broom.

The stone lay back in its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft}, two years gone and cold, unreconciled with the debt by not trying, which is not forgiving. From below, the boy filed only the remembering of the small dry scrape heard on another afternoon, on a place of its own: not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping and not a payment, not laid across the three held changes and not stood up with them into a rule.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped and were not given it again and joined it to nothing. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the road and no collector went down it. The knife lay at the edge of its sill with the seventh year standing at the lip where no thumb of the house had gone, and the count went forward one more day exact and unspent in the dark of the wool.
