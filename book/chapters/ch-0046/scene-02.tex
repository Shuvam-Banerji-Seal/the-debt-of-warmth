\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} while the bell was yet going over the wet boards, and the two hundred at \place{the Cross} leaned the way a stood sheaf leans when a hand passes through it and then settled back into themselves. He had not hurried. A man who has carried clay learns not to arrive at a moot out of breath, because breath is a thing a crowd can hear and count against you. Under his coat the red wool cord of his office was folded square, not worn across his chest, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} sat at one corner of his mouth where the habit of tasting had put it and where no wiping would take it off before the reading. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before he touched anything, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and then let the hands hang as though they had finished their work and were answerable for nothing further.

He held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned toward his sleeve, and passed a finger over the figure without looking at it, letting his eyes rest on the place a breath longer than the writing required, the way a man tries a seam with the pad of a thumb before he puts his weight on it. Then he read. “One in twelve, as the notice says,” he said low into the top of the crowd, and the crowd had to lean in to hear him, which was the reason he never raised his voice. The figure went twice over the basket in \place{the moot-post}'s finger's width of shade, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and the boy lying there with cold iron near his cheek heard a number said over him and had no way to answer it and no hand to put a figure anywhere. A reading is a thing said twice over a body that cannot speak; he had known the shape of it before the first stroke, and knowing it bought him nothing.

“The levy is called,” Berr said. “It is not called by me.” To a man who had brought a number up out of a fist and held it toward the block, he said there would be a slate at the watch if \emph{your house} wanted it again --- \emph{your house}, never \emph{I} --- and took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on. He did not say whose house wanted it or what the number had cost anybody. The printed figure stood where the notice had put it and nobody leaned in to challenge it; the month's arithmetic changed where two hundred people stood and could not be changed back by dusk. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid. The true subtraction went away up the clay road in the locked box under his coat, and the boy could not have put a finger on which of the two figures was the true one, because no figure in the square knew the man who had been carried out of \place{the Clay Yard} the day before with the pale line at his gum, and no collector, no slate and no notice had entered what that carrying cost. The pale line lay where it lay, on a place of its own, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, and the reading went over the top of it without stopping.

Berr went off toward \place{High Kiln} with the cord folded and the box locked, believing he had done the district a kindness. The bell went four, four and a gap over the going of him, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself. The boy kept the heard bell beside the told bell of the Long Night, four, four and a gap spoken in the dark, and neither was asked to account for the other; two hearings of one shape standing side by side will call themselves a rule if no hand holds them apart, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-nine days holding such hands apart with hands that could not lift a spoon. Under the boards of the house up the road \lore{the carried fire} answered four low pulls and missed its fifth, its payer still without a name; through the finger's-width door at home the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the six hearings of \lore{the full note}; overhead the boards kept four nails, four, and a knot the width of a thumb. Three counts in three places that did not become one. The last stroke of the bell died into the pigs and the turnip tops and the wet boards, and the square went back to its own noise as water goes back to its own level in a pail that has been struck.

\person{Sella Vail} stood half a pace off with the basket set in the thin shade where no slate lay, her burn shut on nothing. She gave the instruction to the wool and to the river wind and gave none to the basket and no reason for the setting. The boy's cheek kept the cold of the moot-post's iron and his hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water and came down against the fold. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing two inches beyond his hand at home, and wanted a number spoken by somebody who would have known what a number counted, and got none of them. The figure that had gone over him was not his figure and did not know him; it would go into a slate in \place{Greybank} before noon and be entered in a hand that had never seen the inside of a basket. He had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} came through the square with the Thane's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned toward her sleeve. Her hand arrived at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd --- the small flat correction of a strap, the turn of a wrist that brought the edge round, the settling of the second shorter strap let out for the long road --- the whole order done before she had looked at a single face. Her coat was shortened at the skirt for the road's mud and the mud had dried on it in a pale scurf she did not brush off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. The thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was. She was a runner and not a reader, and the figure on the slate remained a thing somebody else had set down.

A woman near the pigs asked what she carried. “I don't read it. I carry it. There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which,” Hessa said, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking.

“Then what's on it?”

“Cross to Greybank. Reading goes up before noon. I've got a road to walk.” No rudeness, no apology. She answered the question put and not the one behind it, and left the second question standing where it had been set down, the way a man leaves a pot where there is no hand to carry it.

The boy filed the order of her reaching on a place of its own, the eighth such hand in the district, joined to none. Below the bridge another hand went to another slate before its eyes went to the stalls, and the two records going inward were two records going inward; a row of such hands will call itself an account if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer, and he would not leave it standing. No figure came off Hessa's slate under any roof in Sill, and no figure came off it into the basket.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk stayed set at \place{the out-of-line landing} for a measure nobody had called, his cuff grey from washing and grey again by midday, his slate to his forearm with the writing turned inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. His hand went to the slate before his eyes went to \place{the stalls}; a buyer named a number and the clerk entered it before he looked up to find the man who had said it. Nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. The market sold its pots and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies. He was not \person{Marra}, was not the grey-fingered officer out of \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing}, was not the soft-handed man at the Court's table, was not the foundling; the boy kept him on his own place and did not make a fourth likeness stand up and call itself a cause.

Across the square at \place{the pot board} a woman stood looking at cups with the sweetish green of lead glaze at one corner of her mouth and clay worked under her nails, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long enough to leave its mark. She turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, said nothing, and went off with the light. The boy called the colour green and nothing else and gave the shape no ear: the sixth green at a mouth, a colour joined to no hearing, held where it was held and not counted again, not joined to the Thane's green at his own mouth and not joined to anything that had ever been spoken in a room.

At the market's edge a small shape in cast-offs worn like a uniform stood with silt dried pale in the creases of his wrists and a green sliver on a string at his throat that caught the light when he turned and went dull when he turned back. No loaf passed to him, no slate came near him, no sale was made, and no sound came out of him that any clerk could take down. The boy did not count him again. A shape is a shape, and the sixth sighting stayed the sixth and was not spent for effect; the green at the child's throat was a colour and the green at the woman's mouth was a colour, and the two colours standing in two places of one square were not joined by any finger of his and did not stand up together and call themselves a cause. He had stopped drawing the line long before he learned what a line was for, which was a small mercy and not a skill.

Sella carried him past the runner at the half-pace and gave the runner no name and asked nothing about the slate. The basket went through the square at the edge of the light where no figure could fall into it and none could be taken off it, and the morning's arithmetic went on over its head in arms and loaves and glances, which was the only arithmetic the house would ever enter.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in long low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of water over water, the two passing on the stair of the evening as they had passed every evening of his life without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either. The arm that had carried him a hundred and eighty-nine mornings kept the height of the handle, the give of the shoulder and the turn of the wrist at a step's end, a record in muscle that remembered weight and gave no account of cost; it would have said the weight was the same and the cost was not, which were both true and neither entered.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim; around \place{the trough} at \place[the yard well]{the well}, bare and dark, whose water was called weather; wide beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; around the empty place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out; and around the place the two carriers had crossed the day before with the glaze-room hand between them. Each got a look that asked for nothing and found nothing, a wide step and no number and no explanation to the basket of why a pot set down here should be carried wide of there. The clean dark line where \lore{Ash} had turned the corner for two years and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} beside it lay unswept, neither with an instruction, and the household went around both.

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof she set him on \place{the bed} and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock; put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face; left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had been given an instruction about the thread; and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said “The house will keep it,” stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go --- pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, set the pot rim out on \place{the loaf shelf} with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, and did not look toward the bed. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, and she did not touch it, because a reading and a dusk are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The two rooms did not touch: hers held a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, and his held a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He had no towel and no instruments and no case, and laid them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not have lifted the least of them.

Six hearings of the full note lay beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at the Cross, and a man giving at the Drawing --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in a hundred and eighty-nine days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it, a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again, and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on the wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any, held apart by hand because a doctrine is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to any of them. A tenth had not stood up before the seventh year was earned; the seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given, the loan that can hear was uncalled, and the Seed 16 breath stood unspent at the top of every reach with the small interval where a breath should have gone in and had not.

Three held changes stood on three places: the fraction from the morning of Day 161, the beat that stayed under the board on Day 166, the half-check on the stones from Day 171 --- each on its own ground and none standing up beside the others to call itself a rule. The scrape of a pot's foot set down on stone in the loft stood on a fourth place, and the pale line at the gum stood on a fifth, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a near-full answer, not a stopping and not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to this. The told bell lay beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid figure; the printed one in twelve lay where the notice had put it and the true subtraction lay in a locked box up the road.

The shelf kept its two unnamed gaps, the old space at the row's end and the palm's breadth that came in on the night of Seed 6, and the charcoal waited under the front edge for a hand that was not hers and a day that had not come; her finger had not gone beneath it this evening, so the beat stayed where it was left and the charcoal stayed unread. The pan's gone round place kept its own tray, and the field's empty place in the returning line stayed where the yard went around it; neither was laid across the shelf's gaps or the fold's crust. No third gap came, no fourth likeness was made a cause and no finger of his drew the line, because three likenesses in a row will stand up and call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and he had spent a hundred and eighty-nine days taking them down one at a time with nobody to help and nobody to see.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note and overhead the boards kept their knot and under the floor the fire kept its gap, three counts in three places that did not become one. Cold had come in its known order to cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as if it had reached the end of its own instruction; the afterdrop waited on the far side of a warming nobody in this house would perform. He wanted the crust two inches from his ear, wanted the breast, wanted the pot handle standing beyond his hand, wanted a number nobody under any roof would speak, and got none of them and had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the note of water over water through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and eighty-nine stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, spoken by nobody, entered in no slate, and the count went forward into the morning of Day 190.
