The afternoon thinned without ending, the way water goes off a stone that has held the sun all day and keeps the shape of the warmth after the heat has gone out of it into the air. It lay along the floor under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in long low bars that did not reach the corner. Hot clay under tallow came first, as it had come at every hour of this day; wet wool under that; and below both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner that no broom in \place{Low Kiln} had been given an instruction about in two years. The boy lay at the low angle where the underside of \place{the loaf shelf} becomes a roof, and the light went over him without stopping, as the reading had gone over him under \place{the moot-post} and had not stopped either.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the shelf with her back to the basket and moved her lips along a row that stood with two gaps in it. There was the old space at the row's end, and the palm's breadth that had come in on the night of Seed six, and she gave neither of them a figure. Her eye went once to the clay jar cold under its glaze on the wall, and her hand shut half over nothing without touching it. \lore{Ash} lay thin along the skirting board in the colour of the clean dark line, and the new finer grey called \lore{the thaw} lay beside it, and neither had been given an instruction and neither was swept. Her finger stayed above the front edge of the board, so the charcoal tally beneath it waited unread, uncounted and uncopied. The held beat that had stayed under the board on an afternoon gone by stayed where it had been left; an afternoon and a woman's back turned were not the same as that night, and she did not go back to the shelf to make them the same.

He had watched the hand come in over the edge that night without looking at what it touched, and go away again, and he watched the hand kept above the edge now. Two ways of keeping, with one woman's back between them. He laid them on two places and did not let either stand for the other; three likenesses standing in a row will call themselves a truth if a man leaves them standing, and a truth is an account that has found a payer and no payer stood to these. No third gap came. No fourth likeness was made a cause. No finger of his drew the line.

At \place{the hearth} she told \lore{the carried fire} it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. \lore{The widow's mark} hung on its cord at her throat where it had hung since the first firing, and she did not touch it, because an afternoon and a shelf are not openings and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. She said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake at the top, the hold, the letting go. The fire answered four low pulls and missed the fifth, and its payer had had no name for a hundred and ninety days. Coal-black hair went back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals.

She set the pot rim out on the shelf with its handle standing two inches beyond the reaching hand, let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. The back of the broken pot was warm where it had crossed the coals; the grey streak went out when she turned from the light. He wanted the handle. He wanted the crust lying leathery two inches from his ear with its empty place unnamed and no third. Neither came nearer, and he had stopped expecting to be told, which was not the same as not wanting it.

\scenebreak

At \place{the kindling} the small knife stood at the very edge of its sill, back where a man could put a thumb and find it where it should be. Six were counted in the worn thumb-mark, in the place where the house could check and verify them; the seventh stood at the lip, where no thumb of the house had gone. Beside the knife lay \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, the one about the man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford, kept where the seventh year was kept and entered in nothing. \person[Tarn Vail]{Tarn} took the broom only after the broom had been given its instruction, as a man takes up a tool already named, and told the wood what he told it at every going and coming of the light.

“Six counted. The next I don't count.”

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. He said “Yes, house” to a neighbour who wanted the thaw early and to another who wanted it late, agreed so entirely that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} and past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the stopped place and were not given it again. He swept the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. No collector came up the road and no collector went down it.

The ear-sentence came to his teeth and stood there with no figure and no name. It went to the word that would call \lore{the loan} and stopped at the threshold, the breath drawn at the top of the reach and not spent, with the small interval from Seed sixteen showing where a breath should have gone in and had not. He did not say the sentence, did not call the loan and did not stand a tenth stopping up before the seventh year was earned. Nine stoppings stood on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. The ninth was the last, and the tenth had not stood up. The seventh year stood nearer its end than it had ever stood and had not given. He said “Yes, house” to the fire and went on with the broom.

In \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He kept the dusty forearms and the clean ones on one tray, as a man keeps two states of one morning in one account without writing a word over either, and he asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it had been a birth-slate. The three or four men who had gone low before first grey into the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} got no name from him, and he did not say the room was cold. The two shut-room tellings stayed two, one from a man at a well and one from a man going up the road with a narrow slate turned to his sleeve, and no third came to stand beside them. His two silences and the clay-bound hand's stayed unjoined under one roof, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, flat and low, and the wheel took the words and went on turning. To the pot under his hands he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left the wheel, knew it in the forearm before the knuckle had proved it, and kept the knowing where it belonged, on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. A man had come up on a wrong wind with the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} at one mouth-corner, and Jorra withheld the knowing from him; no pot was lost in the yard, and no saying of a pot singing before its time was made true as a lesson so soon after the carrying-out of the day before.

When Sella's voice went low at the hearth and did not finish --- \emph{The fire's low. It wants---} --- his bent finger went to the cold iron of \lore{the damper} and held there until the sentence reached the pot, and then he moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left the finger standing. The boy read the iron again as the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in \lore{Grathic} that would carry both mercies in one mouthful, and filed it as one object worth nothing joined to no kiln. The holding was remembered, not made again; it lay on its own place and was not paired with the beat under the shelf or the half-check on the stones.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light she carried him home up \place{the clay road}, woodsmoke going before her in low bars out of the chimneys and the river wind coming up behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}, the two passing on the stair of the evening without taking each other's colour or spending a word on either, as they had passed every evening of his life. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket, and gave the basket no explanation of any turn she made. Behind her the kiln kept its breath in fours with a gap.

At the edge of the yard she went around the round place on the wall where the shallow clay pan had stood mouth-up through Reed and was standing there no longer, with no scrape, no crumb, no print in the dust and no chip of a rim. She went around \place{the trough} at \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, whose water is called weather; wide around the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody; and around the empty place in the returning line where a hand went in on Seed fifteen and did not come out. She had already gone wide around the place the two carriers crossed with the glaze-room hand between them, with the same look and the same wide step. Each turn had a look and a wide step and no number, and none of them went into the basket. The clean dark line where Ash turned the corner and the new finer grey called the thaw lay unswept where they lay, and the household went around both.

Under the roof she set him on the bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, base first and the weight settled the way a woman sets a vessel that must not rock; put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without once looking at his face; left the thread of drool frozen and unwiped, because nobody under any roof has been given an instruction about the thread. Her hand rested a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting needed before it went away --- the half-beat she learned in the winter she sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and has never told anybody, and would not call kindness now if a slate were held under it and a clean hand set to the figure. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, said \emph{The house will keep it}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward nothing entered, pushed coal-black hair back with the burn-wrist so the grey streak showed pale off the coals, and did not touch the mark, because a dusk and a reading are not openings. She went to her own bed. The two rooms do not touch.

In the small hours he turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel before a case begins, each thing going into its own hollow and nothing resting across anything else, the order of the laying being the whole of the discipline and the discipline older than any word in this country. He has no towel and no instruments and no case, and lays them out anyway in the dark with hands that could not lift the least of them. Six hearings of the full note lie beside and never among --- five from four sources, a cup across a birth room, a pot in the shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and a man giving at \lore[the Hearth Drawing]{the Drawing} --- and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a person as the men lifted, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh came in a hundred and ninety days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged and a finding carried into a room with a signature at the bottom is a finding that will be believed. Three near-full answers stand on their own tray, advanced and not paid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper keeps back from a man come up on the wrong wind. None was promoted into the hearings and no pot was lost in the yard.

Nine stoppings stand on the interval tray --- Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29 --- objects and not a rule, intervals and not payments, no payer named to any. A tenth has not stood up before the seventh year was earned, and the seventh year stands nearer its end than it has ever stood and has not given. The told bell lies beside the heard bell, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself. The told figure lies beside the paid figure. Three held changes stand on three places --- the fraction at the iron, the beat beneath the shelf, the half-check on the stones --- the loft-sill scrape on a fourth and the pale line at a gum on a fifth, and no payer stands under any of them to call them a cause.

The month's first figure had gone up the clay road in a locked box and had passed over the basket at the Cross without knowing what the yard carried on its hands the day before. It was a figure said and a cost borne, and no slate in the district had joined the two. There was no number for the man carried out breathing between two carriers, no collector called for him, no lesson of the sweet on a wrong wind or of a pot that sang itself out early, no cause laid across the pale line and any absence in the yard. The cost went as a direction to the pot and the weather and arrived nowhere and was entered in no slate. He had one possession in the dark and it was this: the distinction itself, exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door and came down against the fold. One hundred and ninety stood exact, complete and unspent in the dark of the wool, and the count went forward. Two rooms that do not touch: hers a pot, a fire and a woman counting under her breath toward nothing entered, his a crust two inches from an ear and an empty place beside it with no name.
