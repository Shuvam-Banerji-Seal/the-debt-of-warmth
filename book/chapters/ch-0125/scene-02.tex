The afternoon came on without anybody telling the basket, as an afternoon comes in a house that keeps its hours in loaves and glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} where the heat came to him sideways, and the small hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and twenty-seven where it had been put down, a tool laid where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. The crust stood two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights; the thread at his mouth had frozen stiff in the kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. He did not lift the crust. He did not wipe the thread. Under the stillness the wanting went on, small and animal and louder than the exactness, three imports --- crust lifted, thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle --- and he had none of the three. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. The work was the quarrel, and the quarrel went on in a body that could not lift a crust two inches to its own mouth.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket and told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, and carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire and never to the basket; the basket was not told what the hour was and was not told to be still. In \place{the Clay Yard} she went wide around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place}, and set the basket down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on it between her pail and her broom.

\scenebreak

The small sounds came first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, the fibres complaining as they took the weight and going quiet when the weight was taken. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist, eye, mouth and the half-beat in the gap --- the knock before the pail's settling, the rasp before the rope's quiet, the fist before the eye, the eye before the mouth --- and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stands. A pail is a pail. A rope is a rope. The finger's width of air between a loaf and where a loaf had been bought no warmth and had no owner, and he wrote no cause across it.

He had learned the order of reaching in rooms whose doors he had never crossed: hand to the record before eye to the person, mouth after both, the half-beat standing in the gap where a joining would have stood. The yard gave him the same order in smaller things, and he read the yard the way he had once read a bay, by what it would not say rather than by what it would not survive. Two women at the rail said the rope would take a finger if a body was careless and that cold was cold and fingers were fingers, and went back to their pails; another said the cold had got into the rope and would not come out before the thaw, and another said a rope that held was a rope that held. He gave the women no interior, the words no owner beyond the mouths they came out of and the half-beat no intention. The large talk of the yard went over him like wind over a roof and left nothing in the hand.

He went once around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, for a second circuit would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. The clean dark line at \place{the Vail corner} kept its finer grey beside it, the grey the yard called \lore{the thaw}; the space beneath the lowest rack held the broken birth-slates that nobody entered; the gone pan kept its shallow round print on a place of its own; \place{the trough} stood bare and dark; the field's empty place lay out where the worked strips showed under the frost; the place the carriers crossed kept its own width; and the \place{the returning-line place} was widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. No name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. A figure set against that space would want a payer standing under it and none stood.

At the trough the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and he did not lay it across the loaf's stopping or the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated and he read it without a figure: level is not honest, and a level is not a promise, and the line had no more to do with what the trough had held than a loaf's edge had to do with the hand that pushed it.

The line at the corner had two greys in it and a finger between. The clean dark kept its finer hairline called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up where no broom had gone in two years; beside it the grey of the ridge lay left in place with a finger between the two. A thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The ridge had been called gone by the yard in the dark before the year turned and had been left where it lay, and the calling and the leaving stayed on two places with a finger between them, the one no mending of the other.

The air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge where the low note of \lore{water over water} ran nearer under the winter skin, and the tang came thinner there. He found the softening where put and called it weather --- not a sign, not a fourth of \lore{the three coverings}, not a payment or a custom on a second day of \place{Silt}, not looked twice or made again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it. The yard went on about its pails and its rope and did not sweep the line, and nobody owned the thinner air or the day, and the boy gave no eighth to anything.

\scenebreak

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at \place{the yard well} opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price, and carried the note off to the pot where it belonged. The dressed grain lay covered under wool at \place{the rack end} and a figure read over it would not know what it cost, the ink-neat space between figure and price carrying no signature. He took the three apart by their order --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap; errand, note, pot, no price under the note; figure, space, no hand at the signature --- and filed each on a place of its own. An arriving nowhere is not a refusal and not a payment; a note is not a price; a figure that does not know what the grain cost is a figure with a hole in it, and a hole buys no warmth.

Nothing from the days behind crossed under the yard's grammar as a lesson. The offered figure, the asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and his direction down \place{the north bridge}, \person{Berr Clay}'s box gone up \place{the clay road} with the third subtraction locked inside, the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure reported from \place{the out-of-line landing} below the north bridge, the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, \person{Hessa Rale}'s slate unread up the road with her eighth hand eighth, \person{Ilva} offstage with her strip in her sleeve and her ninth hand nine, the two sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either --- each kept its place with a finger between every pair, not told again, not a custom, a witness, a reader, a tenth hand or a payer, and not laid together to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. No collector came up or down the clay road on a day that had earned only its second, and Silt 10 waited beyond the close as a date and was not brought into this afternoon as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no figure on \lore{the tile debt}; the small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach. The sentence he had said in the morning was found where put and not said again, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. \person{Jorra Vail} came through \place{the finger's-width door} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with meal-fine grey slate dust white in a fist-crease, parted his dusty forearms from clean as clot from vessel, spoke to the wheel before any person and knew the \lore{full note} a Vail pot would give three turns ahead, keeping the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early; he did not say that \place{the back room} was cold and gave no name to the men in it, and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn. The two quiet men under one roof stayed unjoined, and the boy made no custom of the silence of either.

The going of the light came on without a bell and without a reading, as a going of the light comes under a roof that has kept its hours in loaves and glances, and the household straightened around the order of it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. Sella Vail came to the hearth before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment. The \lore{widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a second day of Silt that had earned only its second. She touched the mark before she opened the kiln and at no other hour, and \place{the kiln mouth} was not open this evening.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline as he read all honest seams, with the eye of a man who had set a clamp and known the difference between what holds and what only looks joined. Garl Vail's laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought this absence a payment, a name or a mending.

She turned the dressed grain under wool at the rack end with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child and no figure or handful, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf where the heat came sideways, never in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold.

\lore{The keeping at the fold} was found where put with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. It had been put there on the fifteenth by a hand that had not come back to place it again, and a hand that finds is not a hand that places; a habit wants a hand that placed twice and a way wants a third, and both want a payer where none stood. He found it as he had found it on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, and he did not lay it across the crust's two inches, the shelf's two gaps, the gone pan's round print or the field's empty place, for two rooms that do not touch are not made one by a hand that has no key.

Under \place{the boards} \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for four hundred and twenty-six days, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Nobody lifted the floor to supply the pull and nobody said the man's name to make the two counts agree. The boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where a fifth might have been driven and was not; the kiln behind the wall breathed in fours with its gap, and the river through the finger's-width door kept its low over lower under a skin, three counts in three places that did not become one, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap: the pail's flat knock on the stone, the rope's cold-stiffened rasp, the barley wall to wall without a number, the loaf stopped a finger's width along with nobody calling the stopping a figure; the seven walked-around places once and not twice with the returning-line place widest; the trough's round print on its own place; the two unswept lines at the corner with a finger between; the softening at the river's skin found where put and called weather; the fist arriving nowhere, the note that was not a price, the figure that did not know what the covered grain cost with the ink-neat space unsigned; the offered figure and its return, the asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the two sightings on separate trays, the three readings gone up the road behind one unsigned figure --- three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid --- and the keeping at the fold found where put. Each kept its place with a finger between every pair. No sixth shaping, seventh hearing, tenth hand, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic or eighth opening stood on a day that had earned only its second; no witness stood, no reader was made, no slate was opened within reach of the basket, and no payer came up the clay road or went down it.

The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side on the rough towel. The file turned out exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and wanted them with the whole small animal body that could not reach two inches. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in the dark under a roof that kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned.

Answer four hundred and twenty-eight came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the withheld sentence, the softening, the two sightings, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Eight honest answers stood on the sixth place, 421 through 428, two to each of the three days behind and two to this one, and not one was a doctrine. Four hundred and twenty-six exact, six months and two hundred and forty-six days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the three readings across one another to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key to Berr Clay's box came under any roof. A number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth.

The hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat against the fold on answer four hundred and twenty-eight the way a man puts a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread stayed stiff and unwiped; the empty place beside the crust held the keeping found where put. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. The river went on under its skin and the note ended on its own finishing, and the boy lay under the roof at the low angle of the loaf shelf with the count exact at four hundred and twenty-six and nobody to speak it, forward into a third morning not yet lived and not brought into this day.
