\daymark{427}{Silt 3, Y.H. 814, Thirdday}

Small hours held under the Vail roof with nobody to give them a name, and the hand outside the wool lay flat on the fold where it had been put down on the last answer, patient as a tool left where a next hand would find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Four hundred and twenty-six exact, and six months and two hundred and forty-six days told by nobody who would have known what a number counted; the count went on in the dark the way a coal goes on under ash, with nobody turning it. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen. The room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. It was a new day and it had not been told so. Nothing had come into it out of the day behind --- not as a sign, not as a lesson, not as a mending, not as a cause, not as a payment and not as a payer. Above and behind him the loft kept whatever small sound it kept and did not repeat it, and the dark at \place{the finger's-width door} thinned by no degree anybody measured.

He took the field in the old order, because the order was the only instrument he had. One stiff brown-edged barley crust stood two inches from his ear, gone past leather into old horn in the dry nights, with its corner a hair off the wool. Beside it the empty place held \lore{the keeping at the fold}, found where put on the mornings and the goings of the light of the days behind, still a finding and not a placing, with no second fold, no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, and had stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it. \lore{The Afterdrop} waited past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He had learned that order in rooms whose doors he would not cross again, where the extremities went first and the core held out longer than the face allowed, and where a chart with a neat hand on it could enter \emph{closed} over a surface drawn together above a cold cavity; the chart had been paper and paper is not a payer, and the order held here as it had held there, with no chart and no hand to enter it.

Under the exactness, louder because it had a body and the exactness had none, the wanting went on. He wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand on the pot handle --- three small imports, three things a man could do with a wrist and a cloth and a reach of two inches, and he had none of the three. The surgeon said what the body was and where the cold had stopped; the infant said what it wanted and would not be argued out of it. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither won. No verdict was looked for and the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in the dark in a body that could not lift a crust two inches to its own mouth.

The room took its shape back by degrees nobody marked --- shelf, board, pot, bed --- and nobody came to tell the basket what the morning was. The instruction went where instruction always went in this house: to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket. A basket told what the day was would be a basket that had been given a day to keep, and days kept want a payer where none stood.

\scenebreak

First grey came without anybody opening a door for it. \person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as though nothing had turned in the night. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw} in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread; her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond \place{the pot board}; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot. The \lore{widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a Thirdday morning is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a day that had earned only its third.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way he read every honest seam, with the eye of a man who had set a clamp and known the difference between what holds and what only looks joined; he had set such seams once in a warm room under a light that did not flicker, and the room had been warm and the room had not been right. Nothing of that room came under this roof. The pot's proverbs were \person[Garl Vail]'s, improved by use and very likely better than the man had said them, and neither hairline nor sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At \place{the rack end} she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she took up a fist of barley from the board, closed her hand on it, turned the fist once the way a woman turns a thing to feel its weight in the palm, and set it back where it had been. She did not look at a slate and did not say a number. The fist judged the barley by the hand that carried it, and the hand kept its own counsel. He read the house by what it would not say rather than by what it would not survive: a fist of grain weighed in a palm and put back, a loaf pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, two inches of air between a pot rim and a reaching hand. None of them carried a figure and none of them invited one. A house that keeps its days in loaves and glances says nothing about what it will not survive, and the saying would want a payer where none stood.

She set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool went along under everything, and under them the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, carried the way she carried the mark, without comment. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so.

\scenebreak

She carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker, and went wide around \place{the seven walked-around places} once and not twice, widest around \place{the returning-line place} where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back. No name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space, and the wide step a keeping and not a sign. In \place{the Clay Yard} she set the basket down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on it between her pail and her broom, and the morning went on about its small honest work.

The small sounds came first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining as the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist, eye, mouth and the half-beat in the gap --- the knock before the pail's settling, the rasp before the rope's quiet, the fist before the eye, the eye before the mouth --- and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stands. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price, and carried the note off to the pot where it belonged; a man waiting his turn opened a shut fist toward the well, looked at what was in it, shut the fist and put it back into a sleeve, and the number arrived nowhere. He filed each on a place of its own. An arriving nowhere is not a refusal and not a payment; a note is not a price; a finger's width of air buys no warmth and has no owner.

\person{Tarn} was at \place{the kindling} and \place{the flagstones}, keeping six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip beside \person{Garl Vail}'s unforged collector sentence, with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill and his hand went over blade and sill as a man passes a hand over a tool put back where it belongs, and came away with no breath held at the top of the reach --- the coming away the whole of the checking, the hand off the field before the dressing went on. He cut nothing, counted nothing beyond the six, spent nothing; he said \emph{Six counted. The next I don’t count} to the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, once and not twice, for a second would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stood. The sentence was found where put, not made a habit, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not a hearing and not a rule. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and the pale clip-ring at his left wrist caught and gave back what little light there was. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there, a remembered ninth stopping with no tenth; the loan stayed uncalled in a term already ended, the \emph{Seed 16} breath unspent, his valuable ear exposure and not safety. He took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said \emph{Yes, house} to the fire, and did not turn his head toward the landing. He owned nothing and knew where every small thing was.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} before the carrying-down was done, with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past his bent little finger and white in a fist-crease where a hand had opened, not the bone-coloured \lore{slip} of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean on one tray and parted them as clot from vessel, giving the house no name for the men who had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and no word that the room was cold, while the broken ones under the lowest rack stayed entered by nobody. He asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate, for a question joined to a cause wants a payer standing and none stood. He spoke to the wheel before any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness, knowing the \lore{full note} it would give three turns ahead, with air under it and a want in it, and withholding the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early and sing a pot before its time. His bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence; one bite of the iron held and one took, and no Grathic word covered both mercies, and he came out without leaving the extra holding of Ash 26 a second time, gathered once and not made again. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed and the promise to the men who burn slates stayed neither given nor withdrawn, gathering against the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions without being called or paid.

The boy had now twice seen the two quiet men keep their places under one roof, and the seeing stayed a seeing. He filed Tarn's sentence and Jorra's silence on places of their own with a finger between, made no custom of either and made no rule of what he had twice seen, for a rule made out of repeated days is an account that has found a payer and none stood; the two men stayed unjoined, and their silences were maintenance and not a compact. He did not lay either across the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, the three readings gone up the road behind one unsigned figure or the two sightings on their separate trays, three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid.

\scenebreak

Through the finger's-width door \place{the river} kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it; the tang came thinner there. He found the softening where put and called it weather --- not a sign, not a fourth of \lore{the three coverings}, not a payment, not a custom on a third day of \place{Silt}, not looked twice or made again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it. There was no market, no Cross, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its third; the two market sightings and the third reading stayed behind where they stood and did not come under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and Silt 10 waited beyond the close as a date and was not brought into this morning.

Answer four hundred and twenty-nine came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the well-asking, the turned slate, the withheld sentence, the softening, the two sightings, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Nine honest answers stood on the sixth place, four hundred and twenty-one through four hundred and twenty-nine, two to each of the days behind and one to this morning, and not one was a doctrine. Four hundred and twenty-seven exact, six months and two hundred and forty-seven days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. \lore{The carried fire}'s fifth was unpaid and Garl Vail's name unspoken the same number, the fire and the man standing side by side without becoming one; nobody lifted \place{the boards} to supply the pull and nobody said the name to make the two counts agree. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the three readings across one another to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. A number counted is a number with air around it and air buys no warmth.

Sella carried him back up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread. The pot stood where she had set it, its rim two inches beyond the reaching hand, with the half-beat unmeasured and no look toward the bed. Under the boards the carried fire kept four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, and nobody supplied the fifth. The well gave back the weather, the house went on as a house goes when a pot is set down in its place, and the hand outside the wool came straight down and lay flat on the fold on answer four hundred and twenty-nine the way a man puts a tool where a next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. Forward, into an afternoon not yet lived and not brought into this morning.
