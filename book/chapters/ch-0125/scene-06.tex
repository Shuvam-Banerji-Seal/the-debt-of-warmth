The afternoon came on the way heat comes into a room whose fire has been banked --- slowly, and from the side, and with nobody called to let it in. Nobody told the basket. \person[Corin]{The boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} in \place{the Vail kiln-house}, \place{Low Kiln}, \place{Sill}, \place{Grath}, under the roof and never in \place{the Debt Loft}, held where the heat came to him sideways and not where he would have chosen; the room kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned. \lore{The keeping at the fold} held its empty place with no second fold, no third crust, no name and no payer, and the crust stood horn-hard two inches from his ear with its corner a hair off the wool. The thread at his mouth had gone stiff in the dry kiln heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

\person{Sella Vail} came to \place{the hearth} before she came to the basket, and the household straightened around the order of it as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted. She carried him down \place{the nineteen steps} at the half-pace from every open slate, correcting the tilt at each turn of the landing before it settled into the wicker. The instruction went to the wool, the river wind, the pot, the fire and the banked fire, and never to the basket; a basket told what the hour was would be a basket given an hour to keep, and hours kept want a payer where none stood. In \place{the Clay Yard} she set him down out of the draught where she could keep an eye on him between her pail and her broom, and the yard went on about its small honest work.

The small sounds came first because the small sounds do not lie and the large ones are mostly weather. A pail went down on \place{the flat stone by the well} with the flat knock of a thing set where a thing has always been set. A rope settled into its knot with the cold-stiffened rasp, complaining as the weight came on it and going quiet when the weight was taken. Barley went wall to wall without a number. A loaf was pushed a finger's width along a board and stopped, and nobody called the stopping a figure. He took each apart by fist, eye, mouth and the half-beat in the gap --- the knock before the pail's settling, the rasp before the rope's quiet, the fist before the eye, the eye before the mouth --- and gave the gap no intention, for an intention wants a payer where none stood. A pail is a pail. A rope is a rope. The finger's width of air between a loaf and where a loaf had been bought no warmth and had no owner, and he wrote no cause across it.

Women came and went at the well and the rail with pails and pots, going about an ordinary Fourthday grammar of hand before eye and mouth after both; none looked into the wicker, and none gave the basket a name, an hour or an instruction. He took their not-seeing as findings and not as slights, for a finding is what a surgeon takes when he cannot ask and nobody in the room can answer.

He went once around \place{the seven walked-around places} with a look and a wide step and no number, once and not twice, for a second circuit would be a custom and a third a way and both want a payer where none stands. \place{the returning-line place} was widest of all, where a hand had gone in on \emph{Seed 15} and had not come back; no name for the hand, no body found, no figure for what any month had cost, no accident written across the space. The wide step was a keeping and not a sign. At \place{the trough} the bare dark water kept the shallow round print of the clay pan lifted away, a ring on a place of its own, and he did not lay it across the loaf's stopping or the ridge at the corner, for two absences laid across one another want a single payer and none stood. The water-line stood level in a vessel nobody had calibrated, and level is not honest and a level is not a promise.

The line at \place{the Vail corner} had two greys in it and a finger between. The clean dark kept its finer hairline called \lore{the thaw}, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of \lore{Ash} coming up where no broom had gone in two years; beside it the grey of the ridge lay left in place, and a thing left in place is not a thing forgiven. The yard had called the ridge gone in the dark before the year turned and had left it where it lay, and the calling and the leaving stayed on two places with a finger between, the one no mending of the other. The air off \place{the river} was less cold at one edge where the low note of \lore{water over water} ran nearer under the winter skin, and the tang came thinner there. He found the softening where put and called it weather --- not a sign, not a fourth of \lore{the three coverings}, not a payment, not a custom on a fourth day of \place{Silt}, not looked twice or made again. The sound came from under the covering and not from the covering, and the note ended on its own finishing before any hand of his went up to it. There was no market, no \place{Cross}, no bell, no Account-day, no called collector and no runner on a day that had earned only its fourth; the three readings and the two sightings stayed behind where they stood and did not come under the roof as sign, lesson, mending, cause, payment or payer, and Silt 10 waited six mornings off as a date and was not brought.

Costs came as directions and went away as weather and left no entry. A man waiting his turn at \place{the yard well} opened a shut fist toward the water, looked at what was in it, shut the fist again and put it back into a sleeve; the number arrived nowhere. A woman sent to ask what a pot cost got a note and not a price, and carried the note off to the pot where it belonged. The dressed grain lay covered under wool at \place{the rack end} and a figure read over it would not know what it cost, the ink-neat space between figure and price carrying no signature. He took the three apart by their order --- fist, eye, mouth, the half-beat in the gap; errand, note, pot, no price under the note; figure, space, no hand at the signature --- and filed each on a place of its own. An arriving nowhere is not a refusal and not a payment; a note is not a price; a figure that does not know what the grain cost is a figure with a hole in it, and a hole buys no warmth.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat with its crease at the left shoulder hung on its peg behind the door; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on that side and nobody was allowed to fill it; the clean dark line at the Vail corner kept its finer grey called the thaw, thickened in the dry cold with the faint sour tang of Ash coming up where no broom had gone in two years; the twice-mended pot on \place{the pot board} kept the hairline of the original break between the older brass staple and the tighter wire; the unforged collector sentence lay beside the seventh notch at \place{the kindling} with no name written on the man who had turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on \lore{the tile debt}. His name had been unspoken under this roof four hundred and twenty-eight days and \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth unpaid the same number, the man and the fire standing side by side without becoming one. Neither hairline nor sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending, and nobody lifted \place{the boards} to supply the pull.

Tarn and Jorra were offstage and found where put. \person{Tarn} was at the kindling and \place{the flagstones} with six pieces in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip, his sentence about an ear stopped at the word that would call the loan, the loan uncalled in a term already ended and the \emph{Seed 16} breath unspent; his valuable ear was exposure and not safety, and the fears he had were his own and not a lesson for any basket. \person{Jorra Vail} was at \place{the Vail wheel-shed} and the cold \place{the damper} with meal-fine grey dust past his bent finger and men unnamed in \place{the back room}, the promise to the men who burn slates neither given nor withdrawn; he had known the \lore{full note} a pot would give three turns ahead and had kept the knowing from any master come up from \place{High Kiln} who would call it early. The two quiet men stayed unjoined, and their silences were maintenance and not a compact, and nothing under the shed or at the kindling had been mended by the seeing.

\person[Ilva]{Ilva} was offstage with her count still in wood, the strip of notched birch in her sleeve unread and her hand ninth with no tenth; the offered figure and its return kept their place beside her hand and the fifth shaping of \lore{the well-saying}, not offered again, not a custom, not a reader made and not joined to anything. \person{Hessa Rale}'s narrow slate stayed unread up \place{the clay road} with her eighth hand eighth, and the soft-cuffed clerk's entered figure stayed a report brought up the yard from \place{the out-of-line landing} below \place{the north bridge}, never a sighting, a witness or a tenth hand. \person{Berr Clay} was gone up the clay road with the box locked and the key in a coat pocket that did not come back down, his \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered each on a place of its own with a finger between, believed and not owed.

The first man's face, the second man's direction and turned slate, the asking answered with the weather, the three likenesses of \place{Slate Row}, the two market sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either, the green at a pot board and the shape at the market's edge with no eighth, the softening at the river's skin kept as weather, and the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf filed on its own place: each kept its place with a finger between every pair, not repeated on a day that had earned only its fourth, not made a custom, a sixth shaping, a witness, a reader or a payer. The burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions stood where put, gathering interest against a promise neither given nor withdrawn. No tenth hand, tenth stopping, eleventh arithmetic, eighth opening, sixth hearing, signature, payer, witness or reader stood on this afternoon, no loan was called, and no slate was opened within reach of the basket.

The going of the light came on without a bell and without a reading, as a going of the light comes under a roof that has kept its hours in loaves and glances. Sella came to the hearth before she came to the basket. She told the coals it was the thaw in the voice another woman would have used for a child who could not hear her, and turned them with the back of a broken pot; the back scraped over them and left the blind ash where it lay. She gave the small intake, the hold and the letting-go toward no opening, figuring neither of the two gaps, and said \emph{The house will keep it} to the fire and not to the basket, because a thing said aloud becomes a thing the Court can enter. Her finger lay along the top of the loaf shelf so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and her lips moved on a two-gapped row. One glance went to the clay jar standing cold under \lore{lead glaze} beyond the pot board; the hand shut half over nothing, and the little-door burn on the back of her left hand, two straight sides with a lintel and a threshold, closed on air and went back to the pot.

Her hands were never still. When they had nothing to do they counted --- spoons, nails, the breaths of a child nobody numbered --- and came back to the hairline at the wire. He had once worked in a room where an instrument count was called aloud before a case and again after, and the count was the only thing in that room that could not lie; if the number came back wrong somebody looked under the drape. Here a woman counted spoons and nails and the breaths of a child nobody numbered and came back to the hairline, and the count came back right, and nobody looked under anything. He read the work and not the woman, which was the whole of what he was allowed. One grey streak at her temple said winter and she said nothing about the winter. She smelled of woodsmoke and wet wool, and under it the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, and she carried the smell the way she carried the mark, without comment.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung and she did not touch it; a going of the light is not an opening, seven openings in seven months are seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Fourthday that had earned only its fourth. She touched the mark before she opened \place{the kiln mouth} and at no other hour, and the kiln mouth was not open this evening.

She lifted the twice-mended pot thumb-first where the tighter wire crossed the crack. The hairline of the original break showed between the older brass staple and the wire, a dark thread with a hand's work on either side, and both proverbs went to the pot and the fire and never to a person: \emph{Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled}; \emph{If it's worth mending it's worth mending twice. The second time is the real one}. Repair was not restoration. A restored thing hides the hand that handled it; a mended thing leaves the hand showing, so that a vessel carrying its break can be read by the next woman who takes it up. He read the hairline the way a man reads a stitch-line he has laid himself, not for the neatness but for the place where the needle went in and came out, because a line that looks joined can still be a line that will not hold; and that reading belonged to a room whose doors he would not cross again, and none of it came under this roof. Garl Vail's laugh had ended in a cough and his proverbs had survived him improved by use, and neither the hairline nor the sayings bought the absence a payment, a name or a mending.

At the rack end she turned the dressed grain under wool with a touch lighter than a woman would use on a sleeping child, set each piece where it had lain and drew the wool back with the flat of her hand; no figure went over it and no handful was counted. Then she set the pot rim two inches beyond the reaching hand with a half-beat and no look toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat was the distance between the intention and the arrest; she did not measure it and did not name it, for a named gap wants a payer put under it and none stood. Two inches of air bought no warmth and she knew it as well as he did, and neither of them said so. Tallow and woodsmoke and wet wool went along under everything, and under them the faint sour tang of the corner nobody had swept in two years, carried the way she carried the mark, without comment.

She carried him up the nineteen steps at the half-pace and set him under the roof at the edge of the light where the heat came sideways, never in the Debt Loft, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and added no second fold. The keeping in its empty place was found where put and stayed a finding, with no third crust, no name, no gift, no payment and no payer. The crust stood unmoved two inches from his ear, horn-hard in the dry cold with its corner a hair off the wool; the thread at his mouth stayed stiff and unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread.

In the small hours \place{the towel} came out at the head of the bed as instruments come out when a case is done and before the next is called, rough from the wash and smelling of tallow and low smoke. He went over the day by the place of each thing with every gap left a gap --- the pail's flat knock on the stone, the rope's cold-stiffened rasp, barley wall to wall without a number, the loaf stopped a finger's width with nobody calling the stopping a figure; the seven walked-around places once and not twice with the returning-line place widest; the trough's round print on its own place; the two unswept lines at the corner with a finger between; the softening at the river's skin found where put and called weather; the fist arriving nowhere, the note that was not a price, the figure that did not know what the covered grain cost with the ink-neat space unsigned. And beside each, on places of their own with a finger between every pair, the offered figure and its return, the asking answered with the weather, the second man's turned slate and direction, the first man's face, the clerk's entered figure brought up the yard as a report and not a sighting, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the three likenesses of Slate Row, the neighbour's near-reaching at the shelf, the two sightings on separate trays with no eighth of either, the green, the shape, the burned slate at five and the four ownerless directions. Each kept its place. Six hearings stood beside and never among, with the seventh place empty and the Drawing's knock outside; the heard bell stood beside the told bell with both gaps in their own squares, and three counts in three places stayed three, for agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and none stood. Nine record-first hands and nine stoppings stood with no tenth; seven openings in seven months were seven and not a rule, and no eighth came due on a Fourthday that had earned only its fourth. Three readings of the year stood behind one unsigned figure, three being three and not one account, the figure read still not the figure paid; the twelve were not laid across the three to make a row come out even, no signature stood on the space and no doctrine stood up out of three, and Silt 10 waited six mornings off as a date and was not brought. Berr's \emph{your house} and his low saying about kindness entered kept their own places with a finger between, believed and not owed.

The surgeon's exactness and the infant's want stood side by side on the rough towel. The file turned out exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air, a restraint and not a payment borrowing no house count and supplying no payer. The unsent call to Nina stayed where it had been put years ago and was not paid again; no name came into the room with it and no hand reached a pocket that held nothing. The infant wanted the crust lifted, the thread wiped and a hand on the pot handle, and wanted them with the whole small animal body that could not reach two inches. Both were true. The second was louder. Neither bought the other, and the quarrel was the work, and the work went on in the dark under a roof that kept its heat the way a cup keeps water, with care, and with one crack nobody had mentioned.

Answer four hundred and thirty-two came out and was filed on the sixth place. It was exact, complete, worth nothing at two inches of air, laid across neither the twelve arithmetics of Y.H. 813, the three readings of the year gone up the road, the printed one in twelve, the fifth shaping of the well-saying, the offered figure, the clerk's entered figure, the two sightings, the neighbour's near-reaching, the widow's mark nor the keeping at the fold. Six honest answers stood on the sixth place, four hundred and twenty-seven through four hundred and thirty-two, two to each of the three days and not one a doctrine. Four hundred and twenty-eight exact, six months and two hundred and forty-eight days, told by nobody who would have known what a number counted. He did not stand the number up as a threshold or a figure, and did not lay the twelve old arithmetics and the three readings across one another to make a row come out even, for paper is not a payer and no key came under any roof. The carried fire's fifth was unpaid and Garl Vail's name unspoken the same number, the two counts standing side by side without becoming one.

Through \place{the finger's-width door} the river kept its note of water over water, a low thing over a lower thing under the winter skin, softer at one edge where the low note ran nearer under it; the tang came thinner there. The note ended on its own finishing. The hand outside the wool came up an inch toward it --- the inch not a reach, not a wanting made into a claim, only the body's small assent to a sound --- took a small intake from the room whose floor does not touch his, held the interval whole on the note's own finishing, let it go as the note ended, and came straight down and laid itself flat against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. There was no next hand in the room. He did not wipe the thread and did not lift the crust. Four low pulls breathed under the floor and the fifth was not supplied. The small hand lay flat against the fold, the thread stiff and unwiped, the crust horn-hard two inches from the ear, the count exact at four hundred and twenty-eight with nobody to speak it. Forward, into a fifth morning not yet lived and not brought into this day.
