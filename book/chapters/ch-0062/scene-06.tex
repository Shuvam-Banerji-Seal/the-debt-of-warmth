The afternoon came down off \place{the clay road} without a boundary drawn across it. Grain went out the way a pot goes out of the sun: no stroke marked the hour, no hand drew a line, and neither of the district's two names for the months was set right or left wrong in the telling. Smoke stood three mornings ahead beyond the door and was not brought into a day that had not earned it. \person[Corin]{the boy} lay at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear and let the afternoon thin around him. It thinned without ending. Heat settled under the shelf's underside, behind the pot, into the fold, and the crust lay where it had lain, one stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust two inches from the ear; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself and no third came to sit there and make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, temple and the small bare joint at the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder in the afternoon, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all and the adult's register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects and not in a story. The salt-stiff coat held a broader man's shape on its peg with the crease at the left shoulder where a man had once leaned to reach a shelf; the seat at the wheel kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side, and no one had straightened it. The \lore{Ash} ridge had turned the corner and become a clean dark line with a finer hairline of grey beside it that \person{Sella Vail} called \lore{the thaw}, and both were left unswept, not because a lesson was made of them but because a line left where it is is still a line kept. A proverb of his had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, and was said now over a corner where a repair had been rubbed out. The debt against a firing of tiles circled without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled. His name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and thirty-nine days. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for that debt on Grain 28, or Grain 29, or Grain 30, and no collector had gone down it; the keeping of him was maintenance, and nobody had made a story out of it and nobody would.

Sella went to \place{the hearth} before the basket, as she had gone to it on the morning of the reading and on every morning since. She told the wool to keep the river wind off his ears, told the fire it was the thaw, and gave the instruction to the pot and the broom and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. \emph{The house will keep it}, she said where another woman would have said \emph{I}, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals when she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn. At \place{the loaf shelf} she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally beneath stayed unread, and moved her lips along a row with two gaps without giving the gaps a figure. The dressed grain lay near her under its wool with no figure over it, counted in glances and handfuls and by a carrying arm that knew weight and not cost; she gave nobody a number for it and laid nothing across the empty places. When she set the pot rim out she put the handle two inches beyond the reaching hand and let her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-nine days, because an afternoon, a shelf, a yard, a basket and a pot are not openings, and a reading is not an opening either.

At \place{the kindling} \person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been instructed. “Yes, house,” he said, and went over the \place{the flagstones} as a man goes over ground he has measured in his sleep. The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill, six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the lip where it could not, with the unforged collector sentence beside it. “Six counted. The next I don't count,” he told the wood in the tone of a man stating the weather, and put the knife back where it could be checked. “It's back. You can check,” he said to the empty yard, though the yard had been told and did not answer.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. It was a remembered stopping, not a new one. Nine stoppings stood on their own place and no tenth stood before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven; the Seed 16 breath was drawn at the top of the reach and not spent; the loan was uncalled. His stroke went over the shallow bright patch without the half-check and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning along it. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His chest had been tight since the dark. Above the shed the small sound of the loft settling came to him as a direction and nothing more. The warm stone Sella had given him in the winter Garl died lay back in its unmarked clay pot up there, two years gone and cold long since, and he had stopped trying to reconcile it with the debt, which is not the same thing as forgiving it.

At the edge of \place{the Vail wheel-shed} \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened, and not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate; the broken ones lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady,” he said, and to a pot on the wheel, “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew \lore{the full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing, keeping it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. \lore{the burned-slate word} stood at four on a place of its own, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; he joined none of them to the dust, the pot, the men or the wrong wind, and no name was given to what had ailed the man carried out on Day 188. Three or more men had gone low into \place{the back room} before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. When Sella's sentence went low and unfinished at the hearth his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing --- a remembered repetition, not a change made again, and not paired with any other fraction. The iron was one piece with two bites and no word in Grathic for both mercies. \emph{He's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as the hour it was spoken; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed or into the basket.

At the going of the light the clay road lay up beyond the yard with its ruts holding the last of the day, and no figure came down it from \place{High Kiln} and no collector went up it toward the house. Sella carried him in at the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner; the finer grey called the thaw beside it, unswept and kept apart; the space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} bare and dark and its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got what it got and none got a figure. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did; a thing gone around is still a thing kept, and no number was laid across any of them. Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it had frozen. She said nothing of what had been read over him in the square and gave no instruction to the basket about having been anywhere at all. Then she went to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves.

The small hours came on and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The fire behind the wall was past feeding and nobody stood over it counting; the room where he lay did not touch the room of the fire, and no rhythm crossed from one to the other on its own. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-nine days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, the fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so the counts stayed where they were, exact and unspent and worth nothing at two inches of air.

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. There was no towel, no instruments and no case; there was only the order of the laying, and that was the whole of the discipline. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources --- a cup across a birth room, a pot in the wheel-shed through a finger's width of door, the district's slate twice at \place{the Cross}, and the sixth, the small flat knock out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, from a person, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirty-nine days and none had been invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid: a pot under the shed wall that answered nearly full with air under the note and a want in it; a rack pot lifted a hand's breadth too high and set down again; and the knowing the wheel-keeper kept back from a man come up on a wrong wind. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule --- a pot that sings before its time mostly sings itself out early; the pot that sang early last month had not been heard from since; and a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw, which wrong-wind voices let stand again. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, because a rule is an account that has found a payer, and none had been named to any of them. The burned-slate word stood at four on its own place and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, one in twelve printed and unchallenged and read twice over the basket in the square, and still not the figure paid.

The dressed grain lay under wool near the hearth with no figure over it, and the figure that had gone over the basket that morning did not know what the grain had cost in hands and damp and days of turning. The space between the two had no signature on it and was left apart, not averaged; the carrying arm that knew the grain's weight knew weight and not cost, and had carried the basket up and down this yard for two hundred and thirty-nine days without once entering what it carried. The true subtraction sat locked beyond this roof in a box gone up the clay road, answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him and no offstage death. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end, and still ungiven. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places --- the fraction at the damper, the held beat beneath the shelf, the half-beat on the flagstones --- with the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the pale line read off the carried man at the well on a fifth, and the held inch on a sixth.

Beside the hearings but never among them, the seventh green stayed a colour and the seventh shape stayed a shape. Across the square at \place{the pot board} a silent woman had clay under her nails in the small crescents, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long, the sweetish green of \lore{lead glaze} in the crease beside her lip; she had turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood. At the market's edge a shape in cast-offs had carried a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver on a string that caught the light and went dull; its head had gone toward a set-down pot a half-beat late and stopped in the middle of the turn. No eighth green had come and no eighth shape had come; no second look had been joined to the first, no name had been spoken over either, no loaf had passed, no slate had come near and no sale had been made. The colour had not been laid across the pale line at the well or the line on the carried man, and the shape had not been joined to the boy's own turning. The returning-line place was still gone around with a look and no number.

And on a seventh place of its own, with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the sayings, the tellings, the hearings and the count, the question stood with four askings and no witness. A witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. Four askings are still not one witness; they are four holes in a silence and do not stand up and call themselves a mouth. No fifth asking had come over the boards in the going of the light, and the row stood where it stood.

He put the question to his hand the sixth way of the chapter. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed. There was no breathing number anywhere in the house now to borrow, no fire-sitter standing over the coals and counting, nothing in his room to hold a rhythm against; he took the inch through the small intake, the hold and the letting go of one of the breaths Sella counted at her fire in the room that does not touch his, kept it through the little silence after her count had ended, and brought it down on the river's next low note. Two rhythms crossed in the inch and nowhere else, one held against the other across a wall that does not touch. It was remembered, not a change made again. He filed the coordination on the sixth place with no payer named, and did not spend it and did not explain it. It was not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. Exactness and humiliation were one motion in that small hand and both were true and neither stood up and said what the other was. Once he had reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now he could not close the two inches to a pot handle, lift a hand to his own chin or lift a crust two inches to his mouth. Fifty-four answers stood on the sixth place now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither was promoted into a sign.

Two hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-nine days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name; the widow's mark untouched with no opening in fifty-nine days; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Grain's third and last reading had gone up the clay road behind them and was unread under this roof, and the figure had gone twice over the basket and neither telling had come home with them. Smoke 1 stood three mornings ahead and had not been brought into a day that had not earned it. The file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing; the hand came down on the river's next low note, and the count went forward, exact and unspent, into a day that had not yet earned its heading.
