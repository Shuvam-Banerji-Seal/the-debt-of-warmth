The afternoon thinned without ending. It lost weight the way a banked fire loses light and keeps its heat, and under the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} what was left of it settled behind the pot and came into the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account, and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it. It had neither a command to go further nor a command to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform.

He read the three points from the inside the way he had once read a patient's skin under a light too dim for the rest of the room, and entered the reading nowhere. The adult who had stood at a warming bed and known the number by the slow return of colour took the same measure now off his own wrist, and the child in him did not care what the number was. The child wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand that could close on the pot handle. He got none of the three and went on wanting them. Both registers were true, and as the light went off the wall the want was the louder of the two, because there was nothing left in the afternoon to set the eye to counting. A man can be exact about a thing and still want it, and the wanting does not make the exactness a lie. He had learned that in a white room with a sheet over a body and had carried it here without ever saying it aloud.

Everything in the room had its own way of being kept, and none of them was a story. \person{Garl Vail} was kept in objects. The salt-stiff coat on its peg behind the door held a broader man's shape with a crease at the left shoulder that no hand had smoothed out in two years; the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on the left, which nobody filled; the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its corner lay unswept, and beside it the new, finer hairline of grey that \person[Sella Vail]{Sella} called \lore{the thaw} had been stood over with a broom and not swept. One of his proverbs had gone years ago to a pot with no crack in it, *leave the crack showing*, and was still said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out; the joke had outlived the thing it was true about and cost more now there was nobody to say it in his own voice. The debt against a firing of tiles circled the house's talk without ever being named — gone around the way a woman goes around a pot left on the floor in the dark, a careful circle and no word for the thing circled. His name had not been spoken under this roof in two hundred and thirty-seven days. The keeping of him was maintenance and not a story, and no lesson had been made of any of it; a line left where it was is still a line kept. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} for the tiles on Grain 28, and none had gone down it.

He set these beside the things he kept himself and did not lay one across another. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with no seventh; the coat and the seat and the clean dark line stood in the room and had never asked to be heard. A house that kept a man in a coat and a boy who kept a month's arithmetic in the order of instruments laid on a towel were doing one motion in the dark, each without knowing the other kept anything at all. He did not call it an equality and did not put a finger between them, because the finger would have been the beginning of an account. He had learned what a row of likenesses will do to a man who wants an answer, and he left the rows standing and put nobody under them.

At \place{the kindling} the afternoon went on in the same thin key. Sella gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody — go around what the water left, it had cost enough to come in and would cost more to take out — and \person{Tarn} took it up the way a man takes up a tool that has already been named, saying “Yes, house.” He took nothing on his own authority: not the broom, not the small knife, not even the breath he had been holding since Seed 16. When he spoke to the wood it was in the tone of a man stating the weather. “Six counted. The next I don't count.” And after a moment, to no one: “I owe six.”

The small knife lay at the very edge of its sill. Six years stood in a thumb-mark worn almost flat by use, where the house could check them; the seventh stood at the lip where it could not, and that was the one entry in this house the house could not check. Beside the knife lay the sentence Garl had never forged, about a man sent for a collector who turned back at the frozen ford — the man had been Garl himself, and Garl had given no name to him. Tarn had never entered the sentence and never thrown it away. It lay where a tool lies that a man may one day need and has decided he will not use. He put the knife back with the flat of his hand and went through the motion of checking it with his eyes, because a man who owns nothing should know where the small things are.

His chest had been tight since the dark. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line along the skirting board, and no scrubbing lifted it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. Above the shed the small sound of the loft settling came to the boy as a direction and nothing more. In its unmarked clay pot in \place{the Debt Loft} lay the warm stone Sella had given Tarn in the winter Garl died, two years gone and cold long since, and he could no more reconcile it with the debt than the house's kindness with the account he kept of it. He had stopped trying, which was not forgiving. The stone reached the boy only as that sound with a direction: a place, a pot, a weight settling on a board, and nothing that could be carried down. Fire had gone out of the stone and the debt had not gone anywhere.

The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to Tarn's teeth as he swept. It went all the way to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it, said “Yes, house” to the fire, and went on with the broom. The stopping was a remembered stopping and not a new one: nine of them stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, nine objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath stayed unspent. The loan was not called, and no collector came up or went down the clay road on Grain 28. He agreed so entirely with those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late that no argument could find a foot, and went on wanting the late one because it puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went.

His stroke went over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check it had once made there, and over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, without once turning to look along it. The stone kept the place where the stroke had stopped and the boy kept the unchecking stroke on a tray of its own, as a surgeon keeps a finding that has not yet been repeated. Two ears kept their silence under one roof and neither man put a word between them. Tarn did not look toward the basket and the boy did not ask about the loft; the keeping went on in both directions, each man sure he was the only one doing it.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door off \place{the Vail wheel-shed} in the middle of the afternoon with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and under the nails, white in the creases where a fist had opened. It was not bone-coloured \lore{slip}. He parted the two as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, keeping dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. Three or more men had gone low into the room off the shed before first grey and he gave the house no name for them and did not say the room was cold. The broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing.

He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to anyone. “It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady.” To a pot on the wheel he said “You'll do,” in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under the note and a want in it, and withheld the knowing, setting it on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them. Three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings and no rule; \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at four on a place of its own, a direction over the boards from \place{Slate Row} that had arrived nowhere, and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and did not become three. No fifth saying stood up, no third telling of the shut room, no fourth likeness made a cause, and no name was given to what ailed the man carried out on Day 188. No pot was lost in the yard.

He grew quieter as the afternoon went on, and the household straightened around the quiet without an order, the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it. The quiet went before him into the shed the way a cold air goes before a door. When Sella's sentence at \place{the hearth} went low and unfinished — “It wants —” — his bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of it, took the cold out of the fingertip, held until the end of the sentence arrived at the pot, moved a hair's breadth and was left standing. It was a remembered repetition and not a change made again, and the boy filed it on its own place, paired with no other fraction of the kind. He read the iron again as one piece with two bites, the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man, with no word in Grathic for both mercies and joined to no kiln. “He's not Vail” stayed sealed where it had been sealed on the hour it was spoken, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn. He did not look toward the bed or the basket, and the boy did not ask about the room, and the two silences stayed unjoined.

\scenebreak

At the going of the light Sella carried him home at the half-pace, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. She gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” Her carrying arm was a record in muscle that knew weight and not cost; it had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and thirty-seven days and the dressed grain under its wool through these same days, and kept both weights and the price of neither. The burn shaped like a little door closed on the handle and opened again as she shifted him.

She went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, \place{the trough} bare and dark beside \place{the yard well}, the space under the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, the clean dark line, the finer grey called the thaw beside it, the field's empty place at \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come back. Seven things standing in a row were still seven and not an account until a payer stood under them, and none did. She had gone around them for twelve days with a look and a wide step, and the number had never been spoken over them and never would be, because a row does not become an account until somebody wants it to be one and is willing to put a man under it. She put nobody under it. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-seven days, because a shelf, an afternoon, a yard, a basket and a pot are not openings, and nothing under this roof had been given a name that could be entered.

At the low angle she set him on \place{the bed} and not in the Debt Loft, put the fold right without a look at his face, left the thread frozen, and went to her own bed before the small hours. The fire went on in one room and the river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

\scenebreak

In the small hours the three counts kept themselves and did not become one. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-seven days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three instruments that would not read against one another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing at two paces of air. The six hearings stood beside and never among with no seventh in two hundred and thirty-seven days and none invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The three near-full answers stood on their own tray. Beside them the three shapings of the pot saying stayed sayings; the burned-slate word held at four, the two shut-room tellings at two, the told bell beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, the told figure beside the paid, and the true subtraction locked beyond the roof answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end; the Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled; the three held changes stood on three places, the loft scrape on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, with no eighth of either, no second look joined, no name spoken over either, no loaf, no slate and no sale. On a seventh place of its own the question stood with three askings and no witness; a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured near the hearth; the returning-line place had gone around with a look and no number. Two hundred and thirty-seven stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-seven days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days. He had kept nothing and spent nothing and joined nothing.

He put the second question of the chapter to his hand under the conditions that had sometimes answered. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed for the small intake, and came down before the letting-go. The fiftieth answer went onto the sixth place with no payer named. It was not spent and it was not explained: not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Fifty answers stood there now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand came down before the letting-go while the fire behind the wall missed its fifth.
