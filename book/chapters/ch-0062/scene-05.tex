\daymark{239}{Grain 30, Tenthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours gave \person[Corin]{the boy} the count before they gave him anything to set against it. Two hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}; six months and fifty-nine days told by nobody; \lore{the carried fire}'s fifth unpaid the same number of days, its payer without a name. He lay under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof at the low angle of \place{the loaf shelf} with the fold at his ear, and the room delivered its smells in the old order --- hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, and beneath both the sour mineral breath of the unswept corner \person{Sella Vail} called \lore{the thaw}. The first sound was the kiln drawing in fours with a gap, not a bell. A bell would come later, from \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; a bell is a thing a morning has to earn, and this morning had earned it. Grain 30 stood here now, one morning ahead at the previous close and arrived at last, the third and last Account-day and market of the month. He had not brought it early into a day that had not earned it, and now that it was earned he did not make more of its arriving than of any other morning's.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear and would lie there into the light; the empty place beside it kept its name to itself, and no third came to make the row come out even. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped where it had stopped the night before, at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as though it had come to the end of a line written out for it and had neither an order to go further nor an order to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and the second was louder in the small hours, because a child's hunger keeps no hours at all and the adult's register goes on being exact while the body in which it sits goes on being hungry.

He turned out the file the way a man lays instruments on a towel, and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, with the sixth out of a person and no seventh in two hundred and thirty-nine days and none invented to fill the empty place; three near-full answers stood on their own tray; three shapings of the pot saying stood as sayings, \lore{the burned-slate word} at four, the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war at two; the told bell lay beside the heard and the told figure beside the paid. On a seventh place of its own the question about the row kept its four askings and no witness, a witness being a thing that stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. He did not lay any of it out differently because a market stood on the other side of the door. An instrument is the same instrument on a market morning, and a figure that has been read over a basket twice is still not the figure that was paid.

Sella went to the fire before the basket. She told the wool to keep the river wind off his ears, told the fire it was the thaw, and gave the instruction to the pot and the broom and never to the basket, because the basket had not been given an instruction and had no need of one. She turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand, counting under her breath toward no opening --- small intake, hold, letting go --- a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals when she pushed the coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn. The dressed grain lay near her under its wool with no figure over it, counted in glances and handfuls and by a carrying arm that knew weight and not cost; she gave nobody a number for it and laid nothing across the empty places. \lore{the widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-nine days, because a hearth, a shelf, a basket, a yard and a pot are not openings, and a reading is not an opening either.

Then she carried him down. It was a half-pace from every open slate, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of \lore{water over water}. At every slate she kept the half-pace and went wide, and she carried him the way a woman carries a pot filled that must not be slopped, level, with the little-door burn shut on nothing and the carrying arm doing the arithmetic the mouth would not. She said nothing to the basket about where they were going and gave it no reason for going; the instruction went to the wool and the river wind, and that was the whole of what the basket was told.

\scenebreak

\place{the Cross} was already loud when they came into it. Two hundred people leaned over \place{the reading block} under \place{the moot-post}, and eight pigs went over the wet boards with a slate kept beside them so that a beast could be entered before anybody could ask which beast was meant; figures were brought to a heap of turnips and left standing side by side without being made to agree. Sella set the basket in the moot-post's finger's width of thin shade where no slate lay, with his cheek to the cold iron of the post, and stood half a pace off. She gave no instruction to the basket and no reason to it, and turned her face toward \place{the pot board} and \place{the square} the way a woman turns a pot so the light falls where she wants it, and did not look into the fold.

He saw what a man carried into a square can be made to stand for. The figure that would be read here did not know what the grain under the wool at home had cost in hands and damp and days of turning; it did not know the empty place in the fold, or the rack space with its broken slates, or the place in the returning line where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Those things were keepings, and a keeping is not a figure until somebody stands under it to pay, and nobody stood. The yard had gone around that place for so many days now that going around it had become the shape of the yard itself, and no number had come of the going. Across the square everything the district had withheld all month stood together in one place and none of it was about to be read.

\person{Berr Clay} came up short off \place{the clay road} to the reading block. The red wool cord of his office was folded square under his coat instead of worn across it, and the green of tasted \lore{lead glaze} stood at one corner of his mouth; under the hot clay of him was the smell of nothing at all, which is the most expensive smell in \place{Grath}. His hands were large and clean. He wiped his thumb twice on his apron before touching nothing, once for the slate and once for the figure that would be on it, and held a narrow slate unopened to his forearm with the writing turned inward where no goer-by could take a figure off it. He passed a finger over the figure and let his eyes rest on the writing a breath longer than the writing required; then he raised them to the crowd, and the order of it was the order of a surgeon entering a field --- the record first, the instrument checked, and only then the faces. The boy had watched that order in a midwife laying slates before turning to a fold, in a clerk with hand to slate before eye to stalls, in a runner settling her strap before her eyes took in the rail, and now in this man, and he kept the orders on separate places and did not join them into an account, for a row of such hands will call itself a rule if a man leaves it standing long enough to want an answer.

Berr read low into the top of the crowd. “one in twelve, as the notice says.” The figure went twice over the basket, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and neither of those tellings came down into the basket or asked what was under the fold. He said, “The levy is called. It is not called by me,” and to a man at the rail who had brought a number up out of a fist he said a number goes into a slate and not into a bundle, and that if \emph{your house} wanted it again there would be a slate at the watch. \emph{Your house}, never \emph{I}. He took himself out of his own sentence as neatly as a surgeon takes his hand out of a field before the dressing goes on, and the crowd, having to lean in to hear a voice that never rose, leaned and heard and went back to the pigs. He did not look into the basket. He was not called for the man carried out on Day 188, and nothing he read knew what that man cost or what ailed him; he was not called for the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 either. He went off toward \place{High Kiln} with the red cord folded square, the narrow slate under his arm and the box locked with the true subtraction inside, believing he had done the district a kindness. The boy watched the kindness go up the road, exact and complete and worth nothing at two paces of air.

The district's arithmetic changed for the third time in Grain where two hundred people stood, and could not be changed back. The printed figure stood unchallenged. A figure read aloud was still not the figure paid, and the figure over the basket was not the figure over the dressed grain under wool at home, and the space between the two had no signature on it and was left apart, not averaged.

The bell went from the Cross. Four, four and a gap. Nobody crossed himself. The boy kept the heard bell beside the told bell of the Long Night, where it had lain all year, and did not let the two become one sound; a bell that is told in the dark and a bell that is heard in a market are two instruments, and even a gap in the counting is not a fifth stroke until somebody stands under it to pay for the stroke.

\scenebreak

Before noon \person{Hessa Rale} took the slate up the clay road toward \place{Greybank}. She came through the square with Berr's narrow slate held edge-on to her forearm and the writing turned to her sleeve, her hand arriving at the slate before her eyes arrived at the crowd, the small flat correction of the strap and the turn of the wrist bringing the edge round; the second strap was let out for the long road and the mud of it had dried in a pale scurf on her coat shortened at the skirt, and she did not brush the scurf off, because a runner's coat is a tool and nobody scours a tool for its own sake. Her thumb lay along the grain of the frame and nothing pressed where the writing was. A woman at the rail asked what she carried.

“I don't read it. I carry it,” Hessa said. “There's a difference, and the road doesn't care which.”

The woman wanted more and Hessa gave her the road instead: Cross to Greybank, reading up before noon, a road to walk. She answered the question put and not the one behind it, without rudeness and without apology, and went on at the pace of a person who has made a bargain with distance and keeps it without thinking. No figure came off the slate under any roof in \place{Sill}. Her order of reaching stayed the eighth such hand in the district, on a place of its own, advanced and not paid; the boy did not join it to Berr's twice-wiped thumb or to the clerk's inward slate or to any keeping, and he did not make her a reader because she carried a figure she would not look at. Eight likenesses held apart by hand are still eight, and a rule wants a payer, and none had been named to any of them.

Below \place{the north bridge} the soft-cuffed clerk was still set at \place{the out-of-line landing} for a measure nobody called. His cuff was grey from washing and grey again by midday; the slate lay to his forearm with the writing inward, and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, entering a buyer's number before he looked up to find the man who had said it. The market sold its pots and its eels over the top of him as water goes over a stone that lies where a stone lies, and nobody stepped around him and nobody took him off. The stone does not rise and does not ask what it is set there for. The boy set the clerk's order of reaching on its own place beside the runner's and the thane's, and joined none of the three, because records going inward are records going inward and a row of such hands will call itself an account if it is left standing long enough.

Across the square at the pot board the seventh green stayed a colour at a silent woman's mouth. She had clay under her nails in the small crescents, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had once been worn tight and long, and the sweetish green of lead glaze in the crease beside her lip; she turned a cup a quarter without lifting it and set it back exactly where it had stood, and said nothing all morning. At the market's edge the seventh shape stayed a shape in cast-offs worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy who was never measured for one --- a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the creases of the wrists, a gap where a front tooth had been, and a green sliver of lead glaze on a string at the throat that caught the light when the shape turned and went dull when it turned back. When a pot was set down near the eel shade its head went toward the sound a half-beat late and stopped in the middle of the turn; a thumb went to the sliver and rubbed it, and the mouth opened on the tail of a sentence and let out nothing any clerk could take down. No eighth green came and no eighth shape came; no second look was joined to the first, no name was spoken over either, no loaf passed, no slate came near the basket and no sale was made. He filed the colour on one place and the shape on another with a finger between them and the hearings, and he did not call either by a name that would let a reader find it.

The dressed grain lay under wool near \place{the hearth} at home with no figure over it, and here in the square a figure went over the top of the basket that did not know what that grain had cost. The two stood apart with no signature between them. The reading answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name and no offstage death, and still no name for what had ailed him. Nothing the district gathered into its square that morning knew the hand that had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come back out. The market would end as it had begun, in arithmetic that did not balance, and the one person who could have given a figure for the grain was carrying it home under wool and had given nobody a number.

\scenebreak

At the market's edge, while a green sliver went dull and a cup was set back where it stood, he put the fifth question to his hand. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the river's low note and stayed. There was no breathing number anywhere near him to borrow, no count in the room that did not touch his and no wall to hold a rhythm against out here in the open square; he held the inch to the river's own low interval, the water going over water through the finger's-width of the morning, and brought it down on the next low note the river gave. The two things crossed only in the inch and nowhere else. It was filed on the sixth place with no payer named, not a hearing and not the note of water over water promoted among the hearings, not a longer reach, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power and not a rule. He did not spend it and did not explain it. Fifty-three answers stood now on the sixth place, each an object and not a power, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The child in him wanted the crust. The adult kept the tray. Neither stood up and said what the other was.

At the going of the light she carried him home. She said nothing of what had been read over him, and gave the instruction to the wool and the river wind and never to the basket. Going up \place{the clay road} she kept the half-pace from every open slate and went wide around the seven walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the clean dark line at the Vail corner; the finer grey called the thaw beside it, unswept and kept apart; the space beneath \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} with its broken birth-slates; the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, no crumb, no print, no chip and no word; \place{the yard well} with \place{the trough} bare and dark and its water called weather; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out with the rest. Each got what it got and none got a figure. Seven walked-around things standing in a row are still seven and not an account until a payer stands under them, and none did; a thing gone around is still a thing kept, and no number was laid across any of them.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right without a look at his face, and left the thread frozen where it had frozen. The dressed grain was where she had left it under its wool with no figure over it. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of water over water; under the floor the carried fire gave four low pulls and withheld the fifth; overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because a man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three and no payer stood to any. The question stood over the boards with four askings and no owner, and the row stood where it stood. No voice came over the boards in the going of the light and no fifth asking was made under the roof.

Two hundred and thirty-nine stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill and Grath; six months and fifty-nine days told by nobody; the fire's fifth unpaid the same number of days; the widow's mark untouched with no opening in fifty-nine days; the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Grain's last reading had gone up the clay road in Berr's locked box and could not be changed back, and the figure had gone twice over the basket and neither telling had come home with them. The basket went under the fold with the figure heard twice and the weight of neither carried into the house. The count went forward, and nothing under the roof had been joined, named, opened, called, spent or ended.
