\daymark{237}{Grain 28, Eighthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay against the fold where it had come down on the river's next low note, and \person[Corin]{the boy} set the coordination down where a man sets a tool on a bench at the end of the work. It was not watched. It was not named. It was not asked to stand up and say what it was. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close the two inches to the pot handle, and both were true and neither was a sign; the two rhythms held one against the other across a wall that does not touch went onto the sixth place with the other answers, an object laid with objects, exact, complete, worth nothing. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof fire went on in one room and river in the other, and neither stopped for the ear under the roof.

He was two hundred and thirty-seven days old and six months and fifty-seven days, and nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} said either number in his hearing or over his head. The count had gone forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-six to two hundred and thirty-seven with no hand to enter it and no voice to say so, exact, complete, unspent. Hot clay came first under the roof in \place{Low Kiln}, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. There was no market on an Eighthday and no Cross and no bell; Grain 30 stood two mornings off and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-seven days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any of them, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. The surgeon's register took the three in at a glance and filed them apart, the way he had once filed three wounds from one blow that had no single instrument behind them.

\person{Sella Vail} had gone to her own bed before the small hours and left the coals to keep themselves, and there was no breathing number anywhere in the house. The two rooms did not touch. In the room that was not his the fire kept its own time under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, and in the room that was not hers the river kept its low note through the gap, and between them a wall stood that had never been given a door. Nothing in his room held a count for him to borrow. He had learned to keep a rhythm without the sound, as a man keeps the step of a road he has walked a thousand times, but he borrowed nothing from the room that does not touch his and did not try. The dark went on being the dark, and the cold went on stopping where it had stopped, and he lay with the fold at his ear and listened to three things that would not become one.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from that ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account, and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped. Nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left.

Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back. It had come to the end of an order written out for it and had neither a command to go further nor a command to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the afterdrop when he saw it. He wanted the crust. Both were true, and in the small hours the second was the louder, because there was nothing in the dark to set the eye other things to count. The adult who had once stood at a warming bed and known the number by the patient's skin took the same reading now from the inside of his own wrist and entered it nowhere. There was no hand to carry the finding to a slate, and no slate under this roof would have known what it counted.

A body read at two paces is exact and worth nothing without a hand to carry it; a body read from the inside is the same finding with the same worthless exactness. He had spent a life learning what a cold patient's skin means and had no way to spend the knowledge on himself. The child in him did not care about the worth of it. The child in him wanted the crust, and wanted the thread wiped, and wanted a hand that could close two inches, and got none of the three, and went on wanting them with a patience the adult had long ago stopped calling patience and had instead entered as a fact.

\scenebreak

Nothing from the old life broke through into the small hours. No voice came with the grammar of a different country, no lit rectangle with a name on it, no file in a white room; the surgeon was present only as a way of noticing, and the noticing was enough. He parted a colour from a hearing the way he had once parted clot from vessel, and a shape from a seventh of a note, and the figure read over a covered basket from the figure paid, and let each lie on the place it had earned. It was a dry and unsentimental business, and it cost him something that he could not name and did not try to name. He had a joke about it that he did not laugh at, because a laugh costs more than a man in his position can spare. The child in him wanted the crust and the adult kept the trays, and the two went on arguing without a word between them.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of \person[Dessa Silt] as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never among the sources. No seventh had come in two hundred and thirty-seven days and none was invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. Three near-full answers stood on their own tray, advanced and unpaid. Beside them stood three shapings of the pot saying, all sayings and no rule; \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at four on a place of its own with a hand between it and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three.

The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself, and the bell of Grain 30 was not lent to a day that had not earned it. The told figure lay beside the paid: one in twelve read low into the crowd on Grain 10 and again on Grain 20, the figure going twice over the basket each time, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and still not the figure paid. Grain 20's arithmetic had been changed and could not be changed back, and its tarnish was kept on its own place, held apart from the arithmetic actually paid, the way a chart's contradiction is held apart from the signature that would close it. The true subtraction stayed locked in \person{Berr Clay}'s box beyond the roof and answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188: no figure, no collector, no slate, no cause, no name for what ailed him, no offstage death entered.

Nine stoppings stood on Reed 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26 and 29, nine objects and not a rule, with no tenth before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft-sill scrape on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, gathered at Grain's first market and carried unjoined through the working mornings since; no eighth of either had come, no second look was joined, no name was spoken over either, no loaf passed, no slate came near and no sale was made. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured near the hearth. The returning-line place had gone around with a look and no number.

On a seventh place of its own, with a finger between each pair and a finger between them and the sayings, the tellings, the hearings and the count, the question stood with three askings and nobody to own them. Three askings are not one witness; a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. He did not make a fourth of them to fill the silence. A question about a row asks for a place, a place wants a witness, and a witness wants a day when somebody stands somewhere and can be asked; he kept the want where it was and put nobody under it.

Likenesses were taken down one at a time before they could stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer, and he had put no payer under any of these. The three pot sayings stayed sayings. The three near-full answers stayed advanced and unpaid. The burned-slate word stayed a direction over the boards and not a fourth saying, and the counting room stayed a room shut in the war and not a third telling. He had learned here that a row is not an account until somebody stands under it, and that a man who leaves a row standing long enough to want an answer will put somebody under it before he knows what he has done. He left the rows standing and put nobody under them. He had kept nothing and spent nothing and joined nothing, and the file lay open on the towel complete and worth nothing at two paces of air.

\scenebreak

Before first grey the house began its ordinary work, and the work went to everything in it except the basket. Sella came to the fire before she came to him. She gave the instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket, which had never been given an instruction and had no need of one. She said the house where another woman would have said I, told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot. “The house will keep it,” she said. The fire answered four low pulls behind the wall and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-seven days, and she went on feeding it as though it had said nothing at all, because a fire that misses a breath is a fire and not an argument.

She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand and counted under her breath toward no opening, small intake, hold, letting go, a count she kept toward nothing and had never offered to any ear under this roof. The wall stood between that count and his, and he took its length only as a man takes the length of a shift he has worked a thousand times; nothing crossed from that room that could be entered. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn, and the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At \place{the loaf shelf} she moved her lips along the two-gapped row without a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch. Then she kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required and not looking toward \place{the bed}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-seven days, because a morning, a shelf, a yard, a basket and a pot are not openings.

At \place{the hearth} she turned the dressed grain under its wool for damp. Her fingers went into it to the knuckle and came out dry, and she spread it thin on the flat stone to finish and gathered it back by the handful, and set the fold flat over it again. It changed state and gained no figure. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, which knew weight and not cost, and entered it on no slate and laid it across none of the absences. She had carried it past the racks and the well on the same mornings she had carried him, and the arm kept both weights and the price of neither.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. “Ask the pot,” Sella said. “The weather knows the rest.” She carried the unanswered question away level, the way a woman carries a full dish across a room, so that nothing slopped and nothing was entered. Later, coming down \place{the clay road} in the morning's thin light, a man brought a number up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so the figure arrived nowhere. \place[the trough]{The trough} stood bare and dark beside it and its water was called weather. In \place{the Clay Yard} the clean dark line and the finer grey beside it lay unswept, and she went around both without a broom. The arithmetic that had been read over the covered grain on Grain 20 kept its tarnish on its own place; the arithmetic paid kept its place beside it; and the true subtraction stayed beyond the roof in a box nobody under this roof had opened.

At the low angle of the shelf she set the basket down, put the fold right without a look at his face and left the thread frozen, and went to the morning's work with her burn shut on nothing. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note. The first smell under the roof was hot clay under tallow, wet wool under that, sour Ash under all three; the first sound was the kiln in fours with its gap. No bell had gone and none would go before Grain 30.

She had carried the cost-question away level toward the pot and nothing in it had been given a figure. He put the first question of the chapter to his hand under the conditions that had sometimes answered. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and stayed for the small intake, and came down at once against the fold. The forty-ninth answer went onto the sixth place with no payer named. It was not spent and it was not explained: not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Forty-nine answers stood there now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand came down at once against the fold while Sella carried the unanswered cost-question away level toward the pot, and the count went forward without a voice.
