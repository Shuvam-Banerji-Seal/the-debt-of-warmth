\daymark{238}{Grain 29, Ninthday, Y.H. 813}

The small hours came on without a boundary drawn anywhere under the roof. They arrived the way heat arrives in a banked fire: not as a thing that began but as a thing that had been going on longer than the ear had been listening. \person[Corin]{the boy} came into his two hundred and thirty-eighth day with nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath} saying either that or the other thing, six months and fifty-eight days, and the count went forward in the dark from two hundred and thirty-seven with no hand to enter it. It stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody. A number kept in a room where nobody keeps numbers is still a number kept. He had stopped expecting anyone to say it, which was not the same as not wanting it, and the wanting went on under the expectation the way a coal goes on under ash.

Hot clay came first under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof, hot clay under tallow and wet wool under that, and under all three the sour \lore{Ash} breath of the unswept corner the house called \lore{the thaw}. The day's first sound was not a bell. Behind the wall the kiln drew in four low pulls and stopped where the fifth should have stood, and the stopping stood where it stood with nobody under any roof to put a name to what stood in it. There was no market on a Ninthday and no Cross and no bell; Grain 30 stood one morning ahead and was not brought into a day that had not earned it.

He used the three counts the way a man uses three instruments that will not read against one another. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} gave four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been, its payer without a name for two hundred and thirty-eight days. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a fifth low thing never counted among the hearings. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Fire under the floor, river through the door, nails overhead: three counts in three places, and none rose into another. A man who wanted them to agree would have to put a payer under all three, and no payer stood to any, so he kept them on three trays and let the trays lie where they lay. The surgeon's register took the three in at a glance and filed them apart, the way he had once filed three wounds from one blow with no single instrument behind them.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear. The empty place beside it kept its name to itself. No third came to make the row come out even, because a kindness with a figure on it is the beginning of an account, and this house had learned to give without entering. The thread at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped; nobody under any roof had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had stopped at cheek, at temple, at the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, as if it had come to the end of a sentence written out for it. It had neither a command to go further nor a command to leave. The afterdrop waited on the far side of all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He read the three points from the inside and entered the reading nowhere. The adult who had once known the number by the slow return of colour under a light too dim for the rest of the room took the same measure off his own wrist, and the child in him did not care what the number was. The child wanted the crust lifted two inches, the thread wiped, a hand that could close on the pot handle. He got none of the three and went on wanting them. Both registers were true, and the want was the louder, because there was nothing in the small hours to set the eye to counting.

He turned out the file as a man lays instruments on a towel and found it exact, complete and worth nothing. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth out of a person, with no seventh in two hundred and thirty-eight days and none invented to fill the empty place, for an empty place kept empty is a finding and a finding filled to make a row come out even is a finding forged. The three near-full answers stood on their own tray. Beside them the three shapings of the pot saying stayed sayings; \lore{The burned-slate word} stood at four on a place of its own with a hand between it and the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war, which stayed two and did not become three. The told bell lay beside the heard, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the told figure lay beside the paid, and the true subtraction stayed locked beyond the roof answering nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year, which stood at its very end and had still not given. The Seed 16 breath was unspent and the loan uncalled. Three held changes stood on three places, the loft scrape on a fourth, the carried man's pale line on a fifth and the held inch on a sixth. Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green was a colour and the seventh shape was a shape, with no eighth of either, no second look joined, no name spoken over either, no loaf, no slate and no sale. On a seventh place of its own the question stood with three askings and no witness; a witness stands somewhere and can be asked, and none did and none could. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured near \place{the hearth}; the returning-line place had gone around with a look and no number. He had kept nothing and spent nothing and joined nothing.

He put the third question of the chapter to his hand under the conditions that had sometimes answered. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the low note through the finger's-width door and was taken through the small intake and the hold, and came down before the letting-go had finished. The fifty-first answer went onto the sixth place with no payer named. It was not spent and it was not explained: not a longer reach, not a miss entered, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a tenth stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, not a power, not a rule. Fifty-one answers stood there now, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. The hand came down before the counted breath was done while the fire behind the wall missed its fifth.

\scenebreak

At the hearth in the morning \person[Sella Vail]{Sella} went to the fire before the basket and gave her instruction to the wool, the fire, the pot, the broom, the running water and the river wind, and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” She told the fire it was the thaw and turned the coals with the back of a broken pot, and the fire answered four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have been. She said “The house will keep it” where another woman would have said \emph{I}, stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening — small intake, hold, letting go — pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so that the grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed.

Then she turned the dressed grain under its wool for damp. Her fingers went into it to the knuckle and came out dry, and she spread it thin on the flat stone to finish and stood over it a while with her hands at her sides, looking at what the year had come to in a thin layer on a stone. It was the first cut grain, dressed on the flagstones with a flail of ash-wood and leather, winnowed so the chaff went up on the river wind, nine days under wool near the hearth. She counted it in glances and handfuls and by the carrying arm's weight, which knew weight and not cost; the same arm that had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and thirty-eight days had carried the grain past the racks and the well on the same mornings, and kept both weights and the price of neither. It changed state under her hands and gained no figure. She entered it on no slate and laid it across none of the absences — not across the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood, not across the empty place at the worked strips, not across the space beneath the lowest rack with its broken birth-slates, not across the fold's one crust and its unnamed empty place in the room the shelf did not touch. A woman who lays one absence across another to make them come out even will soon want somebody to stand under the sum, and there was nobody in this house who would stand.

A neighbour came to the threshold with a pot hitched against her hip and asked what the storing would take now the grain was under wool. “Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there,” Sella said. “The house has said it.” The woman had wanted a number and was given the weather; she shifted the pot to her other hip and went off with it, and nothing entered the house. The boy lay at the low angle and watched the question go the way such questions went in this district — out of a fist, into a mouth, onto a shoulder, into the pot, into the weather, arriving nowhere with nobody standing under it. He had learned to read that route the way he had once read the route of a note from the nurse who wrote it to the hand that signed past it. A question answered with the pot is not a question refused. It is a question that has found nobody to stand under its answer, and a clerk can enter only what somebody stands under.

A man came down \place{the clay road} with a number brought up out of a fist toward \place{the yard well}, looked at it as though it might have grown legs and gone away of itself, and put it back into his sleeve. The figure arrived nowhere. It did not go into the well's dark water, which was called weather by those who did not taste it; it did not go into a slate, for there was no slate within reach of any hand that morning; it went back where it had come from, into the closed fist and up the sleeve, and the yard went on without it as a body goes on with one small thing missing. On Grain 10 and again on Grain 20 a figure had been read twice over the covered grain, once for the moot and once for the households that came late, and the printed one in twelve stood unchallenged. That figure read was not the figure paid. The read figure knew nothing of the hands that dressed the grain or the cost of bringing it in; the true subtraction lay in the locked box that \person{Berr Clay} had carried up the clay road toward \place{High Kiln}, and it answered nothing for the man carried out on Day 188. He had kept the read figure and the paid figure on two places with the space between them left open, as he had once left a contradiction on a chart open rather than close it with a signature. No figure came for the grain under the wool and no figure came for the hand that had not come back out of the strip. Two absences standing side by side are not a subtraction until somebody puts a man under them, and nobody did.

A wrong wind came up off High Kiln in the middle of the morning. It came the way a wrong wind always came in Grath: from the direction where the glaze rooms stood, warm and a little sweet, carrying the green edge of \lore{lead glaze} ahead of it like a smell that had forgotten to ask permission. It went over \place{the drying racks}, over the boards fronting \place{the loaf shelf}, and touched the fold where the dressed grain lay. The sweet edge lay on the wool for the length of a breath and went out on the wind that brought it. A direction arrived nowhere. It was not a second edge paid, and no man stood under it to say what it had cost or who had tasted it. He held it as a colour and not a cause.

Beside the hearings but never among them the seventh green stayed a colour. It had been gathered at Grain's first market at the mouth of a silent woman across the square: clay under the nails in the small crescents, a pale line across the back of one hand where something had long been worn tight, the sweetish green in the crease beside her lip, a cup turned a quarter without lifting and set back exactly where it had stood. The wind brought the edge of that colour and no woman, no mouth, no eighth sighting. The seventh shape stayed a shape: cast-offs worn the way a uniform is worn by a boy never measured for one, a man's shoulders on a child's frame, breeches cut off at the knee, silt dried pale in the wrist creases, a gap where a front tooth had been, a green sliver on a string catching the light and going dull, a head that went toward a set-down pot a half-beat late and stopped in the middle of the turn. No eighth shape came on the wind and no second look was joined to the first. He named neither. There was no name for the colour and no name for the shape, and no name spoken over either now; no woman and no child was called by a name in the telling of them, no loaf passed, no slate came near, no sale was made and no romance gathered interest where none had been entered. Six greens behind a seventh green make a row of greens, and six shapes behind a seventh shape make a row of shapes, and a row is not an account until somebody finds a payer to stand under it. He had put nobody under either row.

On the same wrong wind one of the three shapings of the pot saying stood again over the boards: *a pot gone quiet on every row come the thaw*. It came as a saying and went as a saying, with no owner, no room, no hand at the foot of it and no reason, and the voice went on to the price of barley and the weather. Sella, who had been moving her lips along a row with two gaps, did not turn toward it. The boy let the shaping stand where the other two stood, with a finger between each pair and no finger laid across: sings itself out early, not heard from since, gone quiet on every row. Three likenesses must not stand up and call themselves a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer, and no payer stood to any of the three. No fourth likeness was made a cause. The burned-slate word stood at four tellings and no fifth came; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stayed two and did not become three. The row stood where it stood, and nobody leaned in to take anything down.

\scenebreak

The morning went on without arriving at anything. At \place{the loaf shelf} Sella moved her lips along the two-gapped row without a figure, the way a woman runs a thumb along a hem to find the tear and does not say what she found: the old space at the row's end, and the palm's breadth that had come in on a night nobody named, and no third. Her eye went once to \place{the clay jar} cold under its glaze on the wall shelf, and her hand started toward it and shut half over nothing without a touch. Then she kept her finger on top of the shelf's front edge so the charcoal tally beneath the board stayed unread, uncounted and uncopied, and set the pot rim out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, letting her hand rest a half-beat longer at the setting than the setting required and not looking toward \place{the bed}. The half-beat lay on a place of its own among the older intervals, an object and not a change made again. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, touched once before the kiln on Ember 1 and at no hour since. There had been no opening in fifty-eight days, because a shelf, a strip, a rack, a yard, a basket and a pot are not openings, and nothing under this roof had been given a name that could be entered. A reading was not an opening, and Grain's third arithmetic had not yet been read under this roof; it stood one morning ahead, unbrought into a day that had not earned it.

She carried him twice that morning at the half-pace between the hearth and the shelf and the drying racks and back, woodsmoke before her in long low bars and river wind behind with the taste of water over water, giving the instruction to the wool and the wind and never to the basket. “Keep it off his ears. It's the river wind.” Her carrying arm was a record in muscle that knew weight and not cost; it had carried him up and down this yard for two hundred and thirty-eight days and the dressed grain under its wool through these same days, and kept both weights and the price of neither. She went wide around the walked-around places with a look and a wide step and no number: the round ring, the bare dark \place{the trough} beside the well, the space under the lowest rack, the clean dark line and the finer grey called the thaw beside it, the field's empty place at \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and not come back. Seven things standing in a row were still seven and not an account until a payer stood under them, and none did. She put nobody under them, and she did not lay any of them across the grain.

Two hundred and thirty-eight stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody; six months and fifty-eight days were told by nobody; the carried fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days, and the question over the boards stood with three askings and no witness. The file lay open on the towel and was worth nothing at two paces of air. He had kept nothing and spent nothing and joined nothing, and the count stood where he had set it, exact and unspent, with the next morning still one morning ahead and unbrought.

At the hearth she gathered the dressed grain back by the handful from the flat stone where it had lain spread to finish, and poured it into its cover, and set the fold flat over it again with the flat of her hand. It was the same grain and the same wool and the same weight in the carrying arm; nothing had been added to it and no figure had come near it. The cover went back flat over the dressed grain while the sweet edge of glaze went out on the wind that brought it.
