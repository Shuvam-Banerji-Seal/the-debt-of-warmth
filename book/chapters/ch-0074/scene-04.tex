The afternoon went out of the day the way heat goes out of a pot set off the coals: not at a stroke, but by the slow returning of the same things to the same places, and nobody counting the degrees. Heat drew back out of the fold where \person[Corin]{the boy} lay and went to sit behind the pot with the rest of the warmth this house kept in objects and never in numbers. There was no market on a Fifthday and no Cross and no bell, and the yard went on with its work the way a pot goes on cooling on a shelf, without being told to.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} had ever been given an instruction about the thread, and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-four days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Clay Yard} the light lay thin and level, the way a thin slip lies on a board before the hand works it, and the day neither ended nor seemed about to. Frost had written white fronds on the stone lip of \place{the yard well} where \lore{the Warm} had lately gone out, and the yard called the fronds \lore{the thaw} by noon, as it called the water in \place{the trough} weather though the trough stood bare and dark and the water in it was only the sky come down to the bottom of a hole. The well rope was mudded to the last strand. Nobody lifted it to look at the white lip and nobody put a name to who had written there.

\person{Ilva} was at the well drawing water. She was of the yard and not of a house, and she drew with the pail against her knee, the way a woman draws who has done it ten thousand times and will do it ten thousand more, and who knows the weight of the thing before her hand closes on the rope. Clay was dried pale in the crescents of her nails, and the finer grey dust of the racks was worked into the seam of one thumb, and she did not scrub the seam out. It was a keeping of a kind, though she would not have called it one; the dust was where the work had put it and there was no instruction to take it out.

A woman came up with a pot and asked what the month's firings had taken. The boy lay at the edge of the work where no slate lay, and he read the order of the answer the way he read the order of a pulse: the hand first, the eyes after, the mouth last.

Ilva's hand went toward her sleeve and stayed there.

The short strip of birch bark did not come out. It was not taken out, read, asked about, or taken a figure from; it was not laid to the charcoal under the shelf, the cord turned so its knots stood, the knots, the six pages, the tins, the missing hand or the pale line where a ring had once been worn tight and long. The count stayed in wood, where no slate could take a figure off it and no Court thumb could read it. Her eyes went to the woman who had asked, and only then did her mouth open, and what it answered was the question put and not the one behind it.

“Ask the pot what it cost. I keep the count, not the account.”

The woman went off with her pot and her question still on her, nothing priced, refused or entered, and Ilva went on with her water. Nobody in the yard turned to look at her twice. The boy filed no tenth record-first hand. The ninth stayed on its own place with a finger between it and the eighth, nine objects and not a rule, for a rule is an account that has found a payer and none stood to this one. He neither read the stick nor asked after it nor took a figure from it. She did not know she had been read, and went on drawing as a woman draws who has never once considered being read. She was not \person{Marra}, not \person{Hessa Rale}, not the soft-cuffed clerk set below the north bridge, not an officer, and she was joined to none of them.

She kept her count in wood because a slate would want a figure put on it, and he kept his answers on the sixth place because a figure there would want a taker. The two keepings ran side by side down the length of the afternoon and never crossed, as the mudded rope and the rack dust ran side by side in the yard and never mixed. The yard had its privacy and the boy had his, and the finger he kept between every pair of his objects was the same small distance Ilva kept between her hand and her sleeve when the question came. Neither distance was a wall; both were the room a thing is left in when nobody is going to enter it.

\scenebreak

Tarn's stroke went over the shallow bright patch on \place{the flagstones} without the half-check, the way a man's hand goes over a place in a floor he has long ago stopped looking at. The stones kept the place where the stroke had stopped on Seed 22, and kept it still, though no stroke stopped there now and nobody would have known which stone it was but for the keeping. The small dry scrape from the sill of \place{the Debt Loft} lay on a place of its own above and behind, a sound with a direction from a room he was never taken into; the carried man's pale line lay on another; and neither was laid across the bright patch, for all three would have wanted a payer to join them and none stood.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through from \place{the Vail wheel-shed} with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them the way a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. He asked no question that would have joined the dust to a cause. The dust was where the work had put it, as the rack dust was in Ilva's thumb seam; the two keepings did not touch, being of a house and of a yard and separated by the whole width of the district's unwillingness to enter either. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person, and told a pot it would do in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either, and the knowing he had of the note that pot would give lay on the near-full tray with air under it and a want in it, advanced and not paid.

\place{the back room} off the shed kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices, and the house gave the room no name and the men no name, and the boy gave both a direction and a sound and nothing more. The burned-slate word came from \place{Slate Row} as a direction over \place{the boards}, told four times and not a fifth; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause. A fourth telling, a third telling, a fourth saying, each would have wanted a payer standing under it, and none stood, so the counts stayed where they were, four and two and three, and were not made to agree.

The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness and no row named. No sixth asking came this afternoon over the boards, and no second shaping, and the ownerless voice was not heard in the yard. Five askings are not one witness, and one shaping is not a row; the answer to which row it was that went quiet went on standing where it had stood through a whole district's seasons, with nobody leaning in to take it down and no hand at a kiln to give it an owner.

The shallow clay pan's gone ring kept its round place on the ground with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say what had stood there. The two gaps in the row on \place{the loaf shelf} in the room whose floor does not touch his, the one crust in the fold with the empty place beside it unnamed, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the hand that went in on Seed 15 and did not come back, each stayed on its own place. No third gap came into the afternoon and no fourth likeness was made a cause. A likeness is a cheap thing: it takes two objects and a man willing to put a payer under both, and there was no such man in this yard.

A child's foot below the sill stayed wrapped in a woman's wool. She called the cold by its name and the yard called it weather, and the two words lay over the same small foot without meeting, as the frost on the well lip and the water in the trough lay over the same hole without meeting. The extremity went before the core, and the afterdrop waited past the wrapping nobody here would perform, and nobody named what had taken the warmth or how to put it back, because a cure wants a payer and none stood to this foot.

\scenebreak

In the small hours there was a woman's low counting in the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. It came through the wall the way a low song comes through a floor, flattened by work and given nobody's face. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door.

It took the small intake of one of those counted breaths, and then the small intake of the next one, and held the two intakes apart with a finger between them. The letting-go that belonged between the two was left where it was, not crossed and not filled, and no house count was borrowed to carry either intake across the space, and no fifth pull was supplied to \lore{the carried fire} under the floor. The two intakes lay as two objects on the sixth place, not one rhythm made out of two, and the finger lay between them. There was no finding in it and no payer named to it. The yard's keeping and his own had run beside each other all afternoon without joining, because a joining wants a payer and none stood, and now the keeping-apart was his to do and cost him nothing and gained him nothing, which was exactly why it was honest. One hundred and twenty-four, filed beside one hundred and twenty-three, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest keeping-apart and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift, because a gift wants a taker and nobody had taken anything. The restraint was the work, and he left it on its own place.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and seventy-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, Sill or \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-four days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The shallow bright patch kept its place on the flagstones. The count in wood stayed in wood. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Fifthday.

The two intakes lay apart on the sixth place with a finger between them, and the boy's hand lay back against the fold with the count still at two hundred and seventy-four --- two objects, one rhythm refused, nothing promoted. Frost went on toward its sixth morning without a boundary drawn, and the next opening stood far off in a later month's first firing.
