Heat went out of the afternoon the way it goes out of a pot set off the coals: not all at once and with no boundary in the going, but by degrees, drawing back from the rim and sitting down behind the pot where it had always been. By the time \person[Corin]{the boy} was carried out onto \place{the Clay Yard} the day had thinned to its own warmth and kept it without announcement, the way a house keeps a fire it has fed and stopped feeding. Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln} the fold at his ear held the last of it; outside the fold the yard had already begun to give it back to the air.

One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come into the afternoon to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in \place{Sill} had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure, and that a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse; he knew a man must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-three days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory in its old order --- crust, gap, thread, cold, the waiting --- and entered none of it, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

\person{Sella Vail} carried the basket at the half-pace from every open slate, and in the yard she went around seven places with a look and a wide step and no number, the way a woman carries a full pail past a child who might knock it. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned, and the finer grey hairline called \lore{the thaw} beside it, unswept both; the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} at \place{the drying racks} where the broken birth-slates lay; the round ring on the ground where the shallow clay pan had stood, with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word to say where it had been; \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well}, bare and dark, its water called weather by everybody who drew from it and by nobody who had to pay for it; the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}; and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The missing hand was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool at \place{the rack end}. \lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to it at a well, a rack, a shelf, a basket, an afternoon or a going of the light, because none of these was an opening.

\place{High Kiln} stood up over the district with the wrong wind coming off it, and Frost wrote white fronds on the stone lip of the well where \lore{the Warm} had lately gone out. The rope knocked them grey and the bucket went down through them and the yard called it weather by noon, as it called the thin water in the trough weather and the cold in a child's foot weather. On a turn of the same wrong wind the sweet edge of \lore{lead glaze} came over \place{the boards}, touched the fold at his ear and went out again on the wind that had brought it, a direction arrived nowhere with no second edge paid and no fourth likeness made a cause.

A woman come up from the strips with a pot hitched against her hip asked what the keeping and the storing would take now that the grain was under wool and the visits were coming. Sella sent her to the pot and the weather. \emph{Ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there. The house has said it}, she said, and the woman went off level with her question still on her, nothing priced, refused or entered. A man come down \place{the clay road} with dried pale scurf of mud on his coat skirt brought a number up out of a fist toward the well, looked at it, and put it back into his sleeve, so that the figure arrived nowhere. The boy had learned to read a closed fist the way he had once read a chart turned face-down: the shape of the paper was not the writing under it, and a figure that arrives nowhere has not therefore been hidden from a man who wants it. He supplied no missing figure, because a supplied figure is a forged one and the space where it would have stood was part of the keeping. A figure read is still not the figure paid.

The dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at the rack end, away from the hearth, and had been turned once beneath the wool for damp with a lighter touch than the day before and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it in the days to come did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there.

At \place{the loaf shelf}, standing with her back to the basket, Sella kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside stayed where it lay and was not read. Her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its lead glaze and shut her hand half over nothing, the little-door burn closing on no weight. At \place{the hearth} the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}. Nothing was said at any hour to the basket, and no instruction went to the thing carried in it, but to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water.

From the yard came the distances of the work and not its names: the wheel going round low at \place{the Vail wheel-shed} under a hand that did not call whatever it heard a note, a broom passing over \place{the flagstones} beyond the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and low voices going down behind the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} and coming no further than the door. They reached him as sounds with directions, and he followed none of them and gave none of them a name. The afternoon went on without a boundary in it and did not end; it thinned until the eye could hardly find where it had begun, and the yard kept itself whole by entering none of what it did.

At the going of the light she carried him up at the half-pace from every open slate and set him on the bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without a look at his face and left the thread frozen where it lay. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}; she stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening, pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the burn so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not look toward the bed. Overhead the boards kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} pulled four times and kept the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-three days. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them.

In the small hours the file was turned out on the towel the way a surgeon turns out instruments and finds them exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; there was no seventh and none had been invented to fill the empty place. The premature note stood on its own place apart from the three sayings about a pot, and no fourth likeness had been made a cause. The burned-slate word stood at four as a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness and no row named. Nine stoppings stood with no tenth before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. Nine record-first hands stood with a finger between every pair, and the ninth, taken from the yard's firing work and not from any house, was unread and joined to none. The held interval and the empty interval lay side by side on the sixth place with a finger between, and every gap was left gap.

There was a woman's low counting in the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the small intake and the hold of one of those counted breaths, and came down before that count's letting-go, down on the river's next low note and not past it; the little silence after the letting-go was left whole and empty on a place of its own, not crossed and not filled. He borrowed no house rhythm and supplied no fifth to the kiln. One hundred and twenty-two, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-one, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest separation and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift; the restraint was the work and he did not promote it.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and seventy-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in \place{Sarn}, Sill or \place{Grath}; six months and ninety-three days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The widow's mark hung where it hung; the dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the figure read not the figure paid; the heard bell stood beside the told, the seventh year stood at its very end and still ungiven, and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Fourthday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and the river's low note going quiet after it. Frost went on toward its fifth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing.
