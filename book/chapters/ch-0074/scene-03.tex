\daymark{274}{Frost 5, Fifthday, Y.H. 813}

Frost came on its own morning. It was not carried in from the day before and set down like a loaf; it earned its light by degrees, the way a pot earns its heat, with nobody at the fire saying what the degrees were. There was no market on a Fifthday and no Cross and no bell. \person[Corin]{The boy} came into his two hundred and seventy-fourth day under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof in \place{Low Kiln}, with the fold at his ear and the small hours going on above him. Two hundred and seventy-three had stood exact at the going of the dark; two hundred and seventy-four stood exact now, and six months and ninety-four days were told by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. The count crossed the way a board laid beside a board crosses --- a dry knock, and no seam asked to answer for it. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-four days, the lean quarter of its warmth lying where it lay with nobody to lift the floor and nobody to supply the pull. Frost 10 stood five mornings ahead and was not brought early into this one.

The house smelled as it had smelled, which is a way of saying that nothing in it had been settled in the night. Hot clay cooled under tallow. Wet wool. Under the two, faint and mineral, the sour breath of the unswept corner that the house called \lore{the thaw}, as though a name laid on a thing were the same as the thing going away. On \place{the yard well} the rime lay heavier than yesterday, white fronds written on the stone lip where \lore{the Warm} had lately gone out, and the bucket would knock them grey when somebody came to draw. Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings. Overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Under the floor the fire kept four and a gap. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any of them. The heard bell's four, four and a gap stayed in its square beside the told bell, not folded in and not made to speak for this roof.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sill had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure. He knew a cold patient cannot clot, and will seem better before he is worse, and must be warmed from the outside and slowly. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-four days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket. That was the grammar of the house and it held on this morning as it had held on the mornings behind it: the instruction went to the coals, to the wool, to the pot, to the broom and to the running water, and never to the thing in the basket. She told the fire it was the thaw, turned the coals with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. Her burn, shaped like a little door, shut on the pot's back and opened on nothing when her hand came away. She pushed a strand of coal-black hair back with the wrist that carried it so the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals, and did not look toward \place{the bed}. She carried him at the half-pace from every open slate and set him under \place{the loaf shelf}'s low angle without a look at his face, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin, and left the thread frozen where it lay. At the fire the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward the bed. The mark hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it, and her hand did not go to \lore{the widow's mark} at a morning, a hearth, a rack or a basket, because none of these was an opening. The hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour.

At the shelf she kept her finger on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside stayed where it lay and was not read. Her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure. The dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth, and she turned it once beneath the wool for damp with a touch lighter than yesterday's and set it back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it in the days to come did not know what it cost. Smoke's third true subtraction was behind and unread in the locked box gone up \place{the clay road} toward \place{High Kiln}, and the figure read was still not the figure paid; the space between the two figures had no signature and no hand to put one there.

\scenebreak

The change came at the shelf, in the middle of a morning that had announced nothing. She stood with her back to the basket and moved her lips along the two-gapped row the way a woman counts spoons and does not say what the counting is for. Then her finger went beneath the front edge, where the charcoal tally lay in its short pressed clusters under the wood, and stayed there.

On Day 166 the finger had gone beneath that edge and stayed for the length of one of the breaths she counted under her breath, and had come away with no word and no going back. This time it stayed for two.

The boy lay at the low angle of the shelf and read the two breaths as a surgeon reads a small change in a field: the same hand, the same place, the same silence after, and one more held interval laid into it. He did not read the charcoal. He did not copy it, count its clusters, or bring it up to the two gaps above, where a row stood short in two places and had been short in two places for as long as anyone under this roof had kept a row. The charcoal stayed where it lay. Nothing was said, and she did not go back to the shelf that morning.

He filed the returned held beat on the shelf's own place, beside the Day 161 fraction at \lore{the damper}, the Day 166 held beat itself and the Tallow 22 flagstone half-check. It was not a new place. It was not a fourth likeness stood up to call itself a cause, for a cause wants a payer standing under it and none stood. It was not a reading, not a payment, not a key and not a rule. No payer was named to it. A house could change by two held breaths and say nothing, and the not-saying was kept on a place of its own, with a finger between it and every other keeping. He would not let it stand up as a gift either, because a gift is a thing that wants a taker and nobody had taken anything. The restraint was the work, and he did not promote it.

\scenebreak

In \place{the Clay Yard} the light came up without any boundary in it, the way heat comes back into a room that has been cold all night --- not at a stroke, but by the slow returning of the same things to the same places. Before first grey three or four men came up the yard with their collars high and went low into \place{the back room} off \place{the Vail wheel-shed}, which kept its finger's width of open and its sound of low voices and got no name from the house. They reached the boy as a direction and a sound, and he followed neither and gave neither a cause.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction. He said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and to those who wanted it late, agreed with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts since the water went. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb where the house could check; the seventh lay at the very lip of \place{the kindling} sill where it could not, and the small knife lay beside it with its unforged sentence and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. Then the sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came up to the word that would call the loan and stopped there with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end, which had still not given. He drew the breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach and did not spend it; a small interval showed where it should have been, and his chest had been tight since the dark. \lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it. The pale clip-ring at his left wrist held what light there was and gave it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} without a check and without a turn toward the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector; no collector came up the clay road on this Fifthday and none went down it.

Above the shed, in \place{the Debt Loft}, the warm stone sat in its unmarked pot and was cold, two years gone and unreconciled by not trying. It came to the boy only as a small dry scrape with a direction, a sound from a room he was never taken into, and he set it on a place of its own and did not lay it across the shelf's returned beat or any other held change. Down below, the salt-stiff coat with the left-shoulder crease kept a broader man's shape on its peg behind the door, and the wheel-seat in the shed kept its twenty-year hollow on the left that nobody filled. No name was spoken over either.

\person{Jorra Vail} came through the finger's-width-open door with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal into both forearms past the bend of his little finger and under the nails, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and parted them as a surgeon parts clot from vessel, asking neither which slate had been broken nor whether it was a birth-slate. The broken birth-slates lay under \place{the lowest rack} where the yard went around them and entered them into nothing. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person: \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. To a pot on the wheel he said \emph{You'll do}, in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the \lore{full note} that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid; no pot was lost and no kiln-master came to make something of it.

Sella had not finished telling him the fire was low. His bent finger went into the cold iron of the damper ahead of her unfinished sentence, moved the tongue a hair's breadth and left it standing, so that the end of her instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence. The boy read again one piece of iron with two bites --- the bite that holds a vessel whole and the bite that can take the hand off a man --- with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The burned-slate word from \place{Slate Row} stood at four, a direction over the boards and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and had not become three; the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn, and \emph{he's not Vail} stayed sealed as tightly as a lid nobody had tried to lift. Jorra did not ask about the ear and Tarn did not ask about the room; the two silences stayed unjoined.

\scenebreak

In the small hours there was a woman's low counting in the next room whose floor does not touch his --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name. It came through the wall the way a low song comes through a floor, flattened by work and given nobody's face. Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the small intake and the hold of one of those counted breaths, and this time it did not come down before the letting-go. It stayed past the letting-go and through the little silence after it, and came down on the river's next low note.

It was a small thing and an exact one. One of the house's rhythms had been carried across the interval and set down on another thing's note, and nothing had been borrowed to make the crossing: no second house count, no fifth pull supplied to the kiln, no figure taken off the two-gapped row, no silence crossed that did not belong to the river. The little silence after the counted breath had not been filled to make a row come out even; it had been passed over and left whole where it lay. One hundred and twenty-three, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty-two, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power, a doctrine or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift; the keeping of it was the work, and he left it on its own place.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Two hundred and seventy-four stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety-four days were told by nobody, and the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days with its payer unnamed. The widow's mark hung where it hung. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the figure read not the figure paid. The heard bell stood beside the told with its gap still in the square; the seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given, and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Fifthday.

Sella's finger came away from beneath the shelf's front edge after two counted breaths with the charcoal still where it lay, and the boy's hand came down on the river's next low note. Frost went on toward its sixth morning without a boundary drawn, and the next opening stood far off in a later month's first firing.
