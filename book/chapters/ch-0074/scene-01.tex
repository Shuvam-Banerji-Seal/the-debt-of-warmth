\daymark{273}{Frost 4, Fourthday, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he hand} lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Outside the wool the small hours went on at their own pace, and the boy came into his two hundred and seventy-third day carried, awake and silent, under the roof of \place{the Vail kiln-house} in \place{Low Kiln}. Two hundred and seventy-two had stood exact at the going of the dark; two hundred and seventy-three stood exact now, and six months and ninety-three days were told by nobody in \place{Sarn}, \place{Sill} or \place{Grath}. The count crossed without a sound, the way a board laid beside a board gives a dry knock and asks nothing of the seam. Under the floor \lore{the carried fire} kept its four low pulls and the silence where the fifth should have stood, its payer without a name for two hundred and seventy-three days, and the lean quarter of its warmth stayed where it was with nobody to lift the floor and nobody to supply the pull.

He took the body in the order the facts presented themselves, as a surgeon takes a field in the order the wounds present themselves. One stiff leathery brown-edged barley crust lay two inches from his ear, gone past the point where a child would eat it, and the empty place beside it kept its name to itself; no third had come in the night to make the row come out even. The thread of drool at his mouth had frozen in the dry heat and was unwiped, because nobody under any roof in Sill had ever been given an instruction about the thread and a basket set down is a pot set down and left. Cold had come to cheek and temple and the small bare joint of the wrist where the wool rode back, and stopped there as at the end of a line written out for it, with neither an order to go forward nor an order to go away. The afterdrop waited beyond all three, past a warming nobody in this house would perform. He knew the composed face that is not composure; he knew a cold patient cannot clot and will seem better before he is worse. He wanted the crust lifted the two inches to his mouth. Both were true, and in a body two hundred and seventy-three days old the second was louder. The surgeon took the inventory and let the boy want, and entered neither, not even on the place where he kept the things he did not enter. He had once reduced a shoulder in the dark by feel alone and now could not close two inches on a pot handle; the thought came and went without bitterness, which was its own small betrayal.

Through the finger's-width door the river kept its note of \lore{water over water}, a low thing over a lower thing, never numbered among the hearings; under the floor the fire answered in fours and withheld its fifth, not a bell; overhead \place{the boards} kept four nails on the near board, four on the next, and a thumb-width knot where the fifth nail should have stood. Three counts in three places, and none rose into another, because agreeing any two would want a payer put under both and no payer stood to any. There was no fire-sitter to supply the fifth and no house count to borrow. The bell's four, four and a gap, heard at \place{the Cross} on the last market-day of Smoke, stayed in the square where it struck beside the told bell of \lore{the Long Night}; it was not brought under this roof on a Fourthday, not folded into the three and not heard as a seventh of \lore{the full note}. Six hearings of the full note stood beside and never among, five from four sources and the sixth, the small flat knock out of a woman as the men lifted her, filed where a thing out of a person must go and never with the things that came from vessels; the empty place kept its emptiness and none was invented to fill it.

\scenebreak

Frost 4 came on its own light, earned by that morning and not brought into a day that had not earned it. There was no market, no Cross and no bell on a Fourthday, and the working week began the way a pot begins on a banked fire, by degrees and without announcement. Frost 10, the month's first Account-day, stood six mornings ahead and was not brought early into the dark of this one.

\person{Sella Vail} went to the fire before the basket and gave no instruction to the basket. She told the coals it was \lore{the thaw}, turned them with the back of a broken pot and said \emph{The house will keep it} where another woman would have said \emph{I}. She stood a moment with the back of the pot in her hand counting under her breath toward no opening --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- and pushed coal-black hair back with the wrist that carries the little-door burn, so that the one grey streak at her temple showed pale off the coals. At \place{the loaf shelf} her finger stayed on top of the front edge so the charcoal tally on the underside remained unread, and her lips moved along a row standing with two gaps and gave neither gap a figure; she glanced once at the clay jar standing cold under its \lore{lead glaze} and shut her hand half over nothing. At \place{the hearth} the pot rim went out with its handle two inches beyond the reaching hand, with a half-beat at the setting and no look toward \place{the bed}; nothing was said at any hour to the basket. Instruction went to the fire, the wool, the pot, the broom and the running water and never to the thing carried in it.

\lore{The widow's mark} hung where it hung on its cord at her throat, a thumb-length of lead-glazed clay with the Vail kiln scratched into it. On Frost 2, before the kiln, she had put her hand to it for one breath and let go, and lifted the clay lid with the back of her hand and tipped the carried coal under a broken pot; the fire had taken, and nobody had praised the opening or named what it mended, and no slate had been entered. At a morning and a hearth the mark hung where it hung, for neither is an opening and the hand goes to the mark before the kiln and at no other hour. The first firing of Frost stood behind the household with no lesson, no sign, no mending anybody would name and no payer. Four openings in four months were four and not a rule, for a rule wants a payer standing under it; the next opening stood in the first firing of a later month, far off and not hoped for.

Out on \place{the Clay Yard} the dressed grain lay covered on the cool stone at \place{the rack end}, away from the hearth, and had been turned once beneath the wool for damp in the first of the light and set back where it lay, changed in state and given no figure. The arithmetic that would read over it in the days to come did not know what it cost, and the space between the figure that would be read and the price that had been paid stayed unsigned, with no mark on it and no hand to put one there.

The yard went around its seven places the way a woman carries a full pail: with a look, a wide step and no number. There was the clean dark line at the Vail corner and the finer grey hairline called the thaw beside it, the space beneath \place{the lowest rack} where the broken birth-slates lay, the round ring where the shallow clay pan had stood with no scrape, crumb, print, chip or word, the bare dark \place{the trough} beside \place{the yard well} with its water called weather, the field's empty place out on \place{the worked strips}, and the returning-line place where a hand had gone in on Seed 15 and had not come out. Seven were seven and not an account, because an account wants a payer standing under it and none stood. The hand in the returning line was carried and not named, not paid and not joined to the shelf's two gaps, the fold's one crust or the dressed grain under wool.

\person{Garl Vail}'s debt against a firing of tiles went around without a name, neither paid nor settled nor done with, and worse for being uncalled; his name had not been spoken under this roof for two hundred and seventy-three days. The salt-stiff coat with the crease at the left shoulder kept his broader shape on its peg behind the door, the wheel-seat in \place{the Vail wheel-shed} kept its twenty-year lean and its hollow on that side and Sella would let no one fill it, and the proverb said over a corner where the repair had been rubbed out went on being said to the pot and the fire and never to a person: leave the crack showing; people trust a thing they can see has been handled. It was a saying about honest repairs said over a pot with no crack in it, and it cost more now than it had when he was alive to say it. Garl was kept in objects and not in a story, a keeping that was maintenance and not an argument, and no lesson was made of any of it. No collector had come up \place{the clay road} on Frost 1, Frost 2 or Frost 3, and none had gone down it; the loan stood in the ruts where it had stood, and \person{Tarn}'s seventh year stood at its very end and had still not given.

\person{Tarn} took the broom only after it had been given its instruction, said \emph{Yes, house} to those who wanted the thaw early and those who wanted it late, agreeing with both so thoroughly that no argument could find a foot, and kept the late because it puts off the paying, though the paying stood in the ruts. At \place{the kindling} the small knife lay at the very lip of its sill with six counted in the worn thumb-mark where the house could check and the seventh at the edge where it could not, beside Garl's unforged collector sentence, with no name on the man who turned back at the frozen ford and no figure on the debt. He told the wood \emph{Six counted. The next I don't count} in the tone of a man stating the weather. The sentence about an ear under this roof being worth more than the debt that held it came to his teeth and went as far as the word that would call the loan before stopping, with no figure and no name --- a remembered ninth stopping, and no tenth stood up before the seventh year at its very end and still ungiven. The breath that should have gone in at the top of the reach stayed unspent, his chest tight as it had been since the dark, \lore{Ash} worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifting it, the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holding what light there was and giving it back. His stroke went on over \place{the flagstones} past the shallow bright patch without the half-check, and the stones kept the place where it had stopped.

Before first grey men whose names the house did not take went low through the finger's-width-open \place{the back room} off the shed, and \person{Jorra Vail} came through with the day's meal-fine grey slate dust ground small as meal past the bent little finger and white in the creases of a fist that had opened, not bone-coloured slip. He kept dusty forearms and clean ones on one tray and asked no question that would join them to a cause, parting the two states as a surgeon parts clot from vessel. He spoke to the wheel before he spoke to any person --- \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady} --- and told a pot \emph{You'll do} in a voice with no welcome in it and no unkindness either. He knew the full note that pot would give three turns before it left his hands, with air under it and a want in it, and withheld the knowing on the near-full tray beside the hearings and never among them, advanced and not paid. His bent finger went into the cold iron of \lore{the damper} ahead of Sella's unfinished sentence, moving the tongue a hair's breadth and leaving it standing so the end of the instruction arrived at the pot and the household straightened around his silence; the boy read one iron with two bites, the holding and the taking, with no \lore{Grathic} word for both mercies, joined to no kiln and entered in no slate. The burned-slate word stood at four from \place{Slate Row}, a direction over the boards arrived nowhere and not a fifth telling; the two tellings of the counting room shut in the war stood at two and did not become three; the three sayings about a pot stood as sayings with the premature Smoke 14 note on its own place and no fourth likeness made a cause. The row question stood at five askings with one shaping and no witness and no row named, and the promise of the boy's name to the men who burn slates was neither given nor withdrawn.

\scenebreak

Under the roof the morning went on doing what it had always done, and the boy lay at the low angle of the shelf in the room whose floor does not touch the room of the fire, carried and told by nobody what any of it counted. Sella had carried him past the open slates at the half-pace and come back to the pot; Tarn had taken the broom when it was named; Jorra had gone to the wheel; the grain lay under wool and the fire missed its fifth and the mark hung where it hung. None of it stood up and called itself a lesson. He kept the not-doings on a place of its own with a finger between it and his own kept gaps, and did not lay one across the other, for a joining would want a payer and none stood. There was a woman's low counting in the next room --- the small intake, the hold, the letting-go --- a sound with a direction and no name, and he took from it only what the hand could take and left the rest where it was. The body offered him two inches of air and a crust he could not lift; the house offered him nothing at all and kept itself whole by not entering what it did. Between the two there was the one thing he could do, and it was small enough to be honest.

Outside the wool the hand went up an inch toward the river's note through the finger's-width door. It took the small intake of a count from the room whose floor does not touch his, held it, and came down on the river's next low note. It borrowed no house count and supplied no fifth to the kiln; it crossed no silence that did not belong to the river and took nothing from the two-gapped row on the shelf.

One hundred and twenty-one, filed on the sixth place beside one hundred and twenty, exact, complete and worth nothing at two inches of air: an honest coordination and not a reach, not a hearing, not a seventh of the full note, not a stopping, not a payment, not one of the held changes, not a key, a power or a rule. He made nothing of it and would not let it stand up as a gift; the restraint was the work and he did not promote it.

The hand lay back against the fold as a man puts away a tool where the next hand will find it without looking. Behind the wall the fire kept four low pulls and withheld its fifth under a floor nobody would lift, the lean quarter of its warmth staying where it was with its payer unnamed. Two hundred and seventy-three stood exact, complete, unspent and spoken by nobody in Sarn, Sill or Grath; six months and ninety-three days were told by nobody; the fire's fifth was unpaid the same number of days. The widow's mark hung where it hung. The dressed grain lay covered and unfigured at the rack end, the figure read not the figure paid; the heard bell stood beside the told, the seventh year stood at its very end and still ungiven, and the loan stood uncalled in the ruts. Nothing had been opened, named, called, paid or ended on this Fourthday; only the hand placed down, forward and unresolved, and the river's low note going quiet after it. Frost went on toward its fifth morning without a boundary drawn and without a lesson made of four months' openings, the next opening standing far off in a later month's first firing.
