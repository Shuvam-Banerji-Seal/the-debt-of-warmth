## Chronicle

The lean week came into Low Kiln on mud set into a brown skin over the ruts, holding a boot's print. Sella Vail told the broom to go around what the water left, for it cost enough to come in; the yard swept around the clean dark line at the Vail corner, the new finer grey called thaw, and the space under the rack where the broken birth-slates lay, entered by nobody. The house kept its days badly, in loaves, glances and a space at the row's end nobody would name. The boy carried at the edge of the work kept seventy-eight through eighty-four against it, and could set none down.

At the thinner Account-day crowd at the Cross, Berr Clay, red cord folded under his coat, wiped his thumb twice and read one in twelve over fewer bodies, took himself out of his own sentence and went off believing he had done a kindness. The bell went four, four and a gap and nobody crossed himself. At the Fishstairs the steps came back with a landing a hand's breadth out of line that the market walked over without a fall, and the soft-cuffed clerk sat for an uncalled measure. Mira Silt answered a question with her own answer and put her thumb to the grey cord, the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted.

Three doors down Alda Reed laid her birth-slates down before she turned to the fold, crossed two fingers at the flower unknowing and covered the sign with the date given a woman forty mornings dead; she said warm and breathing, exact and not true, and did not open the tin in her bodice. Two private records stood unread in one room. Jorra Vail came back with grey slate dust worked past his little finger and put his bent finger to the damper ahead of Sella's sentence. Tarn set his knife on its sill with six counted, told the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count*, and wanted the late thaw because it puts off the paying.

Word came that the weak board at the Weir was gone or trodden flat, and the honeyed ice and stopping hairlines were called the thaw that was. On the last day a second man come downriver from Greybank told the bucket-round of a counting room shut in the last war with the damper closed and its men dead of the breath; the yard took it as an old curiosity with a date; nobody joined it to a kiln. The week changed nothing but the count and the file, which gained a second telling held apart. At dusk Sella set him on his bed with one stiff brown-edged crust in the fold and the empty place unnamed, left the thread at his mouth frozen, and the river's note came through the door while the fire's missing breath came through the floor, kept apart. The count crossed into Silt 26 unspent.

## Continuity

- **Exact final moment.** Dusk of Day 84 under the Vail kiln-house roof. Sella Vail has set the basket down and laid the boy on his bed (not in the Debt Loft), put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread of drool at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note from the Ilm comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, the two sounds in two separate places as for eighty-four days. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Nothing has been joined, paid or explained. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down; the count crosses into Silt 26 unspent. He is eighty-four days old and nobody has told him so. Sella is the only person present at the close.

- **Where everyone is at the close, and what they want.** Corin/Dan: on the bed under the roof, awake, silent, hungry, wants the crust, the breast, the bucket two inches beyond his hand and his number spoken by somebody who would know what it counted; gets none and can ask for none. Sella Vail: at the fire, broom and bed in the Vail house; she heard the Greybank man's story, told the fire it was the thaw, made nothing of it, did not connect it to her roof, touched no widow's mark on this day and kept her private roll unshown; wants the fire kept, the house kept, the keeping never explained. Tarn: yard and roof, small knife on its sill with six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held, broom put away; wants the late thaw because it puts off the paying and wants the seventh year not to end; the loan that can hear stays uncalled. - **Items gained, lost, used; money; powers and cost.** Nothing gained that can be spent. The private file gained a duplicate: the second telling of the shut counting room, filed on the same tray as the first (Ilmen 26) without letting the two touch, with the likeness and the difference (breath through nose against breath in throat; both saying *not the war's hand*) kept together. One brown-edged crust carried uneaten through all seven days, still stiff and past smelling of anything; no third came and the empty place is still unnamed. Nothing physical lost that was not already lost: the pale river stone, the smoky crust, the coal taken by the brine-gull, the weak board, the broken birth-slates under the lowest rack. Held unchanged: Tarn's knife on its sill, six counted; the charred slate in the kiln ash; basket and wool lent by Sella; the clay jar on the house shelf; the round pewter tin above the empty bed with its pale fold and thumb-bright rub, unopened; the hollow in the grey wool where the stone lay; Alda's little tin in her bodice, touched and unopened, and her sixth narrow slate turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name (six pages, all unread, no seventh written); Berr's box locked; Mira's grey cord with the second twisted twice beneath it, neither knot counted, never upriver to Nine Pots or lifted at a door; Orra's forty-first knot in the Eel House; the widow's mark untouched; Sella's private roll unshown. Money: none. Powers: none usable, no rank change, no experiment, no clamp, no fire, no Grathic. Counting is an old-life habit, not a gift, and the exact seven days set edge to edge bought nothing; the surgeon's reading of the cold foot, of Tarn's held breath, of the district going thin and of the anatomy of the sealed room all worked perfectly and were worth nothing.

- **Facts revealed, lies told, promises, debts.** Berr read one in twelve over a thinner crowd, took himself out of his own sentence and kept the true deduction locked in his box: a figure read aloud is not the figure paid. The printed figure stands unchallenged over fewer bodies. Alda's *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down* remains exact and not true, and the date she laid over the birth-flower sign --- the seventh of Stone, account up on the eighth, the date she gave Dessa --- now stands over a woman forty mornings dead and uncollected, which no living person can take off. Dessa's number went into the water with her thumb. The gone board, the out-of-line landing, the stopping hairlines and the early Sill note are spoken of only in the past tense as *the thaw that was*. The shut counting room has now been told twice, eighteen leagues east, with a date in it, and joined to no kiln. Sella has lost a husband to that danger and has never described it that way herself; she said nothing about the man or the room at dusk. Promises unchanged: the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn; he's not Vail stays sealed. Debts unchanged: Garl's against the firing of tiles is never paid, never settled, never done with; Tarn's seventh year presses toward its end and is still held; the payer of the coal goes unnamed; no collector came and nobody named the omission.

- **What Chapter 18 must not forget.** Day 85 is Silt 26, Sixthday, Y.H. 813, and opens with no recap of the lean week. The count is eighty-four, unspent, and goes on exactly while the house keeps it badly. The week settled nothing: Account-day, the closing market of Silt and the turning into Seed are still ahead.
