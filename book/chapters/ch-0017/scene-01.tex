\daymark{78}{Silt 19, Y.H. 813}

\dropcap{T}{he mud} had set into a brown skin over the ruts, and it held the print of a boot the way a cracked cup holds the shape of the hand that lifted it, faithful to a thing gone. It gave the boot back slowly. All morning \place{the Clay Yard} went around that faithfulness with brooms and buckets, the way a ward walks around a bed where somebody lies who will not be moved and will not be spoken of, and nobody said what the brown skin remembered. The water had gone out three days ago and left this behind: not a flood now, not weather, not even the thaw that was, but a surface with a memory in it, taking a heel and letting go of it a breath later as though the ground had changed its mind about what it had seen.

\person{Sella Vail} stood at the rack end with a broom in both hands, the burn across the back of her left hand shut on the ash handle. “Go around,” she said to the broom. “It cost enough to come in. It’ll cost more to take out.”

She gave the broom its instruction before she gave it to anybody, and the two women at the pan-stack waited until the instruction had arrived before they took up their own sweeping. She said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and she did not look down into the basket at her hip. \person{Corin}, seventy-eight days old and carried at the edge of the work, kept her half-pace from every open slate without being told to, which in this yard is the same thing as being told and not needing the telling again.

“There’s a boot in it,” a woman said, resting her broom to point with the handle. “You can see the whole sole. Heel and all. The mud’s took it and won’t let go.”

“It’ll go when it goes,” the other said.

“Ask the pot,” Sella said to the first. “It’ll tell you what a wet thing costs set down there.”

The woman went back to her sweeping as though she had been answered, which in this district is the same thing as being answered. The broom went around the pans and around the little stand where a slate had once lain, and came back to the brown skin as though it had never left it. The boy watched the bristles go around a finger’s width of dark at the corner of the Vail wall. He had seventy-eight slates set edge to edge in a place no clerk would look, with no writing on any of them and no hand to set any of them down; the house kept its count in loaves nudged along a shelf, in a glance at the clay jar on the wall, in a space at the row’s end that nobody called a number. Two accounts of the same days, one exact and one kept badly, and neither of them spoken aloud. The surgeon who had once read a chart with the note turned down knew the difference between a thing entered and a thing left lying, and the infant in the basket was hungry, and the hunger was the louder of the two.

A man come up from the road with a figure shut in his fist, the way a man carries a hot coal he means to set somewhere and has not decided where. “Four loaves short of what was,” he said, meaning the week and its baking and the three days of water in one breath. He looked at the women, and the women looked at the mud, and he opened his hand in the general direction of the well. The figure went out of the fist and travelled as far as a figure can travel when nobody will receive it, and arrived nowhere. The boy set it where it fell and did not enter it. He had learned in a better-lit room that a figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and here the figure was not even read aloud, only carried from a fist to a shoulder and let fall into the general air.

At the corner the broom went around two lines and took up neither. The clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned lay where it had lain for a year and two days; the new hairline, finer than the old, lay beside it and was called thaw and would go when it went. Sella stood over both with the broom in her hand and swept neither. Under \place{the drying racks} the space beneath the lowest rack was gone around as the water had gone around it, and the broken birth-slates lying there were entered by nobody. She carried the basket past at the half-pace and did not turn her head toward the space, and the boy kept the two lines and the space under the rack on three places that did not touch.

\scenebreak

Under \place{the Vail kiln-house} roof the carried fire lay where it had lain all week, under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. The boards overhead kept four nails and four and a fifth that was not a nail but a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet, and nobody had thought to enter the place where they failed to. Nobody had said who paid for the coal that kept the fire giving, and the payer went unnamed as he had gone unnamed for seventy-eight days; warmth came into the house from somewhere the boy could neither see nor reach, and he set the payer on a place of its own and wrote no cause across it. The fire took what it was given and gave back what it could, and the gap fell in the fourth breath as regularly as a fault in a stone.

\person{Garl Vail} was kept by his salt-stiff coat on its peg, by the seat worn hollow on the left, by the clean dark line and by a proverb that went to a pot with no crack in it. Leave the crack showing. People trust a thing they can see has been handled. The proverb cost more now that there was nobody to say it in his own voice, and the house went on saying it to the pots and the fire. The debt against the firing of tiles remained neither paid nor settled nor done with and went around another year as Sella went around Ash. The boy kept the coat, the seat, the line, the new grey and the uncalled debt in five places that did not touch, and did not make a sixth of them.

\person{Tarn} had set the small bench knife on its sill before the light and told the empty yard where it was, the way he had told it every morning of six years. Six years stood in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb, and the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house that the house could not check. “Six counted,” he said to the kindling. “The next I don’t count.” He said it as a man says the weather, and went on holding the breath he had decided not to spend, though his chest was already tight with it. Ash was worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line, and no scrubbing lifted it, and he did not scrub at it. His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. From \place{the Vail wheel-shed} came the small sound of a pot set down and the wheel turning over, and his head did not turn, and he did not name what had come past the door. A loan that can hear is a loan that can be called; the sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, and the two silences kept their equality without a word between them.

\scenebreak

At \place{the yard well} in the afternoon a slate stood propped at the rack end to dry, its writing turned under, and Sella went around it as wide as she would go around a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it over. She drew with her little-door burn closing first on the rope and then on nothing, and gave the bucket its instruction before she gave it to anybody. “That’ll do,” she said to the bucket. She did not touch \lore{the widow's mark} at the well, because a well is not an opening and a basket is not a door, and when a woman asked her for a figure she said \emph{the house} and told her to ask the pot what a wet thing costs set down there.

Below the sill the child’s cold foot was still the cold to the woman who had wrapped it and weather to the yard, and the two facts went past each other again in the light of the afternoon and never met. The boy carried the wool and the dark foot on a place of his own with no name, no cause and no cure. He knew the extremity going before the core, the blood going back too fast and too cold, the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform; the reading worked perfectly and was worth nothing. He could have named the order of it in the tongue of the other life and had no word for it in \lore{Grathic}, and the woman who had wrapped the foot went on calling it the cold while a woman passing with a bucket called the water weather, and neither altered by a word. He did not lay Sella’s not-going beside it as a cause. It was told of her that she had once sat up three nights with a child of her own who cooled while everybody was kind, and he had put that under her roof and not across the yard, beside the fold put right without a look and a woman’s not-looking and a man’s taking-himself-out-of-his-sentence, four not-lookings of one shape and not a cause.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear, gone past smelling of anything weeks ago, and the empty place beside it was still unnamed. No third had come through the three days of water and none came now. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the bucket two inches beyond the reach of his hand, and the wanting was a thing the body did without asking him. The adult who had once sorted twenty injured people in ninety seconds and chosen who would be worked on and who would be waited on lay in a basket at the edge of a well and could not lift a hand to any of the three. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek and temple and wrist, and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from his wrist so the cold found the place.

Near the well a small foundling in cast-off clothes worn like a uniform stood watching the grown faces, with a gap where a front tooth had been and a bit of green glaze on a string at his neck. A kiln-hand set a pot down on the well-lip. The boy’s head went toward it before the knuckle and before the note. The child’s head went toward it half a beat after the boy’s, after the knuckle and after the note, and stopped and came back. His thumb found the green sliver and rubbed it, and he could not say why he had turned; he looked as surprised as anybody at the answer his own body had given him. Nothing passed between the two heads, and no slate was seen.

Into the file went four objects and not one joined to another: a head turned after the knock and not before it, a green sliver rubbed with a thumb, a warm loaf that had gone into a pocket holding no slate, a mouth that had opened on a sentence and had not finished it. The shape of a keeping that changes hands without a slate. He had learned in a better-lit room what three likenesses in a row will do to a man who wants an answer, and here were four, and he kept four. His own turning toward the pot lay beside the child’s and did not touch it. No second loaf was given, no secret changed hands that any clerk could read, and no betrayal was paid.

Dusk came on without a bell for it. Sella lifted the basket off the ground with her knee and carried him back up the yard at the half-pace from every open slate. At the corner she stood a moment with the broom and went around the two lines with her eye and not with the bristles, taking up neither the clean dark line nor the new grey finer than it. “Go around,” she said to the broom. “It’s an old yard. It’ll stand another winter.” The boy was carried past with one brown-edged crust stiff in the fold and the empty place beside it still unnamed, and he kept the broom’s going-around on its own place beside the fire’s four, four and a gap, and did not let either become the other.

Behind them at the well a pot was set down on the lip, and the small foundling’s head went toward it a half-beat late, and his thumb found the green sliver at his neck. The boy did not turn back to look at the turning; he carried the shape with him in the basket where the day’s other shapes were carried, and wrote no cause across any of them.

Under the roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. “Keep it off his ears,” she said to the wool, and the wool did less than the wind wanted. The door stood a finger’s width open. Through the gap came the river’s low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire, four, four and a gap, and the boy kept the two sounds in two places as he had kept them for seventy-eight days. Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stood at six, three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid, the word forty lay on a place of its own with the readable double turn beside it, and one crust lay stiff in the fold with its empty place beside it, and none of these had joined and none had been spent.

His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count stood at seventy-eight.
