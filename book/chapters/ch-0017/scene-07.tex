\daymark{84}{Silt 25, Y.H. 813}

The bucket-round at \place{the yard well} had begun before the light had decided what it wanted of the day, and the rope came up stiff from the cold, keeping the shape of the hand that had hauled it. A skin lay on the water where the bucket broke it, thin as the skin on milk gone cold, and the women at the tubs spoke of that skin to the bucket and not to the child in the basket. Keep it off his ears. The instruction went to the wool and the wind, and \person{Corin}, eighty-four days old, lay in the lee of the well's little roof with the fold turned right under his chin and was not part of the sentence. \person{Sella Vail} had carried him down before the light at the half-pace from every open slate, going wide around one propped at the rack end as though it were a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and she had not looked into the basket since. She said the house where another woman would say I, and gave every instruction to the fire, the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. A basket set down is a pot set down and left; that was the whole of the rule, and he had learned to lie under it without expecting to be asked.

A second man came down the road from the north with road-mud dried grey on his boots to the calf and the salt-and-hot-clay smell of the east road on him, a smell that opened the yard's nostrils the way a door opens a warm room. He was not one of theirs. He waited his turn at the bucket with a bag across one shoulder and the patience of a man who has walked far enough to know a queue when he sees one, and when the bucket came up he held it a moment before he drank, as a man who has come that far does not put his mouth to water without looking at it first. He was not the man who had stood here in Ilmen with the same smell on him and the same mud; that one had gone back down the road with his bag and his story and had not been seen again. This one had the same walk and a different set to his shoulders, and the yard looked at him and at his mud and went on with its buckets.

“You'll have come from \place{Greybank},” a woman at the tubs said.

“From east of it,” he said. “Near eighteen leagues. The road's a pot that's boiled over since the thaw.”

He drank and wiped his mouth with the back of a hand that had carried a load, and then he told them, because a man who has walked that far owes the yard a thing to say for the loan of its water. He told it the way such things are told so that nobody has to believe them, with a date set into it like a nail driven to hold a board.

“There was a counting room shut up in the last war,” he said. “East of us, in a house I could name if I had a mind to. Shut for the duration, they said, with the men inside it keeping the accounts. They closed the damper to save the coals ---” and here he drew air in through his nose and let it go against his teeth, the small sound of iron shut on iron, a thing he had no word for in his own mouth “--- and the men in the room died of the breath. Not the war's hand. Their own heat went out of the room and there was no way to feed it. That was a year two wars back, before your folk counted it.”

The boy watched the man's hands as he drank. Broad knuckles, a pale line where a strap had worried the palm, nails grey with road-dust: the hands of a man who had carried a load and meant to carry another. He held the bucket with both hands and would not let it rest on the lip, as though water set down were water lost. A pulse beat in his throat, slow and steady, and the breath came short through a nose caked with clay dust. Nothing about him was dangerous. There had been a night when the boy sorted twenty bodies in ninety breaths by just such signs --- the grip, the breath, the colour under the nails --- and got every one right; here he could not sort one stranger out of a queue or tap his chest and ask him to say it again. The skill had not left him. It had lost its handle. He had watched another man tell this same story over this same well in Ilmen, and had kept that man's hands and that man's pause in a place of their own. Now there were two men and one story, and the second reading went where the first had gone, without either being laid over the other.

The yard took it as it had taken it once before, as a thing that had happened eighteen leagues east and was therefore nobody's to own. A woman with her sleeves in the wash said the war did for them, which settled it. A man at the racks said a room shut up was a room nobody had entered on any slate and the men in it had died of being uncounted, a joke that cost nothing because the dead could not hear it. Somebody said the frost would take the pits early at this rate, and somebody else said the pits had been worked early already and the colour of the water would tell you. The thaw took the argument after that: early, said one, and late, said another, and the man who wanted it late said a longer week of a man's own fire was worth having, and the woman who wanted it early agreed with him so entirely that there was nowhere for an argument to put its foot, and they went home warm on the word. Nobody said kiln. Nobody said Vail. Nobody looked toward the chimney across the yard standing with its breath held under blind ash. The story went around the bucket and came back as an old curiosity with a date, and the yard took up its buckets where it had left them.

\scenebreak

Sella heard all of it, as a woman who keeps a fire hears every word said about heat, and made nothing of it. She told the fire it was the thaw and told it to be fed. “Cold in the east,” she said to the coals that were not there, and then, to the bucket: “Easy. There's a skin on it.” She had lost a husband to the thing the man had just described and had never once described it that way herself. The house kept its own low word for it, a word that named the danger and the mercy in one breath, and she had said that word years ago to a boy at a kiln mouth and warned him away, and did not say it now. A room shut up eighteen leagues east was a room shut up eighteen leagues east; the mouth that told it had mud on its boots and no reason to lie, and no reason to be heard either. If she would have put anything away she would have put it where she put the rest of it, in the private roll nobody was shown. There was nothing to put away. She touched \lore{the widow's mark} at no hour of this day, because a well is not an opening and a basket is not an opening, and a story told over a bucket is not one either.

The boy heard the breath and the damper where the yard heard a date. There was no word in \lore{Grathic} that held either thing the way the man held it, not for a room, not for the stopping of air in a room, and the two phrases lay in the basket beside him like tools with no handle. He knew the room before the man had done describing it, having lain awake under a roof and counted \lore{the kiln's breath} in the dark --- four low pulls and a silence where the fifth should have been, four and a gap, night after night. He knew the anatomy of it as he knew a wound that closes over a cavity and seals what is in it: a vessel with no way to feed what it holds, the contents cooking their own heat out of the air, the face arranging itself in the ordinary way at the end. He had once been the man who wrote down how such a room would finish, in a calm voice, and gone on to the next body. The register did its work and said nothing of where it had learned it. Here there was no word at all, and no room to put one, and no hand to open a door if there had been one.

He filed the second telling on the same tray as the first. The first man's telling lay where it had been set down on Ilmen 26, with the small sound of iron closing on iron in it and a year two wars back pinned to it like a label; this man's telling went beside it with the same facts in it and the same date loosely kept, and the two did not touch. He had noticed that the second man drew his breath in through his nose where the first had made the sound in his throat, and that both had said the words \emph{not the war's hand}, and he kept the likeness and the difference on the same tray without letting either become the other. Beside that tray, in four places that did not touch, he kept the bell told in the dark of \lore{the Long Night}, four, four and a gap; the bell heard in the square at \place{the Cross}, four, four and a gap with nobody crossing himself; the early note out of the stone at \place{the Singing Sill}; and the breath of \place{Vail kiln-house} itself, four low draws and a missing fifth, warming the boards by degrees under blind ash. Four low things in four places. The river's own note of water going over water was a fifth thing on a place of its own and not a hearing at all. The telling of a shut room had come twice into this yard and had stayed a story with a date both times, and he wrote no cause across any of them, because a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not a hand puts one there, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can make of two tellings of the same thing. He kept them four. The tray did not become a cause. It was the whole of the work available to him and it bought him nothing he could lift a hand to.

\scenebreak

Six hearings of \lore{the full note} stayed six --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person who had been warm that morning --- beside and never among, and no seventh came to the well. Three near-full answers stood advanced and not paid on a tray of their own, neither hearings nor the third thing, and the boy had no hand to tap the pots and ask them to say it again. The word forty was on a place of its own with the readable shape of the double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb on wicker apart from both, and it was not spoken in this yard on this morning. Word, shape, cords, crust, hands, near-misses: each kept its place, and the tray of the two tellings was set down among them without touching any. A room can be sealed and the bill paid by the bodies inside; he had performed that arithmetic himself once, with a clamp and a hand held up for silence, and had never found a clean way to enter it. Here the arithmetic was done by strangers over a bucket twice in one year and called a war, and the second telling had added nothing to the first that any slate could take up. His private file had gained a duplicate and no spendable thing. The lean week would end as it had begun, with the count exactly kept and the house keeping it badly.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear with the empty place beside it still without a name. No third had come through the lean week and none came now. It had crossed another seven days without being eaten, lifted or spoken of, and it was past the smell of anything and had never been a sermon. He wanted the crust, wanted the breast, wanted the bucket's rim two inches beyond his hand, and could reach none of them. The body humiliated him exactly, as it had every morning of eighty-four. A thread of drool froze at his mouth and nobody wiped it. His head went where it wanted and made the small wet sound when it went too far. Cold arrived in its known order, cheek, then temple, then wrist, the wool doing less than the wind wanted and riding back from the wrist so the cold found the place. The hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the rim and came down. Reducing a shoulder had been ordinary work in the life that was not this one; keeping his own hand on a lip of cold iron was not, and the joke had not gone stale and had never once been funny enough to say aloud. He was the only adult at this well and the least use in it.

All week under the roof the new finer grey had lain beside the old clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its own corner, unswept because the house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes. Sella stood over both with a broom in her hand and swept neither. Behind the boy the carried fire lay under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Four, four and a gap. Nobody had said who had paid for the coal, and the coal that had gone up with the brine-gull weeks back was entered in no slate and missed in none. \person{Garl Vail} was absent and kept by objects, which in this house is a way of keeping a man no Court can enter: the salt-stiff coat held a broader shape on the peg and the seat kept its twenty-year lean. The boards above kept four nails and four and a knot the width of a thumb, and the two counts did not meet. A line left where it is is still a line kept, and a line a whole household steps around is known more surely than a line entered and filed. He kept the two lines on two ends of one tray and did not lay the thaw over either.

Below the sill a child had come out of the falling water with a foot that stayed dark and cold, and a woman had wrapped it twice in wool and pressed it between her palms and called it the cold, while the yard called the water weather; the two facts had gone past each other again this week and had never met. The boy had read the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting on the far side of a warming nobody would perform, and had no word, no hand and no standing to put on the shin. The reading worked perfectly and was worth nothing. \person{Dessa Silt} was dead and uncollected and her number had gone into the water with her thumb, and no collector had come and nobody had named the omission. The cold foot stayed the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard, uncured and unjoined, carrying its name years away from a house that had no Grathic for it and no reason to look for one.

\scenebreak

The man finished his water and gave the bucket back with a nod that cost him nothing and was worth something, and the mud on his boots dried lighter where the sun found it. Somebody asked him what year he had put to the story, and he gave a year two wars back and a month the yard did not use, and the answer made it more true, not less, because a date is what a thing has when it has stopped happening to anybody. He shifted the bag on his shoulder and went back down the road the way he had come, giving no name and asking for none, with the salt-and-hot-clay smell going off him into the yard's own smell of wet wool and woodsmoke. By the second bucket the room shut eighteen leagues east had become a bad business in a war and then weather and then nothing at all, and the yard went on with its morning as though no stranger had spoken. The man had brought the danger under this roof into a yard that had no use for it, as another man had brought it weeks before, and had left it lying where any passer-by might take it up, and gone home with his bag and his mud. The boy kept both tellings where they were set down.

At the turn of the wind Sella lifted the basket level and carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace from every open slate, going around the space under the lowest rack as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered by nobody. Under the Vail kiln-house roof she set him on his bed and not in \place{the Debt Loft}, and put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face. She left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stood a finger's width open, and through the gap came the river's low muttered note from \place{the Ilm}, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and through the floor came the fire's four, four and a gap, the two sounds kept in two separate places as they had been for eighty-four days. She said nothing about the man with the mud on his boots, or the room shut up eighteen leagues east, or the damper closed. If she had connected any of it she would have put the connection where she put the rest of it, in the private roll nobody was shown; but she had not connected it, and there was nothing to put away.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Nothing had been joined, paid or explained. The second telling lay on its own tray beside the first with no cause written across either, and the six hearings stayed six beside them and never among, and the three near-full answers stayed advanced and not paid, and the word was not spoken and no collector came. The lean week had changed nothing but the count and the private file, and the boy was eighty-four days old and nobody had told him so. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count crossed into Silt 26 unspent.
