# Chapter 17 — The Lean Week: beat sheet

**Mode.** Passage, the cap of seven lived days: Day 78 to Day 84, Silt 19 to Silt 25, Y.H. 813, Ninthday to Fifthday. Each day opens with its own `\daymark{N}{date}` (Scenes 1 through 7 each carry one) and is lived in full for well over 800 words --- roughly 2,600–2,800 words to a day, one scene to a day. No day skipped, poured into another or summarised; Day 77 is finished and is not revisited. Day 85 belongs to Chapter 18. Seven scenes, ~18,800 words total. POV stays inside Corin/Dan in close third-person past tense. No present-tense Chicago break, no `\memory{}` tag, no Court, Waiting Room or collector interior. The old life colours the noticing (a figure read aloud that is not the figure paid, a chart closed over a thing left lying, the order of the hand to the record before the person, the afterdrop waiting on a warming nobody will perform) without crossing into speech. Open in the mud the flood left, not by recapping the flood.

**Chapter movement.** The lean week is the district counting what the water took, and finding that the counting changes nothing. The house keeps its arithmetic badly in loaves, glances and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number; the boy keeps his exactly, seventy-eight through eighty-four, one slate set edge to edge against each day. Meaningful changes across the week: the Account-day crowd is thinner and one in twelve is read over fewer bodies; a second man come downriver repeats the saying of the shut counting room, advancing T065 without joining it to the Vail kiln; and the count crosses into Silt 26 unspent with a second telling added to the private file. Threads advanced: T071/T072, T077, T033/T034, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T031, T093, T086/T060, T065/T088, T015/T041/T047, T090/T080, T078, T025/T059/T024, T084, T089/T074, T007/T008/T073, T035/T057, T061/T082, T036/T076. Do **not** pay T033 (the sale is a shape, not a betrayal) and do **not** pay T023 again (the sixth page was written on Day 64, unread). Do not solve the crust, the stone or the coal, do not name Dessa, speak forty, count a knot, open a tin, give one in three, bring Marra onstage, write a seventh page, add a seventh hearing or a sixth sighting of the neat dark hand, make an accident of the board, give the foundling's name, call the loan or end anything.

**A note on the watcher.** Ulda (sheet `characters/ulda.md`) is glimpsed **once**, silently, in Scene 1 --- a small foundling of three or four in Y.H. 813, not the eleven-year-old of the sheet's later dialogue. He turns his head toward a pot a half-beat after the boy's, rubs the green sliver at his neck, and is as surprised as anyone; the loaf, the unfinished sentence and the half-beat are carried as four objects already in the file from Chapter 16, not as a second sale. No secret passes and no slate is seen; T033/T034 stay planted. He is not Marra, not the Fishstairs clerk, not the Court-table clerk and is joined to none of them. The Greybank man of Scene 7 is a second walk-on who speaks once and gets no sheet; he is not the Ilmen 26 traveller of Chapter 13 and must not have his telling joined to that one or to any kiln.

**Continuity anchors carried from Day 77.** The boy is seventy-eight days old on the first page and eighty-four on the last, carried everywhere in a basket that is not his, mute, cracked, unranked, below Thin. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; no third comes. The new finer grey lies beside the old clean dark line in the Vail corner, unswept and called thaw. The broken birth-slates lie under the lowest rack. The carried fire lies under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four. Six hearings of the full note stand at six --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person --- beside and never among; three near-full answers stand advanced and not paid, three intervals on another tray, the word forty on a place of its own with the readable double turn beside it and the weight of a thumb apart from both, four low sounds in four places with the river's note a fifth thing. The soft-cuffed clerk is still set at the Fishstairs; both cords, Orra's knot-string, Alda's sixth page, the pewter tin and the little tin stay unread and unopened; Dessa is dead and uncollected and the count of her mornings keeps rising; the cold foot stays the cold to one woman and weather to the yard.

## Scene 1 — Seventy-Eight
**Day 78, Silt 19, Ninthday. Morning to dusk.** Opens with `\daymark{78}{Silt 19, Y.H. 813}` and a drop capital.

**Place.** The mud of the Clay Yard; the drying racks; the yard well; the corner under the Vail kiln-house roof. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Tarn, Ulda (glimpsed once, unnamed in the narration), yard women, a kiln-hand and a man with a number (walk-ons). Jorra at the wheel-shed, heard and not on stage; Alda and Mira offstage; Orra present only as her board. Garl kept by his coat, seat, clean dark line and proverb.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Open in the brown skin the mud has set into, faithful to a thing gone, holding the print of a boot before it lets go. No recap: the water is a shape the ground remembers. The sweeping goes around the clean dark line, the new finer grey and the space under the lowest rack; Sella gives the broom its instruction before she gives it to anybody --- go around, it cost enough to come in and will cost more to take out (T009, T013, T092, T031).
2. The house counts what it took in loaves moved along a shelf, glances at the clay jar and a space at the row's end nobody calls a number. A man brings a figure from the road in his fist and lets it go toward the well, where it arrives nowhere. The boy, seventy-eight days old and carried at the half-pace from every open slate, keeps the number exactly against the bad arithmetic and lets the figure lie where it fell (T071/T072, T077).
3. Four objects come into the week on four separate places and are not laid together: a head turned a half-beat after the knock, a green sliver rubbed with a thumb, a warm loaf into a pocket that holds no slate, a mouth that opened on a sentence and did not finish. Near the well a small foundling in cast-off clothes worn like a uniform turns his head toward a pot a half-beat after the basket turns, stops, rubs the green sliver and cannot say why; the boy files the delay beside his own turning and does not let the two touch (T033/T034). No second sale, no secret, no cause.
4. The child's cold foot from below the sill is still the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard, and the two facts go past each other again in the same mud. The boy reads the extremity going before the core and the afterdrop waiting past a warming nobody will perform; the reading works and buys nothing (T093).
5. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed; no third came through the three days after the flood and none comes now. He wants the crust, the breast and the bucket two inches beyond his hand and can reach none. Cold arrives cheek, temple, wrist; the wool rides back from his wrist (T086, T060).
6. Sella gives every instruction to the fire, the wool, the bucket and the broom and never to the basket, says *the house* where another woman would say *I*, goes wide around a slate set out to dry and does not touch the widow's mark, because a well is not an opening (T058, T079). The carried fire keeps four, four and a gap; nobody has said who paid for the coal (T065, T088).

**Emotional turn.** The lean week opens not on loss but on the ordinary work of going around it. The surgeon reads the mud as the record of a vessel that has lost warmth; the infant is hungry, and the infant is louder. The watcher's half-beat is held as an object, not a suspicion.

**Sensory anchors.** Brown mud that keeps a boot's print and lets go slowly; broom bristles around a finger's width of dark; grey finer than the old line; tallow, wet wool, river silt and the sour tang of Ash; the little-door burn closing on the rope and then on nothing; the green sliver rubbed with a thumb; one stiff crust at his ear; drool frozen and unwiped.

**Threads advanced.** T071/T072, T077, T033/T034, T058/T079, T009/T013/T092, T031, T093, T086/T060, T065/T088. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the half-beat and the loaf held for the day the watcher's sale lands as a betrayal; the cold foot held toward the silt-leach years; the space under the rack and the broken slates beneath it; the two lines left in place toward the later day a house is left where it is and still kept. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Sella's broom going around the two lines in the corner with neither swept, the boy carried past at the half-pace with one crust in his fold and the empty place beside it, the small foundling's head going toward a pot a half-beat late and his thumb finding the green sliver, and the boy's hand outside the wool going up an inch toward the mud and down with the count at seventy-eight.

## Scene 2 — A Thinner Crowd
**Day 79, Silt 20, Tenthday, Account-day. Morning to afternoon.** `\daymark{79}{Silt 20, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** The Cross, the block and the moot-post's finger's width of shade; the Fishstairs below the north bridge. **Present:** Corin (carried, set in the moot-post's shade), Sella Vail, Berr Clay (short), Mira Silt, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (silent walk-on), the Court-table clerk (silent walk-on), market and yard voices. Tarn, Jorra, Alda offstage; Dessa dead and uncollected.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella carries him down at the half-pace from every open slate, sets the basket in the moot-post's thin shade with his cheek to the cold iron, and does not look into it. She does not touch the widow's mark, because a reading is not an opening (T058, T079).
2. The crowd leans in thinner than at the flood's Account-day --- the water and the lean week have taken bodies out of the market --- and Berr reads one in twelve over fewer people, wiping his thumb twice before he touches nothing, passing a finger over the figure, taking himself out of his own sentence and going off toward High Kiln believing he has done a kindness (T015, T041, T047, T077). The green of tasted glaze stays at one corner of his mouth; the red cord is folded under his coat.
3. The true subtraction goes over the basket and the noise of the stalls, spoken by nobody. The printed figure and the true one lie on two trays that do not touch. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid; a figure let fall from a fist toward a well is not entered either (T077, T072).
4. The bell goes four, four and a gap over the square and nobody crosses himself. The boy keeps the heard bell beside the told bell and both beside the early note and the kiln's gap, in four places, and makes nothing of the likeness (T078, T061).
5. Down the road at the Fishstairs the steps have come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line, and the soft-cuffed clerk sits for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls; he is not Marra, not the Drawing collector, not the Court-table clerk, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand comes (T090, T080).
6. Mira works the eels with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been. Asked about the run she answers her own answer and moves eel-bones while deciding; when a figure is named her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and shifting a creel shows the second twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted, the thin child's knot untouched. Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it (T025/T059, T024).

**Emotional turn.** The institution's arithmetic is unchanged, and the change is in the space around it: fewer people to hear it. Berr's reason stays good and his hands stay clean; the district's grammar takes the speaker out of the sentence as neatly as a hand out of a field. Mira's not-looking is a keeping where his is a pricing, and the boy keeps the two apart.

**Sensory anchors.** The moot-post's cold iron at his cheek; the square's four ways out; the twice-wiped thumb; green glaze at a mouth corner; a bell longer than a church bell with nobody crossing; eel slime dried pale; a grey cord under an apron and a second twisted twice beneath it; the missing top of an ear.

**Threads advanced.** T015/T041/T047/T077, T090/T080, T078, T072, T025/T059/T024, T084, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the thinner crowd as the district's own uncounted subtraction; the two cords and the knot-string held for the later reading of the private accounts; the uncalled measure still set ahead of a death. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** The basket lifted off the post in the late afternoon and carried back up the Clay Yard with the boy's cheek still cold from the iron, the printed figure and the true subtraction lying on separate trays that did not touch, and the count at seventy-nine.

## Scene 3 — Two Records in One Room
**Day 80, Silt 21, Firstday. Midday to dusk.** `\daymark{80}{Silt 21, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** House Silt, three doors down; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Alda Reed, the neighbour scrubbing in the next room (walk-on). Mira offstage, present only as her board; Orra offstage; Dessa dead forty mornings.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Sella sets the basket just inside the finger's-width-open door at the edge of the light and away from every place a slate might lie, opens no slate, turns no errand into a walk to Slate Row, and stands a half-pace off with her little-door burn shut on nothing (T058, T081).
2. From the basket's low angle the round pewter tin above the abandoned board-bed keeps its pale fold at its seam and thumb-bright rub on the lid, unopened; the fold of grey wool below holds only the hollow where the small pale river stone once lay, and nobody says either is gone (T089, T074, T086).
3. Alda comes short with soap-and-milk gone cold under river silt and lye sour under the soap, nails scrubbed to the quick with clay still under them. She lays her birth-slates writing-down on the bench before she turns to the fold, presses the fold too long at his shoulder, crosses two fingers at the red edge of the birth-flower before she knows it and covers the sign with the date she gave Dessa --- the seventh of Stone, the account up on the eighth (T007, T008, T073).
4. To Sella's *he lies quiet* she says *Warm and breathing, that's all I've set down*, exact and not true, and goes on with dates after the question has stopped, because dates are a handle on a thing too large to carry. The mercy-lie has curdled through to a woman forty mornings dead and uncollected and no living person can take the date off the sign (T008).
5. Her hand goes once to the little tin inside her bodice and comes away without opening it. Her sixth narrow slate stays turned face-down toward a floorboard in a house the boy cannot name; the hidden record stands at six pages, all unread. Do not write a seventh page and do not pay T023 again (T023, carried).
6. The neighbour scrubbing in the next room goes on with her cloth and does not look up at the writing or the tin. The boy keeps the two private records in two materials on separate trays, beside a third board standing downriver beside a knot-string, and reads none of them (T089, T059/T025).

**Emotional turn.** Two women keep the same child by keeping records they will not show each other or anyone. The kindness is exact and the exactness is the lie; the boy's surgeon's register notes that a beautiful entry can stand where a true one should and stay there.

**Sensory anchors.** Soap-and-milk gone cold under silt; lye sour under soap; the chipped cup and brown ceiling stain; the pale fold and thumb-bright rub on the pewter lid; the hollow in the grey wool where the stone was; the two-finger sign at the flower's red edge; a slate turned writing-down toward a floorboard.

**Threads advanced.** T089/T074, T023 (carried, not repaid), T059/T025/T024, T007/T008/T073, T081, T058, T086, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the convergence of the private records after Alda is gone; the date laid over the sign standing as a fact no return can alter; the hollow where the stone lay. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** The basket carried back under the Vail roof and set on the bed, the fold put right without a look at his face, the thread at his mouth left frozen and unwiped, one crust stiff in the fold and the empty place beside it, with the pewter tin and the turned slate still shut three doors down and the count at eighty.

## Scene 4 — The Dust Comes Back Again
**Day 81, Silt 22, Secondday. Before light through midday.** `\daymark{81}{Silt 22, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** The Clay Yard; the Vail wheel-shed and the room off it (not entered); the drying racks. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Jorra Vail, Tarn. The two men in the back room named by nobody; Garl kept by his coat, hollow seat and proverb.

**Beats, in order.**
1. The room off the wheel-shed keeps its finger's width of open. Men came in since first light and spoke low and the house gives them no name for them (T035).
2. Jorra comes through with fine grey slate dust worked into both forearms past the bend of his little finger, not the bone-coloured slip of the wheel. The broken birth-slates stay under the lowest rack, gone around and entered by nobody; the boy keeps dusty forearms and clean bone-coloured ones on one tray and asks neither which slate was broken nor whether it was a birth-slate (T035, T031).
3. He speaks to the wheel before he speaks to anyone --- it does not care whose hands, only if they are steady --- tells a pot *You'll do*, and knows the full note of a Vail pot before it leaves the wheel and will not say so before a kiln-master who would call it early and make something of it (T035, T061).
4. Sella has not finished telling him the fire is low; his bent finger goes in ahead of his hand at the damper and moves it, so the end of her instruction arrives at the pot and the household straightens around his silence the way cloth straightens when a weight is lifted (T035).
5. He does not say the back room is cold and does not look toward the bed or the basket. *He's not Vail* stays sealed as tightly as a lid nobody has tried to lift in three years, and the promise of the foundling's name to the men who burn slates is neither given nor withdrawn (T057, T084).
6. Tarn's small knife is on its sill and announced to the empty yard; his broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector without once turning along it, and his head does not turn when a vessel gives its note past the door (T036, T076, T009).

**Emotional turn.** The dust is a record no roll can enter, and the boy can read the state of a man's forearms without reading a word of Grathic. Jorra's two kinds of obedience --- to the wheel and to the house --- stay in one man without either cancelling the other, and the question the boy cannot ask is kept where questions last for years.

**Sensory anchors.** Grey slate dust in the creases past a bent little finger against bone-coloured slip; the finger's width of open at the shed door; the hollow wheel-seat and the salt-stiff coat; a pot's full note known before it leaves the wheel; the damper moved ahead of a sentence; a broom over the collector's road without a turn.

**Threads advanced.** T035/T031, T057/T084, T061/T082, T036/T076, T009, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the broken birth-slates under the rack and the ungiven name held for Jorra handing Corin to the people who burn slates; the two silences under one roof; the ear worth more than the debt that holds it. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Jorra's dusty forearms going back into the wheel-shed without a word and Tarn's broom going over the collector's road without a turn, the boy carried past the lowest rack with the space under it gone around, and the count at eighty-one.

## Scene 5 — Six Counted
**Day 82, Silt 23, Thirdday. Before light through dusk.** `\daymark{82}{Silt 23, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** The Clay Yard; the Vail wheel-shed; under the Vail kiln-house roof. **Present:** Corin (carried), Tarn, Sella Vail. Jorra at the wheel, heard and not on stage; the yard's early and late thaw arguers (walk-ons).

**Beats, in order.**
1. Before the light Tarn sets the small bench knife on its sill and tells the empty yard where it is, the way he has told it every morning of six years. Six years stand in a mark worn almost flat by his thumb and the seventh is nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house cannot check (T036).
2. He tells the kindling *Six counted. The next I don't count.* Something must give at the end of the seventh year, but the end is not written here, and he goes on holding it as a man holds a breath he has decided not to spend though his chest is already tight (T036).
3. The neighbours argue the thaw early against late; he says *Yes, house* to both, agrees so entirely that no argument can find a foot, and keeps wanting the late one because a late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying has stood in the ruts all week (T036, T077).
4. Ash is worked into the creases of his knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line and no scrubbing lifts it; the pale clip-ring at his left wrist holds what light there is and gives it back (T013, T036).
5. His broom goes over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it; no collector goes down it for the woman in the brook and he does not say the thing that has not happened has a name (T009, T090).
6. A vessel gives its note past the door and his head does not turn and he does not name the sound in Grathic, and the boy does not call it a seventh hearing. Two ears and two silences keep their equality under one roof and the loan that can hear stays uncalled (T061, T076).

**Emotional turn.** A man who owns nothing keeps one exact account --- six years spent, one held back --- and the holding is the only thing he has that the house cannot check. The boy's surgeon's register reads a term running out and a throat tight with a breath not spent, and cannot put a hand to either.

**Sensory anchors.** The small knife on its sill and the worn mark on its handle; Ash in knuckles the colour of the clean dark line; the pale clip-ring catching light; a broom over the collector's road; a vessel's whole note past the door with no turn of the head.

**Threads advanced.** T036, T076, T009, T061/T082, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the seventh year pressing to its end and what must give; the uncalled loan that can hear; the two silences kept as an equality neither will name. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Tarn's knife left on its sill with six counted and the seventh held, his head not turning at the note past the door, the boy on his bed with the thread frozen at his mouth and one crust in the fold, and the count at eighty-two.

## Scene 6 — The Thaw That Was
**Day 83, Silt 24, Fourthday. Morning to dusk.** `\daymark{83}{Silt 24, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** The plank walk downriver at the Weir; the Fishstairs below the north bridge; the Clay Yard and the Vail roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, Mira Silt, the soft-cuffed Fishstairs clerk (silent walk-on), a weir woman with salt-white wrists and market voices (walk-ons). Tarn, Jorra, Alda offstage; Orra offstage with her knot-string.

**Beats, in order.**
1. Word comes up that the weak board in the plank walk is gone or was trodden flat by the flood and nobody has been down to see which. No accident is made of it. The boy sets the gone board beside the missing Fishstairs step and the hairline that stopped short of the edge in three places that do not touch (T083).
2. The honeyed ice, the hairlines that stopped a thumb's breadth short and the early Singing Sill note are spoken of now only in the past tense, as *the thaw that was* --- a thing said after the fact treated as the same as a thing said before it. Nobody owns the day; the yard goes home warm on the word (T085, T092).
3. The steps have come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line that a whole market walks up and down over carefully without making a fall of it; the soft-cuffed clerk still sits at the landing for a measure nobody called, hand to slate before eyes to stalls (T083, T090, T080).
4. The new finer grey lies beside the old clean dark line in the Vail corner, unswept and called thaw and left to go when it goes; Sella stands over both with a broom and sweeps neither (T092, T013).
5. Mira works the eels below the bridge with pale slime on her sleeves and silt in her neck, answers a question about the run with her own answer and moves eel-bones while deciding whether to lie. When a figure is named her thumb goes under her apron to the grey cord and the second twisted twice beneath it shows, never carried upriver to Nine Pots, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted (T025/T059). Her board lies beside Orra's knot-string and does not touch it (T024).
6. The bell from the Cross comes back four, four and a gap and nobody crosses himself; the boy keeps the told bell beside the heard bell and both apart from the low note out of the stone (T078, T061).

**Emotional turn.** Danger that was named in advance is renamed after the event into a season everybody can go home warm from. The gone board is a blade left in its sheath; the misaligned step is a gap a whole market steps over without seeing. The boy keeps each on its own place and writes no cause, because three likenesses in a row will grow a cause whether a hand puts one there or not.

**Sensory anchors.** Salt dried white at a weir woman's wrists; a plank that is gone or trodden flat under silt; honeyed ice said past tense; four steps, four and a landing out of line; grey finer than the old line; eel-bones moved hand to hand; a bell longer than a church bell.

**Threads advanced.** T083/T085/T092, T090/T080, T025/T059, T078, T071/T072. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the gone board and the out-of-line step held for the later Breaking and for the room he finally stays in with the gap left showing; the thaw read backwards on a later day; the uncalled measure still set. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** The market going up and down the four-and-four steps over the landing nobody made a fall of, the boy carried past it at the half-pace with one crust in his fold, and the count at eighty-three crossing toward the last day of the week.

## Scene 7 — What Another Man Says of a Shut Room
**Day 84, Silt 25, Fifthday. Morning at the yard well through dusk.** `\daymark{84}{Silt 25, Y.H. 813}`.

**Place.** The yard well and the Clay Yard; under the Vail kiln-house roof at dusk. **Present:** Corin (carried), Sella Vail, a second man come downriver from Greybank (walk-on, speaks once, no sheet), the bucket-round at the well (walk-ons). Tarn, Jorra, Alda, Mira offstage; Dessa dead and uncollected; Ulda not seen again this chapter.

**Beats, in order.**
1. A second man come downriver from Greybank waits his turn at the well, road-mud on his boots and the salt-and-hot-clay smell of the east road on him, and tells the bucket-round what was told once before: that in the last war a counting room was shut with the damper closed and the men inside died of the breath, eighteen leagues east. He says it as an old curiosity with a date in it and goes back down the road, giving no name (T065).
2. The yard receives it as history. A woman speaks of the war, a man of a room shut up being a room nobody counted, and somebody says the frost will take the pits early; the argument goes off into the thaw and none of them joins the sealed room to any kiln in Low Kiln (T065, T085).
3. Sella tells the fire it is the thaw and feeds it, and does not connect the story to her own roof; if she would put anything away she puts it in the private roll nobody is shown, and there is nothing to put away (T058).
4. The boy files the second telling on the same tray as the first, beside the told bell, the heard bell and the kiln's own missing breath, and writes no cause across any of them. He does not let the second telling join the first to any kiln --- a likeness laid on a likeness will grow a cause whether or not a hand puts one there, and he has learned to keep them four (T065, T078, T061/T082).
5. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed, carried through another week without a name and with no third coming (T086/T060). The new finer grey lies beside the old clean dark line and is not swept; the carried fire keeps four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer still unnamed (T092/T013, T088).
6. The child's cold foot below the sill is still the cold to the woman who wrapped it and weather to the yard, uncured and unjoined (T093). The six hearings stay six, three near-full answers advanced and not paid, the word forty unspoken, the four low sounds in four places with the river's note a fifth thing (T061/T082, T024).

**Emotional turn.** The week ends with an old danger spoken twice and still unrecognised, and with a house that goes on keeping warmth it will not name the payer of. The boy's private file has gained nothing he can spend --- only a second telling held carefully apart from the first --- and the count has gone on exactly while the house kept it badly.

**Sensory anchors.** Road-mud on a traveller's boots; the salt-and-hot-clay smell of the east road; the damper and the breath named in a story told to a bucket; a fire under blind ash and the back of a broken pot missing one breath in four; one stiff crust and an empty place at his ear; the river's low muttered note through a finger's-width-open door.

**Threads advanced.** T065, T086/T060, T093, T061/T082, T092/T013, T088, T071/T072, T058. **Foreshadowing planted/carried:** the shut counting room held on its own tray toward the later naming of the kiln's breath and the sealed file; the cold foot toward the silt-leach years; the empty place and the one crust toward the gift entered on no slate; the new grey toward the later day a house is left where it is and still kept. **Paid:** nothing.

**Exact last image.** Dusk under the Vail roof. Sella sets him on his bed and not in the Debt Loft, puts the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and leaves the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped. The door stands a finger's width open: the river's low muttered note comes through the gap, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the carried fire's four, four and a gap comes through the floor, the two sounds kept in two separate places as they have been for eighty-four days. One brown-edged crust lies stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still unnamed. Nothing has joined, been paid or been explained. His hand outside the wool goes up an inch toward the water and comes down. The count crosses into Silt 26 unspent.

## Pull into Chapter 18
The last image leaves the count running, not stopped: eighty-four days carried into Silt 26 with one crust, an empty place, an unswept new grey and a second telling of the shut room filed on a tray that does not touch the first. Chapter 18 opens on Day 85, Silt 26, Sixthday, with the last ten days of Silt going around under the same broom and the same arithmetic, the boy still carried at the edge of the work and keeping the count exactly while the house keeps it badly. The lean week has settled nothing; Account-day, the closing market of Silt and the turning into Seed are still ahead, and the private records, the cold foot, the watcher's half-beat and the loan that can hear all go forward unopened and unpaid. Forward, not closed.
