\daymark{83}{Silt 24, Y.H. 813}

Word came up the river road before the morning had settled what sort of day it would be: the weak board in the \place{plank walk} at \place{the Weir} was gone, or it had been trodden flat by the flood and left under the silt, and nobody had been down to find out which. It travelled the way such word travels in that quarter, not as news but as the thing that comes after news, passed off a boat to the bank and from the bank to a woman with salt dried white at her wrists, who said it over her shoulder to a basket rather than to the child in it. \person{Corin} heard it from the low angle of the basket and made nothing of the way it was given, because a thing said over a shoulder is a thing said to nobody, and a thing said to nobody can be gone around by everybody without anybody owning it.

\person{Sella Vail} had carried him down before the light with the little-door burn across the back of her hand closing first on nothing and then on his weight. She set the basket where a pot would have been set, out of the draught that came off the water, and gave the morning's instruction to the wool and not to the basket, keep it off his ears, it's the river wind, and kept her half-pace from every place a slate might have lain, though there were no slates out here, only boards and salt and the rope of the traps stiff with old water. She had brought him to the Weir as she brought him to \place{the Fishstairs}, to see a trade and not be seen into, and she did not look down as the weir woman spoke. The salt at the woman's wrists had dried in white seams that cracked when she moved her hands, the way dried slip cracks over a pot set too near the fire, and the boy noted the likeness and let it go, because a likeness is not a thing and he had learned to keep them on separate places.

“Gone, then,” the woman said. “Or trodden. Who's to go down and see?”

Nobody answered, and the not answering went off into the morning with the rest of it.

The boy had learned early that a danger which cannot be placed is a danger nobody has to act on. The weak board had been heard of in Ilmen, heard of again in Silt, said plainly to be still there and trodden by nobody, and now said to be gone; through all of it nobody had asked which board, because a board without a place is a story, and a story can be told over a shoulder and carried on with. He had once watched a man in a better-lit room ask which vessel, which bay, which hour, and been impatient with the questions until the answers came and he saw what the questions had bought; here nobody asked them at all, and the not asking stood in the same relation to the gone board that the gone board stood to the men who might have crossed it. Two absences, one shape, and he kept them on two places rather than lay one over the other.

The boy set the gone board where he had set other absences. It lay on a place of its own beside the missing step at the Fishstairs and the hairline that had run a thumb's breadth short of the edge, three gaps in three places that did not touch. He had no word of \lore{Grathic} in which to call any of them a danger and no hand that reached two paces to point at one, and the surgeon in him read the three as he had once read three entries on the turned corner of a chart --- a thing looked at, a thing looked past and a thing left lying where the next man would step --- and wrote no cause across them. A blade left in its sheath is still a blade; a sheath is also a thing a whole district can walk past and call a board. He kept the reading and bought nothing with it, as he had bought nothing for eighty-three days, and the infant in him, who wanted the crust and the breast and the bucket two inches beyond his hand, won that argument as he had won every other.

\scenebreak

By noon the whole Weir had the word in its past tense. The honeyed ice that had made the water taste of something held a moment too long, the hairlines that had stopped short of the edge, the low note \place{the Singing Sill} had given before its time --- all of it was spoken of now as the thaw that was, as though a thing said after the fact were the same as a thing said before it. A man who had been certain the river would break early said it had broken early, and a man who had been certain it would break late said it had broken late, and neither remembered having been uncertain, and the argument that had held the yard a month went home warm on the word. Nobody owned the day. It had happened to the river, which was weather, and weather was what this district called the things it had not kept.

He had been awake through the days when those things had not been past tense at all but present and under argument: the taste in the water, the vein in the ice, the note that came early and was answered Weather. To hear them recalled now as the thaw that was was to hear a man describe a wound after it had closed and call the closing the whole of the story. The warmth of the telling was the warmth of an ended thing. It was the afterdrop read backwards --- the recovery named and the fall that preceded it left out --- and the distortion was exact and ordered and went the one way, toward a season everybody could go home warm from. He had no word for afterdrop in Grathic and no hand to put on the cold extremity of a district that had stopped feeling it; he kept the reading as he kept the rest, and it worked and bought him nothing.

The boy had kept the before and the after on two trays that did not touch. He had heard the ice called honeyed when the current ran under it in veins and had filed that beside the stopped hairline, the early note and the higher clay-heavy brook, four things in four places, and the district had folded the four into one past tense the way a hand closes over four eel-bones and comes away with one. A chart closed with a neat line and a signature at the foot does not change what the note beneath it said; it changes who has to read the note. He had learned that in a better-lit room, where the past tense had been a clean department and the note had been somebody else's to carry, and he had no Grathic for it here and no standing to say it. He lay in the basket at the edge of the salt and kept the trays apart, and the market went up and down the steps over the out-of-line landing as though a season named after the fact were a season anybody had survived by naming.

\scenebreak

The steps at the Fishstairs had come back four, four and a landing a hand's breadth out of line, and a whole market went up and down them carefully without making a fall of it. A woman with a creel shifted her weight at the landing, felt the give of the board under her heel, stepped across the place where a step should have been and did not look down at it. Another followed. A man with a jar of eels set it down for a breath at the out-of-line landing, took it up again and went on, and nobody said the step was wrong, because to call it wrong was to ask who had put it right, and that was a question with a levy in it. The mud line lay along the risers where the water had stood and gone, a thin brown edge that stopped and did not go around the whole step, and the boy set it on the tray with the stopped hairlines rather than with the steps, because an edge that stops is one sort of thing and a step that a market walks over is another.

The soft-cuffed clerk sat at the landing with his slate to his forearm for a measure nobody had called. His cuff had gone grey from washing and his hand went to the slate before his eyes went to the stalls, the way a man puts a hand to a rail before he looks over the edge. \person{Mira Silt}'s board lay a few paces off; his board lay where it lay; neither was the other's. The boy had set the clerk's order of reaching on a tray of its own months ago, apart from the woman in the grey coat at the flare, the grey-fingered man at the Drawing and the clerk behind the Court's table at \place{the Cross}, four hands in four places, and no sixth sighting of the neat dark hand had come to join them. The clerk did not look into any basket and did not speak, and the boy did not make a fifth of him or set him down into any woman he had seen before. A measure nobody had called was still a measure set down ahead of a death in this district; the order of things here put the slate down before the body arrived, and he kept the uncalled measure with the other things that stood ahead of a death and made nothing follow from it.

The measure sat on the landing the way a pot sits set out for a guest who has not come, and the market went around it with its creels and its jars, and the boy set the sitting measure beside the sitting pot in the Court's room and the sitting cup across the birth room, three vessels set down ahead of bodies that had not yet arrived or had already gone, and kept the three in three houses that did not join. A clerk who sets his slate down before he looks up is not doing a cruelty; he is doing the order of his trade as the boy had once done the order of his own, hand to chart before hand to patient, and the chart had been clean and the patient had died, and the two facts had sat side by side in a room where nobody made them agree. He did not forgive the clerk the order and did not condemn him for it. He entered it and went on.

\scenebreak

Mira worked the eels below the bridge with pale slime dried on her sleeves, silt in the hollow of her neck and her head held right where the top of her left ear had been, the small smile kept on her face the way a door is kept on the latch. The weir woman with the white wrists came down the out-of-line steps and asked whether the run would be thin now the water had gone off.

“Thin?” Mira said. “Who's to say thin? It comes when it comes. Ask the river.” She moved two eel-bones from hand to hand while she decided whether to lie and put one away when the deciding was done, and she did not look into the basket.

When a buyer named a figure her thumb went under her apron to the grey wool cord and stayed there while she decided; shifting a creel showed the second cord twisted twice beneath it, never carried upriver to \place{Nine Pots}, never lifted at a door, neither knot counted by any thumb but her own and the knot for the thin child upriver untouched. Her board lay beside \person{Orra Weir}'s knot-string and did not touch it. The boy kept the not-looking, the bones moving hand to hand and the thumb to the cord on three places of their own: a keeping, a deciding and a record, and not one of them a cause. Mira's not-looking was a keeping where the clerk's not-looking at his slate was a measure and the man on the block's had been a pricing, and he had no word in Grathic to tell one from the other and kept them apart without one. Three likenesses in a row will grow a cause whether a hand puts one there or not, and he had learned in a better-lit room what a man can make of three; so he kept the three on three tray-ends and let them stay three.

The bell came back from the Cross in the middle of the afternoon, four, four and a gap, longer than a church bell, and nobody crossed himself at it. The boy listened to it longer than he needed to, as he had listened to it through every market of his eighty-three days, because it was the only sound in the district that wanted nothing from him. He kept the heard bell beside the told bell of the Long Night story and both apart from the low note that came out of the stone when the river ran fast under its skin, and kept the told gaps with the intervals on the tray where the gaps lived. Six hearings of the \lore{full note} stood at six --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person --- beside and never among, and no seventh came to join them. Four things in four places, and the gap in each of them the part nobody had thought to enter. A Court instrument doing a temple's work, and the district's one word for the whole of it weather, and no cause written across any of it.

\scenebreak

At dusk Sella Vail carried him up \place{the Clay Yard} at the half-pace and set him on his bed under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. In the corner the clean dark line where the \lore{Ash} ridge had turned its own corner lay where it had always lain, and the new hairline of grey, finer than the old, lay beside it unswept, because the house said it was the thaw and it would go when it goes. She stood over both with a broom in her hand and swept neither, and gave the instruction to the broom and the fire and the wool and never to the basket, and did not touch the widow's mark, because a bed is not an opening and a dusk is not an hour for one. The carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees and missing one breath in every four, its payer still unnamed. Through the finger's width at the door came the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and he kept it in its own place apart from the fire's gap in the floor, as he had kept them apart for eighty-three days.

She said the thaw to the fire more than once that week, and the boy had stopped hearing it as an explanation and begun hearing it as a word she used the way another woman uses a hand over a child's mouth: not to hide the noise but to keep the cold out. The new grey was finer than the old line and lay beside it without touching, and the two of them made a record no roll could take up, the one line for what had been left and the other for what had come back on its own and been left too. He had learned in this yard that a house can keep a thing by leaving it where it fell, and keep it more surely than a house that puts the thing on a shelf and writes it down; a shelf can be emptied and a slate can be turned, but a line in a corner that nobody sweeps is a line the whole household steps around, and stepping around is a form of knowing. He kept the two lines on two ends of one tray and did not lay the thaw over either.

One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear with the empty place beside it still without a name. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. Corin was eighty-three days old and nobody had told him so. The count crossed toward the last day of the week, unspent.
