\daymark{82}{Silt 23, Y.H. 813}

The dark under the \place{Vail kiln-house} roof was cut in two by the finger's width at the door, as it had been every night of the boy's eighty-two days. Through the gap came the river's low muttered note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming. Through the floor came the carried fire, four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, warming the boards by degrees. He kept the two sounds where he had always kept them, each in its own place, and neither went into the other. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold at his ear with the empty place beside it still without a name. Cold arrived in its known order --- cheek, temple, wrist --- and the wool did less than the wind wanted, riding back from the wrist so the cold found the place. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the dark and came down. \person{Corin} was eighty-two days old and nobody had told him so.

Before the light was in \place{the Clay Yard} \person{Tarn} was already in it, moving in the dark with the speed of a man who has done the same thing at the same hour for six years and would rather be quick than seen. He set the small bench knife on its sill. The mark worn into the handle by his thumb had gone almost flat, six years of rubbing against one small place, and the seventh was nearly gone and still held, the one entry in this house the house could not check. “It's on the sill,” he told the empty yard. “You can check.” The yard did not answer and he had not expected it to. He was telling the yard so that the telling would stand whether or not anybody was there to hear it, the way a man sets a pot where it will be found and does not stay to watch the place.

He told the kindling what he told it each morning. “Six counted. The next I don't count.” He said it to the sticks in a flat voice, with no defiance in it and no request, and went on holding the seventh as a man holds a breath he has decided not to spend though his chest has already tightened around it. Something must give at the end of a seventh year. That much he had carried for six years as he carried the worn place in the knife handle, without looking at the end and without putting a date to it, and he did not write the end here. He set the knife where it could be checked and swept toward the day.

\scenebreak

\person{Sella Vail} lifted the boy down before the yard had properly woken, the little-door burn across the back of her hand closing first on nothing and then on his weight, and carried him at the half-pace she kept from every open slate. She gave the morning's instruction to the wool before she gave it to anybody, keep it off his ears, it's the river wind, and said \emph{the house} where another woman would have said I, and gave none of it to the basket, because a basket set down is a pot set down and left. The mud had gone hard in the night and kept the print of a boot without letting go, and the ruts stood with their edges sharp, so the yard looked less like a place that had been under water than a place that had been cut and left to dry. Behind \place[the drying racks]{the lowest rack} water went over water with the low drag of a thing that had somewhere to put what it carried, and the space under the rack was gone around as wide as a pail of hot water set where a child might knock it, and entered by nobody.

From the low angle of the basket the boy watched Tarn. He had learned to read a man's hands before his mouth, because hands know what a face lies about, and there was no hands in the yard more legible than this man's: the worn thumb-place on the knife, the grey in the creases, the pale ring at the left wrist. The surgeon in him read the term running out the way he had once read the date column of a chart --- six years entered and a seventh left blank, the blank doing more work than the six --- and read the tightness in the man's chest as a breath held against a pain that would be worse for being let go. He had held breaths like that himself, over open cavities in a white room, and knew the exact cost of the holding and the exact cost of the spending, and that one of them was always chosen and neither was ever free. He could put a hand to neither here. He had no hand that reached two paces and no word of \lore{Grathic} in which to say what he saw, and the reading worked and bought him nothing, as it had bought him nothing for eighty-two days. A man who owns nothing keeps one exact account, and the keeping is the only thing in the house the house cannot check; the boy had no standing to enter it and no wish to, and so he kept it for him.

\scenebreak

By the time the yard was properly awake the argument about the thaw had started up again at the tubs, as it did every morning now, a woman who wanted it early against a man who wanted it late, each certain the river was listening. “Put it early,” the woman said, “and the pits'll be worked by Kindling.” “Late,” said the man, “and the levy comes after the grass, not before.” They said it at the yard and not to each other, the way the yard says most things, and the man shut his mouth on the part that would have said what a late thaw really put off. Tarn went on with his broom and said \emph{Yes, house} to both, agreeing so entirely that no argument could find a foot to stand on. He agreed with the early because a quick thaw let the pits be worked, and with the late because a late thaw put off the paying, and had the two been put to him together as a single question he would have agreed with that too. The boy had learned that a man could hold both sides of a thing without either side noticing, and that the holding cost nothing in words and everything somewhere else. Tarn kept wanting the late one. A late thaw puts off the paying, though the paying had stood in the ruts all week, in the hard mud the broom went over and over without lifting a figure out of it.

The house kept its number the way it kept everything, badly and in materials no officer would think to read: loaves moved along the shelf, a glance at the clay jar on the wall, and a space at the row's end that nobody called a number. A neighbour come up from the road held a figure shut in her fist and let it go toward the well, where it arrived nowhere, as figures had arrived nowhere all week. The boy kept eighty-two against that arithmetic, one slate set edge to edge for each of the days, and let the figure lie where it fell. A figure read aloud is not the figure paid, and a figure let fall toward a well is not entered either; he had learned the two grammars in the same yard and kept them on separate trays that did not touch. The printed figure and the true subtraction lay side by side and went on not agreeing, and no hand in the yard joined them, and the week went on.

\lore{Ash} was worked into the creases of Tarn's knuckles in the colour of the clean dark line where the ridge had turned its corner, and no scrubbing lifted it. He had scraped at it with the small knife that morning and the Ash had come back into the same creases as though the knife had only pressed it further in, the way a thing kept out of an account comes back in another material. He held the pale clip-ring at his left wrist up to what light there was, and the ring held the light and gave it back, a small pale circle where the iron had sat since Y.H. 806. The boy had entered the ring among the house's objects without putting a name to it. A pale place where iron had been is a record as truly as a mark on a slate, and a record is a record whether the house can read it or not.

His broom went over the road a house sends a man down when it wants a collector, over and over, without once turning along it. The road lay in the hard mud with the two ruts the flood had cut and the drying had kept, and the broom crossed the mouth of it as though the mouth were a thing swept like any other thing, as though sweeping it could keep what lay down it from ever coming back up. No collector came down it for the woman the brook took, and no collector had come for the man under this roof two winters gone, and Tarn did not say that a thing which had not happened had a name. He did not go for one. The omission stood in the ruts with the rest of the week's arithmetic and was entered in no roll the house showed. \person{Garl Vail} was kept in this house by a salt-stiff coat on a peg, a hollow seat, a clean dark line and a proverb said to pots with no crack; he was not kept by a collector, and the boy had learned early that a house could keep a man in objects and let the man himself go uncollected without anybody finding the two facts contradictory.

\scenebreak

\person{Jorra Vail} was at the \place{Vail wheel-shed} before midday and the boy heard him before he saw him, the way a note comes through a wall. The door kept its finger's width of open and the sound of the wheel came through it, and with the wheel the flat instruction Jorra gave it each morning, \emph{It doesn't care whose hands. It cares if they're steady}. He did not come out onto the yard in this hour and the boy did not see him; a wheel turning in a shed is a wheel turning in a shed, and a room standing a finger's width open is a room standing a finger's width open, and neither is an invitation to a child who cannot walk. Sella carried the basket past the shed at the half-pace and gave the instruction to the wool and the fire and never to the basket, and the household straightened around the sound of the wheel as cloth straightens when a weight is lifted off it.

In the middle of the morning a vessel gave its note past the door, a whole vessel carried by somebody going about the yard, and the sound came across the mud and under the roof and into the basket. Tarn's head did not turn. His broom did not check. He did not name the sound in Grathic, and the boy did not call it a seventh hearing. He wanted the crust, and the breast, and the bucket two inches beyond his hand, and got none of them, and the wanting was louder than the reading as it had been from the first day; the infant and the surgeon had quarrelled over every object in the yard for eighty-two days and the infant had never once lost. Six hearings of the \lore{full note} stood at six --- five from four sources and the sixth out of a person --- beside and never among, and a note that was not turned toward had entered nobody's keeping. The boy kept Tarn's not-turning beside Jorra's not-looking of the day before and did not let the two touch, because two silences of one shape under one roof are an equality and an equality is not a cause. Three likenesses in a row will grow a cause whether a hand puts one there or not, and he had learned in a better-lit room to keep them apart.

The loan that can hear is a loan that can be called. That sentence had never been said under this roof and lay under it all the same, as the river's note lay under the door and the fire's gap lay under the floor. Two ears and two silences kept their equality without a word between them: a man who could hear a pot before anyone tapped it, and a boy who could hear a district straining under one slow certain failure, each keeping the other's secret without knowing the other kept anything at all. The ear was worth more than the debt that held it, which made it dangerous, and the loan stayed uncalled. Something must give at the end of a seventh year; the end was not written here, and the breath was not spent, and the yard went around its work with the paying still standing in the ruts.

At dusk Sella carried him up to the house and set him on his bed under the roof and not in \place{the Debt Loft}. She put the fold right at his ear and under his chin without looking at his face, and left the thread at his mouth frozen and unwiped, and gave the last instruction to the fire and the wool and the bucket and never to the basket. Behind her the small knife lay on its sill where it could be checked, with six counted and the seventh nearly gone and still held. On the floor the carried fire kept four, four and a gap under blind ash and the back of a broken pot, its payer still unnamed. Through the finger's width at the door came the river's note, nearer at the going of the water than at its coming, and the boy kept it in its own place apart from the fire's gap in the floor, as he had kept them apart for eighty-two days. One brown-edged crust lay stiff in the fold with the empty place beside it still without a name. His hand outside the wool went up an inch toward the water and came down. The count stood at eighty-two.
